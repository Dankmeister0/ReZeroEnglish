Arc 4 Chapter 43: "And Then There Were None—"

The thing that brought him back to consciousness was the sensation of water droplets falling on his face.

The cold water, striking his cheek in a steady rhythm, dragged his awareness upward. As his mind surfaced, the reality of being alive slowly seeped into Subaru's body.

The most unmistakable part of that reality was a primitive, intense stimulus that needed no words to explain—pain.

"...Guh."

As if to welcome Subaru's return to consciousness, the agony that had been waiting with open arms rushed in for an embrace. Once the first jolt hit, there was no way to dodge what followed in its wake.

His split forehead, his shattered right arm, his spine creaking from the violent impact—all of them screamed. But louder than any of those, the loudest scream of all came from—

"Sh-shit, that's the worst..."

When he turned his eyes to the source of the sharpest pain, he found a tree branch about two fingers thick had pierced clean through him near his right collarbone. Its tip glistened, stained red with blood, and when he tried to pull it out despite the agony he knew it would cause, his flesh had clenched too tight around it—it wouldn't budge an inch.

It was a small mercy that it had snapped partway through; ignoring the visual and the pain, it wasn't the kind of thing that would actually impede his movement.

"...This fashion statement is a little too out there, don't you think."

Somehow, forcing a body that refused to obey, he hauled his upper body upright, leaned his back against the nearby rock wall, and let out a breath. Looking around, it seemed he was near the mouth of some small cave. Dew, or something like it, had apparently been dripping down from above the entrance and striking his cheek. *Dew*, he registered, and then—

"Morning...!?"

His brain registered the merciless passage of time, and the moment his body flinched, agony tore through it. A sensation like the back of his eyes flushing crimson, like needles driven into every inch of him. Tears welled in his one remaining eye as, slowly, understanding caught up with Subaru's thoughts.

What had happened to him before his mind went dark. He remembered it, and—

"—Ah."

Subaru remembered just how meaningless a massacre his own existence had wrought.

Hesitantly he lifted his gaze skyward; through the gaps in the trees, the dawn light was spilling into the forest. And there, catching that light, on the far side of the slope down which Subaru had tumbled—what sight could possibly be waiting there?

"——Nn."

Holding his breath, tormented by a regret that made him wish he could die right then and there, Subaru crawled forward at the pace of a caterpillar, heading for the other side.

Restricted in movement by the branch impaling him, slow but certain, the moment drew nearer.

The old Subaru would have imagined the sight waiting there and quailed, refusing to look, running away. But the Subaru of now was not permitted that.

He had to witness it, swallow it, and make it his fuel.

That was the duty of Natsuki Subaru, who had failed to die when he should have.

"Haah... haah..."

He crawled up the slope one step, then another, using only the left half of his body. His breath came ragged, and the dried wound on his forehead wept fresh blood, mixed with sweat. He wiped it away roughly with his sleeve and crawled on, his face smeared with blood and mud.

He passed alongside part of the wrecked Dragon Carriage, skirted a felled great tree, and at last his fingertips reached the height he was aiming for—the ground where Patrasche's devotion had thrown him clear.

"——"

For just a moment, he hesitated.

If he lifted his head, craned his neck, and looked over to the other side, Subaru would come face to face with a reality he could not escape. He would no longer be permitted to retreat into imagination, to indulge in the fantasy that after Subaru had been thrown clear, some miracle had occurred and most of the refugees had escaped alive.

"Am I an idiot. ...No—I *am* an idiot."

With only this one eye's field of view, Subaru had clearly seen the moment Patrasche was torn apart by fangs. The instant that loyal dragon, who had thrown away everything for Subaru's sake, was lost—it was still burned into the back of his eyelids even now. To call that a dream, to retreat into a convenient illusion, would be an insult to her, who had burned the last embers of her own life down to nothing for Subaru's sake.

Lighting a flame of grim resolve in his heart, Subaru scraped together what little willpower remained and opened his eyes. Dragging his upper body up in a posture like he was gnawing at the dirt, he hauled himself over a thick, curving branch blocking his view, and beyond the open forest, the scene of the massacre—

"——Eh?"

There was nothing there.

There was nothing at all.

"That... ah, eh?"

Subaru, his face already twisted in anticipation of the horror he imagined would greet him, blinked rapidly, unable to accept the sight before his eyes.

Scattered wreckage of the Dragon Carriage. Several great trees uprooted and overturned. Deep claw marks gouged into the ground, traces of destruction and violence strewn across the whole area.

And yet the one thing that would have broken Subaru's heart most completely was absent—the results.

The aftermath of the carnage. The remains of the villagers who had, in the truest sense, staked their lives to let Subaru escape. The corpse of the earth dragon, torn in two after giving its all in loyal service.

Nowhere. They were nowhere to be found.

"——"

That the clash with the beast had been neither dream nor illusion was proven by the wreckage of the Dragon Carriage scattered across the scene. And yet, from that scene, only the *results* of the carnage were missing.

Unsteadily, he leaned on a nearby tree to haul himself upright. Fortunately, once he'd weathered that first jolt of impact, aside from bruises and scrapes there seemed to be no notable injury to his legs or hips. He stood, and, steadying his throbbing right arm with his left hand each time he swayed, looked around.

"Why? Where's Patrasche... everyone... Otto?"

It wasn't that he wanted to see corpses, of course.

If he were honest, nothing would have pleased him more than for everyone to have survived. But Subaru's own body knew, better than anyone, that the situation had not been one where such a fairy tale could come true.

Above all, before he'd lost consciousness, Subaru had already seen several lives claimed by that beast's hand.

A gaunt young man, fighting with everything he had, crushed without landing so much as a scratch. A woman, blown clear along with the carriage, thrown out to her death. An old man, snapped like a dead branch by a single swipe of a claw, left an unbearably wretched corpse.

Every time he recalled those deaths, pain and regret gnawed away at Subaru's heart. And yet even those deaths, which he himself had witnessed, had been stolen from this place.

"Patrasche... Patrasche...?"

Thinking of the lives that had been lost, Subaru called desperately for his partner in a thin, cracked voice.

He had truly seen it—had heard it—the moment her body was torn in two, the high, anguished death cry that rose from her. So he held no fragile hope that her life might somehow remain. And yet, even so, finding the remains of her soul-emptied body, delivering his words of apology, and giving her a proper burial—that was something only Subaru could do.

His pace was slow, his strength nearly spent. His search crawled along, weak and halting; just circling the area took him more than two hours.

And yet, for all that time spent, all Subaru could find in the vicinity was—

"Some baggage mixed in with the wreckage, scraps of cloth, and... that's it."

An overwhelming amount of bloodstains.

As if to confirm the near-certainty already forming in his mind, the site where the beast's claw marks had been gouged into the earth bore great pools of blood scattered here and there. There was likely a choking stench of blood hanging over the area, but Subaru's sense of smell, caked and clotted with his own dried blood, wasn't functioning—he couldn't catch any of it.

The circumstantial evidence had piled up to the point of being undeniable. All that was missing was decisive proof, and there were plenty of mysteries surrounding how even that had vanished.

Above all, as he searched the area like this, a question that had come far, far too late began burning itself into the back of Subaru's mind. Namely—

"Why wasn't I killed...?"

That Garfiel had failed to finish him off—it might be hard to believe that Subaru, wounded as badly as he was, could have survived at all. But even so, to return without so much as checking a single corpse was far too sloppy in the part that mattered most. To begin with, Garfiel's target should have been Subaru.

Why those claws had turned on the refugees as well remained a mystery even now, though perhaps it had been meant as a warning aimed at Subaru.

If that were the case, all the more reason the disappearance of the bodies made no sense.

"Even assuming they were carried off somewhere..."

There had been forty-two refugees in total. Even assuming all of them had become corpses, carrying every single one of them off wasn't realistic. And that would include the earth dragons too, Patrasche among them.

"But then again..."

He didn't want to imagine it, but the possibility that the beast had eaten them all—that too ran into the same problem of sheer numbers, making it unrealistic. The idea of them being carried off was still easier to swallow, but he couldn't think of any reason worth that much effort just to hide the bodies from Subaru.

And before he even got to doubting whether that beast would have done something so roundabout, there was the more pressing question of why it had left him, covered in wounds as he was, without delivering the finishing blow.

"——"

Then, abruptly, Subaru realized this bore a striking resemblance to the state the Sanctuary had been in when it turned empty of people.

The preconditions were different from before, but the results shared a lot in common. The traces of destruction all around were the marks of the great tiger's rampage, unrelated to the disappearance of the refugees and the beast itself. Setting aside those visible traces, this—with not a single soul in sight—closely resembled that earlier situation.

Which meant—

"Th-the Sanctuary... is it in the same state as before, too...?"

Reaching that conclusion through ragged breaths, Subaru once again summoned every last ounce of strength to stand. Then, checking his position and the lay of the road, he got a bearing on the direction of the Sanctuary.

—It was now the morning of the sixth day.

At the mansion, the deadline had likely been the previous night. He couldn't be certain, but if Elsa's assault had come, then an irreversible catastrophe would already have occurred.

And something had happened at the Sanctuary as well—something that had made Garfiel, turned into that great tiger, abandon finishing Subaru off. That, most likely, was the reason every presence had vanished from this place. Why it hadn't affected Subaru, and what its true nature was—all of that remained a complete mystery.

"——"

There was only a moment's hesitation over which direction to head.

What crossed his mind was a faint ache rooted in warmth. The faces he'd left behind in the mansion, likely already under attack, and the unresolved regret and guilt for the sleeping girl there.

Gritting his teeth, Subaru shook off the feeling and set his feet toward the Sanctuary.

Slow, at a pace far too sluggish, as if testing each step, Subaru made his way toward the Sanctuary.

Whatever waited ahead, he intended to burn it into his eyes as something worth the value of the lives he'd lost—in search of whatever trigger, however slight, might give even the faintest chance of victory to a life he meant to spend entirely on atonement.

—On the way to the Sanctuary, he passed through a place where Ram and Garfiel appeared to have fought.

Traces of wind-blades storming through were carved into the trunks of trees all around, and familiar claw marks had gouged the ground and rock with even greater violence—a scene of devastation.

He circled the area a little, but there, too, Ram was nowhere to be found—her form, likely reduced to a corpse by now, simply wasn't there.

If the guess that the great tiger was Garfiel was correct, then Ram was, to him, someone he'd carried feelings for over many years. If that affection ran deep, there was a chance, he'd wanted to believe.

"Getting to the point of trying to kill each other, and still going on about liking someone or whatever... I've read too many light novels, apparently."

Theirs was a relationship where neither could yield something to the other, and it had escalated all the way to a fight to the death.

Whatever love or affection had existed between them—how much of a deterrent could that possibly be? If it really were enough to stay a hand wielding a weapon, then it should have stopped things before they ever began.

Having started without that stopping it, it couldn't very well serve as a reason for it to end.

"...Sorry."

He offered an apology to someone whose form he could not see, someone who had nonetheless spent everything she had for Subaru's sake.

Her hard-fought battle rendered meaningless, Subaru turned her feelings to waste and returned toward the Sanctuary. Because what he needed—for a Subaru who intended to die soon—lay ahead.

Adding yet more regrets, Subaru pressed on with all his strength toward the Sanctuary. The road that he'd presumably set out on before noon was a distance that, astride Patrasche, would have taken barely ten-odd minutes.

Pushing his wounded body through it at the pace of a caterpillar, it was nearly evening by the time Subaru finally reached the vicinity of the Sanctuary.

"Took... half a day... but I finally... made it."

He'd made it back this far.

He nearly collapsed on the spot from relief, but there was not a shred of accomplishment in it. If anything, what welled up fiercely inside Subaru was helplessness and anger at himself. And rising darker still, outshining even that self-loathing, was—

"So you came back here too, huh... Garfiel...!?"

—a searing hatred and fury toward that blond bastard he'd been telling himself, over and over, not to think about.

There were three major reasons he'd returned to the Sanctuary.

One: he wanted to get to the bottom of the mysterious phenomenon that kept emptying the Sanctuary of people. At the very least, he wanted to grasp some piece of its cause—a necessary element for the future.

Two: to confirm the safety of Emilia, whom he'd left behind in the Sanctuary. If the depopulation phenomenon had struck again, her safety was no exception. This, too, tied into the first reason, and he wanted to confirm it.

And three—a reason so fierce it dwarfed the first two combined, a flame scorching the very depths of Subaru's heart.

The explosive urge to tear that violent, monstrous tiger limb from limb.

He stepped into the Sanctuary. Two moss-grown pillars served as its entrance. Passing through them into the Sanctuary, Subaru repeated low, quiet breaths as he looked around.

As silence fell over the Sanctuary, there was, as he'd more or less expected, no sense of human presence. Even before that—throughout the walk here through the forest, Subaru hadn't heard so much as a single insect's cry.

The disappearance of the Sanctuary's residents wasn't the extent of it; the whole area maintained a stillness as though every living creature's activity in this whole stretch of land had simply stopped.

"——"

Even his own breathing felt too loud against this silence, and Subaru demanded the utmost stealth from a throat that wanted to seize up with pain. Repeating short, unbroken breaths, Subaru dragged his feet toward the deepest part of the Sanctuary—the building where Emilia should have been.

—At this hour, before evening, Emilia would be spending the time until the Trial hugging her knees. It was a habit that had become pronounced past the third day, and this time in particular, it was happening with no support from Subaru at all. Likely, more than in any previous loop, her heart had been worn down by loneliness and anxiety.

"Not here, huh..."

Pushing the door open, peering inside, surveying the empty interior, Subaru murmured.

There was no sign of Emilia, but the sheets on the unused bed were thoroughly disheveled, and an overturned chair lay on the floor just as it had fallen. Whether this was the result of resistance against the depopulation, or the product of a cornered Emilia's own impulse, he couldn't say.

Still—on the road here, Subaru still hadn't run into anyone at all.

"Should I go check on Roswaal...?"

Having accepted that Emilia wasn't here, Subaru's mind, weighing his next move, was eerily calm.

The course of action he voiced was necessary in the sense of ruling things out, but inwardly, part of him had half-resigned himself to the fact that heading there too would be a wasted trip.

The Sanctuary had emptied out just as he'd feared. The moment Emilia's presence—the thing that should have been here—was lost, Subaru's attachment to this place was already gone.

And the disappearance of Emilia, the very thing he should have been attached to—even to that, Subaru realized his own heart was barely stirring.

Had he attained some unshakable, tempered, gleaming heart of steel that nothing could move?

*No, that's not it,* Subaru immediately shook his head.

A heart of steel—the thing Subaru aspired to—was far too different from this current state of mind that had simply lost anywhere to put its emotions. Out of sheer rage, out of the impossibility of understanding, worn down to nothing, his heart had simply blown a fuse.

It wasn't that it wouldn't waver. It was that it had been hollowed out entirely.

—A lack of the will to live.

Of course it was.

The current Subaru wasn't living because he wanted to live. He simply couldn't die until he'd made up for the reason he had failed to die and lived on when he should have.

If anything, it wasn't the will to live but the will to die that his mind was fixed on.

In a world like this, how could anyone go on surviving?

Emilia wasn't here. Rem was gone too. Patrasche had been lost, and Ram and Petra had probably died. Whether Otto lived or died wasn't even worth considering.

No one was left. Everyone was gone. Because Subaru's head wasn't good enough, because Subaru's strength wasn't enough, because Subaru's effort wasn't enough, because Subaru's wish wasn't enough, he couldn't save anyone. No one was saved. And it was something only Subaru could have done.

"That's why... I have to..."

Take everything back. See everything through. Set everything on the right path—that was his responsibility.

Something only Subaru could do. Something Subaru had to do.

The sacrifices paid for that had to remain within Subaru, forever.

Everything lost for that, only Subaru had to go on remembering.

The price paid for that, only Subaru had to go on paying.

He would pay the price. He would pile up the sacrifices. And he would take back everything.

"——"

Swaying, his upper body unsteady, Subaru stepped out of the building.

His feet turned not toward the cottage where Roswaal was convalescing, but toward the deepest part of the Sanctuary—the direction of the graveyard. The place where, in the previous loop, when the Sanctuary had emptied out and Subaru had tried to go there, he'd been interfered with and killed by "something." And once again, this time, he shuffled his way toward it, heedless of the danger.

For what purpose? Of course—to be killed.

If he set up the same conditions as before, whatever it was would likely try to kill him by the same means.

And if he knew it was coming, he judged, he ought to be able to at least dodge a blow that would be instantly fatal.

Even if he ended up being killed on the second strike, so long as he could get a grasp on his attacker's identity, that would be enough.

Resolved to face death, Subaru advanced step by step, steadily, toward his destination.

The place where he'd been run through from behind, through the stomach—he didn't remember the exact spot, but he was certain it had been just before reaching the entrance to the graveyard.

The top of the graveyard came into view in the distance, and at once his heart began to pound, pumping blood both cold and hot through his entire body. Whether he was heating up or cooling down, he couldn't even tell.

His body burned, his limbs went numb. And yet his fingertips felt stiff, as if cold lead had been forced into them, while his mind had cooled enough to view the situation with a strange objectivity.

Knowing full well he would die, walking into the field of death—a foolish existence.

He had sworn to himself, firmly, that he would stake his life to obtain results. And yet his expression was far from the strength of that resolve; the corners of his eyes drooped, he bit his lip, and his limbs trembled beyond his ability to suppress it.

He hated and cursed himself, the plating peeling away at the eleventh hour to expose the weakness beneath. Pushing those feelings aside, Subaru still didn't slow his pace.

However weak, however frail, however foolish a self he could not change—he wanted to always be the self who found the courage to move forward from that self anyway.

With a warped kind of forward momentum, like weakness multiplied by weakness, a negative times a negative, Subaru kept treading the path to the graveyard, the path toward death, as if to make certain of every step.

The graveyard drew closer. His heartbeat quickened, and he could hear, with startling clarity, the sound of blood coursing inside his skull. A sensation of stomach acid backing up from his empty stomach and searing his throat. His trembling knees felt on the verge of giving out at any moment, and sweat clouded the clarity of his one remaining field of vision.

He roughly raised his left arm and wiped his eyes, then faced forward again. And then, he noticed.

"—Huh?"

Just as he lifted his foot to step toward the graveyard, Subaru saw a change occur before his eyes.

Not even the cry of an insect could be heard; all that reached him was the occasional rustle of leaves brushing together in the wind. Into that, something suddenly intruded—a small, faltering cry.

At first, Subaru thought it was a small tuft of white fur that had tumbled in on the wind.

But the tuft of fur came to a stop a few paces ahead of Subaru and began trembling in small twitches, and then—before Subaru's dubiously furrowed brow—it raised two long ears.

"A... a rabbit...?"

A small creature with two long ears and soft, white fur. Its two round red eyes were its most distinctive feature; it moved its mouth in small, restless motions as it looked busily around, and when it looked up at Subaru, it tilted its little head and let out a high-pitched cry.

A small rabbit. An impossibly small one. It was about the size of Subaru's clenched fist—at a glance, not much bigger than a hamster. Its ears were large enough to rival the rest of its body in size, which made "palm-sized" a bit of a misleading description.

Insects, people, earth dragons—every trace of every living thing had vanished from this place, and yet here, suddenly, a rabbit had appeared.

It might have simply been a creature living somewhere in the forest, but to Subaru, who hadn't encountered a single other living thing the whole way here, its appearance felt far too improbable to be a coincidence.

"Why is there a rabbit here, of all... it is a rabbit, right?"

His questions had no end, and Subaru, bewildered, looked around, searching to see whether any creature besides this rabbit before him had wandered into the Sanctuary. Then, without particularly thinking about it, he reached his hand toward the rabbit, meaning to touch its fur in hopes of learning something—

"——"

The next moment, everything from Subaru's left wrist down had been torn clean off.

Blood sprayed from the rough, ragged stump, and blue-green blood vessels dangled from the wound. Those thin white strands—muscle fiber or nerve, he couldn't tell which—either way, the sight of a human body being destroyed was grotesque, and for a few seconds he tried to escape into denial of the reality of it.

Then agony on a wholly different plane struck Subaru's brain like a fist, and his body, writhing in pain, collapsed to the ground. The branch piercing his collarbone snapped further from the impact, adding a fresh, sharp stab of pain. Pain, pain, pain.

"Gah!? Ah, ohh! Oh, ohhhh, aghhhhh!!"

His thoughts went white-hot.

*It hurts, it hurts, the feeling has taken over his whole body it hurts he can't even properly grasp reality anymore it hurts why does it have to hurt this much why does he have to suffer this much it hurts where did it come from it hurts what happened where is this it hurts where is this why it hurts what part of him hurts why in the world it hurts it hurts it hurts it hurts it hurts it hurts it hurts it hurts—*

Writhing and writhing, pressing his blood-gushing left wrist against the ground, Subaru unconsciously bit into the dirt, chewing mud in some senseless, meaningless act. The bitterness of the soil and the suffocation brought a sliver of thought drifting back; his vision spun, searching for what had happened, and Subaru spotted, at his own feet, the little white tuft of fur from before—its white coat now stained with red flecks, its tiny mouth stuffed full, cheeks bulging beneath its black nose. Poking out from its mouth, he could see—the little finger of his left hand.

He understood. He got it. What had happened. He'd been eaten. Eaten. It had eaten him.

"Guh, gaahhh!!"

Letting out a voice on the verge of madness with both understanding and pain, Subaru rolled his body toward the rabbit. His right arm was broken, useless; his left arm ended at the wrist, the rest inside the rabbit's belly. There was nothing he could really do, but at least, to confirm what this thing truly was—

A searing sensation ripped through his calf. A stimulus so sharp it was like being scraped by a merciless file down to muscle and bone—no joke, his eyes rolled back white, foam bursting from the back of his throat. If only his head could just drop and let him black out, but the sheer intensity of the agony refused to let go of his consciousness.

Blood-foam spilling from the corners of his mouth, he convulsed like a fish hauled onto land. That his ears were still functioning at this point was a miracle—an act of divine mischief far too cruel.

Pit-pat, pit-pat—countless footsteps caught against his eardrums.

Hopping movements. Small, light bodies. The number of overlapping cries was immense—even if his vision had still been intact, he doubted he would have had the will to count them.

And it was truly fortunate that, in this moment, only his ears were still functioning.

At the same instant, he felt fangs sink into his flesh across his entire body, and Subaru realized, through the pain of being chewed, that the threat surrounding him now numbered well over a hundred.

A scream. Rolled onto his back, he stared up at the sky, throat trembling. In an instant, a furry creature forced its way in through his open mouth. His tongue was bitten off, sharp fangs slashed through the back of his throat, and the path from esophagus to stomach was devoured from the inside.

A threat that had invaded through his anus collided within his body with the one that had entered from above, and as though competing with each other, they devoured his organs left and right, mincing Natsuki Subaru to pieces.

The reality of being turned, while still alive, into fragments of meat inside living creatures' bodies.

There was no more fear now. He no longer felt pain. He didn't know why he was even still conscious.

Being eaten. Being devoured. His left eye was eaten. His ears were already gone. Every organ devoured entirely, and now the skin of his face was being peeled away. A hole opened in his skull, and fangs pushed in as if to lap up the brain matter threatening to spill out—
