Scorpion Tale Chapter 3: "Memories of Beard"

1

—Leilani's existence had become something exceedingly complicated for Shaula.

"Sure, we go way back, but I've got Master's instructions and a Grand Order from Mama, so I can't be givin' you special treatment."

—So she said, keeping the top priority within herself unmoved; but Shaula was well aware that every part outside of that carried no persuasive weight whatsoever. She'd been beaten to a pulp so thoroughly that she couldn't even reach for the "It is truly regrettable, but my secretary did it without my knowledge" excuse.

"Shaula-sama, it is a presumptuous request, but might I borrow your hand?"

"My hand~? What's this, you wanna go toddling around again? Wouldn't it be better for the baby in your belly if you just sat around spacing out and behaving yourself?"

"Undiscerning and unmindful though I am, I have humbly surmised that moving my body somewhat would be for these children's benefit as well. Within a tower I am unaccustomed to, should something happen it could never be undone, so if I might borrow your strength…"

"But there's no reason I've gotta go along with—"

"Unworthy though I am, I am a woman with child."

"You sure do make convenient use of that swollen belly, 'Leilani'!"

In fact, however stubbornly Shaula wanted to refuse, the instant Leilani used the child in her belly as a shield, "his" orders and Shaula's own feelings would spark against each other inside her. Naturally, in that clash her own feelings never stood a chance of winning; her record was in tatters.

In the end, this time too she lent her hand to the triumphant Leilani and wound up walking a circuit of the fourth layer.

"If you seriously wanna exercise, then instead of this piddly little stroll, how about an all-out sprint up the spiral staircase between the fourth and fifth layers! Wouldn't that be way better?"

"My apologies. Unlike yourself, who are so hale and robust, for the legs of a poorly made woman such as me that would be aiming far too high. Moreover, were I to exert myself recklessly, I might go into labor with these children partway up the stairs."

"Well, if that happens, why not just pop 'em out quick? Then I could send you go-away past the Sea of Sand without feeling guilty about it."

"Unruly and unstudied as I am, even I wish to respect your feelings as far as I am able… but the uncleanness of this place gives me pause. Presumptuous though it be, if as a mother I am to wish for these children's happiness, I would attend their birth in a place and at a time befitting it."

Gently, with the hand opposite the one Shaula was pulling, Leilani stroked her own belly. While Shaula thought, *huh, so that's what her wishes look like*, she couldn't quite help wondering: if you started saying things like that, wouldn't it mean there was nowhere at all you could give birth?

Leilani seemed to sense that doubt of Shaula's from the furrow scrunched between her brows. "Just as your noble thoughts suggest, Shaula-sama… that crucial moment is very slow in coming. Which is why I have already been carrying these children for three years."

"Three years! Haah~, that sounds like a chore. Having some other living thing inside my belly, with my freedom getting limited because of it — forget a day, I'd hate that for even a second."

Picturing herself with her prized waistline gone and her belly bulging, Shaula was seized by violent revulsion and rejected the possibility with a "Bleugh," tongue stuck out.

Shaula loved "him," and she never ceased believing in a future where she and "he" grew intimate — but on the other hand, the notion of wanting a child with "him" had never occurred to her. If "he" absolutely insisted on one, that was another matter, but of her own accord she hadn't the slightest wish to bear a child.

"If my belly went and got all swollen and I couldn't make it in time when Master's in a pinch, that'd be no joke, and with Master's real luck, that kinda thing could totally happen."

"…It is because you hold that person so dear that you do not wish for it, is it not."

"When you put it all nice like that, yeah! Also, anyone besides me getting loved by Master is just an obstacle, so Master's love is my monopoly market."

She thought it as she was saying it, but looked at that way, humans were strange creatures.

Making a child with someone you loved could well be an act that reduced the affection you received from that someone. What's more — unthinkable from Shaula's standpoint — there was even the risk that the child's existence would shrink the portion of affection you handed over to that someone.

Wanting a child with the one you loved badly enough to shoulder risks like that—

"—Do you really love them, though?"

"—"

"It's like a variant of that thing 'Leilani' talked about before, how if you only ever eat appa you stop tasting it eventually… wanting an orage once in a while, isn't that just cheating?"

Yes — just as she had back then, Shaula felt like cocking her head.

She'd thought so then, but now she thought so even more. Because her unchanging love for "him" was the very reason Shaula had managed to keep at being the Star Watcher for several hundred years.

"—"

Listening to Shaula, Leilani had stopped walking and lowered her eyes. Shaula frowned at her reaction with a "Hmm~?", and after mulling it over for a while, belatedly realized that what she'd just said had sounded exactly like a reproach aimed at Leilani, who was in fact carrying a baby.

"Ah~, it's not like I was saying Leilani did anything wrong, okay? But what happened to the hubby? One human alone shouldn't be able to make a baby."

"…I was parted from these children's father by death, through my own carelessness. For unworthy me, these children were the only trace I could leave behind of that person."

"I see, I see, so it's that type."

If the baby's father was gone, then Shaula could more or less understand why Leilani would seek his likeness in the children. She could more or less understand it, but—

"Even if Master vanished, I don't think I could come up with something as gross as consoling myself with a stand-in for Master, so that's pretty amazing."

She wouldn't think of it in the first place, but the sheer drive to actually carry it out once thought of was impressive. Even if she couldn't endorse the result in the slightest, the value system itself was astonishing.

"…Thank you very much."

At Shaula's assessment, Leilani smiled faintly and offered her thanks. —For someone giving thanks, her face hadn't looked much like it, Shaula thought, but to Shaula that was a trivial matter.

Falling in step with Leilani as she resumed walking, she cooperated with the stroll that took them all the way around the fourth layer.

However—

"—Shaula-sama, what has become of that closed room?"

What caught Leilani's attention along the way was one of the stone chambers whose entrance had been sealed shut.

It was the stone chamber Shaula had used for carrying out her "daily routine," and also the place she had vowed to seal forever, its door shut fast.

"If the memory of one as unaccustomed and unfamiliar as I is correct, the entrances of the other rooms all stood open, so its being closed caught my attention…"

"Hmm~, it's not for any big reason, and there's nothing big in there either, so just figure it's a place that's no big deal and let it slide."

"Is that so? But…"

"Just let it slide."

Shaula cut off Leilani, who had tried to press the point with unnecessary curiosity, with a second refusal.

Feeling that her tone had come out harder than she'd intended, Shaula realized that, apparently, she didn't want that "daily routine" of hers known.

Thinking about it that way, letting the sealed stone chamber be noticed by "Leilani" — violent, high-handed, brimming with curiosity and drive — had been a blunder from the very start.

"Well, the details don't matter, so let's get moving. Ah~, there's one more room that connects to the third layer, and that one's off-limits too."

"Understood. There is no need for concern; I shall commit no such insincerity, so please set your mind at ease."

"Is that so, I wonder. Sounds fishy to me."

At the very least, from their reunion up to now, she'd been jerked around nonstop by "Leilani's" every word and deed. Praying there wouldn't be any more of it — a sentiment hardly befitting one who bore the name of a star — she tugged on the woman's arm.

2

"—That's quite a reckless argument. Still, it's an opinion worth thinking about."

Saying so with an intrigued laugh, the bearded man traced his reddish-brown chin whiskers with a finger.

Given the flow of the conversation up to that point, Shaula didn't understand why Beard had laughed like that. She'd brought it up half expecting him to get angry.

Beard, with one crippled leg, whose body couldn't walk properly without a cane. —In such a feeble state as a living creature, how could he go on living so unbothered?

"Beard, who has trouble walking, and Mama, who has trouble living — to me it's like, isn't being alive a total pain for you? That's why I asked."

"Setting aside your assessment of Witch-kun, your question is a proposition that cuts deep for me as well. …No — that there's a proposition that cuts mighty deep for me an' all."

"Oh, the goofy accent came out."

Shaula reacted to Beard, who had restated himself as though suddenly remembering, all while stroking his chin whiskers.

Lately, Beard had been deliberately tinkering with his way of speaking like this. Honestly, it was a manner of speech that couldn't shake its goofy impression, but Shaula had no objection to it whatsoever.

"'Cause it was Master who came up with it. If it's a dazzling idea overflowing out of Master's head, then no matter how freakish it is, my big love affirms it one hundred percent!"

"Callin' it freakish is a bit harsh, ain't it. I'm bein' real careful in the selectin' so it don't turn out that way. …Well, I suppose it can't be helped if it mutates as it gets used."

"Oh, he's back."

The way Beard shuttled back and forth between his normal speech and the goofy speech was entertaining. When he was in goofy mode his gestures and expressions got exaggerated too, which made for good viewing.

"If that's how ya feel about it, then developin' this accent was worth the trouble."

At Shaula's assessment, Beard smiled in high spirits, as if to say *exactly as I intended*. —Somehow, it was unbearably irritating.

"Hi-yah."

"Gwah! W-why did you just jab me in the ribs?"

"That smug face of yours got on my nerves. Plus I didn't like being treated like a cheap woman who moves exactly the way you planned."

"The second half is a false accusation, and I'd appreciate being forgiven for a little smugness in the first."

*Thwack* — having taken the fingertip strike, Beard leaned his back against a nearby wall and sighed.

Seeing that, Shaula pouted, "Again~?", as his walked-himself-tired break time activated. Lacing her hands behind her head, she idly looked up at the stone wall Beard had put his back to.

That great stone rampart, wrapped snugly around all four sides of the town, was a thing that hadn't even existed until a mere half-month ago. Originally, all that stood around the town was a shabby iron fence, low and unreliable — the kind Shaula could have hopped over in a single bound.

A protective barrier like that couldn't hold back the coming beast calamity. —That desperate appeal bore fruit, and permission was granted to build these white ramparts.

Although—

"We had to build it at a crazy pace, but seriously, Mama and Reid are monsters."

Hugging her bare shoulders, Shaula recalled with a shiver how the ramparts had gone up. The necessary stone, Mama had cut out of a distant canyon with magic and hauled over; and once it encircled all four sides of the town, the redhead — hungover from heavy drinking — had grumbled his way through slicing it into shape.

The result was a defense for the town that, while crude and utterly without charm, was sturdy and unyielding.

And that rampart, which disregarded sunlight rights and everything else wholesale, proved tremendously effective against the foreseen beast calamity and splendidly rescued the town from the brink of annihilation.

"Ever since then the way people look at us has changed. What a mercenary bunch."

"Can't be helped. It's the first time we've set foot in this land, and I'm well aware we look like a suspicious lot at a glance. You included, of course."

"And the second those guys turn out useful, they flip their palms over. If you ask me, that's how a mob character lives. Pitiful creatures, tossed around by the tides of the era."

"Harsh."

Beard gave a wry smile at Shaula, who pouted as she pointed out the fickleness of the human heart.

In any case, it was only natural for the townspeople to show them gratitude. Especially considering the role this Beard had played — no amount of improved treatment could be too much.

After all, it was Beard's gift for persuasion that had talked the townsfolk around — those who had stubbornly refused to accept the rampart's construction or the reality of the beast calamity — and won their consent. In that regard the blond and "he" had been considerable presences as well, but the central figure had been Beard. Of course, in the actual repulsion of the beast calamity, Mama's and the redhead's contributions loomed large, but this victory could never be spoken of without the rampart.

The reason Beard was now trying to tour the post-calamity town on his own two legs was surely that same sense of responsibility for having carried the success or failure of this operation.

"But being dragged along on this dawdling walk is more than I can take. Honestly, you're too frail. How many intervals are you gonna cram in along the way?"

"Awful sorry for the trouble. But there weren't nobody else but you. Try walkin' around with Reid-kun sometime. That fella's a genius at startin' a riot on flat ground."

"He's just a super-nuisance jerk. Well, compared to that, Alec is ten thousand times better."

"Ten thousand times — now that's a generous figure. Shame there's no reproducibility when Reid-kun's involved."

Shaula had stopped pointing out each and every one of Beard's deliberate bursts of accent. And flattery aside, it was a fact that she found this ten thousand times better than being hauled around by the redhead.

In truth, since the beast calamity more townspeople called out to Shaula and the others, but when she was with the redhead — savior of the town though he was — a certain percentage of what flew their way was cursing and abuse. And since the redhead would fire back with filthy language of his own, that man was utterly unsuited to a lady's education.

By contrast, when walking with Beard as now, the voices calling out were mostly friendly and favorable, and Shaula, being at his side, got to share in the spillover — appa and the like pressed into her hands — which wasn't half bad.

Yes — and while she was busy comparing two companions who didn't even bear comparison, the redhead and Beard—

"Why go on livin' when ya can't live without borrowin' other folks' hands… eh."

At the murmur that suddenly slipped out of Beard, Shaula — who had been waiting for his break to end — creased her brow with an "Ahn?" and puzzled over what the murmur meant.

At her reaction, Beard smiled wryly. "No, no. You're the one who said it, ain't ya. Askin' why me and Witch-kun go on livin' while causin' trouble for folks."

"The nuance is a bit off. I said that when you're that weak, being alive is a pain, so with what face are you still living."

"That's a sight more brutal than I'd been thinkin'!"

Beard retorted in a loud voice, but to Shaula it was a natural way of thinking — from the very beginning, the purpose Shaula had been made for was to protect "him," to serve "him," to live for "him." To fulfill that purpose, toughness and physicality were required subjects.

In other words, within Shaula, those stats carried a high degree of importance.

"So I'm skeptical about what living means for the guys whose numbers are low."

"Witch-kun is technically the reason you were able to be born into this world, y'know. Even so?"

"I'm seriously grateful to Mama for creating me. So it's more like, good work, now take it easy."

"That's an unshakable set of values, that is. …I envy ya."

At the words of Beard, who shrugged along with his sigh, Shaula closed one eye and looked closely at his face. The accent made certain nuances hard to read, but even so, Beard's words didn't seem to be sarcasm or a jab of any sort — they seemed to come from the heart.

Having taken it that way, Shaula put a hand to her forehead with an "Ah~." "Wait, did it hurt your feelings, me treating you like a useless lump? If that's it, I could put in a word with Mama for you, y'know. Pull out just Alec's soul and set it back into a nicely designed container — a remake-slash-reboot kinda deal."

"Much obliged. But I'll pass on that. Sure, there's plenty I can't do, but I've got no regrets about bein' born as myself."

"But I'm the one who's gotta help you walk, and that's a pain, y'know?"

"When ya put it that way I've got no comeback! —But there really is a conviction of my own in it."

Because he'd put his speech back to normal, Beard's statement carried weight. Overwhelmed into an involuntary "Whoa," Shaula got a taste of frustration.

To feel like this about a man with a bad leg — a man she'd beat one-sidedly in a brawl.

"Holy shit…! If I punched you I'd definitely win, but I've got this super, super strong feeling that punching you would mean I lose…!"

"To be able to grasp that — that's emotional growth, that is. —Hup."

Strangely, Beard had a way of gloating that made him hard to lay a hand on. With one breath, he pushed his back off the wall. Instantly his body nearly buckled at the knees, and Shaula's hand caught it with an "Whoa there!"

Held up by Shaula's reflexive hand and thus spared a fall, Beard laughed "Much obliged" with a face that showed no sign of humiliation.

"You'd have gone sprawling if I weren't here — so what's so funny?"

"Because the moment I got you to lend a hand, it went just the way I wanted… that'd be why."

"—"

"It's true, exactly as you say — I can't even walk a road properly without borrowing someone's hand. And still the reason I walk this road… this wasteland, is that I have a goal I can't reach any other way."

Borrowing the strength of Shaula's arm partway through, Beard pulled himself upright and planted his cane. At that sharp sound striking hard against the flagstones, Shaula felt the air go dry.

It was strange. That beings who might be stronger than her, like the redhead or Mama, could give off pressure that made the air crackle — that she could more or less understand.

But every so often, ones who couldn't fight at all, like Beard or the blond, could give off pressure that raised gooseflesh on even Shaula's skin, and that she couldn't explain.

And that connected, exactly as it was, to "his" strength as well. —To the reason for a strength that shook Shaula's soul, something beyond the mere weakness of being in love.

"I'm awful sorry to say it, Shaula, but I'm usin' you too. As ya can see, a man with a bad leg has to count on the strength of them around him, or his dream won't come true."

"…That's a shocking, next-level kept-man theory."

"Naturally, I'll do my best not to end up carried piggyback and cradled the whole way."

Smiling wryly, Beard let go of Shaula's hand and stood on his own two legs with his cane. Dissatisfied with both that smile and that attitude, Shaula sighed, judging him the sort who wouldn't listen no matter what she said.

He looked at a glance like someone you could hold a decent conversation with, but in the end Beard too was a companion of the redhead, the blond, and Mama. This wasn't a journey that the sort of person who couldn't state an opinion or speak of a dream could keep up with.

"Speaking of my dream — want to hear it?"

"Interest level zero."

"Then all the more reason to tell ya. After all, it's precisely the folks all over the world who've turned their backs whose cooperation I'll be needin' for my dream."

See — refuse, and he was still like this.

Deciding it wasn't worth silencing him by force, Shaula declared her position with nothing more than turning her back, and left the rest to whatever came out of Beard's mouth.

Following Shaula as she started walking, the sound of his cane keeping time, Beard drew a breath. "I want to gather up scattered people and build a single nation. One where blood and history aren't what do the talking — where a person's own qualities and abilities are what get evaluated, and they're given a place to shine. —A nation like that."

"…That's an idiotic dream."

"I'm told that often, and laughed at. I nearly laugh at myself, too — that a man who was nothing but a common recluse has come to speak of such a grand dream. But—"

Cutting his words there, Beard quickened the pace of his cane and drew level with Shaula. When she peeked sidelong at him, she could see his profile, set higher than the still-growing Shaula's own.

For an instant, it was a profile that made Shaula mistake him for carrying in his eyes a light the same color as "his" — and as that of every one of the people who traveled at "his" side.

"The accumulation of your days makes you who you are, and that becomes the real figure reflected in other people's eyes. —Any bluff, kept up long enough, becomes indistinguishable from the genuine article."

"Huuuh. And step one toward building this nation is a goofy accent?"

"In truth, when you're making an entirely new country from scratch, I think it'd be best to create a language unique to it — but in the world as it stands, preparing a tongue that can't communicate with other nations isn't realistic. So I figured a national accent or dialect, something along those lines, would be about right, and took the liberty of consulting your Master about it. —No, I mean, that's why I went an' consulted 'im."

"Your choice-and-selection of consultant was a total bull's-eye, but unless Alec the head of state can handle that accent properly first, the best you'll manage is a reputation as a cringey guy."

"When ya put it that way, I've got no comeback there either."

Perhaps he'd taken Shaula's advice on board at once, for Beard laid the accent on especially thick.

Whether the grand dream Beard was scheming toward came true or not, Shaula had little interest. Villages, towns, cities, nations — places where humans swarmed in droves — for all of them, the only thing that mattered was whether "he" was there. If "he" was, she'd treasure it with everything she had; if not, no touch.

That was how Shaula drew her line. However—

"Farsale's the same way, but you people and your disposition for gladly taking on huge things — that's rough. If I'm ever bored to death, I'll at least help carry the luggage."

In ordinary humans, Shaula had no interest whatsoever. Not a fragment, not a speck.

In Beard and the blond she didn't have much interest either. But "not much" was distinctly different from "none at all." So, deciding she'd say that much at least, Shaula spoke it to Beard's profile.

There hadn't been any great intent behind it. And yet, upon hearing it, Beard's eyes went round, and then at once he clapped a hand to his face and burst out laughing. "Well now, if that isn't a line to give a man confidence. …Shaula, I could go an' name you the very first citizen of the country I build, y'know."

"No thank you! My rear is firmly parked in the seat of Master's mistress!"

"Hold on, the mistress?! What in blazes!"

Beard's retort came loud and forceful at Shaula, who had crossed her arms into an X in front of her chest. She could tell the people around them turned to look in surprise — *what's that, what's that?*

At that resonance and the reaction around them, Shaula found herself impressed: I see, maybe Beard's accent really was a useful tool for making Beard's dream come true.

"Not bad, eh?"

At Beard's all-out smug face, which had seen right through her admiration, Shaula jabbed her finger into him once more.

3

"—I told you this one was closed and you were supposed to just let it slide."

So saying, Shaula fixed cold eyes on the back facing her.

They were inside the stone chamber that was supposed to be shut tight, tight shut. The one who had pried open the sealed door and set foot inside was now face-to-face with Shaula's "daily routine," carved across every inch of the chamber's walls. —Walls scored with tally marks, counting one by one each day Shaula had spent left behind in this tower.

—Packed dense, filling the entire room, those tally marks were proof of the time Shaula had piled up.

It wasn't as if she'd done it like some yandere or emotional wreck, aiming to make "him" keenly feel the weight of the days she'd spent once he eventually came back here.

The existence of those accumulated tally marks was the fruit of the time Shaula had spent thinking of "him" — which was to say, proof of love. So being likened to obsession was something she flatly refused.

That said, Shaula was perfectly aware this was the sort of thing that would make anyone who saw it recoil, so she'd sealed the door and hidden the room where no one would see. —And yet.

"I'm sure I told you. Don't go getting the wrong idea."

True enough, up to now Shaula had failed to carry herself like a Star Watcher and had done as the woman said. It was possible the accumulation of that attitude had led her to misjudge how much latitude she had.

But no matter what the other party might be thinking, one who treads on a lion's tail gets their throat torn open, whether or not there was ill intent.

Even if that one were a pregnant woman whom "he" had sternly ordered her to treat with care—

"You're gonna take the payback for doing what you shouldn't have done."

"—"

"—? You listening? Breaking my instructions and then ignoring me — are you trying to test the limit of how high my anger voltage can be cranked—"

*—or something?* — as she moved to demand an answer, Shaula strode briskly over, seized the shoulder that stayed stubbornly turned away and spun her around — and caught her breath.

Because she saw Leilani, staggering weakly, shedding great tears that spilled one after another.

Still meeting the breathless Shaula's eyes with a gaze blurred by tears, Leilani said, "My… apologies… Presumptuously, unbefittingly, outrageously, I felt in this unworthy body of mine one small part of the weight of the days you have spent, and was overcome."

"W-what are you crying for! I'm the one who wants to cry! You go into the room I told you not to enter, and then on top of that you react like that…!"

"—Shaula-sama, through all the pain until now, you have borne it so very well."

Leilani, who from the momentum of being turned around had half tumbled into Shaula's chest, said it while gazing steadily into her eyes.

With the height difference now reversed from the "Leilani" of before — peering down at the face of the "Leilani" she had once looked up at — Shaula let out a breath.

"…Is that… how it is."

What suddenly set Shaula's lips trembling was a feeble voice even she couldn't believe was her own.

A certain feeling that might have been hidden away inside her, one she hadn't been able to notice until Leilani said it to her just now. —Had she, left behind in the tower, been in pain?

"Amidst all that was painful, agonizing, sorrowful, and lamentable — how very hard it must have been."

Saying so, Leilani's hand reached toward Shaula's head. The sensation of Leilani's fingers combing through Shaula's hair, stroking the head bound up in its scorpion tail.

Since long ago, "he" and his companions had often patted Shaula's head like this. "Leilani" especially had had many such occasions, and the memories of those days came flooding back.

"Leilani" had taught Shaula things Shaula didn't know, and had been outrageously good at putting Shaula's unvoiced emotions into words. Was this part of that as well?

Just as "Leilani" said — had Shaula been wounded by as much as those tally marks?

"I…"

Had she felt the days of keeping her promise with "him" to be a burden?

"—I have, most improperly, broken my promise with you. And yet I now believe it was right that I did. The unprincipled, immoral deed of mine spared me from overlooking the great anguish you carry. …For it spared me from tasting the greatest of all regrets."

She listened to Leilani's voice shake as it said this, her hand still stroking Shaula's head.

Hearing "Leilani" say she had been spared from overlooking the worst possibility, Shaula thought vaguely that perhaps she really had been saved.

That perhaps Shaula truly had been standing in a dangerous place she herself couldn't perceive, and "Leilani," by the act of breaking the prohibition, had halted it at the very last moment.

"Thank you for having remained safe. And upon that, if an unworthy woman's ill-matched request may be permitted… please, do not carry it all alone."

Against Leilani's words, reaching out to draw close to her heart, Shaula could summon no will to resist. Because even though Shaula's raison d'être existed for "his" sake, so much of what composed Shaula had been taught to her by "Leilani," and "Leilani," and "Leilani."

—Shaula only went on accepting that palm upon her head, as though affirming it in silence.
