Arc 6 Chapter 25: "The One Who Waits on the Second Floor, Electra"

It's been a while! Here's the main story!

I've been writing this while trying to recall where things left off, so the pacing might be a little off — thanks for bearing with me.

I'll be picking things back up little by little from here on.

---

There was a woman. A single woman.

The woman was abused. By her family, by her clan, by the kin she should have loved, she was despised and shunned.
Before she was even born, expectations had been placed upon her. Her birth betrayed them.

What was left to her was discouragement and disappointment; what she was given was contempt and derision. Those were the woman's only possessions.

The clan held a cherished wish: proof of former glory and prosperity, the return of the god-called progenitor.
Every secret art and every atrocity, countless taboos committed — and their fruit was the woman.
But the woman who was born betrayed that cherished wish, and the clan, in despair and hopelessness, gave up on her.

"Live and die wherever you please. That is your sin to bear."

Abandoned by family and kin alike, she was cast out into the wilds as a mere infant.
Thrown into the wild with no means to live, there was no way a mere child should have survived.

And yet, though the woman had no means to live, she had the strength to live.

Ironically, that strength was born of the very taboos committed, the sins piled up, the negative banquet spawned by the clan's cherished wish.
Knowing no words, possessing no wisdom, following only instinct, the woman endured and survived in the wild.

She killed beasts, drank their lifeblood, crawled across the earth, lapped up muddy water.
Eventually time passed; the infant became a girl, the girl became a woman, and living in the wild grew difficult.

Rumors spread of a woman ravaging the mountains like a wild beast, and men sought her life; the woman was captured.
The captured woman was beautiful to behold, and the men did not take her life — instead, she came to be kept.
Obeying the men's every word, little by little the woman's awareness descended from the wilds into human settlements.

No longer needing to struggle merely to survive, exposed to the men's desires, her days became just that — days of merely existing.
Before she knew it, in their beds, she came to receive from the men things she had never known.

Words. Knowledge. A way of life. And feelings.

Before long her treatment changed; she was given things to adorn herself, and the woman was treated like a jewel.
In the midst of those days, the woman came to hold a strange sentiment toward the men.
That it resembled something like gratitude, or a sense of indebtedness — the woman of that time could not recognize.

Still holding that sentiment, the woman broke the necks of every single man in the dwelling where she lived.
Easily, in a single breath, without letting them suffer, in an instant, she laid every one of them to rest together.

Slipping out of the dwelling and heading into the wild, without a moment's hesitation, the woman made her way back to her homeland.
Toward the kin who lived there — the clan that shared her very blood, people whose bodies were far larger than her own — the woman felt a strange sentiment.
That it was hatred and a desire for revenge — the woman of that time did not realize.

Only, the wailing voices crying and begging forgiveness were rather delightful, and for the first time, the woman laughed from the heart.

"Perish here, and die a wretched death. — That is your sin to bear."

To attain that joy, the woman ground down her kin one after another — her blood relatives, the clan's cherished wish — with her own hands.
Carefully, meticulously, leaving not a single one, sparing none, taking her time, one by one, without fail.

At last, when every last one of her kin had died out, when everything had come to an end, the woman returned to the dwelling where she had once lived with the men.
She discarded the corpses of her kin, but buried every one of the men's bodies with care.
And so, alone now in that dwelling, the woman let out one long, long breath.

That this was peace, that this was rest — for the first time, the woman knew happiness.
No one would interfere anymore. With no need to think, no need to act, she fell into sleep.

—The 'Witch of Sloth' continues, endlessly, to indulge in an unending slumber, an insatiable peace.

—The staircase leading to the second floor, 'Electra,' boasted a majesty that could only be called a Grand Staircase.

Compared to the long, long spiral staircases they'd climbed from the sixth floor to the fifth, and then up to the fourth, this Grand Staircase was considerably wider, with each step considerably taller.
To begin with, an entire room had been rewritten wholesale into a stairwell — one could only imagine how overwhelming that made it.

A blank room that should have held nothing at all — and in that empty room, a Grand Staircase had appeared.
Standing before it with his arms crossed, Subaru let out a long sigh. And then—

"Feels like we've been stuck in place here for a real long time, somehow..."

"Huh? We just found the stairs — what's gotten into you all of a sudden?"

At Subaru's oddly heartfelt remark, Emilia, standing beside him, blinked in surprise, her eyes wide.
At her reaction, Subaru shook his head — "Nah" — and then turned to look behind him. There stood his companions, all of whom had discovered the Grand Staircase and shared in appreciating the tower designer's sheer nastiness.
At the sight of those unchanged, familiar faces, Subaru felt, for some reason, a strange sense of relief.

"—? Subaru, why are you kneading Betty's hand, I wonder?"

"Just wanted to feel like I'm really here... ahem, never mind that. Sorry."

Beatrice made a dubious face at having her joined hand kneaded, and Subaru gave her an ingratiating smile.
Then, turning back once more to face everyone in the room, he pointed at the Grand Staircase behind him.

"So, anyway, to get back on track... this Grand Staircase — it's not like it was here from the start and all of us just somehow overlooked it together, right?"

"It's hard to imagine we'd overlook a staircase this blatant. That said — if we're talking about this room specifically, I can't entirely rule out the possibility."

"Meaning?"

"She means that odd feeling we had while exploring. Yeah, I get that one myself."

In response to Subaru's question, it was the lady-and-knight pair of Anastasia and Julius who nodded to one another in agreement. Subaru tilted his head at their mutual understanding, but then Emilia raised her hand — "Ooh, I know" —

"It's about when you were asleep, Subaru — we'd all been gathering here on the fourth floor. Partly because Rem and Patrasche were resting in the green room, and partly because we were tackling the puzzle on the third floor too. So we were all walking around trying to decide which room to put our luggage in, but..."

"At the time, everyone avoided this room without really meaning to. It was unconscious, but now that I think back on it..."

"You mean, we might've been kept away by some kind of perception-blocking method?"

Beatrice picked up where Emilia's words left off, and Subaru voiced the conclusion. Emilia and Beatrice both nodded at the guess, and Anastasia and Julius likewise agreed.

A perception-block of the sort that everyone only notices with a "now that you mention it" after discovering the Grand Staircase — if that were the case, then perhaps this Grand Staircase had been here in this room from the very start. Some kind of Magic that kept the stairs from being seen, an unnoticeable camouflage?

"Well, otherwise, when the puzzle with the stone tablet on the third floor got solved, someone who happened to be in this room could've ended up crushed by the staircase appearing out of nowhere..."

"Subaru, it's not a stone tablet — it's a Monolith. Let's avoid confusion and keep our terminology consistent."

"Monolith, Monolith, Monolith! Happy now? Let's move on."

Subaru caught sight of Julius looking dissatisfied at the perfunctory response out of the corner of his eye, but ignored it, thoroughly fed up. He turned his gaze instead to the two who hadn't tried to join the conversation up to this point — Shaula, playing by the stairs, and Meili, clinging to her back.

"Hm? What is it, Master? Ha! Could it be, has my moment to be useful finally come?! For Master's sake, I'll do anything-ssu! I'll even co-sign your loans-ssu!"

"You definitely don't know what that even means! That's not something you should offer to anyone, not even family or friends. Just to check — do you know anything about these stairs..."

"After all my years of hardship living here — several hundred of them — this is the first time I've seen it-ssu!"

"Meili, go ahead and play. Pull on her scorpion tail for me."

"I was gonna play anyway even without you telling me, mister~."

"Ow ow ow-ssu!"

As ever, Shaula was of no use whatsoever in conquering the tower. Leaving her chastisement in Meili's hands, Subaru looked up once more at the Grand Staircase.

Unlike the stairs they'd climbed up to the fourth floor, this Grand Staircase wasn't spiral in structure. Like an ordinary staircase, it ran straight up to the floor above. And yet, given the tower's structure, a straight staircase running from here, skipping past the third floor to connect to the second, seemed like it could only end up jutting outside the tower itself—

"I guess some mysterious power's handling that somehow..."

"Or maybe it just looks like it connects to the second floor, but it actually leads somewhere else entirely... would that be too mean, though?"

"Being in this tower, I'm worried Emilia-tan's innocence is getting corrupted, and she's slowly turning into someone who thinks like a nasty person. I really want to clear this place soon."

"—?"

Emilia tilted her head, not quite following. But discovering this Grand Staircase, and even the hint that had been necessary to clear the third floor in the first place, both traced back to something Emilia had said.

This building — the Pleiades Watchtower, or the Great Library Pleiades — reflected its designer's mindset in the form of a number of thoroughly nasty gimmicks rigged throughout.

That Emilia kept unraveling them one after another was reassuring, but at the same time, as someone who wanted her to go on living a wholesome, healthy life, Subaru couldn't help worrying whether her personality might end up twisted.

"Still, our pace isn't bad. I mean, I cleared the third floor's 'Trial' on my very first try, so you could say we finished a third of the tower in three days."

"Considering there'd been no progress at all for over four hundred years, that's an outrageous pace, I suppose."

"Now that you put it that way, yeah, that is pretty nuts. ...Well, but I guess I'm the kind of guy who moves history that hasn't budged in centuries. 'The man who moves history' — man, that's a hell of a power phrase."

Looking back, there was his help in the subjugation of the 'White Whale,' the defeat of the Witch Cult's Sin Archbishop of 'Sloth,' and the subjugation of the 'Great Rabbit.' He'd also contributed in part to breaking through the 'Trials of the Tomb' at the Witch of Greed's mausoleum, defeated the Sin Archbishop of 'Greed,' and reached the Pleiades Watchtower that no human had ever set foot in before. And now, having cleared one of the 'Trials' that no one had ever attempted, he was about to face a second—

"Leaving out the process and just listing the achievements, I sound like an idiot..."

Given how many times he'd died along the way, he could hardly puff out his chest about it, but even so, he'd moved the history of this world far too much in just the past year.

He didn't mean to be doing it on purpose, but considering he'd still need to make the Sin Archbishops of 'Gluttony' and 'Lust' pay up eventually, he felt like telling the world: brace yourself, because history's about to get moved at least twice more.

"Wait, huh? What's up, Emilia-tan? Grabbing my hand all of a sudden."

"...No, it's nothing. I just think you should treat yourself a little more — a little more gently, Subaru."

"There's hardly anyone as ridiculously easy on himself as I am, and with you and Beako already being so kind to me, anything more would just be greedy."

Once Rem woke up, surely Rem would join in too. Strict, but kind.
And adding Petra, Patrasche, Garfiel, and Otto on top of that, there was no room left at all for Subaru to go easy on himself.

"—————"

At Subaru's response, Emilia's lips trembled as though she wanted to say something, but the words wouldn't come, and she was left simply gazing up at him.
The grip on his hand tightened just slightly, and Subaru tilted his head.
But—

"I think this thing with Natsuki-kun is a problem right down at the root. I don't think it's the kind of thing that'll just fix itself in a day or two. Though I guess it'd be a different story if Emilia-san used, well, everything she's got to teach him..."

Clapping her hands to cut into the conversation, Anastasia glanced at Emilia. Emilia wore a serious expression at that look, but it apparently wasn't the reaction Anastasia had been hoping for—

"—Guess that's a lost cause, then. Not exactly her strong suit."

"—? I don't really get it, but if you teach me how, I'll give it my best try, okay?"

"No way, absolutely not... I don't wanna get kicked by an Earth Dragon, thanks."

"Getting kicked by an Earth Dragon — is that like the saying about getting kicked by a horse...?"

Separate from the Garfiel Sayings, a new page had been added to the rare-but-recurring Book of Other-World Sayings.

In any case, there was plenty in Emilia and Anastasia's exchange that left even Subaru puzzled. As he and Emilia stood there companionably tilting their heads together, Anastasia sighed with a weary look, then pointed at the Grand Staircase.

"Come on now, quit stalling and let's go on up, shall we? Might need to rack our brains like on the third floor... or maybe Natsuki-kun here will solve it in no time flat."

"If it's as nasty as the third floor, I'm not so confident, honestly."

Frankly, the reason Subaru had managed to clear the 'Trial' on the third floor, 'Taygeta,' was simply because it happened to deal with stars and constellations, landing a critical hit on his own knowledge.

Constellations — and moreover, a puzzle requiring knowledge from the original world Subaru knew. From that fact, Subaru had made a guess: that Flugel, the designer of this tower, must have been a modern-day person summoned to another world, just like himself.

On that basis alone, there was a good chance the second floor's 'Trial' would likewise require modern knowledge, in which case Subaru might well prove useful — but—

"Unlike the third floor, it'd be a problem if it demanded knowledge I don't actually have... I mean, it's Flugel — if it's something like German history, I wouldn't have a clue."

Vague as that worry was, he couldn't bring himself to voice it, too conscious of the expectant looks around him. Subaru was weak to those looks from Emilia and Beatrice.
Whatever scraps of German knowledge he had, however lacking, he found himself wanting to dredge them up and somehow make it work.

"—Well, can't be helped. Literally, the staircase to the next step is right in front of us. If I don't go for it now, I'm no man. Let's dive in quick and crack this thing wide open."

"Unfortunately, everyone here besides you and me happens to be a woman."

"It's about the spirit of the thing, don't rain on my parade! Alright, let's go, Beako!"

"Nkyaa, I suppose!"

Just as he'd clenched his fist and declared his resolve, someone threw cold water on it. Sticking his tongue out in retort, Subaru scooped Beatrice up, and holding her light body in his arms, set foot on the staircase and began charging straight up it.

"Ah, Subaru, wait!"

Emilia hurried after Subaru as he bounded up at full speed. Shrugging at each other in exasperation, Julius and Anastasia followed as well.

"Isn't the naked lady coming~?"

"Naked, naked, you keep saying that, quit it, Little Squirt Number Two-ssu. Besides, I'm not naked-ssu. I'm properly dressed in a sexy-cute outfit-ssu. Don't go badmouthing Master's selection-ssu."

"It's not about badmouthing anything~, but you'll be in trouble if you get left behind, won't you~. Come on, hurry, hurry."

"Such a bossy little squirt, and all you're doing is riding on my back-ssu! I'm gonna have Master discipline you properly later-ssu! I want to be disciplined too-ssuu~!"

Swishing her scorpion tail side to side, cheeks reddening as she grumbled, Shaula set foot on the stairs. With a lightness that belied the girl riding on her back, she took the Grand Staircase five steps at a time, chasing after those who'd gone ahead.

"Ba-da-da-da-da-da-da-da-da—!"

Paying no mind to the exchange going on behind him, Subaru, carrying Beatrice, dashed up the stairs. Partway up, she deftly shifted her posture in his arms, and before he knew it she was clinging to him in a princess-carry position, her blue eyes narrowing.

"I really can't help but think space itself is warped here, I suppose. We've gone this far straight up, and there's no sign at all of bursting out of the tower."

"Maybe the tower's just shaped that way and you couldn't tell from the outside."

"So it juts out just for this staircase section? But then it'd look strange from the outside, and that would defeat the whole point of hiding the stairs in the first place, I suppose."

"Yeah, figured. I was just throwing it out there."

Agreeing with Beatrice's reasoning, Subaru looked up overhead, his breath coming a little quicker.
Strangely enough, for all the stairs they climbed, there was no sign of the upper floor drawing any closer. Despite how much running they'd done, there was a futile feeling to it, like running the wrong way up a descending escalator.

"Don't tell me the nasty trick Emilia mentioned is actually starting to feel real..."

The possibility that only the staircase had been prepared, with no actual connection above — Subaru shuddered at the chilling thought, and at almost the exact same moment, the space overhead abruptly opened up.

"Wh— whoa—!?"
"Hyah."

They'd meant to be dashing up an unlit, stone-built stairwell, but were suddenly greeted by a white light instead, and Subaru and Beatrice cried out together.
Stumbling forward and instinctively stopping in his tracks, Subaru found that the staircase had abruptly ended without his noticing, and he'd already set foot into a new floor.

There, waiting—

"A white room, again... or something."
"I suppose."

Coming to a stop, he slowly set Beatrice, whom he'd been carrying princess-style, down onto the floor.
The staircase had turned out to be a far longer journey than expected — long enough that even with his somewhat improved stamina, he couldn't stop himself from bracing his hands on his knees, breathing hard.
Stroking Subaru's back, Beatrice tapped her toes against the floor as she surveyed the room. Following her gaze, Subaru swallowed hard.

Having climbed the stairs to the top, the two of them had arrived at a white space that looked exactly like the setting where the 'Trial' on the third floor, 'Taygeta,' had taken place. Both floor and ceiling seemed to stretch on endlessly, an unfathomable structure that threw off one's sense of perspective at its very root.
The only thing that gave any real impression that this white space was fixed in place at all was, just as on the third floor, the gaping stairwell opening that connected down to the floor below.

"Whoa, this room again?"

One after another, from that stairwell, Emilia and the others emerged following Subaru's group. They were equally startled by the white scenery that greeted them, and all alike let their shoulders slump in dismay.
It felt as though they'd just been handed an unmistakable sign that another 'Trial' was about to begin.

"There's no chance we accidentally ended up back on the third floor, is there?"

"I don't think so. Going from the fourth floor up to the third was fifty-four steps, but here it was four hundred and forty-four. That's about ten times as many, isn't it?"

"Y-you counted them, Emilia-tan?"

"Fufu, actually, counting the steps of staircases has become a bit of a secret hobby of mine lately... wait, why's everyone patting my head?"

Having proudly confessed her secret hobby, Emilia had her head patted in turn by Subaru and the others. Even Julius and Meili, who didn't join in the patting, sent her subtly pitying looks.

"Somehow, this feels reeeally weird, though..."

"A-anyway, huge credit to Emilia-tan. Thanks to her, we now know we've climbed roughly ten times the third floor's staircase in one go. There's a quiet theory floating around that maybe we skipped the second floor entirely and ended up on the first, but for now, let's assume this is the second floor and think from there..."

"The 'Trial' should be about to start. That's probably the trigger for it, I suppose."

Setting Emilia's doubts aside for later, Subaru fixed his eyes on the direction Beatrice was pointing — the spot directly ahead after coming up the stairs, where an object asserted its presence.
On the third floor, 'Taygeta,' too, something like that had sat planted right in the center of the room. The moment you touched it, the 'Trial' began. If the same conditions applied here, then that was probably—

"It's not a Monolith. — It's a sword."

Narrowing his yellow eyes, gazing at 'it' standing planted in the center of the room, Julius said.
Just as he said, 'it,' present in the white space, was not the Monolith they'd seen on the third floor.

—'It' was a sword.

A bare blade, freed from its sheath, stood with its tip driven into the white floor.
Hilt pointed skyward, standing perfectly upright, the sword struck Subaru's eyes as terribly beautiful.

It wasn't ornately decorated. Subaru had no way of judging the quality of its materials.
But simply the way it existed — with no excess ornamentation, nothing but the bare minimum of steel — struck him as beautiful.

"Going by the atmosphere alone, I'd call it something like a 'Sword of Selection'..."

"—————"

"Yes, yes, well done holding back. Good boy, good boy."

Catching Subaru's muttered remark about the scene, Julius raised an eyebrow but somehow restrained himself. Leaving Anastasia to praise him for his forbearance, Subaru turned his eyes to Shaula for the moment. She wore a face brimming with confidence, arms crossed as if to show off her ample chest.
Her expression practically read, "I'm fully prepared to answer 'I dunno' no matter what you ask me-ssu!" — so Subaru finally stepped forward without asking her anything at all.

"Subaru."

"It'll be fine, I think. Going by the third floor as an example, it's probably not the kind of thing where an instant-death trap suddenly triggers."

Nodding to a worried-looking Emilia, Subaru slowly advanced toward the Sword of Selection. Emilia and Beatrice followed along as though it were only natural. Everyone else waited near the stairs, poised to react instantly if anything happened.

"Be careful when you get Anastasia out of here. The stairs are so long that if you accidentally shove her down, she'll roll all the way to her death."

"Duly noted. See that you don't neglect your own care for Lady Emilia and Lady Beatrice, in turn."

"It's alright. I'll properly protect Subaru myself."

"Yeah, I'll let myself be protected."

Giving her a thumbs-up in response, Julius sighed at the sight of Emilia's fully fired-up determination. Watching that play out, Subaru came to stand before the Sword of Selection.
It was within arm's reach. At this point, the sword clearly had a solid sense of reality to it, here in front of him. Unlike the Monolith, there was no impression of it being some inexplicable object.

"Still, that would make it a sword that's been standing here, stuck in place, since the tower was first built. The mere fact it hasn't degraded at all is plenty abnormal on its own."

Muttering that before the Sword of Selection, Subaru took a light breath — and then reached out for the hilt.
Gripping it firmly, feeling a faint resistance, he put his strength into pulling upward.
For a moment, the thought crossed his mind that if this really was a 'Sword of Selection' as its name suggested, there was of course the possibility he simply wouldn't be able to draw it — but that worry proved needless almost at once.

"—————"

The instant he applied even the slightest bit of force, the tip embedded in the floor slid free with surprising ease.
It came free with a fluid grace, as if drawn from a sheath, as though this were simply how it was meant to be.
And right after that, it came.

"—Fool who has reached the Heavenly Sword, obtain that one's forgiveness."

"—!"

A voice that seemed to bypass the eardrums entirely and resound directly in the brain announced the outline of the 'Trial.'
Since it was a development he'd more or less expected, he at least avoided the indignity of dropping the sword in his grip, but the same old inexplicable phenomenon still left him thoroughly uneasy.
As always, this voice resounding in his brain sounded a great deal like his own.

"Feels kind of like carsickness... was that just now audible to everyone else, too..."

Had it been heard not only by Subaru, who'd drawn the sword, but by everyone around him too? With the Monolith, the voice had been audible to more than just the person who touched it.
So this time too — meaning to ask as much, Subaru turned around, and noticed—

"—————"

—that everyone had gone stock-still, holding their breath, eyes fixed on a single point.

"—————"

Drawn along by their gazes, Subaru turned his own eyes that way. What they were looking at was directly ahead of the Sword of Selection Subaru had just drawn — a spot the same distance from the sword as the sword was from the stairs.
And there, a single silhouette had appeared.

"—Fool who has reached the Heavenly Sword, obtain that one's forgiveness."

The voice, though it should have been no more than a low murmur, sounded strangely loud to Subaru's ears.
It was the exact same phrase he'd heard the instant he drew the sword. But this time, it wasn't his own voice resounding directly in his brain — it was unmistakably another's voice, coming from the figure standing right in front of him.

"—Fool who has reached the Heavenly Sword, obtain that one's forgiveness."

The voice repeated the phrase over and over, like a broken record that knew nothing else.
Mechanically, if you wanted to call it that — automatically, in a sense — or perhaps instinctively.
He couldn't hide his shudder at the gap between the eerily repeated phrase and the tone of voice carrying it. The figure's voice was terribly direct, as if the tip of a blade were tickling straight at his heart.

"—————"

He couldn't tell whether the shudder he felt was fear, or emotion, or pleasure, or grief. Having his feelings toyed with this freely by another was proof of an absolute difference between them as living beings.
At this distance, by voice alone, his very life was being toyed with.

"—Fool who has reached the Heavenly Sword, obtain that one's forgiveness."

—It was a man, his long red hair falling carelessly down his back.
He was quite tall — a full head taller than Subaru, his body covered in sturdy muscle to match his height.
What he wore was no armor, but a crimson kimono, worn casually without a hakama, offering not the slightest protection — his right arm bare, slipped free of its sleeve, and even at a distance one could make out the white cloth wrapped around his midsection.
His hair, the color of burning flame, reached to the middle of his back, and his left eye was covered by a black eyepatch marked with a crude, ugly pattern. His uncovered right eye, meanwhile, was the color of a sky beyond reach.
Had he simply stood there in quiet, unhurried stillness, his features would surely have been striking enough to make anyone turn and look, beautiful enough to want captured in a painting — but a savage, cruel, and mad smile ruined all of it.
He was a ferocious beast wearing far too beautiful a form.
The most beautiful savage beast in the world — faced with such a being, Subaru forgot to breathe.

"Hi—"

Amid the illusion of time having stopped, the first thing to break that stillness was a groan.
There came a light thud, and right after it, a girl's yelp of "Eek!" In his frozen field of vision, he could just barely make out at the edge — a black-haired woman collapsing onto her backside. Shaula had crumpled to the floor, and beside her Meili's eyes were wide with alarm.

"Hih, hih..."

Shaula was so shaken she looked liable to lose control of herself at any moment.
Her already-large eyes were opened even wider, her face contorted into an expression a beautiful girl should never make, conveying both shock and the sheer scale of the panic raging inside her.
Had it been possible, she would surely have bolted straight out of the room, following her instincts.
It was only that her trembling legs wouldn't allow it.

—Shaula, who had one-sidedly dispatched even that Centaur, frozen in fear, unable to move?

"—Fool who has reached the Heavenly Sword, obtain that one's forgiveness."

Though Shaula's terror must surely have been within his sight, the voice went on repeating, unchanged.
The eeriness of the situation only deepened — but at the same time, there was something Subaru noticed.

For all the pressure the man was giving off, he hadn't moved a single step from where he stood. —No, the man wasn't giving off any pressure at all. He was simply standing there.
He was a being who, by his mere presence, radiated a sense of existence to rival even that of a 'Witch.'

"—!"

Holding his breath, Subaru forced shut his eyelids, which had forgotten even to blink, and for a moment steadied himself.
Then, still not tearing his eyes from the man, Subaru took one step backward. The Sword of Selection in his right hand, Beatrice's hand gripped in his left, he drew her — frozen stiff — back with him.

"E-Emilia..."

"I— I know..."

Unable to leave her behind, Subaru called out to Emilia, who was likewise frozen stiff. At his call, Emilia nodded in a trembling voice. Unable to stop her knees from shaking, she moved slowly, and Subaru retreated in step with her.

"—Fool who has reached the Heavenly Sword, obtain that one's forgiveness."

The distance widened. Still, the man didn't move. He merely kept repeating it.

"—Fool who has reached the Heavenly Sword, obtain that one's forgiveness."

Pouring every nerve into retreating, he made his way to the side of the still-collapsed Shaula. Shaula's face remained frozen in terror, and Meili, gripping her arm, couldn't move either.
Could he take them and retreat from this place? Both of Subaru's arms — the fingers gripping the sword and Beatrice's hand — were locked rigid, showing no sign of loosening. He couldn't picture himself grabbing Shaula and Meili's arms and hauling them to their feet. And to begin with, would that man simply let it happen? What was that man's purpose? Why had he come here — what was this 'Trial,' what was this sword for—

"—Fool who has reached the Heavenly Sword, obtain that one's forgiveness."

The repeated words, the phrase — it was the pronouncement of the 'Trial' on the second floor, 'Electra.'
What meaning could there be in the man repeating it? The content of the 'Trial' heard the instant the sword was drawn, the man repeating it, the fool, the forgiveness—

"—Fool who has reached the Heavenly Sword, that one's... forgi... ...ness..."

"—Ah?"

At the very moment Subaru's thoughts raced ahead and a terrifying conclusion began to take shape in his mind, the man's voice — which had been repeating the phrase without a single hitch — faltered for the first time, at the exact same instant.
Subaru let out an involuntary groan, and he felt Emilia and Beatrice tense at his carelessness, but that had no bearing whatsoever on this change.

—More than that, the change in the man was unmistakable.

"Heavenly Sword, the fool's... forgiveness... aah, aahoh, ohh-ohh, aah..."

"Wh— what? What is it, what, what's happening?"

"Ah, ah, aaaaaaaaah—!!"

"Piggyeek!"
"Wagh!?"

It was far beyond a mere falter.
The man, who had been standing stock-still, developed a strange hitch in his speech — and in the next instant, it exploded. Clapping his hands to his own head, he screamed, clawing violently at his hair.
At this sudden behavior, Shaula, finally unable to hold back any longer, let out a pig-like shriek and lunged at Subaru. Naturally, he couldn't support her. Wrapped tight in her arms, he was knocked flat onto the floor without even a chance to brace himself.

"—!"

Sparks flashed across his vision for an instant from the impact. Even so, the man's screams and Shaula's whimpering — clinging to Subaru's waist, shaking her head in refusal — kept pouring into his ears.

"Aaaaaaah—!!"
"Hyaaaah! Master, Master, Master, help meee! No-ssu! Help meee!"

"Y-you, what've you been going on about—"

"—Shut it, you!! It's ringing in my hungover head! Quit your hollering, you!"

"Ahfft..."

Before any calming words could reach her, Shaula's mind finally hit its limit.
Whatever all her thrashing had been about, Shaula's eyes rolled back with startling abruptness, and she went still, still clinging to Subaru's waist.
She'd lost her mental balance and fainted.

"You've gotta be kidding me..."

"Buburbuburble..."

Kindly and unmistakably conveying just how thoroughly she'd fainted, Shaula was completely out of the fight. Given her position as the floor's examiner, expectations that she'd be of use in conquering the tower had already been low — but honestly, a reaction of this magnitude was unexpected.
After all, her combat strength alone had always been a given — and for her, of all people, to be terrified to this degree—

"I would judge that you are no ordinary man."

"Aahn?"

A single sharp footstep rang out, and the man's displeased growl came from the very same cause.
It was the elegant knight stepping forward, the heel of his white shoe striking the floor — Julius, his cheek tense, the sword Subaru had let go of when he fell now in his hand.
At the sight of Julius, the man twisted the corner of his mouth in irritation.

"The hell're you, huh. And more to the point, where the hell is this. You messin' with me, you?"

"No, I am not toying with you in the least. We ourselves are at a loss as well. You appeared here quite suddenly. — I would ask you to understand that some wariness on our part is unavoidable."

"The hell're you, huh. Don't go talkin' all fancy and complicated, you. Don't go talkin' like one of my flunkies, you. You one of my flunkies, huh? Nah, you ain't. If you ain't, then don't go pullin' confusing crap like that, I'm sayin', you."

At Julius's manner of speaking — thoroughly courteous, yet plainly wary all the same — the man clicked his tongue, his displeasure only deepening further.
It was far more human than the earlier state of merely repeating the same phrase, but that hardly meant the situation could be called a properly functioning conversation, either.

"—Fine woman, fine woman, sexy woman, brat, brat, flunky, small fry."

"Unfortunately, I am not your flunky."

"Hah! The way you say that, it's even more like a flunky, don't go copyin' me."

At Julius's retort, the man laughed with genuine good humor for the first time.
Abnormally white teeth, an unnaturally well-formed beauty, and an alien madness all mixed together into a shark-like grin.

"—————"

At last, in that grin, one could just barely glimpse a trace of humanity in the man's figure. Or rather, one should say they'd finally confirmed him to be an intelligent life form capable of holding a conversation.

"Oi, you. Explain yourself. What is this place. What're you doin' to me, huh, you. Don't go givin' me none of that fancy talk, you. Spit it out quick, you."

"You show up out of nowhere... and you're acting awfully high and mighty, you."

"Aahn?"

The man shoved a hand into his bared chest, scratching carelessly at himself. Toward that man, Subaru — having finally gotten himself upright — flung out the words as though wringing them from himself.
At that, the man's single right eye widened, and he glared down at Subaru, still sprawled on the floor—

"The hell're you, huh. Why the hell're you lyin' down, you. Livin' the good life, huh, you. Got yourself a sexy woman for a meat blanket, that it, you. Trade places with me, you."

"Unfortunately, out of respect for the lady's own wishes, that request is denied..."

Straining his trembling knees, Subaru somehow forced himself to his feet. In the process, he forcibly pried loose Shaula, who'd been clinging to him. He didn't manage it with much care, and she ended up dropping head-first onto the floor, but he had no room to spare for her.
Still—

"Ah, ah, aah? What, you. That it, huh, you. You messin' with me, you?"

"...The moment you look at someone's face, you're rude — is that it, you?"

"Hah!"

As though grinding his back teeth, the man let out a hard, ringing sound as he laughed ferociously.
Then, ignoring a bewildered Subaru, the man swept his single eye around the white room. Nodding as if in understanding — "Oh, oh, yeah, yeah" — he said:

"Got it. — Well then, shall we get started."

"Started, you say— wait! You've been moving this conversation along all on your own this whole time!?"

"Shut up, you. I already explained it plenty, talkin' in my sleep just now, didn't I. Listen when people talk, you."

"'Just now,' he says..."

"—Fool who has reached the Heavenly Sword, obtain that one's forgiveness."

Subaru, unable to keep up with the situation, blinked in confusion. In his place, it was Emilia who murmured the oft-repeated phrase, word for word without a single mistake.
Their frozen stillness, too, finally began to thaw at last. Following Julius and Subaru, Emilia and Beatrice, and Anastasia and Meili as well, managed to free themselves from the stopped time.
Only Shaula remained completely unconscious—

"Hah! That fine woman over there ain't no small fry. If she had a body of flesh, she'd be tonight's company, you. ...Lookin' closer, damn, you. The hell's with that face, way too gorgeous, you."

"Gorgeous...?"

"So you're the one! The examiner for this floor! That's how it is, right!?"

The man, his eyes carrying something faintly lecherous as he looked at Emilia. Cutting in between the man and Emilia, Subaru jabbed a finger at him and said as much.
At that confirmation, the man deepened his shark-like grin—

"—Dunno. Ain't got no interest in titles some other guy slapped on me. I'm me. You're you. What more d'you need than that. If you wanna have a proper talk with me, then try movin' me from this spot, even one step, you."

"—————"

The declaration of a man standing utterly defenseless, doing nothing but standing there, unhurried.
That no one could laugh it off was because the condition the man had set was accepted, without resistance, as difficult enough to genuinely deserve the name 'Trial.'

—Fool who has reached the Heavenly Sword, obtain that one's forgiveness.

If the man standing before them was the 'Fool who has reached the Heavenly Sword,' then they had already been told the method for obtaining his forgiveness.
All that remained, purely and simply, was whether or not it was actually possible.

"Of the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica, a member of the Royal Guard. Julius Juukulius."

Before the battle — no, the 'Trial' — began, Julius, in keeping with proper etiquette, gave his own name.
The bare minimum of respect owed to an opponent one was about to face on equal footing to settle matters.
At that, the man narrowed his single blue eye in amusement, an abnormal killing intent leaking freely from him—

"Ain't got a name worth givin'. — I'm just a 'stick-swinger.'"

The Trial of the Great Library Pleiades, second floor, 'Electra.'
Time limit: 'Conditionally unlimited.' Number of attempts: 'Conditionally unlimited.' Challengers: 'Conditionally unlimited.'

—Trial, begin.
