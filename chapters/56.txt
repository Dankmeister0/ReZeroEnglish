Arc 2 Chapter 32: "Because I Cried, Cried My Eyes Out, and Finally Stopped Crying"

"Falling into a peaceful sleep on a girl's lap while she strokes your hair... taken by itself, sure, that's a top-tier event. But still..."

Scratching his head, Subaru muttered that with an awkward look on his face. Eyes downcast, biting back sigh after sigh, his profile red to the ears, the speed of his hand clawing at his own scalp only increasing,

"Bawling my eyes out in front of the girl I'm into, whining and moping, face an absolute mess of tears and snot, and then just passing out cold. And on top of that, hogging her lap for hours in that state... what kind of humiliation play even is that anymore?"

What came back to him was the feel of Emilia's lap, and the wreckage that his own hours of deep, unconscious sleep had left of it. Tears, drool, snot, and worse had left it in an awful state — unforgivable both from a hygiene standpoint and, purely as a matter of Subaru's own boy-index, on principle.

And yet, of her own accord, she hadn't tried to shake him awake even once, and despite having her clothes ruined like that, she hadn't blamed Subaru when he woke up and started apologizing profusely.

"As long as you got even a little rest, that's all that matters. Besides, Subaru, you don't get it."

"Huh?"

"When someone says sorry over and over, it doesn't satisfy the other person nearly as much as one 'thank you' would, at a time like this. I didn't do it because I wanted an apology. I did it because I wanted to."

With a finger pressed to the lips forming his apology, and one eye closed as she said that — was there a man alive who wouldn't fall for it, just like that? No. There wasn't.

And sure enough, Subaru fell, easily, helplessly. He'd already been head over heels for her to begin with, so that was just the final push, doubling down on an already lost cause.

Parting ways with Emilia, who'd said she was going to change clothes, he'd wandered the mansion in a dazed, dreamlike state for a while, and upon coming back to himself, found himself here. Looking back on his own conduct that day, the sheer wretchedness of it made him want to cry — though after crying that much already, he doubted he had any tears left to shed.

"No good, I'm no good at all. What was all that about not wanting to show Emilia-tan my weak side? I just showed her the single most shameful part of me there is. There's no way I can ever show my face to you again, seriously."

"...You barge into someone's room late at night, and this is what comes out of your mouth? I thought a day without seeing you meant peace, but apparently that was wishful thinking."

Seated on the middle rung of a stepladder, Beatrice cursed under her breath, eyeing Subaru sidelong as he writhed near the entrance.

Having wandered the mansion in a state of mind too raw to face anyone sober, Subaru had ended up here — this forbidden little room that no one else's hands could touch. Though from the perspective of the girl who ruled it, his arrival was nothing but an imposition.

That said, that wasn't the only reason he'd come here.

"Don't say that, Bea-ko. We go way back, you and me."

"What relationship could there possibly be between Betty and you— wait. Before that. What did you just call Betty?"

Raising one eyebrow, cheek twitching, Beatrice posed the question. Subaru clapped his hands at her reaction.

"Ah, Bea-ko, right. I figure once you feel a certain level of closeness with someone, you need a nickname for them. Up till now, out of everyone in this mansion, you're the only one that feeling never once occurred to me for, but..."

What came to mind was the time before — that stretch he'd spent utterly steeped in despair. Smooth-talking, silver-tongued as she could be, and even if from her side it hadn't felt like being talked into anything, there had been a Contract genuinely exchanged between the two of them. Beatrice had kept to it faithfully, and had even deliberately kept the terms of the Contract vague in order to help Subaru. Even if she herself had forgotten it, there was no way he could forget how saved he'd felt in that moment. And so —

"Whatever you think of me, I'm going to call you Bea-ko. That's the fullest measure of affection I'm capable of showing you — !"

"That makes Betty exactly zero percent happy, I suppose! What is that, some kind of forced-on-me goodwill!? It's not just off-putting, it's downright nauseating, I suppose!"

"What, what's with that tone! I'm being dead serious trying to express my heartfelt gratitude here, this isn't the moment to be making fun of me!"

"If you can look back at what you just said and honestly claim you're not joking, then what's going on between you and Betty right now isn't a conversation anymore, I suppose — it's something else entirely that merely resembles one!"

Having tried to volley a conversational back-and-forth, only to be told he'd apparently been playing an entirely different sport altogether — kabaddi, maybe — Subaru fell into a stunned silence. It had been his own way of expressing affection, but unfortunately, it seemed not to have struck much of a chord in her heart. Genuinely disappointed by that, he found himself drooping despondently.

"Well, be that as it may, I'm still gonna keep calling you Bea-ko."

"An unwanted persistence, I suppose. Then Betty, for her part, will absolutely refuse to respond to being called by that name. On that much, at least, our opinions align, I suppose."

"Don't be so cold, Bea-ko."

"............"

At Subaru's call, Beatrice, however, said nothing, eyes remaining fixed on her book. It seemed she intended to put her own just-stated policy into practice. Approaching the stubborn girl, Subaru began circling round and round the stepladder, chanting,

"Bea-ko Bea-ko Bea-ko Bea-ko Bea-ko Bea-ko Bea-ko Bea-ko Bea-ko..."

"—You are an unrivaled specimen of sheer irritatingness, I suppose! What is wrong with you!!"

Repeating her name over and over like he was playing a game of kabaddi himself — with it whispered ceaselessly right by her ear, Beatrice's emotions finally reached their boiling point, and Subaru pumped his fist, having achieved his objective.

Turning back to face her properly then, mouth twisting at the corner as he regarded the girl whose shoulders were still squared with anger,

"I'm backed into a corner, no way out in any direction. Frankly, I want to borrow your help."

—Having wailed pathetically, having lost his composure in the most unseemly way imaginable, and having arrived, at the end of all that, at this one conclusion — that was what he told the ringlet-haired girl.

Having poured out everything weighing on his heart in Emilia's lap, having let flow every last drop of the ugly feelings he'd bottled up and the tears he could no longer hold back — what remained inside Subaru, once all of that had been washed away, was his own purely, unadulteratedly greedy self.

He loved Emilia. He loved this life at the mansion he got to spend with her. He loved this environment where he could learn all sorts of things. He loved Ram, brusque as she was, always fussing over him in her own way. He loved Emilia so much. He loved Rem, who guided him while subtly berating him in polite language all the while. Emilia-tan was seriously a goddess. He owed Roswaal a debt of gratitude for providing this paradise-like situation. He'd received from Beatrice, too, a debt he could no longer ever fully repay. He held an affection he couldn't bring himself to cast away for the people living in this mansion. He couldn't bring himself to give up the happiness of being here, among them.

A flood of feeling, so overwhelming it choked his chest. Memories of happiness too vivid to wipe away — the very wellspring of the vitality that drove Subaru forward.

But even as he felt all that — 

He loved Emilia. He hated how inadequate he was, how lacking in the strength to protect her. Life at the mansion allowed for no letting his guard down. He never knew when he might be written off. Ram, who had severed his leg with a blade of wind and bored a hole through his skull, terrified him. Rem, who tortured him again and again — even healing his wounds just to keep going — and crushed his skull with an iron ball, horrified him. And the fathomless madness of Roswaal, who commanded those two and, on top of that, could presumably give the order to have Subaru exterminated without a shred of mercy — that turned his stomach. The time he had to spend, every time he woke, checking whether he was even still alive, wondering whether despair was creeping up on him again — that was agony, sorrow he could barely stand.

That, too, was an unerasable truth living inside Subaru. These two irreconcilable feelings could not permit each other's existence, and Subaru had spent his days struggling to live on while carrying both of them, on the surface and deep within, all at once. Like walking barefoot across thin ice, forced to keep smiling without ever letting on that the skin on the soles of his feet was peeling away.

That anguish of Subaru's, that wretched torment as he fought against despair — Emilia hadn't overlooked it. She had scooped it up.

Maybe it was Puck who'd noticed first. A Spirit who could read minds would have seen through Subaru's clumsy mask easily enough. But even so, it was Emilia who had comforted Subaru's heart — a heart that had been on the verge of fraying to nothing.

Even if this was dependency, he didn't care. That the driving force behind Subaru's heart right now was his feelings for her — of that, there wasn't a shred of doubt. Right. Which is to say—

"Emilia-tan is seriously an archangel."

"Didn't you just spout something incredibly stupid?"

"No way. I was just reaffirming the single most important thing in my life."

He could answer that with full confidence, chest puffed out. And with his statement thus wholly affirmed, Beatrice, apparently too weary even to bother looking exasperated, let out a sigh instead.

"Let's get back to the matter at hand, I suppose. ...The part where you said you wanted to borrow Betty's help."

"Yeah, I mean it in the sense of, like, praying to a god as a last resort. I can't think of anyone else to cling to."

At present, the one person in this mansion Subaru could place his full trust in was, of course, Emilia — but her existence was, at the very same time, the single most important factor in his calculations. In short, she was the one person Subaru absolutely did not want to see put in danger. To Subaru, who prioritized his own life above all else, hers was the one thing that, without exaggeration, weighed evenly against his own life on the scale. And so, in this loop with its eye toward the coming confrontation with the Curse Arts User, borrowing her help was an option he wanted to avoid until the very last possible moment. And that, in turn, made it naturally difficult to borrow the power of Puck, her attendant Spirit as well. Which left, as the remaining option—

"Which, all things considered, actually makes you a pretty soft touch underneath it all — a real pushover heroine, Bea-ko."

"Even without understanding the content, I can sense the general vibe of an insult, I suppose?"

"No such intention whatsoever. ...Truth is, right now, in this whole mansion, you're the only one I can rely on."

Ram and Rem, obviously, were out of the question, and he couldn't confide in Roswaal either. If there was anyone in this mansion Subaru could turn to besides Emilia right now, it could be no one but her.

"Please. —Like this. I'm begging you."

And so Subaru knelt on the floor before her, bowed his head, and pleaded. For her cooperation. For her to become the flame lighting the path Subaru needed to walk, to bring an end to this repeating chain of despair.

"I need your strength. All of it, everything put together — I want to protect the place where I get to be happy."

"————"

Head bowed and unmoving, a silence fell between the two of them. As he waited for her reply, Subaru's heart pounded anxiously against his chest. But as the silence dragged on, that pounding gradually receded, and, growing uneasy, Subaru glanced up. And then —

"...Beatrice?"

Without thinking, forgetting the exchange from moments before, he called her by name. That was how much emotion, steeped in sorrow, was wavering in the expression with which Beatrice was gazing at him.

Brow faintly furrowed, lip bitten, Beatrice was glaring at Subaru. Glaring — and yet, to Subaru, it looked like a face on the verge of tears.

Holding his breath, he waited silently for her next reaction. Beatrice let the moment sit, as if steadying some wave of emotion rising within her, and then, eyes still closed, said quietly, weakly,

"...There's no reason for Betty to answer your request, I suppose."

That alone, faintly, was all she gave back.

"Bea-ko... I—"

"Silence, human. Betty isn't going to keep being kind to you forever, I suppose. If you're getting carried away with yourself, I won't forgive it, I suppose."

Cutting him off, Beatrice rattled that out in a rush. Stunned by the sharpness of her reaction, Subaru was baffled — what in what he'd just said could possibly have struck such a nerve? That must have been exactly what had hardened her already uncooperative stance further, inviting this worsening of the situation.

He'd meant to convey sincerity, but it had backfired on him. A result he couldn't help but regret endlessly — but Subaru's mental makeup wasn't delicate enough to keep dwelling on it now.

Something backfiring on him — at this point, under these circumstances, that was practically old hat. Had the dice he'd thrown ever once come up good, across these five repetitions of the world, often enough to even remember? Once he thought of it that way, he could just chew right through any smoldering weakness of heart. Besides, that weak self who might've stopped in his tracks over something like this — he'd already turned all of that into snot and drool and washed it clean away, right there in Emilia's lap.

He decided he hadn't cried. Crying in front of — no, in the lap of — the girl he liked, of all places, would make him the absolute worst kind of man there was. A man had his pride, after all. He was a boy, wasn't he.

"Listen to me, Beatrice. I get it, you wanting to push me away like that. From where you're standing, I'm nothing but some rude bastard who got carried in here barely a day ago, who you don't even understand."

"If you understand that much, you should understand Betty's answer too, I suppose."

"You're the one who closed up the wound in my stomach — I'm grateful for that. You probably don't even realize it, but there's a mountain of stuff I owe you a ridiculous amount of gratitude for, and on top of that, I still can't even hold my head up without relying on you more. It's pathetic. It's hopelessly humiliating. But there's no one else. Just you."

Pressing forward relentlessly, he poured out every word rising in his heart. He was aware it was a self-serving, forceful, pushy argument that gave no thought at all to her feelings — and even so, Subaru kept his head bowed.

Looking down at Subaru's incoherent words and his lowered head, Beatrice gave a small, characteristic huff through her nose, and said,

"Lamenting your own inadequacy, groveling on the ground like this in the most unseemly way, begging and clinging. Do you have no pride whatsoever?"

"I've seen clearly what matters to me. If smashing my head into the ground ten times, a hundred times, would be enough, I'd do it as many times as it takes."

He had no petty pride left to spare on clinging to his own insignificance.

Head still bowed, Subaru held his posture of pure supplication without wavering. He knew it was an underhanded tactic. Across these five repeated worlds, Subaru had crossed paths with Beatrice again and again, and each time, they'd gone on bickering just like this. It was precisely because of that kind of relationship they'd built up that he knew it — that the girl named Beatrice, however standoffish she might act on the surface—

"Raise your face, I suppose."

Some voice, tinged with a note of resignation, gentle, reached his ears, and Subaru thought his lowly plea had finally gotten through. Reaffirming his own smallness all over again, he felt something like self-disgust at this behavior of his — sincerity in form, without sincerity in substance, put on for her sake. And yet, it had also genuinely been a necessary step. He had to make his peace with that. Above all, he had to resolve to use every means available to him in order to obtain the future he wanted—

"Bea—"

"Eat this, I suppose."

"Gyabh—"

But into that face, freshly set with such grim resolve, something hard came flying without warning, slamming into him with full force. Still in his kneeling bow, only his neck snapped backward, and the resulting sound — indescribable — echoed through the forbidden library, a wordless scream reverberating in its wake.

Subaru writhed, arched backward like a shrimp. Unable to process the damage to his cervical spine, he thrashed, his gaze wandering, until it locked with Beatrice's — arms crossed, one leg still raised. Understanding dawned. The girl had just driven a merciless yakuza kick straight into the face of Subaru mid-dogeza.

"You— this— guh!"

"I can't fathom the shallow reasoning that a hundred heads' worth of some worthless human like you would even come close to matching Betty's effort, I suppose. However many copper coins you pile up, they'll never add up to a single gold sovereign. Did you not know that, I suppose?"

"No, even copper coins, if you pile up a few thousand of them, they'll add up to a gold sovereign eventually. If we're comparing in terms of value, that's exactly how it works, you know? You doing okay on basic math?"

"Stop looking at me like I'm some pitiable child, I suppose! That is not the look a man who was, until a moment ago, trying to beg for Betty's help should be giving!"

And so the shrill, bickering war of words between Beatrice and Subaru began. Across these many repeated worlds, this back-and-forth had never once started over the same reason twice. As the familiar, well-worn argument with her wore on, the grim resolve that had briefly filled Subaru's heart came, before he knew it, to seem like something almost laughably silly.

And after all that exchange, the conclusion of their war of words ultimately came down to—

"Fine, I'll play my trump card. If you agree to help me, I'll let you drain my mana a few times and put up with it."

"Your mana isn't worth as much as you seem to think, I suppose? Sure, compared to ordinary mana... well, I suppose the compatibility was decent, and it tasted better than most."

"My mana's got a proven track record — Puck's sucked on it too. In other words, that makes it indirect mana drain. ...You get what that means, right?"

Beatrice's expression changed color, and, biting her lip in frustration while Subaru basked in the smug satisfaction of having gained the upper hand in negotiations, she nodded — and that was how it was settled.

Looking back on it, Subaru thought, that had been a fairly anticlimactic way for things to end. Of course, it had been by steering things down that path that he'd managed to grudgingly secure Beatrice's cooperation.

—Though he understood full well that it was the little mage who had arranged things to end up that way, all along.
