Arc 8 Chapter 43: "Each Their Own True Desire"

—Aldebaran knows what the Stargazers are.

Once, the one who told Aldebaran of it was a being who knew nearly everything there was to know in this world, and yet still hungered greedily for the unknown.

A complicated relationship, that one.
Not someone who could be described in words like "like" or "dislike."
If asked whether he was grateful or not, he supposed he was grateful. But he also harbored a resentment, in equal measure to that gratitude, over things that could never be reconciled. That was the kind of relationship it was.

Either way, regardless of who had taught him, he knew the circumstances surrounding the Stargazers.
And that knowledge contributed nothing to whether Aldebaran's long-cherished wish would succeed or fail. More important than any interest in the Stargazers, or whether they were involved at all, was that he'd managed to drag *that* into this.

At first, when Priscilla announced she was heading to Vollachia, he'd tried everything he could think of to stop her, but once she made up her mind about something, nothing on earth would change it.
So instead he'd settled for accompanying her, doing his best to serve as whatever insurance he could — but running into *that* here in the Empire was, for Aldebaran, nothing short of a twist of fate.

Fate always intervened in Aldebaran's life in the most unpleasant ways imaginable.
So he held not the slightest goodwill toward fate — but this once, he was grateful.

If *that* was here, everything changed. If he could drag *that* into this, the situation would shift dramatically.
If this fell within the scope of things *that* could not bear to abandon, and if that scope kept growing larger and larger, too vast to hold in one pair of hands — then Aldebaran's long-cherished wish would be fulfilled.

Once, Aldebaran had thrown everything away.
He'd kept walking through the darkness, relying on nothing but the faint light of stars, until he gave up, deciding it was all pointless.
That was precisely why the sun had been so blinding. As if there were no darkness at all, his resignation had been burned away.

To protect that dazzling sun, he'd lick fate's boots if it came to that. He wouldn't hesitate to endure agony that tore at his very body, or to look *that* straight in the eye.

Witch, Stargazer, Great Disaster — it made no difference what stood in his way.

—Just, please, don't get in my way.

—Twenty-two times, all told.
That was the number of attempts it took Al to grasp what was happening to him. A whiteout so sudden he hadn't even registered his own body evaporating in an instant—

"—Nah, hold on, the cause isn't cloud or snow, it's fire, so that'd make it a redout. Or I could call it a 'crimson-out' instead — sounds cooler."

He let out a deep breath and muttered the useless nonsense.
He couldn't say for certain whether his mind was sound, but at least he'd managed to talk himself into the flimsy conclusion that maybe it wasn't.
For now, that would do. If there was a problem, it was—

"At this level of fight, there's no room left for me to get involved!"

As Al roared that out, right before his eyes, one of the roles that had to be fulfilled in the capital was completed — the conquest of one of the five points of the star-shaped ramparts.
After all, the formidable ramparts that had boasted such overwhelming defensive strength had been blown away without a trace — if that wasn't accomplishing the objective, what was?
That said, at the second vertex — where the towering walls and surrounding buildings had been swept clean away — an obstacle greater even than the vanished ramparts now stood as the next barrier.

—A girl who dyed the overcast sky red, suspended in the air by whatever law of nature allowed it.

With short silver hair, red eyes, and a great deal of tan skin exposed, the girl was lovely enough to catch the eye — and yet her appearance stirred an instinctive sense of danger in anyone who looked at her, powerful enough to override that beauty.

Leaving the Lord of Thorns to Groovy, and making use of a Werewolf pelt, Al and Cecilus had reached the second vertex without incident — no, they had been *trying* to reach it.
Strictly speaking, the second vertex had been obliterated, and Al — caught up in that obliteration — had suffered damage beyond his own comprehension, over and over, before finally grasping the method and circumstances needed to move forward.
That said, even if told to do the exact same thing again, he didn't even want to think about how many extra rounds it would take to reach this same result.
So—

"—Restarting the thought experiment. Redefining the domain."

Updating the matrix in increments of ten-odd seconds, or maybe just a few, Al watched with all his focus for any opening to flee.
Just as he'd shouted a moment ago, there was no room for Al to intervene. And yet, if the world simply reset to a fixed point every time Al took fatal damage, nothing would ever progress.
If Cecilus would just scoop him up like he had with that sniper, and get them both out of the kill zone together, it'd be a different story — but—

"Sorry, Al-san. But my instincts are telling me — this is my moment to shine, and fussing over you would spoil the whole thing."

With that, Cecilus wasted no time abandoning any pretense of protecting Al, and challenged the girl in the sky — Arakiya.
This was how many times now that Al had laid eyes on her? Looking back, he'd fought alongside her back when she was still small, during his days as a Sword Slave on the Sword-Slave Isle; then they'd been enemies at the walled city of Guaral; and now, in the capital, she'd made him evaporate — their relationship deteriorating right on schedule.
But aside from the perspective of a middle-aged guy who'd crossed paths with her more than once, Cecilus was right — anyone could see at a glance that Arakiya was in no ordinary state.

"——"

Arakiya writhing in midair — her state was clearly far from normal.
He'd heard a little from Priscilla about the nature of a "Spirit Eater." He'd even seen with his own eyes how she could transform into fire or water — but this clearly contradicted all of that precedent.
Unlike when she'd merged her flesh with water, or flown as a fragment of flame, Arakiya now looked as though she were on the verge of being devoured from within by an enormous white light.

From within her slender, tan-skinned body, thin, faintly yellow-tinged transparent crystals jutted out one after another, or seemed to be growing directly from her flesh.
Highly pure magic stones are called magicrystals, and these now enveloped Arakiya's entire body.
If she'd looked composed through all this, he might have taken it as just another facet of her power as a Spirit Eater — but—

"—!"

Arakiya's red eyes, unable to properly focus on anything in the world, let tears spill from her clouded left eye, while her lips let out ragged, pained gasps begging for help.
No one could possibly call this normal, or believe Arakiya herself wanted any of this.

It was the same as when a child opens its mouth wide to wail, streaming tears while striking out at you again and again — even if you shut your eyes, ears, or sense of touch, you'd still know it was crying.
What Arakiya was doing right now was exactly that.

"Don't tell me — you're not about to say you can't just leave a crying kid alone!?"

Clutching his head, desperately fleeing from destruction, Al shouted half in despair at the back of Cecilus, who was instead charging straight toward it.
Hearing that, Cecilus didn't even turn around, only shook his shoulders just enough to show he was laughing, and said,

"The tears of a child or a woman can be the spark that sets a story in motion. So it'd be perfectly natural for me not to be able to overlook them — but that's not it this time."

"Then—!"

"But — I have business with those tears."

The instant he finished speaking, using a chunk of molten rubble as a foothold, Cecilus's body leapt at lightning speed.
All around, the barricades built to defend the second vertex had melted, transforming the area into a hell where magma seemed to have been poured in. Carelessly step a foot into that magma, and it wouldn't just be chip damage — the part that went in would carbonize instantly, leaving a wound too gruesome to look at twice. Source: himself.

But Cecilus dove straight into that district-turned-garden-of-magma, making full use of his limited footholds to close in on Arakiya floating in the sky.
His speed and daring defied description — no, the truly indescribable feat came right after, accomplished while he hummed a little tune.

"You gotta be kidding me!?"

In midair, Arakiya's whole body flared white, and in an instant, a flash of light scorched the spot where Cecilus ran.
The spear of light she loosed plunged into the magma, and a beat later, the space within several meters around it was compressed into a sphere before instantly bursting apart. The imploded magma and the destructive force behind it spread outward, scattering across an area nearly ten times the size of the compressed space.

Al was struck speechless, and even knowing the blast was too far off to reach him, he instinctively shielded his head with his arms — a single shot alone carried terrifying power, and yet it kept firing without pause.
One shot, two, three, four in rapid succession, and each time, the very face of the capital was remade.

Streets stopped being streets; the earth stopped being earth.
The blasts chased after Cecilus as he ran along the ground, fired one after another, and even Cecilus—

"Ta-ta-ta-ta-ta-ta-ta-ta-ta-ta-ta-ta-ta-ta——!!"

Dodging gouts of magma like scalding spittle and the blinking gaze of ruin that was each flash of light, Cecilus tore through the white dust, sprinting furiously through a space where death rained down.
Through a space where there was no longer even solid footing, where magma had replaced the earth beneath his feet, Cecilus went tearing along in sandals that surely had no fire resistance whatsoever.

Watching that sight, what flashed through Al's mind was that ridiculous water-walking trick of the shinobi — stepping out with the left foot before the right one sank, and the right before the left sank.
And that was exactly what Cecilus was doing now, except on magma instead of water.

"That's not possible!?"

"For those who think that way, it never will be!"

Answering Al's scream with breezy cheer, embodying an outrage that scoffed at the very laws of physics, Cecilus barreled straight into a house that had somehow escaped the destruction — and the next instant, the house collapsed, and a pillar kicked out from within the crumbling building shot into the air like an arrow.

An arrow far too thick for its proportions closed on Arakiya at a speed that looked like it could skewer the torso of some giant beast. But before it could strike home, it caught fire and burned away completely in midair.
Around the glowing Arakiya, however much heat was being generated, even the sky itself looked warped, let alone her form — no half-hearted attack could even get close.
Cecilus must have understood that too. And yet—

"Hup! Hup-hup! Hup-hup-hup!!"

Amid a thunderous roar, Cecilus smashed through building after building, kicking pillars, roofing, and furniture from the crumbling structures at a speed too fast to follow, hurling them all at the airborne Arakiya.
Of course, none of it was ever going to reach her — every last piece vanished in midair before it could connect.
And what's more, the reply to those attacks that never landed was an arrow of light that would deal a certain fatal wound with even a graze.

"You idiot, stop it, don't you get it!? If you keep drawing her attention like that for no reason—"

"What are you saying, Al-san! Backwards, backwards, backwards, all of it backwards! If anything, I have to draw her whole heart and soul over here, onto me!"

"This attention-hog— ...no, wait."

Cecilus, contributing to the destruction of the capital's scenery — buildings on his own initiative, the surrounding area as a side effect. Al tried to dismiss his usual leading-man posturing with a hollow laugh, and then he realized something.
Cecilus's positioning always kept Arakiya toward the interior of the capital while he circled around to the outside, drawing her attacks toward himself — in other words, a fight designed so that none of her attacks would be aimed further into the city.
Only once he understood that did Al finally grasp what Cecilus meant.

"That woman has neither consciousness nor reason right now. All she has is instinctive self-defense, to keep from bursting apart and from being killed. Left alone, she'd probably drift toward the middle of the city. And what do you think happens if she goes on a rampage right in the middle of it?"

"...A crater opens up that nobody could live in for the next hundred years."

"And a great many people would die. If they were enemies, that'd be one thing, but having a great many people who aren't die isn't very desirable. It makes the world a lonelier place."

Saying so in a quiet tone, Cecilus dodged a beam of deadly light grazing past him and gave himself over once more to lightning speed, in service of the very words he'd just spoken.
Unable to hide his surprise at Cecilus's unexpectedly sincere intent, Al hooked a finger under his heat-warmed iron helmet, adjusted its angle, and held his ground.

—Just as Cecilus said, in her current state, Arakiya had no room to spare for turning her awareness outward.
Having taken in something beyond even Al's imagination, Arakiya was desperately holding back its overflow, and simply reacting on reflex against any threat that might obstruct that effort.
And Cecilus, believing this was necessary to keep it from destroying the capital, deliberately kept himself within a lethal margin of distance, needling her again and again, holding Arakiya in place right here.

"——"

Cecilus's aim, and the strange situation Arakiya found herself in.
The option of turning his back and running, telling himself there was nothing he could do, kept flickering at the edge of his mind — but even Cecilus could lose a leg in this.
If Al's presence could undo that even once, then there was value in his being here.

"—Guess I'll do it, then."

Shaking his head slowly, Al let out a long breath.
Having already drawn the joker twenty-two times right out of the gate, there was no telling how many more times he'd be forced to draw it from here on — or what would become of his own sanity as a result.
But sane or not, there was no mistaking it — the sun was blinding.

"Well then, that's fine by me."

Flicking the metal fitting of his helmet with a finger to make it click, Al stepped forward.
And then—

"——"

The white light loosed at Cecilus detonated a dozen-odd meters ahead, and a bolt of magma flung out by the aftershock caught Al, unable to dodge it head-on—

"—Next."

Within the redefined domain, he steeled himself to step onto the grand stage that was, for an ordinary man, his own.

—Once, Cecilus Segmunt had told Arakiya something.

A killing match between the "First" and the "Second" — the kind of thing regarded as an everyday occurrence in the capital.
On a scorched wasteland of a battlefield, while Arakiya — the loser — and Cecilus — the victor — chatted amiably, Cecilus had cut a cloud with his sword and called the technique nothing more than a street performance.
In truth, Cecilus himself thought the technique had no use beyond startling people, and Arakiya, having witnessed it firsthand, judged it likewise to be a sword technique of no practical value.

It was a divine, void-cleaving feat that Rowan Segmunt had spent his entire life devising — and yet, to monsters who had transcended the human, it amounted to nothing more than a flashy-looking trick.
In other words—


—A long leg cut through the air, and the belated gale that followed blew the dust away.
Ducking to instantly dodge the powerful kick, he drew his blade toward the opponent's slender waist, left wide open by the big swing, meaning to cut her in two — when an impact slammed into his chest.
The air was wrung out of his lungs, and as his eyes went wide, what had wrenched at his sternum, down below, was a bundle of tails growing from the rear of the woman who'd thrown the kick.

"—!"

A fox's tail, soft with beast fur, struck with unbelievable force, sending the body that took the blow flying backward, bouncing off the ground.
He watched the sky and earth flip over once, twice, and just as he was bidding farewell to the sky for a third time, he drove his sword into the ground to brake his momentum. Crushing the earth beneath his heel, gritting his molars, he sheathed the blade at once and moved into a draw-strike stance—

"Surely you understand by now?"

"—!?"

A single flash of the blade — the opening motion of the technique he'd meant to unleash the instant it left its sheath — was stopped by the woman's hand, laid upon the hilt of the sword still in its scabbard. In the instant he was struck speechless, right in front of him, the woman lowered the corners of her narrow eyes as if in pity, and said,

"You, sir, are not fit to be my opponent."

"Ohhhh—!!"

As if to cleave that pity in two, rather than yanking free the sword she held pinned, he instead kept the hilt fixed in place and drew the scabbard itself, spinning halfway around to slam the Witchbeast-bone sheath into the side of her face.
The momentum, the angle, the feel of a clean follow-through — all of it was more than enough to shatter a human skull.
And yet—

"——"

It was the scabbard that shattered into pieces from the impact, and the woman — the Fox Human who called herself Iris — showed not the faintest sign of pain on her face.
She only trembled her lips, still touched with pity, and lifted the hand that had been holding down the hilt.

"I won't let you die, but this will hurt like death."

A palm strike drove into his forehead, and his body went flying backward, flipping end over end.
This time, any posture of breaking his fall or bracing himself was shattered along with his rattled brain, and he went bouncing straight down the long, long avenue before the Crystal Palace, tens of meters, without catching on anything at all.
He bounced, and bounced, and bounced, then rolled, and rolled and rolled, and finally sprawled out flat on his back, arms and legs splayed.
And then—

"—Ah."

Beaten half to death in the span of the briefest fight, Rowan Segmunt stared wide-eyed.

Too strong. Far, far too strong.
An opponent strong almost beyond belief. Of course, he'd been well aware she was strong. And yet, one way or another, he'd been certain that in the end, he would be the one to win.
Just as it always had before — surely it would be the same this time too. That's what he'd thought—


—Here, let us speak of one particular misfortune belonging to the man named Rowan Segmunt.

Heinkel Astrea, who had traveled alongside him, was an unfortunate man who had never once been chosen for the things that could only be obtained through being chosen.
Rowan Segmunt, on the other hand, was a man who had been chosen, again and again, for the things that could only be obtained through being chosen — and had become unfortunate precisely as a result.

Rowan had a long-cherished wish. There was something he had never stopped seeking. A prayer he had never stopped craving.
To reach the Heavenly Sword, he had endured every hardship, done everything and anything that needed doing, and carried a hunger to accomplish it no matter what demon or monster he was called.

There was not a shred of falsehood or deceit in that wish. It knew nothing of compromise or resignation.
Not once had he been dishonest with the sword and techniques he sought to master, nor had he ever once neglected his training.

But Rowan Segmunt had simply never encountered them.

He had never met a worthy rival who could raise him up as they raised each other; never met a formidable enemy who could spur him on with the need to surpass them; never met a beloved person who could push him up to a plane he could never reach alone.

He cut down everyone he met, one after another, and by whatever twist of fate, only ever crossed blades with those his own swordsmanship could overcome. He kept missing every chance to clash with the many transcendent beings the world held, and at last, in despair at never reaching his goal and on the verge of wishing for death, he was granted a heavenly mandate and became a Stargazer.

Given the opportunity, Rowan Segmunt might have made his swordsmanship resound across the world.

But Rowan had no worthy rival, no formidable enemy, no beloved person — he remained alone, always.
It wasn't that he'd resolved himself to the idea that feeling had no place on the path of the sword, nor that he'd suffered some bitter betrayal at the hands of someone he'd bonded with.
It was simply that Rowan had never been able to meet anyone who could tell him where he currently stood, nor anyone who could push him up and beyond it.

He had been able to dodge Iris's opening attack when she dropped straight down from the Crystal Palace only because she — loathing the death of others — had never intended to actually land a hit on him.
Balleroy Temeglyph, turned Undead, had fled from the Cloud Cutting because he feared an unexpected counterattack from Cecilus Segmunt and chose to avoid pressing the pursuit.
He had come through unscathed so far amid the Great Disaster wrought by the army of the Undead only because no one but opponents his own swordsmanship could handle had ever appeared before him.
When he had once incited an assassination attempt on the Emperor, and Cecilus Segmunt — who'd refused to take part — had spared Rowan's life instead of taking it, it was only because Cecilus had thought, on a passing whim, "It's probably impossible, but wouldn't it be exciting if Father really got stronger and came back to cross swords with me someday!"

The reason Rowan Segmunt had gone on living, right up until today, was nothing other than this: in every situation where the scales between fortune and misfortune might tip, they had always, without fail, tipped toward fortune.
And now, the opponent Rowan had encountered in the capital turned Undead City was, once again, the one and only enemy with no intention of taking his life.

—The world would not grant Rowan's wish, but it never once stopped lighting the path by which he would survive.

"——"

"—Will you keep going?"

Iris frowned at Rowan as he slowly pulled his sprawled body upright.
Though tens of meters now separated them, Rowan could hear Iris's words perfectly clearly — perhaps because of a presence so overwhelming there was no way to look away from it.

—No, or perhaps this was a change brought on by encountering, for the first time, an enemy of such overwhelming might.
Maybe the reason was a sensation like some unbreakable shell that had always surrounded him having finally cracked open. Because of it, he understood, with total clarity.
Straight ahead, the powerful mana coursing through Iris's entire body as she stared him down — its sheer, overwhelming volume — had become unmistakably clear in the single clash they'd just exchanged.

In Lupugana, turned into a city of the dead, Iris had stood in his way as the guardian of the Crystal Palace, where the mastermind behind the enemy presumably resided — this woman was, without question, the strongest being in the entire Vollachia Empire.
A final existence made for this calamity, one that even Cecilus, celebrated as the "First" of the Nine Divine Generals, could not match—

"Ka-ha-ha, what fortune, what fortune — what a fine day this is...!"

Feeling that truth in his very skin, Rowan bared his teeth in a grin, a ringing in his skull like his son singing at the top of his lungs.
To reach the Heavenly Sword, he had thought there was no way but to someday cut down the son who had reached it himself. But now, having encountered a being that surpassed even Cecilus, matters were much simpler.

Not someday — now.
In this very moment, Rowan Segmunt would reach the summit of the sword and claim the seat of the Heavenly Sword.
To that end—

"—Swordsman, Rowan Segmunt."

Once more, he sheathed the sword he'd managed not to let go of, spread his feet, and settled into a low, coiled stance.
Iris stood far off in the distance — the gap between them the end of his luck — no, the reward of all his days spent bleeding sweat and blood in reach of the summit.
All that remained was to collect a fitting price for it, from Iris's slender throat.

"—Cloud Cutting."

A flash of the blade loosed the instant it left its sheath, streaking straight toward Iris where she stood.
Cutting through the very air along its path, cutting down a leaf that dared interpose itself, leaving behind even sound and wind — this single stroke was the single most finely honed blow of Rowan's entire life.
It would take the head clean off Iris, the woman in the beautiful dress—

"—That's as far as it goes."

Tilting her head, and with that alone, dodging the finest stroke of Rowan's life, Iris murmured the words like an exhaled breath, stamped hard against the ground, and closed the distance.

The final being of the Great Disaster gave Rowan no time to loose a second stroke.

Struck by an impact slammed straight down from above, Rowan was driven helplessly deep into the street below.

"——"

Looking down at the swordsman half-buried face-first in the ground, Iris swept the hem of the dress that had done it and turned her back on the man she'd faced.
He was, by now, surely an opponent with no strength left to fight — no threat, even with her back turned.
Sadly enough, even if he'd had strength left and tried a sneak attack, it wouldn't have reached her anyway.

"While you still have your life, leave the capital."

Words she would speak to the weak with a single flick of a finger, and to the strong only after a fight to the death.
As someone to say this to, Rowan was an extremely troublesome case. He was not weak. But neither was he strong. If she had to put a name to it — he was the summit of ordinary men.
And in the capital as it stood now, that reality alone was enough that simply being present might be called a sin.

"A sin, is it. And just what right do I have to say such a thing..."

Gently pressing a hand to her own chest, Iris murmured it as though cursing herself.
And yet, she had decided. She had gone and decided. And she had carried it out in a form that allowed for no excuses. No matter how many people came at her, so long as she could push every last one of them back.
If she did that, then the story of Iris and Eugard would—

"—Why are you standing?"

Halting her steps, Iris questioned the presence behind her without turning around.
It was the sense of the swordsman Rowan — the one she'd just knocked senseless with that last blow — rising to his feet. She'd thought she'd stripped away his consciousness, and now regretted that she'd gone too easy. And yet, any more force than that might well have cracked his skull. She had no intention of taking his life, after all.
Had her belief that she'd made the difference in power abundantly clear been too naive? There were times a person couldn't back down even facing such a gap in strength — was this one of those times too?

"There are times a person fights with their back to the water, certain the other side has no intention of letting them go. But I—"

"...That's just it. That you don't intend to. That's the problem, you see."

"——"

The faint, rasping voice that answered her was beyond Iris's understanding.
If this was a warrior's pride, or a man's stubborn dignity, it was something Iris couldn't grasp.
Iris couldn't help but think of the thing she herself wanted to protect, more than any of that.
And so—

"If you still can't bring yourself to give up, then—"

—Then I'll keep going until that heart breaks, even if I have to listen to the sound of my own heart cracking.

Just as Iris resolved to face Rowan's resolve head-on — that was when it happened.

"...Eh?"

Turning around, Iris stared in stunned disbelief.
It wasn't some anomaly like Rowan having closed the gap in strength between them in the blink of an eye, nor had anyone else arrived to level a weapon at Iris in Rowan's stead.
Only Rowan was there — and yet it was unmistakably Rowan who had shocked her.

—With the sword in his own hand, he had slashed his own throat, fatally.

"Wha—"

A beat, and the next instant, blood erupted with tremendous force from the neck whose vessels had been severed.
Before her eyes, the street was dyed with the spurting blood, the very wellspring of life draining out of Rowan Segmunt's body, soaked up by the ground.

"Why...?"

At a sight that leapt clean past understanding, a breath escaped Iris's lips like a gasp.
At the sight of Iris like that, Rowan — blood still spraying from his throat — opened eyes lit with a mad gleam, and laughed, blood backing up and spilling from the corner of his mouth.
He laughed, and spoke.

"Even in death, I—"

Midway through those words, Rowan's blue eyes rolled back, showing white, and he collapsed.
Sensing that this was not a loss of consciousness but a loss of life, Iris nonetheless rushed to his side on instinct, reaching out toward the life of the man whose life alone she had tried to save.
But her hand never reached the man's corpse.
Because—

"—I shall reach the Heavenly Sword," the voices said, together —

for descending upon Iris, who had broken into a run, came the Undead of Rowan Segmunt, freshly dead, multiple bodies attacking all at once from every direction.

—When the Dragon's breath was loosed and came bearing down, piercing through the cityscape of the capital, there was almost nothing Heinkel could do.

He'd only swung his sword as his survival instinct demanded, digging a hole in the ground that offered little more than false comfort, and just barely made it in time to slide his body into it.
Though that false comfort was just that — false — since the Dragon's breath gouged deep even into the ground along its line of fire as it came on, so it might not have even qualified as comfort at all.

And so, the life of Heinkel Astrea — current head of the Sword Saint's line, which had long protected the Dragonfriend Kingdom — was, ironically, on the verge of being wiped away without a trace by the breath of a Dragon—

"—Oi, oi, you don't get to die that easy, old man."

Grabbed by the scruff of his neck, Heinkel was hauled bodily out of the hole he'd forced open. His body was yanked up with tremendous force, and right after, he caught the scorched smell of the Dragon's breath burning the world.

"Whoa, whoa, whoaaaa!?"

His vision spun round and round, blood streaming from a split forehead, stomach acid spilling from the corner of his mouth — scattering the contents of his body without the slightest reserve — as the sensation of floating gave way to his body crashing down onto the ground.
Thrown down without even managing a proper fall, he propped himself up on the ground with his arms and looked around.

"—Ugh."

A groan slipped out unbidden as he laid eyes on the devastation where he himself had been standing only moments before.
The spot had been grazed by the Dragon's breath and had vanished completely, still hissing white steam. Had his escape come even a moment later, he'd have become part of that steam himself.

"My job was supposed to be raising hell down south and drawing attention so the boss and the others could get in easier... Hah! Couldn't believe my eyes."

"Ahh...?"

"I figured for sure you'd run off, but turns out you've got some guts after all, old man."

A voice, rough-edged but carrying unmistakable praise, came from beside him. When Heinkel turned toward it, standing there was the silhouette of the one who'd pulled him free.
Backlit just so, the face wasn't clearly visible. With his ears still ringing from the shock of the Dragon's tail strike moments before, he couldn't even tell for sure whether it was a voice he recognized.
But the figure, as if none of that mattered, clacked its fangs together and stepped forward.

With Heinkel kneeling on the ground behind him, the figure faced down the massive Cloud Dragon glaring down at them.
And then, striking both fists together powerfully before his own chest, he said—

"—I'll lend you a hand, old man! Like they say, 'Kuwein's Stone can't be lifted alone'!!"
