Arc 5 Chapter 10: "The Way Back Through the City of Water"

The negotiations between Emilia and Kiritaka Muse proceeded with surprising calm.

Kiritaka, being a merchant of some standing, had no wish to leave a bad impression on the figurehead of so great a power as a Royal Selection candidate. That Emilia herself had come in person carried great weight, for a merchant like Kiritaka, on its own.

Of course, Emilia — placidly sipping the tea she'd been served — had no such calculation in mind. Neither did Garfiel beside her, who was still deciding whether to help himself to the sweets.

In the reception room of the Muse Company, it could be said that only Otto, seated across from him, truly grasped what was going on in Kiritaka's mind.

"Thank you for troubling yourself to come all this way. I know it's improper of me — I'm a busy man myself, and I understand I shouldn't be so restless about running around — but I've made a habit of touring the sites in person."

"I think that's an admirable way to think. And it's we who should apologize for taking up your time when you're so busy. I hadn't expected to be received so soon after arriving."

"In all things, haste serves best. Especially in negotiations."

Trading pleasantries dressed up on the surface, Otto studied the man that was Kiritaka.

The merchant seated across from him — Kiritaka Muse — carried himself with the dignity and bearing one would expect of a leading merchant even in a city like Priestella. Small wonder.

He was still young — probably somewhere in his mid-to-late twenties. His slim, tall frame was wrapped in well-tailored clothes, his pale blond hair carefully combed back. Ornaments, tasteful enough not to seem gaudy, lent him an air of poise and refinement, and both his words and his gestures betrayed the polish of a fine education.

As the Emilia Camp's steward, having accompanied Roswaal and others, Otto had — not entirely by design — accumulated some experience negotiating with men of influence and power. He judged that Kiritaka's manner matched that standard without strain.

Frankly, he was privately relieved he'd come along. If Emilia had faced this man alone, she would surely have been talked into something or other and come home having bought an overpriced vase. Over this past year, Otto had come to rate Emilia's negotiating instincts at about that level.

"Then may I ask your business? I understand from Lady Anastasia's messenger that there was something among our company's goods that you desired."

"Then this will go quickly. Actually..."

As Kiritaka moved to broach the subject, Otto paused a moment and glanced at Emilia.

As a rule, Emilia left negotiations to Otto. Garfiel, for his part, was busy nibbling a sweet while sharply glaring at a corner of the room — where a man dressed all in white, head to toe, stood at his ease. Too self-assertive a presence for a mere employee, the man was, by Kiritaka's account, a bodyguard; these days, he'd said, things were dangerous enough to warrant asking for his presence.

Otto had a notion who the white-clad man was. Likely a member of the mercenary company "White Dragon's Scales," said to be favored by the Muse Company here in Priestella — a band with some renown even in Lugunica, and a long history behind it. He'd heard they were something close to a jack-of-all-trades outfit, and it was well known that they had been on good terms with the Muse Company for several years now. Seeing them here like this, it was probably Kiritaka's own decision to bring them in.

"Our request is a single one. Among the magic stones your company deals in, there is a colorless one of exceptional purity. We would like you to part with it."

Otto — inwardly labeling the man someone not to be underestimated — cut straight to the point. There was no sense in a roundabout request. The moment this meeting had been arranged, the reason for their visit was already an open secret to Kiritaka. And Kiritaka, for his part, knew perfectly well he couldn't refuse a request from Emilia.

"I see. We do indeed pride ourselves on the magic stones we carry — of a quality quite superior even compared to our competitors. As for the colorless stone Lady Emilia desires, we could prepare any number for you—"

"Kiritaka-san. Let's set aside the roundabout talk. Our request, as I've told you, is for a single colorless magic stone of the highest purity. That's all."

"...Forgive my rudeness."

It wasn't quite stubbornness. Simply a verbal dance. Kiritaka, feigning obtuseness though he understood perfectly, kept probing to gauge how deep their pockets ran. As far as he was concerned, the handover of the goods was already decided — the only question left was what price tag to attach.

"For our part, since you're asking something unreasonable of us, we're prepared to bend the market rate somewhat in your favor. That said — Margrave Mathers, Lady Emilia's patron, holds the mining rights to the magic stones of the Elior Forest, does he not—"

"A swindle to dodge the market rate wouldn't be worth the trouble in a single-item deal like this. Our business runs on trust, top to bottom. Surely you understand that, Otto-dono."

So he even knows my background, Otto thought inwardly with a sigh. The Suwen family, as a house of commerce, fell far short of the Muse family — but it wasn't so small that its background couldn't be traced. In the camp of a Royal Selection candidate deciding the fate of the nation, of course anyone well-informed would dig into the backgrounds of its key figures. Though — the truly frightening thing about the Emilia Camp was how much even that couldn't trace.

Garfiel, who had shut himself away in the Sanctuary for so long. Emilia, who had spent over a hundred years in the Elior Forest. And then there was the "Girl-Charmer" — both the charmer and the charmed, of unknown origin. Compared to them, Otto's own background, so easily uncovered, was fair game for anyone.

"Otto-dono? You don't look well."

"Ah, no — please don't mind me. My thoughts just wandered somewhere unreasonable for a moment, and it put me in a strange mood."

Shaking his head at Kiritaka's words, Otto set aside his idle thoughts for the time being and prompted Kiritaka once more for his answer to the main matter. Kiritaka made a show of deliberation, then said,

"For our part, it would be strange indeed to refuse to hand over goods we have on hand when they're requested. Naturally, we intend to comply with Lady Emilia's request."

"Then—"

"However, the magic stone you desire is a special one. You see, when I was entrusted with running the Priestella branch of the Muse Company, the head of our company — my father — passed it on to me himself. It's a piece I value more as a keepsake than as merchandise."

"—"

Whether that was truth or embellishment, it was a clever opening, Otto thought, biting his lip. As Kiritaka himself had said, rare as the stone might be, this was still a single-item transaction. Measured against a deal of the company's scale, it wasn't one that could be expected to yield much profit. So where would the added value beyond the price tag come from? Not from the value of the goods, but from the human touch. Since he was parting with something precious to himself, he was pressing them to recognize that as added value.

"Oh... something that precious."

Indeed, Kiritaka's explanation was already shaking Emilia quite badly. Seeing the guilt written plainly across her face, Otto thought — she really can't play a poker face. I need to stay sharp, he thought, clearing his throat.

"We understand your feelings well. But we must ask you to bend on this, all the same."

"I understand. I too am a merchant who deals in magic stones. It stands to reason that things like this shine brighter in the hands of someone who needs them than sitting on a shelf. I will part with it. — Only, there are a few conditions."

"—Conditions. Let's hear them."

Having sufficiently prepared to drive up the price, Kiritaka pressed into the real negotiation. Otto braced to meet it, and Kiritaka held up three fingers. What manner of outrageous demand would come flying out? Otto's stomach churned in anticipation.

"First. Please understand that the Muse Company was not maliciously concealing the colorless magic stone Lady Emilia has been searching for all this time out of any ill will."

"...Of course. Now that you've explained the circumstances, there's no reason for us to suspect otherwise."

First, Kiritaka moved to dispel any lingering suspicion the Emilia Camp might harbor toward them. For the sake of a smooth conclusion, there was nothing to do but nod along.

"And second. We'll exchange the formal papers later, but we'd like the Hoshin Company brought in as a witness, so that a record remains showing this was a legitimate transaction."

"We'll accept that as well. ...No problem there."

It seemed the Muse Company did business with the Hoshin Company as well. Kiritaka intended to make it known, both within the industry and without, that Emilia now owed Anastasia a debt. It stirred some unease, but being a legitimate enough request, it couldn't be refused either.

But everything up to this point could still be undone. It was the third demand — saved for last — that would reveal Kiritaka's true position.

Before Otto, who held his breath, Kiritaka gave his third finger a light shake.

"Third. — I ask that you not make contact with the 'Songstress' Liliana, who is currently staying in this city."

"—What?"

At the sudden, seemingly unrelated name, Otto was dumbfounded. Naturally, so were Emilia and Garfiel, seated with him — no, in fact the two of them hadn't changed their attitude in the slightest this whole time. Emilia sipped her tea; Garfiel glared at the man in white. Though the negotiating had been left to Otto, their unwavering composure was almost admirable.

"I'm sorry. Unless I've misheard, it sounded like you asked us not to make contact with the 'Songstress'..."

"Yes, that's correct. That concludes our request. If there's a problem with it, we may need to reconsider this deal..."

"It's no trouble at all, but might I ask why? So far it seems the 'Songstress' has nothing to do with our transaction."

"...I don't believe I need to explain that. The question is simply whether you'll agree or not."

Lowering his voice, Kiritaka let slip an emotional side he hadn't shown until now. Faced with this baffling demand, Otto hesitated a moment over how to respond. Frankly, the third demand had been unexpected, and even accepting it posed no apparent hindrance to Emilia's future. Emilia might be disappointed not to meet the "Songstress" who had come up in conversation with Anastasia, but that was something she could simply chalk up to achieving today's goal.

Thinking it through that way, the answer came surprisingly easily. If it meant sealing the deal, there was nothing more to ask for. Better to settle this before Subaru, left behind somewhere along the way, caught up and complicated things.

"Lady Emilia, is this all right with you?"

"Mm. It's a little disappointing, but it can't be helped."

At the confirming question, Emilia dipped her chin, and Otto moved to give his answer of consent. Kiritaka accepted it with satisfaction, and with that, the preliminary skirmish over the magic stone concluded. All that remained was to settle on a price and take delivery of the goods.

"The item itself is here. We've also prepared a few other high-quality pieces... would you like to see them?"

Kiritaka rose and brought out a wooden box from a shelf at the back. Set upon the table, it burst into a jewel-box gleam the moment it was opened, making Emilia and Otto blink at the sight. Magic stones of every color lay arranged with care upon a cushion. Among them, one stood out with a singular brilliance — a transparent magic stone untinted by any elemental color.

That, surely, was none other than the colorless magic stone Emilia sought.

"Would you like to hold it and confirm it for yourself?"

At Kiritaka's words, Emilia raised her face. When Kiritaka nodded, Emilia let out a small breath and gently reached her fingers toward the stone. But then —

"Lady Emilia."

"Young master."

Both of the room's combat-capable figures called out to their respective charges at the same moment. Exchanging a glance with each other, they turned toward their masters, who now wore puzzled expressions.

"Somethin' noisy's comin', dammit."

"Someone's coming up in a hurry from below. I'll confirm."

The man in white moved soundlessly toward the door, while Garfiel rose halfway from his seat. A quiet tension gripped the reception room. And the commotion they'd noticed seemed to have reached right up to the door itself, when—

"You're all so noisy, chirping away! Knock it off already! Emilia-tan and the others are negotiating in there right now—"

Just as the familiar voice registered, the man in white opened the reception room door. Beyond it stood a man with a blank look on his face — recognizable from any angle, with a small girl trailing on either side of him — Natsuki Subaru, living up to the name "Girl-Charmer."

"—Subaru?"

Called by Emilia, Subaru finally noticed the assembly before him and went pale. Otto, inwardly, wanted with all his heart to let out a sigh, but for the moment held his tongue, mindful of the reactions around him. Subaru then raised a hand weakly, with the look of a man resigned to his fate.

"H-hey there, Emilia-tan. Fancy meeting you here."

"Fancy meeting you — what's with all that noise... oh, Kiritaka-san?"

At Emilia's puzzled voice, Otto too was slow to react. Looking over, he saw Kiritaka had risen with a hand thrust into the wooden box, his eyes now alight with a manic gleam, glaring at Subaru. In his hand was clenched a blue magic stone of high purity —

"D-d-d-don't you touch my Liliana!!"

A cracked shout, and the hurl of a magic stone. No one needed to stop it — the throw flew straight and true, sinking into Subaru and, with a burst of blue light, sending his body flying.

"Sorry about that. Our young master's usually sharp as a tack, but when it comes to little Liliana, he gets even sharper — in a bad way. I'll try to talk him down by tomorrow, so for now, why don't you head on home?"

After the commotion, once both parties had somehow been calmed, the middle-aged man in white bowed his head apologetically as he mediated. Behind him, in the closed room, Kiritaka's shrill voice could still be heard even now. Apparently it wasn't a sight fit for outsiders, so the room was now completely off-limits.

"Honestly, Kiritaka-san is such a handful. Trying to steal away my chance to talk with Emilia-sama and Natsuki Subaru-sama. I'm miffed, positively miffed!"

Meanwhile, Liliana — who'd had schemes hatched against her without her knowledge — showed no sign of letting her indignation go. Puffing her cheeks and voicing her anger in sound effects, she had, moments earlier, tried to produce a new song titled "The Legend of the Instant-Defeat Hero" based on the sight of Subaru laid out from the impact, and had thereby earned Beatrice's displeasure.

"Liliana, girl, just this once, won't you go along with the young master's feelings?"

"...........................................................I understand."

It took quite a while for her to understand, but Liliana finally relented. As a result, thanks to Subaru's magnificent performance, the Emilia Camp ended up making its triumphant return from today's negotiation bearing, splendidly, no results whatsoever.

"So basically, that Liliana girl is a total celebrity fanatic — a total groupie — so Kiritaka wanted to keep us away from her. That's what the third condition for handing over the magic stone was all about."

"And just as it was about to come together nicely, Natsuki-san went and smashed it to pieces. I see, I understand now. You really never do anything right, do you!?"

"I feel bad about it too this time, but who could've predicted picking up a flag like that after getting seasick... it's a total case of force majeure..."

With the negotiations having fizzled out, Otto's dissatisfaction was plain to see. Subaru's excuses lacked their usual sharpness too — partly from lingering damage, but also because Kiritaka's manic display suggested tomorrow's negotiations would be an uphill battle.

"Now, now, Otto-kun, don't get so worked up. It's not like Subaru meant any harm — these things happen sometimes."

"That's right. Emilia-tan, tell him some more."

"Subaru needs to properly reflect on this. Setting Liliana-san aside, causing that much of a ruckus in someone else's place of business is a nuisance, isn't it? Even the receptionist must have been troubled."

"Yes, ma'am. Sorry."

When Subaru bowed his head obediently, Emilia nodded and said "Good." Watching this exchange between master and servant — one that somehow took the sting out of the moment — Otto let out a sigh.

"In any case, that's the end of today's negotiations. For now I'd like to head back to the Water Feathered Inn to rework our strategy, but... I have some business to attend to, so I'll be stepping away for a while."

"Business?"

At the unexpected remark, everyone tilted their heads, and Otto gestured toward the far side of the Muse Company building.

"Since we've come all the way out to Priestella, we should establish connections wherever we can. Today I'll just be having a talk, but it's possible Lady Emilia may need to make an appearance later on. When that time comes—"

"Yes, understood. But is it all right if I don't come with you today?"

"If Lady Emilia shows up somewhere without prior notice, no one will be able to give a proper welcome, and it'll turn into quite the ordeal. Not acting rashly is part of being considerate, too."

"Okaaay. Understood. I'll remember that."

At Emilia's agreeable reply, Otto added, "Please head straight back, all right?" — as though speaking to a child — before heading off into the depths of the second district. Garfiel showed signs of wanting to accompany him, but was turned down with, "Seeing Lady Emilia home safely takes priority."

"So Subaru, what were you and Liliana-san talking about?"

"Oh? Is Emilia-tan curious about what I was talking about with another girl? Hmm, I dunno — little changes like that make me really happy, somehow."

"No, I'm just curious what kind of person the songstress is. No need to misunderstand."

"I'd have been fine with letting me misunderstand!?"

As ever, Emilia's innocent bluntness sliced through Subaru's heart without mercy. Still, since there was no reason to stay quiet about it, and it was par for the course anyway, Subaru quickly recovered and told Emilia about what had happened along the way.

"When I first found her singing in the park, man, I thought I was seeing something amazing. Her voice totally lived up to being called the 'Songstress.' Right, Bea-ko?"

"That much I won't deny, I suppose. That much, I won't deny, perhaps. Only that, mind you."

"Beatrice, you're really driving that point home — did something happen?"

"I suppose Liliana demonstrated to us a rather thorny proposition — that for one talent to shine so brilliantly, other important qualities might have to be whittled away, I suppose."

Subaru could understand why Beatrice felt the need to press the point so hard, again and again. Liliana had poured everything into her singing voice, and the result was, without a doubt, that unfortunate girl before them.

"People don't always get the talent they want, but even if you do get the talent you wanted, whether you turn out to be a wonderful person is a whole different question."

"Ohh, gettin' pretty philosophical there, ain't ya, boss. That singin' really got you thinkin' like that, huh."

"I won't deny it. Not that. This answer's basically universal when it comes to Liliana."

Marveling at its versatility, the group made their way back — on foot this time, unlike the trip there, since everyone had come along. Part of it was the recurring problem of leaving Subaru behind on the waterways, and part of it was —

"Since it's such a beautiful city, I really want to walk around properly and take it all in. Otto-kun said there's nothing left to do today, so..."

Emilia seemed to feel guilty about voicing such a selfish request, but it was far too cute a wish to even call selfish. Naturally, Subaru raised no objection, nor did Garfiel or Beatrice.

"Now the only worry is whether we can actually get Emilia-tan safely back to the inn while showing her around."

"No need to worry, I suppose. If it comes down to it, Betty can keep her left hand on the wall, perhaps."

"I believe we already discussed at length the flaw in that method."

"Ain't neither of you gotta worry. I got my nose. Knows the way to the inn, knows the shrimp's scent through and through."

"—Oh?"

At the mention of scent as one of Garfiel's clues, the fact that Mimi's presence was among them made Subaru grin despite himself. That little cat girl's reactions to Garfiel were a bit too overflowing with energy to read easily, but it could only be read as an expression of affection. Age-wise they were well matched too, and it was a pairing whose future was fun to look forward to. Incidentally, Garfiel continued, as ever, to be endlessly spurned by his true love, Ram, who seemed to hold no feelings toward him beyond those for a little brother.

"Anyway, Garfiel, I sincerely hope you find happiness — as my little brother figure."

"Huh? Don't start sayin' creepy stuff outta nowhere, boss. Well, can't say it feels bad, though..."

At Subaru's knowing pat on the shoulder, Garfiel cocked his head but grinned, baring his teeth. May happiness come to his honest, unhateable little brother figure. Subaru wished it sincerely, from the bottom of his heart.

"Still, what a lovely town. Everywhere I look is fresh and new — it makes me feel restless."

On the way back, Emilia seemed in high spirits at the ever-changing scenery. Just watching Emilia's cheerful expressions shift was enough to lift Subaru's own mood, and indeed, the cityscape was one that made him nod along with her words. The city's meticulously calculated architectural style was as exquisite as a work of art or fine craft. Even the canal flowing through the city's center held a beauty that went beyond mere function.

"Though the origins of this city itself were nothing but shady rumors."

"The limits of the technology back then, or trying to seal away some evil Witchbeast, or something. But no matter the reason it started, the fact that what's here now is beautiful doesn't change. Right?"

Emilia stopped on the bridge, smiling as she gazed down at the great canal. Lost in the sight of her, Subaru nodded, "Yeah," his heart light and clear.

Whatever the reason it started, all that mattered now was what was here now. The feelings that drove one toward a result, and the result one finally obtained — if you could reach it, the beginning didn't matter. What mattered wasn't the start, but the end.

"Right, Mom?"

"Did you say something just now?"

"Just remembering some magic words from the woman I respect most in the world."

Even after all this time since the memory, there was still much that could give him courage even now. He would never forget, and he kept on receiving things too precious to forget. Natsuki Subaru, even today, lived on dragging along those feelings he'd been given.

As Subaru and Emilia exchanged smiles like that, Garfiel and Beatrice read the mood and waited a little way off. Preparing these small moments for the two of them, so busy and so short on time, was a shared consideration among the people around them. Even Beatrice admitted as much, honestly.

"His face is all slack-jawed and pathetic, I suppose."

"That's just how it is when things are goin' good with the girl you're sweet on. Makes me feel better, if anything. Shows the boss is honestly a man, after all."

"And what exactly is that supposed to mean, I wonder?"

"Nah, it's just — most the girls 'round the boss are younger than him, and 'sides that it's mostly guys... figured I oughta wonder if he wasn't just cozyin' up to Emilia-sama for show."

"Subaru has no problem whatsoever being a pervert and a manly weirdo, I suppose! He's always touching Betty and Petra all over for no reason, perhaps!"

"That kinda guarantee don't sound like somethin' to be happy about, does it?"

The two of them traded talk of Subaru's proclivities right there in the street. Incidentally, for the sake of Subaru's honor, it should be noted that his contact with those younger than him carried no intent whatsoever beyond an expression of affection. Unaware of the unsavory conversation happening behind him, Subaru went on enjoying the City of Water with Emilia.

"Well, let's start heading back soon. I want to get a proper look at tonight's inn too. It had such a strange shape to it."

"That'd be the wahu-style architecture. Honestly, I'm kind of curious too. Though maybe not for the same reason as you, Emilia-tan."

"Really? Fufu, then we'd better hurry."

Letting go of the railing, Emilia stepped lightly backward with a bright smile. But — perhaps because she was a little hurried — Emilia stepped back without checking behind her, and—

"Ah."

"Whoa there."

She backed straight into a hooded figure who happened to be passing by just then, bumping into their back. The figure caught Emilia, who let out a small cry, with a steadying hand on reflex.

"I—I'm so sorry. I wasn't looking behind me properly..."

"Sorry from me too. This girl's a bit careless. I'll make sure to have a word with her."

Turning around in a fluster, Emilia apologized to the hooded figure. Subaru hurried over as well, standing beside Emilia and bowing his head to the stranger. Not mentioning Emilia's name on reflex was a small precaution — there was no telling who might notice her true identity. If Emilia's status became known out in the street, there was no telling what kind of commotion it might draw. Naturally, Emilia was also wearing her perception-obstructing coat.

For that reason, such a small brush with a stranger shouldn't have developed into anything serious. This time, too, should have been no different.

"This time the fault was mine as well. For a moment, I found myself entranced."

"...Um?"

"By the beautiful silver hair of the young lady who bumped into me, that is. Long ago, there was a woman I meant to take as my wife who had hair every bit as beautiful. It brought back the memory, and I failed to step aside in time."

Depending on how one took it, the remark sounded almost like flirtation, and the voice carried a thick, intoxicated tone. The hooded figure was a slender man, but from the timbre of his voice, likely still young. Subaru found himself puzzling over just how long ago this business of "meaning to take a wife" might have been. Still, on pure instinct as a man, he judged this was not someone he wanted getting too close to Emilia.

"Well, let's just call it mutual carelessness this time. Our apology's gotten across, I think, so we'll be on our way."

"Wait, Subaru. That's such an insincere way to apologize..."

"It's fine, okay?"

"—"

When Subaru pulled Emilia back by the arm, Emilia fell silent too, as though she'd sensed something. Watching the exchange between the two of them, the hooded figure shook his head slowly.

"It's no trouble. For now, I bear you two no anger, nor any business with you. If you wish to go, then go. Should something arise, fate will simply arrange the occasion again, on its own."

"Ah, right. Well then — here's hoping fate leads us to meet again tomorrow."

Answering the man's poetic phrasing with a chuunibyou-flavored line of his own, Subaru took his leave of the scene. Pulling Emilia's arm along, Subaru glanced over at her — and found her, for some reason, stealing repeated glances back at the man, worry plain on her face.

"I know my attitude was a little rough, maybe, but that was to protect Emilia-tan from a weird bug, so I need you to just let that one go."

"Eh? Oh, no, that's not it. I did think Subaru's attitude wasn't very nice, but honestly the carelessness was mine to begin with. But that's not it. That's not what I mean..."

She cut herself off there, doubt lingering in her eyes. But, unable to hold her tongue, her lips trembled as she spoke,

"That person just now — I feel like I might have met them somewhere before... that's the feeling I got. I couldn't get a good look at his face, so I'm not sure."

"Someone Emilia-tan's met before? If that's the case, I'd probably know them too, generally speaking."

"Yeah... but I don't know. Who could that have been?"

Apparently troubled enough by it, Emilia glanced back once more. But the figure from before was already gone, and it seemed no answer would be forthcoming.

"Yo, boss. You came draggin' her back in an awful hurry — got worried some pretty boy was gonna steal her away from ya?"

Teasing, Garfiel called out as Subaru led Emilia back across the bridge to where he'd been waiting. Subaru clicked his tongue at him.

"Idiot, this isn't the time to be joking around. What good are you if you don't show up when some weirdo's bothering her? If it'd been someone I couldn't handle on my own, Emilia-tan could've been in danger."

"That's exactly where a man's supposed to step up and protect her with his own body, ain't it, boss?"

"Sure, if stopping with one layer of my meat shield is enough — but what happens if it goes clean through? I don't exactly have confidence in my thickness. Not as a shield, and not as a person, either."

At Subaru's self-deprecating assessment, Garfiel gave a wry laugh. Garfiel seemed to think of it as a kind of humility on Subaru's part, but from Subaru's own point of view, it was simply a fair evaluation. If anything, the others thought too highly of him.

"Relax. If I figure the guy's dangerous, I'll come flyin' in myself. No need to worry 'bout that guy from before at all. Way he walked, where his eyes went, everything about him screamed amateur. Guy ain't never been through a real scrap, let alone touched a lick of martial arts."

"You can really say that for sure?"

"Just gotta look, is all. I clocked that you used t' swing a sword too, boss, just from watchin'. Way you move your feet, way you swing your arm — got a real distinct habit to it."

"Seriously? That's a creepy skill to have."

Subaru hadn't mentioned to Garfiel that he had kendo experience. It had already been proven that his middle-school-level kendo counted for nothing in this world. Even so, apparently the mark of ingrained training could be read by anyone who knew what to look for. If Garfiel was saying so with that much confidence, maybe this wariness was needless worry after all.

"Anyway, that settles it, let's go, Emilia-tan. Or are you still worried about it?"

Setting his own thoughts on the matter aside for now, Subaru asked Emilia. She was still glancing around, but eventually shook her head, resigned.

"No, I'm fine. Sorry for worrying over nothing. Let's head back."

"Let's do that. Once we're back, cuddling Mimi ought to help you feel better. Oh, and don't worry — I'm satisfied just cuddling Bea-ko, so no need to get jealous."

"Betty didn't say anything of the sort, I suppose!"

Beatrice reacted with sharp indignation at Subaru's remark, and Emilia laughed. Then, still holding a hand to her mouth, she said,

"That's true. If I cuddle Mimi-chan, I bet I'll feel really satisfied. Let's do that."

And with that, wearing an expression that seemed to forget her unease, she started walking once more.

"...I see. Now I finally understand what 'if you come here' meant."

Swaying the hem of his loose-fitting coat, the figure murmured in a low voice. It was the hooded man. He pictured, behind closed eyes, the young woman he'd just brushed against, and a smile rose to his lips — a dark smile, one that carried a faint hint of something malevolent.

"To think it dragged me all the way out here in person. If it had turned out to be nothing, I wasn't planning to show any mercy — but if something like that is around, that changes things."

His tone was flat, but the voice carried a sticky sort of heat within it. What could be glimpsed there was the end result of an obsession — simmered down in a pot until it scorched, curdled over months and years into something viscous — a fragment of a loathsome, all-consuming fixation.

"I never let go of what's mine. I must be complete, as myself, by myself. It's precisely because nothing is missing from that completeness that I am who I am. To be fulfilled means to remain fulfilled, continuously. So of course, the vacant seat must be filled."

Rattling this off quickly, the man tilted his face upward. In that instant, his hood slipped off, and the white hair hidden beneath was laid bare. A wind bearing moisture stirred his white hair, and he clicked his tongue, faintly displeased.

"I suppose I'll have to go and take the seventy-ninth wife — the one still left vacant."

So saying, the white-haired Warlock wore a mocking smile in the City of Water.
