Arc 7 Chapter 73: "Weitz Rogen"

——Weitz Rogen never believed he could live or die like a normal person.

If nothing else, the world tends to undervalue the phrase "like everyone else."
"Like everyone else" is used to mean ordinary, unremarkable, nothing that stands out — but from the perspective of something less than a person, even that is a standard set somewhere up in the clouds.

The iron-and-blood city of Glarasia — that was the name of the hometown where Weitz was born and raised.
It was a deranged city where craftsmen forged steel day and night, pouring their energy into making weapons meant to wound others — the arms and armor and countless tools of war used across the entire Empire were produced there.
Roughly half the residents were involved in arms-making in some form, and Weitz was no exception. Though by the time he was old enough to understand anything, he had no parents, and as someone saddled with odd jobs in a craftsman's workshop, he had neither interest in nor pride in that kind of work.

He worked to eat, and the only reason he did chores in a workshop was that the place he happened to be born was the iron-and-blood city.
If the main industry had been harness-making, he'd have helped make harnesses; if it had been farming, he'd have held a hoe.
With neither attachment nor interest, Weitz worked in silence, glancing sideways at the craftsmen forging steel. He kept poor company and no one could tell what he was thinking; some called him a hard worker, but most people probably thought of Weitz as something unsettling and unknowable.

Weitz didn't mind that. He had no wish to get chummy with anyone.
Since he'd been alone from the moment he was born, it was only natural he'd be alone when he died too.
That kind of fleeting, indifferent way of living was probably what people hated about him.

When suspicion arose at the workshop he frequented that stockpiled materials were being smuggled out and sold off, the first eyes to turn in accusation landed on Weitz.
He acted alone most of the time, and had no relationships, no one who would vouch for him — Weitz had no way to clear the suspicion cast on him.

In the end, he could not clear his name of the false charge, and Weitz was judged guilty of a crime he had never committed, becoming a criminal. ——The first tattoo inked into his skin was the mark of punishment, meant to identify a criminal at a glance.

From the elbows down on both arms — in a city always wrapped in the heat of steel forging, no one wore long sleeves, so the tattoos were always exposed.
Even if he had worn long sleeves, it would have instantly given away that he had something to hide.
The moment the criminal's mark was inked into him, he could no longer live the life he had before.

Even before that his life hadn't been a fine one, but it was at that moment he let go of "being like everyone else."
He could no longer work at a properly run workshop, and to scrape together a day's living, Weitz turned to thievery, beginning to steal and traffic materials himself, until he finally became a real criminal.

——In short, the crime caught up to him afterward.

When he was suspected of smuggling and no one would listen to what he had to say, Weitz was not yet a criminal. But afterward, in the end, Weitz became one.
It was the kind of story where he was bound to become one, one way or another.

"If I'm something vile, then fine…"

Tattooed and made a criminal, cast out of "being like everyone else" in everyone's eyes. Doing wrong to earn his daily bread, still unable to get along with others any better than before.
Then he might as well cut off all those tiresome relationships from the very start.

The tattoo that had only covered his arms from the elbow down, he began inking further himself, larger and larger — onto his shoulders, his chest. Eventually it covered his upper body, then his legs, and finally reached his face and head.

The tattoo on the arm was the mark of a criminal, but a tattoo covering the whole body was a mark of danger — don't come near.
Let everyone fear him and clear the way. The pathetic man once abandoned by all and fallen into criminality no longer existed; what remained was only a troublesome "less-than-human" with nothing left to lose.
And so——

"——Halt, criminal! Don't think you'll get off easy!"

Surrounded by the city's guards, weapons leveled at him, Weitz surrendered at once.
Lying bloodied and collapsed at his feet was a man who had once worked as an apprentice craftsman at the workshop — the very place where Weitz had first been made a criminal.
His ear torn off by teeth, weeping blood and tears as he screamed — that man was the one who, in a drunken slip of the tongue, had let out that he was the true culprit behind the false charge pinned on Weitz that day. Weitz had heard the rumor and taken his revenge.

Spitting out the ear he'd held in his mouth, Weitz was seized by the guards and thrown in a cell.
Weitz was known as a marked man in the iron-and-blood city. No path of mercy was offered him; before long, his sentence was decided — to be sent as a criminal slave to the infamous Sword Slave Isle.

He had no will left to resist, and accepted it. Without even the will to struggle, he crossed the lake.
At the place he was sent to, he was paired off with other wretches in the same situation and forced to take part in rituals fighting terrifying Witchbeasts.
He didn't care how it turned out. He had not the slightest intention of dying.
Even if it meant sacrificing someone else, even if it meant betrayal — he would survive, and someday.

Someday, be treated like a person — "like everyone else"—

"——Weitz! Take up your sword! You're the one we're counting on!!"


——He had wanted to be treated as a person, "like everyone else."

"Cover him...!"

The instant he saw the kicked guard's head come spinning through the air, spraying blood, Weitz shoved Schwartz — who was right beside him — into Tanza.

This girl in the kimono had, despite her childlike appearance, tremendous strength.
But she hadn't learned to wield that strength properly. Her body and her heart were mismatched in strength, too precarious to be relied upon. ——With Schwartz, though, she made a good pair.
Weak in body but strong in heart, Schwartz. He would be able to make good use of Tanza.
So in that instant, shoving him at Tanza — strong in body but weak in heart — had to be the right call.

And as for Weitz himself, who had done the shoving——

"O-oaaaaaah...!!"

He ran forward, and with the arm he swung up, he knocked the flying guard's head down onto the corridor floor.
In an instant he'd beheaded one of his own and kicked the head flying — a shocking act meant to freeze Weitz and the others' thoughts. But it couldn't be just that.
In fact, the other four's reactions had lagged by a beat, but Weitz had been able to move. Schwartz was wary of this man for a reason that surely went beyond a mere shock tactic.

That was the thought Weitz had, the very next instant——

"——Ugh!?"

Glaring straight ahead at the man closing in with an axe in hand, Weitz felt something crash in right beside him — a black monstrous bird, a beastwarrior, smashing through the corridor wall as it barreled through.
It shattered the wall half a step behind Weitz, tearing sideways through the corridor on the island's upper level.
An attack by a flying beastwarrior, aimed at Weitz and the others — no, wrong. It was a beastwarrior, no doubt, but that bird hadn't been aiming at them at all.

"————!!"

The monstrous bird burst through the wall, and its beak drove straight into the head of the guard sprawled on the floor.
The beheaded guard's head — that was what the bird had come crashing through the corridor to reach. What it meant, Weitz didn't know, and there was no time to think about it.

"You...!"

Amid the tremendous impact half a step behind him, showered by chunks of exploding wall and grazed by the beastwarrior's sharp wing, Weitz gritted his teeth and did not let his eyes leave the man before him.
The man who, even on hearing Schwartz's true identity, had only sharpened his killing intent instead of groveling — dangerous.
A genuine threat, unlike the false menace Weitz had built for himself out of tattoos.
He had to stop it, no matter what.

"————"

Seeing Weitz advance, the man narrowed his eyes cruelly.
Without a word, that look alone told him the man meant to kill him. And as the man raised his axe with his left arm, Weitz raised his own left arm in turn.

The arm first marked with a tattoo, the mark that had made him a criminal, the arm that had set him on the path that washed him up on this island.
He poured all his strength into it, meaning to take the man's blow head-on——

"Gh, ggh...!"

Mercilessly, the swung axe came for the left side of Weitz's head.
He cut his raised arm into its path, angling the arm sideways rather than up, to meet the axe blade. At the moment of impact, the bone in his elbow shattered, and the bones of his upper arm and shoulder were crushed flesh and all.
His vision went red with shock and pain, and his left arm turned a blackish color entirely unlike the tattoo.
But——

"I held..." he managed.

Rather than waiting for the blow, stepping into it had slightly weakened the axe's force.
That was the biggest reason it hadn't crushed Weitz's skull, hadn't ended his life. He'd always had the kind of nerve people called abnormal, but never the judgment to match it.
That nerve and that judgment had only come together as a result of meeting a child even more reckless than himself.
And it was because of that meeting that Weitz's arm now reached the man——

"You didn't hold."

The instant his left arm was destroyed and he tried to seize the man with his remaining right arm, the man's figure vanished.
——No, not vanished. He'd simply become impossible to see.

When something enters the outer edge of one's field of vision, the figure disappears into a dark, blurred region. Something like that had just happened. But it was strange. Wrong, somehow. Suddenly, on the right side of Weitz's vision, that unseeable region spread out all at once.

As if the right side of Weitz's field of vision had suddenly been crushed by something.

"Weitz——!!"

While Weitz, disoriented, still couldn't find the vanished man, someone called his name.
Slowly, he turned to look back, and saw the broken corridor and billowing dust. And there — the vanished man, bringing down his axe to sever the neck of the monstrous bird that had wrecked the corridor.

"————"

Beyond the man decapitating the bird, on the crumbling corridor, he saw the other four.
Some had stumbled back, some had fallen on their backsides, but all of them seemed to have escaped the bird's sideswipe. Weitz was a little surprised at his own relief over that.
Even surprised, he found himself wanting to say something to them as they stared back at him, dumbstruck.

His broken left arm hurt. For some reason, the right side of his face had started to hurt too.
Pulling his consciousness back from where that pain seemed to be dragging it far away, he said,

"It was the first time..."

"Weitz...!"

"...someone said they believed in me."

Murmuring that, Weitz understood — that was what had been lodged in his chest all along.
Understanding it, the strength drained from his knees, and he collapsed where he stood.

"Weitz——!!"

Hearing his name shouted again, emotionally, he closed his eyes.
Somehow, it felt strangely good.

As he tried to rush to fallen Weitz's side, a slender finger reached out and stopped him.
Turning back, Subaru saw the kimono-clad girl shake her head with tears in her eyes, refusing to let his impulsive action through.
And before he could shake that finger off——

"————"

An ugly cry tore through the air, ear-splitting, and the ceiling of the corridor above came crashing down.
Right over fallen Weitz, the corridor gave way under the weight with a violent noise, crushing down, swallowing Weitz beneath the falling ceiling, and the floor gave out.
The broken corridor punched through to the floor below, and Subaru witnessed Weitz and the enormous gray frog that had crushed the ceiling both plunge straight down together.

Weitz — whose left arm had taken an axe blow, whose right eye had taken a knife strike.

"Weitz——!!"

Reaching out toward the crumbling floor and ceiling, toward the falling corridor, Subaru screamed.
Weitz had fallen. Weitz, who had leapt forward to protect Subaru and the others. He had to jump down, now, now, and go save him.

"Lucky the second one dropped out of the fight. Witchbeasts are a pain to kill."

While Subaru's thoughts spun out of control, Todd, glancing sidelong at the collapsed corridor, muttered that.
At his feet lay the corpse of the great black bird, its head severed. It was the Witchbeast that had come barreling into the corridor, then been killed by Todd's axe.

Everything happening was moving too fast to process.
What Todd had done, what had just happened before his eyes — all of it. Until he could process those, he couldn't think of anything else. ——He couldn't think about Weitz.
He couldn't think about what had happened to Weitz, what had become of him.
He couldn't think——

"——Witchbeasts, you know, eat the corpse of whoever broke their horn."

"————"

Suddenly, Todd, axe slung over his shoulder, said that.
Subaru, still unable to fully accept what was in front of him, and everyone else besides, drew a sharp breath at Todd's words and turned to look at him.
Receiving all four sets of eyes, Todd pointed at the corpse at his feet.

"Break the horn and you can make a Witchbeast obey you, but I bet it builds up a hell of a grudge while it's being ordered around. When the one who broke it dies, the Witchbeast eats the corpse out of spite. Gross, right?"

Shrugging, Todd spoke of little-known Witchbeast ecology.
How he'd come to know it, Subaru didn't want to know, and to begin with, it wasn't the kind of information that did anyone any good to know.
Still, it explained what had just happened.

That great bird had come barreling into the corridor because the guard whose head had been severed was the one who'd broken its horn, and the bird would go after that corpse — Todd had known that in advance.
He'd kicked the head flying, meaning to drag Subaru and the others into the great bird's path.
But it hadn't turned out that way. Why, why, why——

Why——

"Weitz—"

"For a guy who looked like that, he sure cared about his friends. ——That's why he's dead."

"—Ah"

An explanation so thorough, so unlike Todd.
Todd's way was to kill without letting anyone hear, know, or learn anything — giving no information at all. So why, this time, had he run his mouth so freely?
To make him listen, to make him understand, to steer Subaru's mind toward the next question.

The connection between the two dead guards and the Witchbeast that had come crashing in.
Once that was resolved, Subaru's thoughts would move on to the next thing. To Weitz.

To the fact that Weitz was dead, beyond any hope of undoing.

"Aah, ahhhhh——!!"

He shouldn't have died. Weitz shouldn't have died.
He shouldn't have tried to protect Subaru and the others, shouldn't have stood up to this terrifying man, Todd. He shouldn't have defied this monster who killed anyone who defied him.
Because if he died here——

"Finally, you're making a proper kid's face."

The instant Subaru understood Weitz was dead, his mind filled to bursting with regret and panic.
Seeing that reaction, Todd smiled — exactly as intended — and raised his axe.

Todd's careful explanation had been meant to make Subaru organize his thoughts.
To force him to face Weitz's death, which he'd unconsciously avoided thinking about, and break his heart with it — to turn an unknowable enemy into flesh-and-blood person.

"Schwartz-sama——!"

Subaru, frozen with shock; Hiain and Idra, still not mentally recovered. Ahead of all three, the one who managed to move her body was Tanza.
Tanza fixed her eyes on the approaching Todd, spread her arms, and stood in front of Subaru.
But at Tanza's action, Todd frowned and said,

"You've got that backwards, kid."

"Eh?"

Saying that before the standing Tanza, Todd dropped to one knee. As Tanza's eyes were drawn by the motion, the crouching Todd slid his arm in under her, between her legs, and swung it upward.
In an instant, Tanza's small body was lifted into the air and hurled sideways — into the large hole in the wall that the great bird had smashed, connecting to the outside.

"Ah"

Even with physical abilities unbefitting a child, she couldn't defy gravity or the laws of physics. Naturally, her body wasn't heavier than a person's — Tanza weighed no more than one small girl.
Caught off guard, Tanza reached out a hand, but her slender fingers found nothing to grab, and her body was thrown down to the far side of the island.

"Go play."

Tanza, thrown down into the sky, her scream growing more and more distant below.
With Tanza, who had stood in the way, now gone, there was no one left to protect Subaru and the others from Todd's axe.

"He's His Imperial Majesty's...!"

"Hm?"

"He's the Emperor's own child!? Are you out of your mind!?"

It was Idra who glared at the turning Todd and shouted that.
Eyes bloodshot, his normally good-natured face burning with anger, his protest accused Todd of remaining unmoved even by the "lie" that Subaru was the illegitimate child of the Vollachian Emperor.
But Todd's answer to that protest was simple.

——Without a word, he swung his axe to split Idra's head open.

"Whoaaa!"

An instant before his head was split, Idra was yanked back by his clothes and fell on his backside. The axe swung through empty air, and behind wide-eyed Idra, Subaru — who had pulled him back — shook his head frantically.
It was hopeless. Words wouldn't reach him. From the very start, this whole threat had been the wrong move.

Subaru had tried to rely on an easy way out.
And as punishment for that, he'd fallen into this situation.

"Dodged it, huh. Miss, miss. But——"

Resetting his stance with the axe, Todd turned to the side with an exasperated look. His gaze landed on Hiain, camouflaged into the surrounding scenery beside Idra and Subaru, who had fallen on their backsides.
Blended into the broken floor and wall, there was no way to tell by sight that Hiain was there.
But he hadn't moved at high speed.

"Why'd you go and disappear right in front of me?"

"Schwa—"

The moment Hiain's eyes met Todd's and he flinched, his camouflage broke.
The perfect blending with the scenery that had held until then became, in that one spot, hideously distorted — as if someone had crushed just that part of a painting, something unbearable to look at.
And now, red paint spurted out from Hiain, adding to the mess.

"————"

Struck by the axe from the shoulder, cut down to below the chest, Hiain fell. His camouflage dissolved as blood poured from his body, and the corpse of a gray Lizard Human dropped with a thud.

Weitz was dead, Tanza had been thrown, and now Hiain had been killed too.
One after another, right before Subaru's eyes.
And then——

"A d-duel...!"

In a trembling voice, tearing his eyes from Hiain's corpse, Idra stood up.
Pointing a finger at the man who had just killed his comrade before his eyes, teeth chattering, his face red with rage, pale with fear, and drifting into white with something else entirely, Idra glared at Todd.

The lie he'd told on the field of the Sparka, to rally Subaru and the others and steel his own resolve.
Unlike Subaru's easy lie, it carried within it his own ideal, given the name of a lie.

"If you're a warrior too——"

That ideal of Idra's — perhaps too idealistic — was betrayed.

"Wah!"
"——!"

Faint as that hope was, Idra's offer had been desperate and earnest.
Todd cut him off with a loud shout, striking at the hollowness beneath Idra's forced bravado. And into that hollowness, he drove his blade.

"Guh"

Falling to his knees, Idra reached a trembling hand toward his own throat. Todd's knife was buried dead center in it, its sharp point jutting out the back of his neck.
Idra's hand grasped the knife's hilt, trying to pull it free. But before he could draw it out, the axe came down from directly above and split his head open.

"—Ah"

Idra fell forward, and Todd pulled the knife from his body. Idra's corpse, carelessly discarded, lay lined up with Hiain's like a nightmare.
Beyond them, Weitz's corpse lay buried in the fallen rubble.
Tanza, thrown down, hadn't come back — had something happened to her, dropped from that height? And come to think of it, wasn't the frog that had fallen with Weitz down there too?
If so, then Tanza, and the other sword slaves on this island——

"So, really, what's the truth?"

"Eh..."

"Are you really the Emperor's kid?"

Todd, axe over his shoulder, looked down at Subaru, who sat slumped with hollow eyes.
Watching Subaru — who had lost his comrades and couldn't even muster the will to flee — Subaru understood: he'd been kept alive until he was the last one left.

The lie Subaru had told, about being the Emperor's secret child, had truly driven a wedge into Todd.
He, too, had been considering the possibility that Subaru really was the Emperor's hidden child.

"Then..."

"Hm?"

"Why not... surrender? If I'm the Emperor's..."

If Todd had considered the possibility that he was the Emperor's son, then Subaru and the others' pleas should have been worth considering.
The reason Subaru had gone along with this plan, with Tanza and the others' misunderstanding, was that he'd thought it the best way to restore the mental state of his fellow "combination" members, and because there was a sliver of hope —
a chance Todd would choose to raise the white flag and survive, rather than keep killing.
But in the end, Subaru had lost every one of his comrades, and now his life was in Todd's hands.

"Why? Why aren't you? You're..."

"If I surrender and get killed for some made-up reason later, that's no good to me."

"————"

"Better to find out the truth once I'm in the advantageous position. I can spend my thinking on the judgment call, less likely to screw it up. ——And besides, it's reassuring."

That last reason, spoken as though it were the most important one, made Subaru let out a breath.
It felt as if Todd had had his hands around his throat this whole time.

"Whether or not you're the Emperor's bastard, the name's got me curious."

"The... name?"

"Natsuki Schwartz. It sounds a lot like the name of someone scary I know. And come to think of it, your smell's a lot like that scary guy's too."

Sniffing lightly, Todd looked at Subaru with cold eyes.
The talk of smell — Subaru had heard something about that once before too, back when he'd first learned Todd had come to the Sword Slave Isle for the first great massacre.
Hearing "smell" again, a bad premonition rose in Subaru's mind.

"You can smell Miasma?"

"Miasma? Ah, no, no, don't get the wrong idea. I mean actual smell, body odor. My nose is pretty sharp, is all. That's what's strange about it."

"————"

"——You got an older brother or something? If you do, I might just keep you alive as a bargaining chip against him."

Denying Subaru's guess, what followed was a startling proposal.
The idea of keeping him alive, coming out of Todd's mouth. And what's more, the "older brother" Todd was talking about was, in all likelihood, none other than Subaru himself.

He thought the Subaru with the stretched-out limbs was the brother of the shrunken Subaru.
More than that, Todd probably had some memory of the name Subaru had used while disguised as a woman, so there might be suspicion connecting him to that "Natsumi Schwartz" as well.
Either way, if the option of "not killing" had taken root in Todd's mind——

"————"

Weitz, Hiain, and Idra's deaths lay right beside him.
If he closed his eyes, Gustav and old man Null's deaths came to mind too. And beyond that, the deaths of the many sword slaves he'd seen along the way, their corpses, rose in his memory.
Everyone, everyone had died. Died, and then.

——And then, Subaru could not die.

"...Do you... know my brother?"

Riding along with Todd's misunderstanding, Subaru shifted his focus to keep the conversation going.
For whatever reason, if Todd was wary of the tall Subaru, then he'd use that wariness to keep the small Subaru alive.
If he could get through this moment that way——

"——Wait."

"Eh?"

Opening his mouth, trying to string together words to get through this moment. But Subaru's face was seized by Todd's reaching palm, and through the gaps between his fingers, Subaru could see his eyes.
Crouched down, gripping Subaru's face, Todd's eyes were terribly cold.
And then——

"——You just tried to work me, didn't you?"

There was a dull thud, and a sharp sensation slid into his chest.
Subaru felt the coldness of that point inside his own body and his eyes went wide. Then, a beat late, a pain enough to numb his hands and feet let out a tremendous scream at the back of his head.
At that tremendous scream, Subaru's own mouth screamed too.

"Gyah, giiiih"

"Miss, miss. Can't let your guard down for a second."

An oversized knife was driven deep into Subaru's chest.
Whether it could be pulled out or not wasn't the issue — it was too big. It had gone in. Whatever it had struck inside, some important thing in his chest, was probably broken.
Blood foam rose at the corner of his mouth, and as red drool spilled out, Subaru's body trembled.

He coughed, and a reddened bundle fell from his mouth.
It didn't matter. That didn't matter anymore either. Useless. Couldn't use it. Dying, mustn't die. Bad if he died. Mustn't, mustn't die.

"Don't move."

As Subaru rolled and tried to crawl away, Todd said that and placed a foot on his stomach. Pinning him down so he couldn't move, couldn't escape.
With him held down, Todd raised his axe before him.

The pain, the fear, how bad this was, how dangerous this was — anyway, anyway, this red, this cold pain, this, this, anyway, stop it, don't, mustn't.

"D-don't... die..."

"One, two—"

Weakly, he lifted his hand to shield his head. It was no use.
The falling blade of the axe crushed Subaru's hand, the head beneath it, his life, all of it——

"——!"

His head split open, everything inside him spilling out.
Feeling as though he'd seen that horrifying sight, Subaru's throat exploded with a scream. He reached his trembling hands to his head, and both hands touched his own skull.

Not split open. His head, unsplit, his chest, unhurt, his life, unlost, all still there.

Confirming that, Subaru let out a breath of relief, and as he raised his face——

"—Ah"

There, slowly turning to face him, was the man covered head to toe in tattoos.
His left arm mangled by an axe, his right eye pierced by a knife, bleeding an enormous amount — Weitz.

"It was the first time..."

Weitz's blood-soaked lips trembled, spilling out words.
Seeing that, Subaru let out a pathetic, pathetic breath — "Ah" —

"...someone said they believed in me."

"Aaaaaaaaah——!!"

Spilling out those weak words, Weitz's body tilted and began to fall.
No matter how far he reached, no matter what he did, there was no saving him now — Weitz's body.

He shouldn't have died. Subaru shouldn't have died.
It shouldn't have been made certain.

Pushed back once more, restarted from "death" — Subaru's starting point——

"Aaaaaaaaah——!!"


——Because whenever someone appears who absolutely cannot be saved, Natsuki Subaru's "heart" breaks.
