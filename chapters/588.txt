Arc 7 Chapter 83: "War and Battlefield"

—Steadily, the flames of rebellion spread and grow across the Vollachia Empire.

These past few days, the empire has been in an uproar, as if the peace of recent years had been a lie, venting a bloodlust that had built up thick and turbid within it.
Generation after generation, the Vollachian imperial line had built a great river of blood—and the present Emperor was supposed to be the one who would put an end to that ghastly tradition.
In fact, under his reign there had been the occasional skirmish, but nothing that could be called a great upheaval had occurred. The people of the empire should have been savoring, for the first time since its founding, a taste of "peace."

And yet—

"Turns out, when you open the lid, the people of the empire didn't actually want peaceful, uneventful days after all."

Seated in his chair, gazing down at the map spread over his knees that showed the state of the war, he murmured the words with something like sympathy.
He had no way of knowing what principles Vincent Vollachia, the Emperor, had governed by, but it surely hadn't been a matter of simply stumbling into peace by luck. Year after year, wearing down mind and body, he had built an era of peace unprecedented in the empire's history.
To learn, so easily, that it hadn't even been wanted—the disappointment that must sit in his heart was beyond imagining. Honestly, the people were fools, and the Emperor was to be pitied.

"From where I'm standing, I don't get why anyone'd go out of their way to raise their odds of dying... Can't understand what goes on in the heads of the crazy ones. That guy too—ah, er, right, Jamal and the rest."

He recalled, whenever there was a spare moment, the man going on about the pride of the empire's people or some such.
Tracing the memory of a man whose face had already gone hazy, Todd Fang steadied himself. If he let something careless slip and it got out that he'd forgotten Jamal, that would be trouble.
At the very least, in front of the woman he loved and the woman he had to treat with the utmost care, Jamal still needed to go on living inside his heart.

"—Todd, are you tired?"

At the edge of his vision, as he spread the map, Todd suddenly saw a woman's face leaning in close.
Frankly, it was bad for his heart. Just a moment ago she'd been up on the roof of the carriage. When had she come to sit beside him? Todd's senses hadn't caught it at all.
Then again, there were countless masters of that caliber in the world. If you let yourself be afraid of every opponent you couldn't beat in a straight fight, the world became an unlivable place.
Most people stumbled because they confused *can't beat* with *can't kill*.

There were opponents you could never beat, and yet could kill with ease.
Todd didn't consider that kind of opponent a threat. Which was why the woman beside him—Arakiya—was no exception.

"Todd?"

Slipping in through the open window, Arakiya sat down on the carriage's long bench—no, rather, ill-manneredly, she folded her knees up onto the seat and crouched.
He'd heard she'd lived in the imperial capital for a fair while, but not a scrap of city manners had rubbed off on her.
Either she'd simply never been taught that kind of thing, or the people around her had never bothered to. It was common with strong people like her—being left alone precisely *because* they were strong.
In Vollachia, strength took priority over every other consideration.
And so a vicious cycle had formed: because Arakiya was strong, no one dared call out her unsightly or rude behavior. Of course, someone stronger than her would be permitted to offer criticism on such matters—

"They say the famed 'Blue Lightning' is an even bigger freak than Arakiya, on top of it all."

Living in the Vollachian Empire, one had no end of opportunities to hear rumors about the empire's strongest, the "One."
He'd like to say "for better or worse," but rumors about the "One" were, even by the standards of the inhuman hellscape that was Vollachia, uniformly bad. Hardly the sort of role model suited to raising Arakiya properly.
As a result, no one had ever bothered correcting behavior like hers.
Not that Todd had any particular reason to fix it either—

"Arakiya, sit properly. It's unsightly."

"Sit? Me, on the chair?"

"What else? What do you think a chair's for?"

Asked back with a blank, puzzled look, Todd offered that reproach in return. At that, Arakiya, still looking a bit confused, obediently sat the proper way on the seat.
Except her legs were spread, so he tapped her knee to make her close them too.

"The people under you watch how the strong carry themselves. In this empire, strength lets you get away with anything, sure, but soldiers have hearts too, whatever else is true. Goodwill or hatred—which one's easier to fight alongside in a commanding officer? You don't need me to spell that out for you."

"...Does it matter? That. I'm the one who fights alone anyway."

"And after you've torn up the battlefield, who hunts down the survivors? Who clears the bodies? Who negotiates with the ones who surrender?"

"—————"

"Besides, if 'alone' were good enough, why would I even be here? Your own words are full of holes."

Refuting her point by point, Todd left Arakiya frowning, at a loss for words.
Fortunately, he could tell by now that this wasn't the face of the empire's second-ranked power quietly stockpiling resentment—it was the face of someone who'd conceded he had a point.
In the early days, he used to worry over every little correction he made. Now there was no more hesitation about it.

Once you got used to handling her, Arakiya was rather like training a sheepdog.
The difference from an ordinary sheepdog being that the dog itself happened to also be a wolf of monstrous, outrageous strength.
Todd found himself smirking at his own turn of phrase.

"Todd, are you tired? You really are."

"If I'm tired at all, it's just from all this traveling. You're quite the hard worker. No wonder His Excellency and the Chancellor value you so much."

"...I'm needed. That's why."

Worried by Todd's silence, Arakiya voiced her concern once more. Todd flatly dismissed her worry and turned the conversation back on her, and Arakiya lowered her eyes.

Arakiya must have had some awareness herself, that she was being used for convenience's sake.
She wasn't the type to bring up her own past unprompted, and Todd, having no interest in it, had never bothered to dig. But given how he intended to handle her going forward, it might be worth asking after all.
If knowing made her feel closer to him without any further effort on his part, it was a bargain worth making.

"You—"

"The map. What were you looking at?"

But before Todd could get his own topic out, Arakiya neatly cut him off with one of her own. Choking on a half-formed "hm," Todd tilted the map so she could see it too.
It showed the whole of the Vollachian Empire, simplified down to just the major cities and terrain—and scrawled across it were Todd's own notes and markings, made according to his personal sense of things.
Arakiya stared at it intently, her well-shaped brows drawing together, and—

"...I don't get it."

"Figured as much."

Arakiya admitted her incomprehension, but Todd didn't fault her for lacking education over it.
It wasn't that he'd given up on her ability to learn—it was simply that the notations on the map had been written so that no one but Todd himself could decipher them.

No special words or symbols were used, but through deliberate reinterpretation of meaning and intentionally wrong symbols, the information was made unreadable to anyone else.
Even if he carelessly dropped the map, no one could trace Todd's movements from it. And if he were ever taken prisoner, there was even a chance he'd be kept alive for the sake of decoding it.
It cost nothing to prepare, a third hand against stumbling—that sort of thing.

"The markings that mean something are records of where fighting broke out and how large it was. The meaningless ones are just decoys. I'm not going to explain which is which to you."

"Can't tell, so telling me wouldn't help anyway. ...Is it a lot?"

"Yeah. Not since the current Emperor took the throne has it ever gotten this out of hand."

The question had no subject, but Todd understood Arakiya meant the rebellions breaking out across the empire.
It wasn't limited to declarations of treason against the Emperor—actual clashes between imperial troops and rebel forces were occurring again and again, and the rallying cry of rebellion was spreading loudly through region after region.
And the spark that had lit the fuse was the rallying symbol of the rebel forces plotting to seize the throne—

"His Excellency's illegitimate child. The black-haired Crown Prince."

"His Excellency has a child... Really?"

"Who knows. The important thing isn't whether it's true. It's that the rumor's spread this far and, rather than dying out, just kept fanning itself hotter."

"—————"

"So the hard workers get dragged out to every corner of the country. Myself included, apparently."

Arakiya, as one of the "Nine Divine Generals" personally chosen by the Emperor, was naturally on speaking terms with Vincent Vollachia—a man who should have been utterly beyond her reach.
It was hard to say how far Arakiya's judgment could be trusted, but even by her account, the rebels' claim of the Emperor's illegitimate child sounded dubious.
Still, as Todd himself had said, whether it was true or not didn't matter.

"If you didn't want it to seem important, you should've stamped the rumor out while it was still a spark. Falling behind on that is exactly how we ended up with this whole circus."

"—. Is it connected? To the Sword Slave Isle."

"That's..."

Beside Todd, who'd closed one eye, his words caught at Arakiya's murmur.
The incident she'd mentioned—the sword slave isle of Ginunhive—was the job Todd and Arakiya had been secretly ordered to carry out by Chancellor Berstetz, the empire's chief minister, and had spectacularly failed.
It had happened only a few days ago. Todd and Arakiya had been ordered to slaughter every sword slave on the isle, had set out full of confidence—and then, right before landing, had retreated, escaping unscathed.

Naturally, Berstetz had reprimanded them for failing the mission, but Todd's instinct for sniffing out danger had flatly refused to let him set foot on that island.
He had sensed, undeniably, that there was a threat there he must never confront.

"—————"

Todd placed absolute trust in that instinct, and he didn't regret not landing on the island.
Afterward, the fact that the isle had fallen under the sword slaves' control and was now contributing its own flame to the rebellions spreading across the empire wasn't something worth weighing against his own life.
If there was ever a hypothetical case where Todd might come to regret that judgment, it would be—

"Ridiculous. Not like me at all."

"Todd?"

"It's nothing. You worry too much. Rebellions are popping up all over, but none of them will last. —They're just chasing a dream."

Slowly, after a delay, Todd shrugged off Arakiya's earlier question.
He wasn't trying to deflect, and it wasn't just a comforting lie. It was true—the rebel forces were living in a dream. And while a dream felt good as long as you were in it, once it broke, it was the kind of nightmare that made you want to die of self-loathing.
Or perhaps it was the kind of nightmare that never even gave you the chance to wake before collapsing straight into the end.

"—General Arakiya! Lance Corporal Fang! We've arrived!"

The sharp voice from the driver's seat reached them just as Todd finished spitting out those words.
The jolting beneath them gradually eased, the carriage slowing as it neared its destination. As the carriage rolled to a stop, Todd and Arakiya stepped out through the door.

"—————"

The two of them stood side by side atop a low hill. The sky, thick with clouds, hung dark and heavy overhead, and it struck Todd as almost as if the sky itself were anxious about the empire's plunge into endless civil war.
Lowering his gaze from that poetic impression, what came into view was a battlefield stretched across a wide-open plain, where a group in familiar uniform clashed violently against a group in unfamiliar arms.

—The Imperial Army and the rebel forces. The clash on the western plain had already begun.

"Who's the enemy?"

One of the imperial soldiers, already encamped on the hill, came up to the two of them.
The moment Todd opened his mouth to ask, the man's face stiffened with displeasure. He wore a cape—likely a "General," then—and had been addressed so imperiously by a mere lance corporal that he was thrown off guard.
But when he noticed Arakiya standing beside Todd, he straightened up at once.

"The residents of Kunoelemente. They killed their governor and are whipping themselves into a frenzy over it. Apparently the governor had only just taken up the post."

"Bad timing to get moved into a post like that. No luck for the man... So, is our little plague god mixed in with that fired-up mob?"

"Unconfirmed. We believe not."

"If he were, he'd have shown his face from the start anyway. —General Arakiya."

"Mn..."

Receiving the "General's" report, Todd dipped his chin and called out to Arakiya. She answered with something like a sigh and turned—this was a scene where the empire's second-strongest could show her full, unrestrained capability.
All that remained for Todd was to make sure no needless obstacle got in the way.

"Give the signal, pull the troops back. I'd recommend they retreat without so much as a backward glance. Otherwise—"

"Otherwise?"

"General Arakiya's flames don't pick and choose who they burn."

It hadn't especially been meant as a threat, but the effect on the "General" who heard it might as well have been one.
The man drew a short breath and cast a quick glance toward Arakiya. As ever, her face gave nothing away, but she noticed the glance and turned to look back at the "General."
Confronted directly by that crimson eye, uncovered by any eyepatch, the "General" stiffened his jaw and—

"Beat the drums! Pull the troops back!"

He switched gears at once, barking sharp orders to his subordinates within the camp.
Unlike unruly rank-and-file soldiers, a battlefield with a proper "General" in command made things go quickly, which was a relief. Not that it made the battlefield any more enjoyable—all the more reason to get an unpleasant duty over with fast.

"Arakiya, don't hit the ones falling back at the sound of the drums."

"The rest?"

"Do as you like."

Told to do as she liked, Arakiya's eyes wavered with a flicker of confusion.
Bad phrasing, Todd realized at once and reproached himself. She was a woman with the power to do something monstrous, but Arakiya, it seemed, didn't particularly like fighting.
So telling her to do as she liked on a battlefield wasn't the right instruction at all—

"—Kill everyone who resists."

That was the correct order to give.

"—————"

The drums rang out, and at once, the battlefield visible from the hill began to change.
The clash of steel on steel that had filled the air receded, replaced by roaring voices, war cries, and the din of pursuers and pursued sweeping across the field.
But even that didn't last long.

—A rain of blazing fire came pouring down, turning the self-appointed hunters chasing fleeing backs to ash.

The ally in front of them, the comrade right beside them, one of their own limbs—swallowed by flame, and only as they were torn from their frenzy did they realize it.
That they were not the hunters here, but merely the hunted.

"—————"

Gripping in one hand a wooden branch a child might play with, both legs from the knee down turned to flame, Arakiya rose above the hill—higher, and higher still, into the towering, high sky—and rained fire down from above.
A terrifying strategic weapon that seemed to repaint the very color of the world—that description suited the scene perfectly.
The flames plunged straight down to the earth, burning the enemy according to the conditions Todd had set. Those who stopped in their tracks and let their guard slip while watching the enemy burn had made an error in judgment.
The moment you stopped moving, Arakiya's fire could no longer tell friend from foe.

"Beat it, beat it, beat it! Sound the drums! Everyone fall back—!!"

Watching the battle's tide be utterly overwritten, the "General" shouted with all his voice.
Imperial soldiers went into every battle resigned to the possibility of death, or so it was said—but that was a far cry from not fearing a meaningless one.
Staying on that field would earn them nothing more than the fate of a stray dog burned alive.
And so—

"—Lance Corporal Fang! Stop General Arakiya!"

As Todd, already feeling like his job was done, watched the rebel formation collapse and the battlefield be licked over by a demon of flame, a furious shout struck his eardrums.
Turning around, he saw an imperial soldier—not the "General" who'd been directing the retreat—his face blackened with soot, screaming with a look of desperation.
Wondering what on earth was wrong, Todd frowned, and the soldier, his face lit red by the flames, cried out.

"The enemy's calling for surrender! The fighting's over!"

"You sure we should let them off that easy? Not to put too fine a point on it, but unlike a situation where we'd take losses if we don't back off, right now we could wipe them out one-sided. Better to cut off future trouble and make an example of them while we can."

If Arakiya's strength were used properly, they could win without losing a single soldier of their own.
Letting the enemy surrender and get cocky about it, versus making an example of them so they'd regret ever joining the rebellion—the latter seemed more appropriate to Todd.
But the soldier's next words overturned that opinion entirely.

"No good! The ones who surrendered—they've got the 'Crown Prince' with them!"

"—What did you say?"

At the soldier's report, Todd found himself grabbing the man by the collar. The soldier choked with a strangled "guh," but Todd, unbothered, leaned in closer.

"Why does the 'Crown Prince' come into this now? Weren't we told he wasn't here?"

"Th-they were hiding him... He's coming out now, of all times..."

"Tch. Was he supposed to be some trump card? Idiots don't even understand the value of their own hand."

Letting go of the soldier's collar, Todd let out a long breath.
Then he looked up at Arakiya circling overhead and hesitated over whether to call to her. He mulled it over for a moment, and—

"Drag the envoy over here."

"Eh? But..."

"Bring him. Tell them to run for their lives if they don't want to be wiped out."

At Todd's flat instruction, the soldier started to object, then immediately realized the futility of it and turned away.
Todd himself had no wish to wield the kind of authority Arakiya's power lent him, but as long as it helped things move quickly, he had no choice but to keep doing it.

—Especially in the worst-case scenario where the enemy had the "Crown Prince" in their hands.

"Lance Corporal Fang."

Before long, the same soldier returned to Todd.
Beside him stood a young man in tattered clothing, and despite the martial bearing of his armor, his expression of resignation and defeat looked pitifully sunken.
No wonder. Todd himself would feel the same if he'd seen Arakiya's violence unleashed from the receiving end.
Though he felt some pity for the man, there was something he needed to confirm first.

"You lot are supposedly holding the 'Crown Prince.' Is that the just cause behind this rebellion?"

"Th-that's right. The 'Crown Prince' desires the throne. We sympathize with his cause and have taken up arms to clear the path for His Lordship..."

"—That's a lie."

"Huh?"

Staring intently into the face of the man spouting such weighty words, Todd cut in with a single line. At that, the man's expression flickered with confusion, and then something far beyond it—terror.
That reaction was enough to convince Todd. This accusation wasn't baseless at all.

"Gah—"

In the next instant, Todd swung the axe at his hip and split open the head of the man standing frozen before him.
The man, axe buried in the crown of his skull, gave a small groan, his eyes rolling back, and collapsed on the spot. At Todd's act of instantly cutting down the envoy, the soldier's eyes widened slightly.

"Lance Corporal! What do you think you're—"

"This one lied. There's no 'Crown Prince' with them. They're the kind who'd choose the worst possible strategy even while surrendering. —Let General Arakiya keep going."

"Gh... What if—what if there really had been a 'Crown Prince'?"

"There wasn't."

Faced with Todd's flat declaration, the soldier could find no further words and fell silent. Todd jerked his chin, and the enemy's corpse, fallen at his feet, was dragged off and carried away.
Todd scraped the flesh and blood from his axe with a finger and let out a long breath.

Traveling from region to region with Arakiya, crushing the seeds of rebellion wherever they sprouted—and every single time, the same obstacle stood in the way: the "Crown Prince."
The illegitimate child of Vincent Vollachia, the sole possible source of legitimacy for the rebellions breaking out across the empire—and how many, how very many, claimed to be that illegitimate child and plotted rebellion in his name.
What made it even worse was Chancellor Berstetz's standing order: capture the "Crown Prince" alive whenever possible and bring him back to the imperial capital.

"Ah, honestly, this is hopeless. —When am I ever getting back to the capital."

Every scheme to return to the capital had come up empty, and somehow this had turned into a tour of the entire empire instead.
Even now, thinking of his fiancée waiting for him back in the capital, Todd bit his lip.

The flames licked away everything on the battlefield, burning those who chose to resist down to nothing but regret.
He pitied, and at the same time despised, those who couldn't see that future coming, who had chosen the wrong side to stand on.

The rebellion would collapse. No matter how loudly they raised their voices, there was no way they could ever overcome the sheer weight of the empire.
He had not a shred of doubt about that—and yet, even thinking so, Todd found himself struck by a sudden thought.

"—————"

The full-blown beginning of civil war, and the flames of rebellion spreading through every region.
As though someone had planned it all, there was someone standing beyond the flames of this spreading chaos.
Someone who, endlessly, endlessly, kept Todd's survival instinct aching.

"—————"

Taking in both the spreading turmoil and the battlefield actually ablaze before his eyes, Todd stood still.
Who, in the end, wanted this war that was swallowing the Vollachian Empire whole? Whatever the answer, he would read it through to the end, and no matter what, Todd would get his hands on what he wanted.
He would spare no means, no amount of thought, toward that end. If there was one thing weighing on his mind, it was this.

*'—. Is it connected? To the Sword Slave Isle.'*

Arakiya's question, asked just before they'd arrived at the battlefield, flickered through his mind.
As a rule, Todd didn't look back on things once they were past. Whatever he'd done, he simply corrected it later if it turned out wrong. And yet that one incident kept snagging at him, over and over.

Without landing, Todd had abandoned the sword slave isle and withdrawn.
That judgment should have been the right one. There was no reason to regret it.

But if, by some chance, for some reason, he ever did come to regret it, it would only be—

"—the day that the retreat that day turned out to be the move that cornered my whole life."
