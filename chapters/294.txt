Arc 4 Chapter 124: "Just Listen, You Idiot"

——How many times, now, has he walked into this room to meet her?

The first time Subaru met Beatrice, he'd effortlessly exposed the illusion trick she used to make him wander the mansion, and stumbled his way into this forbidden archive.
Their first impression of each other, he thinks, had been terrible on both sides.

His mana drained from a body still recovering from illness, Subaru had collapsed pathetically. Afterward, burning for revenge, he'd pestered Beatrice again and again.
Every meeting was an exchange of insults, and yet somehow they'd gotten along all too well — and Subaru, without ever meaning to, kept stumbling straight into the archive she was trying to hide in.

Over the roughly two months Subaru spent at Roswaal Manor, he and Beatrice had gone at each other, spitting and shouting, in one childish exchange after another, over and over again.

Once the Royal Selection began in earnest, in the days after returning from the royal capital, even that back-and-forth started to change.
Beatrice, rejecting Subaru. Why she clung so stubbornly to her ways — Subaru came to understand a piece of that, learning the girl's past and fate in a place called the Sanctuary, where she herself was absent.

And then, speaking as if he understood, trying to grasp the girl's loneliness — he'd been crushed by Beatrice's grief, tears that had long since run dry over four hundred years.
After that, there had been nothing he could say to console her exhaustion. In the very next moment, Beatrice's life was lost — the last expression he saw was of the girl vanishing as she shielded him.

That face was burned into him, and it was a fury hot enough to scorch his own body that had driven Subaru back here.
——This time, no matter what it cost, to get the girl out of here.

"Take Betty out of here...?"

The moment he stepped into the room, Subaru had declared it outright, and Beatrice's answer came back bewildered. She hugged the Gospel tight to her chest and drew her knees up on the stepladder.

"Uncalled for, I suppose. No one asked you to do any such thing."

"This ain't about being asked or not asked. I'm taking you out of here. That's already decided."

"Then hurry up and leave, and go be comforted on that little girl's lap, why don't you."

"You— that's war, you know that's war...! Bring that up and it's war...!"

Reminded of the time he'd been pushed to his limit at the mansion before, Subaru forced out his voice to cover his inward humiliation.
At Subaru's reaction, Beatrice snorted and looked pointedly away.

"—Anyway, I don't have time to mess around with you. We're short on time, more than you'd think. You have any idea what's happening outside right now?"

"...ベティー knows some uninvited guest has come into the mansion. After the big maid and the little maid did something or other, two absurd people have been tearing the place apart, I suppose."

"One half of the 'absurd' is the backup I brought along. I don't think he's losing on raw fighting power, but unfortunately, I think one step — the gap in resolve — is probably gonna decide the winner. So I can't exactly sit back and take my time."

"So the plan is, while the backup buys time, the rest of the mansion gets away... An arrangement that isn't quite clear on whether you trust your ally or not, I suppose."

"It's a plan that only works because I know all too well that he's too kind a guy."

Garfiel's current condition, thanks to the recovery effect of the Divine Protection of the Earth Spirits during the time it took to get back to the mansion, was roughly eighty to ninety percent of his full strength. Add to that his lack of hesitation about fighting, and his combat rating was fairly high. But Subaru doubted he'd managed to steel himself to actually kill his opponent in time, and that shortfall in resolve was probably going to cost him — a slight mark against him.

Elsa, on the other hand, was naturally at full strength. Her inexplicable, abnormal combat power was, from Subaru's perspective, a fair match for a fully-fit Garfiel. Her tendency to enjoy the fight a little too much was a slight mark against her — but she had that incomprehensible immortality of hers. Whether killing her a few times over would actually finish her off seemed unlikely, judging by her own words. Provisional assessment: Elsa, slightly ahead.

"Anyway, if our plan goes the way it's supposed to, while Garfiel's pinning down Elsa, Frederica should be recovering Rem. Petra already linked up with Otto, so out of everyone who needs saving, there should only be one person left."

"Someone who needs saving... I see. And that last one is Betty, I take it."

"That's right."

Petra, who'd rejoined Subaru on the first floor of the mansion, had been sent to meet up with Otto, who'd guided the villagers of Arlam Village to safety, helped with the mansion's small preparations, and then been told to get out.
By now, with Subaru having finally reached the archive, the two of them should already be clear of the mansion.

"So, you're gonna let me take you out of here. If you don't wanna hold hands and run, I'll carry you piggyback or in my arms or however you want, so just come along quietly and..."

"Must I say it again and again? I don't need your help, I suppose."

Beatrice's low voice rejected Subaru as he took a step closer, reaching out a hand. As he stopped in his tracks, Beatrice turned her head as if to indicate the archive around them.

"Understand something, would you? This is a space cut off from the world, reached through a corridor of time, within Betty's reach. Beatrice's forbidden archive. Whatever threat is out there, it can't possibly reach Betty's archive, I suppose. Your worry is nothing but needless anxiety, I suppose."

"That ain't how it's gonna go. Your archive's randomness is a hell of an advantage when it comes to giving people the slip, sure... but it's got a fatal flaw. And on top of that, the person out there knows about that fatal flaw."

"A fatal... flaw...?"

Beatrice knit her brow — this, at least, she clearly couldn't let pass. But Subaru nodded at the girl's sharp gaze and pointed at the door behind him.

"Your power that randomly connects to doors around the mansion is strong, sure. But — your power only works on 'closed doors' in the mansion. Which means, if someone just leaves every single door in the mansion open, sooner or later they're bound to reach this archive, and it'll stop connecting anywhere else."

"—————!"

"It's a stupid method, so even you didn't notice it, I bet. Even I couldn't believe I hadn't figured out something that simple until I actually saw it happen."

He remembered the moment Elsa had discovered the loophole in the 'door crossing' and exposed the archive.
With the obstacle named Garfiel out of the way, Elsa would undoubtedly come here using that exact same method. And then she would take Beatrice's life.

"Obviously, I'm not saying you'd go down easy the second that thing got in here. But that thing's got a level of 'who the hell knows what she is' that's off the charts even by my life experience. If we don't have to fight her, better not to."

Defeating Elsa was, ideally, a condition Subaru wanted to achieve, but it wasn't a strictly necessary one to break out of this loop. If the client was Roswaal, then at the very least, once the time limit on the events tied to the Sanctuary passed, Roswaal would have no reason left to keep employing Elsa.
In that case, Elsa withdrawing was something already proven by the whole incident with the emblem in the royal capital.
For now, if he could just weather this assault on the mansion—

"Beatrice. This place isn't safe either. If it weren't for you, that thing probably wouldn't even bother trashing the archive. So right now..."

"How does that woman know how to break Betty's 'door crossing,' I wonder?"

"————"

Having laid out his reasonable persuasive material to get her moving, Subaru urged Beatrice toward escape.
But Beatrice — the one who mattered — whether or not she'd even been listening to everything he'd said, only murmured something completely different from what he'd wanted to hear.
Before Subaru, tongue-tied, Beatrice remained perched on the stepladder and went on.

"There's no way someone could encounter Betty's 'door crossing' for the first time and instantly hit upon how to break it, I suppose. Whoever handed her that scheme must be someone who knows about Betty."

"Beatrice. Now isn't the time for that kind of talk..."

"—It's Roswaal, I suppose."

There was no fooling her.
At the speed with which she'd leapt to that conclusion, Subaru couldn't help but catch his breath.
Seeing Subaru's reaction, Beatrice understood everything. That it was Roswaal who'd hired Elsa, and that his purpose was to have Beatrice killed. Which meant—

"Roswaal's Gospel had it written that Betty should be killed, is that it, I wonder?"

Hah, Beatrice let out a small breath, without waiting for Subaru to confirm or deny it.
Surely it wasn't his imagination that there was something like relief in that breath. Unable to let it pass, Subaru pressed in close to her.

"What the hell was that sigh just now? Why do you look like you just got something figured out!?"

"As you can see, I have, I suppose. If it was written in Roswaal's Gospel that ordered Roswaal to do it, then Betty's fate was decided by that as much as anything, I suppose."

"What the hell is that... Roswaal's book is Roswaal's book, and your book is your book! Does yours say something about letting Roswaal kill you!?"

Pointing a finger, Subaru glared at the Gospel Beatrice held.
If it was the same as in the previous loop, then inside, for four hundred years, nothing but blank pages should have continued, unbroken.
At Subaru's outburst, Beatrice lifted a gloomy expression and opened the pages of the Gospel. Then she spread it open toward him so he could see the contents — showing him a book that held nothing but blank page after blank page.

"Nothing at all is written, I suppose. As always, it stays blank."

"—! Then there's no reason at all for you to let yourself be killed the way Roswaal's book says! Same as always — you decide what you do!"

"...The same as always, Betty decides?"

"That's right! If nothing's written in it, that means you've had the choice all along, this whole time! From the small stuff to the big stuff, you've decided your own path yourself, every time! So this time too, there's no reason for you to be danced around on someone else's choice—"

"In all of Betty's days up until now, what has there ever been that Betty decided?"

The force behind Subaru's words was crushed flat by the sheer, aching sincerity of that question.
Beatrice tilted her head slightly, gazing at Subaru with nothing but forlorn eyes. Turning blank page after blank page, she traced through the "empty time" where nothing had been drawn, as if following it.

"At Roswaal's mansion, keeping watch over the archive entrusted to me by Mother, on and on, piling up time alone, alone, alone... where in all that time was there ever a moment that belonged to Betty? What has Betty, who's lived on through blank time where nothing is ever recorded, left anywhere in this world? Beatrice — just what is that, what has she ever done, whose story is that, even?"

"Bea... trice..."

"Betty's life, Betty's four hundred years, are just like this Gospel — pure white. Blank. Nothing chosen by myself, nothing gained by myself, nothing that could prove myself even exists... none of it exists."

Closing the Gospel with a snap, Beatrice set it gently on her own lap. Stroking its nameless cover, the girl spoke in a quiet voice.

"A blank book, that's all it is. Even if Betty were lost here, all that would be lost is one more blank book with nothing drawn in it, I suppose. Nothing to anyone, not meant to be anything, just a book stuck on a shelf — better off gone, I suppose. That would be a relief."

"So what happens to the person who'd be in trouble without that blank book?"

With a fragile expression, Beatrice looked ready to simply write off her own four hundred years and her future without a second thought. Trying somehow to hold her heart in place, Subaru forced the words out.
Even now, inside himself, Subaru still had no answer to that weeping, screaming question Beatrice had asked back then.

And yet, if he didn't keep talking here, she would give up on herself.

"You said it was nothing, a blank space. But that book's still there, stuck on the shelf. There's someone who knows it's there. Maybe there's someone out there who's always thought, someday I'll pick it up — you think I'm gonna let it get thrown out on its own say-so?"

"A book with no title, no author's name, I suppose. Even supposing some peculiar someone like that existed, they'd only open it, see the contents, and be disappointed, I suppose. Even that blank book would rather not have to watch someone's face fall in disappointment after they picked it up, I suppose."

"Then! Then why was that book stuck there on that shelf in the first place!?"

"————"

Beatrice stared at Subaru, pressing his point, with emotionless eyes.
It felt as though he'd been confronted, before he could even get the words out, with the notion that there was no meaning to find in this exchange. Even so, Subaru raised his face and kept reaching toward Beatrice's distant heart.

"If no matter who picks it up, they're just gonna be disappointed by the contents... then what was that book there *for*? Books get made because they mean something, don't they?"

"...Whoever wrote the book made it for someone, I suppose. A book that looks blank to anyone's eyes, but is made to look different — only to that 'someone.' So if it means anything at all, the moment it reaches that 'someone' is exactly the meaning the book was born for, I suppose."

"Then—"

"Until it reaches that 'someone's' hands, it shouldn't be thrown away — is that what you're saying, I wonder?"

He caught his breath.
Subaru realized, an instant too late, just how cruel a hope he'd been about to voice. Beatrice looked at his face and wore a painfully bitter smile.

"That's right. If Betty really were nothing but a single book... then it would have been fine to just keep waiting for that day, I suppose."

She could have gone on waiting for the day some 'someone,' eventually, would turn her pages.
If Beatrice had only been a book.

——But Beatrice is not a book. Through the long, blank stretch of time, she is a girl trembling in loneliness.

"If I had no heart, no self, if I were nothing but a single book... I could have gone on believing Mother's instructions without ever doubting, without end. I could have gone on being Mother's dear little Beatrice forever, I suppose."

If she could have been a heartless thing, like a doll, only ever decorated, she wouldn't have wavered.
If she could have been like a single book, unshaken by the passage of endlessly flowing time, she wouldn't have grieved.

But Beatrice couldn't be that.

"But Betty has a heart. Enough that, given time, all sorts of thoughts creep in, until I can't even believe the things I want to believe anymore, I suppose. I fall into doubt. There were nights, so many nights, when I couldn't remember Mother's face, her smile, and tried to scrape together fragments of memory to cling to!"

"————"

"There were times I couldn't stand being alone anymore, times I wanted so badly to touch someone! But no matter what, everyone leaves Betty behind! Saying something incomprehensible about something more precious than me, making some excuse, and leaving Betty behind! Mother did! Roswaal did! ——Even Ryuzu did!!"

Face crumpling, on the verge of tears, Beatrice cried out.
At the name Ryuzu, spilling from her mouth, Subaru remembered the past he'd heard about Beatrice in the Sanctuary. Ryuzu Meyer, the origin of the Ryuzus who exist now.
The story of the brief but genuine bond Beatrice had forged with the girl who became a sacrifice to protect the Sanctuary — the scar that still remained, even now, in Beatrice's heart.

"—So, it's fine now, I suppose."

With that, Beatrice suddenly lost all her momentum, her voice dropping sharply in tone.
The expression twisted with violent emotion returned to its usual flatness, and she drew the book on her lap in close.

"Betty's Gospel does not record Betty's future. ...It's something I already knew, I suppose. Betty's fate was abandoned by Mother a very, very long time ago."

For the future to go unrecorded meant the Gospel's owner's future had reached a dead end.
That was how Beatrice had once described, to Subaru who carried Petelgeuse's Gospel, a book whose entries had stopped. And the same thing, she said, was happening to herself.

"If Roswaal's Gospel has recorded Betty's fate... how ironic, I suppose. But it's also a relief, I suppose. If it's Roswaal, he'd never go easy on me, I suppose."

"You might get killed by someone you've known that long... and you're calling that a relief?"

"Obviously, I suppose."

At Subaru's strained voice, Beatrice nodded once.
Then, at the corner of her mouth, a fragile smile bloomed — one with something almost fond in it.

"Whether it's in Roswaal's or not, if the Gospel has Betty's name in it at all... then it means Mother never forgot Betty. Not once, I suppose."

——It's twisted.

Watching Beatrice's smiling face, Subaru realized he was on the verge of being swept up in a torrent of emotion.
It's twisted. Beatrice, looking as if she were glad to have touched her mother's love, in a moment like this — it was too twisted, too unbearable. As if something like this, something like *this*, could ever be called a mother's love.

"...What do you intend to do, I wonder?"

Biting his lip, holding back what was welling up inside him, Subaru had already taken a step forward.
Sensing the extraordinary intensity radiating off him, Beatrice's expression sharpened with wariness.

"————"

"Betty asked you something. What do you intend to do? I'll warn you — if you intend to do anything, I won't hold back, I suppose. Betty has already accepted her fate."

"The hell is 'accepted your fate.' You're no different from Roswaal. No — you're worse than him, and at least he's self-aware about it. You're hopeless. Completely twisted up inside."

Anger welled up.
Ever since he'd become entangled with the string of events tied to the Sanctuary, Subaru had fought this same feeling over and over, again and again.

Anger at himself for facing the Trials of the Tomb; anger at the witches toying with him; anger at Garfiel, underestimating him with a child's stubbornness; anger at Roswaal, trying to affirm the fragility of his own feelings by dutifully obeying what was written; anger at Emilia, unable to believe in herself or in Subaru's feelings for her—

——And now, once again, anger at Beatrice, and at everyone around her who'd driven her into a corner like this.

"You're an idiot. Fate this, Mother's instructions that — it's painful to watch, honestly. You have a heart? You can't just be a single book? Of course you can't, you dumbass. Holed up in this musty room this whole time, has that made you forget even something that obvious!?"

"Y-you...!"

Eyes wide at Subaru's shout, Beatrice's face went from stunned to furious.
The girl rose to her feet atop the stepladder, the hem of her skirt swaying, and pointed at Subaru.

"You! Who exactly do you think you're talking to, saying that!? Idiot, idiot!? How dare you say that... to me! What could you possibly understand about Betty!?"

"I understand you a hell of a lot better than you do, since at least I know you're an idiot and you don't even realize it yourself! Idiot! Idiot! Idiot! Idiooot!!"

"Y-you, you, you...!!"

At Subaru's raised middle finger and the stream of insults he hurled, Beatrice's face went bright red and the words caught in her throat. She was too furious to find a comeback in the moment.
And once a gap like that opened up, barging right through it uninvited was Subaru's specialty.

"Four hundred years of blank space? Don't act so high and mighty! For four hundred years you just sat here sniveling with your knees pulled to your chest! With all that time to think, why the hell have you been gnawing on the same single answer this whole time!? The book had nothing written in it, so what, you're gonna say 'I did nothing, and I did it very thoroughly'? Idiot!"

"I-it's not as if I never thought about it, I suppose! Obviously I did! How many times, how much, has Betty tested whether the Gospel's writing would ever change...! But no matter what I did, no matter how long I waited, nothing ever changed! So!"

"That's what makes you an idiot! The book had nothing written in it, so you tried real hard to make the writing show up on the page — what, like some New Year's card trick with invisible ink held over a flame? Nobody does that anymore! If you tried that and it didn't work, doubt something else already!"

"S-something else...?"

"Right on the nose. Like the possibility your mom handed you the wrong book."

At Subaru's words, Beatrice was struck speechless.
But almost immediately, she snapped back that it was far too ridiculous an answer.

"That's quite enough, I suppose! There's no way Mother would do something so foolish, I suppose! You... you couldn't possibly understand Mother's profound thinking, I suppose!"

"Yeah, I don't know, whatever, idiot. I don't give a damn what your mom was thinking. Right now I'm talking about you. You just said it, didn't you. 'There's no way she'd do something so foolish' — you said that. Is that really true? Can you really say that for certain? Have you never once doubted your mother, not even once?"

"W-what are you...!"

"Four hundred years! The book that was supposed to show letters stayed blank the whole damn time! The person you were told to wait for never showed up, no matter how long you waited! You spent that whole time alone, with more time to think than you'd know what to do with, and you never once thought — not once — that something might be wrong here!?"

To go on believing in someone, single-mindedly, for four hundred years.
At a glance, it might seem like a beautiful state of heart. But something like that is truly twisted. All the more so if all that time was spent thinking only of that person, only of their words.

All the more so for a Beatrice who had begun to give up, believing her wish would never be granted.

"M-mother would never do anything wrong, I suppose! O-obviously not! It's Mother we're talking about! Can you say the same — that you could doubt what your own mother says!?"

"Damn right I can! You have any idea how flimsy my mom's credibility is!? When she heard a satellite fell 'into the atmosphere' and misheard it as falling 'into Aichi Prefecture,' I stopped believing whatever big news comes flying out of her mouth without checking it myself! And that was in third grade!!"

He'd never forget the day he took it at face value and spread it around, only to be laughed at by the whole school.
Ever since that day, Subaru had stopped believing his parents' claims outright. As for his father's credibility, he'd lost faith in that far earlier still.

"Four hundred years and you never once doubted her!? I haven't even lived twenty years, and the number of times I've come to blows with my old man is more than I can count on both hands, back and forth. And that's twenty years. In your case, twenty times that long — you never once felt anything like that?"

"You... what is it you want Betty to say!? I don't understand at all, I suppose! Your goal, the meaning behind what you're saying, Betty doesn't understand any of it! I don't understand!"

"Then I'll say it plain! Loud enough for you, idiot that you are, and your idiot mother to hear!"

Approaching Beatrice as she tried to clutch her head, Subaru took hold of both her hands.
Bringing his face close enough to feel her breath as she looked up at him, Subaru declared it flatly to the tearful girl.

"Stop letting yourself get jerked around forever by a blank book and a verbal promise from four hundred years ago. ——What you want to do, you get to choose, Beatrice."

"————"

"Four hundred years. That's more than enough time for at least one round of rebellious phase to roll around, don't you think?"

Beatrice, trying so earnestly, faithfully, to keep obeying her mother's instructions.
That stubborn insistence on keeping the promise was exactly what had produced the girl's loneliness, her hollow, empty stretch of time.

To her mother Echidna, even that time of anguish had apparently been something sweet. But from Subaru's point of view, it was an outrageous vice.
Forgetting even how to want to cry, forgetting how to cry at all — and calling that a "state of heart"? It made him sick.

Still gripped by both arms, Beatrice turned her face away from Subaru atop the stepladder.
Seated on the top step, the girl's eyes were almost level with Subaru's own. Having turned her face away, she eventually looked down, and then her lips began to tremble.

"I-in other words... is this what you're saying? That Betty should break Mother's instructions."

"............"

"That I should throw away the thing I've believed in without fail for four hundred years, and become free... is that something you can just say to Betty, so easily, I wonder?"

The trembling voice gradually steadied itself.
And in the voice that began to carry something other than agitation, Subaru felt the hairs on his arms stand on end. Since coming to this other world, this particular sense — of a presence bringing serious danger — had been honed without any need for doubt.

"—To this Beatrice! Telling me to break the Contract! Do you think you can just say something so glib!"

"—Wuh!?"

As if hit head-on by a blast wave, Subaru's body was flung backward.
Rolling across the archive floor on his back, caught in the wind, he was slammed into the wall and the breath was knocked out of him. Every bone in his body groaned, his vision flickering, as Subaru forced his head up.

Still atop the stepladder, but now with a face contorted in rage, Beatrice glared down at him.

"A Contract is absolute! Absolute, I suppose! Especially one exchanged between a witch and a spirit. And you'd have me break it unilaterally, and from the spirit's side no less!? You understand nothing at all, I suppose! Such a thing is not permitted! No matter who! No matter what kind of being! Not even Betty herself would ever forgive it, I suppose!"

"—That's rich, coming from someone who was looking for a back door out of that contract, and figured she'd just get herself killed if she couldn't find one."

"————!"

Breathing out as if to push the pain out of his body, Subaru slowly pulled himself up.
Beatrice, still bristling with anger at his words, had painted her lovely face with all the menace she could muster. Looking up at that face, Subaru gave a wicked grin of his own.

"What you're saying doesn't make a damn bit of sense, Beatrice. You haven't noticed how contradictory you're being? No way you haven't — you're a smart girl, after all."

"Silence, I suppose."

"Nope, not gonna shut up. Break the contract? Fine by me. Literally — if keeping your promise is something you'd rather die than keep doing, then quit. Nobody's gonna blame you for it."

"Betty will blame herself! Why don't you understand that!? A Contract is absolute, and keeping it..."

"You're the one who doesn't get it. If keeping the contract means you die, and breaking it means you live — living's better. Is it really that strange that I'd choose that?"

Beatrice, clinging so fiercely to the Contract, was left speechless by Subaru's readiness to simply tear it up. Right now, she probably saw him as some incomprehensible monster.
Being seen that way was, to Subaru, far stranger.

Keeping a promise is important, of course.
He'd been called out by Emilia more than once for breaking promises, and it had hurt, more than once, because of it. So Subaru understood perfectly well how important keeping a promise is.

And yet, here, he felt no hesitation at all about making Beatrice break hers.
The reason for that was exactly what he'd just told her.

If someone said Beatrice had to die for the sake of keeping a promise, he'd flip that person off and tell them so.
Make her break the promise, and let Beatrice live. That wasn't even something worth agonizing over.

"S-so, digging in your heels — how utterly, viciously stubborn, I suppose..."

"I know I'm digging in my heels, and yeah, I feel bad about it. But this matters too much to back down."

Subaru's stance had been set from the very start. From the beginning, all that was left was whatever was in Beatrice's heart.
At Subaru's attitude of treating the Contract with such disregard, Beatrice couldn't hide her confusion and bewilderment. And of course she couldn't. In this world, to a being like a spirit, a Contract carried that much weight.
Watching the relationships between spirits and their spirit users over all this time, Subaru understood that it was something rigid, weighty, not meant to be shaken.

Knowing all that, still, Subaru would say it.
That she mattered more than something like that.

"I-if you... were 'that person'..."

Subaru's attitude toward the Contract — far, far too reckless.
Hearing it, Beatrice's expression faintly cracked, tinged with weakness.
What spilled from her lips was that shapeless someone she'd believed in and gone on waiting for, through all these four hundred years, trusting her mother's word.

The imaginary being Echidna had, without a shred of mercy, conjured up so she could learn "who that child would choose."

Beatrice wanted to be saved.
Her eyes glistening, shaken by Subaru's words — that alone was proof enough.

"You..."

Beatrice's glistening eyes focused on Subaru, standing frozen before her.
And the girl, lips trembling, spoke as if clinging to him.

"Will you... become Betty's 'that person'?"

It was a question that might have put an end to those four hundred years.
Or perhaps, exactly as Echidna had intended, it was the very words the witch had wished for.

Whether Beatrice could ever find that shapeless being called "that person."
The witch had entrusted her own curiosity's satisfaction to her daughter, and made her spend four hundred years alone because of it.

The fruit of all those days lay in this question, right now.
Looking straight at Beatrice, who held her breath, Subaru said it, clearly.

"You idiot. — There's no way I'm gonna be some nonsense guy called your 'that person.'"

In the archive, after the fierce shockwave had blown through and settled, Beatrice was returning the books that had been flung about by the wind to their proper places on the shelves.

Fortunately, though some had fallen to the floor, she found none with their bindings torn loose.
Reflecting on having used her power inside the very archive she'd been told to protect, Beatrice was relieved that the damage had turned out to be so minor.

These were comrades-in-arms who had spent all four hundred years of Beatrice's solitude alongside her.
Her earlier remark — *if only I could have been a single book* — had been no lie. Just like these books, she'd fantasized more than once about being a being utterly unmoved by being made to wait through long stretches of time.
A foolish thought to have mistaken for hope, she thought now.

"No wonder I'd be laughed at, I suppose."

That was how pathetically cornered she had been.
She mocked herself over it. But more than that, there was fury still burning in her small chest.

"That man... just what, exactly, is he...!"

Recalling the man who irritated her just to think about, Beatrice nearly stamped her foot on the ground.
She wanted to hurl her tangled feelings at something, somewhere, but every corner of the archive was a precious place her mother had told her to protect.
There was nothing here fit to take a tantrum out on, so all she could do was wait for the swelling feeling to subside on its own.

Returning the last book to its shelf, Beatrice let out a breath and straightened herself up. Then she settled into her usual spot on the stepladder and reached to gather the black-bound book into her arms — and stopped.

The blank book. He'd said, so many times, so easily, to just throw it away.
And yet, at the crucial moment, when Beatrice had tried to choose the very trigger for her own discarding, he'd rejected it. Honestly, truly — it made no sense at all, and it was infuriating.

"...I'm so tired, I suppose."

But even that fury couldn't sustain itself for long.
Beatrice stopped puffing out her cheeks and drew the book she'd hesitated to hold close against her chest.

In the end, right up to the very last moment, there was nothing left to protect her heart but leaning on this.

Just as Roswaal's Gospel had written, an ending would come, in time, for Beatrice too.
With what feeling was she supposed to wait for it?

Should she think of it as, at last, an ending?
Even though that was surely what she'd thought all along, now that it was actually drawing near, she found herself unsettled.

——*You're an idiot,* he'd said. And somehow, that had lodged itself in her chest like a knot that wouldn't go away.

Blown off his feet by the shockwave, sent rolling down the corridor, slammed back-first into the wall, Subaru writhed in agony. A pillar had caught him square in the side, and he groaned, thrashing.

"Guah! Uwogh... th-there's no way... right in the middle of the conversation, that idiot...!"

Before his eyes, the door slammed shut with a bang, and Subaru reached toward it with a resentful expression. Of course, through the gap he'd managed to leave open, the scene on the other side had already changed from the archive — just an ordinary guest room now.
The 'door crossing' had activated, and Subaru had been thrown out of the archive.

"Seriously, mad enough to throw me out entirely...? Damn it, wrong choice of words...!"

What he'd wanted to say wasn't wrong, but there'd been a mismatch between how he meant it and how it landed.
As a result, Subaru had been ejected from the archive, and his goal had slipped further away.

"Anyway, can't just sit here. Gotta get to another door and back to Bea-ko's place...!"

"N-Natsuki-san...?"

A voice called out to Subaru as he turned, ready to try every other door at hand. Startled by the familiar voice and the way it addressed him, Subaru's eyes went wide.
There, peeking cautiously out from the next room over, was Otto — who was supposed to be gone by now. And below Otto, in the same crouched posture, was Petra's face, peering out as well.

"Y-you two...? Why are you still in the mansion? I told you, once the doors are done, just take one building's worth and run, didn't I?"

"Unfortunately, the situation outside has changed drastically, and..."

At Subaru pressing in close, Otto shook his head, face pale.
There was no way he was joking, not in a situation like this. If Otto had given up on fleeing, then something had happened significant enough to warrant it.

"What happened? Give it to me short."

"Witchbeasts. For some reason, the mansion's surrounded outside by a swarm of Witchbeasts, and we can't move."

"Witchbeasts!?"

At the unexpected word, Subaru's eyes bulged, and he looked to Petra to confirm. The girl nodded her head repeatedly at his gaze.

"There are lots of Witchbeasts, not the same as those magic hounds... two-headed snakes, pouch-rats, all sorts."

"Are those the ones that live in the forest around here?"

"Yeah, but... but they shouldn't be able to get past the barrier to here."

"The barrier, again..."

During the previous Witchbeast disturbance, the re-binding of the barrier between Arlam Village and the forest around the mansion had been confirmed. Attention to any slackening of the barrier afterward had also been treated as top priority — there was no way a mistake had been made in such a short span.
More than that, why would Witchbeasts that had breached the barrier end up surrounding the mansion?

"Same as with that pack of mutts before — is there some strange will at work again...? What about the people of Arlam Village? Are they okay?"

"No Witchbeasts were confirmed at the point we guided them out, and since we mobilized every Dragon Carriage entrusted to us by the Duke to get them away, they should be safe. Patrasche-chan is guiding them, too."

"Good. Then that's a relief."

Better to leave it to that clever Earth Dragon than to have some inexperienced guide leading the way — far more reliable.
Praying that Patrasche would manage well, Subaru ground his teeth at yet another situation unfolding differently from the flow he knew.

An attack by Witchbeasts that had never happened before.
Naturally, there was no way it wasn't connected to the timing of Elsa's assault.

"What about Frederica and Rem?"

"We haven't been able to reach Frederica-neesama or Rem-san... a-and I don't think we can cut through where those things are to escape."

"So the two of them are still inside the mansion, then. The one saving grace is that the Witchbeasts haven't gotten inside yet — question is how far Garfiel can hold out."

Patting Petra's head, Subaru praised the strength of heart she'd shown, not falling apart even under such extreme conditions. A Subaru her age would hardly be strange for wetting himself and bawling.
But the fact remained that the situation wouldn't allow them to stay put like this.

"Where are we right now? Which wing of the mansion?"

"The East Wing. Garfiel and the others should be fighting it out in the West Wing, so we avoided that side for now, just so the damage wouldn't spread here, but..."

"Then the escape route we can use is..."

Recovering Beatrice was, of course, essential — but getting Otto and the others out was just as much a non-negotiable condition.
Subaru fell into thought, trying to trace out an escape route from the floor plan laid out in his head. But a voice tore through his concentration.

"—Oh my? All gathered together in a place like this, waiting just for me?"

A chill, like a blade drawn across the nape of the neck, froze every one of their bodies at once.
Instinctively pulling Petra into his arms, Subaru turned around, fearfully.

Down the far end of the corridor, along a passage where light slanted in at an angle, someone was approaching, footsteps ringing out.
Before long, that figure stepped into the band of light, and—

"What the hell is that idiot Garfiel doing!?"

"All three of you together — I'll get to see such lovely intestines—"

Screaming, Subaru watched as the Bowel Hunter kicked off the floor right before his eyes, a black shadow leaping straight at them.
