Arc 8 Chapter 67: "The Cards in Hand"

—Having taken the 'Stone Mass' Muspel into herself, Arakiya had placed her own life and death upon the same scale as the fate of the Vollachia Empire.

Just as Priscilla had said, the combination of the Soul Marriage Technique and the 'Dream Sword' Masayume had brought about a turn of events that threatened to throw Sphinx's plans badly out of joint.

Sphinx had become the bearer of the Great Disaster and buried the Vollachia Empire in the dead for the sake of two purposes—and of those, she had already succeeded at one: the recreation of the soul of the Witch of Greed. All that remained was for this soul and its vessel to finish becoming one, until the Creator was moved to paint over Sphinx's own existence entirely.

And before that happened—before Sphinx vanished from the world—there remained her other purpose. Let us call it plainly what it was: revenge. The completion of her revenge against Priscilla Barielle.

To that end, she believed she had played the best card available to her.

And yet—

"—So you stop the Magic Crystal Cannon."

Watching one miscalculation after another unfold before her, Sphinx murmured quietly.

The surface of the water mirror she had conjured reflected the many places throughout the imperial capital of Lupugana where forces capable of delaying the Vollachia Empire's destruction were in motion.

What drew the attention of Sphinx's black eyes—set now in a face the very image of the Witch of Greed's—above all else were the boy and the spirit who had stopped the shot fired from the Magic Crystal Cannon, the Crystal Palace's own trump card.

That spirit in the shape of a girl had blocked the Magic Crystal Cannon the same way once before, back when Sphinx, as the Great Disaster, had led the dead and launched her full assault on the Empire. But by Sphinx's reckoning, that act should have cost the spirit its own annihilation as the price of its sacrifice.

For the same feat to be pulled off again, unscathed, after only two or three days—that made no sense.

The cause that had bent that logic out of shape was surely the boy, bound in contract to the spirit now held in his embrace.

"You again."

He was the inexplicable boy who, time and again, had joined forces with the Vollachia Emperor to thwart the schemes of Sphinx, the incomplete "Witch," before she had recreated the Witch of Greed.

In truth, had she not seized the final fragment needed for the soul's recreation at the very last moment, more than three hundred years of Sphinx's tireless striving would have been brought to an end by them.

And that threat persisted even now, after she had at last become the Witch of Greed.

"—The Sunlight Sword, Vollachia, and the Authority of Gluttony."

Even having succeeded at the long-cherished ambition of recreating the Witch of Greed, Sphinx remained one of the dead, and for her those two things remained fatal weaknesses, unchanged.

For that reason, even as she spawned multiple instances of the same self, sharing the same soul, Sphinx took care to keep herself apart from the Sunlight Sword's bearer, and from that boy, the Spiritual Arts user.

And beyond that—

"I will win—with every card that I hold."

"——"

At Sphinx's murmur, the expression on Priscilla's face, seen through the water mirror, shifted.

She was smiling, ever so faintly. —And it was to twist that smile into something mixed with a fragment of sorrow, rather than mere displeasure or discomfort, that Sphinx would stake everything she had.

Far off in the sky, a hole had opened, swallowing the flame of Vollachia's ruin.

Cutting into the path of the fired Magic Crystal Cannon, Natsuki Subaru had achieved his purpose. It meant that the 'Extoller' Halibel, Spica, and Beatrice had each fulfilled their own roles as well.

"Well fought. —But."

Even as he acknowledged that accomplishment, Vincent, the Emperor, felt a flicker of concern at the edge of his mind.

The neutralizing of the Magic Crystal Cannon, the Empire's decisive weapon—that it should happen so repeatedly was itself a problem.

—A nation must always possess deterrence enough to make other nations hesitate before invading.

An army of soldiers unmatched by any neighboring power, a champion of strength beyond compare, a weapon mighty enough to overturn any crisis—lacking even one of these, the Empire's present glory could not stand.

And that solid weight of history was being stripped bare, without a trace, by the Great Disaster that had sprung from civil strife.

"In that sense, all of you have already slain the order of the Empire that the Iron-Blooded Code was meant to protect."

Biting down on a mortified thought with his back teeth, Vincent raised the Sunlight Sword high.

Before him as he did so, he saw undead of a different breed than any he had faced yet, leaping past the line of flame drawn by the sacred blade.

Just what was different about them? Simple. —Vincent did not recognize them.

"That vexatious 'Witch'—did she learn something, before her end?"

In the garden before the Crystal Palace, now a battlefield, ancient undead who had no doubt carved their names into the history of the Vollachia Empire appeared one after another before Vincent, who cut down the line to hold the front.

Vincent could match every face and name among the present-day imperial soldiers, but he had no way of identifying the undead raised up from a past he had no direct knowledge of.

From his sense of things thus far, the dead summoned by the Great Disaster were overwhelmingly weighted toward those close to the present day. To put it in extreme terms, one who died in the very midst of fighting the undead might be raised as an undead almost instantly. —It was only a guess, but perhaps even souls had a kind of freshness to them.

Or perhaps, more simply, the further back one reached into the past, the harder it was to revive the dead—or the greater the price it demanded.

For that reason, most of the undead raised were close to the present, and for that reason, Subaru and the others' Starving Eclipse, combined with Vincent's memories, had proven so effective—but—

"So these are ones on whom it won't work."

Whatever additional conditions, large or small, might be imposed on their revival, preparing undead who fell outside Vincent's memory was the surest way to seal off the Starving Eclipse.

It seemed the "Witch" had grasped that, before her soul was burned away by the flame.

Which meant that no means remained to defeat these unfamiliar warriors of old standing in his way, save for Vincent's Sunlight Sword. —No, that was not quite true. If it was simply the Sunlight Sword, there was another besides the one in Vincent's hand.

"—Priscilla."

Speaking the name of the sister whose whereabouts, since the retreat, remained unknown, Vincent resumed his engagement with the undead bearing down on him.

Prisca Benedict—now going by the name Priscilla Barielle—his unworthy sister.

When she had left the Empire, changed her name, and become one of the Royal Selection candidates of the Kingdom of Lugunica, Vincent, upon confirming the facts of the matter, had very nearly fainted. He had held himself together, though.

That she had rushed to her homeland's hour of crisis was not for any endearing reason like concern for the Empire or for Vincent. It was to silence the wielder of the blade sent as an assassin against her own life.

Though the reckless fool presumed responsible had already lost his life.

"Chisha, damn him."

Chisha, who had deceived even Vincent while resolving to defy the world's fate in the form of the Great Disaster, was one of the very few who knew that Prisca had survived and become Priscilla.

Chisha was exactly the sort to exploit the fierce temperament he'd known from her days as Prisca, and to take the drastic measure of summoning her here as one more hand to stand beside Vincent in driving back the Great Disaster.

There was no telling whether, behind that white Noh mask, he had even worked out how the throne should be handled once the war was over—

"That His Excellency should require so much of the stage set for him beforehand—I find myself obliged to reconsider my estimation of him."

Vincent clicked his tongue at that echo of Chisha's voice, a lump of disrespect and disloyalty toward his Emperor if ever there was one.

Setting aside his irritation at a fabrication of his own imagining that felt all too much like the real Chisha, Vincent reserved for himself the conclusion on Chisha's parting gift—the matter of what was to be done with Priscilla.

For now, that Priscilla's life was safe had been confirmed by the very fact that Sphinx had tried to bring Vincent to her. That would do, for the moment.

As for making up the shortfall in Sunlight Swords, that would be resolved simply by Vincent adapting further as the summit of this nation of sword-wolves. —It was in that very instant that it happened.

"—A magnificent struggle. I, too, take pride in it, as one who came before."

A low, resonant voice struck Vincent's eardrums amid the clash of blades.

At the same moment, his field of view was cleaved diagonally in two by a transcendent flash of swordsmanship—one that made even Vincent, who had seen the blade-work of countless warriors, call it exceptional.

That single stroke swept away the ancient warriors who had dared, however presumptuously, to wound Vincent's own sacred person, and set ablaze the bodies—no, the souls—of those who failed to evade it entirely.

In other words, it was a strike from the Sunlight Sword.

"——"

Only a moment before, he had judged that no reinforcement from that crimson sacred blade could be hoped for.

So at the appearance of this impossible aid, Vincent's eyes widened, just slightly, and he stole a glance at the profile of the one now standing back-to-back with him—and held his breath.

It was a face he knew. —He had seen that figure before, in an old painting.

"—The Thorned Emperor?"

"The Emperor's crown has already passed to the next generation; it belongs to you now. It would hardly stand to reason for me to still call myself 'the Thorned Emperor.' —I am Eugard Vollachia."

"——"

"Hm. A clever face, and a well-formed one. Thinking back on it, I never once saw the face of my own child, or my descendants, up close. If it were possible, I would like to exchange many words, and look upon that face for a long while."

Vincent fell silent before the one who continued speaking so—the undead who called himself Eugard Vollachia.

The cool, clear air he carried, the reason and vitality that lived in his eyes and the color of his skin, set him apart entirely from the many undead Vincent had seen thus far. Why did he show none of the hostility toward the living, none of the loss of free will, that every other undead shared alike?

"It is my love for my Star."

"What?"

"I took it that you wanted the answer to why I am as I am. Was I mistaken?"

For a moment, Vincent tried to probe the true meaning behind those words, then immediately gave up the attempt.

He reconsidered. If Eugard Vollachia truly was the man from the old tale of "Iris and the King of Thorns," told since ancient times, then it would be foolish to even doubt such idle talk.

And so—

"The current Vollachia Emperor. Vincent Vollachia."

"Understood. Then let us march. —O children of my children."

Eugard answered Vincent's declaration of his name, and the two Emperors stood side by side.

It was, by the very form of the Iron-Blooded Code of the Vollachia Empire—the Imperial Selection Ceremony, by which the throne passed hand to hand only through death—an impossible alliance between two sword-wolves.

—In the imperial capital of Lupugana, the fierce battle that would decide the fate of the world raged on.

One by one, the battles among transcendent figures contesting each of the summits reached their conclusions, and among both the camps of the living and the dead, each side gave everything it had to give—some fell, others pressed onward.

As though mocking every one of those individual struggles, two calamities had sought to sweep it all away in an instant—the falling starlight from the heavens was cut down by a swordsman who would not let go of his dream, and the flame of ruin, forged by binding together the lives of countless spirits, was stopped by a boy who defied fate and the companions who supported him.

Both of those calamities had been averted only by each side pouring out the fullest measure of the strength they possessed.

And yet, in this state of affairs, with the starlight severed and the flame of ruin snuffed out, the one who had contributed most to the fortunes of the living was neither of those just described.

It was a person who, alone, had reached and thwarted the Great Disaster's next move—a move no one else could have noticed, and which even if noticed, no one could have reached in time—

"—Man alive, workin' an old fart with one foot in the grave this hard."

Grumbling so, the diminutive frame of the 'Vicious Elder' Olbart Dunkelkenn sprang forward.

The eerie old man, head of the Shinobi, moved in low leaps, a strange crawling motion close to the ground, making for the undead sorcerer guarding a magic circle of red blood drawn deep in the passage.

The sorcerer turned a palm toward Olbart, and black filth welling up from the surrounding walls and floor formed into chains that groaned as they lunged to bind the approaching old man's limbs—but—

"I been at this trade ninety years or so, y'know."

Faster than the chains could reach him, Olbart swung his right arm—the arm that ended at the elbow, missing everything beyond—and from within that empty sleeve hurled a kunai.

It buried itself at hypersonic speed between the eyes of the sorcerer, who had been wary only of a closer approach, and sent him flying back to slam into the magic circle on the wall. An instant later, Olbart's left palm-heel strike drove clean through the sorcerer and the magic circle both.

The pinnacle of martial skill, honed since before he could remember, shattered sorcerer and magic circle alike into fragments.

He had been under strict orders not to kill undead beyond necessity, but granting release to twisted forms whose souls had been tampered with, or dealing with the sort of sorcerer he had just faced, fell outside that rule.

Sorcerers guarding the magic circles that were warping the castle's space—he had now dealt with some ten of them—but—

"—Hah. Finally comin' undone, is it."

Sensing the change in the air through his dry, cracked old skin, Olbart murmured.

Surveying the interior of the Crystal Palace with the eyes hidden beneath his bushy brows, he could tell that the strange atmosphere that had spread through it—the disturbance that had transformed the familiar structure of the castle into an unfamiliar palace of sorcery—had come undone.

Doors that ought to connect failing to connect, windows he climbed through never delivering him where he wished to go—whether that was some technique conjuring illusions or magic that twisted space itself, either way—

"Well, that takes care o' one job, at least… wait, oi oi, ain't this bad?"

One trouble no sooner past than another arrived, and Olbart's voice turned sour at the relentless string of disturbances.

No sooner had the warping of space righted itself than the Crystal Palace's next disturbance made itself plain—the whole castle beginning to carry a fierce surge of mana. The Magic Crystal Cannon had activated.

"Tch, that Moguro—needs to hold out a bit longer, don't he."

At the tremor of the activating Magic Crystal Cannon, Olbart voiced his complaint toward Moguro Hagane, the core of the Crystal Palace—called the "Steelfolk," the very Meteor that was the Crystal Palace itself.

Up until now, the valiant Moguro had surely been holding fast to Vincent's orders, enduring so as to keep the Magic Crystal Cannon from being turned to the Great Disaster's use—but it seemed that resistance had now been broken through.

For the Magic Crystal Cannon to be unleashed as a weapon for the enemy was extremely bad.

"If it goes after Garkla too, that ain't no laughin' matter."

The "Fortress City," which should be drawing in the great host of the dead and fighting a siege on which the survival of the Empire hinged, surely depended on its sturdy walls as the key to that battle. Should the Magic Crystal Cannon blow those away, the tide would turn all at once, and the city would be annihilated.

And that would amount to the same thing as the Vollachia Empire suffering damage beyond recovery.

Even if Olbart and the others managed to destroy the mastermind behind the Great Disaster, losing the personnel now in the Fortress City would make rebuilding the Empire impossible. —That would mean the end of the Vollachia Empire.

—Olbart Dunkelkenn had an ambition.

He was the head of the Shinobi, a people required to work in the shadows behind history—and his ambition was to carve his own name flamboyantly, magnificently, into the history of the Empire, shattering the very foundation of the way of the Shinobi to which he had devoted his entire life.

Toward that end, he had time and again felt the pull of some quick, showy outrage—treason against the Emperor, or an assassination. The arrival of the Great Disaster had thrown all of that into disarray, and he could not shake the sense that the moment for betrayal had slipped past him—but here, now, it had come.

If the Magic Crystal Cannon were used in a way that sealed the Great Disaster's victory, then Olbart would fulfill his ambition not as a General of the Empire, but as a lone Shinobi.

Whatever the Great Disaster was scheming, he would dash gallantly to Vincent's side, snatch that victory right out from under him, and let himself fulfill his own personal ambition.

And so—

"—And here I thought this'd be my last chance to turn traitor."

Racing all the way up through the castle now freed of its spatial distortion, Olbart reached the Magic Crystal Cannon's platform—only for the shot to be fired by the narrowest of margins ahead of him. His white eyebrows fluttering in the aftershock, he watched the loosed flame of ruin be swallowed by the great hole that had opened in the sky.

Had the Magic Crystal Cannon sealed the Empire's destruction, Olbart could have betrayed it without a shred of regret.

But that was not how things had gone.

"Kah-kah-kah! That little runt—pullin' this off here too, not just in the Magic City! Not a shred of respect for their elders, kids these days, I swear!"

At the sight, off in the distant sky, of the black-haired boy and the little spirit girl, Olbart burst into booming laughter.

Counting Chaosflame, this made twice now that the boy had upended Olbart's plans. But there was no anger or hatred in him over it.

It wasn't for some reason like growing old making it easier to let things go. Olbart was, if anything, bad at letting go. So this wasn't resignation—only the simple acknowledgment of a loss.

"If the other fella's better'n me, well, ain't nothin' for it, is there."

Having lived nearly a hundred years, Olbart had lost count of the dreams he had failed to realize. Of course, there were plenty he had forced into being through sheer strength, too. That was simply the way of things.

And so Olbart could let go of his desperate, long-cherished ambition—assassinating the Emperor out of sheer recklessness to leave his name in history, an outrage that, by the Empire's own reckoning, would rank alongside the Wolf Human who slew Iris in the tale of "Iris and the King of Thorns."

And so, readying the left hand left empty now that he had let go of that ambition into a knife-hand stance—

"—So how about you keep me company for my next dream instead—bein' the hero who saved the nation?"

"——"

Before Olbart, laughing low, a white-haired woman stood right beside the gun platform.

At the uppermost level of the Crystal Palace, on the platform of the Magic Crystal Cannon that had only just finished firing its flame of ruin, a suffocating torrent of mana raged, scrambling every one of his five senses at will.

At its center—the pedestal set with the magic core, the heart of the Magic Crystal Cannon, the keystone of the Crystal Palace built from crystal stones great and small—the woman in white kept her hand resting, and smiled at him.

And then—

"Your presence here is not desirable. —Elimination is required."

"Not good, Olbart. That one there—she's the 'Witch.'"

Around the smiling woman—the "Witch"—a black wind began to churn, and as though to urge caution against it, the magic core itself spoke in Moguro's voice.

At that all-too-obvious warning from the "Steelfolk," Olbart gave a wry laugh.

"Didn't need you to tell me that. —Ain't gone senile yet, even at my age."

Kicking off the floor, the "Vicious Elder" brought his own dream to an end—and in doing so, delayed the destruction of the Empire that should have come in that very instant.

"Don't think I'll let you have your way! —Bring it on, Fate!!"

Beatrice's All-Shamak, cast while locked in an embrace, sent the shot fired from the Magic Crystal Cannon flying off into the distance.

Feeling the power for it being wrenched from him in great pulsing draws, Subaru nonetheless held a baseless conviction that he could fully support the reckless thing Beatrice was doing.

—No, now that it had succeeded, he could call it a conviction with grounds after all.

"It's probably the power of our bond!"

Subaru's Cor Leonis, which took on burdens and could also disperse them, remained connected even now to every member of the Pleiades Battalion, scattered across far-flung places.

It raised up the strength of everyone united as one, while at the same time distributing the vitality needed to lend a shoulder to anyone about to be left behind, to push their back and run alongside them.

It was, precisely, the power that made real the ideal of "one for all, and all for one."

He suspected, probably, with fairly high odds, that in this very instant a huge burden had suddenly been dumped on everyone fighting for him in the Fortress City—but he wanted to believe it would surely be fine.

Either way—

"Stopping that was huge, I should think!"

"Yeah! I figured for sure it was aimed at Garkla, though…"

Watching the hole in the sky close, Subaru answered Beatrice as she raised her voice.

Subaru and the others, driven by the single thought that they had to stop the Magic Crystal Cannon, had thrown themselves into its line of fire without a second thought—but they had assumed the muzzle was aimed straight at the Fortress City.

Far more frightening than blasting away some corner of the capital would be landing an irrecoverable shot on the Fortress City, where the battle of the century was currently unfolding.

But now that they had actually stopped it, it turned out the Magic Crystal Cannon had been aimed at the southern side of the capital.

"There's no way they'd fire off somethin' pointless. There's gotta be a reason."

"—. Some troublesome someone down south. Garfiel, perhaps?"

"I mean, sure, our Garfiel's a kid brother I could show off anywhere without shame, but…"

At the First Summit, the southernmost point of the capital, the 'Cloud Dragon' Mezoreia had been stationed with the role of guarding the widest area, and Garfiel had been entrusted with defeating it single-handedly.

Honestly, it was less an unreasonable ask than an unreasonable-impossible-reckless ask of a role, but it wasn't one that could be entrusted to anyone but Garfiel. Given how important it was, it wasn't an impossible thought that the enemy had targeted Garfiel.

"And anyway, who fired it? Now that we've taken down Sphinx, who's running things next?"

"…A revived Vollachia Emperor seems suspicious, I should think."

"Please don't go giving me more reasons not to want to help the Empire at this point in the game…!"

He'd just finished defeating Sphinx, who led the Great Disaster, only to have some past Vollachia Emperor lament how spineless the present-day Empire was and get promoted to final boss—please, no.

For one thing, if that pattern were allowed, there'd be no telling how many Emperors he'd have to defeat.

"But I did feel a clear intent behind how that attack was put together. So…"

"—Bea-ko!"

Beatrice, her drill twintails streaming in the wind of their free fall, had her soft cheeks squeezed between both of Subaru's hands, and with a startled "nya!" he forced her to turn around.

In the field of view that opened up before Subaru and Beatrice, a fearsome shape came tearing furiously through the sky toward them—the "three-headed" Blight Dragon they had encountered aboard the Coupled Dragon Carriages, the one Halibel had once brought down.

Of the Blight Dragon's three heads, six golden eyes fixed on Subaru and the others, and it roared.

"This is bad, real bad, we can't even move!"
"—! It's coming!"

Faced with unmistakable hostility, Subaru and Beatrice braced themselves.

Against a Blight Dragon that could slice through the sky at will, Subaru and Beatrice—locked in each other's arms, still in mid-air, still in free fall, separated from Spica and Halibel and, incidentally, Abel, and utterly at a loss—

"—!"

His thoughts burning white-hot, the world slowing to a crawl, Subaru desperately flipped through the cards in his hand.

There was so little he could do. Whether the next Return by Death restart point had even been set was unconfirmed. There wasn't a single ally he could call to his side. Magic or Authority—he had, at most, one move left in him.

On a split-second impulse, he pulled Beatrice in close and opened his mouth to speak it aloud—

"—Unlike me, you two can't fly freely, can you? Do try not to keep me on tenterhooks like this, plea-ease."

In that instant, a smile tinged with exasperation swept Subaru and Beatrice away.

Exactly one second later, the breath unleashed by the Blight Dragon scythed through the sky where they had been—but Subaru and the others had already made a hair's-breadth, miraculous dodge, and Subaru's eyes lit up at the feel of the slender arms that had carried it out.

"—Nice timing, Roswaal!"

"How humiliating, I should think! Betty will not permit being carried like this, I suppose!"

"O-o-oh my, my, such opposite reactions on either side."

It was Roswaal, wryly amused at the opposite reactions from Subaru and Beatrice tucked under each of his arms.

Just like Garfiel and Halibel, he had been entrusted with a role in capturing one of the capital's summits, and now, flying freely through the sky with his magic, he harried and disrupted the three-headed Valgren as though mocking it.

Even as he waged this literal aerial battle, Roswaal glanced over at Subaru and the others.

"Well do-o-one, stopping the Magic Crystal Cannon. What's the situation?"

"Sphinx is defeated! We've started taking the castle that's the source of the zombies! No idea what the Magic Crystal Cannon was aiming at!"

"Concise and clear. …I see, so you defeated Sphinx. That is both a great relief and something I feel rather sorry about, but…"

"—? Have you some worry or complaint, I wonder?"

Hearing Subaru's report, Roswaal's expression clouded over, and he hedged his words.

Beatrice, who was strict with Roswaal over every little thing, wasn't being needlessly suspicious with her narrowed eyes this time. Subaru, too, felt a twinge of concern at the thoughtful look on Roswaal's face.

To the looks Subaru and Beatrice gave him, Roswaal said, "No, well," and continued.

"That one has already perished once before, in the past. I don't care to admit it, but her tenacity in pursuing her goal, and her thoroughness in doing so, would give even me a run for my money. —I simply cannot bring myself to be certain yet."

"Of course, I won't go so far as to say she isn't defeated. It's only that, if she were the sort of enemy whose plans would fail simply because she herself died, she could never have remained an enemy of the world for over three hundred years."

Subaru could not simply set aside Roswaal's speculation as mere pessimism.

Just as he'd said, if the "Witch" Sphinx had been nothing much of an enemy, the Vollachia Empire should never have come this close to ruin in the first place.

In fact, even the Magic Crystal Cannon that Subaru and the others had just stopped had been fired after Sphinx's destruction.

There was still reason to fear that the "Witch" had further moves in play to destroy the Empire.

"Then we just have to crush every last one of them. For now, though…"

"Let's make quick work of sending that big dragon—three times louder than an ordinary one—packing, I should think! Fly briskly, Roswaal!"

Roswaal gave a wry laugh at Beatrice egging him on so—and the aerial battle with the Blight Dragon began.

—Vincent and Eugard, two Vollachia Emperors who should never, by rights, have stood back to back, danced their swords together, crimson flashes of blade wreathed in flame raging like a storm.

"Amazing…"

At that sight, one that gave the illusion of searing the eyes just to look upon, Tanza murmured, dazed.

Eugard, sensing the presence of battle from the direction of the Crystal Palace, had gone on ahead, and by the time Tanza, hand in hand with Yorna, caught up, the two Emperors' joint battle was already underway.

Lit by the flashing brilliance of the Sunlight Sword, the undead whose souls were being burned away one after another were all seasoned fighters, and Tanza didn't think there was much gap in skill between them and that undead named Rowan.

And yet the raging red gale of blades gave them no quarter, and through a coordination that exchanged neither word nor glance between them, Eugard and Vincent overwhelmed them completely.

"…This one has never once seen His Excellency wield a blade together with another, like that."

Yorna murmured that, gazing at the same sight as Tanza.

The complexity of feeling packed into those words was beyond Tanza's reckoning. —Both the wavering of her eyes and the trembling of her voice were things forged by years Tanza could do no more than stand beside, never truly share.

She didn't know the full circumstances, not clearly.

She only understood that Yorna was in love with Eugard, revived from an age long past, and that there was some reason behind it beyond simply having lived a long life.

That accumulation of a bond Tanza knew nothing of felt, to her, like a measure by which she and Eugard were being weighed against each other.

"This one loves both Tanza and His Excellency. Both of you are precious to this one's heart—it is only that, within this breast, the manner of loving differs."

"Ah…"

Found out by Yorna in the very anxiety that had slipped into her heart, Tanza wanted to hide her face.

But relying on the feel of the hand she held, and holding fast in her heart the reason she stood here at all, Tanza stopped herself from pitying or demeaning herself any further.

Her face had to stay lifted.

That was the one thing every person Tanza had ever thought amazing had in common.

"—!"

Biting down hard on her teeth, Tanza sharpened her nerves to a fine edge.

She had been entrusted with the duty of protecting Yorna, who was not at her best—perhaps having taken some wound to the heart in that terrifying battle with Rowan. She could rely on neither Eugard, busy fighting, nor Emilia, who had split off from them on the way here.

So, unable to carelessly join Eugard's fight, the one thing Tanza could do was notice, as quickly as possible, any disturbance in their surroundings—

"—Yorna-sama! Look, there!"

That vigilance bore fruit.

Her round eyes flying open, Tanza pointed with the hand not joined to Yorna's toward the uppermost level of the Crystal Palace. Looking up at the same thing as Tanza, Yorna's own narrow eyes went wide as well.

What their two gazes pierced was a dome-shaped tower near the very top of the Crystal Palace—Tanza had no way of knowing it, but it was the tower housing the platform of the Magic Crystal Cannon, the Crystal Palace's own trump card.

The tower lit up from within, and in the next instant its walls were blown apart with a violent roar. Tanza blinked at the sight of the tower, destroyed, scattering fragments of the castle in every direction.

And then, out of the billowing smoke that rose thick, a small silhouette showed itself—

"Elder Olbart?"

There was a note of bewilderment in Yorna's voice, proof enough of how much what she'd seen had startled her.

Slowly, Olbart emerged from the broken wall, and he showed none of his usual aloof, breezy manner—instead, his tattered robes were stained through with blood.

Just as he was, Olbart caught sight of Tanza and the other living folk below—

"Yer Excellency! It's bad if things keep on like this! The 'Witch' ain't done with her tricks yet!"

He shouted it, in a voice fraught with desperation that only underscored how unlike himself he seemed.

△▼△▼△▼△

And so, at that very same moment Olbart cried out in his blood-soaked robes—reflecting hard on having acted, on nothing but a hunch, outside the plan—Emilia stood there.

Going against the careful thought Subaru and Abel had poured into working out what was best, and choosing instead to split off from Tanza, Yorna, and the others, had been a huge decision.

"If whatever it was that caught my attention turns out to just be me imagining things, I figured I'd have to hurry right back to Tanza-chan and the others, but…"

Chasing after a slight snag, a change in the air, something she might easily have brushed off as an illusion, Emilia had run not toward the Crystal Palace, but further north still.

And there, she had found someone who shouldn't have been there at all.

If someone was simply there, she shouldn't jump to assuming they were up to something, no matter who it was. But this particular someone was a bit of an exception.

After all—

"I'm reeeally surprised. —Why is Echidna here?"

At Emilia's call, the white-haired "Witch" turned around.

With just the faintest trace of surprise mixed into her elegant features, she said:

"I rather think that surprise would be more appropriate coming from me. —Witch of Envy."
