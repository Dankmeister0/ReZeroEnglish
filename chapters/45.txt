Arc 2 Chapter 21: "A Game of Tag at the Witching Hour"

In that instant, what dominated Subaru's mind was a complete blank.

Not even a clinging, desperate plea to deny the scene before his eyes came to mind. Purely and simply, Subaru's thoughts were blanketed in white.

His throat froze, he forgot even to breathe, his heart seemed to stop beating — a paralysis broken only by the sensation of a single drop of sweat sliding down his forehead, brushing his skin.
The feel of that sweat falling from the tip of his chin to the ground brought Subaru's consciousness back from the blank.

But facing reality, there was no denying that the person standing before him was someone he hadn't wanted to believe could be there.

—Bad. Bad bad bad bad bad bad.

Following the blank, what filled his thoughts was a jumbled litany of panic and confusion.
He couldn't think straight about anything. Was the person standing before him really the Rem that Subaru knew? Or could this be an impostor? No, there was no way it was an impostor. There'd be no point in that.

"Don't tell me you're actually triplets or something..."

Even if that were somehow true, Subaru himself knew better than anyone that it wouldn't be the slightest comfort.
Watching Subaru, his will to fight having evaporated entirely, Rem gently stroked her short blue hair with her free hand.

"If you don't resist, I'll make it quick and painless for you."

"You think there's a person alive who'd hear that offer and go 'oh sure, sounds great, please and thank you'?"

"My apologies. Yes, indeed — the customer was exactly that sort of person."

The polite little bow she gave was so at odds with the mood of the scene that it was almost enough to make him mistake this for some ordinary moment back at the mansion. Her bearing was utterly unchanged from usual.
Which made the crude, alien weight of the weapon gripped in her hand all the more surreal.

"A girl wielding a hulking great weapon does have a certain romance to it, sure, but..."

An iron chain tipped with a spiked iron ball — a lethal bludgeoning weapon built to mince anything it struck directly. Whoever had chosen this for her clearly had appallingly bad taste.
Subaru had already tasted its power once, had already had his life scattered by it. He knew from firsthand experience that she could wield it exactly as she pleased.
He swallowed involuntarily, turning his anxiety into beads of sweat on his brow even as he forced out words.

"Why are you doing this... actually, am I allowed to say something that clichéd?"

"It's not so complicated. When in doubt, punish. That's a maid's duty."

"Isn't there a saying like 'love thy neighbor' or something?"

"Both of Rem's hands are already full, I'm afraid."

She didn't seem inclined to play along with his stalling — Rem's gaze, even as she answered him, never once wavered from Subaru.
He had no idea what she was planning. But there was one thing he did know.

If he moved now, he would die. Certainly.
Subaru's instincts — honed, however dubiously, by five deaths — were screaming that alarm at the top of their lungs.

It was less a standoff than a one-sided endgame. Subaru raced his mind, straining to wring out even a scrap of information.
He had to avoid Rem's wrath, pique her interest, drag some kind of dialogue out of her. Telling himself that was a way to look away, if only for a moment, from the despair bearing down on him.

Because if he faced it head-on, there was no way he could keep his knees from buckling.

"—Does Ram know about this?"

The name that slipped out of his mouth, unbidden, belonged to the sister who shared Rem's face down to the last detail.
Unfriendly, foul-mouthed, ill-mannered — her maid's abilities fell short of her younger sister's across the board, a stat sheet that was kicking a man while he was down. But she was also the person Subaru had spent the most cumulative time with at Roswaal Manor.
If even that girl, whose bickering he'd genuinely enjoyed, had turned against him too — the mere thought made his body shake. But—

"I intend to finish this before my sister sees."

The answer Rem gave was, unexpectedly, exactly the answer Subaru had been hoping for.
The situation remained dire, but at least his spirits were spared from sinking to their absolute lowest.

He let out a long breath, and it startled him to realize it had come from relief.
That he could find something to be optimistic about in a situation this dire — just how soaked in lukewarm complacency was his thinking, really?

Raising his head, Subaru met Rem's eyes head-on. Watching her frown faintly under his returned glare, he pressed on.

"So this is on your own initiative. Not on Roswaal's orders."

"I will remove with my own hands anything that might obstruct the fulfillment of Roswaal-sama's cherished wish. You are simply one among those things."

"Can't even train your own dog properly, huh? Spare a thought for the poor bystander getting bitten, would you?"

"I will not tolerate insults to Roswaal-sama."

Subaru's cheek exploded as he lightly provoked her, trying to probe her true feelings.
His vision swam with the impact, and when he reflexively touched the sharp pain, his fingers came away slick with bright red blood.
She had used the chain still connected to the iron ball at his wall — using it like a whip, an iron lash across the side of his face. His cheek was torn open in the jagged pattern of the links, and blood running from his forehead stung as it ran into his left eye.

That was the price for one flippant remark. But it had been worth it — he'd learned something.
At the very least, her loyalty to Roswaal was, first and foremost, genuine. And she likely truly believed that disposing of Subaru served Roswaal's interests.
Subaru leaving Roswaal Manor for the outside world had been judged detrimental to Roswaal, who was backing Emilia in the Royal Selection.
Putting it all together, in short, her reasoning came down to—

"Ah, I see. So that's it. —You didn't trust me that much, did you."

"Correct."

The unhesitating nod struck Subaru like a blade driven straight into his chest. The answer stirred up an ugly suspicion in him, and having that suspicion confirmed would recolor every scene of the days he'd spent there so far.
So Subaru did not voice the doubt now sprouting in his heart.

Instead, twisting his mouth in self-mocking derision at his own pathetic foolishness, he said,

"Pathetic, aren't I. Actually thought I was pulling it off. What a joke."

"...My sister—"

"I don't want to hear it! —Take this!"

Shouting, and in the instant Rem showed the faintest hesitation, Subaru thrust forward the phone he'd hidden at the small of his back — an instant later, white light sliced through the darkness-sunk forest, and for a split second it froze Rem's movements.

"—Take THAT!"

"———!"

He lunged, driving a shoulder tackle into her small frame and sending her flying.
Whatever the trick behind it, Rem could swing that instrument of violence with impossible physical strength — but in a straightforward collision, Subaru had the advantage in build and weight.
The unrestrained charge sent her small body flying backward; she lost her balance and crashed to the ground. He tore past her without so much as a glance back.

Gasping, forcing air into his lungs, he pushed both his thoughts and his legs to run.
If this was Rem acting on her own initiative, there was still a chance to salvage his life. If he could make it back to the mansion and appeal directly to his employer, there was still a possibility.
But if Roswaal's opinion aligned with Rem's, then this was nothing but the folly of escaping a lion's cage only to bolt straight into a wolf's.

"Even so... Emilia... surely she..."

The silver-haired girl who shone more brilliantly than anyone else in his memory — surely there was no room for doubt with her.
Her, her, her—

—She who was a party to the Royal Selection herself, who, if anything, might find Subaru's very existence the most inconvenient of all?

"———!?"

A sudden bloom of unease rising in his chest. Realizing it was spoken in his own voice, dark and cold beyond measure, Subaru felt struck as if by lightning.

Unmistakably, it was himself, in his own voice, doubting Emilia's heart.
That straightforward, earnest girl who never hesitated to put herself at a disadvantage for someone else's sake — Subaru, who knew her, who *knew* that about her, had doubted her.

Change your position, and your thinking changes too.
Just as Rem had, in fact, done — how could he say for certain that no one else in that mansion was after his life?
To begin with, it had been a mistake not to suspect an inside job after his second death. It had happened inside the mansion. Of course whoever had the easiest access would be someone from within.
Blinded by relationships that were only skin-deep, he'd overlooked this — and this was the result.
Fleeing gracelessly through the mountains, without a single workable countermeasure to show for it.

—So much for "this time I'll focus purely on gathering information."

Now that a threat had actually arrived, in a form he hadn't anticipated, all he could do was cling to life while spitting it out in gasps like this.
He'd been arrogant. He'd been complacent. He'd been shallow.

Breathing hard, half-stumbling, half-tumbling down the slope, what filled Subaru's mind wasn't any way to break through his situation — only an endless litany of regret.

"What was I even... doing all this for...!"

A whimper spilled out, and tears blurred his vision without his meaning them to.
His footing grew unsteady, and when the forest suddenly broke open, Subaru saw night closing in over an open sky.
And then—

"—Ah?"

A blade of wind from some tremendous height flashed once — and blew away his right leg below the knee.

Watching the severed lower half of his own leg arc high through the air, Subaru's body lurched violently off balance. His footing gave way, the momentum of his run spun him around, and he slammed into the ground. The impact reopened the wound on his cheek, and his shoulder crashed hard into the trees with a dull, resounding crack, sending pain shooting through him as though electrodes had been jammed straight into his brain. But above all—

"Gyaaaaaaah! M-my leg, my leeeg!!"

The lack of pain — that in itself was the most terrifying sensation of all.
His right leg ended below the knee; the severed fragment had flown off somewhere beyond the underbrush. The wound displayed vivid pink flesh, and bright blood gushed out belatedly, as if only now realizing it had been severed. At the same moment, the pain, too, belatedly began to ravage his nerves.

"————————!!!"

He clawed at the ground, letting out a wordless howl of anguish.
He clutched at the wound, thrashed his body, and with his free right arm beat at the ground, punched at the trees, his nails tearing away wholesale, the searing heat of it boiling his mind.

He suffered, and suffered, and suffered, suffered to the very limit of suffering.
The pain filed at his nerves like a rasp, planing directly at his brain, a sensation of himself being whittled away. With every passing second, blood was lost at a horrifying rate — which meant, just as directly, the loss of the very source of Subaru's life.

And then, into this—

"Ah, finally caught up to you."

Her maid's skirt swaying, Rem appeared, dragging her weapon behind her.
She looked at Subaru writhing on the ground, took in the absence of his right leg, and after a moment's silent consideration, nodded.

"It must hurt. It must be agonizing. Please, wait just a moment."

Crouching, Rem turned her pale palm toward his wound.
His throat was already hoarse from voiceless screaming, and the thrashing agony had drained him of the strength — and the will — to move.
Subaru moved his clouded eyes and silently watched what Rem did. Then—

"O Mana of water, grant this one healing."

She murmured, and suddenly a soft, faint light shone from her palm.
The light gently enveloped the stump of Subaru's right leg, and with that faint glow began to make up for the pain and loss.
Feeling something like relief in a sensation almost like an itch, Subaru dragged his fading consciousness back and stared, stunned, at Rem's face.

The pain wasn't entirely gone, but an even greater astonishment had taken hold of him.
He couldn't understand why, at this point, Rem would heal him. Catching his gaze, the faintest hint of a smile rose on her face.
In that, he found the barest sliver of hope—

"If you die easily after surviving this far, I won't be able to get anything out of you."

Her next words drove home just how fleeting and foolish that optimism had been.

Rem drew back her arm, and with the ringing of chains, the iron ball came flying.
It struck the ground just beside Subaru's upper body as he lay there — and up close, the closer he looked, the more it revealed itself as crude, brutal, an instrument built with singular focus on threatening life.

That Rem had gone out of her way to place the ball where he could see it — her intent came through all too clearly.
It was a plain, unmistakable show of force: a reminder that his life was hers to hold.
Seeing resignation settle into Subaru's eyes, Rem prefaced her next words with a simple "Now, then," and asked,

"I will ask you this. Are you with a faction hostile to Emilia-sama?"

"...My heart's always on Emilia-tan's side."

The instant he said it, the slackened chain lashed viciously across his upper body.
The undershirt already torn by branches along the way gave way easily, and the skin beneath it was carved with an equal measure of lacerations, Subaru's scream tearing through the forest.

"Who is employing you, and for how much?"

"Emilia-tan's smile. Priceless."

The same punishment, delivered with a flick of her wrist. Even as he registered, with his body, the sheer precision with which she struck the exact same spot without the slightest deviation, he found himself praising her skill through screams of agony.

More of the same followed — similar questions, similar answers.
For every one, the chain rang out, followed by a chorus of groans and screams.
Every time his consciousness threatened to be blasted away, Rem's own hand would heal him with recovery magic, cycling him endlessly through this purgatory of healing and violence.
His spirit wore thin, his awareness kept flickering in and out. And still, through it all, his heart alone refused to yield to what Rem was doing.

Perhaps growing weary of Subaru's stubborn defiance, Rem suddenly glanced up at the sky and murmured,

"I should be heading back soon. Dinner preparations will fall behind schedule."

"...Dinner, huh. What's... on the menu... today..."

"Let's see. How about a minced meat pie, perhaps?"

Rem rattled the chain deliberately, flaunting the presence of the iron ball, and all Subaru could manage in reply was, "Pass on being the main course tonight."

At Subaru's unrelenting stream of quips even now, Rem finally let a sigh betray some flicker of feeling. Then she fell abruptly silent, and looked down at Subaru with eyes emptier of emotion than ever before.

"—Are you affiliated with the Witch Cult?"

An unfamiliar term burst out, and Subaru's brow furrowed in confusion.
What did that word have to do with anything happening here? Unable to grasp Rem's meaning, he held off answering. To Subaru's silence, Rem said,

"Answer me. You are 'one bewitched by the Witch,' aren't you?"

"...Bewitched... by a witch?"

"Don't play dumb!"

Her voice rose as though in fury, and Rem's eyes, sharper somehow, bored straight through him. It was, through every scene from their true first meeting all the way to this very struggle over his life, the first moment Rem had ever bared open emotion.

Her face, normally never losing its composure, was now faintly flushed with anger, and she looked down at Subaru with something that could only be called murderous intent.

Faced with a killing intent he had no memory of deserving, Subaru weakly shook his head.

"I don't... know what you're talking about... to begin with, my family's never been... religious, generation after generation..."

"Still playing dumb. Reeking of the Witch that strongly, and pretending to have nothing to do with it — that's a lie too blatant for words."

Rem swung her arm down before Subaru, twisting her lips into a mask of fury. Seeing this unprecedented display of emotion, Subaru's eyes widened, feeling as though he'd brushed against something of her true nature.

"Even if my sister and everyone else fails to notice, Rem alone notices that stench! That foul reek, that lingering trace of a sinner — it fills Rem with revulsion, with the urge to spit."

Before Subaru's silence, Rem bit down on her lip so hard it seemed she might grind her teeth.

"Even while watching my sister converse with you, Rem felt she might go mad with anxiety and rage. The very cause of what happened to my sister, someone connected to it... shamelessly here, in this place so precious to Rem and my sister..."

Disjointed words spilled out like a curse, and a breath heavy with resentment washed over Subaru without mercy.

"Because Roswaal-sama said to welcome you, Rem kept watch, kept her patience. ...But now even the time spent watching has become unbearable. Even knowing that my sister is only pretending to look after you, only acting friendly toward you!"

As though releasing all the hatred she had bottled up at once, Rem hurled fury at Subaru as if to make up for all the emotion she had withheld until now.
Having said her piece, her shoulders trembling, fury burning in her eyes, she glared down at him. And then, suddenly, those eyes widened in shock.
That was because—

"—Why..."

Before Rem, who had just spoken of hatred, Subaru was quietly weeping.

"I knew. ...I could imagine it, you know."

His throat hitched, and the hot welling feeling rose and spilled one after another over his eyelids, down his cheeks. Streaming unstoppable tears, Subaru spoke in broken, tear-choked words.

"Going through all this... I already knew there was something behind it, even when you were being kind to me. But... I didn't want... to hear it."

The two of them who had taught someone who could do nothing the basics of housework.
Ram, who mocked him for not even knowing how to put on his butler uniform; Rem, who scolded her sister while helping him get dressed.
He'd been hopeless with knives, couldn't even peel a vegetable properly, and yet Ram had patiently, persistently taught him anyway. Rem, too, whenever Subaru struggled in the kitchen, would offer him a taste each time a dish was finished.

"I don't cut myself peeling vegetables anymore. And laundry — I learned different fabrics need different washing. Cleaning, I'm still learning, but..."

Four days and four days weren't enough to progress much further than that.
But he'd thought that if he got past a few more sets of four days, in the days beyond that, he might come to know even more.

"Reading and writing... just the simple stuff, but I can do it now. I read a children's story, you know. Thanks to you two..."

"What... are you talking about?"

At Subaru's delirious, feverish voice, Rem's tone dropped, as though she found it unsettling. Subaru looked up at her, straining to force out each word.

"I'm talking about... the things you two gave me..."

"I have no memory of any such thing."

"—Then why don't you remember it!!"

At the sudden burst of raw emotion, Rem's foot involuntarily took a step back.
Pushing his prone body upright, scraping at the ground with an unbalanced upper body, baring his teeth, Subaru howled.

"Why is everyone ganging up to leave me behind...!? What did I even do...!? What am I even supposed to do...!?"

He couldn't control his feelings. Even knowing full well this was nothing but a childish tantrum, Subaru's heart — his very soul — couldn't stop itself from screaming.
Summoned to another world, driven into one unreasonable situation after another, and still he'd gritted his teeth and kept going. But now he was at his limit.

"What did I do wrong...? What was so bad about me...? ...Why do you all hate me so much...?"

"—Rem—"

"I... care about you gu—"

—The impact cut off the rest of his words.

The force of the surprise attack rocked his body, and Subaru's body slowly slumped into the tree behind him.
A rasping breath and a sound like gushing water reached his ears close by, and his gaze wandered, searching. It found the cause almost immediately.

His throat.
The right half of Subaru's throat had been gouged out by something, and air and bloody foam were violently erupting from partway down his windpipe.

Right in front of him, he saw Rem's face, staring in blank shock at the wound.

That was the last thing he registered before the light went out of Subaru's eyes, rolling back to white.
His voice failed. His consciousness dropped out as if a switch had been flipped.
Everything receded. There was no pain. Anger, sorrow, even feeling itself — all of it vanished somewhere.

Only, at the very last,

"—My sister is... too kind."

He thought he heard, faintly, someone's sorrowful voice murmur that, and nothing more.
