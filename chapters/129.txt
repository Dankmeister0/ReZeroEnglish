Arc 3 Chapter 52: "From Zero"

—He'd lost the bet.

What filled Subaru's heart, struck down by Rem's tearful smile and by his own words come back to haunt him, was the enervating helplessness of having lost a gamble he'd staked everything on.

If it were her—her, who clung to Subaru with such fierce dependence—maybe, Subaru had believed. No, he'd hoped.
That maybe, just maybe, she would throw away everything else and choose him.

It had been a fleeting dream. A conceited notion. He should have known better from the start.
It was precisely because he could find no value in himself that he'd arrived at the choice to run away—so what had he been expecting?

"Right, now… maybe it's not something to laugh about, but… look, once we actually go through with it, I'm sure we'll be able to laugh, really, that's how it—yeah, that's how it is. So, um…"

Even though the conclusion had already been reached, what next slipped from Subaru's mouth was a weak, unmanly attempt to paper over it.
He couldn't think of an effective rebuttal to Rem's words. But if he didn't keep talking, hope would run out. Maybe, just maybe, she would still change her mind.
If he kept talking to her, surely— though that too was just wishful thinking.

"...Rem has thought it over too."

Still gazing at the clinging Subaru, Rem closed her eyes and murmured that.
She tilted her lovely face slightly upward.

"When we arrive in Kararagi, first we'll rent lodgings. We'd like a house, to build a foundation for our life, but considering our funds, we can't overreach. First we find a way to get a stable income."

She held up one finger, as if adding onto Subaru's earlier vision of the future.

"Fortunately, thanks to Roswaal-sama's arrangements, Rem has had an education, so I think it will be fairly easy to find some kind of work even in Kararagi. Subaru-kun… might have to look for manual labor, or maybe just take care of Rem's daily needs."

She gave a small laugh, teasing Subaru for the fact that there was nothing he could actually do.
For someone as shallow in this world's learning, and as thoroughly unskilled, as Subaru, it was probably a fair assessment.

"Once the income is stable, we should find a somewhat better place to live. In the meantime, Subaru-kun should study so he can take up other work… until he can actually work properly—a year, maybe. Let's have Subaru-kun work hard, and get him standing on his own feet sooner than that."

Her educational policy turned out to be surprisingly Spartan.
When she occasionally stood in for Ram to oversee his studies, her teaching manner was gentle, but she showed no mercy in the assignments and problems she set. He suspected it came from her strictness with herself, and even as he grumbled about it aloud, it was one of the things he found endearing.

"Once we're both working and we've saved up a certain amount… maybe we could buy a house. Maybe even run some kind of shop. Kararagi is a thriving merchant city, so I'm sure there'll be room for Subaru-kun's outlandish ideas to be put to use."

Rem clapped her hands, sketching an overly optimistic future in the air.
And to Subaru too, it felt as though he could clearly see the scene she envisioned.
There, without a doubt, Subaru would still be causing Rem trouble, still being spoiled by her, and yet still working hard, sweat on his brow, with at least some sense of responsibility.

*I really wish it could be like that,* he thought, truly.
For her, only for her, to be able to work that hard—how happy would that make him.

"Once the work is on track… well, it's embarrassing, but… children, maybe. Since they'd be half-oni, half-human, I'm sure they'd be quite the rascals. Boys or girls, twins or triplets, they'd all be adorable."

Her cheeks colored, and Rem gave a small shake of her head at having gotten a little ahead of herself in her imagining.
She counted off on her fingers, and when the number reached the frightening point of past ten, she stopped counting.

"I'm sure it won't be all fun and games, and I don't think everything will go exactly as imagined. Maybe no boys will be born and it'll be all girls, and Subaru-kun will end up feeling out of place at home."

"...Rem."

"But, but, even when the children grow up to be the kind of age where they treat Subaru-kun coldly, Rem will always be on Subaru-kun's side. The neighbors will call us the famous lovebird couple, and we'll spend time together slowly, in the same way, and grow old together..."

"...Rem..."

"I'm sorry to say this to Subaru-kun, but if possible, please let Rem go first. On the bed, with Subaru-kun holding my hand, surrounded by our children and their children, quietly saying 'Rem was happy,' and being sent off like that..."

He couldn't keep his face raised.
The future Rem was describing was quietly, gently wounding Subaru's heart.

"Happily, happily… I could end my life."

"That's enough—!"

Rem closed off that future, so bouncingly told, so cringe-inducing to listen to, so aching in a way that made you want to claw at the depths of your chest—a future overflowing with such sorrowful happiness.
What remained in Subaru's chest, having heard it all, was a surge of unspeakable, grief-laden passion.

His throat trembled. Something heavy sank in the pit of his stomach. His head ached. Something hot welled up behind his eyes, and he shook his head to try to hide it.

"If you feel that much... then...!"

*Then run away with me, all the way, as far as we can go—*

But that plea—

"If Subaru-kun would smile, and wish for that future… then Rem truly thinks it would have been fine to die like that."

—did not reach her, as she held back a sorrow greater than Subaru's own, and still smiled.

Stunned, staring at that aching smile, Subaru finally understood.
No matter how much he clung and pleaded, he could not overturn Rem's resolve.

He himself, in a way so unmistakably, hopelessly clear, had lost the bet.

"――――"

A weariness like something heavy pressing down on his shoulders assailed him.
Barely managing to hold back the disgrace of collapsing right there on the spot, Subaru covered his face with his palms and despaired.

Rem had refused to come with him.
And that meant the means to save her were now cut off. If he stayed by her side to protect her as things stood, what awaited was the cruel fate that would strike her on her way to the mansion—an unchangeable tragedy, and the dead end of a merciless destiny.

Then again, should he abandon Rem and flee alone?
Doing so would leave the unease of an uncertain future, but it would at least let him escape the despair bearing down right in front of him, the despair with no way to avoid it. Of course, nothing about the outcome of events that would happen whether Subaru was there or not would change. It would merely mean Subaru wouldn't have to witness the fact—covering his eyes and ears and pretending not to know, so he wouldn't have to face the reality directly. That was the extent of the mercy it offered.
Even a mercy that meager was, right now, something Subaru wanted badly enough to cling to. And yet, what salvation was there in accepting it all alone?

Whether he challenged fate, sank into a sea of madness, or threw everything away and ran—fate would never allow any of it.
Then what, in the end, was left for Subaru to—

"If Rem can live with Subaru-kun… the fact that Subaru-kun thought to take Rem with him to the place he meant to run to—right now, that makes me happy from the bottom of my heart. I'm happy. —But it's no good."

Placing a hand on her chest, Rem tore up the ticket to escape that Subaru had offered, and yet, her cheeks coloring with overflowing feeling, she lowered her head.
Running, running, and at the place they ran to, the fulfillment of the fairy tale she'd just told—she herself, more than anyone, knew it would come true.
That happiness, that story she declared with such certainty—and yet the reason she still denied it was—

"Because I'm sure that if we ran away together right now… I feel like Rem would be leaving behind the Subaru-kun that Rem loves the most."

"――――"

What was she saying? What was Rem saying?

Slowly lifting his face, Subaru looked at her with dazed eyes.
Rem kept a sorrowful smile turned toward Subaru, yet her eyes, resolute, pierced straight through him. Overwhelmed by that spirit, Subaru heard her continue.

"Subaru-kun. Please tell Rem what happened."

He shook his head. Impossible. If he did that, Rem would die.

"If you can't tell me, then trust me. Rem will surely find a way to do something about it."

He shook his head. Impossible. If he let her try, Rem would die.

"...But, at least for now, shouldn't we go back? If we take our time, calm down and think it over, maybe a better idea will come to us."

He shook his head. Impossible. If he waited for that, everyone would die.

"I've already agonized over it… I've already thought it through. I've suffered through it… that's why I gave up."

No one believed in Subaru.
No one expected anything of Subaru.
Everyone had thrown out warnings against Subaru's shallow foolishness, telling him to do nothing.

He'd kept ignoring that, wearing himself down, making a fool of himself over and over, until he'd arrived at where he stood now.
That time, that mental erosion, had been for Subaru—

"—Giving up is easy. But—"

Suddenly, Rem's rebuttal to Subaru's feeble words.

—*Giving up is easy.*

The instant those words entered his ears, an incomprehensible shock ran through Subaru's entire body.
An overwhelming impact, as if struck by lightning from the crown of his head, something beyond words that exploded inside his chest, an all-consuming, searing sensation as if every pore on his body had opened up at once.

"Giving up is… easy…?"

"Subaru-kun?"

"Don't… screw around…!"

To Rem, bewilderment now in her eyes, Subaru's voice leaked out, thick with something like a grudge, his teeth gritted.

Not a chance. Giving up is easy? Simply casting aside your goal, showing your back, and running off with empty hands—as if that would be so carefree?
There was no way such a stupid thing could be true.

"There's no way in hell giving up is easy!!"

The pent-up, festering emotion he could no longer hold back burst out, riding straight onto his tongue and spilling out.
Rem flinched, startled at Subaru raising his voice in anger, and the people passing along the street at the royal capital's main gate turned to look at what the commotion was, at Subaru's furious state.
Ignoring the crowd's rude stares, Subaru glared only at Rem, standing before him.

"You think that's what I'm doing—doing nothing, thinking nothing, cutting everything off without a second thought, throwing it all away without a care, and calling that giving up!?"

It had been an agonizing decision. A decision reached only after an experience like weeping tears of blood, like screaming until his throat ran dry, and being made to understand, again and again, that it still wasn't enough.
Giving up on everything—put into words, that was all it amounted to as a conclusion. But how great a sacrifice it had taken to arrive at that conclusion was something that must never, under any circumstances, be made light of.

"Even giving up wasn't easy...! Fighting, trying to somehow make it work—that would've been so much easier...! But there's nothing I can do—there's no path anywhere! The only path left leads to giving up...!"

The dead end of destiny blocked every path offered, mocking Subaru.
No matter how many times he challenged it, no matter how many times he stood against it, no matter how he schemed, no matter how he tried to entrust it to others, and even when he tried to run.

It was no longer possible to pick everything up.
Even the people he'd wanted to save had refused the hand he held out—so how could he still say he ought to keep trying? Who had the right to tell Subaru he was giving up too soon?
Could anyone say the same words after going through as much as Subaru had, after tasting the same pain and hardship as Subaru, after seeing the same hell as Subaru?

"If there were something I could do… I would… I would...!"

He truly, sincerely wanted to do something.
He truly wanted to save them, to rescue them, to feel that he hated having them taken from him.
But it wouldn't reach. It wouldn't let itself be reached. All of it was the result of everything Subaru had piled up until now, all that time, baring its fangs at him.
And so Subaru—

"Subaru-kun."

Wringing out his voice, having wrung out every last drop of emotion, Subaru hung his head, and Rem spoke to him.
His ears were ringing terribly, and he was bitterly ashamed of having laid bare such an unsightly truth—he couldn't even look up at her face.
To such a pathetic, hopeless, helpless loser defeated by fate—

"Giving up is easy."

"――――"

"But—"

Rem repeated, once more, the very words that had set Subaru off before.
Feeling something unbelievable in those words, Subaru's lips trembled as he raised his face.

Why wouldn't she understand?
Even after all this, could she still not comprehend Subaru's anguish?

That inner turmoil, that discontent, that self-pitying sentiment lashing out—

"—It doesn't suit Subaru-kun."

—dissolved before her, as she stared straight into Subaru's black eyes and declared it.

Rem spoke it plainly, as if she believed it to be an absolute truth.

"Rem doesn't know how painful it's been for Subaru-kun, or what Subaru-kun knows that's making him suffer so much. Rem knows that it's not something I can casually say I understand."

"――――"

"But even so, there's something Rem does know."

"――――"

"It's that Subaru-kun isn't someone who can give up on something halfway through."

To the man before her, in the depths of grief, having thrown everything away, having just now spoken words of surrender, Rem wove her words without shame, without fear, without wavering.

"Rem knows."

"――――"

"Rem knows that when Subaru-kun wishes for a future, he's someone who can talk about that future with a smile."

"――――"

To the man who had, his face a mess of guilt and regret, described a world with Rem, in the place they'd run to, a world that surely would have been peaceful and full of ease—Rem, without scorn, without disappointment, wove her words with unwavering eyes.

"Rem knows."

"――――"

"Rem knows that Subaru-kun is someone who can't give up on the future."

Rem declared it to the bowed Subaru, as if savoring each word.
In her eyes there was only a sincere light, and in it, nothing but a color of utter, unwavering trust in Subaru.

Subaru was overwhelmed by that fierce, powerful light.
Because that was nothing but her misunderstanding. A mistake almost comical in its scale, words that gave a man like Subaru far more credit than he deserved.

He had no idea how noble, how proud a person the Subaru reflected in Rem's eyes must be.
But the real Subaru could never be such a grand person.

Whining, breaking under adversity, painfully aware of his own pitiful smallness, coated in a sense of defeat and trying to run away—that was Natsuki Subaru.

"That's not... I'm not that kind of person... I'm..."

"You're wrong. Subaru-kun hasn't given up on everyone... not on Emilia-sama, not on Onee-sama, not on Roswaal-sama or anyone else."

She denied it with a fierce edge to her voice. But she was wrong. Subaru had thrown them all away.

"I gave up, I gave up. Trying to pick up everything was impossible from the start... my hands are too small, everything spilled through them, nothing's left...!"

"No, that isn't true. Subaru-kun has—"

Endlessly, endlessly, she denied Subaru's surrender.
Why, to this extent, why would she refuse to acknowledge the fault of a Subaru who had exposed himself in such a disgraceful way? What did Subaru look like, in her eyes?

That was so unbearably infuriating, so intolerable, that—

—*No matter how much you say, no matter what you say—*

"—What do you know about me!? What do you know about anything about me!? What could you possibly understand about me!?"

Raging emotion, a flame burning inside his chest, a scarlet heat surging out with force.
Raising his voice, Subaru slammed his fist into the wall beside him. A hard sound; from his shattered fist, red blood scattered across the wall, and he roughly smeared it with his palm.

"I'm this much of a man! No strength, but sky-high hopes! No wisdom, but nothing but dreams! Nothing I can actually do, but flailing pointlessly all the same...!"

Everyone has at least one redeeming quality.
And everyone develops that one quality, and goes to the place suited to them.
—But Natsuki Subaru didn't even have that. Didn't even have that, and yet the height of the place he aspired to was absurdly, undeservedly high.

"I...! I hate myself, more than anything!!"

Laughing it off, deflecting with jokes, dodging with clowning around and fanfare, having never seriously faced reality—faced with it now, Subaru bared his true feelings for the first time.
Natsuki Subaru hated himself more than anyone, more than anything.

"Always all talk! Acting so high and mighty when I can't do a damn thing! Never lifting a finger myself, but the moment it comes to complaining, suddenly I'm a professional! Who the hell do I think I am!? How the hell can I go on living without being ashamed of myself!? Huh!?"

The pettiness of being unable to elevate himself, and so trying, relatively, to elevate himself by putting others down instead. The baseness of not wanting to admit inferiority to others, and so nitpicking at their faults to protect his own paper-thin pride.

"I'm empty. I'm hollow inside. Of course I am... yeah, obviously. Obviously I am! Do you know what I did, before I ended up here, before things got to the point where I'd meet people like you!?"

Before he fell into this other world.
In the original world, in unchanging, ordinary, tedious days—what had he done?

"—Nothing. I did nothing."

He'd wallowed in idleness, sunk into torpor, spent his days with nothing to do with effort or self-improvement.
And yet he hadn't given up on himself either, kept telling himself convenient things like, someday, when the time came, he'd really give it his all.

"I never did anything... not a single thing did I do! With all that time I had! With all that freedom I had! I could've been anything, and I did nothing! And this is the result! This is what I am now!"

If he'd used that overabundant time productively, Subaru surely could have become anything.
But in reality, Subaru had squandered the time given to him, and as a result, had neither gained anything nor produced anything at all.
So now, when he genuinely wanted to do something, he had no strength, no wisdom, no skill with which to accomplish it—he'd never built up any of it.

"My helplessness, my incompetence, all of it! It's because of my own rotten nature...! Wanting to accomplish something after doing nothing—there's a limit to how conceited you can be... The price for having been lazy, my grand habit of wasting my whole life, is going to kill me and you."

A self beyond saving. A self beyond help.
Even if he could be born again, surely he would walk the same path, waste the same amount of time in the same way, arrive at this same place with the same mindset, and reach the same regret.
His rotten nature would not change. The man called Natsuki Subaru possessed nothing but that shallow a character. That fact would not waver.

"That's right, my nature hasn't changed at all... even coming to live in this place, even thinking that, nothing changed. That old man Wilhelm—he saw right through that part of me too. Isn't that right?"

Staying in the royal capital, at Crusch's mansion, Subaru had received instruction in swordsmanship from Wilhelm.
Struck down over and over, and even as he grew ragged, still throwing himself back into the fight—the old man had seen through his true intentions.

*"I simply thought that there was little meaning in preaching the mindset needed to grow stronger to someone who has no intention of actually growing stronger."*

In the midst of their training, after felling Subaru, the old man had spoken of the mindset one should hold while wielding a sword—and then shaken his head like that.
At the time, Subaru had denied it, saying he didn't understand, that he had no idea what the man was talking about—but in his heart, he'd understood exactly what it meant.

"It wasn't that I was trying to get stronger, or trying to make something work out... I just didn't want to be someone doing nothing, wanted to be seen as making an effort... striking an easy-to-understand pose like that, to justify myself..."

Abandoned by Emilia, having shown himself to be more pathetic than ever before at the Royal Selection.
Unable to bear the eyes, the awareness of everyone around him directed at such a self, he'd tried to protect himself by putting on the appearance of "making an effort," so it would look that way to those watching eyes.

—*I'm doing my part properly. I'm not doing nothing. I'm trying to change.*

That was all it was—searching for a reason to compromise, and arriving at that act.
Even though the very notion of "trying to change" was itself proof that nothing had changed at all.

"I wanted to be able to say it couldn't be helped! I wanted to be told it wasn't my fault! That's all it was! That's the only reason I put on that act of putting my body on the line! Even dragging you into studying with me—that was just a pose to cover up my own embarrassment! My roots, the small, cowardly, filthy roots of me that only care about how I look to others out of self-love—none of it! None of it has changed...!"

Peeling away, his false bravado. Crumbling down, his vanity.
The vanity of not wanting to be thought ill of by others, the self-interest wanting to insist he wasn't wrong—breaking through their thin shell, spilling out.

"...The truth is, I knew. I knew all along that it was all my fault."

Blaming someone else, finding some reason, and loudly attacking it—that had been easy.
He wouldn't have to look at his real self, wouldn't have to show his real self, and as long as the surface never peeled away, no one would ever see what he was carrying inside.

Weak, selfish, nothing but crying out, and yet wanting to be loved.
Not having to see that ugly self, not having to look at that ugly self—that was why.

"I'm the worst. ...I hate myself, more than anything."

Having spat out every last bit of dim darkness pooled in his chest, Subaru let out a ragged breath.
That murky, filth-like sludge—the pent-up, festering thing since coming to this other world, no, since even his time in his original world—he'd flung it all out to his heart's content.

Even he thought it was a personality wretched enough to make him sick.
Even having spat it all out, the nauseating feeling in his chest wouldn't fade. If you release what you've bottled up, shouldn't it, by rights, feel at least a little lighter?
Not the slightest bit of relief came—and on top of that, having finally become aware, in words, of his own foolishness, a shame that made him want to die right then and there came crashing down over him.

And having spilled out that much filth, the fact that, in the very next moment, he was still thinking only of himself—that weakness—was more contemptible than anything else.
Right now, before Rem, who stood there with such trusting eyes, he had, in effect, smeared black paint over everything she saw as shining, telling her it was all a sham—and rather than worry for her, Subaru had put his own self-pity first.

In the end, that's all it was.
Acknowledging, truly feeling, the parts of himself he hated, the flaws, the things he lacked—none of that meant he could suddenly fix them. If anything, the glaring hole was so bottomlessly deep and dark that it might have stolen away even the will to try to do something about it.

The hollow, gaping void was, as it stood, proof that the man called Natsuki Subaru was a lacking person. And the fact that even faced with it, he had no will to move, was itself further, passive proof of the same decay.

Not even worth pitying, that was Subaru. Having heard the filthy dregs of self clinging to the bottom of his heart, even the blue-haired girl before him, finally—

"Rem knows."

"――――"

"That no matter how deep the darkness with no end in sight, Subaru-kun is someone with the courage to reach out his hand."

—Even so, Rem did not give up on Subaru.

In the face of absolute affection, of total trust, Subaru felt an urgency unlike anything he'd known before.

Having reviled himself so viciously, having exposed such ugly true feelings, having confessed outright that it was all lies and that he was a hopeless piece of garbage—

—*why* was she looking at him with eyes so full of tender love?

"I like it when Subaru-kun pets me. Through your palm and my hair, I feel like I'm connected to Subaru-kun."

Quietly, haltingly, to the silent Subaru, she suddenly began to let those words spill out.

"I like Subaru-kun's voice. Every time I hear a single word, I feel my heart grow warm. I like Subaru-kun's eyes. They're usually sharp, but when he's trying to be gentle with someone, they soften—I like that."

As if piling on, one after another, to the speechless Subaru, Rem went on.

"I like Subaru-kun's fingers. He's a boy, but he has such pretty fingers—and yet when he holds my hand, they're strong and slender, and it makes me feel, yes, he really is a boy. I like the way Subaru-kun walks. When we walk side by side, sometimes he turns back, as if checking to make sure I'm keeping up—I like that way of walking."

His heart was screaming.
Every time Rem strung together more words like that, a shriek echoed inside Subaru's chest.

"...Stop."

"I like Subaru-kun's sleeping face. Defenseless like a baby, his eyelashes a little long. When I touch his cheek, it grows calm, and even when I mischievously touch his lips, he doesn't notice... it makes my chest ache so much, and I like it."

"Why...?"

*Why go on saying such things?*
Why keep hurling words like that at a Subaru who was this foolish, this empty?

"When Subaru-kun said he hated himself, I wanted him to know—I wanted him to know that Rem knows there are this many good things about him."

"That's all just... a sham...!"

What Rem was seeing was a convenient illusion.
The real Subaru wasn't a person like that. The real Subaru was far filthier. There existed a Subaru far more filled with malice, the exact opposite of the one Rem so favorably regarded.

"You just don't understand! No one knows myself better than I do!"

"Subaru-kun only knows about himself! How much does Subaru-kun know about the Subaru-kun that Rem sees!?"

He'd raised his voice reflexively, and Rem's shout came crashing down over it, even louder.
Having come to this place, for the first time raising her voice like that, Subaru was startled. Startled, he caught his breath, and finally noticed that in the eyes of Rem, who was straining to keep her expression neutral, large tears had welled up.

There was no way she hadn't been hurt, hearing Subaru's words.
There was no way her kind heart hadn't ached, hearing the full extent of Subaru's self-loathing.

And yet, even so, she believed in Subaru.
Even having heard his inner self spoken of so viciously, Rem still believed in Subaru.

"Why... why are you so... to me. I'm weak, and small, and I run away...! It was the same before too, I ran, and even so, why...?"

—*How can you believe in me this much, when I'm this pathetic, this unreliable, always losing to my own weakness? How can you believe in a me that I myself can't believe in?*

"—Because, Subaru-kun is Rem's hero."

At those words of unconditional, total trust, Subaru's heart trembled quietly.
No matter how much ill fortune was piled onto him, no matter what flaws were paraded before her, that single line held within it a wish strong enough to repel every last bit of that malice.

And then, belatedly, Subaru finally understood.

He'd been mistaken. He'd misunderstood. He'd been wrong, and nothing but wrong.
He'd assumed that she, that Rem alone, would forgive Subaru's degeneration no matter how far it went. He'd mistakenly believed that no matter how weak or pathetic a disgrace he displayed, she would forgive him.
That was an error. A mistake. A fatal folly.

—Rem alone would never forgive Subaru's self-indulgence.

Do nothing, stay quiet, don't do anything pointless—everyone had told Subaru that.
No one expected anything of Subaru, everyone kept saying his actions were futile.

—Rem alone would not forgive that weakness in Subaru.

Stand up, don't give up, save them all—she alone kept saying it.
No one expected anything of Subaru. Even a Subaru abandoned by Subaru himself—she alone would absolutely never abandon him, would never accept his surrender.

That was the "Curse" that Natsuki Subaru had placed upon her.

"In that dim forest, in a world where even your sense of self grew hazy, when Rem could think of nothing but rampaging blindly, you came to save me."

"――――"

"To protect Rem, who had woken but couldn't move, and Onee-sama, exhausted from using too much magic—you became the decoy and stood against the Witchbeast to let us escape."

"――――"

"With no chance of winning, with your life truly in danger, you still survived… and came back to Rem's arms, still warm."

"――――"

"Waking up, smiling, saying the words Rem most wanted to hear, at the moment I most wanted to hear them, said by the very person I most wanted to say them."

The many "Curses" Subaru had placed upon her were spoken now from her own mouth.
Those "Curses" were deep and gentle, binding her heart in chains called trust, and even now, they bound her still.

"Rem's time had been stopped, this whole while. Since that night of flames, the night I lost everything except Onee-sama, Rem's time had been stopped, this whole while."

Speaking a fragment of her harrowing past, Rem gazed steadily at Subaru.
In it was devotion without the slightest deviation.

"The time that had stopped, the heart that had frozen—Subaru-kun melted it so sweetly, and gently set it moving again. In that moment, that morning, how much was Rem saved. How happy Rem was—I'm sure even Subaru-kun couldn't fully understand."

"So," Rem continued, placing a hand on her chest,

"—Rem believes. No matter how much pain, how much suffering there is, even if Subaru-kun is on the verge of losing. Even if no one in the whole world believes in Subaru-kun anymore, even if Subaru-kun himself can't believe in himself—Rem believes."

Speaking, Rem closed the distance by one step.
Within arm's reach, she stretched out both hands and wrapped her arms around the neck of Subaru, who hung his head, unable to move. The pull was not strong, and yet the unresisting Subaru was helplessly drawn into her embrace.
His head cradled against the chest of Rem, taller than him, he heard her voice fall from directly above.

"Subaru-kun, who saved Rem, is a real hero."

Her lips brushed his forehead, and he felt the warm sensation touch him there.
From that point of contact, warmth spread, and inside Subaru's chest, an incomprehensible emotion swelled.

Blood coursed back into limbs that had gone still, and the noise that had filled his skull began to clear.

"No matter how hard I tried, I couldn't save anyone."

"Rem is here. The Rem that Subaru-kun saved is here, right now."

"I'm empty, I've done nothing. No one—no one will lend an ear to me."

"Rem is here. Rem will listen to anything Subaru-kun says. I want to listen."

"No one expects anything of me. No one believes in me. ...I hate myself, more than anything."


"Rem loves Subaru-kun."


The hand touching his cheek was hot, and the eyes gazing at Subaru from so close were wet with tears.
That figure, her very being, affirmed the sincere "truth" of those words, and so—

"Someone... like me... is really okay...?"

No matter how many times he'd tried, no matter how many times he'd started over, each time he'd ruined everything.
Everyone had died. His hand hadn't reached them. He'd let everyone die. He hadn't thought things through enough.

Empty, powerless, foolish, even slow to act, even his wish to protect someone wavering, half-formed.
Could even a self like that be okay?

"Subaru-kun is fine."

"――――"

"It has to be Subaru-kun. No one else will do."

If there was someone who believed in him, even when he himself couldn't believe in himself—
Could Natsuki Subaru go on fighting?

—Could he not give up on fighting against fate?

"If you can't forgive yourself for being empty, for having nothing—then let's start, right now, from here."

"Start... what...?"

"Just like Subaru-kun set Rem's stopped time moving again, now, let's set the time that Subaru-kun thought had stopped moving too."

He'd been trying to turn the days he'd done nothing, the times he'd been unable to do anything, all that time spent in vain, into regret, into shame, into resignation.
To that Subaru, Rem smiled, and said—

"Let's start from here. From one... no, from zero!"

"――――"

"If walking alone is too hard, Rem will support you. Let's share the load, and walk while supporting each other. You said that to me, that morning, didn't you?"

*Walking side by side, talking about the future with a smile*, Subaru had said.
*Leaning on each other, supporting each other, walking forward like that*, Subaru had said.

"Show me something to be proud of, Subaru-kun."

Because he'd kept showing her nothing but the pathetic parts.
Because it was Subaru himself who had cast that unerasable "Curse" upon her.

Subaru had an obligation to fulfill that responsibility.

"...Rem."

"Yes."

She answered his call quietly.
He raised his face. Looked ahead. Gazed into Rem's eyes. Calm, gentle, waiting for the answer Subaru would speak.
And so Subaru, because he wanted to be the Natsuki Subaru that she loved—

"—I love Emilia."

"—Yes."

At Subaru's confession, Rem nodded, still with a smile that seemed to have known it all along.
At that smile, at her kindness, Subaru, knowing full well how cruel it was—

"I want to see Emilia smile. I want to help her toward her future. Even if she tells me I'm in the way, even if she tells me not to come near her... I want to stay by that girl's side."

Now that he'd received the feelings Rem had given him, he reaffirmed that unchanging feeling, that determination.
But the way he felt that welling emotion was different now from before.

"Using the fact that I love her as some kind of indulgence, thinking I could get away with anything because of it... that's arrogant, isn't it."

"………"

"It's fine if she doesn't understand. Right now, I want to help Emilia. I want to pull her out of the painful, agonizing future waiting for her, and into a future where everyone can laugh together."

So—

"Will you… help me?"

He held out his hand, asking Rem, right there beside him.
Even as he answered that he couldn't reciprocate the feelings she'd offered him, even knowing it was cowardly, even knowing he was using her feelings—still, because she loved the Subaru who couldn't give up on the future of someone precious to him—

"I can't do anything alone. I'm lacking in every way. I don't have the confidence to walk straight ahead. I'm weak, and fragile, and small. So, so that I can walk straight—will you lend me your hand?"

"Subaru-kun is a terrible person. Asking something like that of someone you just rejected?"

"Well, it's not exactly easy for me either, asking this of someone who just turned down the proposal of a lifetime."

Subaru gave a weak laugh, and Rem, unable to hold it in, let out a small burst of laughter.
The two of them laughed together for a while, and then Rem straightened her posture, elegantly gathered her skirt, and gave a bow.

"Rem humbly accepts. If it means Subaru-kun—Rem's hero—can smile and greet the future."

"Yeah, watch me do it. From the best seat in the house."

She took the hand he offered, and Subaru drew Rem, who had sealed the vow, close to him.
A small "Ah" escaped from Rem, and her small frame settled into Subaru's chest. Grateful for the presence of this soft, warm girl who had loved him,

"—You're going to see the man you fell for become the most amazing hero there ever was!"

His chest was hot.
Rem, held in his arms, pressed her face into Subaru's chest, hiding her expression.
Her breath was hot. Her forehead, her cheek, pressed against him, were hot. And surely, the tears flowing from her eyes were the hottest of all.

—Even now, Subaru still couldn't love himself. He was still someone he hated.

But there was a girl who had told him she loved him, hating self and all.
There was a girl he wanted to keep being loved by, even as that self.

—*You*, Emilia, are watching. *You*, Rem, are watching. So he wouldn't hang his head.

"――――"

Because even if the courage was borrowed, he could believe the feeling in his chest was real.

From here, let's start from zero.

The story of Natsuki Subaru.



—Starting Life in Another World, From Zero.
