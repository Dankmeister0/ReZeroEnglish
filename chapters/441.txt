Arc 6 Chapter 37: "The Dream a Papier-Mâché Sees"

——The search of the third floor, "Taygeta," ended without result.

"I'm sorry. I looked really hard for a clue, but…"

At that disappointing outcome, Emilia bowed her head to Subaru with a grave expression. Subaru, embarrassed, accepted her apology, but honestly, he wasn't all that disheartened.

Of course, it would have been best of all if he'd recovered his memory here. But he'd never had much sense of having *lost* memories to begin with, and on top of that, he couldn't bring himself to feel that their search had turned up nothing at all — not emotionally, anyway.

"—? Master, what's up?"

"Nah, it's nothing."

Glanced at, Shaula tilted her head with a carefree expression. Watching her long, jet-black braid sway, Subaru shrugged, feeling faintly ticklish about it.

No ulterior motive, no malice, no deep thought behind it at all.
In other words, she wasn't thinking about anything — but right now, that attitude of Shaula's felt like a small salvation to Subaru.
At the very least, far better than having everyone constantly reading his face, worrying over him excessively, watching his every move with anxious eyes.

"From what I can tell, there doesn't appear to be any regularity to how the books are arranged. It's hard to believe they're ordered by title, or that the timeline is consistent. In fact, even books that were supposedly found yesterday — I retraced my memory, and they aren't on the same shelves anymore."

"Well, that's a troublesome, unfriendly sort of library to have."

At Julius's remark as he surveyed the stacks, Subaru wrinkled his nose.
Not only did the place boast an overwhelming quantity of books, but even their order and position were all jumbled up — as a library patron, that was a complete surrender flag.
Even if the book you wanted was somewhere in here, at this rate you'd never find it.

"Like, what if we just pulled books out one after another and stacked them on the floor? Start from one end of the shelves — well, there's no real 'end' since they wrap all the way around the room, but pick some starting point and go through them one by one — wouldn't that get somewhere eventually?"

"In my imagination, it'd be wiser not to do that. It probably touches one of the forbidden rules within the tower. It might be considered disrespect toward the library."

Tilting his head, Subaru had made the suggestion, and Echidna shot it down. At Echidna's opinion, Beatrice folded her short arms and said, "Is that so, I suppose?"

"Leaving books scattered on the floor — even Betty, who has experience as a librarian, wouldn't overlook that. Such disrespect toward books simply isn't allowed, I suppose."

"Lady Beatrice, I recall you leaving quite a lot of books piled on the floor rather often."

"Those were books whose proper method of preservation happened to be lying on the floor, I suppose."

At Beatrice's strained excuse, Ram narrowed her pale pink eyes and said nothing. Just a deep sigh escaped her, and Beatrice looked shocked at the sight.
Amid that little librarian talk, Subaru tilted his head. "Forbidden rules?"

"Sorry, that also sounds like something I haven't heard about. What are these forbidden rules?"

"Oh, sorry. Seems like we never properly explained it."

Emilia raised a finger toward Subaru.

"Actually, there are a few things you're not allowed to do inside this tower. Um, like you can't leave the tower without finishing the 'Trial,' and you can't do bad things in the library, and you can't destroy the tower — rules like that."

"I see. So leaving books on the floor counts as doing bad things in the library. …By the way, who decides whether a rule's been broken?"

"Oh, oh, oh! That'd be me, the Star Watcher! I mean, if there's a rule violation, somethin' just kinda comes over me all of a sudden, and then I turn into a merciless killing machine, ssu! So, like, please try not to make that happen, ssu!"

"A merciless killing machine… you?"

Watching Shaula raise her hand energetically, Subaru snorted, unconvinced.
Setting aside how much of Shaula's glib talk to believe, it was hard to imagine this lively, noisy girl turning into a merciless murder-machine. And to begin with — how much could a girl's slender arms even do? — though maybe that logic didn't apply in a fantasy world absolutely riddled with magic.

And in fact, Shaula's position wasn't as an ally of Subaru and Emilia's group — he'd heard she was the Star Watcher of this so-called Pleiades Watchtower, its overseer.
An irregular position, different in shape from Meili's, apparently meant to serve as a coordinator to keep the tower's "Trials" moving along smoothly. — Not that she gave off that impression at all.

"If it's really such an important position, then for old times' sake between you and me, how about you look the other way on a few things?"

"Ah, what a bad man, ssu! Master, you're seriously dirty, ssu! But that kinda naughty side is also totally irresistible, ssu! If it's a request from a Master like that, there's a mountain of things I'd wanna grant you, ssu, but this is pretty much unrelated to my own will, so, like, please just give up and go at it fair and square with good sportsmanship and team spirit, ssu."

"Useless."

"Ahin."

The scheme to corrupt the overseer turned out to fail, mostly because the overseer's actual authority turned out to be far too shallow.
As a bonus, Subaru flicked a fingertip against the forehead of Shaula, who'd been leaning her face in close during the conversation, sending that endearing face reeling backward. Then Subaru sighed listlessly.

Then—

"…Somehow, Subaru and Shaula have gotten awfully close, haven't they."

At that exchange between Subaru and Shaula, Emilia murmured as if to herself.
Taking it as something meant approvingly, almost like "how sweet," Subaru turned back with a touch of embarrassment. "Nah, nah, it's not like that."

"――――"

But the momentum behind that quip was stopped by the expression on Emilia's downcast face.
Emilia wasn't smiling. She'd simply lowered her eyes, framed by long lashes, her violet-blue irises trembling with something lonely.
Subaru caught his breath at the sight, and Emilia, noticing, waved her hands hastily.

"Ah, no, that's not it. Getting along is a really good thing. A gloomy face doesn't suit you, Subaru, so… yeah, it's totally fine."

Forcing a smile, Emilia said it as if convincing herself of something.

"Ahh, Emilia-chan. Um, listen, I…"

Hand on his head, Subaru couldn't find the words to say to her.

Right now, it felt like whatever he said would be the wrong answer.
To begin with, the correct answer in this situation existed only within the memories that had vanished somewhere far away. Every option presented to him was a wrong answer — that was the nightmarish state of things right now.
In a situation like that, what words could he possibly offer to the girl in front of him, enduring her grief—

"Hey, so what do we do after this?"

And into that near-silence, a saccharine voice tossed in a stone.
Looking over, it was Meili, sitting on Taygeta's floor with her chin propped on her hand. From her low vantage point, she swept her gaze around all the adults' faces.

"You didn't find a clue to Onii-san's memory. That's a shame, and it's fine either way, but so what's next? Are you still gonna search hard for memory clues? Or…"

There she cut herself off, raising the finger of the hand still resting against her cheek and pointing it at the ceiling.
Then she made a somewhat provocative little smile.

"Try aiming upward again? Put Onii-san's memory on the back burner for now?"

"…Yes, that's… That's worth considering, too."

It was Echidna, wearing a pensive expression, who responded to Meili's words. At Echidna's affirmative reaction, Emilia's eyes widened.

"Wait. I feel the same urgency you do. But I can't just leave Subaru like this."

"I understand how you feel. Julius and I, and everyone else here too, no doubt all wish for Natsuki-kun's memory to return. …But couldn't we consider the situation this way as well? That Natsuki-kun's loss of memory might be part of the tower's 'Trial.'"

"Subaru's memory, part of the 'Trial'?"

At Echidna's unexpected words, not just Emilia but everyone else tilted their heads. Amid that, only Echidna closed one eye and said, "Shall I explain?"

"I think it's beyond question by now that common sense doesn't apply within this tower, and it's hard to believe what happened to Natsuki-kun is unrelated to the tower. That's precisely why we've come all the way here to Taygeta. — Broadly speaking, one could say his memory was taken by the tower."

"'Memory taken by the tower' sounds pretty cool, real chuunibyou vibes. If it weren't my own problem, I'd probably be getting excited and going along with it too, but… wasn't the story that Taygeta's 'Trial' was already over? Isn't it weird for memory to get pulled out here?"

"Right. So I think the reason the memory was taken isn't directly the 'Trial' itself, but rather that some forbidden rule was broken. Even if Taygeta's 'Trial' is over, the tower's overall functions aren't suspended. Like how you can't take books out, or show disrespect to the library."

Echidna's theory laid out, Subaru wracked his head and neck thinking it over hard.
In short, what Echidna's reasoning was trying to say was this:

"So yesterday-me broke some forbidden rule here and got his head go-boom, is what you're saying. Honestly, trying to find a loophole sounds exactly like something I'd do, so I can't really argue against it."

"But then, shouldn't Shaula be angry about it?"

Subaru's conclusion earned a nod from Echidna, but Emilia cut in there. Raising her hand, Emilia pointed at Shaula, reminding everyone of the earlier exchange.

"If a rule got broken, Shaula ought to be angry, right? That's a completely different matter from memory."

"Indeed. I understand it's possible that Barusu foolishly broke a rule, but Shaula still hasn't bared her fangs at us. How do you account for that?"

Picking up Emilia's thread, Ram said, "Or," and fixed her pale pink eyes on Shaula.
Under that gaze, Shaula gave an "uhi" and shrank uncomfortably behind Subaru.

"Don't tell me she's seriously abandoned her role for Barusu's sake?"

"Even I'd call that too optimistic. Still, as Lady Shaula herself just admitted, her self-awareness as the one who carries out this tower's punishments seems rather thin."

Shrugging, Julius stood beside Echidna, backing her opinion.

"We've been presented with four forbidden rules. It's possible the punishment for breaking them isn't uniform across the board. Lady Shaula might turn hostile and attack us in some cases, but for instance, the punishment for disrespecting the library might be loss of memory…"

There, Julius cut himself short. His yellow eyes turned, just for a moment, toward Subaru.

"――――"

The emotion that flickered in the depths of those eyes was too complicated and strange for Subaru to read its substance.
But Julius quickly continued, "Though," and added:

"Whether this hypothesis is correct or not, we have no time to spare to test and verify it."

"If losing memory really is all that happens, there's also the option of sacrificing the memories of the past four or five hours since Barusu woke up, just to confirm it. The damage would be minimal."

"Don't turn people's memories into guinea pigs! Memories are what make a person who they are! Even just four or five hours — I'm still me! I'm not, uh, evil Natsuki Subaru!"

"It was a joke."

At Subaru, who shook his head and limbs furiously like jelly, Ram said curtly.
Whether it truly had been a joke or a lie, honestly, was a bit suspect. Perhaps he thought so because, though he'd only known her a short time, he could tell she was probably the proud sort, unwilling to show weakness to others.
In short, it wasn't hard to imagine — uncharitably — that she genuinely wanted to erase the embarrassment of having inadvertently shown Subaru a moment of weakness.

In any case, whether Echidna and Julius's theory was fact or not, it was worth serious consideration as one possibility.
At the same time, Subaru had his own thought about this loss of memory.
Which was—

"Honestly, setting aside whether you two are right or not, I'm against us thrashing around at random here too. Somehow, I don't get the feeling that flailing around desperately is gonna turn up the memory I dropped."

"Is that a statement with some basis to it?"

"Just a gut feeling. Plus, if this really is part of the 'Trial,' then maybe once I clear the tower's 'Trial' completely, my memory'll just pop right back up, right?"

"――――"

At Subaru's remark, finger wagging side to side, Emilia and the others exchanged glances.
But it wasn't a negative reaction born of distrust — quite the opposite, a reaction like picking up a seed of hope out of surprise.
Fist clenched tight against her chest, Emilia nodded again and again.

"That's… right. If Subaru's memory got sucked up by this tower, then surely, once the tower's 'Trial' is over, we might get it back."

"'Sucked up' is a bit too cute an expression, but that's the spirit, that's the spirit."

"Mm, that spirit!"

At Emilia's cute little fist-pump, Subaru smiled, relieved.
Watching that, Beatrice shook her head, exasperated.

"Honestly… I can't even tell anymore who's supposed to be cheering up whom, I suppose."

That was what she muttered.
Indeed, the roles were reversed here — but Subaru thought this was fine as it was.

Having found the thread of hope worth following, strength had returned to Emilia's eyes. That fact gave Subaru a great sense of relief.
A gloomy face didn't suit this girl. And she cared for Subaru with all her heart, too. That he himself was the cause of that darkness on her face was, honestly, unbearable.

"And thus, I made such a move against a girl who was far out of my league…"

"I don't know what you're talking about, but let's set Subaru's memory aside for now, I suppose. As for ending the tower's 'Trial,' if we go by what Meili says, there's nothing for it but to go up."

"Up, huh. Um, since this is the third floor, next would be the second floor, right?"

Saying so, Subaru looked around the library restlessly. He hadn't walked all around it, but it was a room he'd already observed more than thoroughly enough.
A room with nothing but bookshelves. Which meant there was no staircase leading upward to be found, either.

"Where's the staircase to the second floor?"

"That's on the fourth floor. There's a staircase from the fourth floor that connects all the way to the second floor in one go, and… well, there is one."

There, Emilia's face turned troubled, and she glanced sidelong at Julius. Receiving that glance, Julius furrowed his brow. "My apologies for the worry."

"I won't act on my own initiative like yesterday. I swore as much at the breakfast table as well. Though, even setting that aside…"

"The first-generation 'Sword Saint' Reid is a formidable enemy, is what she means. Well, now, what to do."

Julius and Ram each exchanged something bitter through their expressions. And it wasn't just the two of them — nearly everyone present shared that same feeling in common.
The exception being Shaula, who grimaced as if she'd swallowed something toxic rather than merely bitter, and—

"Ah, sorry. So, um, who's this 'Reid' guy?"

—Natsuki Subaru, left behind by the situation as always.

——The first-generation "Sword Saint," Reid Astrea.

That, apparently, was the name of the examiner stationed on the second floor of this Pleiades Watchtower.
To begin with, the title "Sword Saint" was news to him, and on top of that it came with "first-generation" attached — in Subaru's subculture-honed instincts, that could only spell trouble in the form of a powerful character.
Living up to that expectation, apparently this "Sword Saint" really was an overwhelmingly powerful figure, as a matter of fact.

Indeed, yesterday, the party that had challenged Reid had apparently tasted a difference in strength like that between an adult and a child.
Even amid that, Emilia, who'd apparently snatched a win through skill and luck, was impressive — but,

"The problem is, me having lost my memory, right when it counts, I'm absolutely no use at all."

At breakfast he'd bragged that all sorts of bold, novel ideas for clearing the tower would come popping out of him one after another, to ease everyone's worries — but from what he'd heard, the second floor's "Trial" was a pure test of skill. In other words, there was no room for cunning or the overwhelming advantage of modern knowledge to intervene.

"Would scattering flour around and taking him out with a dust explosion work…?"

The cheap, ultimate-move dust explosion — even to actually trigger that, he'd heard, required fairly fine calibration of the amount of dust. And to begin with, in a situation where food had explicitly been declared precious, Subaru didn't have the nerve to scatter flour around lavishly.
For that matter, whether there was even any flour to be had was an unknown quantity in itself.

"In that case, I guess I'm hoping for my cheat ability from being summoned to another world."

Placing a hand against the stone wall, Subaru tried channeling his will with a "Haaah!" But no shockwave burst from his palm, nor did the stone wall shatter to pieces. All that happened was a rough, gritty sensation against his palm, and a vaguely hollow feeling welling up in his chest.

Unfortunately, it seemed the cheat ability granted to Subaru wasn't the straightforward physical-enhancement type.
Hand still against the wall, he twisted his body slightly and threw a kick. His leg rose surprisingly lightly, leaving a shoe print at a high spot. His toes hurt. That was a miss too.

"In that case, it's got to be magic. Beatrice, tell me what magic I can use."

"Magic's something Subaru can never use again, I suppose."

"Never again!? Why!? Did I go dabbling in forbidden spells or something!?"

"You used a beginner-level spell way too much, one you'd been told not to use, and broke your Gate, I suppose. So Subaru can never use magic again, I suppose."

"I broke it with a beginner spell!? That's so lame!"

Rather than having used some grand spell at the cost of his life as a mage, hearing that he'd broken the very stopcock with a beginner-level spell made Subaru want to wail at the heavens over the sheer, unimaginable extent of his own uselessness.
Regardless of whether his memories were present or not, it seemed Natsuki Subaru's life in another world had started from rock bottom.

"Being blessed with good people around me — that's the one small mercy, I guess."

Opening and closing his palm lightly, Subaru heaved a deep sigh along with that sentiment.
Even if one-sided, having people who knew him was a mercy. Without that, Subaru would have been thrown into a completely unknown world with no special ability of any kind. Honestly, he wasn't skilled enough a person to survive with absolutely nothing to rely on.
So the current situation was a mercy. — Even if it meant making someone's expression fall.

"――――"

Holding his breath, Subaru crouched down where he stood.
He'd checked his body's condition, his magical aptitude, and whether he had any special abilities.

It was a sudden, unexpected pocket of free time before heading up to the second floor.
While Emilia and the others prepared to take on the second floor's wall of a challenge, Subaru had spared no effort in checking whether his own body was equipped with any sort of special power.

Striking the wall, willing power into the wall, fuming over the fact that his magical talent had run dry, dashing around the tower's interior a bit — after considering it from every angle, one conclusion emerged within Subaru.
Which was—

"This… maybe I really didn't get given anything at all…"

Sprinting full-tilt down the corridor, breath faintly ragged, Subaru accepted that fact.
He felt like he had, just slightly, more stamina than the self he remembered. When he'd kicked the wall, his hip flexibility had seemed almost unrecognizable too. But that, like the wounds all over his body and the grotesque state of his arm, was probably a result cultivated over this year he had no memory of.

The level of skill an ordinary person, unable to live up to his parents' expectations, might win through proportionate effort.
There, he felt no trace of the blessing of some special power supposedly granted by a so-called "god."

"I did try the transformation poses too, for what it's worth."

He'd tried Ultra-warrior style, Kamen Rider style, and even a pretty-cure-cure sort of thing, plus something like a sailor-uniform beautiful-girl warrior pose — none of it had any effect.
So indeed, Subaru was the type of otherworld traveler thrown in with nothing but his own body.

"All that's left is to gamble on the chance of awakening in a pinch. …Damn it."

Scratching his head roughly, Subaru swallowed down the seed of anxiety welling up in his chest.
In front of Emilia and the others, he might have managed to fake it somewhat by keeping himself tense, but now, taking a fresh, bird's-eye view of the situation, there was nothing but anxiety in Subaru's position.

That his memory was gone — that was, by now, beyond doubt.
There was simply too much evidence to believe otherwise, and honestly, right now the desire to *want to believe it* was stronger. If he couldn't believe it, how could he possibly still remain here, in this place?
He wanted to be here. Right now, here was all there was for him. So he wanted the strength for that.

"In the end, all I can do is cling to bonds I don't even remember. It's enough to make you cry."

Only ever receiving, only ever consuming — Natsuki Subaru hadn't changed even in another world.
The more he could feel that Emilia and the others were seriously, earnestly worried about him, the more Subaru cursed himself for basking in a position he'd borrowed without earning it.

"Not that there's much I can actually do by going up to the second floor…"

That was probably why his heart had refused to be left behind, refused to be the one who stayed to mind the fort.

After explaining about the second floor's guardian, Reid Astrea, opinions among Emilia and the others had split over whether to bring Subaru along for the "Trial."
The one who argued most strongly that bringing him carried too much risk was Emilia, and while the others weren't quite as insistent as she was, they too seemed opposed to Subaru coming along. It seemed they judged that even if he came, the odds of Subaru being useful in clearing the second floor were low, and that Reid Astrea's temperament, as described secondhand, would be poison for Subaru in his current state.
So, left as it was, Subaru might well have been left behind — but—

"—Even so, it was me who insisted on coming along."

The reason Emilia and the others had tried to leave Subaru behind was out of concern for him.
Being treated so gently was, right now, both gladdening and a vicious poison to Subaru. The Natsuki Subaru from before he lost his memory would surely have gone along with them without a second thought, and they, too, surely would never have had to debate whether to bring him or not.

It seemed unlikely that the presence or absence of memory had any effect on Subaru's actual abilities. In other words, aside from a lack of knowledge, the present Subaru ought to be capable of the same work as before he lost his memory.
He'd been groping around trying various things toward that end, but so far, no results.

"I forced Beatrice to leave me alone for this, and already it looks bleak."

He recalled the sour face of the little girl who, till the very end, had been reluctant to leave Subaru by himself.
After trying to confirm whether he had any cheat ability in terms of body, magic, or skill, when it came to the part where Subaru tried out transformation poses, he'd asked Beatrice to please step out.
Trying out several poses in front of someone else was, admittedly, a bit too embarrassing. Of course, Beatrice had dug in her heels quite fiercely, and there'd been a real tug-of-war between her and Subaru over it.

"When Subaru's left alone, nothing good ever comes of it — you drop your memories and all sorts of nonsense! Betty won't budge, not even with a lever, I suppose!"

"I appreciate the feeling, I really do, and I'm grateful, but I'm about to do something I can't show anyone!"

"There shouldn't be that kind of standoffish consideration between Subaru and Betty, I suppose!"

"No, I mean, I'm gonna get undressed. I'm gonna do a Moonlight Makeup transformation."

And so, pushing through with sheer force, he'd basically bulldozed her over with earnest enthusiasm.
In the end, things were settled only after Beatrice extracted a very, very strict promise from him: that he wouldn't do anything unnecessary, and that he'd absolutely rejoin the group.

"'Contractor' doesn't really ring a bell for me, but from the sound of it, I seem to have been getting along pretty well with Beatrice. Not very like me. …Was that really me?"

Twisting his lips, Subaru drew the whip that had been fitted at the small of his back, and, without much thought, swung it, striking the tip against the wall. It recoiled. It struck his own leg.

"Guoh…! H-hey, isn't this the kind of thing your body's supposed to just remember…!? Or was it all just for show, and I never actually knew how to use it even before I lost my memory…?"

Struck hard around the shin, Subaru rubbed his leg with tears in his eyes.
To begin with, there was something questionable about choosing a whip as one's weapon of choice in the first place. Choosing a whip over a sword or a gun rather suggested a personality that mistook "different from everyone else" for "cool."

"If I really knew how to use it, wasn't it just the contents of my head that got lost?"

Rubbing his leg, kneeling there, Subaru pondered that. And having thought it through, his face twisted at the sheer, hopeless bleakness of the possibility.

If, hypothetically, this reasoning were correct, then what an unsightly thing was left here.
Having lost his memory, worrying the people who'd surely been with him, losing everything he'd built up and becoming useless in the end — all that remained was a history carved into his body, with only the outer shell kept in order.
Wasn't that just a papier-mâché prop?

"Ha."

Exhaling lightly, Subaru got to his feet.
Inwardly, the four-character word that had floated up in his mind was ridiculous enough to make him want to laugh it off.

Papier-mâché, of all things, at this late stage.
— When, exactly, had Natsuki Subaru *not* been a papier-mâché prop?

"Ah, forget it, forget it. This is stupid. Is now really the time to be tanking my own motivation…"

Rapping his own forehead with his fist, Subaru sighed and gathered up the whip. Not quite sure how to coil it properly, he struggled a bit before somehow managing to return it to his hip.
As he did, he noticed the calluses on his palm — different from the kind you get from bokken swings, these felt like something accumulated over a whole year. He traced them with his tongue without really thinking. Hard. Bitter.

"For times like this, I should've kept a diary of what I've been doing. Useless."

Directing his unreasonable irritation at his pre-memory-loss self, Subaru began walking slowly.
He hadn't been able to confirm any cheat ability, but in a sense, that non-confirmation was itself a harvest of sorts. Now, at least, he wouldn't have to act relying on something that didn't exist.
Even if that was a rather negative sort of positivity.

"Whoa, whoa, wrong way."

Taking one wrong turn, Subaru found himself on a different floor.
Right ahead, spreading out wide, was a spiral staircase — an abnormally massive spiral-staircase floor meant for descending to the tower's lower levels.
In a tower said to consist of six floors, climbing from the sixth floor up to the fourth probably meant ascending a good hundred-odd meters via stairs.
It might be easier than mountain climbing, but with the somehow gloomy, heavy air that filled the tower's interior, it surely demanded more effort than it looked like it should.

"Come to think of it, this tower's got a strange structure too… though I guess that's a moot point in a fantasy world at this stage."

This was a world where there existed plants and flowers that healed you just by being nearby. And even in his original world, there had been plenty of structures that seemed to defy human comprehension. The pyramids were probably part of that.
Thinking about it that way, it seemed like there wasn't all that much difference between this tower and the pyramids.

"Looking for common points with the original world — I wonder if the old me did that kind of thing too."

The phrase "the old me" struck him as faintly funny.
It felt like some gear had gone askew between before and after losing his memory. In principle, there shouldn't be a "before" and "after" of himself. Past and present, he was one continuous self.
So even here, Natsuki Subaru was—

"—Oh?"

Suddenly, as if shaking off that sentiment, Subaru shook his head and let out a sound like a breath escaping.
It really was nothing more than an idle exhale.

Something he let slip, without meaning to, at something unexpected.
No more, no less than that.

Just, pushed at the back by a shock that slight—

"――――"

Having let out that breath — no more, no less than a breath — heaven and earth flipped upside down.

"Huh?"

His feet had left the ground. — No, it wasn't only his feet that had left it. His whole body.
His entire body torn from the ground, thrown into open air, all sense of up and down utterly lost, swallowed by a feeling of weightlessness.

Swallowed by weightlessness, the existence of Natsuki Subaru — falling.

"Wa—it?"

A sound like a violent, roaring wind tore through his eardrums.
He didn't understand. He understood nothing. Right now, Natsuki Subaru was falling. Was falling. Spinning like a drill through the air, plunging straight down.

Falling, falling, falling, and in the midst of falling, consciousness finally caught up with reality.
He was — falling.

"W-wai— wait, wait, wait—"

His vision spun, his limbs clawed at empty air, and by the time he'd lost track of how many seconds had passed since being thrown out, Subaru finally understood what was happening to him.

He was falling. Plummeting. Plunging. From some far height, toward some far-off ground.
Thrown well off the spiral staircase, swallowed by a huge, gaping black darkness. Straining his eyes desperately in every direction, he could make out plain, unadorned stone walls streaming past at high speed from below to above. — No, it wasn't the walls streaming past. It was Subaru, falling, and falling, so his field of vision streamed past upside down. Streamed away, falling.

The sensation of being flung about, and along with it, a surging nausea — sour stomach acid spilled out into the air.

"—Guoeh."

His breath couldn't keep up, his throat choked by bile.
A stinging pain shot through the back of his nose, and every organ in his body seemed to have come loose from its proper place, thrashing about in a chaotic frenzy inside him. His face, his clothes, surely the whole floor, all about to be soiled with vomit.
Emilia would be exasperated with him. Beatrice would scold him. Ram would give him a cold look. Julius would sigh. Echidna would shrug. Meili would laugh. Shaula would point a finger at him.

At that wildly misplaced sense of panic, Subaru lost sight of, and lost hold of, his very own self.
Clawing at it with his fingers, he couldn't find it. It wasn't in the sky. It wasn't within himself. Where had it gone. Where was it.
After memory, now he'd lost himself too — nothing but losses, one after another.

"Mom."

A murmur tinged with sourness slipped out, and at the same moment, Natsuki Subaru lost consciousness.
His awareness cut off, the thread of memory grew hazy, and then—

And then—

And then——

And then——————————————.



A hard shoc—

"—Subaru! Hey, Subaru, are you okay?"

Waking, the first thing he heard was a voice like a silver bell.
The touch of slender fingers against his arm, breath felt close by. Guided by those sensations, Subaru's consciousness rose slowly, and his heavy eyelids opened.

—Right in front of him was a frighteningly perfect fairy of the moon.

"No wait, Emilia-chan…?"

"Oh, Subaru, thank goodness. You're awake. I was so, so worried."

At Subaru's words, the girl before him — Emilia — pressed a hand to her chest, visibly relieved from the depths of her heart. Eyes wide at the sight, Subaru looked around him.
A room covered in green ivy, himself lying on a bed woven from that same ivy. Emilia, chest hand pressed in relief, and beside her, the beautiful little girl with the drill curls, standing quietly.

"Emilia, being that gentle with him, Subaru won't reflect on anything, I suppose. You have to scold him more sternly, or Betty and the others' worry won't get through, I suppose."

"That's true. See, Beatrice says so too, right? She was in such a panic when she couldn't find you, and when she found you collapsed she looked like she was about to cry…"

"You didn't need to say that part, I suppose!"

Beatrice's face flushed red as she huffed indignantly at Emilia's guileless remark.
Watching that exchange, Subaru could only tilt his head, thoroughly puzzled.


"Huh? Wait, was that… a dream?"

"—?"

At Subaru's remark, Emilia and Beatrice both tilted their heads in unison.
