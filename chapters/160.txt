Arc 3 Chapter 83: "Checking the Answers"

—The Dragon Carriage's speed picked up, and a terrible shaking and the roar of wind echoed through the cargo bed.

"Waah—!"

"It's okay, just hold on tight. There's nothing to be afraid of."

Pressed close together, enduring the shaking, Emilia forced a brave smile. Seeing that smile, Petra, whose eyes had been clouded with anxiety, bit her lip and nodded, "Mm."

*She's a strong girl,* Emilia thought, feeling the girl's resolve. The other children too, following the older girl's example, huddled together and gritted their teeth against their fear.

Every one of them was strong. Surely their hearts were on the verge of tears from anxiety and fear, and yet none of them gave in to that weakness—they held their voices and kept themselves together.

It was precisely because she stood before such strength in these children that Emilia, too, could keep herself from showing an unseemly face.

Normally, a running Dragon Carriage should be protected by the Earth Dragon's Divine Protection of "Windbreak." The Windbreak Protection extended to the carriage the Earth Dragon pulled, shielding whatever was under its care from outside influences—the resistance of wind and jolting and the like.

But now this carriage had fallen outside that Windbreak Protection, and the full burden of their headlong flight was bearing down directly on Emilia and the children inside.

The reason the Protection had lapsed was simple: the carriage had come to a stop once.

The Earth Dragon's Windbreak Protection was conditioned on the dragon's running—it was the type of Protection that unfurled continuously while in motion. But it had the drawback that once the motion stopped, the effect of the Protection cut off, and it took some time before it could be deployed again.

Given that, common sense held that once a stopped carriage set off again, it should wait long enough for the Protection to redeploy before resuming its journey.

"——!"

But this carriage, racing on without even sparing the time it had lost while stopped, had no time left to spare for observing that common sense.

Emilia squeezed back the warmth of the hand gripping hers to reassure herself, then lifted her face, straining her ears toward the world outside the carriage—beyond the canvas cover.

The carriage they rode in had originally been used for peddling goods, and naturally the cargo bed—aside from the driver's seat—had never been designed with comfort in mind. To protect the cargo from damage, the bed was covered with canvas and had nothing like a small window; inside, with no sunlight reaching in, the only light source was the faint glow of the Lagmite Ore they'd brought along.

Blankets had been layered on the floor to make a makeshift seat, and everyone sat huddled together atop it. Besides Emilia, there were seven children aboard, and the one serving as driver was a young man from the village's youth corps.

The carriage carrying Emilia and the others led the way, with about four more carriages carrying villagers and merchants following behind, all escorted by a single knight riding an Earth Dragon alone.

To protect themselves from the bandits that had appeared in the territory, they had left the village under the pretext of evacuation two hours ago—and it was only some ten-odd minutes ago that the situation, calm until then, had suddenly turned.

So as not to provoke the group said to be lurking in the forest, the procession of carriages had left the mansion and village prioritizing stealth as much as possible. In total, twelve sets of evacuation carriages had set out, splitting partway along the way into the royal capital group and the sanctuary group; after barely an hour, they had parted ways with Ram and the others leading the sanctuary group.

For the hour after that, though they couldn't see outside, the carriage's journey along the highway should have remained fairly peaceful. And yet—

"Emilia-sama, might I have a moment of your time?"

Feeling a light touch against her back, Emilia realized the carriage had come to a stop.

Before she could even wonder what that meant, the canvas at the rear of the carriage was lifted, and a white-haired old man's face peered in.

The old man, who had introduced himself as Wilhelm van Astrea, was a knight of the Crusch Camp, and even with her inexperience, Emilia could feel in her bones—as one who knew something of combat—that the skill behind that gentle, mild smile was extraordinary.

Precisely for that reason, Emilia could not help but feel an unnecessary tension whenever she dealt with this man. Even now, she was a beat slow to respond upon seeing the one who had spoken to her.

It took her a moment to manage a short "Yes," but the old gentleman, without remarking on the delay, gave a small nod and said,

"A small concern has arisen. For that reason, I would like to take a few knights and go 'have a look around.' If I might have your permission."

"A look around... you mean?"

"Yes, a 'look around.' It won't take long. I intend to catch up and rejoin you shortly. Would that be acceptable?"

Emilia pondered Wilhelm's words as he bowed respectfully.

There was something off about the old man's choice of the phrase "have a look around," and Emilia quickly realized it was out of consideration for the children around her. Wilhelm was choosing not to use direct language, opting instead to send only those under his protection—Emilia and the children—on ahead. If he was taking a fighting force to deal with this "concern," then—

"I..."

"—————"

"Am I not needed?"

She knew it was rude to voice that question given his consideration, but she couldn't help asking it aloud. Hearing Emilia's words, Wilhelm narrowed his eyes—the reaction she had expected. And yet,

"Please continue on with the carriage as planned, Emilia-sama. I leave the children in your care."

His mouth softened, and in the eyes with which Wilhelm regarded Emilia, there was no trace of the negative emotion she had expected. There was only a warm, generous fondness, like one watching over a troublesome child, and noticing it left Emilia bewildered.

Without letting her pursue the reason for that bewilderment, Wilhelm let go of the canvas and turned his back to her.

"Since we've stopped, I expect there will be a good deal of shaking along the way. Please, do not let go of the children's hands."

"Ah..."

"Master and vassal alike, it seems. —Your eyes are just like his."

With that pensive murmur as his last words, Wilhelm's figure was cut off from view by the canvas.

Emilia furrowed her brow, not understanding the meaning of that murmur, but the carriage soon showed signs of starting to move again, and she had no time to dwell on it.

And so the story returns to the present—thrown into a state of being tossed about by violent shaking and the roar of wind, huddled close with the children.

Wilhelm had taken several knights and left the line of carriages, leaving only two knights as escort for the evacuation group. Just like this lead carriage, Emilia could tell that the villagers riding in the carriages behind were being shaken by the same impact—which was to say, the same anxiety.

She couldn't see what was happening outside, but she had a means of knowing. And that was—

*"Someone just clashed with the old man and the others further back. It's turned into a fight."*

*"Can you tell how many?"*

*"Eight on our side, sixteen enemies... exactly double. But I don't think it's a problem. That old man was seriously skilled, no joke."*

To Puck, who sent his thoughts with a joking air, Emilia responded with a small tilt of her chin.

This was a conversation of thoughts with Puck, who was not materialized—through the Mana filling the air, Puck was able to grasp the situation outside to some degree. Through him, Emilia was learning of the outside conditions, working to keep track of the situation—that was the arrangement.

The presence of Puck, a High Spirit, actually invited considerable waste of Mana just from being materialized. Once combat began, that consumption would double, and maintaining his presence for a long time was a difficult feat even for Emilia.

So for now, given the pressing nature of the situation, Puck's form was sealed within his Anchor, the crystal stone. It was to preserve the strength to act at full power when an emergency truly demanded it.

*"Well, it's also so I don't jump out defenseless in front of the kids and get treated like a toy."*

*"Puck, you look like the type kids would like, so if it could ease the worried ones' feelings, that might not be so bad either."*

*"Now you're thinking scary thoughts, my daughter. Anyway, that's how things look outside."*

Puck responded to Emilia's light quip—or in this case, light thought-quip—and Emilia nodded inwardly with an "Mm," while biting her lip at her own helplessness.

Emilia had offered her help, and though he sensed her intent, Wilhelm had chosen words of refusal. That wasn't a sign of her lacking skill—more accurately, it meant a lack of trust. Even a Wilhelm behaving kindly toward her could not, considering Emilia's position, lightly accept her proposal. Even as allies, Emilia and their lord were, in the end, destined to become political rivals. She had been made to understand, without meaning to, how difficult it was to build a purely friendly relationship between them.

Right now, that burden of position was nothing but an obstacle.

She had neither the ability befitting that position nor the authority to wield it. It was known both within and without that she was mere decoration, and in truth, she couldn't in good conscience call herself deserving of it either.

Bound by that shackle, her freedom of action was restricted, and even now she couldn't be of use to the trembling children reaching out their hands to her.

If it came to this, then just what was she—

"—Subaru."

The moment she spoke the boy's name in a small voice, Emilia realized her own incorrigibility. Her vision seemed to darken at the shamelessness of calling that name in this situation, and the foolishness of having thrown away her right to do so.

Her own weakness and shamelessness—to try, once again, to cling to the boy she had abandoned, now that she'd been driven into a corner. She had hurt him so badly for the sake of her own conduct, leaving wounds that would never heal, gouging even his heart and pushing him away—how could she now call his name?

If she had any right now to call his name, if there was ever a scene in which she would be permitted to call his name, it would be—

"It's all right, everyone, don't worry! No matter what happens, Onee-chan will protect you all!"

—only at a moment like that boy's, when she resolved in her heart to protect someone and could act on it.

At Emilia's call, the children, curled up small and holding hands, lifted their faces. Tears welled in their eyes, and their expressions showed lips bitten in trembling anxiety. But the moment they saw Emilia's violet eyes, that all retreated somewhere, and after nodding to one another, they spoke together in unison,

"W-we're okay!" "You're the one who should be careful, Onee-chan!" "W-we promised, so it's fine, we're not letting go, no matter what!"

Voices that were obviously forceful bravado, and the children clung to Emilia's entire body. Clinging to her fingers, wrists, shoulders, even her waist, Emilia—who had never before experienced this much of another person's warmth—stiffened in surprise. But it was by no means an unpleasant sensation.

Still, there was something in their words that felt oddly hard to put into words—

"Promised... you promised someone? What did you promise?"

"Not to let go of Onee-chan." "Because if we're not with you, you'll do something reckless." "Someone has to watch over you, or it's worrying, he said."

At the answers that came back one after another, Emilia was struck with surprise, taken aback. She felt a strange defiance rise at those words, and yet she fell silent, unsure what she herself would truly have done without these children. Was that, too, one of Ram's instructions? —But she wondered if Ram had even had the time for such a thing. And besides, that way of speaking was just like—

"—————"

Having thought that far, a strange premonition made Emilia's chest give a faint throb.

Once noticed, the throbbing accelerated its insistence, wearing away at the patience of Emilia, whose eyes wavered with confusion. She fell silent as if deep in thought, then wet her lips with her tongue. As her lips, drier than she'd realized, loosened with moisture, she let out a breath and posed her question.

"That you were worried about me... who told you that?"

"Ah, no, that's..." Petra changed her expression at that question and tried hastily to cut her off. She shook her shoulder-length reddish-brown hair and clung to Emilia to stop her, but it was too late.

"Subaru!" "Subaru said it!" "That the lonely Onee-chan was worrying him!" "Subaru... ah, wasn't I not supposed to say that...?"

The children blurted out the name one after another, eager to be first, and by the time the last one realized her slip and covered her mouth, everyone had noticed. Petra put her palm to her face, murmuring "Oh no," and Emilia blinked at the name that had come up so many times.

"Suba... ru...?"

She'd had a premonition. From the way the children spoke, Emilia recalled his figure.

But the feeling of denial rushed ahead—*that can't be.* Because Emilia had hurt him with such terrible words and left him behind in that royal castle.

In that place where, more than anywhere, he had surely wanted her to reach out her hand to him, Emilia had withdrawn the hand she'd offered and turned her back. At the most crucial moment, she had rejected him when he most needed something to cling to.

Why, of all times, did his name come up now, at the moment she most resented her own helplessness?

That shouldn't be. That couldn't be.

Emilia's life had been one utterly unconnected to expectation. Expect something, and it would betray you; hope would wear thin and lose its shine before you knew it. Through countless experiences, Emilia had come to accept that sad reality as simply how things were.

That was why she instinctively feared holding expectations toward those around her. Even in moments she wanted to, fear came first, and she couldn't open her arms.

Being kept at a distance, being pushed away—that was, for her, only natural. Being drawn close, being needed—that was, for her, something that couldn't be.

That was why she had rejected even Subaru, who had behaved so warmly, who had been willing to throw everything away for her.

She couldn't believe it. Not his actions, his attitude, his words—she couldn't believe the state of her own heart that would receive them.

Expecting, expecting, expecting—and when the thing she had built up came crashing down, Emilia would be crushed by pain worse than being cut open.

The fear of that was what made her reject the time spent continuing to face Subaru. If she was going to be pushed away eventually, better to push herself away first.

Before something decisive built up between the two of them and came crashing down.

And yet—why—

"Subaru was... in the village? He'd come back?"

Amid the children's awkward silence, only Emilia's stunned murmur escaped.

The carriage still hadn't stopped its headlong flight, and its cause hadn't been shaken off either. She couldn't free the children from their anxiety, and yet she herself couldn't move from this spot.

In the very midst of that situation, her heart was shaken violently by the name she'd heard. The children's silence, by itself, was already affirming her question.

If he—if Subaru—had returned to the village, and was involved in this evacuation too, then a great many things made sense.

Ram, who should never have been able to leave the village, had communicated with the subjugation force with an almost eerie smoothness. The villagers, who should have grown tense from days of mounting anxiety and dissatisfaction, hadn't shown reluctance and had followed along with this evacuation. And the members of the subjugation force, visiting a land for the first time, shouldn't have been able to so easily create this situation without friction with the people living there.

All these many difficulties resolved themselves once that boy's piece was fitted into place.

If it was Subaru who had led the subjugation force back, then Ram, too, could have approached negotiations with them without preconceptions. The villagers would show a willingness to accept a proposal from Subaru, to whom they owed such a great debt, and he wouldn't have let the subjugation force he'd brought be treated poorly.

And above all—remaining behind in the village with the subjugation force, drawing the threat to himself so the villagers and Emilia could flee—thinking about it, that was just too much like him. Too much like him.

Because that was, in every way, exactly the kind of thing Natsuki Subaru she knew would do.

"Why..."

The murmur that escaped was tinged with a tragic color, her violet eyes trembling faintly with the shudder of emotion.

If these actions were Subaru's doing, then they were far too little different from all his past actions. Even after being hurt so badly, pushed away so much—still, he remained Subaru.

"I hurt him so much, made him suffer so much, made him wear such a sad face... and yet, why would Subaru, once again, try to..."

She didn't know if he was trying to save her again.

The question she had thrown at Subaru, fallen to the very bottom in body and soul, on the grounds of the Royal Selection, at the training field.

At that time, Subaru had not given Emilia an answer. What came back made no sense; the words he tried to rephrase were swallowed and never spoken, and it ended in a parting between Emilia and him.

She had posed the question, but no answer had come back.

So even now, she still didn't know the answer.

She had believed that ending without knowing was the right thing for the two of them.

"Why...!"

"That's not..."

At the words that seemed to catch in Emilia's throat, Petra reddened and twisted her lips. Emilia turned a pleading gaze toward the girl, whose manner suggested she somehow knew the answer to Emilia's question. But before either of them could open their mouths further,

"—!?"

The carriage swerved with tremendous force, and the bodies of everyone inside were violently thrown about. Emilia instinctively grabbed the wall and, with her other hand, embraced Petra beside her. Petra in turn embraced the boy next to her, and the chain continued until, barely, everyone's bodies were linked together.

But there was no time to feel relief; the carriage kept swerving. It moved as though fleeing from something, and—

*"Lia, something's coming from behind at tremendous speed—!"*

*"—!!"*

At Puck's warning voice, Emilia lifted her face and turned it to the rear.

Fluttering in the wind, past the canvas that occasionally revealed glimpses of the outside—there, the cause of this swerving, the shape of something in pursuit.

*To intercept it,* Emilia moved on instinctive judgment. But her body, trying to rise, was held back by a light weight and couldn't move.

Dropping her gaze, she saw: curled up right beside her, refusing absolutely to let go of the hem of her clothes or her hand, the children looking up at her with desperate faces.

"I won't let go!" "Don't go!" "We promised, I'm keeping it!"

The children held fast to Emilia and would not let go.

Faced with a restraint she could easily have shaken off if she'd wanted to, Emilia found she couldn't move. Looking up at her face, Petra opened her eyes wide, on the verge of tears,

"Is Onee-chan going to be the one to make Subaru cry this time!?"

"—!?"

A violent shock ran through her at that cry, and this time Emilia truly couldn't move.

And the instant her body stopped, the centrifugal force of the carriage skidding to a stop with a violent noise struck.

The sudden braking put a turning weight on them, and their light bodies floated and were thrown about, dragging the children along. But being flung roughly against the floor was avoided thanks to the blankets laid down beneath them. Swallowed by the impact, Emilia lifted her face and shook her head,

"What in the world is..."

"Lia! It's right behind us!"

Puck, materialized in this emergency, floated in the air and pointed to the rear in the tilting carriage. Following his voice and motion, Emilia turned her head and, instinctively shielding the children behind her, looked toward the canvas.

A vortex of magical power began to build, centered on her, and a chill slowly began to cool the cramped interior of the carriage. Then came the sense of someone rushing up, and the canvas was lifted.

And the face of Emilia, who had been about to begin chanting an incantation, went blank with astonishment.

"How... could..."

Breathing hard, shoulders heaving up and down, a lone boy climbed up into the carriage. At that sight, Emilia's violet eyes shook violently with confusion.

Her lips trembling, forgetting the situation entirely, Emilia called that name in a thin, faint voice.

"—Subaru."

She called that name.

—Looking back, Subaru thought, it really had been a terrible first meeting.

An hour or so after being summoned to another world, thrown into a situation where he had no idea what was going on no matter how many times he looked left, right, and left again—he had, quite literally, been at a loss.

He'd wandered into a back alley and, sure enough, run into thugs who preyed on the weak, exactly like Subaru—and had come within a hair's breadth of losing his life just hours into his trip to another world.

Beaten within an inch of his life, covered in dust and mud, at the very moment he was about to be killed, Subaru had encountered that silver radiance.

Subaru remembered every single thing about her—her words, her bearing, her nobility—from that moment, without missing a single one.

Because he could never, ever, ever, ever, ever forget it—that was why, even now, Natsuki Subaru could stand on his own two feet and live in this world.

"Natsuki-san, look, there!!"

Having severed Petelgeuse's obsessive onslaught, racing along the Lifaus Highway—amid a grassy plain stretching as far as the eye could see, the carriage carrying Subaru had Otto, who'd been scanning ahead, calling out to Subaru in a high voice.

Drawn by his shout and gaze, Subaru looked that way and could make out something faintly stirring, barely visible at a point grazing the distant horizon.

Confirming that it was clearly no optical illusion, but a real presence, Subaru clenched his fist.

"That's it! Otto! Full speed, please!"

"You don't need to tell me—I'm already going full speed!"

The reins snapped, a dry crack rang out, and the whinnies of the two Earth Dragons rose up.

The jet-black Earth Dragon stared straight ahead, and Subaru could tell it was pouring out everything it had to fulfill the will of the one standing in the carriage it pulled.


He'd been saved by their first meeting, and had accompanied her briefly on her search for something she was looking for. Even though he was of no use, she hadn't left him behind—she, who surely had her hands full with her own troubles, had never given up on either him or herself.

Stubborn, putting on a brave front, obstinate, and kind—that was what Subaru had come to know of her.

He remembered feeling an out-of-character self-consciousness and a pounding heart, walking beside her.

And he remembered how those sweet feelings had been ruined by his own unforgivable actions.

His belly torn open, sinking in a sea of blood, consciousness stolen away by the heat of agonizing pain and the chill of blood loss.

And yet only her voice, and the moment her body was torn apart, remained carved so strongly into his heart that he could never forget it for anything.

That was when he swore it. Subaru truly, certainly, swore it.

*"I will—without fail—save you,"* he swore.

He had run himself ragged to keep that vow. Piling up deaths, carving out a path through fate, somehow overcoming the suffering—and at the end of it, Subaru had met her, saved her, and won a smile.

Subaru would never forget the overwhelming, heartfelt emotion of that moment.


Ahead, the scenery blurred by the Earth Dragons' dash began to resolve into shapes.

It was a scene of battle between a group clad entirely in black and knights mounted on Earth Dragons. Already several corpses lay fallen on the ground, and darting about among them with tremendous force was—

"—Wilhelm-san!!"

"Subaru-dono!?"

At Subaru's call from the racing carriage, Wilhelm, who had single-handedly cut down a Witch Cultist, raised a voice of surprise.

He shook the blood from his sword, and his face showed a questioning look at Subaru's sudden appearance, but—

"Where's Emilia!?"

At Subaru's following cry and the urgency filling his expression, Wilhelm instantly discarded whatever doubts he had and pointed with his sword tip further along the direction of travel,

"Ahead! Straight on! Toward the Great Tree!!"

Lifting his face, Subaru looked further beyond the horizon.

Before he knew it, the Lifaus Highway had already passed its midpoint toward the royal capital—they had reached the vicinity of Flugel's Tree, where the decisive battle with the White Whale had taken place.

Nodding, without slowing his speed, Subaru passed straight through the battlefield where Wilhelm and the others were locked in combat. He didn't stop to ask after their safety. That would be an insult to their hard fighting, and besides, Subaru had already exchanged the words that needed exchanging at their parting.

Subaru had entrusted Emilia and the others to Wilhelm's care. Wilhelm had responded to that request, telling him to leave it to him.

So there was no need for Subaru to stop here, nor any need for Wilhelm to ask Subaru much of anything.

Their eyes met for only an instant, and Subaru's carriage left Wilhelm behind.

At the carriage's sudden appearance, the Witch Cultists stopped, and several made as if to push off the ground to give chase. But—

"Your opponent is me."

Two of the black-robed figures, having carelessly left an opening, were cut down by the blade of the sword devil closing in from directly behind.

Bathed in a spray of blood, the sword devil, half his body stained red, watched the retreating back of the carriage with satisfaction,

"A perfect opportunity to repay a debt to the one who saved me. And the good fortune that the very man who asked it of me understands, even without my saying so. —What an honor this is."

With the treasured sword entrusted to him by his lord in his right hand, Wilhelm took the knight's sword thrown to him by a subordinate in his left. Crossing the two blades before him, his gaze pierced through the enemy from beneath the gleam of the blades.

"Who do you think will get in the way of a man going to see a woman? Both you and I are far too soaked in blood to be present for a reunion scene. —Every one of you shall end here, your corpses exposed to the elements."

At that death sentence, the Witch Cultists, whose emotions should have been erased, stiffened their bodies.

Amid the taut tension, the sword devil, a smile rising on his lips, charged in with a forward lean. That smile was like that of a fiend delighting in bathing in blood, and also like that of an old man wryly smiling at the mistakes of his youth—a complicated thing.


—The day he truly came to believe he was going to live in this other world.

Reuniting with her, meeting a new cast of faces, Subaru had found a place where he could live.

If he could stay by her side, be of use to her, live doing something for her sake, then even without seeing the future, that alone would satisfy him.

Those days had suddenly cracked apart, and time crushed by despair had repeated itself.

There had been people he wanted to believe in, people he wanted to protect and had pushed himself too hard for, and the suffering of not being trusted in return—times he'd nearly thrown everything away. And there had been someone who accepted that weak self of his.

Saved, protected, having shown his unsightly side over and over, he finally managed to stand again.

Standing, running around, getting hurt and starting over, girding himself and reaching for his ideals—Subaru met the happiness of everyday life once more, and spent that same time with her in celebration.


"Natsuki-san, I see them! That must be the evacuation carriages!"

"Yeah—no doubt about it!"

Spotting the group of carriages fleeing the battlefield with all their might, Subaru felt his own pulse quicken sharply.

Before Subaru could put his impatient feelings into words, Otto, grasping his intent, urged Patrasche to accelerate the carriage.

As the distance closed rapidly, confusion visibly spread through the group ahead as they noticed the pursuers catching up.

At the sight of the carriages beginning to swerve, Subaru felt an impatience even though he understood it couldn't be helped. He raised his voice desperately, calling out so the drivers could hear him and stop.

"Stop! It's me! I'm not the enemy! Stop, please stop—!!"

A desperate call, but the ones fleeing were desperate too.

With their intentions crossed, it was only once they'd closed to nearly parallel distance that the driver finally noticed Subaru,

"Subaru-sama—!?"

"Stop, please! It's an emergency! I need to check inside!"

Sensing something extraordinary in Subaru's urgent expression, the driver worked the reins to signal the Earth Dragon to stop. At once, unable to fully kill the momentum it had built up, the carriage swerved wildly, bouncing with such violent noise it nearly tipped over halfway.

"Ia, come out! Otto, unhitch Patrasche for me!"

Too impatient even to wait for it to fully stop, Subaru's body leapt down from the carriage as it began to slow. There was no way he could land gracefully—thrown about by the force of inertia, he fell in an ungainly heap, rolling his body to absorb the impact, then immediately stood and rushed to the carriage.

The one he ran to was the lead carriage, a canvas-covered cart tilted sharply from the impact. At Subaru's call, Ia darted out from his hair and settled on his fingertip, asserting its presence with a crackling heat.

Feeling reassured by that heat, Subaru pulled back the canvas of the carriage he'd run to and thrust his face inside; as the light streaming into the darkness opened up his vision,

"—Subaru."

The moment he realized that name had been called by a voice like a silver bell, Subaru was struck by a shock so great he nearly collapsed on the spot.

Ahead of his gaze, staring at Subaru with stunned eyes, was a beautiful girl with silver hair and violet eyes. A girl whose figure he had chased after so many times, wished for so many times, had his hopes broken so many times, and yet still could never give up on.

Overflowing emotion pounded at his chest, and Subaru desperately held back the unbearable surge of feeling threatening to spill from his mouth and the corners of his eyes.

Shaking his head, he cut off that hesitation in an instant,

"Ia! Can you tell!?"

The faint glow residing at his fingertip danced into the air, flitting around inside the carriage as if answering Subaru's call. That fantastical scene of searching soon came to an end.

Ia's trajectory, which had been hovering in search of its target, suddenly wavered, and the spirit drew close to the edge of the carriage, bobbing up and down as if to say there was something there.

Rushing over, Subaru narrowed his eyes and searched for the anomaly at the spot the Minor Spirit indicated. One of the floorboards was slightly discolored, and he could tell that a newer-looking board had been used just there.

"Puck! Can you peel up just this part without damaging the rest!?"

"Showing up out of nowhere and suddenly what... ah, I see how it is."

At Subaru's one-sided voice after so long apart, Puck looked displeased for a moment, but only a moment. Whatever the Minor Spirit had sensed beneath the floor Subaru pointed to, the Great Spirit apparently sensed the same thing.

With a wave of his small hand, a converging torrent of Mana froze one of the floorboards solid. Stomping it in from above, Subaru reached his hand into the hole that formed. And—

"—Found it!"

Pulling a burlap sack out from the bottom of the hole, Subaru hurriedly peered inside.

Just as Otto had said, it was packed tight with magic ore. Faintly glowing, it seemed to be gradually building up heat even now—no, in fact, it was clearly beginning to give off intense heat.

—Perhaps it had a time limit, or perhaps finding and pulling it out had triggered it, or perhaps the impact of the carriage just now had set it off.

Whatever the reason, one thing was certain: the magic ore in his hands was heating up—meaning it had entered a sequence just short of detonation.

"Puck, can we stop this from exploding..."

"Stopping it, no chance. Protecting everyone from it, though—that I could manage."

Puck shook his head at the plea, but hinted at a trump card, assuring Emilia's safety. That surely meant Puck manifesting in his true form and using his overwhelming power to forcibly shield everyone from the blast—a drastic measure. Subaru understood the principle, and in truth, Puck probably could do it. He probably could, but—

"That won't do!"

Shaking his head, Subaru rejected Puck's proposal.

That plan, too, could certainly protect everyone. But if it came to that, everyone here would see the Great Spirit's form, and that strange visage would be carved into all their memories.

Overwhelming power, and Emilia, the Contractor who commanded it. For her, whose relationship with the villagers still bore deep, lingering scars, how profound an impact might that impression have?

He put a hand to his forehead, spinning his thoughts. *Think of something. Come up with some way.* The total volume of magic ore was considerable—if handled poorly, it could reduce this whole area to a burnt wasteland. In a situation on the verge of exploding at any moment, even the option of running far away to discard it was uncertain whether it could be carried out. But leaving everything entirely to Puck would cast a shadow of unease over Emilia's path forward. Even granting that nothing could outweigh a life, it was too soon to throw in the towel before even thinking it through. Whether or not something outweighed a life—that wasn't a judgment Subaru had the right to make. So he thought. For her, for Emilia's sake—this time, in the truest, truest sense, what he could do for her sake.

"—I see."

A murmur slipped out. One single thought had flashed through his mind.

Whether it could actually be carried out was uncertain, a conclusion he could only call absurd. But if there was any possibility at all, this was the only method he could think of, and a miraculous chance of success remained.

The instant the thought struck him, Subaru's body was already moving as if flung into motion.

Hefting up the burlap sack, heavy even to lift with both hands, he tried to leap out of the carriage still carrying it. But to his back, as he did,

"Wait...!"

Emilia had called out to stop him, her voice trembling.

His feet, which shouldn't have stopped, stopped. His body, which shouldn't have turned back, turned back. His eyes, which shouldn't have looked, looked straight at her. In a time with no time to spare for exchanging words, he nearly found himself exchanging words after all.

"Subaru... why...!"

That "why" carried within it every "why" there had ever been, not just this single moment. It was about him rushing in at this very instant, and it was about him having created this situation in the first place, and it surely reached back much, much further than that—it was surely, too, a redo of the exchange from that room in the royal castle.

At that time, Subaru had not been able to give her an answer.

The tangle of unresolved feelings from that time came flooding back. Not one of them, individually, had been wrong. But none of them had been right, either.

It had been a moment where he'd gotten one chance, lost it again, and put it off. Reunited with Emilia, given a chance to exchange words, there had been a mountain of feelings and words he wanted to convey. As many as the Stars. So many that no matter how much he gave, they would never run dry.

Countless, countless words rose to mind, welled up to his lips, and vanished.

Overwhelming emotion, feelings piled up over time—his entire body and soul demanded this moment with everything they had.

What should he talk about, what should he convey?

What words should he choose, what attitude should he face her with?

"Why...?"

The question again.

Taking a small breath, Subaru told her, in just one line.


"—I love you, Emilia."


—That was the one and only meaning behind Subaru living on like this, covered in scars.


※※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※


Saying it in one breath, he burst through the canvas as if tearing it and leapt out of the carriage.

The instant the sunlight seared his eyes and he winced, a black shape stood before Subaru, blocking the light. Patrasche. Before even hearing Subaru's voice, his partner had turned her back to him with a bearing that seemed to sense everything already.

Reaching out, Subaru scrambled up onto Patrasche's back as if leaping. Cradling the heat-filled sack between his own chest and Patrasche's back, he gripped the reins and pulled them taut. The Earth Dragon's whinny, and after a beat, Patrasche took off running. Straight for the sun.

Behind him, Otto's face showed surprise at Subaru's actions. The buzz-cut young man who'd been driving stood stunned. And then, through the canvas, the children—Emilia—burst out.

He could hear a voice. A voice calling his name. He didn't turn back. He couldn't afford to turn back.

The feelings he needed to convey, the words he'd wanted to say—all of it, he had managed to leave behind.

There was nothing left for Subaru to do back there. Now, there was only what needed doing.

Patrasche became the wind. Because of the stop, the Windbreak Protection hadn't deployed, and the shaking and wind assailed Subaru mercilessly, trying to throw him off. But the jet-black Earth Dragon moved with skill to protect her master, and Subaru, too, entrusted his body to her with complete faith.

Tossed about by the shaking and wind, Subaru stared single-mindedly ahead.

*It should be here.* His certainty quickly became fact, and Subaru drove Patrasche toward the thing that came into view.

The heat given off by the magic ore—the ore heating up beyond the burlap sack began to glow red, and the intense heat scorched Subaru's belly and Patrasche's back where he held it clutched. Gritting his teeth, he wrapped his arms deep around it, determined never to drop it. Amid the encroaching pain, Subaru saw it through eyes welling with tears.

—It was the legendary Great Tree, snapped off at its base, lying across a span of long, long years, and lying fallen at its base was the corpse of a massive Witchbeast that had lost its head.

Carrying off the head alone must have taken everything they had. The remaining massive body had been frozen solid with tremendous density to prevent decay, and just approaching it brought a cold so intense that breath turned white in the air.

Driving Patrasche toward that huge mass of flesh, Subaru made for the center of the fallen White Whale's carcass—there, a deep gash existed, made by the sword devil's blade.

"—————!"

Leaping down from Patrasche's back, Subaru shoved the burlap sack he carried into the wound. Even with the frozen body, the gap was large, and more than enough to swallow the sack whole.

Instantly turning, Subaru grabbed the reins of Patrasche as she rushed up and wheeled around at once. Circling around the carcass, he plunged to the far side of the fallen Great Tree.

Dragging Subaru, half-dangling, Patrasche ran across the grassland. He could feel Mana converging behind them, and both Subaru's throat and the Earth Dragon's throat let out a roar.

Only the violent shaking and the roar of wind wrapped the world. Carried along, his body flowing with the motion, he felt the sensation of a collision and realized they'd reached their destination.

His body pressed hard against the trunk of a tree, Patrasche curled her body to cover Subaru's completely from above. —The next instant,

"—————!!"

A violent shock and blast wave, a detonation so loud it deafened the ears, thundered across the highway, and a heat wave leapt over the carcass and the Great Tree, scorching the skin of Subaru and Patrasche as well.

Even past his closed eyelids, a red glow pressed as if to pierce the eyeballs hidden within, and Subaru, gripping tight to the weight pressing down on him, gritted his teeth and endured the agony.

His whole body felt as though it might be swept away by the shockwave, and as he felt the sensation of even that immensely long Great Tree being violently shifted by the force of the explosion, the shock and heat wave eventually began to converge—

"—?"

Realizing that everything he'd been sensing had vanished, Subaru lifted his face in a daze.

He'd meant to speak to confirm it, but his ears rang so terribly he could hear nothing. Nothing reflected in his eyes, which he was sure were open, either—it seemed even his optic nerves had suffered severe damage.

His outstretched hand touched the skin of the Earth Dragon beside him, and through his palm alone came the certainty that her body had indeed moved—somehow, the strength drained from his shoulders in relief.

He couldn't see anything, couldn't hear anything.

But he was alive. And Patrasche, it seemed, was alive too.

He felt his soot-covered face being repeatedly stroked by something damp. He couldn't see, but it might have been Patrasche's tongue. A feeling of fondness, like with a dog, and a fear at the roughness, as if his skin were being sanded, welled up at the same time.

But no voice, no finger, nothing of his would move to stop it.

He was, as expected, utterly exhausted. Because too much—truly, far too much—had piled up all at once.

Maybe it was all right to rest, just a little, now.

He felt his consciousness slowly slipping away, and he let himself surrender to the encroaching darkness.

His vision didn't clear. His body didn't move. His ears, too, still heard nothing.

"—————!"

Only a faint vibration in the air brushed his skin, and his neck, moving somehow, turned toward it.

He couldn't hear anything. He shouldn't have been able to hear anything—and yet, for some reason, he felt terribly, wonderfully at ease.

He couldn't hear anything. Right now, nothing at all—

"—Subaru!!"

Ah, well. —He could hear it after all, couldn't he.


With that sigh of relief as his last, Subaru's consciousness sank down into a deep, deep sleep.
