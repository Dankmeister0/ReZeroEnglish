Arc 10 Chapter 43: "'Shatterer' Clarissa"

—She had always loved the sound of things cracking and breaking.

The thin skin of ice that formed over puddles on mornings when her breath turned white.
The old plate, yellowed in patches, that held her meager meal.
The countless windows that made the streets of her hometown glitter when she looked down on them from the hill.

She would pick up a chip of ice, a shard of pottery, a sliver of window glass, and rub it between her fingers.
With a faint, dry rasp, the fragment would crumble in her hand. Then, as if following its lead, everything like it within sight would crack and break all at once.

The collapse traveled from one end to the other, just like a sandcastle coming down.

She wanted to hear more. More sounds, more things breaking, bigger things.
White stone had made her hometown famous for its stonecutters. It was used in every house, every building. She smuggled a piece out and climbed to the highest spot she could find.

Up on the hill, where she could see her hometown in every direction, she lifted her hand, her heart trembling, and ground the little piece of white stone to dust between her fingers.

—The sound of everything cracking, the sound of everything breaking, made her tremble from head to toe with joy.

Someone's tiresome voice, the hateful ties that bound her to people, the irritation of having to deal with anyone at all. That sound drowned it all out, and none of it reached her ears.
Someone had had something in mind. Someone had worked so hard, put so much of themselves into making something so splendid. And now it was cracking, breaking, crumbling to the ground. She couldn't get enough of it.

So she went wandering in search of the sound. More, more, more and more and more.

She left her hometown a heap of rubble and wreckage. She made heaps of rubble and wreckage wherever she went, looking for the perfect place to make her next heap of rubble and wreckage, walking and walking and walking.
Crack it and break it. Crack it and break it again. Crack it as many times as it took.

She wanted to crack and break something bigger, something prettier. So she pointed a finger at that grand old castle, a castle well worth cracking, and—

"—My, my. Somebody's a naughty little girl, ain't she? That's my castle you're eyeing, and don't you have some rotten manners. Bet you'd be worth teaching a lesson, huh?"

At the sound of that voice, she heard something crack and break very, very close by.
Why was it? She had loved the sound of things cracking and breaking so much. But that one sound, the one she could still hear all the time, made her sick. So sick she couldn't stand it.

And because she wanted to drown it out, she couldn't help cracking and breaking something else.

"—I broke it. ...I was sure I'd broken it."

Clarissa stared wide-eyed, dumbfounded by what lay before her.

The long span of the Milesta Dragonback Bridge had twisted and tilted sharply. It was only a matter of time before it fell. With the end so close, the screaming grew louder, and everyone went on making one wrong choice after another.
People had given up trying to stave off the end. They shrieked and wept and begged for mercy, and to Clarissa their voices were an unbearably wretched noise. —Why did people always want to be near things that were big and beautiful and grand?
That hateful human habit spoiled the sound Clarissa longed for: the stirring sound of something big and beautiful and grand cracking and breaking.

As far as she was concerned, people could stay dead until there was something worth cracking and breaking to build. Then they could stir themselves, swarm together to finish something grand and beautiful, and go right back to being dead.
She wanted them to spend eternity building things for her to break, and whenever she broke one, to build something new out of the rubble and wreckage.

Breaking things that had no shape would never quench Clarissa's thirst.
That was why she'd followed Dandon's instructions when he told her she could break something big and grand. She'd come all this way, even though the orders hadn't come from Mama.

And all because she'd been told she could crack this bridge and break it.

"—!"

Gritting her teeth, Clarissa ignored the slope underfoot and ran to the crack in the roadway, the wound that had made the bridge scream. She stared at the hateful light filling it.
That deep violet glimmer was the trace of a magic arrow fired from the castle that had appeared out of nowhere. The arrows had stained the places they hit violet, plugging the wounds that had opened all over the bridge.
Like the ice a moment ago, it was a makeshift measure to hold the failing bridge together. But there was one crucial difference. —Clarissa had never shattered anything like it.

Stone, glass, iron: she could grind any of them to powder with the fingers of her gauntlets.
But these deep violet crystals, these things made by magic, resisted her fingers. They refused to shatter.
And so—

"I can't... break it...!"

She was so close. Just a little more. But like this she couldn't break the bridge. And she still couldn't drown out the sound in her head, the sound of her own soul creaking and cracking.

The situation had changed so fast that everyone was left reeling with questions.
In a crisis this immediate, it was easy to be swept away by one sudden turn after another. Yet in all that confusion, the first person to move was Felt.

The moment she saw that help from the castle had braced the failing bridge and stopped the roadway's downward tilt, Felt spun around to find the enemy.
She didn't have to look far. The woman hadn't even tried to run. She was on her knees at the crack right in front of her, desperately scratching at the magic light that filled it.
Honestly, Felt had no idea what she was doing. But—

"Beats me what you're doing, but I'm sure as hell not letting you do it!"

Whatever the woman was up to, letting her carry on couldn't do any good.
The green-haired woman's cheek was twitching as she clawed frantically at the roadway. If this was her way of fighting back after her plan had been foiled, she had a reason for it.
Felt kicked off the ground and raised her dagger again to stop her. Just then she heard something whistle through the air behind her head and threw her weight sideways.

"—?!"

A sharp point grazed Felt's cheek and neck and stabbed into the railing. At the crash and the force that seemed to shake the whole bridge, Felt's eyes went wide. She turned to see what had hit it and could hardly believe her eyes.
Standing there was—

"...A huge damn spider?"

An enormous, repulsive black spider loomed in front of Felt as she muttered in disbelief. The thing was the size of a dragon carriage.

"————"

It had eight legs, each thicker than a person's torso and bristling with coarse black hairs that looked as hard as iron. As it bent and straightened its joints, its shell gleamed dully as if it were wet. Its huge jaws worked open and shut, the rows of daggerlike fangs within clacking against each other.

Hideous and gigantic as it was, so far it matched what Felt knew of spiders.
But there was one detail, just one, that put an end to the resemblance. —The eyes.

Two eyes were set in the middle of its round head, above the huge jaws and their forest of drooling fangs. Felt had never stared all that hard at a spider before, but she knew its eyes were supposed to look like solid black lumps.
What she saw there were human eyes. Two human eyeballs.

"That ain't a witchbeast...!"

Faced with a creature this enormous and misshapen, Felt's first thought had been that it might be some unknown witchbeast. The eyes alone were enough to rule out the likeliest explanation.
Something else had made this thing. Something malicious.

"What the hell do you think people are...?!"

Felt was trembling with rage at the thought of what had warped it into that shape. The spider lifted its forelegs and stepped across her path to Clarissa, knocking a stranded dragon carriage clear over. An earth dragon that hadn't escaped let out a ringing cry—

"Shit!"

As if on cue, the spider lunged at Felt, stabbing at her with its pointed legs.
Lift a leg and bring it down, over and over. It was a crude way to fight, but every thrust was sharp and deadly enough to punch a hole in the paving stones. With a size difference like that, Felt had no hope of blocking or parrying.
All she could do was keep leaping backward as the spider pressed forward.

"Get clear! The bridge'll hold for now! Help the people around you and run, run, run!"

Felt shouted at the top of her lungs as she barely evaded the spider's relentless attacks.
She couldn't spare a glance to see how the evacuation was going. But both the spider's eyes were fixed on her, so apparently it had no attention to spare either.
Felt took advantage and focused everything on dodging. Her eyes couldn't keep up. She trusted the sharp prickle on her skin and jumped, twisted, leaned, slid, dodged.

Dodge, dodge, dodge, dodge, dodge, dodge dodge dodge dodge—!

"At this rate—"
"Gwah?!"

Her mind racing, she'd just decided she could do it and was about to push past her limits.
She ran straight into someone standing behind her. The two tangled up and went over. Felt wondered what had cushioned her and found herself wedged against a chest conspicuously large for the nun's habit fluttering around it.

"Filore! Get the hell outta my way!"

"I wasn't in your way! I had just taken a crying little boy back to his mother... Wait, is that a spiiider?! Why is it so big?! I-I-I can't! I've been afraid of spiders since I was little!"

"Funny coincidence. I just started being afraid of 'em myself!"

Filore clutched Felt's head against her chest and hugged her in a vise. While Felt struggled to get free, the spider aimed two legs at their heads. In that instant, Felt sensed its attention split between them, where before it had been entirely fixed on her.
Maybe that was just because it now had two victims within reach. Or maybe it had something to do with the flicker of hatred in those two eyes, so full of human feeling. She couldn't tell.

"—! I'm Felt's big sister—!!"

Before Felt could make sense of it, Filore twisted and pushed her down to shield her from the falling legs.
What an idiot of a sister. If one of them got hurt, both had a better chance of surviving if Filore was left standing to heal them with her Sacrament. She hadn't stopped to think about any of that.
Felt tried to shove her off and spare them both at least some of the damage, but—

"Won't make it—"
"—My apologies for the delay."

The two legs were about to pierce Felt and Filore through the chest and body when a pair of arms shot out, catching them and holding them in midair.
Before their astonished eyes, muscle swelled in the thick arms gripping the spider's legs. Its other six legs came up out of the roadway, and the spider went flying over the railing.

"————!!"

With a screech like nails scraping glass, the spider spun out of sight over the edge. Felt watched it go in a daze, then slowly looked back.
Her eyes met those of the man as he turned to look at her, and—

"M-M-M-Marcos-sama!! You saved us! We barely escaped with our lives!"

"S-so it would seem. I'm glad I made it in time. Are you unharmed as well, Felt-sama?"

"By a hair. Another second and the whole debate about which of us is the real one would've been settled for good."

"Please don't talk like that! We both survived! Can't we both be the real one now?!"

Of course they couldn't, but Felt could see why she'd want to say it and let it pass.
Filore got up first and gave Felt a hand. Once on her feet, Felt looked up at the towering Marcos, who had just saved them both.

"Thanks for the save, but you got time to be dealing with us? Some of those carriages are about to go over..."

"As for those, that person is seeing to them."

"That person? ...The hell is that?"

Felt looked where Marcos pointed and made a face. The large merchant caravan had been in a particularly bad spot, its wheels swallowed by fissures in the damaged bridge. Now people were picking up its earth dragons and dragon carriages with their bare hands and carrying them away as if they weighed nothing.
Unless all those people had suddenly developed tremendous strength, the trick probably lay with the shady figure in the mask and black clothes, hollering at them as if to kick them into gear.

"Ku ha ha ha ha! Come on, hurry! Run! Get yourselves across! Who'd have thought the magic I filched from that pipsqueak 'Librarian' would come in handy? You lot must've done something right for a change!"

For all his high-handed talk, the suspicious stranger was doing his utmost to help people.
His manner made Felt a little uneasy, but with timing like that, he had to be connected to the castle that had brought Subaru and the others. In fact, he was probably with Subaru.

"Feels like big bro's always bailing me out in the weirdest places..."

Come to think of it, he'd been doing it since the Loot House. The 'Bowel Hunter' who'd hired Felt for the theft had tried to kill her there, and Subaru had stepped in just in time.
Emilia had been there too. —But Old Man Rom and Reinhard weren't here.

"...This ain't the time to get all solemn. Stone-Face! That woman over there!"

"Yes, I'm aware. However—"

Felt pointed at the culprit, just visible past the overturned carriage, but Marcos sounded hesitant. She soon saw why. The massive creature he'd thrown over the edge had landed back on the bridge.

"Eek, the big spider...!"

"Tch! Of course the bastard had a thread to climb back up on..."

"—Both of you, please stand back. I'll deal with it."

Felt half stepped in front of a shrinking Filore and glared at the returning spider. Marcos moved in front of them both, holding out an open palm to keep them back.
The sight of that palm made Felt's eyes widen. "Stone-Face...!" It was burned raw and seeping blood.

"How'd that... Was it that bastard's legs?! Poison?!"

"That's what happens when you touch it carelessly, I imagine. Judging by the way the paving is melting, it's highly acidic. I could hardly forgive myself if it damaged either young lady's skin."

"Is this the time?! That stuff could melt you into a puddle too, you know..."

"—Right. I was being too polite. You're in my way. Stay back, you brats."

"M-Marcos-sama?"

Felt recoiled at the sudden roughness, and beside her Filore's cheek twitched. Before them, Marcos clenched his badly injured hand. With a slow grinding sound, rock began to cover it, then his arm, then his shoulder.
And then—

"If you're so proud of being too dangerous to touch, you'd have to be the biggest idiot alive to get in my way. I've had my fill of idiots I can't punch. I'm going to beat you to a pulp."

As he spoke, a ferocious will to fight came over him, and armor of stone spread over his body. He'd already been tall enough that she had to crane her neck. Watching him grow larger and larger, Felt realized there was no point sticking around and grabbed Filore's hand.
"Ehhh?!" Filore yelped as Felt pulled her by the arm and ran down the bridge.

"Stone-Face! The big bastard's yours! We're going this way!"

"B-but, Felt! Marcos-sama! The spider's poison is turning him to stone!"

"Stone-Face is doing that himself!"

"Himself?! Why?!"

Filore looked anxiously over her shoulder as Felt dragged her along. Felt's eyes widened at the question. "Ain't it obvious?" she murmured with a fang-baring grin.

""—Makes it easier to punch.""

Felt's mutter overlapped with the muffled voice from inside the stone helmet, and a heavy foot came down. The spider rained down a barrage of thrusts, but every one glanced off the solid stone armor. Neither the force nor the poison could get through the 'Monolith.'

"'Die' sounds a little rude. —Drop dead!"

He answered the attack by driving a fist like a boulder straight into the spider's face.

—When a castle she had never seen before broke through the river mist and rose into the sky beyond the bridge, everyone around Emilia stared in astonishment. Emilia alone had her eyes fixed on one spot on its terrace.

Of course she'd been surprised to see a castle come flying out of the mist too. She had never imagined a castle could fly, much less why it might. Besides, she was trying to hold up a bridge that could fall any moment. The ice that should have held it together had been shattered, she was all out of options, and her head was a jumble.
But maybe that was exactly why, Emilia thought.

Whenever Emilia was in a really, truly 'big pinch,' it was always her dear knight who came to help her at just the right moment.
So when a flying castle appeared, she was startled, but she knew right away that Subaru had something to do with it. Once she knew that, she only had to look for Subaru, who loved to stand out. That was how Emilia spotted Natsuki Subaru before anyone else while everyone was still gawking at the castle.
Only afterward did it hit her that the castle was flying. "Oh! How's it doing that?"

"—Honestly, you always find a way to surprise me."

Emilia couldn't help smiling at what her knight had done.
Beatrice's yin magic had rained down from the terrace, crystallizing parts of the Milesta Dragonback Bridge just as it was about to fall and holding the whole thing in place by force.
Minya, a yin spell, worked by distorting the passage of time in whatever it struck, making it brittle until it fell to pieces. It was a frightening spell. But Beatrice was a Great Spirit of yin, and in her hands its effects could be turned to uses besides breaking things.
That was how the Dragonback Bridge had been saved when Emilia couldn't save it on her own.

"Now that Subaru and the others are here, the bridge will be all right. What I have to do now is..."

First she had to help the people still calling for rescue and get everyone across. Much as she wanted to see Subaru and Beatrice again, it would have to wait.
So—

"Please stop getting in my way! You might fall too if this keeps up!"

"—Yes, exactly! I was thinking the same thing! You're really in the way! Why don't you give up already instead of carrying on like this, you half-devil?!"

"You shouldn't call people names when you've only just met them!"

As she spoke, Emilia formed twin swords of ice in her hands and sprang at the blonde girl who had appeared out of nowhere to block her way.
The pretty girl, her golden hair cut straight at the shoulders, opened her round eyes wide at Emilia's rebuke and broke into a grin.

More than that, the length of the blades and even the 'design' of the hilts and grips were virtually identical to Emilia's ice swords—

For someone who kept saying she felt the same way, the girl didn't seem to share a single feeling with her. And her movements were impossible to predict. They hardly resembled ordinary swordsmanship. There was something animal about them that made her very hard to fight.
She made weapons of ice in midair and mixed her blade work with magic and kicks. Ice stakes, blades, even footholds of ice: she conjured whatever she wanted, changing it up so wildly that nothing made sense.

"If I could just fill the ruts with ice, I could push the stranded carriages out."

None of the noise around Clarissa had reached her ears.

Tremors and crashes, screams and impacts passed over her head or crawled along the ground, each trying desperately to tip the scales that held the fate of the Milesta Dragonback Bridge.
She could sense that much. —No, that was a lie. Even that was outside Clarissa's world.

All Clarissa wanted in her world were beautiful things that would break, and the sound of breaking them.
She had no need for people or for things that weren't worth breaking. And nothing beautiful should ever be unbreakable. That was why she couldn't let the bridge stand as it was.

The deep violet light that held together the cracked, half-collapsed Dragonback Bridge was beautiful.
Beautiful enough to make Clarissa's heart tremble and groan. That was exactly why the thought that she couldn't break it with her own hands was unbearable.
She scratched desperately at the light, panting, sweat on her forehead. She knew something that could hold a falling bridge in place was tougher than any stone or steel.

One blade would do. Just one knife with magic in it.

"Yes, of course! I was just thinking I wanted to give you a present! If you want something like this, you can have it! Here you go, you cheapskate!"

The ice dagger Luchiereka tossed her with the insult shattered while Clarissa was using it. She took a shard and kept scraping frantically at the deep violet light. Her nails tore off and her fingertips were drenched in blood. And finally, just one little chip came loose, and then—

"—That's enough for now! They told us to come back, and I thought that was a good idea too! So get up and quit dawdling, Clarissa! You dawdler!"

That noise again.
The noise wanted to take Clarissa back with it. It had to be joking. The 'Shatterer' power depended heavily on how far Clarissa was from her target and what she could see. She might be able to crack the crystallized bridge from a distance and bring it down from the shock. —To hell with that.

"I won't... I won't be going."

"—Clarissa?"

"Go back on your own... Please go. I have things I need to... to do."

Perched on the ugly monster's back, Luchiereka's round eyes went rounder still. Then she gave Clarissa the same brilliant smile as ever, like the happiest girl in the world.

"Right! That sounds good to me! I always thought being all alone suited you! Nobody likes you anyway, you nasty, miserable little thing!"

With that parting insult, Luchiereka slapped the spider's back. It sprang over the railing and swung away on a thread it shot into the wall of the gorge.
The empty bed of a dragon carriage shot after it like a cannonball, but one of Luchiereka's ice projectiles knocked it out of the air before it reached the spider. It had been thrown by an ugly, hulking figure clad from head to toe in stone armor.

"Don't come. Don't come any closer. Please."

The people who had been fighting Luchiereka and the spider, the people trying this and that to stop Clarissa, the people scrambling in the grip of the chaos: every one of them was a nuisance.
She wanted to break something beautiful right now. Only that sound could save her. There was a hideous sound inside her, hounding her, giving her nowhere to run.

"This—"

She held the deep violet crystal between the claw tips of her gauntlet. If she rubbed her fingers together and crushed it, the destruction would spread and the Milesta Dragonback Bridge would fall.
She had no need of a plan to get away. Right now all she wanted was to drown out this noise—

"—?"

She tried to rub her fingers together to stop the noise, and couldn't. For some reason her fingers wouldn't move. It wasn't just her fingers. Her arm, her shoulder, her whole body. Worse, the glow of the crystal between her fingers was moving away from her.
The crystal tilted and receded, and she couldn't stop it. Yet she felt no panic. A faint sound had taken up all her attention.

It wasn't the sound of something beautiful cracking and shattering under the 'Shatterer' power.
It wasn't the agonizing sound of Clarissa herself breaking inside.

—It was a sound so clear it was frightening, like a transparent wind brushing the surface of water.

"Shit."

The slightest trace of noise intruded on that clear note.
The crystal continued to recede, and a red-haired man passed at the edge of her vision, his face drawn into a grimace. Even her eyes were no longer hers to direct. The clear sound had come from the man's hand.
A flash of a sword drawn from its sheath. A single stroke honed by relentless practice, driven by sheer tenacity. Then she understood. That stroke had taken her head off.
The man who had beheaded her put the sword that had made that clear sound back in its sheath and muttered—

"She's still a damn kid."

She could pretend she hadn't heard that bit of noise.
Clarissa's body slowly crumpled. Her head rolled away, and she could no longer see the crystal her hand had held. But that was all right. Maybe that was all right.

The sound of something breaking inside her had stopped.
If the last thing she'd heard was that clear, beautiful sound, well, that wasn't so bad, she thought.

Because it had been such a very, very clear and beautiful sound.

—They got the last earth dragon across and made sure nobody was left. Then the magic was released, and the Milesta Dragonback Bridge's three-hundred-year history came to an end.

The moment the deep violet light faded and vanished, the spans it had held in place tilted all at once. An immense mass of stone fell to the bottom of the gorge. The roar came a moment later and shook the road itself. Emilia narrowed her eyes in frustration at the number and height of the columns of water that rose.

"People got hurt, but at least no one died..."

Even that had been a close thing. If one thing had gone differently, so would the outcome. And if it could have gone worse, she thought, it could have gone better too.
There must have been a future where the bridge had survived to add to its long history. Felt would probably tell her that there was no point dwelling on something that had already happened.

"I want things to turn out better next time. So I can't just agree with that right away."

Having made up her mind, Emilia saw a crowd aboard a boat disappearing into the river mist, waving up at her on the cliff. She waved back, finally able to uncurl the fingers she'd held tightly closed for so long.
Among those gathered on the far side of the bridge, voices celebrating their escape mingled with others lamenting lost cargo. As she'd seen, several dragon carriages had overturned as the bridge shook, and many people had been hurt. Filore was rushing from one to the next.
Emilia was about to go and help when—

"—Emilia-tan!"

"————"

At that voice, Emilia turned with a start. The castle hovering over the gorge had brought its terrace alongside the bank. A boy leaned out over the edge, as though he couldn't wait a second longer, and jumped across. He landed neatly and ran straight toward her, still carrying Beatrice in one arm.

"Emilia-sama, Ram will handle things here. Go and quiet that noisy Barusu."

"Ram... Yes! Thank you!"

Ram was as busy as Filore tending the injured. Emilia nodded at her. Beyond her, Felt gave a thumbs-up and grinned, showing her fangs.
Emilia felt a little bad with Felt still separated from Reinhard, but—

"Subaru...!"

Unable to wait any longer, Emilia ran to meet him. They both slowed without thinking when only a single step separated them.
It had been so long since she'd stood face to face with Subaru, and he was exactly as she'd known him. He scratched his cheek with his free hand, still holding Beatrice.

"Sorry I'm late, Emilia-tan. I wanted a ride flashier than a pumpkin carriage, but the castle stalled on me."

"Sorry. I have no idea what you're talking about."

"Honestly, Subaru. And I take offense at you making it sound like Mother's castle was at fault, I suppose. No other castle could have brought us to the bridge this quickly, in fact."

"I know, I know! I'm sorry! I was just embarrassed!"

Beatrice protested from his arms, and Subaru's voice cracked in that familiar way. Even that made Emilia feel more at ease. She tilted her head to look at Beatrice.

"Thank you so, so much, Beatrice. You saved the bridge for us."

"You'd better be grateful, I suppose. Subaru and Betty are completely spent again, in fact."

"One fight after another, and we were running on fumes. Good thing we had just enough left."

"Mm, really. ...Really."

"Emilia-tan?"

Subaru gave her a bashful smile. Emilia nodded and tried to go on, but somehow she couldn't get the words out. Subaru looked puzzled too.

"...Huh? I wonder why. I'm being silly."

The corners of Subaru's eyes crinkled in the smile Emilia knew so well. She'd had so much to tell him, ever so much, and none of it would come out.
For some reason, it felt as though her heart was too full.

"Well, um, you see..."

"Take your time, Emilia-tan. I don't know how else to put it, but I've been two months without my Emilia-tan, and I'm getting pretty dizzy from Emiliase deficiency. That kind of shortage takes a while to make up, so get ready to lose some sleep keeping me company while I talk your ear off."

"I suppose. There's only so much you can do to make up for Emiliase with Beatromin, in fact."

"Hehe. Really? Is that so? Then, yes, I think I might have been a little short on Subaru too."

Once she said it aloud, she knew there was no 'might' about it. Of course she'd missed everybody terribly while they were apart. But with Subaru standing right in front of her, she was sure. Being without him had hurt.
So—

"Let's take turns. I've got a lot to say too, just like you. How about we go one at a time?"

"Yeah, I'd love that. ...I'm really glad you're safe."

In that last quiet remark, Emilia felt she'd caught a glimpse of Subaru with his guard down. A faint breath escaped her lips and she reached for his cheek. She felt that if she didn't catch him like that, he'd hide the look on his face in another moment.
She always wanted Subaru to smile, but knowing there were faces he tried to hide from her made Emilia feel so very—right then—

"—Ah."

The instant her fingertips touched Subaru's cheek, something deep in her chest tightened like a drawstring.
She reached for Subaru's clothes without thinking, but her hand seized the front of her own dress instead. She couldn't even draw a breath to find out what was happening. She stared at Subaru in front of her.

"...Emilia?"

There was no pain or grief on his face, and Emilia felt relieved. That meant she was the only one who felt that terrible pressure in her chest.

"I'm—"

*I'm all right.* That was what she'd meant to say, but she couldn't.
They'd finally found each other. She hadn't wanted him to look at her like that. A soundless groan of pain escaped her lips, and her knees gave way.
Subaru's arm shot out to catch her. At that instant the strain deep in her chest flared hotter still, and Emilia's consciousness went white, white.

"Emilia?! Emilia! No, this can't be happening, Emilia—!!"

Subaru's voice sounded far away.
He was right there. They were touching. Yet he seemed so very far away, and she felt so alone.

She was so happy to see him and so lonely at how far away he felt that tears spilled from her eyes.
By the time those tears fell, Emilia was already unconscious.
