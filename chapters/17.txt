Arc 1 Chapter 16: "Negotiations in the Slums"

Back on the shopping street, Subaru was already hard at work hunting down the mystery fake Satella.

That said, all he could really do was stare bug-eyed at the crowd and glare. In the third loop, he'd run into her not far from the vegetable stand that served as his starting point, so for the time being he'd staked out that area as his fishing hole.

Though the increasingly sour look on the vegetable vendor's face, visible at the edge of his vision, was starting to bother him.

"Sorry things got off on the wrong foot last time. ...But I know you're actually a kind-hearted guy deep down. I know it!"

He threw up a thumbs-up and a grin at the scowling man, who finally looked away with an expression that said he'd just seen something he'd rather not have. Feeling vaguely dejected, Subaru lowered his thumb and turned his attention back to the street.

As ever, the shopping street was a sardine-packed mob of people from every race under the sun. Amid the endless variety of fantasy-world inhabitants, Subaru finally spotted what could only be called a "beastfolk" — some kind of ridiculously fluffy, two-legged dog-like race — and his excitement shot straight to MAX. He wanted to pet it. He wanted to pet it *so bad*.

"What is that overwhelming loveliness... Born into the winning team from the start, that's what that is. Reinhard, fake Satella, and now this — the world's just sinning left and right."

Short and toddling along on unsteady legs, the creature radiated an almost small-animal cuteness. Honestly, it was borderline arrestable for crimes against fairy-tale charm.

"Guh... down, my right arm... down! This is no time to go fluffy-crazy, have you forgotten the mission!?"

He managed to restrain the arm that had started reaching for that white fur, and watched the retreating back with no small amount of regret before finally dragging his gaze back to his post.

Even so, more than ten minutes had passed since he'd started his stakeout of the shopping street, and there was still no sign of anyone matching her description. He checked the time on his phone — nearly an hour had passed since this loop of the world began.

"My sense of time's kind of off, but if memory serves, the theft should've happened by now, right...?"

The moment the words left his mouth, an unpleasant premonition brushed across the back of his mind.

Once that kind of *what if* chill sets in, there's no escaping it. Subaru scratched his head, let out a string of "Aah!"s and "Ugh!"s, and then reluctantly turned his feet toward the stall in front of him.

"Hey, old man."

"What do you want, broke boy."

The vegetable vendor made no effort to hide his displeasure as Subaru showed his face again.

Even though it was technically true, it stung to hear it put so bluntly. Still, Subaru pressed on undeterred.

"I mean, yeah, 'broke' is accurate, can't argue with that... Anyway, old man, quick question — has there been any kind of pickpocketing commotion around here recently?"

"You've got some nerve asking questions without buying anything. ...And besides, sorry to say, that kind of commotion's nothing rare around here."

"Seriously!?"

Subaru couldn't help but yelp in surprise at the shopkeeper's spiteful answer. Though, thinking about it, it wasn't entirely implausible. The Loot House, after all, was where stolen goods from all over the royal capital ended up. A place that vast, filled to a reasonable degree with loot — you could guess the general state of public safety from that alone.

"Guess that's a dead end for information, huh...?"

"—Though, come to think of it, the commotion earlier was unusual enough that I remember it. A couple magic blasts went off right on the street. Look."

The vendor leaned forward and pointed off to the left, toward a stall about four storefronts down. Subaru followed the gesture.

"Whoa, that's nuts."

"Ice pillars, flew like arrows and stuck right in. Melted away pretty quick, though."

Right beside the stall, in the wall by the entrance to a narrow alley, there were holes bored clean through.

Four of them, each roughly the size of a five-hundred-yen coin. Punching holes through a stone wall like that — the sheer force and speed behind it was enough to send a chill down Subaru's spine just imagining what it would feel like to be hit.

"That's a whole different league from just chucking ice shards around... Is she going all-out this time, the fake Satella?"

If he mishandled the approach, he himself might end up on the receiving end of that. Just thinking about it made a cold sweat bead up on his forehead.

"Man, but this means I'm behind again this time."

Setting aside that frightening mental image, Subaru muttered to himself while wiping the sweat from his brow.

Those magic scorch marks were almost certainly the fake Satella's doing. And if there was a theft incident tied to her, it was safe to conclude that Felt had made off with the insignia.

Which meant the option of linking up with the fake Satella from this end was gone.

At least, in Subaru's experience, there was no reliable way to catch up with her.

So the priority now had to be —

"Linking up with Felt while she's still got the insignia. Ideally I'd catch her before she gets to the Loot House and trade the phone for the insignia right there."

Having been brutally murdered there twice already, Subaru's honest feeling was that he wanted to avoid the Loot House altogether. Strictly speaking, what he wanted to avoid wasn't the Loot House itself, but the moments when Elsa was involved with it.

"Go too late and it's a repeat of round one. But if I team up with Old Man Rom and wait for Felt, I'll just be retracing round two step by step..."

In the end, the key variable was Felt's location.

Right now, she was presumably running around desperately trying to shake off the fake Satella on her tail. She surely couldn't be running the entire time, so there had to be some point where he could catch her — but for someone with a far worse sense of the royal capital's geography than the average person, that was easier said than done.

"If it's a 'Return by Death' situation... should I just treat this loop as a throwaway and spend it gathering intel...?"

It seemed like a viable strategy on paper, but Subaru shook his head and rejected the idea immediately.

For one thing, having already died three times, he couldn't imagine himself ever being able to make the decision to die on purpose just to reset. Dying, all three times, had been agonizing in the most literal sense of the word. If he could avoid experiencing it again, he wanted to save that particular sensation for the very end of his life, on his deathbed.

And besides, there was still the nagging uncertainty about the actual range and limits of "Return by Death." Treating a loop as disposable and ending up with zero lives left wouldn't even make for a decent punchline.

"In the end, as long as I'm alive, all I can do is struggle as hard as I can. Yeah, obviously."

He twisted at the waist, ground through a stretch, then bent his knees deeply right there on the spot as a warm-up.

To the shopkeeper, who looked distinctly unimpressed at having radio calisthenics performed in front of his stall, Subaru raised a hand and gave a light jog in place, body now nice and warm.

"Well then, thanks for the help, old man. I'm off for real this time. Next time, I swear I'll buy some Appa off you."

"Ah, if you're buying, then you're a customer. That I'll welcome. Now get to work, penniless."

"Yes sir, yes sir. —Next time, I really do hope I'll have money on me."

Seen off with a half-hearted wave, Subaru took off running.

His destination: the slums — though a different direction from the Loot House. The BAD flag waiting at the Loot House had already been established by his earlier reasoning. So, better to attack from another angle.

"Felt's den, huh. In that case, it's two streets over that way."

"Thanks, man. You're a lifesaver, bro."

"Don't mention it, bro. —And, well, y'know... stay strong out there, yeah?"

With that, the man gave a pained smile and vanished behind a creaking door, his middle-aged face disappearing from view.

Recalling how that strained smile had never once lost its tinge of pity throughout, Subaru quietly clenched a fist in satisfaction — his strategy was working.

Caked in mud and dust, looking pathetic enough that even the slum-dwellers pitied him.

"Figured this out from my first two runs through the slums... and yeah, it's working like a charm."

As he spoke, Subaru brushed at the sleeve of his tracksuit, stiff and crusted with dried mud.

Ever since arriving in the slums, the brilliant scheme Subaru had come up with in the process of tracking down Felt's whereabouts was this pitiful-looking performance.

Back in the first loop, when he'd gone around the slums with the fake Satella and gotten absolutely wrecked for no good reason, the residents' response toward him had been fairly sympathetic and cooperative.

In the second loop, when he hadn't taken any particular damage, their reaction had been cold and curt by comparison — night and day. Remembering that contrast, this time he'd gone out of his way to look a bit over-the-top pathetic.

"Well, I did step in some unidentifiable animal poop along the way."

He shuddered retroactively at the thought of how close he'd come to actually rolling in it.

Losing only a pair of sneakers to the cause, with his dignity — mostly — intact, was probably the best he could hope for.

Unfortunately, that was purely Subaru's own assessment; in his mud-caked state, there wasn't really any dignity left to speak of.

"That makes four sources now... and so far, three of them line up."

Out of sympathy for Subaru, he'd been given four pieces of information total. So far, three of them agreed on where Felt's usual haunt was. Unless the residents were somehow conspiring together to set him up, it was safe to assume the information was more or less accurate.

The one outlier had come from a rather sadly hard-of-hearing old woman, so there was surely no intent to deceive there. Maybe.

"So now I've got a bead on her sleeping spot, or den, or whatever... The question is whether she'll actually come back to it."

He'd secured information on Felt's usual residence, but given that she was currently on the run, whether she'd retreat back to it was pretty much a coin flip. If she was worried about her pursuer tracking her location, she might actually avoid her own den on purpose. —In that case, there wasn't much to say except that his luck had run out.

"No use worrying about it. Don't stress over questions with no answer. Onward."

No point agonizing over things there was no point agonizing over. Subaru's decisiveness shone through even here.

Knocking clumps of dried mud off himself, Subaru broke into a light jog deeper into the slums. The first time he'd come here, he hadn't even managed to ask around properly — he hadn't even been able to get out from behind the fake Satella. He'd come a long way since then. He kind of wanted to pat himself on the back.

"A reward for all my hard work... guess I'll crack open another corn potage, hm."

He dug a hand into the plastic bag, rummaging through its contents, just as he approached a street crossing. Just then, a figure suddenly emerged from around the corner, and he nearly collided with them.

He flinched out of the way in a panic, his back slamming into the narrow alley wall. He let out an involuntary "Ugh!" as the air was knocked out of him. Watching him, the person he'd almost run into made an unhurried gesture and said,

"Oh, my. Sorry about that. Are you all right?"

"Fine, fine, totally fine. Believe it or not, being tough is my whole th—guh!"

Trying to save face, Subaru lifted his head to confirm who it was — and his voice cracked mid-sentence. Hearing that high-pitched squeak, the black-haired woman let out a small "Fufu."

"You're an amusing one. So, really, are you all right?"

She brushed back the hair falling across her ear — a simple enough gesture, and yet somehow sensual, and Subaru thought to himself, not for the first time, that every single one of her movements was absurdly erotic.

A beautiful woman whose every gesture dripped with sultry charm — the absolute last person on earth he wanted to run into again.

The one who had twice now spilled Subaru's guts across the ground — Elsa — stood before him.

"—You don't need to be so frightened, you know. I'm not going to do anything."

"S-scared, who's scared, I'm not scared at all, y'know? What makes you think you get to slap the 'scaredy-cat' label on me huh? Seriously, that's low, that really gets me down, y'know, scared, pfft—"

"You smell..."

Ignoring Subaru's bluster, Elsa narrowed her eyes into a lovely, languid smile. As Subaru cocked his head at "smell?", she gave her shapely nose a small sniff.

"When someone's afraid, they give off a certain scent. You're afraid, right now. ...And also — angry. At me."

She said it almost gleefully, exposing his inner thoughts, peering up at him from beneath her lashes. Subaru answered her with a silent, forced smile, taking deep breaths in an effort to still his racing heart.

What she said was the truth.

Subaru was afraid of her. Right now, in this entire world, no one terrified him more than the woman standing in front of him.

And also — no one in this entire world did he hate more than her.

If he could beat her senseless, he genuinely wanted to. And well before that thought, what he wanted more than anything was to run from this spot immediately.

She was the one who had killed him — twice. Who could possibly laugh at Subaru for trembling down to his very core?

Elsa's eyes narrowed like a snake's at Subaru's silence. Pinned by that gaze, he still refused to show the weakness of looking away.

She wet her lips with her tongue at his bravado, and said,

"...It's a bit tempting, but never mind. I can't afford to cause a scene right now."

"Th-that's a real ominous thing to say. Keep talking like that and you'll ruin the whole beautiful-woman thing you've got going, y'know?"

"Oh, flattery. —It'd be even better if you could actually hide your hostility."

A finger extended and gave his forehead a light push, and with just that touch, the rigid tension in his body released. As Subaru exhaled and let his shoulders drop, Elsa pressed the finger that had touched him to her own lips.

"Well then, if you'll excuse me. I have a feeling we'll meet again."

"Next time, somewhere bright with lots of people around — I could relax a bit more then."

That parting jab was the best he could manage.

Leaving behind only a faint, troubled smile, Elsa turned with a swirl of her black cloak and melted into the darkness of the alley. Watching her vanish, quite literally, Subaru sagged against the wall, hit by a sudden wave of exhaustion.

"Whoa... talk about an unexpected reunion, seriously. She was prowling around this area even before the Loot House thing...?"

The unforeseen encounter had nearly broken his spirit.

In terms of sheer mental preparation required, she was an even bigger shock to Subaru's system than the fake Satella. If at all possible, he wanted this to be the last time he ever crossed paths with her.

"Felt's den should be up ahead somewhere, but... there's no way Elsa's already been through here and torn the place up, right...?"

She was a maniac who got off on slicing people open.

It wouldn't be at all strange for her to carve up two or three people just to kill time before some meeting. And with the location being deep in the slums no less, the ugly imaginings just kept piling up.

"I-it's fine, probably. Didn't smell blood, didn't see any blood spatter on her either. Maybe."

Between the garbage and the general stench of the slums, he couldn't have picked out the smell of blood anyway, and it had been too dark to have any confidence about spotting bloodstains — but he was pretty sure there hadn't been any. He wanted to believe there hadn't been any.

He couldn't quite muster the courage to break into a run, so he resumed his advance at a cautious, creeping pace instead — yelping "Hii" or "Wahyah" at every buzzing insect or half-empty liquor bottle he came across.

He eventually arrived at a filthy little shack about five minutes after his encounter with Elsa.

"According to my info this should be it, but... this is where a person actually lives?"

Standing in front of a wooden board that seemed to serve as a door, Subaru couldn't help but tilt his head. The shack in front of him was roughly the size of two portable construction-site toilets put together. The kind of place where you could stand in half a tatami mat's worth of space and lie down in one full mat's worth.

"Well, if this is what they call a 'den,' I guess it checks out, but..."

Still, the thought of a small girl like her actually living here struck him with a pang of pity. It almost made him feel like he could forgive her money-grubbing, upwardly-mobile ambition.

"Living in a place like this, making her already tiny body even smaller just to get by. No wonder her personality got all twisted up. Ah, no wonder. Poor thing. Ah, poor, poor thing."

"That's laying it on way too thick, you creep. What's with that face, staring at someone's bed like that, huh, mister?"

Right as he'd fully sunk into pity-plus-resignation mode, a voice called out from behind him, and he turned around.

There, glaring at him with narrowed, suspicious eyes, stood a small blonde girl — Felt.

Her clothes and posture looked the same as before. If anything, her usual grubby outfit looked even grubbier than usual — a sign of just how brutal this round's chase must have been.

"Why're you looking even more pathetic-sorry for me now? You think I'm some pitiful little dirt-poor brat you can look down on?"

"Nah, this is a different flavor of sentimental... anyway, more than anything, I'm just glad I found you."

At the girl's blatant, undisguised displeasure, Subaru let his shoulders sag in relief.

Finding Felt, the person he'd been looking for — that alone was honestly something to celebrate. After that near-miss with Elsa, he'd been worried how things would turn out, but as it happened, things were off to a decent start.

At Subaru's muttering, she gave a snort and said, "Huh, a customer?"

She kept her guard up, eyes fixed on him.

"You came all the way here, so you must want somethin' from me, huh? ...And from the looks of you, you're not from around here."

"Oh? Not lumping me in with the locals — you've got a good eye."

"Even the folks around here keep themselves cleaner than that. You're overdoing the filthy look way too hard. Honestly, right now you're grubbier than my own crew. Grubbier than me, even."

*Man, this brat never lets up*, Subaru thought, already regretting the sympathy he'd just felt for her. But ignoring the vein throbbing on his forehead, she took a step back and said, "So? "

"What's the job? If it's a heist, I want payment up front. Depending on the target, I might charge extra."

"'Heist request'... you're running quite the shady little business. Proud of your sticky fingers, huh?"

"It's a survival thing. Without this, I'd have nothin' left to sell but my body. So, what's it gonna be? Or is it something else? Depending on what it is..."

Felt wiggled her fingers in quick little movements, showing off her dexterity.

In her palm, as if by magic, a small knife suddenly appeared. A warning, no doubt, that if it came down to it, she'd use it to defend herself.

And in truth, if it ever came to a real fight with her, the moment she had a blade in hand, Subaru had no chance of winning. Lose sight of her thanks to her speed, and next thing he knew, he'd be collecting the "Stabbed to Death in a Back Alley, BAD End 2" route.


"I've got one request. —I want to buy back the insignia you stole."
