Arc 9 Chapter 59: "—Not"


—I love you.


△▼△▼△▼△


I let out a deep breath, and deep in my heart, I drew back the bowstring of resolve.

"—"

The girls I'd just spoken with — Petra's consideration especially — had left an ache in my chest. Even Meili, with her contrary, mischievous little-devil act, and businesslike Flam, had, in their own ways, clearly been looking out for me. Beatrice chief among them — quite the fine assembly of young girls.

"Guess that's what you'd call a proper 'Little Girl Handler.' Not much of an honor for either side, that."

Whatever it meant when the name first stuck, Petra and the others wouldn't stay children forever. But so long as he kept eternally-young Beatrice at his side, that epithet would never peel away from him. Of course, that would be a different story if he and Beatrice ever had a falling-out—

"If that were even possible."

This plan, in all likelihood, could have been carried out at a far lower difficulty.

In fact, aside from Beatrice, I'd never once come across a great spirit of the shadow element friendly toward humans. That alone made me suspect she'd been deliberately designed that way — though what someone in the lineage of the "Witches," someone other than the "Witch" I knew, had intended by it, I couldn't say.

Either way, Beatrice — an expert in the shadow element, her very origins bound up in it — posed the risk of unraveling factors essential to the plan. That was why timing mattered.

Beatrice, too — bound soul-to-soul with her contractor through their Contract — would have to be drawn in by the Forbidden Arts.

"—"

I could feel, with a creaking strain, the bowstring drawing taut.

And so, into a sight that overwhelmed me no matter how many times I saw it — shelves packed with countless books filling the vast floor of the "Taygeta" archive — I stepped, and watched.

"Murak."

With the full support of a great spirit, and maximum effect wrung from a short incantation, the man hopped up onto a tall bookshelf and quietly tucked a single book away there.

"—"

Even from a distance I could tell it was a book, but I couldn't make out whose name was written as its title.

Every "Book of the Dead" in this archive looked identical apart from its title — no difference even in thickness. Odd, when you thought about it: a child who fell ill and died at five, and someone who lived a hundred turbulent years before a peaceful end — there was no way both should come out the same thickness of book.

No doubt this archive made use of the properties of Od Lagna, but it must have been the "Witch" who arranged for it to be output in the form of books.

So then, why had the "Witch" gone out of her way to make such a hard-to-navigate archive?

"Because you wouldn't want to muddy that once-in-a-lifetime encounter, the moment you pick one up, with needless noise, would you?"

It was the sort of thing the "Witch" might say. Having voiced it myself, it started to feel like the right answer after all, and that left a sour taste in my mouth.

I'd been trying, as much as I could, to avoid even thinking of the "Witch" in the first place. But — ever since she died, as if in reaction to all that restraint, I found myself remembering her.

'—No one can beat you, the one I made, you know.'

For a moment, I thought: if this archive housed the books recording the lives of the dead, then naturally, the "Witch's" book, too, must be somewhere in here.

And then—

'—There, you see? Victory is mine again.'

The voice I'd covered my ears against, averted my eyes from, refused with my very soul and pushed away — its owner's "Book of the Dead" was here too, no doubt.

"...What was it you won, Priscilla?"

In that unforgettable last exchange, our final exchange, there had been a triumphant smile on her face. I still didn't understand what that declaration of victory, spoken alongside that smile, had meant.

Not that understanding it now would change anything. She was already gone. And with the dead, words and feelings could no longer be exchanged.

But this Great Library, Pleiades — even if it could only go one way, it made understanding possible.

"...Am I an idiot? No — I am an idiot. That's nothing but an excuse, and I know it."

All of it was for the plan — a cover story prepared to make those men console a heartbroken me, so as to bring to fruition the scheme with the "Witch" that I'd once abandoned.

To have my heart genuinely shaken by my own ploy — that went well past the mummy hunter turning into a mummy himself.

More than anything—

"Don't save me."

If I read that book — if I let myself be scorched once more by the brilliance of that sun which had already set — I might waver again, just as I had the day I gave up being a "Star That Chases After."

A foolish star unworthy of being saved — becoming one again.

So—

"—The star was to blame."

With those words, I resigned myself, and resumed my stalled footsteps, advancing through the archive.

The single book hidden quietly atop the shelf — whose title I hadn't been able to see — but whose name it bore, I knew without needing to look.

If there was a "Book of the Dead" that man would sneak around hiding because it was inconvenient for him, there was only one title it could bear. —And finding that book, too, was another piece of the puzzle this plan required.

The two books I'd wanted to find — my own "Book of the Dead" and that man's — had both been located.

Then all that remained was the moment to act—

"Geez. You'd think you were some mischievous kid causing trouble in a library..."

Grumbling that, I walked slowly up behind the man scratching his head. Unaware of my approach, he stepped away from the shelf where he'd just hidden the book, muttering—

"It'd be bad if that curiosity-monster Ezzo-san found it... Actually, come to think, more and more people might keep coming through here — guess I gotta dispose of all of them? How many are there in total—"

"—Dispose of what, exactly, bro?"

"—!"

The instant I called out to that muttering back, the man's shoulders jumped theatrically. Turning to face me slowly, his expression was faintly stiff — it was written all over him that he'd been up to something he felt guilty about.

At his reaction — so bad at hiding things — I made a point of giving an exaggerated shrug.

"Whoa, hey, easy there. Way too jumpy. I was just making small talk."

"...Ah, sorry, my bad. Getting caught talking to yourself is pretty embarrassing, y'know?"

"Can't say I don't get the feeling, but don't go making me the bad guy over it."

"Bad guy, huh..."

As he tilted his head, I jerked my chin, drawing his attention behind him. From that direction came Beatrice, her cute little footsteps pattering along.

With her small body, she wedged herself between the two of us, puffing out her cheeks.

"Hey, you — don't go startling Subaru out of nowhere, I suppose. When Betty's not looking, is Subaru the type with a flea's heart, I wonder."

"A flea's heart... bro, seriously? I don't figure he's got that kind of nerve."

"If that's a rating based on how thick-skinned or shameless I am, I'll take it, but what exactly have you seen of me to think that?"

"—Well, who knows. Maybe I actually know a lot more about you than you think, bro."

At the man's words, said while patting the head of Beatrice as she shielded me, I thought it over a moment before settling on a safe reply.

If I said I knew more than what had just been brought up here — even things like the subtle rifts with his parents from before he came to this world, or his feelings of inferiority — what would he think?

Of course, I wouldn't do anything so foolish. I'd been driven by that impulsive urge more than once, but each time, I'd fiddled with the fittings of my helmet and held myself back.

It was my ritual for when I needed to think — a metallic clink to warn myself not to forget the plan.

"That sounded awfully suggestive, I suppose. Saying you know so much about Subaru, right in front of Betty — you must be underestimating the sweet relationship between a spirit and her contractor."

"Sweet, is it. Geez, my bad for interrupting such a red-hot honeymoon."

As I clicked at the joints of my helmet, Beatrice, arms crossed, looked thoroughly cross. I shrugged, careful not to sour her mood further, and the man scratched at his cheek awkwardly with a finger.

"Nah, don't worry about it. Bea-ko and I get lovey-dovey whenever time allows anyway. Right now, well, y'know — the archive here's the top priority, right?"

"Thanks for the thoughtfulness on every count. No, seriously, joking aside, let me thank you properly again. Even just three days must've been rough, carving out time for my sake. And Bea-ko-chan, sorry for dragging you along too, bro. Thanks for coming with him."

"Betty and Subaru are two halves of one, so of course we'd be together, I suppose. —Also, Betty isn't merciful enough to keep giving the same warning over and over, so mend your ways yourself, I suppose."

"Got it, got it. —She's a kind one, your girl."

"Mm, that she is."

The man gave an oddly halting, wry smile, and Beatrice quietly took his hand in hers, gentle.

Somehow, the conversation wasn't flowing. Calling it "quick on the uptake" would suggest we were well-matched, which isn't a thought I relish, but even so, our back-and-forth should have had a decent rhythm. For it to grow this stilted, he must have felt he'd been caught at something seriously inconvenient. —Or else, the book he'd hidden bore a different title than the one I'd expected.

For instance, one of the two books I'd just been picturing a moment ago.

"Al, so, uh — how's it going?"

"—. What's with that question, like some parent of a teenager who can't figure out how close to get. ...As you can see, no results yet. I figured three days might do it, but I'm genuinely at a loss here. With this many books, isn't this basically a needle in a desert, for real?"

"—! But don't give up, okay? Let's keep looking properly! I'll help however I can!"

"Whoa, hey, bro?"

No sooner had I said that than the man leaned sharply forward with such force that I instinctively reeled back.

He'd sworn it to me again and again, that unwavering sincerity of his, that he'd help. I didn't think it was a lie, nor did the promise itself irritate me. —After all, I wasn't expecting anything.

The thing I'd most wanted, most hoped he'd do for me, was never done.

So—

"Cut it out with that intensity, seriously — I can't even tell anymore which of us wanted to come here more. If I don't get just as sweaty and worked up as you, bro, this whole thing stops making sense."

"Then be it. Let's both be it, desperately. Three days — let's go all out and see it through. That's..."

His voice shook. —Not mine; I heard the tremor in his voice.

Maybe he'd gotten too caught up in it emotionally, or maybe a wave that should have already passed had rolled back in, and the feeling had resurfaced. Regret, remorse — he felt them more keenly than most. That much, I understood.

That was the Curse called Authority — a thing only the two of us, myself and this man, possessed in all the world.

There, simply, stood a man who could use it to save the one he loved, and I, who could not.

And if that man's love were allowed to go on, unchecked, it would eventually give rise to — a being that would end the world.

To stop that, at the very least, I had my duty as the "Star That Chases After"—

"—Subaru."

Beatrice, her hand joined with the man's as he trembled and hung his head, called that name quietly.

The feeling packed into that call was hushed and still — a mystical solemnity, at odds with her adorable appearance, that testified she too was a great spirit who had lived through long ages.

"—"

At the great spirit's call, the man himself held his breath, unable to speak.

At his reaction, fiddling once more with the joints of my helmet, I decided. —After one or two more exchanges in this back-and-forth, I would make my move.

Just as he'd said, the period allotted to search the archive in this tower was three days. The more that time ran out, the more his mind would start turning to other matters. So, before that happened, I had to act.

"—Three days — is that not enough?"

The man's words sounded wrung out, and for some reason, like he was clinging to something — proof, perhaps, that I too had grown nervous. —I found myself, for a moment, actually considering it.

Three days, searching for the "Book of the Dead" just as he'd suggested — and suppose it were found, and I came to know, without a single thing left out, the feelings that woman who was gone had left behind at the very end — what then?

I can say this with certainty. —Nothing would change.

This wound wouldn't heal. The pain wouldn't vanish. And in the first place, that wasn't even what I wanted.

So, to the question he'd thrown at me, knowing full well my answer would land wide of the mark, I deliberately answered like this.

"—The star was to blame."

Even I couldn't say whether I meant that star with eyes of affirmation or condemnation.

Setting the two stars present here against each other — Pleiades and Aldebaran, no, Subaru and Rigel — even I didn't know whom I was blaming as at fault, and whom I was consoling as blameless.

"—"

I'd let myself get too sentimental. This time, I was just as bad at the conversational back-and-forth as he was.

Keep exchanging words in that mood, and I risked getting dragged along into saying something that would raise suspicion myself. I'd already decided. Next — I'd make my move on the next exchange. That was settled.

The man who had fallen silent — who bore the name of a star, who gave a star's name — once he finished saying his next words, I would strike. —I ran mana through my Gate, wove the formula, and opened the gate of the Forbidden Arts.

And then—

"—Al."

And my eyes met Natsuki Subaru's, his face on the verge of tears.

"—Ol Shamak."

It wasn't me who'd chanted it. —I understood.

Aldebaran had lost, once again, a battle he absolutely had to win.


'—There, you see? Victory is mine again.'

I heard the beloved woman's voice, and her triumphant smile that could never be recovered again. —In the next instant, Aldebaran's world went dark.

"—Natsuki-dono, I doubt this will be any comfort, but your concerns were spot on. He was gathering mana, weaving a formula. Had a single move gone differently, the tables would have turned. I stand fully behind you — your judgment was the best one to make."

The words of the Dwarf who'd watched the whole affair unfold — Ezzo Cadner — were, just as he himself admitted, no help at all in comforting Subaru's heart.

Surely there had been some other way, some solution that could have settled things without hurting anyone.

"Boss, I don't get it, not one bit. But — but, y'know! Even me, who don't understand a damn thing — I can say this much! ...I'm just glad you're safe, Boss."

His little brother figure, who — like Ezzo — had been given the role of watching over the situation from outside and must have felt just as frustrated and helpless, said this while wrapping a sturdy arm around Subaru's shoulders in his usual straightforward way.

And he wasn't the only one standing close by Subaru like that.

"Subaru made a proper decision, I suppose. ...Even though Betty could have taken it all on in his place instead, and now he's hurting for it — what a fool, I suppose."

"Beatrice..."

"But that Forbidden Art — Betty and Subaru did it together, I suppose. Subaru prepared the formula, Betty built the spell, Subaru chanted it, and Betty ran the mana through. So this is split fifty-fifty with Betty, I suppose. Taking on more than your share would be cheating, I suppose."

"—!"

Subaru ground his teeth, and his right hand — fingers clenched so tight they wouldn't open again — gripped, with a force that his own inadequacy had left as the only option, a black orb.

—A magic orb that held Al sealed within it, the only way there'd been to stop him.

"—Sorry. There's somewhere I have to go."

Feeling every organ inside his chest, inside his whole body, tremble, Subaru — who had been on his knees — rose to his feet and slowly left the "Taygeta" archive.

With Beatrice's hand in his, and Garfiel and Ezzo — both worried — in tow, Subaru scolded his legs, which threatened to give out at any moment, into moving, made his way down the corridor, and arrived at the room he was aiming for.

And then—

"Ohh? What's up, mister? What about your search in the archive?"

Spotting Subaru appear in the doorway, Meili, perched on a barrel in the corner of the room, tilted her head along with the little crimson scorpion sitting on top of it.

The sight of her, safe and every bit as cheekily impudent as ever, struck something in his chest — but more than that—

"—Subaru, what's wrong? Did you forget something?"

At Meili's call, the girl who'd had her back turned looked around. She'd been putting away the pot used for dinner, and now her round eyes, reflecting Subaru, blinked at him.

"—Ah."

At the sight of that girl right in front of him, Subaru's mind and body froze, unbidden.

She looked a little puzzled for a moment, then straightened her bent knees at once, saying, "Subaru?" And she stepped up to meet Subaru face-to-face as he tottered forward on unsteady legs.

"Beatrice-chan, did something happen? Subaru looks kind of..."

"Something did happen, I suppose. But Betty won't be the one to say what, I suppose. ...It just seems like, right now, he wanted to see Petra something fierce, I suppose."

"Me?"

Blinking, the girl — Petra — showed surprise at Beatrice's answer. Watching that rich, shifting range of expression up close, Subaru slowly felt the stiffness in his own cheeks begin to loosen.

With Meili behind him letting out a suggestive little "Hmm?" of a sigh, Subaru gazed at Petra standing face-to-face with him, hesitated a moment, and then—

"Petra, this is gonna sound weird, but... can I hug you?"

"—. You could've just hugged me without saying anything, you know. Ah, no, wait, that's no good either. Just thinking about it makes my heart pound so hard it might brea—mmph."

Before Petra, smiling mischievously, could finish her gentle reply, Subaru had already reached out, pulled her body close, and folded her into his arms.

"Um, uh..."

Petra couldn't hide her bewilderment at the sudden embrace. For a girl who had, without anyone quite noticing, grown thoroughly steady-nerved and increasingly unshakable, it was an unusually plain display of fluster.

But not a single person present was in any position to tease her about it.

Because—

"Hey, Subaru, are you okay?"

Gently, a hand reaching up from within his embrace touched Subaru's cheek, soft.

At such close range, right in front of him — a distance so near their breath mingled, closer still, where even their heartbeats seemed to collide — the girl's question made Subaru catch his breath, and he forced a smile.

"Of course I'm—"

—of course he was fine. Of course. Because here Petra was, safe and well.

Not just Petra. Beatrice, Garfiel, Meili, Ezzo, Flam too — none of them were hurt. Everyone in the tower, and everyone who hadn't been able to come to the tower, was safe.

Every bad thing that had happened had been made into something that never happened at all.

Of course, he didn't know everything that had happened, and he also knew that not everything that had happened had been bad.

There had been conflict and reconciliation, betrayal and friendship, parting and reunion, curses and vows, hatred and love — there had been Aldebaran, and there had been Natsuki Subaru.

"—Maybe... I'm not okay."

Holding Petra close, gazing at the magic orb that would not leave his hand, the words slipped out, quiet.

And once that weak admission had spilled from his lips, it couldn't stop at just a strained, cracking voice. Drop by drop, his true feelings turned to tears and began spilling from the corners of his eyes as well.

"—Not."

He'd wanted to stand tall and say he was fine, to repay Petra and the others — the ones who'd sent Subaru back through "Return by Death," who'd let him take back so much that should have been irretrievable, who had accepted a world left behind in exchange.

He couldn't do it. It was too sad, too frustrating; his own inadequacy was too pathetic. He couldn't stand tall.

"I'm not... okay...!"

To Petra, who had offered up so much as a price; to Rem, who was supposed to have gotten everything back; to Emilia, who had refused to give up on talking things through with Al right down to the very last moment; to Beatrice, who — even while sealed away together with him — had deciphered the Forbidden Arts formula and given Subaru, who kept returning by death, a trump card to hold; to everyone who had taken part in that battle, the ones Subaru knew and the ones he didn't, every single one of them—

And last, to Al, with whom he hadn't even managed to create a chance to talk—

"I'm sor—"

"—Subaru!"

The sudden, high, sharp voice cut off the apology he'd been about to speak.

It was Petra's voice, peering up at Subaru from where he'd swallowed his words against her. Her round eyes trembled with some huge, huge emotion, and as she kept her hands cupped to Subaru's face — tears spilling down it in a steady stream — she said,

"You finally said it. That you're not okay."

"—Ah."

"You don't have to be scared like that. You don't have to carry it all by yourself. Right, Beatrice-chan?"

"...Of course, I suppose. Betty is Subaru's partner, I suppose."

Prompted by Petra, Beatrice clung tight to Subaru's body. To Subaru, bewildered by the warmth of the two girls, Petra smiled on and said,

"Meili-chan."

"Eeh? I'm good, really. You and Petra-chan are plenty..."

"Meili-chan."

"...Fiiine."

Meili hopped down off the barrel with a sulky look, radiating reluctance from every inch of her, and came to stand beside Subaru, giving the hem of his clothes a light pinch. As if standing in for its mistress, the little crimson scorpion that darted out of her hair climbed onto Subaru's shoulder and nuzzled its body against his cheek.

"Garf-san."

"Ohh! Boss!"

"Gwoh!?"

The force of him leaping in, as if he'd been waiting for this moment, slammed into Subaru's back, wringing an involuntary groan out of him. Desperately he planted his feet, holding fast against toppling forward and crushing Petra — and he held. He held, but—

"Ezzo-san, Flam-chan, are you coming too?"

"I'm an outsider here; I'll pass. Though I am on Natsuki-dono's side."

"I've only just arrived, so I haven't quite grasped the situation, but... very well."

A presence that had appeared only just now came, without hesitation, to nestle gently against his right side, near the hip.

With that, the omnidirectional, inescapable embrace formation was complete — swarmed by all that warmth, Subaru let out an involuntary breath, a huff.

"What even is this."

From an outside view — and indeed, there was Ezzo's outside view right there — what a ridiculous state he and everyone must look like right now.

All bundled up together in one fluffy clump like this — it was ridiculous.

"It's fine! It was Subaru who showed me I'd been the foolish one, after all."

"Did something like that happen?"

"It happened."

"...Out of everyone I know, there's probably nobody smarter than Petra."

That's what he thought. Surely that was true. Which was why someone as clever as her could see right through everything. Amazing. This was what being truly clever actually meant. A world apart from a clever fool like Abel.

"I've decided."

His face was still a soggy mess of tears, he was a wreck, hugged from every side by everyone's kindness, and he'd only just admitted he wasn't the least bit okay — but he'd decided.

"Al, seems like you hated me enough that you couldn't forgive me, but—"

The real reason behind it, he didn't know. But not knowing wasn't going to be the end of it.

So many things had been on the verge of ending, and so much will, refusing to let them end, had kept them from ending — and so Natsuki Subaru, who was not okay in the slightest, had made up his mind.

And that was—

"—I swear I'll save you too, no matter what."

That was the vow of Natsuki Subaru — who had used "Return by Death" to undo everything, good and bad alike, bundled together as one — even if fulfilling it meant an act violent enough to turn the whole world against him.

Hearing that vow from Natsuki Subaru, Petra Leyte nodded, satisfied, and said,

"That's my Subaru!"

—Yes, the girl who never became the "Witch of Melancholy" smiled.
