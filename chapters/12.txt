Arc 1 Chapter 11: "A Resistance Too Late"

The keen tip of the glass held a bead of blood upon it, and tracing that blood back to its source led to old man Rom's throat.
His arm lost, his throat torn open, old man Rom spewed a great froth of blood from his mouth, the light fading from his gray eyes as he collapsed to the ground.
His convulsing body already held no strength left in it, and there was no doubting that life had left him.

Toward the great fallen body, Elsa bowed elegantly, as if paying her respects.
At the feet of old man Rom, still faintly trembling, she set down the glass that had served as her final murder weapon.

"I'll return this to you. You won't be needing it anymore."

She said it with cold cruelty, spinning the kukri knife in her hand.
She flicked the blood staining the blade red, and once more turned its point toward the two of them.

Subaru, collapsed where he sat, had no words.
He was utterly consumed, his consciousness swallowed by the merciless slaughter that had just unfolded before his eyes.
Someone he'd been exchanging words with only minutes before was dead. And not through accident or illness, but taken by another's plain and deliberate malice.

"—My, you're the braver one, aren't you."

Unable to move, Subaru raised his head at the sound of Elsa's admiring voice.
His limbs would not obey his will. Before him, dazed and vacant, stood Felt—who had struck her own trembling knees as if to rally herself, and risen to her feet.
She straightened her legs, smoothing back her blood-soaked golden hair.

"You've got some nerve, doing that to him..."

Subaru, behind her, couldn't see her expression.
But the wrung-out resentment in her voice was, without question, not the voice of tears.

"Fighting back any more might just get you hurt."

"Like you weren't gonna kill me even if I didn't fight back, you damn sadist."

"If you move around, my hand might slip. I'm not very good with blades, you know."

Elsa spun the knife in her hand with practiced skill, running through a rehearsal of how she'd slice a fish for namasu.
Felt, in contrast, stood empty-handed—there was no way she could win.

Subaru's mind had reached its conclusion: he should speak.
He should call out, draw Elsa's attention to himself, and buy Felt time to flee.
At the very least, buy her time to call for someone—or time enough for her alone to escape.

Analyzing the gap in strength between them with what little experience he had, Subaru had arrived at that conclusion.
And yet his throat, as if frozen solid, produced no sound.
No fighting spirit reached his limbs; they only rattled, trembling like something gripped by cowardice.

"...Sorry for dragging you into this."

"...I—I'm—"

What Felt tossed to the immobile Subaru were small, thin words of apology.
Hearing them, Subaru jerked his head up as if struck, and—forgetting even the cry he should have given—let slip only the beginnings of a whimpering plea for forgiveness.

And Felt left that sentimentality of Subaru's behind forever, and broke into a run.

The sound of her footfall rang out sharp and high, her leaping body light as a feather.
Felt took off—and in the next instant, a veritable gale tore through the storehouse.

In the instant after that burst of speed, to Subaru's eyes, Felt seemed to simply vanish.
She disappeared from his sight, and the next place her figure appeared was right beside Elsa—her slender leg buried in the side of Elsa, whose eyes had widened at Felt's speed.

Landing the blow, Felt's body sprang back like a rebound, catching the wind once more at top speed.
Within the confined space of the storehouse, treating even the walls as ground, her movements were erratic and tremendous. Even Elsa could not help but marvel at such acrobatic technique.

"Divine Protection of Wind. Ah, how lovely. The world loves you, doesn't it. —How I envy you."

Her smile, brimming with rapture, flipped in an instant to something else—a pitch-black hatred lodging in her eyes, and Elsa's arm whipped out with a hiss.
That alone was enough.

"—Ah."

—Felt, cut cleanly from shoulder in midair, tumbled across the ground without even a chance to break her fall.

The wound ran from her left shoulder clear through to her right side, deep enough to sever bone and reach her internal organs.
From her body, collapsed face-up, blood spurted like a fountain in time with her pulse; consciousness had likely already left her from the pain and shock of the blow, for she did not so much as twitch.
Within a few seconds the blood lost its force, and in silence that too made plain the end of her life.

His body would not move.
He wanted to go to fallen Felt's side, to stanch her wound.
If that was already far too late, he wanted at least to close those open eyelids for her.
Even that, his body refused him—it had become nothing but a useless machine, pumping blood through his whole body along with pain, and nothing more.

"The old man and the girl have both fallen, and yet you don't move. Have you given up already?"

She said it in a voice almost pitying, looking at the dazed, vacant Subaru with something like boredom.
Approach him, one flash of the knife, and it would all be over. Perhaps because she could already see that outcome, there was not a shred of tension in her bearing—if anything, there was something in her manner like she was stifling a yawn.

At that attitude of Elsa's, Subaru felt an anger he could not suppress.
It had been barely an hour since they'd met—a bond of scarcely more than that.
But it was someone he'd talked with, shared some measure of his feelings with, however small. And that she could kill them so easily and feel not a shred of remorse for it—that was unforgivable.
And above all, what he could not forgive, more than anything, was himself—who had stood by and let two people die before a foe he should have spat on.

Anger, too late in coming, brought the drive to move back into Subaru's limbs.
He planted his trembling arms and legs on the ground, and began, somehow, in a beastlike posture, the sequence of standing up. Whether the trembling of his body came from anger or fear, or both, he could not say.

"Ah, finally standing, are you. Slow, and dull, but not bad."

Baring his teeth, Subaru threw himself with everything he had at Elsa, who stood ready with her kukri knife.
He leapt, meaning to beat her down with an arm swung past its limits.
That desperate charge—

"But not nearly enough."

—was met head-on and struck down by an elbow strike that crushed straight into the bridge of his nose.
Turning her body, she drove her elbow home with minimal motion, and as Subaru reeled backward, her long leg arced through the air to strike him directly, sending his body flying effortlessly backward.
He crashed into a shelf of scattered pottery, scattering shards as he fell.

In that single instant of exchange alone, his nose and front teeth were ruined. His side, struck directly by the kick, ached beyond reason, and he could feel that several ribs had been taken from him.
Even so, he slammed his fist into the ground and rose again without a beat's pause. Endorphins coursed through his whole body, refusing to let him register pain he'd never known before.
Riding the ragged breath of his excited state, Subaru charged again, thoughtless as before—and was struck down again.

His flailing arm never reached Elsa; her whipping arm, knife reversed to its blunt spine, shattered his shoulder.
As if his groan of pain were merely an annoyance, she kicked him straight up under the chin, forcibly cutting off his cry; his broken front teeth spilled out, and Elsa looked down at Subaru as he crumpled.

"Utterly hopeless. Just as you look—an amateur, sloppy in your movements. No Divine Protection, no technique, and I'd thought perhaps you might at least have some cunning, but no, not even that. Whatever makes you keep challenging me, I wonder."

"Shu' up... I got my pride... how many times ya gotta beat me down..."

With his nose broken, he couldn't even properly string together a curse.
His arm had been wrecked in that last counter, hanging limp and useless from the left shoulder down. He felt no pain, but the ringing in his ears was terrible. Letting fury and the nausea that had returned spill freely from his mouth, he rose unsteadily to his feet once more.
His body was battered from head to toe. He had zero chance of winning, and even the chance of landing a single blow in return was one in ten thousand.
With not even a sliver of hope in sight, still Subaru rose—and at that, Elsa let out a small sigh.

"I'll give you credit for sheer grit, at least. If only you'd shown it a little sooner, things might have gone a bit differently for these two."

With the knife dangling in one hand, Elsa gestured toward the two corpses she had cut down.
Following the motion of its tip, Subaru's eyes fell upon the two bodies, and his brow furrowed at a sudden, inexplicable sense of wrongness.

Why was it—this sight felt like something he'd seen before?

The storehouse turned into a sea of blood. The great body, one arm lost, throat severed, fallen. And in the dim, light-drained room, a bronze-red blade dully gleaming—.
A thought flashed through Subaru's mind like a bolt of lightning. It was—

"Let's finish this. I'll introduce you to the angels."

Licking her red lips with her tongue, her bewitching smile dissolved into the darkness.
At her footwork, which seemed to sink into shadow itself, Subaru's throat let out a groan as he lost track of the threat.

"Wh-where is she—!?"

His eyes darted restlessly about, nerves straining toward every sound and presence, trying to read her next move.
It was, in truth, nothing but the pitiful spectacle of a weakling waiting to be hunted by a beast of prey. To Elsa, it must have looked almost disappointingly defenseless—like a carp laid out limp on the cutting board.
And so, her strike, emerging as if slipping free of the shadows, came in a clean, straight line—

"Wha—!?"

Subaru, having judged her aim to be his stomach, managed to dodge with a hair's breadth to spare.

He leapt back a short distance, pulling his body away and drawing in his stomach, reducing the horizontal slash to a mere graze. The skin of his belly split thin, and a sharp pain shot through him, which he endured through gritted teeth on sheer nerve alone.

"Rrrrrraaaaaaaaaahhh!!"

And then, a full-force roundhouse kick struck Elsa's face dead-on from the side.
A twist of the hips, a clean solid hit—the tremendous impact through his foot sent a surge of certainty through his chest: *I got one in on her.* And then—

"Ah, that one, I really felt that."

The second kukri knife, drawn from Elsa's hip, had already carved open some seventy percent of Subaru's torso, spilling blood and viscera out of him.

"—Ah?"

One step, two steps, he staggered forward unsteadily, hit the wall shoulder-first, and slid down in a collapse. Looking down at himself, blood poured endlessly from his abdomen, and unable to withstand the pressure, his insides threatened to spill out entirely.
With a trembling arm, he tried to push his innards back into his stomach, but was thwarted by the surging mass of blood clots blocking the way.

"Surprised? I opened your stomach as we passed each other. This much, at least, I'm quite good at."

Saying this with a laugh, Elsa walked across the sea of blood, footsteps dripping with wet sound.
She came to the side of Subaru, who could only let out groans of pain with no words left, and gazed with rapt, loving adoration at the organs spilling into the pitch-black blood.

"Ah, just as I thought— your intestines are such a beautiful color."

This woman was deranged. Out of her mind.
His vision blurred with agony so intense not even the endorphins could mask it, and before he knew it, Subaru's body had toppled sideways onto the floor.
In that position, his trembling arm slowly extended, weakly grasping at Elsa's foot before him.

"Ngh... ah..."

"Does it hurt? Does it ache? Is it painful? Is it sad? Do you want to die?"

Even with her ankle held, Elsa bent her knee and brought her eyes level with Subaru's.
Her eyes were filled with rapture; at the very moment of reaping a human life, she felt no emotion at all about it. No—she did feel something.
Nothing less than pure happiness.

"But not so easily."

Smiling a bewitching smile, still crouched, she swung her murderous blade.
And that was the last sight Subaru ever saw.

The slash ran through cleanly, cutting across his face in a graze. The result—

"————————————gyaaaaaaahhh!?"

—was that both his eyelids were sliced open, and he lost his sight forever.

Collapsed on the ground, Subaru touched a hand to his deeply cut eyes.
Blood and tears mingled together; from his mouth, screaming, he vomited blood without end, and in his stomach he was gripped by a sense of hollowness, as if every last drop of blood and every organ had spilled entirely out of him.

A state where it was strange to still be alive. A state where being alive was hell itself.
Unable even to see his own state, in a dying condition where he had no idea when death would finally come.

"Slowly, slowly, slowly, slowly, slowly, writhe."

As if tormenting him, as if savoring him, as if mourning him, as if cherishing him, as if showing him tenderness, Elsa's voice gently struck against Subaru's fading eardrums.

Pain, suffering, anger, sorrow—all of it was simply painted over by a pitch-black terror.
In a world where sight was gone, a world where he had no idea when the flame of his life would go out, what ruled over Subaru's now-hollow heart was nothing but the relentless, oncoming terror of death.

When would he die? When would he die? Was he still alive? Wasn't he already dead?
What defines life? Could a state lower than an insect's even be called living? Could having his life and death toyed with in another's palm be called living?
What even is life and death? Why is dying so frightening? Is living necessary? Or not?
Scared scared scared scared scared scared scared scared scared scared scared scared scared scared scared scared scared scared.

An endless, surging wave—an instinctual, absolute rejection of death.
It filled up the mind of Subaru, who had already all but reached the end, and his shut-off vision was dyed pure white, and—

—Ah, I died.

With that thought as his last, Natsuki Subaru's life was snuffed out, just like that.
