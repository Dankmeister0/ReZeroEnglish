Arc 4 Chapter 124B: "The You Reflected in the Mirror"

Facing the witch staring back at her from within the mirror, Emilia let out a breath.

Echidna, the "Witch of Greed," clothed head to toe in nothing but black and white.
Having found Echidna within this dream world that recreated her own room, Emilia truly felt that this place had been born from within her own mind.

A gentle world where the peaceful, tranquil days spent in the forest simply went on and on.
A world where Fortuna, Juse, Archi, and everyone in the forest could spend their days laughing together.

"But... a world like that doesn't exist anywhere, does it..."

"Indeed. This is a temporary world, shaped from your memories and wishes. That said, the world-building power of the spell that governs the Trial surpasses human comprehension. The people living in this world are exactly as they would have been, breathing and alive just like this, if only a single button had been fastened differently."

The truth of the day the Elior Forest froze over—the truth Emilia had only just remembered.
If the disaster of that day had never happened, if the forest had gone on enjoying its peace uninterrupted, could everyone have greeted this very day with smiles?
The image of Fortuna and Juse sitting side by side at the table, so close and affectionate, was burned into her eyes.

That was nothing less than the very scene that the young Emilia of that final day—and the present Emilia, her memories now restored—had wished from the bottom of her heart to see.

"Having seen this now that could never have been, didn't you want to sink into this world?"

As though peering into the depths of Emilia's heart, Echidna cast out her sweet temptation.
Echidna watched Emilia raise her head with eyes as cold as her voice. Smoothing her snow-white hair, letting it fall down her back, she went on,

"Your mother, and her husband. Seeing the two of them so happy, didn't you wish they could stay just like that forever? Didn't you dream of spending your days finding the sight of the forest's people, the warmth of the friends who cared for you, heartwarming things to behold?"

"...What are you trying to say?"

"It's little more than jealousy, really. The fact that you found me means you've already given your answer to the sight of this world. And I already know—tediously enough—that your answer is to choose reality over the dream. If the flow of things was going to end in a foregone conclusion anyway, I thought I'd leave at least a small mark of my own."

"—————"

"You choose the reality in which your mother and the people close to you met unhappy ends, over their happiness. In the end, that is the result of the Trial: you are a shameless woman who puts herself before all else."

Echidna's scathing criticism became a sharp spear, driving straight into Emilia's chest.
The words cut with an edge sharp enough to conjure the illusion of real pain, and though no actual blade had touched her, Emilia pressed a hand to her chest and took an unconscious step back.
At Emilia's reaction, Echidna gave a small huff through her shapely nose.

"It's good to see some self-awareness taking root. Besides, the Trial doesn't take a challenger's character into account. So long as one is qualified, they are accepted equally, whether they're a person of broken character or a mass of egotism and self-love. Rest easy—your goal will soon be realized."

"You really... go for the sore spot, don't you. Are you like this with everyone?"

"Hardly."

At Emilia's words, spoken to cover her discomfort, Echidna shrugged.

"There are only two other people in this world I bear such ill will toward."

"Being one of only three people chosen in the entire world doesn't make me happy at all. ...I don't recall talking to you enough for you to dislike me this much, though."

"You needn't look so anxious. My dislike of you has nothing to do with your being a Half-Elf. I don't question the rights or wrongs of your birth. Regardless of blood or nature, I simply dislike you. ...No, perhaps that's not quite right either."

"—?"

Echidna bowed her head, an expression of unease crossing her face at the latter half of her own remark. Emilia furrowed her brow at the witch, who seemed lost in thought over something, and gave a small shake of her head.
She couldn't simply slink away with everything left unanswered.
More than anything, there was much in Echidna's words that had to be denied—not for Emilia's own sake, but for the honor of everyone in the forest.

"I think it's all right if you dislike me. Being liked by absolutely everyone is a hard thing—I understand that well enough. I've been told I'm disliked plenty of times before."

"All the more reason you should have known your place and stayed shut away in the forest."

"I can't do that. I already said as much in the last Trial. I'm going to melt the ice in the forest and save everyone. And then I'm going to tell them proudly that the world has become a place that's easier to walk through."

""Easier to walk through"—now that's a bold lie to tell. Discrimination between races remains deep, and people's hearts do not easily accept those different from themselves. That is precisely why a place like the Sanctuary has never lost its purpose. The world's victims of the sort of friction you speak of only go on increasing, endlessly. Am I wrong?"

"...You're not wrong."

At Echidna's fierce words, Emilia nearly looked down.
Emilia's slender body still remembered the days she'd spent in the forest with only Puck—still remembered the time when she'd been feared by the nearby villages, subjected to no small amount of malice and abuse.
From Echidna's merciless manner, Emilia was reminded of those days. However hard she tried not to remember, the wound never healed over its scab, and went on insisting upon its pain.

"But I'm going to try to make it different from here on."

"—————"

Still conscious of that wound's pain, Emilia argued back at Echidna in a firm tone.
Watching Echidna narrow her eyes, Emilia bit her lip and put strength into her gaze.

"Being different from others sometimes creates painful friction. Whether you're part of the majority or the minority can, at times, be a major factor dividing victim from aggressor."

"Looking at history, that has repeated itself over and over. People cannot accept what differs from themselves. And a difference in numbers is, as it stands, a difference in power. The many persecute the few. So what will you do, having understood the principle and grown a little wiser? Gather the few together and create a paradise for the weak? Isn't that exactly what a place like the Sanctuary is, at its very core?"

"That's... one option, I think. But I want to choose a different path. Even if what happened—being a victim, being an aggressor—can't be changed, the future can be different, can't it?"

The instant the word "future" left Emilia's mouth, Echidna's expression froze.
To Emilia, it looked exactly like anger—as though Echidna, of all people, did not want to hear that word from Emilia's mouth in particular. And yet, Emilia continued regardless.

"As I go forward through the Royal Selection, I'm sure I'll do all sorts of things. I might be showered with more malice and abuse than ever before. But I want to keep saying it, without ever stopping: what's so wrong about being different from someone else? Is it really such a frightening thing, that the person standing next to you is different from you?"

"I'd rather not repeat myself, but that is the principle at work. People cannot accept the differences between themselves and others. At their core, living creatures want others to be the same as themselves. To like the same things, to love the same things, to hate the same things, to loathe the same things—that is what brings them a sense of security, and what lets them feel affection for what they can understand. Your argument will be cast aside, dismissed as the ravings of the weak."

"That's just refusing to think! It's uncool!"

"Uncool...?"

At Emilia's raised voice, Echidna's eyes widened, her face that of someone confronted with the unexpected.
And to the faltering Echidna, Emilia declared, "That's right!" and thrust out her chest with pride.

""The person next to me is different, so I don't like them"—that's uncool. That's something a child would say. I can't stand it, having my ears shut off for a reason like that. To someone that stubborn, I'll say it as many times as it takes. That it's easier to stop and think a little, just to shut up this girl who keeps saying the same thing over and over, than it is to stop thinking and just scream that you don't like it."

"How self-centered. What self-deception. You'd cast aside another's wish not to listen, just to force your own opinion on them?"

"I wouldn't cast it aside. Whether they take their hands off their ears is up to them. —I'm just confident about which of us, them or me, is more stubborn."

With her hands on her hips, Emilia declared to Echidna that her resolve would not bend.
Hearing this, Echidna made a face like she'd bitten into something bitter, and looked away from Emilia. Then,

"Whatever your argument, there's no mistaking that the world hasn't changed yet. The people of the forest, trapped in the ice—even supposing their lives remain, even if you free them into a thawed world, that world isn't ready to welcome them. All you'd be doing is throwing the people who were kind to you out into rough seas. For the sake of your own self-righteous feelings."

"............"

"You want to free your people as soon as possible. But if you free them, they'll be tormented by the wall of the world's rejection. To live is suffering, and to die is suffering. How will you handle a world like that, with nothing but your own resolve? How will you change it? How will it change?"

That was, in its truest sense, a question from Echidna to Emilia.
Through the two Trials—the past, and the impossible present—Echidna had confirmed Emilia's resolve. And now, with words, she was asking what lay beyond that resolve.

The vision that lay beyond carrying that will through.
The path to reach the future she envisioned.
The concrete grounds for what she would build that road upon.

To that question, Emilia nodded, and said,

"I'll think about that after the Trial is over!"

"—Huh?"

"It'd be putting the cart before the horse if I got so caught up thinking about what comes after that I neglected what's right under my feet. I know it's a strange thing to say about myself, but I'm clumsy. If there's a wall right in front of me that I have to get over, and I try to do something while worrying about what's beyond it, I'm pretty sure I'll fall into a hole right in front of the wall."

Through the Trials, and through her argument with Subaru, Emilia believed she had learned to look at herself fairly objectively.
And she believed she'd given herself an honest, unsparing assessment just now, too.

She wasn't skilled enough to take on many things alone.
Only by throwing everything she had, working desperately at what was right in front of her, might she manage to produce a result at all.

There was hope for the future; there was a vision.
But first, she had to decide to aim for it, and take the very first step toward that aim.
What she had to carve out here was that first step.

"...I'm reminded, rather belatedly, of the futility of arguing with you. It was quite a foolish thing for me to do."

"I know you're smart, but I really think it's kind of unfair to shut down someone's opinion like that."

"Do you think this was an exchange of opinions? In response to the problem I raised, you did nothing but line up pretty words. I'd forgotten—that you're a hopeless child, a weak woman who can't stand on her own and relies entirely on those around her."

"That's true... I think I am a weak girl."

At the fierce words, Emilia lowered her eyes and gave a small shake of her head.
But she soon raised her face again, and looking back at Echidna, went on, "But—"

"Is being weak really such a bad thing?"

"...What?"

"The person who taught me something important would surely say it like this: it's not that being weak is bad. What's bad is trying to stay weak."

In her mind's eye, she pictured a black-haired boy with a bad look in his eyes.
A boy who grieved his own powerlessness, but who—because he was so kind-hearted—ran himself ragged so that he could be hurt more than anyone else. A boy who mattered to her.

That person, who borrowed everyone's strength and yet still stood in the hardest place of all—he would surely answer just like that.

"That's just brazening it out."

"Yeah. I was slow to brazen it out."

At Emilia, who even wore something like a smile now, Echidna realized this time, truly, that there was no room left for argument.
Echidna had no way to stop Emilia, who had gone past merely positive and was now leaning forward, headlong.
Any further interference than this, moreover, would touch upon her own pride as a Witch.

"...Do enjoy the rest of the Trials, at least. Once they're done, a reality far harsher than the Trials awaits you. There, you'll come to realize just how difficult it is to keep those pretty words you've lined up."

"Thanks for that. I'll remember your words properly. And..."

She was, most likely, on the verge of vanishing from within the mirror.
Watching Echidna's reflection in the mirror's surface begin to blur, Emilia went on. Echidna knit her brow in the world beyond the mirror. To that sour face, Emilia said,

"Thank you for showing me this world."

"—————"

"This might be a world that could never have been, but it's still, without a doubt, the world I wanted to see. I never thought there'd be a day I could see the two of them laughing together side by side like that—Mother, and... Father Juse. So, thank you."

Being told outright that it was a world that would never come to be—there was a keen, aching feeling in that.
But even if it was an impossible world, Emilia had seen the sight that should have been possible.
What had been there was certain happiness, and surely love—and it was so joyful and so sad that it made her tremble.

She could think, from the bottom of her heart, that she was glad to have encountered this sight.

"...You..."

That was the gratitude Emilia conveyed with all those feelings—and upon hearing it, Echidna's expression changed.
It was unlike any of the many expressions she'd shown so far—not the face from before, as though she'd seen something distasteful; not the manner of someone enduring displeasure as she spoke; not the contemptuous bearing of someone looking down on Emilia's actions.

—Echidna was simply looking at Emilia with a face on the verge of tears.

"Echidna...?"

"I hate you. —I simply hate you."

Not responding to the call of her name, Echidna, head bowed, answered in a strained, wrung-out voice.
With that, the reflection in the mirror's surface warped, and the white-haired witch's figure vanished from within the mirror in the space of a blink. In her place, what the mirror now showed was a single girl with long, flowing silver hair—

"—!"

A feeling of revulsion pierced through her chest, and Emilia instinctively tore her gaze from the mirror.
Her pulse quickened, her breath growing faintly ragged.

She was supposed to have made up her mind, and yet it terrified her, seeing herself reflected in the mirror before her.

"—————"

Emilia, sealed away in ice together with the Elior Forest, and rescued at last by Puck's hand after a hundred years had passed—she had never once seen her own grown form in a mirror.

The reason was simple. She was simply afraid.

Having spent a hundred years asleep, the girl named Emilia had her heart remain childlike while only her body grew into that of a woman.
When her consciousness returned and she first realized she couldn't properly control her own body, Emilia was struck by the illusion that her body wasn't her own, and spent night after night in tears.

What spurred on that trauma of hers was the reaction of the people living in the nearby villages.
The villagers feared Emilia, who shared the same physical features as the "Witch of Envy," as though she were a demon. Even once they realized she meant them no harm, their attitude of keeping their distance never changed.

Once it became clear beyond doubt that Emilia would do nothing, what awaited her instead were days of persecution soaked in abuse and malice. Through it all, it was carved into her—at least somewhere in her unconscious—that the reason they loathed and reviled her was because her appearance was the same as the Witch's.

It was, properly speaking, from that point on that she began refusing mirrors, behaving so as never to lay eyes on the form of herself that everyone around her hated.
Puck, noticing Emilia's psychological trauma, eliminated one by one anything that might reflect her form. Even when drawing water from a water source, he would go so far as to keep talking to her, so her attention would never turn toward the water's surface.

—The clause in her Contract with Puck, the one stating that Puck would take charge of Emilia's daily grooming, had in truth probably been there to protect Emilia in just this way.
To protect his beloved daughter, who couldn't bear to look in a mirror, Puck had concealed her trauma under the guise of a Contract.

"...I really was protected by so many people, wasn't I."

How many feelings had she failed to notice, sulking there all on her own?
So the time in which she could remain ignorant of all she'd been given—that was over now.

"————"

She drew in a breath, and held it.
And then Emilia raised her face, and with what was, for her, a momentous resolve, glared straight at the mirror.

Reflected in the mirror's surface was a girl with long, flowing silver hair and violet eyes.
With a face as though greeting the end of the world, she stared hard, glaring right back.

"—Huh."

A voice of deflated anticlimax slipped out.
Looking at her own greatly grown self within the mirror, Emilia let the words spill out like a sigh.

"...Too bad, I don't look as much like Mother Fortuna as I thought I would."

The moment after she murmured this, sounding a little sulky, the world shattered into pieces with a sound.

A dream world—happy, one she didn't want to let go of, but one she had to part with all the same—came to an end—.

"—Ah, hff."

Coming back to consciousness, Emilia realized she'd fallen asleep leaning half her body against the wall.
She'd sat down on the floor, legs to one side, leaning fully on the wall where Subaru's carved message remained. Smoothing down what was probably messy hair with her hand, she pictured, behind her eyelids, the last self she'd seen.

So that was the appearance that so many people feared as a "Witch"—and that Subaru, at every opportunity, called "cute" or said he "loved."
Which was the correct way to see it, Emilia—who understood little about beauty and ugliness—couldn't say.
Still, Mother Fortuna was, in Emilia's mind, the very symbol of the most beautiful, most wonderful person there was. So she didn't think having a bad look in one's eyes was a bad thing—and in truth, she didn't dislike the bad look in Subaru's eyes either.

"This is no time to be thinking weird thoughts, right after getting back."

Putting a hand to her cheek, Emilia put a stop to her own train of thought.
She was far too spineless, whatever the excuse. Just because she'd safely finished a Trial and made it back, just because she'd gotten to see Subaru's handwritten message—how giddy could she possibly get?

"But... that just now was the end of the second Trial, right?"

Murmuring to no one in particular, Emilia rose to her feet and turned her thoughts over the question of success or failure.
Judging by Echidna's manner as they parted, the Trial was probably over. Unlike the first Trial, though, she had no firm sense within herself of having overcome it.
Even so, it was certain that she'd torn her heart free from what had nearly ensnared it, and made it back.

"—————"

Fortuna and Juse. Recalling how close and happy the two of them had looked together, her chest ached.
But pushing down that sorrowful feeling, Emilia turned her back on the chamber of the Trial.

If there was a third Trial, it would, like the second, require going in and coming back out.
She would carry this momentum straight through to clearing the third Trial as well, and free the Sanctuary.

For Subaru's sake, for the sake of Ram's wish, and to make good on the bold words Emilia herself had flung at Roswaal—what was needed now was the drive to act.

"—It's already pitch dark out."

Passing through the dim ruined corridor, her footsteps echoing off the stone-paved floor, Emilia narrowed her eyes at how faint the light spilling in from the tomb's entrance was.
Perhaps the moonlight was blocked by clouds; the pale, thin, misty-seeming light was maybe starlight instead.

In the Sanctuary, where nearly every light source went dark come nightfall, the only thing that could cut through the night's darkness was the natural light pouring down from the sky.

"—Eh."

So Emilia was thinking as she walked.
Which was why, the instant she stepped out of the tomb, she found herself choking on the sheer number of gazes turned upon her slender frame.

"Ah, she's here!"

The moment someone called that out, a stir spread through the surroundings like contagion.
Before the faltering Emilia, the stir spread all at once, and the attention of the overwhelming crowd gathered there converged on Emilia alone.

—It was the people who lived in the Sanctuary.

The people of the Sanctuary, other than Garfiel and Ryuzu.
Up until now, Emilia had had no more contact with them than necessary. Partly that was because her own state of mind hadn't had that much room to spare, and partly it was because they, for their part, had never actively tried to approach her either.

As before, in the gazes turned toward her from all around, there was a kind of resignation.
And in the villagers, there was distaste for what Emilia was, but also expectation for the role she would play in freeing the Sanctuary, and above all, the suspicion that they had to determine for themselves whether she was truly fit to stand before them.

So Emilia had believed there was no way they would show themselves like this, en masse, until she had actually produced the result of freeing the Sanctuary.
Emilia herself had never doubted that any dialogue with them was the sort of thing that could only come into being once it was accompanied by the fruit of results.

So why, then, were they gathered here like this, now?
And moreover, in the gazes they turned on Emilia—there was, stronger than distaste, expectation.

"It was a rather mean-spirited business, mind you."

Before the bewildered Emilia, a single girl stepped forward from among the villagers.
The figure with long, pale pink hair was Ryuzu.
As the village's representative, she came forward as though leading the villagers, and smiled at Emilia.

"Everyone here has been dragging their feet, one way or another. Wondering what answer Emilia-sama would give to the Trial, and... well, some of them have been troubled over how they'd conduct themselves once the Sanctuary is freed, too."

"...I think that's only natural. But how does that connect to it being mean-spirited?"

"Oh, it's a simple thing. I told everyone in the Sanctuary a bit here and there, about the fight between Gar-bou and Su-bou, and the argument between Emilia-sama and Roz-bou... well, and various other scenes besides. Just a little at a time, the details of it all..."

"Y-you told them!?"

Watching Ryuzu scratch her cheek as though it were awkward to say, Emilia's cheeks went red.
The clash of wills between Subaru and Garfiel was one thing, but the argument that happened afterward, between herself and Roswaal, had also been her forcing an immature opinion on him.
She'd resolved to herself that she wouldn't be embarrassed no matter who heard it, but being told after the fact that someone had actually heard it made her embarrassed all the same.

"But even if you say you told them... Ryuzu-san, where did you even hear it?"

"Ah, well, you see... I may not look it, but I've got frightfully sharp ears. Within this Sanctuary, there's hardly a secret that can be kept from me at all."

"Is that so... Amazing."

At Ryuzu's declaration of eavesdropping, Emilia found herself impressed before she could get angry.
Not noticing the old woman with the appearance of a little girl sticking out her tongue, Emilia nodded, as though confirming to herself the reason so many people had gathered here.

And then, to Emilia,

"E-Emilia-sama."

"Y-yes?"

"What a way to start off—like a formal matchmaking meeting."

One of the village's residents—being someone who lived in the Sanctuary, he was surely a half-Demi-Human as well.
A man with slightly elongated canine teeth and eyes with slit-narrow pupils. Looking to be around Roswaal's age, or perhaps a little older, the man stepped forward before Emilia with an air of nervousness.

"I... no, we... that is... to be honest, we still haven't been able to make up our minds."

"—————"

"Whether we can trust you, what it even means to know the outside of the Sanctuary. Frankly, we're terrified of the outside—it's nothing but unknowns to us. All of us were born and have lived our whole lives in here. We know nothing about the outside world."

That was the very nature of the Sanctuary that Garfiel too had argued.
The yoke of the barrier, continuing since four hundred years ago, forced generation after generation of the people living within it to remain rooted to this place. Had there never been any means of leaving, perhaps they would never have needed to give it a thought.

But now the means of leaving lay right in front of them, in a form everyone knew of, and it was being unraveled by the hand of Emilia—a woman with whom they had no connection at all.

There couldn't possibly be anyone who felt no anxiety or resentment at that. Fewer still, surely, were those who could stride confidently out into the outside world.

Privately, Emilia had been afraid that Garfiel's misgivings might represent the consensus of the entire Sanctuary.
And now, in truth, the words of the man before her seemed about to prove it.

"Even if we could rely on Roswaal-sama's care out there, then what would even be different from here... Frankly, my anxiety outweighs my hope. Change is frightening."

"...Mm."

"But—"

Emilia drew her chin in, nearly lowering her eyes at his words. But the man's next words stopped that motion.
Standing up straight, drawing himself up, the man went on with a tense face.

"Garfiel's... that boy's voice. We all heard it."

"............"

"What that hard worker was thinking, what he was feeling... we came to know it. And the black-haired young man's response to that hard worker, and what came after—the exchange between Roswaal-sama and Emilia-sama too."

The man's face crumpled, even as he kept his back straight.
At his expression—frustrated, on the verge of tears—Emilia's chest tightened.

"Honestly, I thought it was pathetic. A fourteen-year-old child worried that much on our behalf, a boy not even twenty shouting like that at us... and then, even after Roswaal-sama told her it couldn't be done, Emilia-sama's words that never bent—I heard all of that too. So, Emilia-sama—"

"—Yes?"

"Whatever the result, and whatever happens after—I think the way you're standing up to the Trials is amazing. It's worthy of respect. Not every single one of us shares that feeling, and even I haven't been able to fully accept you yet. So, please—let us see it through to the end."

There was no need to ask see what through.
Bathed in his gaze, so charged with strong resolve, Emilia looked past him to the villagers behind him, nodding along with the stance he represented, and drew in her chin.

"I understand. I'll definitely finish it safely... and then I'll have you all listen to what I have to say."

"Yes. I promise. Judging someone based on nothing but hearsay, without ever even meeting them... that wasn't something we, of all people, should have done. —Wahya!"

The man's shoulders slumped—and then Ryuzu suddenly pinched him on the side from behind.
The man jumped and spun around as if to protest, but Ryuzu just scoffed at him.

"You do go on, and so earnestly too. And partway through you slipped back from *watashi* to *ore*, you know. That's what you get for trying something you're not used to."

"...S-sorry."

"Anyway, that's about the sum of our thoughts. Sorry for the meddling."

With that heartwarming, everyday sort of exchange, Ryuzu had the stiffly formal man step back.
At the sight, Emilia drew a deep breath, filling her chest with oxygen, and with something else besides.

Ryuzu's thoughtfulness, and the people of the Sanctuary who had come to see her through.
Just that alone—how reassuring it was, right now.

"Thank you, Ryuzu-san. This makes me feel like I can really give it my all again."

"Is that so, is that so. Well, good, then. ...The next one should be the final Trial, I take it."

"Yes, that's right. —I think I'll go take it on right away."

With the strength she'd just been given still coursing through her, Emilia turned to face the tomb once more.
But partway through, she stopped, as though something had occurred to her, and turned only her head back toward Ryuzu,

"Oh, wait... come to think of it, Ryuzu-san, have you seen Ram? I'd like to properly let her know that the second Trial is over too."

"...Ram, it seems, had some task of her own, so she isn't here. She only said she was praying for Emilia-sama's success. 'Emilia-sama has her own part to play, and Ram has hers. Let each of us fulfill it,' she said."

It was such a Ram thing to say that, even knowing it was only a relayed message, Emilia nearly smiled wryly.
Ram's part to play—where it was, and with whom she would fulfill it.
There was a faint stirring of unease in her chest, but Emilia consciously pushed it down.

Ram had trusted Emilia. So Emilia would trust Ram in turn.
Just as Subaru and the others had made a path for her that way, Emilia too wanted to go on making a path for those who would follow after her.

"I'm going."

At Emilia's words, Ryuzu nodded, and the stir of the villagers' voices pushed at her back.
With resolve stronger than the first time, stronger than the second, Emilia stepped into the tomb.

And then—

'Face the calamity yet to come.'

The final Trial was coming—.

Deep in her chest, Ram could feel her heartbeat quicken, faint but unmistakable.

Never once had she been subjected to this much hostility from the person standing before her.
To touch him, to exchange words with him, to be given some order by him.

To Ram, all of these were the highest happiness there was, the very meaning of her life.
And so, even now, faced with open hostility, she was delighted to find within herself a maiden's thrill of excitement.

"...We-e-ell, aren't you bold, showing your face here-e-e."

So murmured the tall man standing across from her, glaring at Ram.

A sweet numbness shot through her brain at the sound of that trembling, beautiful voice.
Just being bathed in that gaze of mismatched colors was enough to make her legs feel ready to give out beneath her.

Of course, she had no intention of showing any sign of such a frail, weak-kneed woman.
A woman like that would only be cast aside as useless even as a mere limb.

"So-o-o, what exactly do you think you're doing here, hm-m-m?"

"—It's a simple thing."

She answered the question as always, with an expressionless, quiet composure.
Her pink hair swaying, Ram drew a wand from beneath the hem of her skirt, and leveled its tip at the handsome man before her—at the master she revered and loved—


"I have come to take you back from the Witch's obsession."

It was a declaration made to burn away, with her own love, the man she loved—one being swallowed by a maddened, obsessive love.
