Arc 9 Finale: "Reweave"

—Watching from a distance as the servants of House Barielle's mansion went about their work, Frederica found herself unable to shake an unsettled feeling.

"Accompanying Master and being treated as a guest is nothing unusual, but... in this mansion, just now, things feel a little different, don't they."

As Frederica murmured this complaint, what filled her view—reflected in her emerald eyes—was the bustling state of the mansion.
It was plain to see that the staff were sharp, well-disciplined, and thoroughly capable. Even after being told the grim news that Frederica and the others had brought back, the household hadn't fallen into dysfunction. —That alone was worthy of admiration.

"It seems Lady Priscilla was truly loved."

The grim news relayed to the servants was this: Priscilla Barielle—mistress of the mansion and their own liege—had lost her life in the Vollachian Empire.

"—..."

Lowering her eyes slightly, Frederica let out a breath as she looked down from the balcony over the garden and the mansion below.
Given her own position, Frederica knew well the feeling of being left behind at the mansion by those she served—Roswaal, Emilia, and the others—with nothing to do but pray for their safe return. She could only imagine how great a blow it must be to learn that such a prayer had gone unanswered.
Indeed, many of the servants grieved, and no small number of them shed tears. That not a single one had left the mansion upon hearing the news could itself be taken as a testament to their bond with Priscilla.
And yet, it wasn't only because Priscilla had been so beloved—

"—it was Shult-sama's resolute bearing that struck my heart."

What had moved Frederica so deeply was the bearing of the young attendant who had accompanied Priscilla to the Empire, shouldered the duty of conveying news of her death and her final wishes, and carried it out splendidly.
Slight of build and seemingly as fragile as a small animal, Shult had nonetheless refused to leave the unwanted role to Roswaal or Frederica, and had accomplished the great task with his own mouth, his own words.
That the servants had chosen not to leave the mansion, but to remain in their posts, owed a great deal to both their yearning for Priscilla and their trust in Shult—of that there was no doubt. Even as an outsider who couldn't help the heat welling at the corners of her eyes, Frederica shared that view.
And so—

"—Oh my, so this is where you were, Frederica. I've gone and searched everywhe-e-ere for you."

"Master..."

Frederica bent her knee and gave a small bow as she received the tall figure stepping onto the balcony with a light raise of his hand—Roswaal.

"My apologies for putting you to the trouble of searching. What did you need?"

"Not that it's much of a nee-e-ed, really. The arrangements here have settled into a lu-u-ll, so I merely thought I'd like a word with you."

"Arrangements..."

"Yes, arrangeme-e-ents, indeed."

Smiling thinly, Roswaal closed one eye—leaving open only the yellow of his mismatched pair—and came to stand beside Frederica at the railing, leaning his back against it.

"Quite a land with much worth noting. I have few acquaintances in the south. The late Lord Leip Barielle, Lady Priscilla's deceased husband, wasn't exactly a man of good repute, after a-a-all."

"To be spoken of so by you, Master—I imagine that gentleman would be none too pleased."

"Oh my, how severe. Are you too being poisoned by little Petra, losing your obedience to me-e-e? Or perhaps—"

Tilting his head, Roswaal peered into Frederica's eyes beside him,

"—you don't much care for my plan to make use of little Shult, is that it?"

"...I don't think what you do is wrong, Master. I understand well enough that it serves the Royal Selection—and by extension, Lady Emilia and the camp."

"Even if it makes sense in theory, convincing the heart is another matter entire-e-ely."

That he said so while fully understanding this only proved just how thoroughgoing Roswaal's ill nature was. Emilia would probably call it a mischievous streak that wanted a proper scolding.

"Given the relationship between Master and myself, that would be difficult, Lady Emilia..."

If Emilia or Petra were here, they might prompt Roswaal to reflect through honest means; if Ram were here, through more underhanded ones. But as if he'd anticipated this, only Frederica was present.
And setting aside the debt of gratitude she could never fully repay, Frederica simply understood—Roswaal's deliberately villainous conduct was, in truth, for the sake of the camp.

—The Emilia Camp's foremost purpose in banding together as a camp was, after all, the Royal Selection.

In that Royal Selection, the entire span spent in the Vollachian Empire had cost them ground. Emilia had already fallen behind at the start even without that. If they meant to make up for the lost lap—and perhaps even gain momentum—they couldn't rely solely on honest means.
To that end, Roswaal's devoted scheming was proceeding methodically, aiming to win over Barielle territory, which had only just lost its absolute pillar in Priscilla Barielle.

"Now that Lady Priscilla has passed, until the next lord is chosen, a provisional administrator will need to be installed. Fortunately, I can put forward a candidate from among those under my thumb, and for a liaison, it would do well to borrow someone from one of Lady Ryuzu's line—the 'Meyers.'"

"...Isn't it a bit premature to involve Granny's children in this?"

"They do say, 'send a beloved child out on a journey,' don't they? Keep them doted upon in a birdcage forever, and they'll never learn how to spread their wings. I'm of the same mind mysel-e-elf."

Watching Roswaal steadily, methodically tighten up his flawless plan, Frederica found herself seriously doubting whether Emilia's assessment of him was even true—wondering if he wasn't simply a villain rather than merely playing at villainy.
Perhaps it was precisely because he could commit to the role of villain this thoroughly, without a single slip, that his campmates could never quite bring themselves to fully open their hearts to him. He was simply too earnest about it.

"Master, if you could at least go a little easier—"

She was just about to point out that this misunderstanding might plant deep suspicion not only within the camp, but in Barielle territory, and by extension, in Shult's heart—

"I—it's, it's, it's terrible, it is! Margrave-sama, where are you—?!"

"Was that Lord Shult?"

Suddenly hearing Shult's flustered voice from below, Frederica exchanged a glance with Roswaal. Roswaal, raising his eyebrows, seemed to have no idea what was going on either; her master pushed off the railing, turned, and called down to Shult—who had come dashing out into the garden below—"Little Shult."
At the call, Shult's round, wide eyes opened even further,

"Margrave-sama! I need you to come at once, sir! There's someone I want you to meet, and a story I need you to hear!"

"—What you've just been shown is the will of my late mistress."

So said the crimson-haired girl who had revealed the contents of the wax-sealed letter—a maid who called herself Yae Tenzen—and at her words, Roswaal's eyes went wide, frozen in place.

"—..."

It was rare to see her master's composure shaken so plainly, but the reaction was understandable. Frederica, having confirmed the same contents, along with everyone else present in the room, was struck with astonishment.
Above all, Shult—the very person at the center of what the letter contained—was the most astonished of all.
The contents of the letter read as follows—

*—I, Priscilla Barielle, current head of House Barielle, in formally designating my successor, do hereby declare the following: that the one named Shult shall have his name changed to Shult Barielle, and shall be received as the legitimate heir and successor child of House Barielle.*

"Lady Priscilla, chose me...?"

Blinking at this unexpected turn of events, Shult wore the face of someone unable to tell whether this was a dream or reality.
But then hands settled on both his shoulders from behind, and Yae—who had revealed where the letter was hidden—smiled and nuzzled her cheek against the boy's, saying, "You're way too surprised, you know."

"Given how much the mistress doted on you, Shult-chan, it's not so out of the question, is it? —Though I think she really meant to keep it hidden until the Royal Selection was over."

"Yae-sama... how long have you known about this, sir? You left the mansion so many months ago, and you've only just come back to us..."

"...I found out just before the mistress and you, Shult-chan, left for the Empire. That's around when she prepared this letter, too."

At Yae's quiet answer, fat tears began welling up in Shult's eyes.
As it turned out, Yae had once worked as a maid in this mansion and had been on a leave of absence for some time, but upon hearing of Priscilla's death, she had rushed back.
As could plainly be seen, Yae had been close with Shult, and it seemed she had also held Priscilla's deep trust, having been entrusted as guardian of the important letter she left behind.
The fact that this revelation brought to light was one that thoroughly upended Roswaal's plans—

"—But, thinking of Lord Shult, perhaps it was for the best."

"...Frederica?"

"It may run somewhat counter to your own designs, Master... but this must surely bring salvation to Lord Shult's heart, and to the hearts of everyone in this mansion. —She truly was a remarkable woman, wasn't she."

Despite being a Royal Selection candidate standing in opposition to Emilia, Frederica—who'd had almost no direct contact with Priscilla—could find no other way to describe her than as a truly great figure.
After all—

"To have so thoroughly overturned your own scheming, Master—she must, without question, have been a worthy rival to Lady Emilia, don't you think."

"—..."

Looking once more at the letter left behind, Yae wrapped her arms around Shult's small, trembling body from behind and let out a breath at her own decision to return to House Barielle's mansion.

—It was all a decision made to help see through the purpose Al had set his sights on.

"No signal came, which probably means the plan at the tower failed, huh~"

In truth, Yae had promised to rendezvous with Al—who had headed for the Pleiades Watchtower—at the nearest town, to lend her aid to whatever mission he had to accomplish, and to witness the moment his life finally ran out.
But when the appointed time came and no rendezvous signal was raised, Yae judged that Al's plan had failed, withdrew from the nearest town—and made straight for Barielle territory.

"Coming back to the mansion out in the open like this—I suppose it's been since Al-sama stopped me from assassinating the mistress, hasn't it."

Though, in less open fashion, she had actually come and gone from the mansion fairly often in order to stay in contact with Al. —Even the letter she'd used as her pretext for returning was information she'd obtained while lurking in the attic during one of those covert visits.
To Shult, and to the wicked noble trying to exploit him—Roswaal L. Mathers—and his attendant maid, she had, of course, lied that the letter had been entrusted to her by Priscilla.

"In reality, all I did was peek and see the mistress prepare it and put it away in her secret safe."

Thanks to the fact that the safe holding the letter was a hidden one whose existence no one else knew, Yae had been able to reveal its location and win the trust of someone entrusted with a secret. For that, using the late Priscilla in such a way pricked at her conscience somewhat, but—

"—But, mistress, did you really think I hadn't seen?"

Honestly, that was a mystery even Yae herself couldn't answer with any certainty.
Priscilla had prepared the letter adopting Shult right before departing for the Empire, and had put it away in the safe around the same time, but Yae—even if only a temporary mistress to her—never underestimated the woman's insight. Had that formidable woman, with eyes that seemed to see straight through to the hearts of others, truly believed in Yae's absence?

*—Use the letter well, and make it serve your own ends. While you're at it, you may as well save Shult.*

Perhaps hiding the letter in a safe that no one but Yae, lurking in the attic, could ever find—short of turning the whole mansion upside down—had been Priscilla's own quiet arrangement, meant just so.
Either way—

"I'll do just that, mistress. —After all, I have to wait for Al-sama."

Having decided to indulge in Priscilla's kindness, Yae had come back to the mansion for exactly that purpose.
As mentioned, Al—unable to give the signal to proceed with the plan—had likely met with failure at the watchtower and fallen into a state where carrying out his plan had become difficult. But Al would not die. He was alive.

"Because Al-sama is a monster, after all."

Al didn't die. He couldn't be killed. It was precisely because he was such a monster that Yae followed him.
So that she might witness him accomplish his purpose and die satisfied—so that she might see the being she feared vanish at last. —To that end, she needed Shult to keep his distance from the Emilia Camp.
If something had happened to Al, the Emilia Camp that had accompanied him to the watchtower was involved. She needed to be somewhere she could move at a moment's notice, ready for when he broke free of it.
And so—

"Shult-chan, let's carry on the mistress's will together. It'll be alright. I'll come back to the mansion from now on too, and help you out, Shult-chan."

"Yae-sama... yes. Yes, it is...! I'll live up to Lady Priscilla's expectations, I will! And I want you to help me do it, Yae-sama!"

At the resolve of honest, adorable Shult—no, of the boy now become Shult Barielle—Yae let her eyes soften theatrically, while the other servants, seemingly moved, welled up with tears of their own. Even Roswaal's attendant maid dabbed at the corners of her eyes, her heart struck by the boy's plucky spirit.
Only the Margrave, whose plans had evidently gone awry, wore an ambiguous expression—but that itself seemed proof that Priscilla, even in death, had outmaneuvered someone's scheme, and there was something satisfying in that.
Yes, Priscilla had been a mistress well worth serving. —That much, at least, was true.

"I'll do my best, I will! Because when Al-sama and Heinkel-sama come back, I want to show them how splendid I've become, I do!"

But at Shult's flushed, spirited declaration, Yae hesitated over how to answer.
Yae, too, longed for Al's return, just the same.
But as for Heinkel—

"...Heinkel-sama coming back might be a little difficult, actually~"

A moment of weightlessness, then he crashed to the ground—Heinkel let out a low, guttural scream.
After groaning for a while, he slowly worked his limbs and freed himself of his bindings. Once his wrists and ankles—bound tight with thin thread—regained their freedom, he took a moment to savor the sensation of release he hadn't felt in some time—

"Goddamn it...!"

he spat bitterly, as if cursing the misfortune that had befallen him.

—The one who had tied Heinkel up and abandoned him in a hut deep in a forest no one visited was the girl named Yae, who had claimed to be Aldebaran's collaborator.
A girl with a baffling history—once a maid serving Priscilla, and also apparently an assassin sent against her—she had lured him in, promising that if he cooperated with Aldebaran, an opportunity would come his way to obtain the thing Heinkel craved—Dragon's Blood. And after luring him in like that, too.

"The second there's no word from that Aldebaran bastard, suddenly it's 'the plan failed'—don't screw with me..."

And on top of that, when he'd demanded an explanation and that the plan continue, she'd driven merciless knee strikes and elbow blows into him, tied him up as he bled from the nose, and vanished without another word.
As far as Heinkel was concerned, he had no reason to deserve being cut loose and left hanging like this.

"Every last one of them...!"

Priscilla, whom he had relied on, was dead; Emilia, whom he'd next tried to rely on, had rejected him; and when he'd tried to cling to the hand Aldebaran had extended, that hand had shaken him off and cast him down into the Abyss.
As a result, Heinkel's prospects had reverted right back to the dead end he'd been stuck in before the Royal Selection even began.

"But even so..."

He couldn't give up. He couldn't bring himself to fully give up. He had never even managed to properly follow through on giving up.

"—Damn it all!"

He staggered to his feet, wiped away his now-dried nosebleed with his sleeve, and started walking. His feet had no destination in mind. But even without one, he couldn't stand still.

—Because Heinkel Astrea was, after all, a husband and a father.

"Wait for me, Louanna, Reinhard. I'll... I'm gonna..."

Dragging his feet with a shuffling gait, one hand resting on the hilt of the sword at his hip, Heinkel walked on.
He had no destination. —Only a prayer, tattered and rust-eaten from years of wear, remained.

His dear Beatrice, admirably enough, had devoted herself to analyzing the Forbidden Art that sealed them and working to dismantle its formula, but all Subaru could manage, on the rare occasions he had anything resembling real consciousness, was to activate the Authority of Greed—"Cor Leonis"—and pick up on the presence of his nearby companions and the shifts in their strongest emotions.

Especially distinctive among them were Petra, whose colors of resolve and determination had burned intensely, and Rem, whose feelings toward Subaru could only be described as truly enormous, swollen beyond measure. —Because of that, Subaru had been able to declare, with conviction, the plan to leave the tower at first light.

In that instant, that fleeting moment when not even words could be exchanged, Rem—her eyes full of complete understanding—had driven her morningstar into Subaru. As she wielded, with brilliant skill, that spiked iron ball he polished every single day, she had, without question, been reclaiming herself. And that was unmistakably the doing of Gluttony's involvement.
Now that he could be certain of this, and given that Gluttony itself was already in custody, there was no reason not to corner it and force it to disgorge everything it had devoured.

At Subaru's declaration of resolve, Beatrice, standing beside him, took his hand; Garfiel stroked his own fangs with a finger; and Meili shrugged the shoulder bearing her little crimson scorpion.
And Subaru met the gaze Petra turned on him, smiling with cool, steadfast strength.

For the sake of Aldebaran—who called himself the "star that trails after Subaru," who had opposed him with the very same power, and whom he had nonetheless decided he would absolutely save—Natsuki Subaru set out for the royal capital.
He would come to know. Because he understood that remaining ignorant would be a kind of dishonesty, one he could never forgive himself for.

And so—

"—Bring it on, Lord Destiny!"

Shouldering his feelings, declaring that he would trample over whatever curse stood in his way, Natsuki Subaru made his vow.

—At that very same moment as Natsuki Subaru's declaration of resolve.

In a land now far distant—at the destination Natsuki Subaru had just declared he would set out for, the royal capital of the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica—a certain strange event was taking place.

"—..."

In the early morning, while most of the capital's people still slept, sleeping through the arrival of dawn from within their dreams, an unusual atmosphere hung over Royal Lugunica Castle, which should otherwise have been wrapped in silence.

The source of that atmosphere was the heart of the castle—the throne room.

With the royal line's extinction by illness—the very trigger for the Royal Selection—the throne room of Royal Lugunica Castle, now without a king, remained firmly closed except for the holding of important ceremonies.
But on this day, this morning, the throne room's doors stood thrown wide open, and gathered within were the kingdom's most eminent figures—members of the Council of Wise Men, retainers who had held the deep trust of the late king, and even kingdom soldiers, their bearing charged with a tension that felt almost ominous.

"—..."

Every one of them bore heavy responsibility, each in their own position, within a kingdom now without its king.
Not a single one had arrived late despite the early-morning summons, and there was not one person present with drowsy eyes or an insolent lack of attention showing in their bearing.
Thus, regardless of the hour, the weight of attention gathered in that room could rightly be called the weight of the kingdom itself.
—And amid all of it, bathed in that attention alone, the woman maintained an air of perfect stillness.

"—..."

Upon the marble floor laid with a red carpet, kneeling on one knee, was a figure clad in indigo robes, hands clasped before her chest, head bowed low. Her posture of prayer was so refined that it called to mind, for the military officials, feats of martial skill, and for the civil officials, the finest points of etiquette—each of them couldn't help but let out a sigh.
That was how thoroughly the woman overwhelmed those around her with the mere act of prayer.

"—Hmm. Would you raise your face for us, please."

In the midst of all this, there was one person alone who, without having his heart stolen away by the woman's posture of prayer, spoke up.
The owner of that voice—calm, intelligent, the sort that naturally left such an impression on those who heard it—was one of the castle officials receiving the kneeling girl. No, his position was such that he could even be called their representative.

Miklotov McMahon. —The central figure of the Council of Wise Men, which steered the affairs of state in a Lugunica now without a king, and, even at this hastily arranged gathering, still the great man wielding the greatest voice and authority to decide.
A sage known for his outstanding wisdom and his extraordinary loyalty and contribution to the kingdom, his presence loomed so large that the king himself had once been moved to joke, "Should the King of Lugunica die, the nation would not waver—but should he die, the kingdom would fall."
His gentle features and mild manner of speech invited easy misunderstanding, but in truth he was the outstanding figure said to be, within the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica, the most reassuring ally one could ask for and the most fearsome enemy one could make. —Miklotov was a man who had held that reputation, unchallenged, for many long years.

"—Yes."

At Miklotov's words, the woman who had been bowed slowly raised her face. And when her closed eyelids opened, Miklotov, meeting her gaze, drew in a faint breath.
Miklotov's reaction was not his alone. Bordeaux Zergev, present as a fellow member of the Council of Wise Men; Marcos Gildark, commander of the Royal Guard standing here as the representative of the military officials; Rickert Hoffman, the central figure among the civil officials; and everyone else present in the throne room to witness this moment—each of them reacted in their own way.
She was a woman possessed of an almost bewitching quality that naturally drew every mind's attention merely by being present. But the reaction of everyone in that moment wasn't only because of her bearing. —It was her appearance.
Her long, lustrous golden hair, and crimson eyes holding a firm, unyielding will, carried a meaning beyond mere beauty, striking straight through everyone present in that room—no, through the people of the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica itself.
And then—

"—First, allow me to thank you for receiving this sudden visit, and for welcoming it so graciously. Considering both our positions, it could hardly have been a desirable thing to do."

As though well accustomed to such scrutiny, the woman, still kneeling on one knee, swept her gaze across the assembled sages and dignitaries and spoke these words.
Her opening words, neither excessively humble nor presumptuous of her position, prompted those present to suppress their momentary shock and unease and leave the response to their representative, Miklotov.
Picking up on that intent, Miklotov, seated in his chair, stroked his own very long beard with his fingers, let out a "Hmm," and said,

"My, my, that's quite the difficult thing to say aloud. Still, empty flattery would serve no purpose here. What you say is, after all, the truth."

"When you put it so frankly, it takes a weight off my shoulders as well. Being as young and inexperienced as I am, I was rather afraid the words might fail me in front of you all."

"Hmm, and yet for all you say that, you carry yourself quite boldly. You seem well accustomed to gathering the attention of a crowd. I'd imagine you have... no shortage of reasons why."

"That's true. I won't deny it."

Though the two of them were separated in age by roughly a grandfather and granddaughter, the exchange between Miklotov and the woman flowed without hesitation, and showed no sign of becoming a one-sided probing born of their difference in stature.
Sensing this, neither one more than the other, both of them let out a small breath and smiled at the same moment,

"I cannot go on carelessly stealing the time of such busy people so early in the morning. So, if I may... allow me to get to the heart of the matter."

"Hmm, that would be best. At my age, rising early is no trouble, but an important matter is a painful thing to have delayed, no matter how old one gets. —All the more so when it comes from the Divine Dragon Church, which has kept its distance from the royal castle."

"—..."

Though he had meant it as a magnanimous invitation to proceed, Miklotov's remark drew the air in the room taut for just an instant. It quickly eased again, deemed rude toward a woman who had come to the royal castle all alone—but the reason it had tensed in the first place was something no one present could simply wave away.

—The Divine Dragon Church that Miklotov had named was, exactly as its name suggested, a religious body that revered the existence of the Divine Dragon Volcanica, and held faith in the Covenant of his protective blessing and power.

As befit its name, the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica was known for its exceptionally deep ties to the Dragons, and any citizen of the kingdom partook, to a greater or lesser degree, in the blessing of the Divine Dragon.
Out of that awareness, the great majority of Lugunica's citizens held a sense of reverence toward the Divine Dragon even without officially belonging to the church. In such an environment, it goes without saying just how strong the faith in the Divine Dragon ran among those who did officially belong to it.
To prevent the state from being swayed by such enormous influence, the Divine Dragon Church did not approach the royal castle that governed the nation's affairs, and each contributed to the people's happiness within its own domain. —That was the unspoken understanding between them.
All the more so, then, was it an extraordinary event for a nun of the Divine Dragon Church to visit the royal castle and speak before a gathering that included the Council of Wise Men.
And yet—

"—There is value in it."

Precisely because she understood how extraordinary this was, she had a reason sufficient to break that unwritten rule.
The woman's flat declaration served, indeed, to raise the expectation that gathering Miklotov and the others in the throne room to hear her out would prove meaningful.
And so, following the sages' silent prompting, the woman told them—the reason for her visit.

"The recent perils facing the kingdom have deeply troubled all the faithful of the Divine Dragon Church. By our unspoken understanding, we had kept our distance from the affairs of state—the battle for the throne, the Royal Selection—but ever since word reached us of the calamity in the Water Gate City, we could no longer simply sit still."

"The Water Gate City... you mean the matter with the Witch Cult in Priestella. We too have been grieved by the damage to that city. Surely the Divine Dragon Church doesn't mean to voice complaints against the Royal Selection candidates involved in the incident?"

"That would contradict the very reason I rushed here, breaking the unspoken understanding, and would also contradict my saying there was value in hearing me out. ...I mean, it would contradict itself."

Her true self slipped out in that sudden rebuttal, and the way she corrected herself afterward betrayed her youth. As if to cover her embarrassment, the woman gave a small cough and continued,

"In any case, I have no complaints against the Royal Selection candidates. There is, admittedly, one whom I wouldn't mind having a word with, but that's not the consensus of the Divine Dragon Church—merely a personal opinion."

"If it isn't the church's official view, then it's simply the candid opinion of a citizen of the kingdom. If anything, we ought to lend it an eager ear... hmm, but that would be straying from the topic."

"True. I'll remember that you, Miklotov-sama, are someone easy to talk to—but back to the matter at hand. —What we wish to discuss is not the pursuit of blame, but the matter of the victims."

"...Let us hear it, then."

"Those who fell victim to the Witch Cult in the Water Gate City of Priestella—I understand multiple cases have been confirmed. The Divine Dragon Church can offer a means of their salvation."

—In that instant, at the weight of what those words meant, the air of the throne room stirred and rippled greatly.
But the woman who was the very epicenter of that stir kept her poised bearing unbroken, standing straight and firm even under the sages' gazes. There was no wavering in her eyes or manner—only the confidence of one who could never have spoken such words without conviction behind them.

"—Hmm."

With that single utterance, Miklotov cut through the murmuring air.
Even so, Miklotov, one of the kingdom's foremost sages, could not help but be struck with surprise at the woman's proposal. The sage, betraying just the faintest hint of agitation in the fingers toying with his beard, said,

"If that is true, it is a proposal beyond anything we could have hoped for. As for bringing this matter to the royal castle..."

"It wouldn't sit well if we simply proceeded on our own, without a word to anyone. And besides—"

"It wouldn't only be us here at the castle who'd frown on that—the Witch Cult would too, I'd wager."

At this obvious truth, the woman drew in her slender chin in agreement with Miklotov.
Naturally, if the damage the Witch Cult had wrought could be undone, that would be nothing but an undesirable outcome for the Witch Cult itself. Precisely for that reason, the Divine Dragon Church desired it.

"—The kingdom's firm protection. That is something which, due to the distance between us who govern the affairs of state and the Divine Dragon Church which governs the faith, has never truly been realized in all these years."

"Yes, exactly. We wish to see that realized, taking this opportunity. That is the consensus of the Divine Dragon Church. —And to give that decision one final push, I have a proposal to make."

"Hmm, a proposal, is it. And that would be?"

"First, allow me to show you. Proof of the salvation we are capable of providing."

Miklotov raised his brows at the woman, who spoke with a hand pressed to her own chest.
It was an offer beyond anything they could have hoped for. Just as she said, it was a proposal beyond reproach as a basis for judgment. However, bringing a victim from the Water Gate City to the royal capital right away would be—

"There should be one person you already have in mind. Let the first case be that person."

"...Very well. First, I grant permission to approach them with this. —Captain Marcos."

"Sir!"

Called by name, Marcos—the towering, armor-clad captain of the Royal Guard—answered in a thunderous voice, stepped forward, and gave a salute toward the Council of Wise Men's seats.

"Would you escort her there? This will be a matter of grave importance to the kingdom. Handle it with care."

"Understood. —I shall accompany her myself."

"Yes, the big-faced one—no, I mean, the big-bodied one. I'll be in your care."

The woman gave a small bow of her head, and some measure of softness came into her expression. Perhaps it was because the conversation had proceeded the way she had hoped, and it seemed the purpose of her visit on behalf of the Divine Dragon Church might yet be fulfilled.
To her, even knowing it would come somewhat as an ambush, Miklotov posed a certain question.
It concerned the reason—beyond simply being affiliated with the Divine Dragon Church—that this woman, who had called herself an envoy of the Church and gathered Miklotov and the kingdom's other dignitaries to the castle so early in the morning, drew such attention.
It was this—

"—Forgive my great rudeness, but might I ask your name, good sister?"

△▼△▼△▼△

"—I am a sister dispatched from the Divine Dragon Church."

Brought personally by Marcos to visit the noble's residence, Ferris couldn't hide his bewilderment before the woman who called herself a nun.

The reason Ferris had been staying in the royal capital together with Wilhelm was to make a direct appeal to the royal castle—to be allowed to join the investigative team sent to the newly opened Pleiades Watchtower, and to learn from the wisdom of the all-knowing, all-powerful Sage some means of saving Crusch, who continued to suffer even now.
Of course, a letter had already arrived from Emilia and the others, who had returned from the Pleiades Watchtower, informing him that neither the Sage's existence nor its wisdom had turned out to be so conveniently helpful. But even so, it still held far more promise than the sluggish, stalled development of healing magic.

"Honestly, it's ridiculous. No matter how much I improve the existing healing magic, all it does is keep me from dying—it doesn't take away even one bit of Crusch-sama's suffering."

His research into Blue magic, born of a desperate, clinging hope, had raised the existing level of healing magic within the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica not just one stage but three, in this short span of time—yet to Ferris now, it held no value at all.
At present, he could not save the one person he most wanted to save. That fact cornered Ferris, dragging him down into darkness.
And so he had tried to rely on the Pleiades Watchtower, on the Sage's wisdom—only for—

"...You can save Crusch-sama? The Divine Dragon Church can? Y-you're serious?"

"I don't lie where matters of faith are concerned, and I have no taste for tormenting those already lost even further. Even if you can't believe me, you should be able to believe your captain here."

Answering Ferris's trembling voice, the nun gestured with her hand toward Marcos, who had accompanied her. When Ferris cried out to him in a high voice, "Captain!", Marcos nodded, his face like solid rock,

"I hear the Divine Dragon Church holds magics and arts never made public. Miklotov-sama and the rest of the Council of Wise Men judged that it's worth putting our faith in."

"...B-but, but if something were to happen to Crusch-sama again because of this—"

"—'Quain's Stone cannot be raised by one person alone.'"

"—!"

At the nun's words, spoken so quietly, Ferris couldn't help but catch his breath.
As Ferris's eyes grew moist with the surging turmoil of emotion welling up inside him, the nun gently reached for his hand, and with her slender fingers, softly unclenched Ferris's tightly balled fingers, one by one.
Letting himself be led, Ferris happened to notice her beautiful golden hair and her crimson eyes holding a serious light, and his own eyes went wide.
It was a realization that only deepened his confusion, and he couldn't grasp what it meant—

"Please. Let me help save the person precious to you. The Divine Dragon Church always wishes for the happiness of the kingdom's people. No matter what beliefs one holds, there is no exception to that."

"W-who... just who are you?"

"—..."

At Ferris's question, she held her breath for a moment, but settled her faint hesitation with a blink, and answered, still gently holding his hand.
It was—

"—Filore. That's my name."

—The daughter of the king's younger brother, spirited away from the royal castle fifteen years ago and never seen again. The nun with the golden hair and crimson eyes characteristic of the royal bloodline had given the very same name as that vanished princess.
