Arc 4 Chapter 100: "Memories Buried in Dust"

――Searching blindly wasn't getting them anywhere.

Subaru arrived at that conclusion after running around the settlement searching for the missing Ryuzu, working his cardiopulmonary system so hard that his lungs ached with every breath.

"――Hah, hah, haah."

Hands on his knees, shoulders heaving violently, he forced oxygen into his aching lungs.
The mental fatigue of the past few days had taken its toll too, leaving his hands and feet feeling as though they'd been stuffed with lead. Even breathing wasn't easy.

"Natsuki-san, are you all right? Even so, you're giving out awfully fast."

"Shut... up... Just occurred to me, but... I was still an outpatient back home, and with all the various problems since I got here, my fatigue is at an absolute peak..."

"Yes, yes, I understand you're just sore about losing, so go sit down somewhere for a bit. Let's rest the body once and get the head working. I'll go fetch some water."

Otto, only slightly winded himself, chided the utterly exhausted Subaru. He pointed out a patch of shade to Subaru, said he was off to fetch water, and headed for the water source.
Watching his back recede, grimacing at his own pathetic state, Subaru sat down in the shade and focused on catching his breath.

"――――"

It had been nearly an hour since he'd parted with Garfiel, racing through the Sanctuary in search of Ryuzu θ.
He and Otto were working a manpower search, but so far, no results. They couldn't openly recruit help for the same reason they couldn't with the search for Emilia.
That Ryuzu, the Sanctuary's representative, had vanished in a way that amounted to abandoning her post — that wasn't something either the Sanctuary's residents or the refugees should learn. Garfiel surely thought the same, which was why he was searching alone.

"Emilia..."

If Subaru were honest, he'd rather worry about Emilia's safety than Ryuzu θ's.
He firmly believed she wouldn't do anything rash, but thinking of how precarious her heart must be, alone as she likely still was, he wanted nothing more than to rush to her side and be her support.
But reality's priorities were merciless, and as he'd told Otto, Ryuzu came first. There was no room to indulge his personal feelings there.

If only Ram, off on her own separate search, could find Emilia.

"Though that'd end up giving Roswaal one more point on me, wouldn't it."

Ram's stance was fairly clearly pro-Roswaal. Her reason for joining the search for Emilia was concern for Roswaal's future standing in this world, not any goodwill toward Subaru and the others. It just so happened that her actions in service of Roswaal currently amounted to helping them out.

The ideal scenario: they secure Ryuzu, and from there smoothly move on to securing Emilia as well. Best of all would be if Subaru could lead the negotiations with both of them.
But an ideal is just an ideal. A theory on paper. Counting chickens before they hatch.

"At this rate, I won't have picked up a single one before time runs out. That's the worst case, absolutely not allowed. ...I've got to do something about this, but..."

No amount of anxious urgency was going to bring back the results he wanted.
Wasn't the probability higher if he just kept combing every inch of the Sanctuary, rather than stopping to think like this?
That's what he thought, but—

"If that'd work, Garfiel would've found her already. It's been almost an hour. If a guy who can move around at more than double our speed still hasn't found her, that means..."

――Ryuzu was still successfully evading even Garfiel.

"――――"

Thinking that far, Subaru felt something catch in his mind and held his breath.
Something felt off just now. Ryuzu was fleeing from Garfiel too. Fine, so far. No — not fine. Why would Ryuzu be fleeing from Garfiel as well?
Hadn't he assumed all along that Ryuzu θ was running because she didn't want contact with Subaru? That she was hiding and running out the clock because she didn't want him drawing out what happened when she faced the Trials of the Tomb.

But that didn't add up.
If θ were seriously trying to refuse that conversation and keep Subaru at bay, all she'd need to do was tell Garfiel exactly that.
Once Garfiel had a clear reason, he wouldn't hesitate to remove Subaru. And if Garfiel came at him seriously, Subaru had no way to resist.

If θ genuinely intended to keep her past hidden, she'd have sicced Garfiel on him.
She hadn't done that. The reason she hadn't — what was it?

"She's running... because she wants to be chased? Is that it?"

"No, she's running because she doesn't want to be caught. What are you even saying?"

A voice doused cold water on Subaru's half-muttered musing as he touched his chin. Looking up, he saw an exasperated Otto holding out a water pitcher, offering him drinking water.

"I get that you've got a lot on your mind and it's turning to mush, but I went four days without sleep or rest once, running around for negotiations and peddling, and by the last day my head was scrambled too."

"Save that hard-luck story for teatime. My head's not scrambled. ...I don't think."

"That's not exactly confidence-inspiring."

Subaru took the offered water and tipped it back from the watering-can-shaped spout. Savoring the cold sensation sliding down his throat, he organized his tangled thoughts.

"How do you read the reason Ryuzu-san went into hiding?"

"...I mean, isn't it because she doesn't want to talk about something inconvenient if asked? Today, if she runs into you, Natsuki-san, she'll be forced into that conversation whether she likes it or not... though given the circumstances around her not being able to go outside, it does feel like a stopgap measure."

"Right, a stopgap. But if she seriously wanted to root out the problem for good, there's an easy way for Ryuzu-san to do that, isn't there?"

"――Garfiel, you mean?"

Given a hint, Otto arrived easily at the conclusion Subaru had reached only after much deliberation. He knit his brows and crossed his arms as if in thought.

"That's true, that angle... so does that mean Ryuzu-san doesn't want Garfiel to know she's at odds with you, Natsuki-san?"

"Well, Garfiel's already caught on that we're connected somehow. Frankly, his theory that anything unusual happening in the Sanctuary right now must be a flag we set is dead-on to the point I had nothing to say."

It was a conclusion that cut right to the heart of things.
In any case, the underlying cause of Ryuzu's disappearance had already reached Garfiel's ears. Ryuzu herself surely wasn't so oblivious that she hadn't considered how Garfiel would react to her own actions.

"That being the case, there are two possibilities."

"That she's decided she shouldn't see either you or Garfiel, and is seriously in hiding. And—"

"Or she knows she'll be searched for like this, and she's waiting to be found... maybe?"

If it was the former, the odds were high that they were out of luck. If small Ryuzu seriously set her mind to hiding, she could easily buy herself half a day. The only one with any real chance of finding her would be Garfiel, with his keen nose and mobility.

Conversely, if it was the latter, Subaru and Garfiel's odds were fifty-fifty.
For that to hold, Ryuzu would have to be arranging things that way herself.

――If there was a way to find her besides searching blindly, combing every corner—

"We should look for somewhere that has some connection to Ryuzu-san."

"Even so, she wasn't at her house... and as for the real one's place, Emilia-sama's disappeared too, making things even more troublesome."

"Right, that's the thing. I can't imagine she'd go to Roswaal's place, and the testing grounds — Garfiel's probably already checked there first thing. So that leaves..."

Somewhere that Subaru and Garfiel had equal chance of reaching, that wasn't the kind of place Garfiel would think to search first, but that θ would choose precisely because of that.
――If such a place existed.

"...Otto, I think I might've got it."

"R-Really? From that tiny exchange just now? Isn't this a misunderstanding?"

"I don't get why you're starting from denial, but the odds are pretty high. Or rather, if she's not there, we're basically out of luck."

Nodding at the stunned Otto, Subaru downed the last of the water in the pitcher in one gulp. Wiping his mouth, he stood and turned his face toward the direction of his "hunch."
If θ was there, then she hadn't fled at all. She was simply waiting in a place fitting for a conversation.

For Subaru, or perhaps for Garfiel.

"Don't you dare notice, Garfiel. ――I'm getting the parent-teacher conference done first."

"I have something important to talk about, so wait here a little while, okay?"

"Yes, Mother Fortuna."

Fortuna led Emilia to the princess's room and told her to wait there; Emilia gave an obedient reply.
Fortuna's eyes widened just slightly at the sight of Emilia smiling and seeing her off. Then she stroked Emilia's long silver hair and let out a wistful sigh.

Until now, whenever Emilia was left behind in the princess's room, she'd made no secret of her displeasure. She thought she was hiding it, but with her cheeks reddened and puffed out, looking pointedly away, anyone could tell the little girl was harboring a grudge.
Since that easy-to-read attitude of Emilia's hadn't been seen for a while now, it was only natural that Fortuna found it suspicious.

"...Emilia."

"Hm, what?"

"――No, never mind."

With her hand still buried in her daughter's hair, Fortuna, about to ask something, shook her head instead, leaving a faint smile for the innocent eyes gazing back at her.
Something didn't sit right, but a low-maintenance state of affairs was, at present, welcome. With someone waiting on her, Fortuna seemed to settle for accepting things as they were for now.

Waving goodbye and seeing Fortuna off, Emilia heard the sound of the bar dropping into place beyond the closed door. The door was securely locked; now the princess's room held Emilia alone.
――No, that wasn't quite true anymore these days.

"Okay, you can come out now."

Waiting about a minute to confirm Fortuna wouldn't be coming back, Emilia turned to face the room and called out to someone in a hushed voice.
Naturally, the only figure in the room was her own, and there should have been no presence to answer her — but suddenly, a pale bluish phosphorescence crossed the dimly lit room.

Watching that faint, glowing light, Emilia's violet eyes shook with delight.
Over the time she'd spent in the princess's room, she had, without quite realizing it, become able to consciously interact with this mysterious presence — a being she called a "fairy," though calling minor spirits that, while not entirely off the mark, carried a somewhat different nuance.
In any case, the minor spirit didn't seem to mind young Emilia's little breach of etiquette.

"Fairy, fairy, thank you again today."

At those words, the girl thanking it for not leaving her alone, the spirit scattered its phosphorescence and danced in place to show its feelings.

"――――"

Watching the dancing minor spirit, Emilia was increasingly convinced that this being of light was, for her, a good ally.
Not only did it rush to her side whenever she felt lonely being by herself, but it also helped her wishes along as far as its power allowed. When her guard was down while wandering around outside and someone was about to spot her, it would give her a subtle warning.

――The escapes from the princess's room had continued to repeat many times since then.

The gap in the tree roots at the back of the hollow, leading to the outside, seemed to have gone unnoticed by anyone, and Emilia could slip out to the outside from there whenever she wished. At first, forcing herself through had sometimes scraped up her clothes and body, but now that she'd learned the trick, there was no such worry anymore.
Having used the excuse of dirtying her clothes two, three times in a row, she was in real danger of Fortuna finally catching on. That had been a close call.

"I bet I was reeeally suspicious. But it's okay now. Hmph."

Puffing out her chest in pride at her own progress, Emilia was met with a swirl of blue light dancing around her head as if in praise. Her eyes quickly began to flicker and swim from the dazzle.

Even so, though Emilia had become a repeat escapee, her great adventures outside remained fairly tame — peeking in and eavesdropping on the adults' talks, just as she'd done the first time, or secretly eating fruit off a tree without permission, or playing pranks by rearranging things in someone's house. That was about the extent of it.
However, this sort of mischievous impulse tends to escalate gradually, and even an innocent girl like Emilia was no exception.

"All right. Let's head out again today, then."

"――――"

The swaying light seemed to agree with her, and with such a reassuring ally at her back, Emilia escaped the princess's room with something approaching boldness.
Widening the gap in the branches, now considerably loosened from repeated use, she squeezed her small body through and pulled herself out of the tree's hollow. The instant she tumbled out of the hole, a cushion of piled fallen leaves softly caught her.
It was an escape device Emilia had prepared after learning her lesson from tumbling out so many times.

"What should I do today, I wonder."

Picking leaves out of her hair, Emilia asked the light beside her. She knew there'd be no answer, but the way it responded through the intensity of its glow made her feel she wasn't alone.
Having made it outside was all well and good, but as usual, she was quickly running out of things to do. Even her pranks — if she kept it up, someone might catch on that it was her doing, and that could lead them to discover she was escaping the princess's room. She couldn't have the hole sealed up.

"Gotta lay low till things cool down, huh."

Muttering to herself with the air of a cunning criminal mastermind, Emilia set off walking through the settlement with no particular destination. It was probably around this time that, in the clearing at the heart of the forest, the usual meeting with the group in black robes would be starting. The forest's adults would be receiving gifts from the people in black.
During that time, the one talking with Mother Fortuna was a tall man; after seeing his face several times, Emilia had noticed he was called "Romanée-Conti," or sometimes "Juice," and had taken to privately calling him "Juice" in her own head.

Still, though the adults' meeting had fascinated her at first, after eavesdropping on it so many times, its novelty had worn off, and Emilia was gradually growing bored.
Much of the conversation between Fortuna and Juice was still beyond young Emilia's understanding. Even so, she kept diligently straining her ears to listen, because — just as the first time — her parents' names sometimes came up, and she was scheming to learn where they were and how they were doing.
Unfortunately, that scheme of hers had yet to bear any fruit.

"What if I just..."

What if she snuck into that cart that Juice and the others brought?

That covered wagon — if she slipped her small body in among the shadows of the cargo, they'd surely carry her right out of the forest without even trying. From what she'd observed so far, Juice and the others never inspected the cart when leaving the forest.
With a fairy's help, sneaking in when no one was looking should be no trouble at all.

"...Mmh."

She thought that far, then immediately gave up, realizing she couldn't possibly do it.
After all, going outside the forest would break the most important promise she'd made to Mother Fortuna.
――Never, ever leave the forest. For Emilia, there were many frightening things outside, and until she was grown up it was dangerous, so it was strictly forbidden — she'd been told so, firmly.

Even though she was already in the middle of breaking one rule, being unable to bring herself to selfishly cast aside other promises along with it just because — that was the virtue of this girl named Emilia.
And so, the escape-in-the-cart plan was scrapped at the planning stage. As for news of her father and mother, she'd have to think of some other way to find out.

"――Hup, hup."

While lost in thought, she arrived again today at the spot where the adults and Juice's group held their meeting. Emilia nimbly climbed a nearby tree and lay flat on a branch, straining her ears.
As usual, alongside the exchange of goods, Fortuna and Juice were chatting amiably. But today, Fortuna's expression seemed particularly soft.

"Lately Emilia's gotten brighter too, reeeally lively. Though it's a bit of a problem how she comes home covered in mud and dirties her clothes right away."

"Is that so... it's a good thing that she's in good health. If you like, I could prepare a change of clothes for her. Winter hibernation season is coming soon outside the forest, so there should be plenty of clothing being cleared out for the seasonal changeover."

"I'm sorry, I keep relying on you, and now it sounds like I'm hinting for more besides. ...Would you happen to have adult clothes as well?"

"Yes, of course. I'm sure they'd suit you very well, Fortuna-sama."

At Juice's gentle words, Fortuna's face colored with a complicated expression. Then, scratching her cheek in embarrassment with a finger, she said,

"...Where do you pick up lines like that. We've known each other a long time, but when did you learn to make jokes like that?"

"It's simply my honest opinion. Did I say something strange?"

"It's infuriating precisely because I know, by your nature, that you're not lying..."

Fortuna pressed a hand to her forehead in exasperation. But the smile curving her lips was proof that this exchange was anything but unpleasant to her.
No, if anything, Fortuna seemed to be rather enjoying the exchange.

"...Mmh."

Watching her mother like that, Emilia somehow found herself in a sour mood.
Fortuna, whose ordinary expression tended to look stern, showed a softness visible even from a distance only a little to her fellow elves, and a great deal to Emilia.
It felt, somehow, as if something precious of her mother's had been taken from her, and it left a bad taste.

"Hmph, stupid Juice."

She hurled a petulant remark at someone she barely knew, taking it out on him unfairly. Puffing out her cheeks, Emilia resolved that if Juice didn't say anything interesting enough today to catch her attention, she'd play some prank on his cart.

Maybe jam a cloth in the wheel, or spill oil on the cargo bed.
Emilia set her heart on such a modest act of revenge, but that revenge drama never made it to the stage.

"――So, is the seal all right?"

Lowering his voice, Juice posed his customary question to Fortuna. Fortuna gave a slight nod, and, exactly as always, replied,

"No change, as ever. Every single time, so conscientious of you."

"That is what my duty is for. Besides, the timing being what it is — there are cyclical concerns as well, but this year in particular there have been many moonless nights, and mana circulation has been insufficient. I can't help but worry there might be an adverse effect on the seal deep in the forest as well..."

"The moon... that explains it. That's why the forest's minor spirits haven't been quite themselves lately. ...Understood. There's no guarantee the seal hasn't loosened, so I'll go confirm it with my own eyes later."

"I appreciate it."

Juice bowed at the waist, and Fortuna nodded back with a serious gaze.
Eavesdropping on that exchange between the two, Emilia murmured "the depths of the forest..." silently to herself.

Emilia had, by this point, thoroughly explored the elf settlement. Naturally, she had also conquered every part of the forest that Fortuna permitted, to the point where — though she never said so aloud — she practically considered this forest her own backyard.
Even so, Emilia had no idea where this "depths of the forest" that Fortuna and the others spoke of might be. She guessed it was probably the deep part of the forest she'd been told was off-limits — but the thought that yet another thing was being kept secret from her only piled her frustration higher.

In any case, by this point Emilia's discontent had been building and building.
As ever, no information about her parents came to her at all, and the fresh excitement of her great adventures outside the princess's room had grown stale with repetition. On top of that, despite the adults' constant insistence that certain things were forbidden for Emilia, evidence kept piling up — in this short span of time, no less — that the adults themselves were the ones full of lies and secrets.

――Let's give everyone a little bit of trouble.

Who could possibly blame Emilia for thinking that?
At that moment, the mischievous impulse that had suddenly sprouted in Emilia swelled unchecked, blamed and scolded by no one, and its result was to hasten the arrival of "that moment."

And so, in later years, when it came to who would be blamed for what happened here, the answer was Emilia herself. She would go on to blame her own foolishness in this moment more harshly than anyone else ever could, and still pile on more guilt than even that, feeling it was never enough.

――But that later regret would never reach the young Emilia of this moment.

Just as Emilia rolled up her sleeves with her misguided sense of purpose, Fortuna and the others' meeting happened to conclude. The exchange of goods ended without any major issues this time too, and the adults saluted as Juice and his group bowed and withdrew.

Watching that through to the end, Emilia lightly leapt down from the branch and hurried back to the princess's room at a run. Diving through the gap in the tree roots, back inside the hollow, she scrambled to fabricate her alibi.
In short order she finished a drawing, changed her doll's clothes, and scattered some snacks about half-eaten.
Just as she wiped the sweat from her brow after finishing this bit of work, Fortuna's voice came from outside.

"Emilia, sorry to keep you waiting. Were you a good girl today too?"

"Uh... y-yes, I was a good girl? I was. Yep, uh-huh, I was."

"――――"

Satisfied with her flawless act, Emilia congratulated herself on the deception. Fortuna quietly narrowed her eyes and stared fixedly at her.
Feeling uncomfortable under that sharp gaze, but thinking that acting strangely now would only deepen suspicion, Emilia pressed on.

"Wh-what, Mother Fortuna? Even if you look at me like that, I didn't do anything. I ate snacks, and drew pictures, and played with my doll. I didn't go outside at all. Really, I didn't."

"――I see, well, that's fine then."

Fooled by Emilia's acting, Fortuna seemed to have believed her completely. Feeling a pang of guilt at deceiving her mother, but scolding herself not to let something this small get to her, Emilia began plotting the scenario for her merciless revenge drama.

Fortuna and the others had been talking about some "seal" in the depths of the forest. A seal, Emilia recalled, meant something like a place where something was hidden away.
Which meant something that would be trouble if it got outside was hidden there.

――In Emilia's mind, the content of her revenge against Fortuna and the others was decided.

She would find that place in the depths of the forest where the secret "seal" was kept, and use it as a bargaining chip to argue back whenever Fortuna and the others scolded her for something.
She'd use the location of the seal in the depths of the forest as her trump card for whenever her escapes from the princess's room were discovered.

Emilia, eyes shining at her own brilliant idea, failed to notice the contradiction: that in order to escape punishment for one forbidden act, she was about to dip her hands into an even worse forbidden act.

Her mother took her hand and led her out of the princess's room. Afterward, Fortuna said she had a little errand to attend to after bringing Emilia home. Going by her earlier conversation with Juice, Fortuna was surely about to go check on that "seal" now.
So—

"――Please, okay?"

Emilia asked the pale bluish phosphorescence to follow after her mother, and gave a small wink with one eye.
By the time her limbs had grown to their full length, the faintest traces of a beauty capable of captivating others with a mere smile had already begun to bud, even in this young girl's form.

This was the second time Subaru had visited this place, and the first time in this particular loop.

A building standing alone atop a small rise. Not so shabby as to be called dilapidated, but with an air of an unremarkable, ordinary house lacking any striking features.
Inside, two rooms — a bedroom and a living room, the living room doubling as the living space with a simple kitchen area, arranged somewhat like an apartment back in his original world. Not cramped for one person living alone, but surely a tight fit for one adult and two children living together.

Only now did Subaru grasp the significance of this building, and the realization stirred something in him.
He stood before the door and knocked from outside. After a moment's silence, a voice from within answered, "Come in," and Subaru felt relief at having guessed correctly.

Then, immediately steeling that relief back into resolve, he put his hand on the doorknob and opened the door.
The faint scent of old wood grazed his nostrils. Feeling the lukewarm air against his skin as he stepped inside the room—

"Took you longer than I expected."

A figure sat on a simple bed at the back of the room. That person seemed to be just about to pour herself another cup of tea, tipping boiled water into a cup.
That must have been the source of the lukewarm air drifting from the room. Subaru noted three cups on the table — though only one was filled — and said,

"So I'm the first guest to arrive?"

"That's right. Sū-bou's first. I've brewed the tea strong — that all right with you?"

"Either way's fine. Strong or weak, leaf-flavor is leaf-flavor."

"Not much satisfaction in brewing for you, is there. I understand now why Ram used to grumble about it."

Ryuzu — θ — gave a wry laugh at Subaru's blunt answer and drew an empty cup closer. Pouring hot water into the cup at hand, letting the tea leaves steep, she pushed it toward Subaru.

"Here, you must be thirsty. First, one for the road."

"If I chug down a 'one for the road,' with my remaining HP that sounds like a straight shot to the church. That aside, thanks for the drink."

Subaru's teasing left a look of bewilderment creasing θ's brow. He blew on the cup handed to him to cool it, then brought it to his lips. The thick, grassy taste rolled across his tongue and down his throat.
No matter how many times, what variety, or whose hands brewed it, leaves were leaves.

"Even Rem's tea I never got used to... my body must just plain reject this stuff by now."

"Good to know the reaction was predictable enough not to even need asking. I'll never brew for you again."

Glaring at Subaru, who was making a face and sticking out his tongue, θ drank down her own cup of tea in one go. Then, smoothing back her own long hair, the hem of her clothes trailing, she settled herself back down neatly on the bed, and, placing Subaru properly in front of her once more, said,

"Meant to ease our minds before we started talking, and instead it stirred up some unexpected discord."

"If we drag side-story content into the main story, readers who happened to miss it are going to end up confused later, so let's skip that. Reset, reset — let's talk."

"Easy for the one saying it..."

Sighing in exasperation, θ then gazed intently at her own small palm. Then her gaze passed over Subaru, seeming to look straight through the door to somewhere outside.

"Even so... it was you after all, Sū-bou. Thought as much. More likely than Gā-bou coming here, that Sū-bou would come alone, or that neither of you would think of it and time would simply run out."

"...Well, that's a pretty unfair set of odds you stacked against Garfiel. If he heard that, he'd probably be pretty upset."

"Upset, raging — that would be fine, if it came to that... I imagine a far more serious reaction, myself. If Gā-bou were here now, that worry, at least, would be laid to rest."

Carving a lonely smile, θ looked toward the wall of the living room.
Following her gaze, Subaru saw hanging on the wall a pair of silver-gleaming metal shields — crossed against each other, mounted on the wall, a sight he'd seen once before.

Shields that carried the memory of Garfiel and Frederica, brother and sister, clashing them together in play as children — meaning this place was Garfiel and Frederica's childhood home.

Why θ had chosen this place as the site for their final conversation, Subaru didn't know.
He only remembered, from a previous loop, having talked here before, and that it had lodged in his memory as a place holding special meaning both for the Ryuzus and for Garfiel.
Relying on that memory to make his way here, he'd found θ waiting for him, exactly as expected.

"Glad you came alone, Sū-bou. This isn't the sort of talk I much want others overhearing."

"Yeah, I left Otto behind. He's not really equipped to keep up with what comes next."

It came out sounding, unintentionally, like leaving behind a warrior who lacked the strength to proceed — though that wasn't the intent. It was only that Ryuzu's past was, inevitably, something that couldn't be spoken of without touching on the Witch. Subaru alone should be the one to step into that territory.
So for now, he'd entrusted Otto with a different role instead.

"θ-san, you're the one among the Ryuzu-sans who went into the tomb, right?"

"Shīta?"

"Ah, sorry. I was calling you that for convenience, on my own. Yesterday's was Σ-san, and the remaining two are α and β. You probably won't like it, so I'll hold off, but—"

"...No, it's fine. So that's what you meant. Ah, I see, I see... not bad, actually, not bad at all."

Muttering "Shīta, Shīta" to herself a few times under her breath, θ loosened her cheeks in a bashful sort of way. Then she closed her eyes quietly, and a few seconds later, opening them again, she said,

"What I'm about to tell you is part of the origins of the Sanctuary, as far as I witnessed them... and part of what happened up until Ryuzu Meyer came to be sealed away in that crystal like that."

"――Okay."

"What you'll think of it once you've heard it, Sū-bou, and what you'll let me hear in turn... as one of the four Ryuzus who represent the Sanctuary, I'll entrust our fate to that."

Saying something so weighty with responsibility, θ smiled at Subaru as he held his breath.

"I'd ask that you face it with all due seriousness, wouldn't you say?"
