Arc 7 Chapter 94: "For Whose Sake, This Resolve"

―A distant, thunder-like roar shook at the consciousness of Beatrice, her eyes closed.

"...What an unpleasant sound, I suppose."

In the mind of the girl who murmured that quietly, memories of hearing similar sounds many times before stirred back to life.
Four hundred years distant now, in the era of the "Witch," when war never ceased, it had not been uncommon for nations to clash against each other, for great numbers of people to cross weapons and take one another's lives.

Beatrice did not know battlefields deeply.
She had been born and raised in the mansion of her mother, Echidna, a place with no connection to the quarrels of men. Even so, the fighting far below had always felt like noise to her. Time passed, and she spent those four hundred years cradling the book entrusted to her; in that span, though the outside world grew noisy now and then, it drifted ever further from the frenzy of that age.
And now, something close to that frenzy she had once kept at arm's length was right there beside her.

―That was the clash of countless lives surrounding the imperial capital.

"Uu — I'm worried, sir. I'm anxious, sir."

"Ū understand how Shū feel. Still, Ū and others go out too?"

"Auu! Uwau! Aa, u!"

"N-no, that's no good, sir! Our job is to stand by! Standing by, sir!"

Such an exchange came flying into the ears of Beatrice, eyes shut, working hard at conserving her energy.
What she heard were children's voices she'd grown thoroughly used to these past few days — Schult, Utakata, and Rui Arneb. The young children waiting alongside Beatrice in the tent pitched behind the main camp — it sounded fine to call it guarding the rear of a headquarters overlooking the battlefield from a low hill, but Beatrice, at least, understood that was nothing more than a pretext no one actually expected them to fulfill.

Properly speaking, as noncombatants, it would have been sensible to leave the children behind in the walled city. In fact, among the residents who had relocated from the demon city together with Yorna Mishigure, those noncombatants unable to endure battle had likewise left the front lines, lending their help instead through logistics.
The reason the children hadn't gone the same way lay with their respective guardians.

'A minor skirmish would be one thing, but for so grand a stage, keeping him at a distance would be the crueler choice. The edge of the battlefield will do. Wherever he stands, he shan't be able to miss my brilliance, after all.'
'Utakata too, one of us Shudrak. Coward who turn back on battlefield, hunting ground — not fit to be one of the People of Shudrak. Naturally, bring her to battlefield.'

Such were the words of Schult's and Utakata's respective guardians, and since Schult and Utakata themselves were plenty eager besides, it was an opinion no one could overrule. Both statements likely sprang from different ethics and convictions, but so long as the parties involved were satisfied, Beatrice had no right to interfere, for now.

Honestly, as a Great Spirit, Beatrice didn't much care for being treated as a package deal alongside these children. That said, everyone in camp had their own role, and it made sense that Beatrice, whose freedom of movement was so limited, would be entrusted with this one.

And that entrusted role was—

"Uu, auu."

Rui, who — like Schult and the others — had been ordered to wait in the tent.

"—."

Only her treatment was clearly different from the other young children's. Unlike Schult and the rest, she had no obvious guardian, but the greatest concern was that she was someone they couldn't afford to take their eyes off.
Naturally — she was a Sin Archbishop. No matter what those who'd come to know her in the Empire might say, one after another, that would never lessen her danger. So, unlike the other children, leaving Rui behind had never been an option.

'Right now, she's the bomb whose fuse is hardest to read. Beatrice-chan, I don't want to put too much on you, but please keep a close watch on her. We can't tell Emilia-sama or Garfiel about this, so let's at least keep our own heads cool.'

So Otto had said, before they threw themselves fully into the great upheaval.
He might have said it to keep Beatrice, who had no choice but to wait due to conserving her energy, from being crushed under the powerlessness of having no role — but it was also a remark that betrayed a certain blindness to what stood right under his own feet, which was worrying in itself.
It was worrying that he thought he had a cool head on, with that face of his. — Petra and Frederica had probably shared the same worry, so no doubt they'd sorted him out somehow.

Anyway—

"Don't be so fidgety, I suppose. Settle down, would you."

"Bē, you awake?"

"I've been awake the whole time, I suppose. ...And that way of calling me is unpleasant, I suppose. It makes me feel like someone's sticking their tongue out and going 'akkanbee' at me."

There was no bed to lie on in the tent, nor Emilia's or Petra's lap to borrow. So Beatrice sat in a plain chair with her eyes shut, and Utakata pursed her lips at her.
At that reaction, Beatrice cracked her eyes open and surveyed the three restless figures in the tent. Schult looked anxious, Utakata had more fighting spirit than she knew what to do with, and Rui — whatever she might be thinking — stamped her feet as if frustrated, flinching at every sound of battle.

"—."

Looking at the gesture alone, there was nothing suspicious about Rui's behavior. Simply a child reacting a touch too sensitively to changes around her — one might carelessly start thinking of her as an ordinary child, the same way those easygoing others who knew nothing of the threat of "Gluttony" did.
But Beatrice couldn't afford that.

"Emilia and the others are working hard for Subaru and the others, I suppose."

Emilia and the rest had gotten involved in an upheaval they'd had no obligation to intervene in, throwing themselves into a life-or-death fight, because there was a high chance that Subaru and Rem — their companions — were there.
And if Beatrice couldn't fight alongside them, then the least she could do was remove any cause for worry left at their backs by keeping watch over Rui, cutting away any room for their attention to wander.
So, amid shallow breaths, Beatrice was steeling herself, when—

"Uu?"

"...What do you think you're doing, I suppose."

Beatrice pressed her lips tight as a small hand stroked her head. Glancing over, she saw it was Rui, brows drooping, watching her carefully.
For a moment Beatrice stiffened at the danger of that hand touching her, but she felt no dangerous air from Rui, nor any sign of an attempt to strip away Beatrice's "Name." As far as Beatrice knew, for "Gluttony" to wield its Authority it needed to speak the target's "Name" and devour it — but to begin with, no sound of proper meaning had ever come from Rui's mouth. Of course, if Rui herself believed it counted, there was still a chance the Authority could activate, so Beatrice kept her nerves on edge, watching whether the hand on her head might travel to Rui's own mouth.

If Rui ever showed the slightest sign of trying to eat Beatrice's "Name," then at that moment—

"Bē, face scary. Still hate Rū?"

"What a thing to say about Betty's adorable face, I suppose. Besides, Betty doesn't hate this girl, I suppose. ...I loathe her, I suppose."

At the sight of Beatrice glaring at Rui, Utakata pulled up the corners of her own eyes with a finger. Even as she insisted she wasn't making such an unlovely face, Beatrice's true feelings slipped out at the end.
Yes — she did loathe Rui. Not just Rui, but the Sin Archbishop of "Gluttony." — Everything that tormented Subaru and the others.

"It's a bitter regret, I suppose."

For the Emilia Camp, and above all for Subaru, the greatest victim of "Gluttony" was none other than Rem. Watching Subaru suffer over that girl who kept sleeping on and on, Beatrice had long regretted the time before she'd Contracted with him, back when she'd been indifferent to everyone around her.

If only, she thought.
If only Beatrice had opened her heart sooner, shown willingness to cooperate with Subaru and the others, things might have gone differently. She wouldn't have let the White Whale, the Witch Cult, all those sorts run wild as they pleased, and Subaru wouldn't have had to keep wearing that sorrowful face beside the ever-sleeping Rem.

So she'd resolved never again to let Subaru suffer that way, and decided to stay by his side always — and yet, here she was. Separated from Subaru in the end, forced to conserve her energy until she couldn't even properly lend her strength to Emilia and the others. — Her reason for existing was in tatters.

"B-Beatrice-chan, speaking that way about Rui-sama..."

"Betty doesn't think 'poor thing' at all, I suppose. Honestly, you're awfully familiar too. Why is it '-sama' for everyone else, but '-chan' for Betty, I suppose? There's not enough respect, I suppose."

"Uuu... I'm sorry, sir, Beatrice-chan."

Sensing the dangerous mood, Beatrice glared sharply at Schult for cutting in. How did she look to Schult, who — frightened as he was — never changed his attitude? Surely he didn't think of her as some little girl, just because she was always asleep.

"Anyway, don't go tiring Betty out, I suppose. Being left here in the first place is meant to keep Betty and the rest of you from doing anything unnecessary. Galling as it is to go along with it..."

"Uu."

"—it's still far better than letting you run loose, I suppose."

Rui's hand was still resting on her head; Beatrice caught it in her own and sat the wide-eyed girl down in the chair right beside her. Deciding she might as well keep it this way, she held onto Rui's hand without letting go.

"And that goes for you two as well, I suppose. Schult, stop wandering about. Utakata, if you've got nothing to do, go tend to your bow or something, I suppose. And then—"

"—It seems there was no need for me to step in after all."

"Hm..."

As she dealt with the restless children, a composed voice suddenly sounded at the tent flap. Looking over, she saw a round-faced man with curly hair peering in — Zikr Osman.
Beatrice frowned at the appearance of a man who should have been directing operations at headquarters alongside Abel.

"Zī! Busy, but come here! Ū and others go now!"

"That's not it at all, I suppose! ...But what business brings you here?"

"My apologies to Lady Utakata, but it isn't your turn just yet. It's only that there's a chance we'll move the camp before long, so I thought I'd let you know in advance."

"Move the camp, sir?"

At Zikr's words, spoken with intelligent, round eyes, Schult tilted his own round eyes in question. Nodding "Yes" at that, Zikr gestured with a hand toward the battlefield behind him.

"At present we're devoting our forces to breaching the walls, but once the summit opens up, we'll be pushing into the imperial capital's Crystal Palace. At that point, the commanders will need to move to the front lines as well."

"Abel doesn't look like he can fight, I suppose. Wouldn't it be safer for him to stay in back, concentrating on giving orders?"

"As you say... but the soldiers won't follow, otherwise. Even if he manages to push His Excellency the Emperor aside, no one will acknowledge him sitting the throne."

"—. What a troublesome national character, I suppose."

"Let the Empire's people be strong" was the basic principle of the Vollachian Empire. But apparently, where seizing the nation was concerned, that applied even to its leaders. Unless the one at the top showed strength, they'd promptly be overthrown from below — that was the punchline.

"Yes, troublesome indeed. But that is our homeland, after all."

Shaking his head slowly, a faint, wry smile rose to Zikr's lips. What emotion was mixed into it, Beatrice found hard to make out. He surely wasn't accepting it gladly, yet neither did he seem to deny it.

"Zikr-sama, could it be you're heading off to battle, sir?"

"Zī?"

Just then, beside Beatrice — who had been watching Zikr's mouth — Schult asked that, and Utakata called his name in a quiet voice.
At the children's gazes, Zikr raised his eyebrows slightly, then said—

"As you've guessed, I'm going out as well. Even though we overwhelm them in numbers, we're at a disadvantage in the count of 'Generals' below Sub-General rank, so I must set things in motion."

"...Why, I wonder."

"—? What do you mean?"

"Why did you bother coming here yourself, I suppose. In your position, couldn't you just order a subordinate? I can't make sense of you showing your face at Betty's tent, I suppose."

"Ah, that."

Right before heading into battle should be exactly the moment one wants to sharpen one's focus. And yet Zikr had shown his face in the tent of children who were of no use as fighting strength, set aside on this battlefield.
Asked why, Zikr laughed, looking bashful.

"Headquarters is all men, and the Shudrak women are out on the front lines too. If I'm to receive cheers before heading into battle, it's a woman's that makes the heart dance, after all."

"...Huh?"

"I see, sir! Indeed, Utakata-sama, Rui-sama, and Beatrice-chan are all here, sir!"

Hearing an answer entirely outside her expectations, Beatrice's eyes went wide. But in contrast to the frozen Beatrice, Schult wore a face of understanding, and Utakata folded her short arms and nodded. Then, turning toward the dumbfounded Beatrice—

"Give up. Zī, like this from start."

"T-thinking he was the one sane person here was a huge mistake, I suppose..."

There had been signs. For some reason he was called a "coward," and rather than argue back at all, he even looked proud of it. It was only that she'd had no reason to get deeply involved, so she'd never said anything. As a result, at this eleventh hour, she found herself forced to witness Zikr's eccentric side firsthand—

"Besides, there's also the calculation that knowing what's at my back will keep me from hesitating."

"—. Don't you have family?"

"A mother, and many sisters. Though the moment I threw my lot in with this side, their standing must have taken quite a blow. I'm an utter disgrace to my family."

Zikr had originally been a "General" on the Empire's side, at Sub-General rank. Now that he'd defected, it was only natural that his family would bear some of the blame. Zikr had found meaning in joining this side, fully prepared for that. Whether that Abel of all people was worth it, Beatrice wondered — but the reason was something no outsider could know.
Still, it nagged at her.

"What do you fight for, I wonder."

"—."

"You shouldn't be like those who fight simply for the sake of fighting. So why, I wonder."

For a common soldier, that might be fine — and the fact that so many warriors held such a philosophy was exactly what made the Vollachian Empire frightening — but a "General," Zikr especially, ought to be different.
At Beatrice's look, Zikr knit his thick brows thoughtfully.

"Naturally, for the tomorrow of the Vollachian Empire, to which I give my trust and devote my loyalty."

He answered with a strong-willed voice, then continued with "Though—"

"—I'm a 'coward' after all, so it's doubtful how far I can hold out. Which is why — might I ask something of you, Lady Beatrice?"

"...Ask what, I suppose."

"That's obvious. — A blessing from a lovely maiden."

As he spoke, Zikr drew the sword at his own waist and held it out to Beatrice. Narrowing her eyes, she looked down at what was offered, then sighed.

"I'll have you know I don't really know the proper etiquette for this, I suppose."

"With this sort of thing, it's the feeling that matters. So long as the hearts of the one giving and the one receiving are connected, etiquette isn't important."

"When exactly did our hearts connect, I suppose. ...Honestly."

Muttering, Beatrice took the sword. Before her, Zikr bowed his head reverently and knelt. At his earnest bearing, Beatrice recalled a certain ceremony from before — the time Subaru had received his knighthood at Emilia's hand — and followed that example.
With the sword she held, she tapped each of the kneeling Zikr's shoulders once.

"Go and do it properly, I suppose. For the sake of your own wish, and no one else's."

"As you wish."

Having received Beatrice's blessing, Zikr answered solemnly and raised his face. He sheathed the sword returned to him, then looked over at Utakata and the others.
At that look, Utakata called to him, "Zī," and said—

"Ū is Shudrak warrior, so not do like Bē. Send off in warrior's way."

So saying, Utakata drew the knife at her waist — one made from a beast's fang — held it to her own palm, and cut a thin line to draw blood. Beside her, Schult made a pained face as Utakata gently pressed her blood-wet palm against the armor Zikr wore, leaving a handprint.

"Shudrak blood. Strong warrior's blood. Zī be strong too."

"I'm grateful."

"U-um, I can't think of anything like what Beatrice-chan or Utakata-sama did, so I'll just cheer you on, sir! Hurrah! Hurrah! Zikr-sama! Sir!"

Following Utakata's lead, Schult too raised his voice to send Zikr off with cheer. Receiving it with a smile, Zikr rose to his feet. Then—

"Whoa."

"Auu, uu-u."

Slipping smoothly out of Beatrice's grasp, Rui threw her arms around the waist of the now-standing Zikr. Startled by the girl's embrace, Zikr let his brows droop, then patted her shoulder.
Even to Beatrice's eyes, Rui's action was clearly an expression of positive feeling toward Zikr. The reason she hadn't stopped her in time was that there had been no ominous sign about it.

"Zikr Osman, heading off to battle now. — Please, all of you, stay well."

"Good fortune in battle, sir!"

Releasing himself from Rui's embrace, Zikr left the tent with a clear, bright expression. Schult waved big at his retreating back, and Utakata narrowed her eyes as she watched him go.
Watching the small back grow distant, Beatrice understood that Zikr was heading to the battlefield strong, and with a resolve ready to die. — Exactly like those days four hundred years ago.

War had been a distant thing. But visitors had often come to her mother's mansion, all of them alike seeking the "Witch's" knowledge, her cooperation. Every one carrying a strong wish to turn a bad situation good.
But no matter what advice they received from the wise, it was they themselves who had to make it real. Seeing their futures in her mother's counsel, many had turned their backs on the mansion and headed toward their own futures. — Zikr's back had looked just the same as theirs, back then.

"You."

"U?"

Having seen Zikr off, Beatrice spoke to Rui. Turning around, Rui's round eyes went rounder still in her young face as she stared back. Thinking nothing at all — no, that was wrong.
Still, looking at that face which seemed to be scheming nothing, Beatrice asked—

"Which side are you really on, anyway?"

Struck in the chest by the swung twin ice blades, Madelyn's eyes went wide as she leaped back hard.

Even without the Flying Winged Blade, Madelyn's dragon claws and monstrous strength were plenty frightening. Emilia's firmly hardened ice, while not as tough as iron, had at least the hardness of a sturdy boulder — hard enough that striking it carelessly bare-handed might break the hand instead. And Madelyn shattered it easily, with nothing more than a hooked finger.

"Sorry."

As the soldier made in Subaru's likeness was destroyed, Emilia heard Beatrice's scream in her head. Apologizing to the Beatrice in her mind, Emilia stayed wary of Madelyn's counterattack — and wary as she was, chose to press forward still further rather than fall back.

The shattered twin blades turned to ice fragments, and in their place she formed a great ice hammer. Right before the eyes of Emilia as she wound up, two kneeling ice soldiers linked arms to form a footing, launching Emilia, who stepped on it, upward. "Eh."

The instant she gripped hard, the hammer's handle shattered. — No, not just the handle; the whole thing broke. The ice hammer that had struck Madelyn's head — it was the hammer that hadn't been able to withstand the opponent's hardness. Emilia's eyes went wide at that realization the same instant the claw-tip below flicked upward.
At the edge of her vision, the instant the claw-tip moved, she cut a small ice shield into its path.

"Auh!"

But the ice shield was smashed in less than an instant, and a pained cry rose up. With the instant she'd bought, she tilted her body; the claw-tip passed by the side of her head, and the shock of it faintly grazing her silver hair shook Emilia's vision hard and violently.

"Don't underestimate a dragon!!"

The roaring Madelyn raised her claw. A wind of destruction arose, peeling up the ground. She leaped back on reflex to dodge the claw, but the wind that came a moment late caught her, flinging Emilia's body back hard as though in return. Swallowed by the wave of upturning earth, Emilia's body bounced along the ground, again and again, and was sent flying.

For an instant Emilia tensed her stomach, trying to brace and endure — but instantly judged this wasn't something to let hit her, reached out a hand, and gripped something cold and hard.

"—."

The ice soldier forcefully hauled her up and flung her onward. In the view of Emilia, her body flipping as she flew, the ice soldier was swallowed by the very blade attack it had dodged for her, and shattered—

"Subaru—!!"

It wasn't Subaru, but Emilia cried out with the feeling that Subaru had been struck down.

"Please!"

The danger of the Flying Winged Blade was such that an ordinary person couldn't tell the difference, but its rotation weakened — just the tiniest bit, just a feeling's worth, just a little. It felt like it weakened, anyway.

"No — it weakened!!"

Declaring it loudly, it really did start to look that way.

"Hyah, yaaah—!!"

Her leg, on the verge of being knocked back, extended fully, and even as cracks spread across the whole of her ice boots, Emilia's kick launched the Flying Winged Blade up into the sky.

"Wha—!?"

Emilia had made that ice wall seriously. Its hardness and density were as described before — it wasn't something to be broken easily.

The things she named, the things standing in her way. Emilia, of course, understood she herself stood in opposition to her. But was that healer and that old fart, too, meant as her enemies?
Madelyn — a Dragonkin, an ally of the Empire, rampaging in opposition to Emilia and the others.

Just as Madelyn said — if she couldn't get Madelyn to open up, then she herself should have opened her own heart first. Even on this battlefield, Emilia had, over and over, put what she wanted to do and say first. So why, at just this moment, had she thought to behave herself?

Why did she fight? Why was she here? The answer to all of it was—

"I came to bring back my precious knight. ...No, everyone's precious knight."

So saying, Emilia touched the shoulder of the ice soldier right beside her — the one made in Subaru's likeness. She thought she'd made it well. She'd managed to reproduce even his hairstyle and his "jarsey" jacket pretty well.
But no matter how well she made it resemble him, she couldn't reproduce Subaru's reliability. His forward-looking words, his dependable manner, the kindness that always entertained her — all of it. Because the Natsuki-Subaru-ness of Natsuki Subaru — the thing that warmed Emilia's chest just by thinking of it — was something only the real him could possess.

"He's a person who matters to me sooo much. Him, and the girl who should be with him, and so many people waiting for them to come home — that's why I'm here. That's what I can work really hard for."

"—."

"That's the reason I fight. Madelyn, what about you?"

Maybe there was someone, or something, she wanted to protect. If so, then maybe they could promise each other — that she would cherish it, that she wouldn't hurt it — and stop fighting. Maybe a path would open to reach Subaru and Rem, the two she wanted to bring home.

But that hope and wish Emilia held—

"—Balleroy Temeglyph."

"...That name is...?"

"The name of the man who was supposed to become a dragon's husband. — That's the whole of a dragon's reason for fighting-cha."

—Betrayed, by a Madelyn fighting for something already lost.

"—."

Hearing that voice, its emotion gone dead, Emilia caught her breath. She couldn't find words to offer on reflex, and the distance was too far to reach out a hand on reflex either, so Emilia was too late.

So—

"—I will kill the man who killed Balleroy. That is a dragon's cherished wish."

With a violent sound, Madelyn crushed the wall of ice in her grip. Literally — there was no other way to describe it but crushed. The hand pressed to the wall clenched shut, and the shattering that sprang from it spread through the entire wall, which broke apart all at once.
Envisioning Madelyn retrieving the blade beyond it and coming at her again, Emilia, biting down on a bitter feeling, moved toward Madelyn together with her ice soldiers.

She stepped forward to cling to this Dragonkin girl, desperate to fill the hole left in her chest by the loss of someone precious to her — and in that very instant.

"Mezoreiaaaaa—!!"

Looking up at the sky, Madelyn screamed that out even as she was showered with the shattering fragments of the ice wall.
As though slicing through the ice fragments scattering and glittering through the air, the high voice echoed up into the sky again and again, and hearing it, Emilia's eyes went wide, and she looked skyward.

That resonance in Madelyn's voice — she had heard it before. Her lips had spun that same sound once before, and when it had been spun then—

"That time, a huge attack..."

A rain of white light had, in the span of a single breath, swept away the walled city and changed the very terrain. That time, Priscilla had been there with her, so between the two of them they'd somehow managed to defend against it. If the same thing happened now, could Emilia hold her own alone?

That would be very difficult. As Emilia panicked over that, above her head, the same thing didn't happen.
But she couldn't rejoice that the same thing hadn't happened, either.

Because—

"—I, Mezoreia. In accordance with the voice of my Beloved Child, I shall become the wind from the heavens."

Beating its great white wings, its huge body wreathed in cloud — the "Cloud Dragon" Mezoreia, summoned by Madelyn Eschart, was descending into the sky above the imperial capital.
