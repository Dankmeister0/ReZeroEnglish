Arc 2 Chapter 29: "Natsuki Subaru's Restart"

—There was only "nothingness" there.

Vaguely, that consciousness idly looked around. "Looked around" isn't quite the right expression in this case.

That consciousness had no eyes. For that matter, it had no hands, no feet, no part of a body at all. It simply floated there, an uncertain thing called consciousness with no substance to it.

Understanding nothing, conveying nothing, it looked around all the same.
Dark, and on top of that, empty—a room. A world wrapped in pitch black where the boundary between ceiling and wall couldn't be told, where there was no way to gauge the size of the room.

Then, suddenly, meaning was born into that world of everlasting darkness.

At the position that would be directly ahead for the consciousness, a human silhouette abruptly came into being.
Slender, and likewise cloaked entirely in pitch black, an indistinct outline. Especially from the upper body up, a haze hung over it, strongly obstructing the consciousness's perception.

At the silhouette's appearance, the consciousness felt something for the first time.
And before that feeling had cooled, the shadow moved languidly, working to convey something to the consciousness.

It didn't understand. Nothing was conveyed.
And yet, for some reason, it couldn't tear its awareness away from that silhouette—.

*"—We still can't meet."*

Leaving behind that faint whisper, the world abruptly vanished.

The instant he woke up, the thing Subaru thought most fiercely was: *Thank you, Mom and Dad, for giving me such a sturdy body.*

He'd thrown himself off a cliff, dropped headfirst straight down onto the ground. There was no doubt his cervical vertebrae had nearly snapped from the impact, but eternal rest didn't come immediately to Subaru in that state—no such luck.

"Seriously, not even managing an instant death, still conscious for a bit... that's not even in the same league as 'unlucky.'"

Right beside the corpse of the horned beast that had fallen to its death, unable to move, tormented by agony, counting down the seconds to death.
He could guess he probably hadn't lasted very long before losing his life, but even so, the felt time of the experience was enough to feel like hours.

If Beatrice had come down to check on his safety and found him still breathing, and healed him—the thought alone was unbearable to imagine.

"No way am I ever killing myself again. Not happening, even if it kills me."

Even recalling the self-questioning he'd gone through up to that moment made him want to die, literally.
For all his posturing, on the inside he'd been that much of a coward. There was no way he could honestly call it a "decision."

But even so—,

"I made it... back."

He reached out a hand, clenched it into a fist, and confirmed his own place in the world.
A soft bed. Neatly made sheets. The reddish-brown, samue-like clothes—familiar, nostalgic, and then—,

"Sister, sister. It seems our guest is still half-asleep."
"Rem, Rem. It seems our guest has been half-asleep since a very young age."

The twin girls stood hand in hand before the bed, gazing at Subaru with two pairs of eyes.

A voice he knew well, a gesture he'd seen many times. A fierce greeting repeated four times before, and now, for the fifth time.

There was so much he wanted to say. Thoughts he wanted to voice piled up enough to strike his chest. And yet none of them would come, as if caught in his throat.
Seeing Rem alive and well, seeing Ram's as-rude-as-ever attitude, feeling the two of them treating him so naturally—an emotion he couldn't control welled up inside him.

"Guest, guest, what's wrong? Are you feeling unwell?"
"Guest, guest, what's the matter? Having some kind of chronic seizure?"

The moment they saw his face, the twins grew confused at Subaru falling silent and pressing a hand to his chest. Splitting to either side, they approached the bed, reaching out to comfort the huddled Subaru as if to ease his pain.
And at their approach, Subaru—

"Gabacho!"

"Eh?"
"Ah."

—said the sound effect aloud as he threw his arms around both twins at once and hugged them with all his might.

Blue hair to the right, pink hair to the left, his small frame easily encircled by one arm each. He pushed his face in between the two of them, brought face to face in front of him, savoring the feel of their hair against his cheeks, and—

"Aaah, I'm back—I'm really back. Finally, here, like this, with these feelings intact. I'm... home!"

He rubbed his face roughly against both their heads, then patted their backs and released them.
The twins hadn't been able to hide their shock at Subaru's sudden embrace, but they quickly regained their composure and sprang away from the bed as if leaping,

"Sister, sister, our guest just made a plaything of me."
"Rem, Rem, our guest just made me the target of his bestial lust."

"Hah, that's not it at all. Sorry, but you two are both, well, subtly, ever so slightly, unfortunately outside my strike zone. Remember that. I don't really go for the younger-sister type! And girls, for me, it's gotta be long hair, long!"

Girlishness dwells in the hair—that was Subaru's personal theory.
Therefore, the longer a woman's hair, the better. And a silver-haired heroine was, for Subaru, pure justice. Add in a touch of an affectionate older-woman quality, and there was nothing more to ask for. Hence,

"Emilia-tan is my strike zone, dead center! Sorry, but you two are just off the mark. Still a nice pitch, though, no doubt about it!"

"Sister, sister. I don't quite understand what he's saying, but I feel insulted."
"Rem, Rem. It's crazy talk, but I feel like I've been degraded somehow."

The twins' response was rather severe toward Subaru as he rattled on rapid-fire.
But at their attitude, Subaru sprang up off the bed. He stood barefoot on the floor, spun sharply on the spot, turning. Facing back toward the center of the room, he pointed both hands' fingers at the two of them, still standing frozen beside the bed,

"Now hear me out, hey, listen up, listen to me. Lay your heads in each other's laps, clean out your ears real good, dig deep, and hear me out."

Holding out his palms toward the silent pair, opening and closing them in a pointless gesture,

"I get it, there's various circumstances, well, I'm sure there's all sorts of complicated backstory going on with you two. I really get it, but even so, I'm gonna say this loud and clear."

As if gauging him, the twins prompted him onward with silence.
He supposed their assessment of him had probably already begun, right from this point. Conscious of that gaze, he had no clever plan to struggle and scheme his way into winning their trust.

To begin with, he was terrible at dealing with people.
If he'd possessed the kind of top-tier social skill needed to read the room and respond appropriately moment to moment, he wouldn't have become a shut-in, and he'd have handled things better here, too.

So even knowing the meaning behind the sharpness of their gazes, there wasn't much Subaru could actually do. Any informational advantage he had was hardly worth mentioning.
So what Subaru said to them at this point wasn't a declaration of war against them—it was purely a warning to himself.
In other words,

"I trust you two, so let's get along, alright?"

Just as the first time, he would simply, desperately, face them head-on with everything he had.
That was surely what Subaru had thought, too, on the very first day he'd been summoned to this other world.
No matter how much he knew what future awaited, he didn't have the strength to make full use of that knowledge. So then, to make the most of the chance he'd been given, all he could do was struggle to live with everything he had in the situation right in front of him.

At Subaru's offer, the two of them widened their eyes, glancing between each other's sisterly faces in a silent exchange. There was no way to know what passed between them during that silent back-and-forth, but Subaru wasn't the sort of quiet, composed person to solemnly await their verdict.
To begin with, he'd decided to leave that quietness behind this time around.

"Being left out is lonely, you know, so mean! The world's a lot bigger and more open than that. So quit soaking in your own little world of just the two of you, and let's open up more, much more! With who? With me!"

"Sister, sister. Our now-awakened guest is an incredibly troublesome person."
"Rem, Rem. Our finally-awake guest is an unbearably annoying person."

"But even so, you two have got to show sincerity to this annoying, troublesome me! Whoa, it's been a while since I've felt this guest vibe! Fleeting, but it feels good, yeah!"

Somehow it felt like all the pent-up frustration was exploding out as excitement.
Relying on his mouth spinning even more than usual, Subaru's tongue scattered words lighter than ever in every direction. And then,

"—Couldn't you have woken up just a little more quietly?"

The voice reached him from the direction of the entrance.
Turning to look, standing right beside the door was the silver-haired girl. She gave a light knock on the already-open door, her lovely brow furrowing in exasperation,

"I'm glad you seem lively, but you're still recovering—you shouldn't be making such a racket."

"No need to worry—Emilia-tan is my number one star!"

"What are you even talking about!?"

He gave her a thumbs-up, flashing his teeth in a triumphant, self-satisfied grin.
At the full force of Subaru's performance, Emilia pressed a hand to her forehead,

"Um... well..."

She let her gaze wander as if choosing her words carefully, and then,

"Anyway, good morning. I'm glad you're safe."

Emilia smiled, relieved.
Subaru nodded back at her smile, and he too pulled his cheeks up into a grin,

"Yeah, morning. —Well then, guess it's time to get started."

The three women present tilted their heads, not understanding the intent behind Subaru's remark.
Subaru gave a wry smile at their reaction, and waving a hand, said,

"Roswaal Manor, one week—time to start clearing this thing."

He declared it, not to any of them in particular, but first and foremost as if telling himself.

—Now then, let's set the story in motion.
To look up at the morning he wanted, with the people he wanted, at his side.

The fifth first day begins—the morning at Roswaal Manor starts anew.
