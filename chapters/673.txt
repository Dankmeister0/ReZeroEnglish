Arc 8 Chapter 56: "Arakiya"

—Not at anyone's telling. That was why Arakiya stuffed it into her mouth.

"——"

Thud-thud, the sound of blood coursing through her surged forward as pain.
Her heartbeat raced faster even than in the thick of battle, and every pulse sent a shock tearing through her whole body, as though a nail were being driven straight into her soul — yet, perversely, it eased her mind.
If her heart was still pumping blood, then she was apparently still shaped like a person.
Even if that was nothing more than a margin-of-error's worth of trivial comfort, it was necessary if Arakiya was to keep from being churned into pieces and having her soul scattered.

—A being as singular as a "Spirit Eater" existed nowhere else in this world but in Arakiya.

The "Spirit Eater" — a forbidden existence born at the end of the twisted obsession of men who deserved to be called deviants rather than researchers — had been named Arakiya, and had been kept alive as one of a kind, under a fate she could share with no one else in this world.
Within a sense of self deliberately diluted, with only the existence of the "Pillar" she held dear as her anchor, Arakiya devoured spirits, comported herself as a being of power, and fulfilled her duty.

She had thought that was enough.
But losing her "Pillar" taught her that it wasn't.
Knowing it came too late — by the "Spirit Eater's" natural end, her sense of self should have collapsed early on, swallowed up by the spirits she'd taken in, her soul painted over and lost.

And yet Arakiya hadn't met that fate. Two factors could be considered for why.
One was that the person Arakiya had made her "Pillar" was like a sun so dazzling that mere separation could never make her lose sight of it.
And the other was a reason Arakiya absolutely refused to admit — that in the days since being torn from her "Pillar," a peal of thunder that made her keenly aware she was Arakiya had ceaselessly rattled her soul, reminding her of her inferiority, and going on stirring up her sense of self.

Lit by a dazzling sun, ceaselessly egged on by an obnoxious peal of thunder, Arakiya had kept hold of her soul.

"—And so this one concludes, though I would very much like to ask whether you yourself are aware of it."

"...No."

This was the year Arakiya's rank surpassed Olbart's and she was promoted to "Two."
Chisha claimed to have combed through what surviving records existed on the "Spirit Eater," and his conclusions carved onto Arakiya's face — for a man whose acquaintance with her had unexpectedly grown so long — an expression of displeasure she'd never seen before.

Small wonder. Chisha was clever and easy to follow when he spoke, but this particular conclusion of his was one Arakiya found hard to accept.
Arakiya herself only understood what it meant to be a "Spirit Eater" by feel, but even so, she wanted to believe that all of her was made of Priscilla.
She didn't want that prayer-like wish of hers diluted, of all things, by some other ingredient.
To begin with——

"...Why'd you bring that up?"

Just as the question implied, the very fact that Chisha had raised the topic at all was a surprise.
That Priscilla — Arakiya's "Pillar" — had been exceptionally allowed to escape the "Imperial Selection Ceremony" and smuggled alive out of the country was a secret among secrets, even within the Vollachian Empire.
Of course Arakiya and Chisha, who'd been involved in that exile at the time, knew of it, but it was a subject neither had spoken of for years, lest anyone else overhear.
So why, at this moment, had Chisha brought it up?

"I merely wished to know whether you are a fragile, fleeting thing bound to vanish, or not. Whether I may count you among the numbers for the great battle yet to come."

"...Doesn't make sense. I'm one of the 'Nine Divine Generals.'"

Naturally she'd be called out to put down whatever rebellion Chisha and Vincent were anticipating.
But at Arakiya's answer, Chisha's eyes softened into an unusual expression — a rare smile from a man who almost always wore either a blank face or one of exasperation.
Catching Arakiya off guard with that smile, Chisha went on.

"It seems it shall turn out to be a somewhat larger affair than the battle you have in mind. This one is aware that you do not serve His Excellency from the heart, nor consider this one an ally, as it were."

"——"

"Even understanding as much, not being contradicted stings a little, all the same."

"...For Hime-sama's sake, I won't disappear."

Without touching on Chisha's reaction as he pressed a hand to his brow, Arakiya answered so while stroking her own eyepatch.
There was a proper answer to why she was herself, to what the soul called Arakiya was. Nothing like what Chisha feared would happen. She hadn't said it to put his mind at ease, but she said it all the same.

"Also, that has nothing to do with Cecilus."

"Heh. It was purely this one's conjecture, after all. If you absolutely wish to prove it wrong, this one would ask that you at least manage to defeat Cecilus once."

"Whaaaat? Weren't you two just talking about me? Come on, come on, don't be so mean, leaving me out like that, Anya and Chisha! Or is this it — are you two discussing poor, undeterred Anya's slim chances of finally breaking her losing streak?"

"Cecilus, die."

In the end, the clash with Cecilus that started right after meant Chisha, unwilling to get caught up in it, fled the scene, and she never got to hear any more.
Nor did she recall ever picking the conversation back up with Chisha afterward.

"——"

She only wondered, vaguely.
When would that great battle Chisha was so worried about arrive? And when it did, would she be counted among his numbers?
If the chance ever came, she wanted to ask him that.

Her acquaintance with Chisha had grown long, too.
Their relationship stemmed from the "Imperial Selection Ceremony" that had torn her from Priscilla, and Arakiya still couldn't forgive Chisha, who was so deeply tied to the reason for that separation.
But Chisha had also taught her how to read and write. She owed him for that. For the sake of that debt, she thought she might as well fight the battle Chisha wanted.

—Or would that, too, end up being a reason apart from Priscilla?

She disliked Cecilus. Vincent was complicated, but because of what happened with Priscilla, she leaned toward not forgiving him. Olbart's jokes were funny. Goz had a funny face. Groovy had a foul mouth but looked after people well, and Moguro would listen to her without ever making a sour face. Yorna was someone she wasn't good with, but she couldn't quite bring herself to dislike her either. Balleroy sometimes let her ride his Flying Dragon and fly through the sky, and she felt like she and Madelyn had always avoided crossing paths. Toward Todd, she'd felt gratitude.

—Would all of that, too, end up being reasons apart from Priscilla?

"——"

Feeling as though she might come apart, might be torn to shreds, feeling the self called "her" slipping away with every memory she recalled, Arakiya scraped together "Arakiya."
Not because, without doing so, her own existence would vanish and disappear—

"...-me... sama"

—but because, without doing so, she wouldn't be able to hold back the thing rampaging within her, threatening to burst.
It was a Thing of unfathomable size. It was a Thing of unbelievable weight. It was a Thing so distorted it was unbearable. It was a Thing that, left alone, would destroy the Empire.
If she couldn't keep it contained, she couldn't protect her "Pillar" — Priscilla.

So Arakiya, at no one's bidding, stuffed it into her mouth. Into her own body — so incomparably slight and small — she took in the "Stone Lump," Muspel.
She could not let the monster who called herself the "Witch" use this great spirit and destroy the Empire.
For that reason——

"I take it you've eaten something bad. ...Honestly, you really are a handful."

An illusion, as if an obnoxious peal of thunder were stitching back together a soul torn into a thousand shreds.
Tormented by a pain and loss like a thundercloud, Arakiya scraped together "Arakiya," scraped her together, scraped her together again, continuing to resist the dispersal of a being being churned into nothing.

She kept resisting, kept enduring. —Waiting, longing for the one possibility clinging to a soul on the verge of ruin.

He'd grown sick of counting, but counting had become a habit.
He wasn't about to say anything clever like how reducing things to precise numbers let him shrink the reality he was facing down to size. Set aside all that spiritual mumbo-jumbo — it had simply become a habit, plain and simple.
That habit did the counting. —This made one hundred ninety-one times.

"Even I'm sick of how mediocre I am..."

Al muttered this, feeling the heat build up and the sweat running down soak the inside of his helmet.
On the stage of the molten Second Point, the futile struggle of a bit player hopelessly out of place continued. Under the influence of the heat wave enveloping the whole area, the air wavered and shimmered, and this space had clearly become another dimension entirely.

Moment by moment, the world was being remade into hell, and no human endeavor could adapt to it.
Trees and buildings burst into flame; sand and the scraps of the streets began to melt. The palm gripping his qinglong blade sizzled audibly, and the clothes he wore were in such a state that they could catch fire at any moment.
It was an environment where simply standing there, he might be a fireball the very next instant — and in fact, that had already happened more than once or twice.
Even in such an overwhelmingly harsh situation——

"Tatatatatatatatatatatatata—!!"

Charging fiercely across magma that bubbled and boiled, a leading actor moving at a speed mistakable for a bolt of lightning put on a grand display of swordplay.
Racing along with his deep, dark blue hair whipping wild and a vividly eye-catching pink kimono fluttering behind him was none other than Cecilus Segmunt, who fancied himself the star player of this world.

The speed of his dash across the magma, leg strength unbelievable for a child's slender legs, an inexhaustible stamina that seemed like it would never run dry, and the nerve and force of fate to contend with an enemy capable of remaking the world in the blink of an eye — every last quality of his was first-rate.
Declaring the world his stage, casting himself as its leading man, mindful even of how he looked to an audience he couldn't see, Cecilus lived up to his own grand boasts without an ounce of shame.

Faced with this off-the-charts, formidable foe, who besides him could have even made a fight of it?

"—Hup!"

With a shout in the high-pitched voice peculiar to children, Cecilus's body shot off at a diagonal.
The instant he kicked off, the ground where he'd just been swelled up and exploded from within. Taking even that blast as an ally, the launched Cecilus twisted his body in midair.
Just like that, a lightning-flash kick powerful enough to blast away even a stone pillar as thick as a great tree's trunk plunged toward the silver-haired Dog Human floating in midair — Arakiya.

"—Ah... uh..."

Her tanned skin bared in many places, Arakiya wept tears of blood from eyes red as jewels.
Toward the torso of the groaning girl, the scorched sole of Cecilus's zori sandal closed in, closed in, closed in — and at the moment of impact, a tremendous sound split the sky.

"——!!"

An instant later, struck down at a speed even greater than the one he'd launched with, Cecilus went hurtling into the ground, bounced violently across the street, and lifted his face while stifling a groan of pain.
A beat late, blood sprayed from his pale forehead, staining his childish young face red.

"You've gotten to where you can time it perfectly, haven't you."

Licking with his tongue at the blood trickling down his well-formed nose, Cecilus assessed the fact that he'd been thoroughly intercepted.
There was no trace of surprise in it. After all, this wasn't the first time he'd been intercepted.
Already more than a dozen times, Cecilus's swift-as-the-wind, sharp-as-thunder attacks had been met and shot down. The tide of battle was turning against him rapidly, and seriously so.

"Arakiya, kid — this is bad."

Watching for a chance to intervene, Al looked at Arakiya, floating there and warping the space around her.
Arakiya's form had grown even more monstrous than it had been just a few dozen seconds before, mana crystals now sprouting as though bursting through the inside of the slender girl's body.
Sprouting from her arms and back, they looked, from a distance, almost like an angel's wings.
But in truth, it was nothing other than the far-too-large existence Arakiya had taken into herself gnawing through the body of the girl confining it, trying to spill out.

"God damn it."

At Arakiya's choice — gradually eroded by that power, straying further from a human shape — all Al could do was spit out a curse for the anger he had nowhere else to put.
He understood wanting a means to make up for insufficient power when your own reach fell short of your wishes.
He could also imagine that Arakiya had far, far more potential to reach greater heights than someone like Al, and probably had no end of options she could reach for.
Even so, if she took in something beyond her own measure, Arakiya's very talent would turn its fangs back on herself, on those around her, on the very things she'd wanted to hold dear.
This present state of affairs was the answer to that all-too-obvious payback.

—At the outset, it had been Cecilus's side pressing the advantage in the fight against Arakiya.

To hold back Arakiya — who had blown away the Second Point and turned into a hazard that might well obliterate the whole capital as she was — Cecilus had deliberately thrown barbed hostility at her, continuing to provoke her instinctive sense of danger to set the stage.

Frankly, this was a battle between transcendent beings who, as warriors, weren't one or two levels above him but a full ten rungs up the ladder.
There was almost no room for Al to intervene even by staying put, but even so, across nearly two hundred rounds of trial and error, he'd managed to stop Cecilus's limbs from being blown off on two occasions.

All the while, Arakiya, as though holding a cup being filled with water endlessly poured in, kept spilling the overflow at random, putting Al's and Cecilus's lives in grave danger.
But if that were all that repeated, then even Arakiya — let alone Al, who was cheating with his Domain — could never have caught Cecilus, bounding around as a being outside the natural order.

But the situation changed.
Because Arakiya's aforementioned transformation into something monstrous had progressed, and her way of fighting had shifted all at once.

"—Here it comes!"

Raising a voice that sounded almost buoyantly eager for battle, Cecilus wiped away the blood, his eyes gleaming.
In an instant, the space around Cecilus warped, and twisted stone pillars shot out from all four sides, striking at the boy like giant serpents. Ducking, leaping back, twisting his body to dodge the pillars snapping at him ferociously, Cecilus burst forward, his speed climbing to top gear.

But the fierce assault of the blood-weeping Arakiya wasn't about to let the lightning flash get away.
The ground along the path of the accelerating Cecilus, kicking off the earth, peeled up, and a number of giant arms made of stone and soil formed, lunging to seize and crush the boy.

"Sorry, but touching the stage's leading man is strictly forbidden!"

So he declared lightly, using the artificial arms swung down at him like flyswatters as footholds to break through, climbing them like a staircase up into the sky. Once he'd secured altitude, he flipped around, using the pounding stone fists against themselves as he tried once more to leap at Arakiya.
But——

"I screwed up—!"

A boulder-fist that would turn an ordinary man to a mist of blood with a mere graze — Cecilus caught it on the sole of his zori, killed the fatal impact using the spring of his entire body, and flew off borrowing only its momentum, shouting as he went.
With the force of a ballistic missile, Cecilus barreled toward Arakiya, and a white light formed along his line of flight, with Cecilus — with nowhere to escape in midair — flying straight into it of his own accord——

"Wraaaargh!!"

Roaring, Al raised his qinglong blade and drove it in before Cecilus could plunge into the light.
The next instant, the light, struck directly by the qinglong blade, flared brilliantly for an instant, swallowing up Al's body as it closed in and Cecilus's body as it bore down, blasting them both away——

"I screwed up—!"

Catching the boulder-fist on the sole of his zori, Cecilus, stealing only its momentum, shouted as he flew like a ballistic missile.
Faster than that shout could even leave his mouth, Al pointed the tip of his qinglong blade at the empty air, and—

"Dohnaaa!!"

Compared to Arakiya, it was Magic with all the difference between the moon and a booger, but the stone shard it produced streaked across the sky and snuffed out the ruinous light just as it was about to form.
For an instant, the strong flash seared Al's eyes even through his helmet, but it carried nowhere near enough force to take Al's life, let alone kill Cecilus's speed.

"Al-san, nice one!!"

Seeing through at a glance whose doing the burst of light was, swallowing down even the emotion of having his life saved in an instant, Cecilus barreled on toward Arakiya.
His raised right hand was held ready as a hand-blade, its edge probably surpassing even a middling famous sword. It flashed once through the air, striking diagonally at Arakiya with her mana-crystal wings——

"—!"

In that instant, Al saw — no exaggeration — a peal of thunder roar out and the sky tear open, or so it seemed to him.
In fact it wasn't an illusion at all — the shockwave of Cecilus's hand-blade strike passed clean through and out the other side, finishing off the buildings beyond that had already been on the verge of collapse, and bringing the whole area down.
But — and this was the important part — as for Arakiya, who had taken that hand-blade strike—

"——"

Her blood-tinged eyes rolled and moved, peering at Cecilus right there before her.
Despite having taken a ferocious hand-blade strike to the forehead, Arakiya, her body floating in midair, hadn't so much as swayed. As if in return, she raised up her wings made of mana crystal——

"——"

Right after her thin lips spun out a faint sound, the blow carved into Cecilus with fatal force.

Moment by moment, time flowed, passed, slipped away, and "Arakiya" faded.

Tasting the sensation of her existence, the very place her soul resided, being gradually painted over, Arakiya, amid pain and suffering, desperately tried to recall the brilliance of her sun.

She had been a being nobody knew what to do with. Despite being created at the cost of so many sacrifices, no one had cared what became of her afterward — she'd simply been a hazardous thing with nowhere to go.
That kind of being — one whose use, whose proper handling, no one knew, one that was simply terrifying — she had calmly set alongside the path of her own life, and declared flatly: come along.

How could anyone possibly have defied that complete, magnificent grandeur?
No sprout of rebellion or resistance could ever have taken root. She was the symbol of everything Arakiya believed to be right, and the very fact of her existence was the meaning of Arakiya's birth.

To keep that brilliance from being lost, to keep from letting slip whatever that brilliance found beautiful, she had chosen to leave her side whenever she thought it necessary, somehow enduring even a feeling like being torn apart.
She endured and endured and endured through it all, because she could believe that the sun would rise again beyond it.

—If she could protect tomorrow's dawn, then it didn't matter if she herself never basked in the rising sunlight.

Screaming recklessly, throwing a fit over what she couldn't have, met with a look of disappointment from the very sun herself — and only then, at last, had she arrived at this conclusion.

She'd thought she had been abandoned by the sun.
She'd thought the sun would never shine on her again.

But at the decisive battle for the capital, facing her, the sun — Priscilla — who had leveled the "Sunlight Sword" at the sobbing Arakiya, hadn't cut her down with that crimson blade.
Afterward, too, when surrounded by the servants of the "Great Disaster" that had appeared, she could have gotten away if she'd tried. But she had chosen to become a captive in exchange for Arakiya's safety.

In her true heart, in her brilliance, nothing at all had changed.
If Priscilla could remain unchanged, then Arakiya wanted to hold to an unchanging vow of her own as well.

—Priscilla Benedict.
The symbol of the Vollachian Empire's sun that sets, and then rises again.

If she thought of her, Arakiya could remain "Arakiya."
Arakiya's soul gained heat, and she could put off the limit at which she'd burst.
But though her loyalty and devotion to Priscilla were eternal, that limit couldn't be stretched out forever.

She held back an existence on the verge of being swallowed up, painted over.
While Arakiya went on resisting this violation of her spirit, in the real world she had no room left to spare attention for, the being borrowing her body was, even now, spreading further ruin.

Destruction was growing more refined, ruin was gaining momentum, and collapse was surely only a matter of time.
The "Stone Lump" — which had spent hundreds of long years in stagnation, indifferent to whether part of its power was used in the conflicts between humans or put toward the enrichment of human endeavors — had, by entering the fragile vessel called Arakiya, come to know resistance against disappearing.
The great spirit taken in by Arakiya was exercising a defensive instinct it would never have shown in its natural state, and, ironically, was intensifying its own threat in order to protect the vessel called Arakiya.

"——"

She could feel someone — someone was fighting to stop Arakiya.
She thought that because that someone was wearing down Arakiya's power, if only a little, it was putting some brake on "Arakiya" being lost.
But it wasn't enough. Weakening her, merely holding her back — that wasn't enough.

To thwart the "Great Disaster's" purpose, Arakiya had stuffed it into her mouth.
The great spirit, which had let itself be done to whatever was done to it, had come to know the fear of its own loss by being taken into Arakiya.
Just one more push. With one more push, the great spirit would understand.

For "Arakiya" not to disappear, she needed the sun.
And she needed it for "Arakiya" to fulfill her vow. —The thunder.

She could feel someone — someone was fighting to stop Arakiya.
It wasn't enough. That someone wasn't enough. She knew exactly who was needed.
So Arakiya, on the verge of bursting, waited and longed.

—Waited and longed for the thunder, the real thunder, to roar out.

"—Kill me, Cecilus."

The moment those words reached him, of all things, his thoughts came to a halt.
Right after he'd driven in a hand-blade strike with everything he had, only to realize it hadn't worked on his opponent in the slightest, the beautiful girl weeping tears of blood had begged him so, crying, in a thin, faint voice.

"——"

An instant later, before he could even swallow down that murmur, Cecilus's chest took a ferocious blow.
The girl's slender arm, though, was as good as a blade with the mana crystals bristling sharp all over it, and poor Cecilus's thin chest was mercilessly torn open, staining his favorite kimono with blood as he fell.

There was pain. There was the palpable sense of having screwed up. There were more than enough signs of a major crisis.
But more than the deeply gouged wound, more than the trail of blood streaking through the air, he couldn't tear his eyes away from the girl's pained eyes and face receding from him, and above all, from the words she'd spoken——

"—I'm terribly sorry, but I must ask for silence!!"

In that instant, Cecilus cried out forcefully against the ceaseless voices of the audience that kept ringing in his ears.

"——"

Ordinarily, Cecilus would never do anything to silence the audience's voices.
He always spent his days perfectly at ease, treating the voices he heard as fuel to raise his own motivation, letting them say whatever they liked and simply hearing them out.
But he'd silenced them. He wanted silence — a silence so complete that even his pain became an annoyance.

"——"

As he fell, Cecilus went on staring at the girl within a silence empty of the audience's voices.
She wept tears of blood, suffered, and destroyed the world as her means of voicing her grief — and at last, amid meaningless groans, she had woven in her own wish.
That it was none other than his own name could rightly be called the destiny of a leading man.
And yet——

"—'Kill me' is not something I can let pass unanswered."

More than the pain or the shock, it was the line she'd spoken that he found hardest to accept, and Cecilus ground his teeth.
In that instant, he folded his knees mid-fall — head-first toward the ground — flipped himself around, and landed without a hint of danger, then dodged, dodged, and dodged again a chain of falling stone pillars aimed right at his landing spot, leaping and scraping his heel across the ground.
Then he lifted his face.

"Those words weren't meant for me, were they."

Of course, he couldn't imagine there being any other Cecilus whose name might be called in this situation.
But even so, he understood perfectly well that he wasn't the one she'd truly called out to, weeping, the one to whom she'd entrusted her wish. —And that made him inexplicably furious.

Call it the big match, the crucible, the defining scene, his own dedicated episode — whatever term you liked would do.
Given such a moment, such a scene, such a showcase, there was no way the Cecilus Segmunt called upon in it could be anyone but himself.
And so——

"—Cecilus! Your wound, is it—"

"I've made up my mind, Al-san."

Answering Al, who — seeing Cecilus's badly wounded, bleeding form — had, for some reason, spoken while holding his own qinglong blade to his own throat, Cecilus tore off a strip from his ripped kimono and used the makeshift cord to gather up his scattered, disheveled blue hair behind his head.

Then, smiling, he looked up at the weeping girl — the heroine — and declared.

"Let the Observers on high bear witness. —To which the world shall choose."
