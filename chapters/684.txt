Arc 8 Chapter 65: "The Star-Falling Imperial Capital"

—The reconstruction of a vessel fit for the Witch of Greed, achieved through the alteration of a soul.

That was the great purpose behind the calamity the Witch, Sphinx, had inflicted upon the Vollachia Empire this time, as its Great Disaster.
Born into this world, having spent more than three hundred years unable to fulfill the purpose for which she had been made — that proposition had remained, through everything, at the very core of Sphinx. If one followed the logic of the Stargazers said to appear only within the Vollachia Empire, this could be called the heavenly mandate Sphinx had been given from birth.
To fulfill that mandate, she would not hesitate to destroy a kingdom, nor an empire.
And in fact—

"—So flooding the Empire with the dead was your aim all along, was it?"

Chained in her cell, arms raised, Priscilla narrowed her crimson eyes.
In those keen, intelligent eyes there was no trace of surprise at what had unfolded before her — only a blazing scarlet flame, fed by the kindling of quiet understanding.
At that crimson gaze, the one who had become the Witch of Greed, Sphinx, twisted her lips into a smile and nodded.

"By nature, vessel and soul are inseparable. I understood as much from the very moment I was created. You might say the long years of my search have been a journey to make the two align."

Though she had been made for the purpose of recreating the Witch of Greed, Sphinx had been given a soul placed into a vessel different from that of the true Witch of Greed, and so she had been born, inevitably, incomplete as the Witch of Greed. She had never thought the more than three hundred years of flawed trial and error a hardship, but even Sphinx felt something like accomplishment now that she had finally reached this horizon.
And, just as Priscilla said, that horizon had without question been reached along the path opened by the Great Disaster she had unleashed upon the Empire.

—As stated before, by nature, vessel and soul are inseparable things for any life. This holds true even for beings without substance, like spirits. What matters is not whether there is a tangible body to touch, but the vessel that serves as the receptacle in which the soul resides.
For that to be mismatched is for a life to exist in an unnatural state. And between an inseparable soul and vessel, a force works to correct that unnatural mismatch.

Of course, no matter what corrective force is at work, it is exceedingly rare for a vessel to change to match its soul. The Shinobi Techniques that alter the soul and reshape the vessel to fit it, or the curse of reincarnation that sacrifices countless lives, are such rare examples — but the former requires a talent for the technique of properly aligning soul and vessel, while the latter cannot come to pass without numerous sacrifices and a strong bond between them and the caster.
Neither condition could be met by Sphinx; both were possibilities she had to abandon for lack of feasibility in achieving her desire.

And so Sphinx made use of it instead — the Great Disaster.

"Reviving the dead across the entire Empire through forbidden arts, and in the process observing an unlimited number of invisible, untouchable souls. Convenient, wasn't it, for the verification your purpose required?"

"That the Great Disaster would occur was foreseen. All that remained was whether my plan would prove equal to it… and the result speaks for itself."

"For all your triumph, it seemed a rather perilous tightrope walk, did it not?"

"I won't deny it. Challenge was required."

Priscilla's words were accurate. It had indeed been an attempt riddled with uncertainty. Whether the modification to the ritual of the Sacrament of the Immortal King, connected to Vollachia's great spirit, the Stone Mass, could truly realize an army of the Undead; whether the created Sphinx would be recognized as a single soul and included among those Undead to be revived; whether, among the Undead, one would emerge capable of bringing about the alteration of a soul that Sphinx herself could not achieve — all of it had been uncertain.

"But."

The revival of the Undead through the recreation of the Sacrament of the Immortal King had succeeded; the plan for Sphinx herself to be revived as one of the Undead had also gone smoothly; and further, beings capable of accepting the alteration of the soul, such as Lamia Godwin and the "Giant Eye" Izmail, had appeared. Bit by bit, the groundwork Sphinx required had come together.
And then—

"It's ironic, isn't it, that the final push came from the flames of the Sun Sword."

"—————"

"Had things remained as they were, I would without question have been destroyed. That very brink became, in the end, the trigger for my final, countless considerations."

"—No. That is not so."

"Not so?"

Sphinx tilted her head at the denial of the fact that she had escaped the mortal danger of having her soul burned.
She was different now, even in the height of her line of sight, from the incomplete vessel of the Witch of Greed she had been until just before. Her face, her neck, her bare skin, were no longer the bloodless, pallid things they had been; her eyes, too, no longer bore the black flecked with gold of the Undead, but had been remade as black eyes. And yet Sphinx remained, unmistakably, one of the Undead.
The recreation of a soul in a state nearly identical to that of the living — the fact that this had been achieved was exerting a great influence on her appearance. And the very fact that she had achieved it was itself proof of nothing less than the correctness of Sphinx's theory.

"And what, precisely, do you claim that I have gotten wrong? Explanation required."

"Do not describe what you accomplished with such bloodless words as 'countless considerations.' On the very brink of your life burning out, you struggled, ugly and desperate, to live."

"…I am one of the dead."

"Do you imagine the dead have no right to struggle to live? The narrow rope you crossed, time and again, was never one that could be crossed by standing idly by, composed."

"—————"

"You struggled, and dragged the outcome you desired toward yourself. Unpleasant and inconvenient as that fact may be to me, recognize it as it truly is — if you would be my enemy."

To Sphinx, who had fallen silent, Priscilla declared this proudly, even bound as a captive.
At the words Priscilla had turned upon her, Sphinx's brow furrowed, ever so faintly.

Priscilla's sharp tongue, cutting in a manner that never betrayed who she was, had nonetheless sounded almost as if it praised Sphinx's struggle. As if it acknowledged the fact that Sphinx's trial and error had borne fruit.

"—————"

That planted, in Sphinx's chest, the faintest splinter — a sentimentality unbecoming of, and never meant to exist in, the Witch of Greed.

"…I ought to avoid engaging with your rhetoric. Self-defense required."

"What is this? Not content merely to bind me in chains, you would shut your ears to my words as well? If that is so, then there is no meaning in your remaining here, nor in keeping me alive. And yet — why do you continue this?"

"I said I would not engage."

"Hmph. Very well, then. I shall lay it all bare myself."

Curving her red lips into a bow, Priscilla ignored Sphinx's declaration that she would not take the bait.
There was a force in her words, in the very timbre of her voice. Once she began to speak, no matter how much one might wish to cover one's ears against the content, there was a power that compelled one to listen.

"That you carried out this plan of yours here in the Empire, and that you remain before me even now, after having succeeded in remaking yourself into that so-called vessel of the Witch — the reason for both is plain as day."

And so, unable to silence Sphinx, Priscilla continued.
And then—

"You mean to let me live, and force me to watch as the Empire meets its end. Why? To show me the fall of my homeland, and shatter my heart with it. — With an inexhaustible hatred for me as its wellspring."

—She had correctly named the Witch Sphinx's two great purposes: the recreation of a soul's alteration as the Witch of Greed, and revenge against Priscilla Barielle.

—To Aldebaran, this encounter was one that should never have come to pass.

"—Echidnaaaaaa!!"

His voice trembling with fury, Al snatched up the improvised stone glaive rising from the ground and hurled it at the Witch of Greed, floating in the air with her white hair streaming.
Toward the Witch, who was warping the space around her like a mirage and preparing to launch some manner of attack, the glaive surged forward, spinning end over end.
The blade flew straight toward the Witch of Greed, hovering at a height Al's own jump could never have reached—

"It's surprising that there's someone who calls me by that name. Explanation required."

Saying so flatly, the Witch of Greed dodged the glaive with the barest shift of her body.
The blade, its aim missed, flew on uselessly, not even grazing the Witch of Greed.
—And that was exactly the plan.

"Eat this!"

The instant Al's gravelly shout rang out, the glaive that had missed its mark flashed and burst apart.
He'd never been so naively hopeful as to think a thrown blade would actually connect. If anything, Al understood well enough that his attacks wouldn't work on most opponents. That was precisely why every single movement in Al's fighting had to lay groundwork, or there was no story to tell at all.

"—————"

The shattered stone glaive turned to shrapnel, showering down mercilessly upon the Witch of Greed's slender frame.
Though hardly a fatal blow even at full exposure, the fragments had been engineered to shatter in a way that dealt damage too significant to ignore — a modest fragmentation charge.
Even with a small amount of mana, he had deliberately shaped the glaive so its fragments would be sharp. Making the most of what he had on hand was something Al was good at.

"That's just the kind of environment where you'd die if you couldn't manage that!"

Above the roaring Al's head, the shrapnel struck the Witch of Greed with a painful clatter. — And yet things did not proceed as hoped.

"Hm."

Against the approaching shrapnel, the Witch of Greed merely narrowed her eyes faintly and did nothing. There was no need to. The shrapnel veered aside on its own, as though avoiding the floating Witch of Greed.
Al's attack could not even breach the residual wind she wore as she flew.
But even that, to Al, was disappointingly close to what he'd expected.

"—!"

Even for a moment, even for a fraction of a second, it was enough if the Witch of Greed's attention turned elsewhere.
Using that opening, Al triggered magic beneath his own running feet — the rising ground became a springboard, launching his body high into the sky.
Then, having leapt up, Al transformed the stone he'd clutched in his grip into a second glaive.
Rather than conjuring fire or water from nothing, shaping a small stone into a large blade with it as catalyst consumed far less mana, and the forging speed was orders of magnitude faster.

"Ohhhhhh—!!"

Using the spring of his entire body, he delivered the swing he'd wound up upon the Witch of Greed.
Unlike the shrapnel, this was not something the wind could auto-guard against. Straight into that too-perfect, infuriating face, Al put everything he had—

"Gah."
"I appreciate the attitude of compensating for insufficient ability with ingenuity, but ability is required."

The Witch of Greed's slender arm moved fluidly, deflecting the glaive smoothly aside, and her long leg struck through the body of the astonished Al with brutal clarity. Al doubled up in midair with a groan; the Witch of Greed added the rotation of her hips and kicked him straight down to the ground.

"Guagah!"

He curled himself up reflexively, avoiding having his neck snapped by the impact of the fall.
But there was no denying the damage of having his whole body struck so ferociously. And the physical impact was not the only thing that had damaged Al.

"That movement just now was…"

"You act as though it were unexpected. It is the fruit of turning a past defeat into nourishment, of making up for insufficient ability and honing it through study."

The Witch of Greed lowered the leg she had kicked with, and her words sank into Al's brain, making him stare.
It was wrong. It shouldn't have been possible. As far as Al knew, she was a woman who dabbled indiscriminately in everything, but whose athletic ability alone had been an absolute catastrophe. No matter how much theory she built up, none of it had ever translated into her body; a stunt like that should have been utterly impossible for her, even standing on her head. For that matter, she couldn't even stand on her head.
In other words—

"Sensei… you're not Echidna?"

"That reaction is quite interesting. Under normal reasoning it should be impossible, and yet — you know my creator. Why is that?"

Pressed with the question, Al looked once more at the Witch of Greed floating in the air — Echidna, or rather, the one who wore a face identical to hers — and found himself at a loss for words.
Her appearance, her voice — it was Echidna, through and through. And yet, it was not Echidna.
This Witch of Greed — no, this Witch's black eyes lacked it. The bottomless, ferocious, ghoulish, insatiable curiosity that the true Witch of Greed ought to possess.

"—————"

At Al, who closed his mouth in that conviction, the being that wore Echidna's face without being Echidna narrowed her white-lash-fringed eyes, faintly displeased, and said—

"A refusal to answer, is it? I would like to know just who you are, beyond simply being Priscilla Barielle's attendant—"

"—Ah, unfortunately, that's no longer possible."

A flash of lightning, an instant's event.
As Al, having barely managed to rise, saw the Witch begin to raise a finger to attempt some sort of action, Cecilus appeared behind her, Arakiya in his arms.
Supporting Arakiya with only his left hand, he drew the Dream Sword one-handed and swung it in a single flash — without hesitation, the Witch's head was severed, and sent flying.

"Nhh?!"

"You said I couldn't kill you — how's that for an answer? Honestly, being talked down to like that kind of stung, so I'm glad if I can wipe out that disgrace with this!"

Without even allowing a reaction, the blade parted the Witch's head from her body cleanly. Above the speechless Al's head, Cecilus stuck out his tongue playfully — the wind of his blade could kill even a Witch with ease.
Even that much, confused as Al was by the appearance of a familiar-faced Witch who apparently was not the familiar face herself, he was able to grasp.

But—

"…Reconsideration required."

The phenomenon that occurred next was not something he could comprehend in a single glance.

"—————"

Scattering white hair caught up in the beheading, the spinning severed head, its beauty faintly intact, curled its lips and sneered thus.

Immediately after, the space around the falling Witch violently warped white—

"A refusal to answer, is it? I would like to know just who you are, beyond simply being Priscilla Barielle's attendant—"

"—Ah, unfortunately, that's no longer possib— whoawhoawhoawhoawhoa!?"

Narrowing her white-lash-fringed eyes, the Witch murmured, sounding displeased.
Cecilus, who had been aiming at her slender neck from behind, was startled by a wall of earth that suddenly appeared before him; he kicked it away at once, retreated from the airspace, spun around, landed, and then turned a fiercely accusatory gaze upon Al.

"Hey now, hey now, Al-san! What do you think you're doing, interfering out of nowhere like that! That was supposed to be the scene where I show off my ability after being cheaply underestimated at the outset and it feels great! The audience is booing something fierce right now!"

"Sorry for stealing your moment, but I couldn't let you do that. Doing that would've meant everyone — well, maybe not everyone, but at least I'd have been blown to bits."

"…Hm."

At Cecilus's foot-stamping protest and Al's reply to it, the Witch let out a breath.
From that one remark, it seemed the Witch had realized that Al had seen through the trap she'd set — a technique that warped space and, in the recoil of it returning, blew away the surrounding area.
It was a dead man's switch that would trigger the instant Cecilus beheaded the Witch and the force maintaining the twisted space snapped — not a bomb that detonated when a button was pressed, but one that triggered when a button was released.

Its power was something no wall of earth Al could raise, no armor of stone he could wear, could withstand.
Honestly, Cecilus might well be able to outrun it even while carrying Arakiya, but for Al it was a deadly roadblock he had no way to avoid.
Which was why he couldn't let the Witch be recklessly killed. — That was a fact Al had confirmed through fifty-three rounds of trial and error.
And what's more, there was one more thing he'd become certain of.

"You ain't Echidna. So who the hell are you, exactly?"

"That question has already been answered. I am the Witch of Greed."

"So that's what I'm saying, that's Echidna's—"

"Wait wait, Al-san, don't you get it? Actually, maybe you've never run into her before."

Cecilus physically inserted himself between Al and the Witch, who were on the verge of a standoff. Kicking off the ground lightly, he positioned himself to block their line of sight, shifting his hold on Arakiya as he spoke.

"That person is the mastermind behind the great calamity currently ravaging the Empire… the very one reviving the dead. Her looks are different from when I last saw her, but I'd say this current form has a much higher villainess quotient — truly fitting appearance for a mastermind, wouldn't you say!"

"She's the one behind the Great Disaster…"

"There's no particular need to hide it, so — yes. I am your enemy."

At the Witch, who stated her position flatly and without evasion, exactly as her words claimed, Al finally shed the humiliating position of lagging behind Cecilus in understanding the situation.
Still, that did nothing to resolve his confusion over the Witch's appearance.

"That look of yours… no. I can't think of any explanation except that Sensei went and did something unnecessary…!"

"I would like, once again, to confirm what exactly you know of my creator."

"But as it happens, I'm right here!"

At Al, who pressed his hand to the forehead of his helmet, agonizing, the Witch turned an interested gaze. But once more, Cecilus stepped in to block that line of sight, standing firm so as not to let her curiosity be satisfied.
But Cecilus's presence here was not working entirely in their favor.
As already stated, the Witch's dead man's switch was still active — until they could get past that, they couldn't afford to recklessly defeat the Witch of Greed either.

"…No. This one isn't the Witch of Greed at all."

Saying so, Al denied the thought that had sprouted in his own mind.
The Witch certainly had a face identical to Echidna's, but the name Witch of Greed was not something given merely for resembling her in appearance. And that wasn't limited to the Witch of Greed. — A Witch is not something so light a thing as that.

"Like hell just anyone can waltz in and become a Witch."

"I have no desire for the vainglory of showing off my achievements, but it does sting somewhat to be told that reaching this point was easy."

"Shut up. Say that after you've died a hundred times, a hundred separate times."

The Witch, too, seemed displeased — but Al was no less so.
In a situation where he wanted to rush to Priscilla's side at the Crystal Palace as fast as humanly possible, being held up by some infuriating-faced Witch was no laughing matter.

"—Thought experiment, restart. Domain, redefine."

Letting his simmering fighting spirit rise, Al updated his matrix and glared the Witch down.
Even with Cecilus present, he couldn't afford to let his guard down. That, together with the fact that he mustn't carelessly let the Witch be killed, was rooted in one more fact Al had become certain of.
And that was—

"Cecilus, hold onto Arakiya-chan tight."

"I certainly don't intend to just toss her aside… but that phrasing doesn't just mean 'treat Anya carefully, like a princess,' does it?"

"That's right. — That foul-tempered, nasty-faced woman's real target is Arakiya-chan's life."

The reason the Witch, who bore the burden of the Great Disaster, had personally come here — it was the fact that it lay in the life of Arakiya, sleeping now in Cecilus's arms.

"Ordinarily, Arakiya would have been unable to suppress the Stone Mass she consumed, and would have burst apart, losing her life. But your calculations went awry."

"—————"

"My Soul Marriage Technique, and the 'Blue Lightning' Masayume, were the unforeseen factors, weren't they. Had it been only one or the other, perhaps — but Arakiya had both. — Though, mind you, had Arakiya herself not endured until both of them came together, your calculations would have come true."

Declaring this with certainty, Priscilla drew confidence in her own reading from Sphinx's silent posture.
There was no sign on Sphinx's face of the pain of having a sore spot struck. Nor did she try to deceive with clever words. Considerable composure, in the face of an "irregular" turn of events.

—Arakiya's survival: that ought to have been an outcome that fell outside Sphinx's plan.

By rights, had things gone according to Sphinx's calculations, Arakiya, having taken in power beyond what her body could bear in order to save Priscilla, should have destroyed herself as a result.
Showing Priscilla that wretched end of Arakiya's should have been one of the schemes Sphinx had prepared to torment Priscilla, alongside the fall of the Vollachia Empire.

That was precisely why Sphinx had deliberately made Arakiya consume the Stone Mass.
But it had backfired. — Because Arakiya had endured without self-destructing, Priscilla and Cecilus's intervention had succeeded in prolonging her life.
As a result, Arakiya the "Spirit Eating," far from merely slipping out of Sphinx's calculations, had risen to occupy a critical position capable of greatly derailing her plan.
Which was to say—

"—The Stone Mass within Arakiya was tamed by Cecilus Segmunt's Dream Sword. Because of that, her life has become one and the same with the Stone Mass itself."

"—Yes, that's right. I have no objection to your assessment either."

Priscilla's declaration; Sphinx nodded in agreement to it as well.
That was an affirmation of the dramatic, fateful shift in the situation — proof of acknowledging that the Stone Mass, Muspel, which had been taken in, and Arakiya, had become bound together deeply, inseparably strong.

Most likely, Arakiya's original aim had been to take in a portion of Muspel and, having thus bound herself to it, have Cecilus kill her — teaching the great spirit the fear of death, and thereby ending the situation in which Sphinx was exploiting the Stone Mass for the large-scale exercise of the Sacrament of the Immortal King.
But, looking only at the result, that scheme had failed.
In the end, for both the living and the dead who stood on either side of the Stone Mass, this situation was an unforeseen "irregular."

"And yet, it remains you who are cornered. Should she — Arakiya, First General — lose her life, the Stone Mass, Muspel, will die with her all the same. The end of the Vollachia Empire cannot be avoided."

"Do you imagine my eyes would cloud with tears over the fall of a homeland I abandoned once already? You underestimate me."

"Then simply sit and watch. Whether it is bluster or not will become clear soon enough."

Crimson and jet black, Priscilla's and Sphinx's eyes locked head-on.
Against the heat and sharpness of Priscilla's gaze, Sphinx's unshakable resolve did not waver. There was a grim quality to it that refused to let the other rejoice unreservedly, even knowing the gears of her plan had gone awry.

Its source: a hatred for Priscilla, built up over the hundreds of years spent pursuing her purpose of becoming the Witch of Greed — a hatred rivaling the sheer depth of that long-standing obsession.

"You hate me that much… no — is it that you long for it, Witch?"

"There is no name for this splinter in my chest. Or perhaps — do you know what this thing within me is? Answer required."

"—I shall not stoop to the tastelessness of naming, on another's behalf, a feeling its owner has not named."

Priscilla had no intention of putting a name to an emotion its owner herself had not named.
Even without knowing its name, a flower in full bloom is still a flower. Even if it is a flower that blooms only atop a mound of corpses, there is no sin in the beauty of that flower.

"—————"

Falling silent at Priscilla's answer, Sphinx snapped her fingers instead.
In that instant, before the Witch had changed shape, the far-seeing image that had been projected onto the magic crystal set into the tip of her staff was displayed now upon the surface of a water-mirror that formed in midair.

—A sight of a being with the very same shape as the Witch before them, bringing an end to the Land of Sword and Wolf, raining down stars from a sky still bright.

"—The moon veiled by clouds, the blossom scattered by wind, as they say."

Setting these elegant, poetic words upon his tongue, Cecilus deliberately dove straight into the storm of shrapnel.
Ahead, the whirling vortex of rubble was a hail of blades sure to prove fatal — but Cecilus, pulling Arakiya tight in his arms, evaded it entirely with the barest minimum of movement necessary. — No, rather,

"Nice! A difficulty level only I can handle!"

Licking the blood trickling from his grazed cheek, Cecilus fixed his gaze on the Witch conjuring the great storm.
Troublingly, the Witch, who kept to high ground, refused to surrender her advantageous position. The surrounding buildings that might have served as footholds had mostly been flattened by Arakiya, and there was no stage prop anywhere on the battlefield to give him the distance needed to leap and close in.
And yet, even if he wanted to lure the enemy to some other battlefield—

"—Al Shario."

The short incantation summoned a mass of flame descending on an unbelievable scale.
Should that falling mass strike the ground, the entire surrounding area would be engulfed in flame, creating a hellscape to rival the battle against Arakiya.
And unlike Arakiya, the Witch had no need to show consideration for confining the battlefield.

"Simply a nuisance! Right in half!"

In an instant, the Dream Sword, drawn at lightning speed, unleashed Cloud Cutting and split the flaming mass above in two.
The mass of flame, having taken the slash of light, released in the sky the destructive force meant to turn the ground into a sea of fire, and the sky, having briefly returned to its usual color, was once again painted a violent, destructive red.
The sight was terribly flashy, wonderfully so — but there was no time to sit back and enjoy the spectacle.
Because the falling mass of flame was not a single shot.

"Gorr. El Gorr. Ul Gorr. Al Gorr."

At the successive incantations, an enormous quantity of destructive fire fell like a nightmare.
Against a rain of fire he could not afford to let a single drop through, Cecilus's eyes gleamed brilliantly — he tossed the Arakiya he had been holding straight sideways, and gripped the hilt of the Dream Sword.

"Dun-dun-dun-dun-du-dun-dun!!"

Cutting a staccato rhythm, the Dream Sword's flashing light sliced apart every last one of the falling flames. A roaring boom, an explosive report, the sound of the world's ending played out, and the sky's precarious brilliance only deepened.
His cheek twisted into a smile, and yet, Cecilus did not welcome being forced into a war of attrition.
Cecilus, who could ignore to the utmost the risk that came with the power of the Dream Sword, could tell that both sides were, mutually, forcibly ignoring what ought to have been a limited management of resources.

Which was to say, the fight between Cecilus and the Witch was currently a stalemate.
If Al's account was true, carelessly cutting off the Witch's head would blow away the whole area. Looking closely, the air around the Witch was distorted, lending the claim considerable credibility.
Even if space itself were blown away, Cecilus might well be able to outrun it — but just as with Schwartz, the fact that Al, who was under considerable suspicion of being able to "scout ahead," hadn't instructed him to do so, gave some sense that it was a bad bet.
Hence the stalemate — and if the one thing that could break the situation wide open was Cecilus, that was a straight line to annihilation—

"So this is your moment to shine, Al-san."

"I know that already! And quit chucking Arakiya-chan around!"

"Chucking her? How rude! It's the trust between me and you at work, Al-san!"

At Cecilus, who had made countless red flowers of flame blossom across the sky, Al — catching the tossed Arakiya one-handed — shouted so.
Of course, he was throwing her because he trusted Al to catch her in a good position, but apparently, from Al's point of view, it looked like she was simply being flung away.
And yet, Al had never once failed a catch, and more than that—

"You'll dodge this too, will you."

Murmuring shortly, the Witch fired a white beam of heat from her finger.
It was aimed not at Cecilus, but straight at Al — no, at the Arakiya he held in his arms; but the attack meant to stop her heart never reached her.
At the very last moment, in a manner one could only call harrowingly close, Al dodged it.
It was a dreadfully clumsy, utterly unconcerned-with-appearances form of evasion.

"Good result and matches the character, so it's fine! But just as Al-san says, Anya is clearly the target here, isn't she!"

"Yeah, seems like when Arakiya-chan dies, the Empire's floor drops right out from under it!"

"I see, not a clue what that means!"

Told something that sounded like a joke but clearly wasn't, Cecilus laughed without laughing it off, snatched Arakiya from Al's hands as they passed each other, and accelerated.

"Come now, come come, catch me if you can — with your eyes, with your heart, with your soul — oh!?"

Kicking off the ground, Cecilus, Arakiya in his arms, closed in on lightning speed.
Forming in the air to surround Cecilus's path of flight were several beautiful mirrors made of water — their surfaces bounced back the white beams the Witch fired, and light danced wildly.
Truly, deadly light was fired wildly from all directions at once, aimed at Cecilus.

"Ow-ow-ow-ow-ow-ow-ow-ow-Idaten!"

Through that raging storm of light, Cecilus tore through as though dancing, as though performing. He tilted his body, bent low, spread his legs and lowered his stance, and at times leapt with wide strides over the light.
And for angles he couldn't fully dodge, he matched the blade of the Dream Sword to them, deflecting the light and shattering the water mirrors. Amid the sound of water splashing, like flat stones skipped across a pond's surface, he tore through the storm, showered with the scattering droplets of the broken mirrors—

"—This is the sensation of making an enemy of the world."

In an instant, the Witch's declaration, along with the sound of the air drawing taut, struck Cecilus's eardrums.
The pain that instantly shot through his whole body was proof that the droplets of the shattered water mirror had frozen in a flash, stealing the heat from Cecilus's body and his freedom of movement.
Even the water mirrors had a second and third layer of defense — thoroughly cautious, without a single opening.

The sensation of making an enemy of the world — that, too, was an elegant, fine phrase.
Certainly, in a Vollachia Empire that originally had scarcely any mages worthy of the name, the chance to cross paths with a caster of this caliber would likely never have come at all.

"But you seem unaware. — The world is always waiting for my moment to shine!"

Opposite the falling temperature, his spirits rose, growing brighter. At once, the mana circulating through Cecilus's body raged with an incomprehensible force, like lightning that had caught fire, evaporating in an instant the ice that had tried to steal his warmth and freedom, and he resumed his charge.
Breaking free of the icy binding with that momentum, Cecilus looked up at the sky and broke into a grin.

That was not a smile exchanged with the Witch in the air. — It was one born of witnessing, beyond that Witch, from far, far higher in the sky, the light of a star falling.

"—Al Shario."

The meaning of the incantation was a declaration that that starlight was by the Witch's own hand.
Faced with the outrageous fact that so overwhelming a mage could even bring down a star, the Dream Sword resting at Cecilus's hip throbbed, as if bouncing with delight.
It was resonating. Masayume, wanting to devour its wielder's swollen "dream."

"—Interesting."

Cut the star, said the falling light.
Cut the star, said the beloved sword at his hip.
Cut the star, said the countless spectators, rising to their feet as one.

Cut the star, said the very soul of Cecilus Segmunt.

"—————"

For a moment, for the barest fragment of consciousness, Cecilus tore his attention from the star and licked his lips.
Now, everything but the "dream" stirring his soul as the supreme proposition became a thing of the past. As a ritual for that purpose, Cecilus laid the girl he'd been carrying down on the ground and stood before her.
He chose as flat a patch of ground as he could. He took off his outer robe and laid it beneath her, showing her the utmost consideration.

From here on, forgive me, but I will exclude even your existence from my field of awareness.

"Ha."

With a rasping breath, after a single beat, Cecilus quietly drew the Dream Sword from his hip.
The unsheathed blade touched the air and began to tremble, as if drawing up the heat from Cecilus's hand gripping the hilt, and began to glow with light.

An enchanted sword that devours its owner's "dream," turning "dream tale" into "masayume" — a dream come true.
Cecilus, focusing his consciousness on the starlight, forgot to blink, forgot to breathe, forgot even the beating of his own heart, pouring all his effort solely into becoming one with the enchanted sword in his hand.

All the while, the Witch seemed to be doing something to disturb Cecilus's concentration.

"Ha."

Cecilus's consciousness concentrated wholly on the light of the star. And to achieve that, he excluded every unnecessary thing. Color, sound, smell, taste, the approaching earth, water, fire, and wind — all excluded, unconsciously.
But mere exclusion was not enough.

—If it is necessary.

"—————"

The light of the star falling toward the earth carried a destructive force that, even without touching the ground directly, would split the earth, ignite the air, and turn light into pain by its pressure alone.
Cecilus Segmunt, looking up to the sky to meet it head-on, felt, in that moment, gratitude.
With that gratitude in his heart, he blessed it all.

"—————"

Who could have noticed that the sound of his escaping breath had changed in pitch?
What Cecilus needed in this moment, what he desired in order to swing the Dream Sword in his hand to its fullest, without hesitation — the answer to that had been brought, terribly faint, by a bloody, intimate feeling.

The final crossing of blades with his father had taught Cecilus Segmunt — the whereabouts of the soul.
The sword of Rowan Segmunt, which had desecrated life and death.

And so—

"—The swordsman, Cecilus Segmunt."

Uttering that solemn declaration, the "Blue Lightning" Cecilus Segmunt — the strongest being in the Vollachia Empire — swung the Dream Sword with limbs fit for the leading role.


△▼△▼△▼△


—Lightning cleaved starlight in two.

If one were to describe what occurred, no expression but that would suffice.
And yet, having actually witnessed what truly happened, there remained only the question of whether one could believe what one's own eyes had seen was real.
And yet—

"No matter how ridiculous it is…!"

Cecilus Segmunt had cut down a star.
The mastermind, wearing the likeness of the Witch of Greed and wielding the forbidden magic she had specialized in, stared wide-eyed, witnessing the same reality as Al.

Bringing down a star is no easy feat.
Even the true Witch of Greed had said she preferred not to drop more than one a day.
In other words, it could not be done repeatedly.

"Ohh—!"

As the severed starlight dyed the sky white and even sound itself seemed to vanish from the world, Al raised his voice, purely to steel himself, and closed in through the air, using the pillars of stone he conjured as footholds.
Toward the Witch, still frozen in astonishment at having dropped a star only to see it cut down—

"It is true that I was surprised. But—"

The stone glaive Al brought crashing down as he leapt was shattered by the Witch's palm strike. It was a fusion of magic and martial skill, super-vibrating the wind she wore on her palm to pulverize the target.
A magical technique that, if it struck true, would tear and shatter flesh and bone alike into fragments.
Still wielding that in her hand, the Witch moved to crush Al's head, helmet and all.

Having witnessed Cecilus unleash a slash of such absurd, unprecedented scale, the Witch, updating her threat assessment of him, revised her recognition of Al from an obstacle in the way of killing Arakiya to a mere pebble to be swiftly cleared away.
But she did not know. — Even a pebble can sometimes kill the one who trips over it.

"Ingenuity requi—"

In that instant, Al's left arm — a makeshift prosthetic fashioned of stone and earth — struck the Witch's head.

"—The stars were against you."

Saying this with a different weight than he usually gave the words, the Witch's eyes wavered.
It was the very instant she was plotting some kind of counterattack or countermeasure. — And that instant was, before Al, who had already finished his preparations, as distant as eternity.

"—Ol Shamak."

Inside his iron helmet, the incantation Al's lips wove responded, and the world itself was altered.
It wound around the entire body of the Witch, who — having taken an unexpected blow and momentarily frozen in thought — found her movement and the activity of the Gate within her body brought to a halt.

It was the trump card Al had mastered, for use against Witches.

"—Ah."

Letting out a feeble breath, the Witch, deprived of her freedom, fell to the ground.
Bound tight in what could only be called black light, her figure resembled nothing so much as an insect's cocoon. In fact, she had been immobilized that thoroughly. — Just as it had once been done to the most fearsome Witch in the world.

"But, with this…gwub."

Landing beside the fallen Witch, Al tried to raise his head, only to be struck by a wave of exhaustion, dropping to his knees and retching.
The makeshift left arm had already crumbled away. Hastily lifting the chin of his helmet with his right hand, he vomited a great gush of yellow stomach bile from his exposed mouth, his entire body creaking under the tremendous exhaustion.

A pounding, violent pain assaulted his head, and his vision blacked out completely.
This sensation — his sight wouldn't return for a while. It was a small mercy that the Witch's binding was already complete, but—

"—Correction required."

"Huh?"

Out of breath, the corner of his mouth stained with drool mixed with stomach acid, Al lifted his face.
Even lifted, his vision remained dark, but he turned toward the direction the voice had come from, raising a question.

What had the Witch just said?
With her Gate forcibly shut, unable to weave magic, what could she possibly—

"What?"

With his sight sealed away, Al couldn't notice — that far to the north of the Imperial Capital, the entirety of the Crystal Palace, called the most beautiful castle in the world, had begun to glow faintly.


△▼△▼△▼△


"—————"

The Crystal Palace flickered, and the Magic Crystal Cannon, the ultimate weapon of the imperial capital Lupugana, fired.
Just before the Great Disaster threatened the entire Empire, it had once been fired during the phase of the Empire's civil war, when the living fought each other over the throne — a weapon so far beyond human hands it could rewrite the very map.

That its sights were now trained upon themselves — no, upon the Witch, now a black mass upon the ground — Cecilus Segmunt, true to form, noticed at once.
That was a self-destruct tactic: locking onto the Witch herself, to guarantee the target was caught in the blast. Whoever had thought of it was a genius of spite, truly wicked and cunning — the doing of a villain.

"Masayume."

Confirming the feel of the enchanted sword in his hand, Cecilus tried to push his body, fresh off cutting down a star, further still.
For Al, who had achieved a monumental triumph, any more of a moment to shine would be excessive; behind him lay a sleeping heroine he had to protect, both for the sake of the story and as its leading man; and there was the Perfect Cecilus, vaguely nourished and revived by his father's death — all the more reason to rally himself.

A star, then the Empire's greatest treasure, and standing against it, himself — the "Blue Lightning" — and it was just then—

"—————"

With one foot raised mid-step, Cecilus's blue eyes went wide.
Then he let out a breath, and set the raised foot back down. Setting it down, he shook his head with a sigh—


"—Al Shamak!!"


Just before the light of the Magic Crystal Cannon could reach them, a great voice echoed across the sky of the Imperial Capital.
Immediately after, the sky's colors, dyed freely in red and white, were swallowed in black, and a bottomlessly vast hole, swallowing even light itself, opened in the heavens.
It was a flashy, uninvited intrusion that gloriously smashed apart the scheme of the Witch, who had tried to steal victory here by sacrificing herself — and the one who did it—

"You'd better show up soon, or you won't have anything left to do, boss."

Catching sight in the sky of the small, small figure who had created that light-swallowing chasm, Cecilus smiled at the sight of the black-haired boy and the girl in a dress, hand in hand.
It surely wasn't that the boy had heard Cecilus's smiling voice, but he shouted all the same.

"Don't think I'll let you have your way! — Bring it on, Fate!!"
