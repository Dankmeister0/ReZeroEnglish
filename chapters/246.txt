Arc 4 Chapter 79: "The End of a Dream"

The instant Subaru noticed oxygen passing through his throat, he coughed violently, as if trying to spit out the clot of blood still lodged in his windpipe.
Lying on his back where he'd collapsed to the ground, he repeated ragged, gasping breaths, grasping after oxygen, after the sustenance of life.

There was no room in his heart to think himself pathetic for it.
Only, having bitten through his own tongue in his desire for "death," the weakness of clinging without hesitation to this path to salvation now flickering before his eyes struck him as terribly wretched.
But even so—

"Do I…!"

"—Hm?"

"Do I have any worth living…? I who won't die… who dies, and does nothing but repeat… do I have any worth beyond that?"

To perform "Return by Death" and pull everyone back from the brink of despair.
The result obtained by paying with his life—he had believed that was the only worth Natsuki Subaru had.
But could he allow himself to think that wasn't so?

"Is it… is it all right for me to think that I, as a person, have worth beyond 'Return by Death'? Is it all right for me to think that the people I love… think of me, too, as someone they love? Is it… all right?"

"…I wouldn't know a thing like that."

To Subaru's feeble question, Minerva answered curtly, turning her face away.
Having lost her right arm and both legs, she deftly put distance between herself and Subaru using only her left hand, then first turned her face toward her own right shoulder—and bit down. An instant later, droplets of light regenerated Minerva's missing right arm.
She opened and closed the fist of her now sleeveless right arm, and then struck both hands against the roots of her legs—the stumps where her thighs and everything below had been lost—regenerating both legs just as she had the arm.

Her already-short skirt became even more precarious, and with her right arm bared to the shoulder her appearance was extremely risqué, but the "Witch of Wrath" Minerva had returned to full readiness.
Standing firmly on her restored legs, she crossed her arms in a pose that emphasized her ample chest, and looked down at Subaru. Then—

"I don't know your worth. But that girl wishes for you to live, worthless or not, and… you saw it yourself, in the two Trials, didn't you?"

"…But the second Trial was about my mistakes, the crime I committed—"

"Are you an idiot? That wasn't meant to make you take responsibility for the world you got wrong. It was meant to show you exactly who was grieving, and how much, because of your mistake. —Isn't that exactly the answer you wanted?"

"——Hh."

He remembered.
The crying voice. The voice that swallowed its own regret. The voice that sent him off with strength. The gentle farewell he always received. The whispered words of love that trusted him. The words from that very beginning that had become the driving force of his fight.

His had been a life he thought held nothing.
Empty-handed, having even dropped the things he should have carried, he had believed he'd been summoned to this world with nothing at all.
To prove his own worth, he'd had no choice but to keep resisting. And to protect the precious things he gained along the way of that resistance, he'd had no choice but to keep walking on, ever more alone.

He had assumed he only ever received—but could he now think that wasn't true?
Would they cry for him?
Would they lament their own lack of strength, for his sake?
Did they wish to see the future together with him?
Would they let him stand, laughing, beside the people precious to him?

A qualification he didn't know if he possessed.
But surely it was something that, once he'd walked to the end of the path he had, until just a moment ago, stubbornly resolved to walk alone, he would no longer be permitted to hold.
At the end of a fight waged with a heart turned to steel, with a will unshaken by anything—surely there would be no softness left even for a smile.
So then, could he believe it?

Even if it meant a choice that traded his own heart away in exchange for a future for the people he loved.
Even if it meant a choice where, desperate to protect his own heart, he could no longer move forward at all.

That there could be a third option, neither one nor the other—a greedy option.
That there could be a choice where he could see the future with the people precious to him while still remaining Natsuki Subaru—that he was allowed to wish for that—could he believe it?

"—I forgive you."

"——"

Subaru's feelings, never spoken aloud, only spilling out as tears.
And yet the timing matched so perfectly it seemed as if they had somehow been carried as sound.

Lying with his body stretched on the ground, Subaru turned his face and saw, beyond Minerva—still kneeling in the meadow, not even wiping the tears wetting her face—a face that was smiling.

That face he still could not see, even now.
Blocked by the veil of darkness, the expression turned toward Subaru remained unknown to him. And yet, that she was smiling came through all the same.
Echidna had said it: the reason Subaru couldn't see her face was because Subaru hadn't accepted her. That only the fact of her smiling came through was simply because Subaru's unconscious mind had decided that what he could, in truth, see was something he could not.

"I was saved by you. And so, I forgive you for being saved. I wish for you to be saved—that is what I hope."

Feeling Satella's words, her voice, soak into his cracked heart, Subaru covered his face with his arm. His face was already a mess of tears, undoubtedly incapable of looking any more pathetic than it already did—but even so, he didn't want anyone to see it.

After having hurled such a torrent of curses, how could he possibly find relief in Satella's words now? Much less let his expression soften because of them.
And yet it was also true that Satella's inscrutable words of "love" had made Subaru understand the true meaning of the Trial.

"…That Minerva broke through Typhon and Sekhmet's interference is surprising enough, but personally, what you two did is the more unexpected part for me."

Paying no mind to Subaru, whose sight was still covered, it was Echidna who murmured this quietly.
Glancing at Minerva, who had restored both her arms and legs, Echidna then turned her gaze elsewhere—toward the scene where Typhon was pinned down by claw-like limbs extending from a black-lacquered coffin, and Daphne and Sekhmet, the coffin's mistress, faced off against her.

At Echidna's words, it was Daphne who laughed, a low sound rumbling in her throat. Having torn the lower part of her restraint garment, she stood barefoot in the meadow, tongue out, and said,

"The one who's got the best compatibility with Ty-Ty is, well, definitely Daphne, isn't it. The centipede coffin doesn't have a mind of its own—it's Daphne's hands and feet, so it's got terrible compatibility with Ty-Ty's Authority."

"Uu—, 'Fune, don't get in the way— Nn! Uu!"

"So, hah. Your real body's the one keeping me in check, is it, huu. Not that I'd expect otherwise from Echidna, hah. Why're you pulling a stunt like this, huu. Unlike Minerva, I don't get why you'd take sides, hah."

Sekhmet scratched roughly at her excessive volume of hair. Pinned down as she was with Typhon effectively held hostage, it seemed she couldn't afford to move carelessly.
In response to Sekhmet's question, Daphne swayed her short, bundled hair and laughed, "Nooo,"

"Su-baru-n went and talked big to Daphne, you see. Something about how he'd already killed the White Whale, so next up was the Great Rabbit? So, I figured, well, at least go far enough to actually challenge it, I thought."

"An interesting opinion. If he were so inclined, that could indeed come to pass. You understand that too, don't you… do you wish for the Great Rabbit to be destroyed?"

"Not really? Once it's been split off from Daphne, those kids' hunger has nothing to do with Daphne's hunger. Wherever it dies, it's none of my concern, but… if it's going to happen anyway, I might be a little curious how the Great Rabbit—the very embodiment of Daphne's unending hunger—ends up."

Because, Daphne added, continuing on,

"If its ending turns out to be a fulfilled one, that'd be an unknown kind of happiness for Daphne too, you know."

For Daphne, forever tormented by insatiable hunger, being sated was a dream forever out of reach.
And the Great Rabbit was, in a sense, another self reflecting that endless hunger of hers—though Daphne herself felt no such kinship with it at all.
If that Great Rabbit were to meet an end different from what she herself had known—whether that would count as being sated or not, and whether there had ever been a possibility of her own fulfillment—that, rare for her, seemed to interest her in a way that had nothing to do with appetite, and she laughed.

Satisfied with Daphne's answer, Echidna nodded, then turned her head once more. Not toward Subaru, nor Satella, nor Minerva. Not toward Daphne, Sekhmet, and Typhon either, who had just fought their fierce battle, but toward a figure standing apart from the group, isolated just as Echidna herself was.
Looking at the "Witch of Lust" Carmilla, Echidna gently smoothed her own white hair.

"Carmilla, what about you? Do you have a reason like Daphne's?"

"Wh-what… are you trying to say? E-Echidna-chan, is…?"

"It's simple. As his consciousness hovered at the brink of death, about to vanish, you called out to him, didn't you? With your Authority as the 'Faceless Goddess,' you must have known exactly what the result of that would be."

"——"

"Your call must have carried every meaning to him. And you must have understood that as well. So I ask—you shouldn't have thought well of him. And yet you did this. Why?"

At Echidna's question, Carmilla pressed a hand to her mouth, her eyes wandering. The gesture, directed toward Daphne and Minerva, was one that hoped for support from someone other than herself.
But there was no "witch" here who could be seduced into covering for Carmilla, beloved by all as she was.
Reluctantly, Carmilla bit her finger, eyes moistening, and looked at Echidna.

"I—it's not… any reason, okay? Echi—Echidna-chan already got, mm, turned down when she invited him, so she's satisfied enough already… and whatever everyone's mad about, whatever they're fighting over, as long as… it doesn't, doesn't spread to me, that's fine, just… only…"

"Only?"

"'Love' is… important, you know? You can't neglect it… mm, you can't. Even if that boy… doesn't want to look at it, 'love' is still there… and what's there, I won't let it be denied. And besides, I… absolutely hate leaving a debt unpaid."

Only at that last part did Carmilla suddenly speak with startling clarity, and Echidna shrugged.
Then, the "Witch of Greed," wearing a wry smile, looked over each of the witches' faces in turn.

"Sekhmet and Typhon tried to respect his will, and Minerva, seeking to respect life, healed him. Daphne cooperated to extend his life so as to see through to the end his so-called will to fight, and Carmilla used her Authority to teach him of the love he kept refusing to look at. —So then, whatever each of you may claim, in the end, every single one of you is trying to help Natsuki Subaru."

As Echidna characterized the witches' actions this way, their expressions all shifted at once.
Pride tilted her head; Sloth exhaled a listless breath; Wrath snorted and crossed her arms; Gluttony devoured one of the legs extending from the coffin and laughed; Lust twisted her face in distaste.
And having watched all of this, Greed placed a hand to her chin.

"How interesting, after all. —Don't you think so, too?"

Loosening her lips into a happy-looking smile, Echidna.
The direction of her words was aimed straight ahead—at Subaru, who was unsteadily rising to his feet, his body swaying.

Even now, wiping with his sleeve at tear tracks not yet fully dried, Subaru finally managed to stand on both legs, but he did not answer Echidna's question.
He only swept his listless eyes over Echidna, over the faces of the witches. And then—

"What the hell… are you all, really?"

"——"

"Curiosity. Sympathy. Pity. A sense of duty. Expectation. Disgust. …I can barely make sense of most of your reasons for taking my side. I get why you're all called witches."

"I take it you've recovered enough energy to hurl insults, at least?"

"…I don't know."

To Echidna, one eye closed, Subaru muttered, placing a hand on his own chest.
The words that escaped him plainly expressed the whole of his current state of heart.

"What I had to do—I thought I'd already decided that. And what I have to do hasn't changed, even now. That's certain. It's not a mistake."

But, he went on, speaking to no one in particular, as if speaking to himself,

"I'd already decided that this was the only way to do it, too. I'd made up my mind to choose it… I'd steeled myself to choose it. And yet, coming to this place, the Trial shattered that."

The second Trial, the "Unforgivable Present"—it had thrust the results of Subaru's actions before him, tearing his heart apart with the merciless reality he had tried to paper over with the word "resolve."
Even after being confronted with that, Subaru had tried to demand of himself that he come to terms with it, that he see through his resolve to the end. In fact, that was what should have happened.

"But then I found out the real feelings of you, who I thought I might be able to lean on for help, and right on top of that, even Satella showed up… my head's a total mess. Don't you all just line up and say whatever you want. I set the task I had to do for myself. And yet…"

Why make him cling to a life he was supposed to treat as expendable, now of all times?
Why teach him to treasure a life meant to be used up entirely, now of all times?
Now, having been made to realize he had been loved all along—what was he supposed to do with that?

"Right now… I don't know what I should do anymore."

Subaru's reason screamed that unless he died, he couldn't protect everyone.
Subaru's memory told him that there were people who would grieve over him wearing himself away.
If he didn't die, someone would grieve. If he died, someone would grieve too.

"—Once more, I will ask you, Natsuki Subaru."

To Subaru, shaking his head that refused to come together, Echidna said, lowering the tone of her voice.
When he raised his face, Echidna, one finger raised, had come to stand directly in front of him.

Seeing that Subaru's gaze had settled on her, she nodded slowly.

"If I cooperate with you, you will without fail reach a future where you save the people you wish to save. There will be no more need to agonize. To put it in the most extreme terms, I will take on the resolution of whatever problems you face. All you need do is throw yourself bodily into carrying it out, focusing solely on clearing each obstacle. If continuing to agonize is too painful, entrusting everything to me is one option. I will not blame you for it, and in a sense, I would even welcome it. So, once more, I ask you."

"——"

"Won't you let me take the hand of you, who doesn't know what to do? I promise I will bring you to the future, without fail."

Saying this gently, Echidna held out her hand to Subaru.
Looking down at those white fingers, and then at Echidna's face as she waited for his answer, Subaru held his breath.

It was the same manner of address as before, when they'd had their conversation of rejection.
Back then, Subaru had come to know Echidna's true nature, and had feared that nerve of hers, driven by nothing but a curiosity so intense it was chilling.

But what about now? If he set aside a little time, and calmly considered her words—what then?
Treating life as expendable, running through every conceivable trial and error, forcing his way through obstacle after obstacle by methods that amounted to sheer brute strength. With Echidna's counsel, Subaru continuing to fight even as it wore his heart down—how was that any different from the Subaru who, even refusing her cooperation, had resolved to keep fighting alone?

Out of sheer stubbornness, unable to bear his revulsion at Echidna's attitude, Subaru had rejected her.
But if he had truly, in the fullest sense, been resolved to throw everything away and sacrifice himself, then he should have shut his eyes even to Echidna's true nature, and used her, exactly as her words suggested.
If even that was something he denied out of his own fastidiousness, and the road he walked led to the same place regardless—then what meaning was there in his stubborn refusal?

He should take that hand.
If he had the resolve to fight on, unafraid of being hurt, swallowing down every pain and every suffering—he should have taken that hand.
And so,

"Echidna."

"——"

"I'm afraid of getting hurt."

"——"

"I hate pain, and suffering, and sorrow, too. I don't want to feel pain, and I don't want to watch someone other than me suffer horribly, either. —I don't… want to die."

"——"

"So your hand, that comes with sacrifice built in—I can't take it. Not anymore."

What Subaru could do, even Subaru himself did not yet fully know.
But choosing the same path Echidna offered—that, he found, he could no longer bring himself to do.

He had become aware of himself as someone who did not want to die.
He had learned that there were people who would accept him even without dying—him, who had believed he could only ever contribute through death.
Natsuki Subaru was not a man whose only worth lay in dying.

The people who had grieved over Subaru's "death" had not grieved for him because they found worth in his "death."
So then—what had they grieved for, in him?

"I still don't know what that is. —But I think I'm going to look for it. If I find it, I feel like I can repay everyone in some way other than 'death.'"

"…But that is a thorny path, Natsuki Subaru. The road where you treat 'death' as a tool for cutting your way forward, advancing even as you wear yourself away—that, however rough, is without question the shortest path that leads to the future. All you had to offer was your own heart. To deny that, and try to have both—your own heart, and the future of those precious to you—is an extraordinarily difficult thing to want. And above all—"

Echidna paused, drew breath.
And then, wearing the most bewitching smile she had shown yet, she said,

"—it's greedy."

The "Witch of Greed," who affirmed desire, accepted Subaru's decision with a pleased expression.
Even having had her proposal rejected, the witch's thinking, smiling all the happier for it, was something Subaru still could not understand. Only,

"It's true that I nearly got saved by you, more than once. …Even if, deep down, you never thought of me as anything more than a lab animal or something, that much is true."

There had certainly been times he'd gotten through hardship with Echidna's presence as a support for his heart.
So, for that time she'd given him, for the measure of grace she'd granted to protect his heart, he felt certain gratitude, at least.

"—The foolish, pitiable Garfiel is afraid of the outside world."

"…Eh?"

"What that child saw in the first Trial has bound him ever since. If you mean to break through the situation by your own strength, you'll need to undo that spell as well."

"Echidna?"

"With the other witches all having behaved so favorably toward you, it would be a fine thing indeed if I alone offered nothing. I'll not have you thinking, deep down, 'all the witches turned out to be good sorts at heart, except Echidna, who stayed rotten to the end.' I am, believe it or not, a girl too, and it's a fact that I hold some favor toward you as well."

Speaking quickly, Echidna gave Subaru's chest a light poke with her hand.
Pushed back by the force of it, by the time Subaru raised his face again, Echidna had already turned her back to him. Her white hair swaying, the "Witch of Greed" put distance between herself and Subaru.
The other witches, too, gazed quietly at Subaru.

"…To me, you're all people I just can't understand."

"——"

"You've confused me to the point I feel like I'm going crazy, and even now I'm still irritated at what you all said. I keep thinking, don't go having conversations I don't know about over my head—and I can't say I like any of you."

It was the honest truth.
Each of the witches held values, unwavering, entirely her own—and those were, without doubt, irreconcilable with Subaru's—no, with any ordinary person's.
So Subaru could not understand them, and could not make sense of their actions.
But, just as with what he'd felt toward Echidna, not understanding and gratitude were two different things.

"Thank you for trying to let me die. Thank you for not letting me die. Thank you for letting me hear an important voice. —For that, thank you."

Bowing his head to each of the witches in turn, he found it satisfying to see them catch their breath.
Then Subaru turned and began to walk.

Ahead of him was the girl still kneeling in the meadow—Satella.

She looked up at Subaru approaching her, and held her breath as if her throat had closed.
At the sight of her, so frightened, so exactly like a small girl, Subaru lost his voice.

Why was it that warmth filled his chest, toward someone he had once thought even loathsome?
What was this feeling he kept holding, toward someone he had never even touched?

Too many unanswerable questions had been given to Subaru in this place.
Unable to produce a single answer to any of them, choosing instead "to keep agonizing over it," Subaru held out his hand to the witch sitting on the ground.
She looked at the offered hand with uncertainty.

"I… don't know what you are. I don't know why you say you love me, or what you mean when you say… that I saved you."

"Ah…"

"But it's true that I've been saved, over and over, by the 'Return by Death' you gave me. It's true that I relied on it completely, and that's how I managed to get this far."

"——"

"To me, is 'Return by Death'… one option among others?"

"——"

"Are you saying that not relying on it completely is what it means to love myself?"

"——"

"I can't just come to terms with it, that easily. —But it's also true, without question, that you—who gave me 'Return by Death'—are the one who made me not want to die."

So,

"Like you say, I'm going to try, just a little more, to… like myself. To treasure myself. I don't know what'll come of it, but that's fine."

"…Are you all right?"

"Yeah… compared to dying, this is nothing."

Answering Satella's worried voice like that, Subaru managed a weak but genuine smile.
Seeing that expression, Satella, seeming relieved, took Subaru's hand.

An instant later, the sound of the world cracking caught in Subaru's eardrums.
The blue sky and green meadow faded away, and the castle of dreams released Natsuki Subaru.

"—Am I going back outside?"

What he'd been doing, how he'd been doing it, how he'd even arrived here—all of it was hazy.
Once outside, what should he do first? Even that, the matters of his heart left uncertain.

"Don't struggle alone. With the people who cherish you, together…"

"——"

"With the people who don't wish for your death, and the people you wish not to let die—fight on, together. …And if, even so, it's beyond your reach, don't forget to fear 'death,' and not to die."

"——"

"Don't forget that there are people who would grieve, if you died—"

The world shattered apart with a sound.
Satella's voice grew distant too. That, terribly, clawed at Subaru's heart.

The joined palm was strangely hot.
He felt, somehow, that he mustn't let go of this hand.

"—I…"

The words of a call would not come.
The voice to call out to her, to Satella, would not come. As if he mustn't speak that name, a feeling of wanting to reject her and a feeling of wanting to accept her fought within him.

The sky came falling down. The ground split open. Light came flooding in, and the surroundings no longer formed the castle of dreams.
The witches' figures vanished into nothing, and in all the world there were only the two of them, Subaru and Satella.

Vanishing. And beginning.
—Subaru gazed at Satella before him, unable to put anything into words.

"——"

Suddenly, the veil of darkness fell.
The jet-black that Subaru's unconscious had, surely, denied him from wanting to see, was swept away.
And seeing the face that peered out from beneath it, Subaru caught his breath.

To Subaru, holding his breath, Satella swayed her silver hair, narrowed her violet eyes, and as tears spilled from their corners—

"And someday—you must come and kill me, without fail."

Vanishing.
Being lost.
The world dissolved away, and even the figure of the girl before him became impossible to see.

"I will, without fail—"

Only gripping tightly, as if to confirm the warmth of her palm alone, Subaru said,

"—I'll save you."

Toward the beloved girl, now vanished from sight, that much, at least, he swore through to the end.
