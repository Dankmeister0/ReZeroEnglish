Arc 6 Chapter 83: "Ram"

—Red, red, she remembers being wrapped in flames that burned everything to nothing.

The calm, stagnant, decadent days came to an abrupt end.
Before that maddened violence, nothing held meaning — not the title of strongest Demi-Human race, not the office of village chief most feared in the village, not even the universal love of a parent protecting a child.

That the spreading slaughter went unnoticed for so long was a bitter regret.
—No, in this case, perhaps the enemy's skill should be praised instead.

They were things despised, shunned, cast out, and persecuted by the world.
Precisely because of that, they knew how to lurk in darkness, kill their presence, banish sound, and creep in unseen.

It could be said that the outcome was decided from the moment of that first ambush.

By the time the horns sensed the faint stagnation mixed into the mana in the air, it was already too late.
First, half the village's people were cut down in the opening move, and of those still able to fight, more than half were lost right there. On top of that, even among those who did sense something wrong, half again failed to recognize the urgency, dismissing it as some mistake.
In short, their hearts had rotted from peace and tranquility.

The Oni Tribe — once called the strongest of the Demi-Human races, said to have been capable of changing the course of the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica's fortunes had they joined the Demi-Human War. But even if that "what if" of history had somehow come to pass, surely the Oni Tribe would never have accomplished much of anything.

In any case, the half that had been culled in the first strike was halved again in the second.
By this point fires had broken out all over the village, and by the time death-screams echoed red against the night sky, every oni in the village had realized something was terribly wrong. And yet, only two of them realized, even then, that the Oni Tribe itself was about to be annihilated—

"—Ram! Break through the encirclement! As long as you live, that's enough!!"

The elder roared those words, his two great horns swollen huge, every muscle in his body bulging.
He shouldered his own weapon, a great blade, and burst from his house, hurling those words at Ram as she carved apart the rank-and-file with wind. Live, he said — not out of concern for Ram.
It was because the elder, more earnestly than anyone, believed that Ram was the Oni Tribe's shining future.

The glory of the Oni Tribe of old, the return of the "Oni God" who had once reigned supreme in the age of the "Witch."
That was the role hoped for the child prodigy named Ram — and surely it was the desperate wish of the last chieftain of an Oni Tribe that had forgotten how to fight.

"Hah!"

She wanted to laugh it off through her nose as absurd.
Even now, at this stage, was the wish of the village's representative oni to entrust an unrealized dream to the future? There was so much else that could be done — quickly rally the others, attempt a counterattack against the enemy. Any number of things.
But Ram had no intention of offering the elder that counsel.

It wasn't because of what was happening tonight.
Long, long before this, Ram had already given up on her own kind.

"The glory of the oni…"

Ridiculous. Ridiculous. Ridiculous.
That the purest blood of such an Oni Tribe flowed through her own veins — even that fact was repulsive.
Certainly, if she wished for power, her blood would boil, exhilaration would seize her entire body, and she would be filled with an omnipotent sense that everything in existence existed for her sake.
In fact, if Ram had grown up healthy and whole, that sense of omnipotence might have become real.
But Ram never wished for it.

Rather than play the god-child in a narrow little world, Ram had a future she wanted to choose.
One worth far more than being celebrated as the return of the Oni God, worth far more than living out a lifetime as the figurehead of a clan clinging to glory long since destroyed.

—To live as ■■'s ■.

"————"

Focusing her awareness on her forehead, she drew the heat-lit mana from her white horns into her whole body.
When she willed it strongly, Ram's perception expanded, vastly, hugely, hijacking the sight of every living thing around her, letting her grasp with perfect clarity everything happening in the small village.

The enemy's numbers were many, and a siege net had been cast to keep anyone from escaping the village.
The response had been too slow from the very start, and only a quarter of the village had managed anything resembling resistance — and even that number had already dwindled to what could be counted on one hand. The Oni Tribe's pride was running out of stock.
The elder alone seemed to be fighting valiantly, but the enemies gathered against him were formidable — the elder, falling into a losing position, was already wrapped in the thick scent of "death."

"—■■"

Wind wrapped around her small body, Ram tore through the village like a gale.
The only thing her lips shaped, the only thing that filled her mind, was the one beloved ■. It wasn't that Ram was heartless. She was simply redirecting her focus.
Because Ram's parents had been among that very first half, and there was already no way to save them.

—She had never hated her parents.
It was only that the two of them, for better or worse, were oni who had chosen to be born in this village, raised in this village, and die in this village, unknowingly accepting a slow extinction.
So in a sense, their lives scattering tonight had also been a kind of inevitability.
That said—

"—That doesn't mean I won't make them pay."

A black shadow stood in her path, an enemy wrapped head to toe in a robe.
At the ridiculous figure wielding a cross-shaped sword, Ram unleashed wind with no restraint whatsoever.

Whether they underestimated her as a child, or simply lacked the strength.
Unable to withstand Ram's wind-blades, the black shadows were cut down one after another, corpses piling up in gruesome numbers. Ram continued on, wind wrapped around her, dancing through the fire as she carried on the slaughter.

Seen from a certain angle, it might have looked like a scene from some beautiful dance.
But in reality, with every swing of her arm, a life scattered, and with every form she caused to lose its shape, a dark joy screamed in triumph within her young chest.

Kill more, it howled and raged.
Take the blood, the flesh, the bone, the soul, the life — the thing within her demanded it.
This was not the first night she had heard that voice's pleading.

—For as long as she could remember, from the moment she was born, that voice had tempted her at every opening.

Surrender to blood, to flesh, to bone, to soul, surrender your life — it demanded the awakening of the thing within her.
Kill more and more, it insisted, break more and more, it demanded.

What could possibly be so wonderful about this — Ram could not understand it at all.
Neither the elder nor her parents, not a single one of them, could understand this either. And to those who demanded of Ram some role other than simply being Ram, she felt no desire to explain it.

It was as though her horns were controlling her.
Without something solid to call herself, her young personality would easily have been swallowed, destroyed, and she would have become exactly the returned Oni God everyone wished for.
But that did not happen. Because—

"—Onee-chan!!"

Called by a high voice, she turned to see ■■ lit by flame.
In that instant, a burst of wind blew away the swarm of black shadows closing in, scattering them all in a single breath.
And then Ram hurried to ■'s side.

"■■……"

Frightened eyes, unable to put strength into her legs, ■■ had collapsed right where she stood.
Ram reached out a hand to her beloved ■, trying to pull her up. As the elder wished, she had to survive. But not alone — together with ■■—.

—And it was in that instant.

Having confirmed ■■'s safety, a single moment's carelessness slipped in.
By the time she noticed the presence surrounding her, she was already encircled, and it was too harsh a situation to even carve out an escape route. Alone, it would not have been impossible. But to survive alone was no different from dying.

Somehow, she had to break this situation open.
For that, she released every restraint she had placed on her sealed power, and turned the raging wind on the enemy—

"————"

That must have been the gap in her heart brought about by that omnipotence she so despised.
A black shadow slipped past her wind-blade, and the slash it unleashed struck her forehead hard, and her vision burst apart.

Reeling from the massive impact, tasting an overwhelming sense of loss, Ram watched.
Spinning, spinning, a white horn flew off into the red night sky, lit by the flames devouring the village, spinning round and round.

Understanding that it was her own horn, her thin throat let out a scream at the pain and the loss.
But even as she screamed, Ram noticed something at the same time.

The voice that had gnawed at her ceaselessly since the moment of her birth — she could no longer hear it.
Ah, was it really this simple, she thought, and found her own foolishness almost funny.

Watching the horn trace its arc across the red night sky—


—Ah, it finally broke.


—was what she thought.

—Through the sight shared by "Clairvoyance," a black Earth Dragon fled, desperately, desperately.

Carried on its back was the girl who should have been Ram's precious other half.
A missing half-wing of memory, leaving nothing behind but a hollow sense of loss—

"—Rem."

Understanding what that meant, Ram's very core trembled with rage.
Galling as it was to admit, thanks to Subaru's cooperation, Ram had reclaimed a portion of the power from her prime, and with it she had overwhelmed the Deadly Sin Archbishop of "Gluttony," Lye Batenkaitos, driving him into a losing position.
Ram herself could say with certainty that if killing alone had been the goal, there had been countless opportunities to do so.

But the troublesome Authority the man possessed — the power to devour others' "memories" and "names" — had made Ram hesitate to carelessly take that blasphemer's life.
It hadn't been mercy. It had been a judgment forced by necessity. But a result was a result.
As a result, Batenkaitos had used his Authority to escape from Ram's grasp, and made straight for securing the person of Rem, Ram's other half.
His aim was obvious — he was certain he could not beat Ram in a straightforward fight.

Fight head-on, and if you sense you're at a disadvantage, retreat and change tactics.
A Deadly Sin Archbishop was no warrior, nothing of the sort. They were beings that acted with pure greed solely to fulfill their own desires — they had not a single reason to care how they won.

Hence, as revenge against Ram, who had made him taste humiliation, and as a means of chipping away at the Hornless Ram's time limit using the "memories" he had stolen from Rem — that was his scheme.
And, infuriatingly, that strategy was the optimal one.

If Rem's person were seized, Ram could easily be neutralized.
Even setting that aside, the longer this dragged on, the more their side's fighting strength would dwindle. With every passing moment, their chances of victory shrank.
So—

"I need to catch up to Patrasche as fast as possible."

Fortunately, Patrasche, entrusted with carrying Rem, was clever enough to be called, among all those gathered at this tower, second in reliability only to Julius among the Earth Dragons.
Subaru and Beatrice were inconsistent, and with Echidna and Meili there was too much unknown. As for Emilia, whose atmosphere made relying on her utterly out of the question — that went without saying.

And this part was complicated, but Batenkaitos was clearly toying with Patrasche.
Though he could have cornered her in an instant if he'd wanted to, Batenkaitos was deliberately easing off his attacks, leaving openings in the pursuit, enjoying a hunt that wore his prey down. All of it, so that he could share this sight, and sear it into Ram's eyes.

She could not let him do as he pleased any longer—

"—ah"

It happened just as she wrapped herself in wind and moved to break into a run.
Her vision, aimed at the upper floor via the spiral staircase, blurred, and for an instant "Clairvoyance" came undone. She kept Batenkaitos's view in her right eye, maintained her own sight in her left — and still the scenery wavered.

That wasn't all. A heavy fatigue she hadn't felt until just now, along with a discomfort and pain as if invisible hands were stirring her insides, came crashing down on Ram herself.

This was, unmistakably, the effect of the lethargy Ram normally suffered.
The price meant to eternally eat away at the "Hornless" Ram — which Subaru had loudly declared he would take on through some Authority of his own — was rebounding onto Ram.

The first possibility that came to mind was that Subaru had died some pathetic, meaningless death.
But she could judge that wasn't the case, given how light the burden rebounding onto her was. Considering the power Ram had drawn out, even for just a few minutes, the price should have been far heavier than this.
It ought to have been suffering severe enough that, quite literally, vomiting blood and writhing on the ground would have been no surprise.

That it wasn't like that meant that, though something unforeseen had happened, it wasn't the reason Subaru had been completely knocked out of the fight.
Or else, one could imagine the exact opposite of what had happened.
That is, a case where some misfortune had befallen someone that Subaru had to take on, one heavier than the burden on Ram's body.

"Lady Beatrice, or Meili, or Sir Julius…"

Those were the possibilities she could think of, but pinning down the answer had no real meaning.
What mattered at this moment was that it had become difficult for Ram to summon the kind of power that had just overwhelmed Batenkaitos. In terms of restraints, she could remove only one more.
If she pushed herself, she might manage to remove a second, but even then it was only a rough guess whether she could sustain it for even a few dozen seconds.

And with that, could she beat Lye Batenkaitos—

"Why grow faint of heart. —All that's left is to devise a way to win."

Even as this passed, the chances of victory for their side kept dwindling.
Ram caught her footing on the step she'd nearly missed, once more, and dashed on up the spiral staircase.

—Feeling the ache of that missing piece within her stir, as though she had, once, run breathlessly like this before, racing to reach a little sister being chased.

"—!!"

"Hahah! You really are something! Way to hang in there, and you're just an Earth Dragon, too!"

Praising the black Earth Dragon sprinting through the narrow corridor even as his thrown dagger tore at its scales.
Blood flowed pitifully from the gouged wound, but the dragon carried out its wise flight, utterly resolved not to let its condition for victory be shaken loose.

The "Divine Protection of Wind Evasion" is a trait every member of the Earth Dragon race possesses.
As long as that dragon keeps running, it can ignore most external factors — wind, poor footing — and affirm the act of "running" toward its goal.
That blessing extends even to a Dragon Carriage bound to the Earth Dragon, or to the rider mounted on its back.
Which is to say, the reason the unconscious "Sleeping Beauty," strapped to the saddle, hadn't been thrown off was largely thanks to being under the effect of that "Divine Protection of Wind Evasion."
Without it, the "Sleeping Beauty" would have long since fallen into Batenkaitos's hands.

"Amazing, amazing, so admirable! Getting your running obstructed this much and every which way, and yet look at you, refusing to stop those legs. Well, I guess I get it, since if you stop moving the Divine Protection of Wind Evasion cancels, so of course you're desperate!"

"—!!"

"Earth Dragons are the best, aren't they, hard workers, loyal to their masters. I bet if you were human, you'd have made a dish we 'Gourmets' would've drooled over! But, but, but, but, but, but, but, but, but! Sadly, an Earth Dragon just won't fill our stomachs!"

It had will, it had a soul, it had "memories," it had a "name."
And yet the Authority of "Gluttony" could not devour that of anything but a human.
So this Earth Dragon, standing so desperately, so valiantly, against an enemy it could not hope to resist — Batenkaitos could not cherish it in the one way he most wanted to.

Something that smelled so delicious his mouth watered, and he couldn't eat it.
It was just like a meal painted by some supreme brush onto a canvas. —They say a picture of rice cake can't fill your stomach, or something like that.

"Ahh, that's it, that's exactly it! When you're hungry, hungry, hungry, hungry, hungry beyond all reason! A picture of such a delicious-looking meal is torture, plain and simple. Isn't this basically child abuse!?"

Chasing after the sprinting dragon, he blew out the clot of blood packed in his nose.
His face had been crushed in the earlier fight, and his left eyeball rolled loose, the eye socket apparently shattered. His fangs were broken, his tongue split, blood soaking his lower jaw without end, but none of it mattered at all.

—Just thinking that Ram might be watching this scene right now sent a thrill through him, right to his core.

"Sister is—"

Pure, noble, perfect, a flawless completed being.
That was what the "memory" sleeping within Batenkaitos insisted, and he considered it a fair and proper evaluation, earned after being beaten half to death without landing a single blow of his own.
When Ram was serious, Batenkaitos was no match for her. —No, probably no Deadly Sin Archbishop, whoever they were, would stand a chance against a serious Ram; she'd wring the life out of them without a second thought.
Regulus, perhaps, might have made a fight of it through the absoluteness of his Authority, but—

"Well, there's no way that idiot could kill Sister anyway. Even if he couldn't kill her, he'd have just ended up throwing her off the Great Waterfall or something."

Even if she couldn't be killed, there were plenty of ways to seal her away.
Just like the "Witch of Envy," who even the Three Great Heroes couldn't kill, and who remains sealed within the Evil Sealing Stone Shrine to this day.
Which is to say, no matter what—

"To properly entertain a sister as splendid and perfect as her, we've got to make preparations to match, don't we, or it'd be rude!"

His unsteady left eye wide open, Batenkaitos wore a dripping, gruesome smile of blood.
The Earth Dragon's speed was impressive, but indoors, that mobility was wasted talent. Especially since Batenkaitos, every so often, crossed space using footwork borrowed from "memory," able to make the distance between them vanish as though it had never existed.

"I've got to grow into someone who won't embarrass Sister, as her little sister."

With a swelling sense of mission in his chest, Batenkaitos pulled out a "memory" from within himself.
Among the Authorities of "Gluttony" there exists an ability called "Eclipse." Simply put, it splits into two — "Solar Eclipse" and "Lunar Eclipse" — but both are extremely difficult to use well.

A "Lunar Eclipse" is the phenomenon of the moon appearing to wane. —By extension, it means drawing out the "memory" of a devoured opponent, and reproducing it with Batenkaitos's own body.
Ordinarily, the ability of Batenkaitos to browse his many and varied "memories" and combine them into a superlative composite body-technique is the true domain of this "Lunar Eclipse."

A "Solar Eclipse," on the other hand, is the phenomenon of the sun appearing to hide. —By extension, it means overlaying not just the devoured opponent's "memory," but their very existence onto oneself, running it exactly as its original specifications intended.
Naturally, the original wielder's own body handles that technique better, and so becomes stronger.
However, when transforming one's body into someone else's, there's a risk of being too strongly influenced by the other's spirit, which could resonate badly down the line. For that reason, unless there's a very good reason, Batenkaitos and Alphard don't make frequent use of these techniques.

The one Lye Batenkaitos and Roy Alphard mainly use is the "Lunar Eclipse."
The one Rui Arneb mainly uses is the "Solar Eclipse" — an ultimate trump card that Rui, who possesses no body of her own and no firm sense of self, can use lightly.

But having been beaten half to death in the fight with Ram, and reproducing the "Leaper" Dorkell through "Solar Eclipse" in order to survive, Batenkaitos broke through his own shell.
He mastered the "Solar Eclipse" he had until now avoided out of fear of losing himself, and found a way to maintain a firm sense of self.
Now he could savor the main course of another's "life" all the more perfectly, without waste.

"To grow in the middle of a fight — that's something that should never happen to an old fossil like me. Hahh! Now this is a masterpiece! Isn't it, Sister!"

Thanks to having established a strong sense of self, he felt remarkably refreshed.
He wanted this awakened self of his to be properly seen by his splendid sister. For that, he had to choose methods that would stir her hatred even more.

The scent of anger, the taste of anger, the texture of anger, a full course of anger.
What kind of "Gourmet" would he be if he didn't savor with all his strength everything he could experience from his beloved person.
For that, he turned his attention to the "self" riding on the back of the Earth Dragon before him—

"Come to think of it, I've never actually done this before. Killing myself with my own hands. Might be a whole new discovery in values, don't you think?"

"—!!"

The moment he winked, the world switched with a short-range spatial leap.
The Earth Dragon, exploding with a groan deep in its throat, was startled by Batenkaitos's sudden appearance right beside it, where he had been behind them a moment ago, and tried to run past him without stopping.
Admirable, truly admirable. But admirable qualities only serve as spice for the tragedy to come.

"—Palm of the Fist King."

A swung fist buried itself gloriously in the Earth Dragon's flank.
Normally that would be a blow launched from Batenkaitos's own diminutive frame, but this was not so for the Batenkaitos who had awakened by staring down the brink of death, broken his shell, and gained new power.
The body that was truly Neiji Lockhart's drove through the dragon's torso with the force of the blow.

The Fist King's iron fist, which in countless death-matches on the Isle of Swordfighting Slaves had felled even armored giants in a single strike.
The dragon that took that blow let out a fatal cry and was slammed hard into the corridor wall. But even as it collapsed, it kept sheltering the girl on its back — from the fist, from the wall, from the floor, and even from its own massive body.
It caught the falling girl's body gently with its outstretched tail and lowered her to the corridor floor.
It should have been female, but its handling was smart enough that any gentleman present should have taken notes. Batenkaitos couldn't help but applaud.

"But — as for you! Even after being kindly laid down to rest, I'm afraid I'll have to politely chop your head clean off, to bring as a gift for Sister."

"—!!"

"There, there, don't struggle, don't struggle, gold star for effort."

Kicking up at the jaw of the fallen dragon as it tried to bite at him.
Its earnestness was almost enough to bring tears, but unfortunately Batenkaitos and the Earth Dragon were enemies. They could praise each other's efforts, but they couldn't look up at the same sky of victory.
If one wins, the other loses. Sad to say, but that's reality.

"You've! Got to! Understand that! Properly! Okay?"

Punctuating each word, each phrase, each syllable, he drove the blows carefully into the black dragon.
Its cheekbone and foreleg shattered, the huddled dragon should now have learned its lesson, along with the pain. Fortunately, Batenkaitos didn't devour Earth Dragons' "memories."
So he had no reason to take its life. He wanted to remember today, together with it. And as for the rest—

"For Sister's sake, Sister's precious Rem, with Rem's own hands…"

"—Don't say such creepy things."

The instant that clear, cutting voice sounded, the face leaning over Rem was violently knocked back.
The face he'd raised at the sound was struck directly by the soles of two shoes closing in head-on. Sent flying hard backward, Batenkaitos slid across the floor on his back.
And then—

"Ahahahahahaha…finally, you've caught up, Sister. We, um— eh? Us? I…we…? —Rem, we've been waiting."

Slowly, from a sprawled position, he rose using only the strength of his legs.
And when he faced his beloved other half head-on, Ram wore an expression she had never seen before.

"…In such a short time, you've become quite hideous."

"…In such a short time, you've become quite hideous."

That was Ram's honest impression upon finally catching up to this thoroughly tasteless game of tag.
Still holding Batenkaitos's sight hostage, Ram dragged her incompletely unbound body, trusting in Patrasche's valiant effort, and raced to the scene.
On the fourth floor, in a corridor not far from the spiral staircase, she found the Earth Dragon, beaten to a pulp, and beside it, Rem laid down.
And leaning over that Rem was—

"How rude, calling me hideous, Sister… We're this, this, this much—! We care so much about Sister and—and-nd-nd-nd-nd"

His words unsteady, his tongue tangled, his thoughts scattered every which way.
That the cause of this was some derangement of the mind, Ram — no, anyone at all — would have grasped at a glance.
Because Batenkaitos's form was, by that point, distorted almost beyond recognition.

"————"

Reproducing a drawn-out "memory" in his own flesh, tracing an opponent's form and figure exactly — that had been Batenkaitos's hidden trump card, shown just recently.
But it seemed that this too was a forbidden move, even for Batenkaitos, because the "Gluttony" standing before Ram's eyes was every one of the "memories" he'd drawn out, and yet none of them at all.

It was all warped together, jumbled into a mess.
There was a part belonging to the bald old man who leapt through space, a part belonging to the obese giant who had withstood Ram's wind-blades, a part belonging to the martial artist whose fighting skill had reached the Sanctum, and beyond that, various other human physical characteristics, all composing this grotesque body.

The length and size of his right hand differed from his left, and if you looked closely, even his face seemed to reference someone different part by part. The only place Batenkaitos's original features still remained, if anything, was perhaps the look in his eyes — though even that might have become borrowed by now.

And, apparently, Batenkaitos himself was entirely unaware of this distorted state.

"—?"

Batenkaitos was on the verge of becoming something that was no longer anyone at all.
Perhaps a being that had specialized so thoroughly in stealing others' "memories" had, to begin with, a thin foundation of self. Because of that, he had broken.
And what had been born in its place was—

"—A monster playing at being Rem. Honestly, I don't think I've ever been this angry before."

Looking down at the face of the sister who filled the missing piece in her heart, Ram felt revulsion at Batenkaitos, who had incorporated even that resemblance into part of his own face.
To think he could grate on someone's nerves this precisely, this thoroughly. It might be a close contest between him and Subaru for who's better at irritating people.

"Well done, Patrasche. Take Rem, and fall back."

"~~!!"

Its response weak, Patrasche, spitting blood, sheltered behind Ram.
Dragging its massive body along, Patrasche gripped Rem's collar in its teeth and pulled back. It meant to draw her far from the battlefield entirely, but—

"That won't do, Sister. That's the precious hors d'oeuvre… before savoring the main course, a side dish is essential, isn't—"

"—Die."

Stepping forward a foot the size of a man's head, Ram raised her palm toward the face about to spout more nonsense.
The whirling blade of wind gathering there was a small-scale storm carrying enough force to shred everything above the opponent's neck into ribbons. —Having come this far, Ram had stopped holding back.

Trying to incapacitate without killing, to search for a way to restore the "memories" — the result had been to put Rem's safety at risk, and, as a fact, to bring Patrasche to grievous injury.
Ram took that seriously. —She would owe Subaru a deep apology. And so that she would never repeat this mistake again, she carried her intent to kill through to the end.

Considering that last deranged utterance, the chances of getting a reasonable answer out of Batenkaitos were slim. All things considered, this choice was correct.

Defeat Batenkaitos, take back the burden on her body from Subaru.
After that, she should return Patrasche and Rem to the Green Room, and hurry off to reinforce whoever else needed her help.
That was as far as her thoughts had gotten.

"————"

Just before the storm could tear his face apart, Ram's eyes widened at a change taking shape.
It was nothing much. Just another shift in Batenkaitos's appearance. And yet, of all people, it was a change that Ram herself could not simply overlook.

—On the forehead of that face composed of features gathered from multiple people, a single white horn had appeared.

"That's…"

If he was truly, honestly, moving entirely on instinct, she had to take her hat off to him.
Every single thing Batenkaitos did seemed calculated purely to grate on her nerves.

"—Sister."

For an instant, in a face truly the spitting image of Rem's, in a voice that could have been Rem's own, she was called.
And into Ram's frozen face, Batenkaitos's massive arm drove a single blow.

Not fatal.

"Please cry, Sister."

"Please get angry, Sister."

"Please smile, Sister."

The affection packed into that voice clawed at Ram's heart with every word.
Rem, who in Ram's awareness had gone on sleeping this entire time. A little sister erased of her "memories," who should always have been at her side through life and yet was nowhere to be found — stolen from her.
Someday, when she woke, Ram had looked forward to being called by her for the very first time.

Whether Ram would have regained her sister's memories by that time, she didn't know.
Whether they had returned or not, that very first word, at that moment, was surely meant to be something as special as a newborn's first cry.

A small, strained breath escaped her.

Down the corridor she'd been sent tumbling into, Ram's sight found Patrasche and Rem.
The distance was, at most, a dozen or so meters — the time Ram had bought had only felt long, stretched out endlessly by the agony she'd endured; in truth it had been merely a dozen or so seconds.

Asking after her safety with a blatant lack of sincerity, Batenkaitos, rather than climbing over the wall he'd smashed, tried to circle around through the corridor toward her instead.

Leaning her body against the wall, she exchanged a glance with the battered Patrasche.

In truth, she had no idea what Patrasche had actually said.
But that her answer hadn't missed the mark by much was proven by the fact that the black Earth Dragon offered no correction.

"————"

Ram had to admit her judgment had backfired.
Not killing Batenkaitos immediately — that had been the trigger for everything that followed. Trying to correct that mistake, she'd moved this time intending to take his life instantly, but Batenkaitos, having survived a near-death state, had cultivated an eye for finding the optimal answer.
As a result, her sleeping sister's living likeness had been stolen from her, and the shaken Ram had been struck down.

She had exhausted every card in her hand, and every card she'd drawn had betrayed her without exception.
Ram was self-aware that she excelled in every regard — but there was one thing, one single thing, that she knew, hopelessly, she did not possess.

—Luck in the moment.

That had not changed since the day her horn was broken, since the day her standing as an oni was ruined.
Though, truthfully, she'd never had much attachment to that "standing" anyway. The white horn growing from her forehead was the thing she herself, more than anyone else in this world, found most tiresome.
And yet, how convenient it would be now, she found herself thinking, if only she still had the horn she'd lost.

—No, that wasn't quite accurate. To be precise, the horn itself still existed.
The horn itself was, even now, in Ram's possession. The staff she always carried, never letting it leave her side — its base was made from Ram's own broken horn.
A horn is a vital organ for efficiently gathering the mana that an oni's formidable body requires.
Because of that, as a catalyst for casting Magic, nothing suited Ram better.
That was why Roswaal had gone to the trouble of retrieving the horn and having this staff specially made.

That horn — inside the staff, or on her forehead, the only difference being location, and yet it made this much of a difference—

"————"

A thought crossed Ram's mind as she happened to think of the horn.
It arose from the fact of the horn's presence or absence, from the fact that Ram had once overwhelmed Batenkaitos, and from mustering every scrap of knowledge she had to break through this situation and keep their chances alive.

Ram, with her broken horn, and Rem, who kept on sleeping.
The latter was a matter of outcome, but the former — why had Roswaal retrieved Ram in the first place?

Ram knew what role Roswaal wanted from her.
She also knew the plan Roswaal was trying to carry out in the process.
She had been told that, when the time came, she would need to be present for that plan, and that she would naturally come to understand the method as well.

So Ram had intended not to pry until that necessary time arrived.
But in this moment, a thought surfaced — one born of necessity, for the sake of Ram herself, of the still-sleeping Rem, and of Patrasche, who had fought so hard for the sisters' sake, to survive.

It was an absurdly far-fetched possibility.
But at the same time, she could hold her head high and call it a reasonable inference.

If it was Roswaal L. Mathers, the man Ram loved—

"He may well be prepared to be called inhuman for doing this."

Murmuring that, Ram drew the staff fitted at her thigh with a trembling hand.
Staring hard at the staff she'd used for ten years, she then swung it with full force against the wall.
From within the shattered staff, that thing she had not seen in so long came flying out.

Slowly brushing away the white dust filling the corridor with a hand, Batenkaitos stepped forward.
His gait graceful, his steps demure, not making more noise than necessary was a maid's proper bearing, the bare minimum consideration so as not to shame her master.

Beyond the smoke, his beloved sister should be lying fallen.
He wanted to see her many expressions. Her pale rose eyes, alight with fierce emotion, burning brightly — he wanted her to look straight back at him with them.

It was a desire so naive it could almost be mistaken for romantic love.

"Oh my."

Beyond the smoke, the first thing he saw was the black Earth Dragon, dragging its leg.
Amid his conversation and contact with his sister, the dragon had somehow slipped from view. It wasn't particularly a subject of interest to him, but he did have business with the being it had carried off.

As he moved to close the distance to the "Sleeping Beauty," that was when Batenkaitos noticed.

For an instant he wondered, puzzled, what the source of that light could be.
