Arc 2 Chapter 40: "Fangs of the Witchbeast"

Heart racing, but footsteps careful.
Subaru pressed on through the depths of the forest, his consciousness hazy, still doggedly following the words Lucas had wrung out and given him, pressing on through the dark night.

His mouth was dry, his throat tight with tension. Holding his breath, killing the sound of his footsteps, he advanced through the forest of Everlasting Darkness, advanced and advanced. There was a faint hesitation there, but no trace of the faintheartedness that would fear stepping forward.

There was the matter of the big talk he'd given Rem, but that wasn't why Subaru believed he had no chance of winning. To begin with, Subaru didn't think highly enough of himself to fearlessly leap into a situation with zero chance of victory.
Just as he'd fallen from grace for no reason back in his old world, the rating he gave himself in this world was rock bottom too — below him, probably, was only room for total nonsense.
It was precisely because he judged himself that way that he wouldn't commit the folly of charging into a hopeless situation. Even in a moment he absolutely couldn't retreat from, he was the small, cowardly sort who'd go looking for excuses.
And it was precisely *because* he was aware of that about himself that he could claw and struggle after a thin thread he might otherwise have overlooked, grasping at any answer within reach.

"Think back — the current situation. If the witchbeast really is that mutt, plain and simple, then..."

He tended to fixate on the ominous look of the word and the curse arts that actually took lives, but from a broader point of view, some chance of victory came into focus.
No matter how lethal a trap it laid, the being who'd set it was, in the end, that frail little pup. If the target really was that thing — small enough to fit snugly in a child's hands, its cuteness apparently equal to its combat power —

"I mean, I ought to be able to beat that, right...?"

A pathetic way to seek out a chance of victory, pinning his hopes on the opponent's smallness.
Still, he didn't think it was off the mark. Subaru's body had already had the spell undone by Beatrice's hand. So if that presence tried to lay its cursed hand on him a second time — he ought to be able to land the first blow.

That thin thread of possible advantage, should it come to a fight, was the driving force sustaining Subaru's steps right now. Of course, he was well aware this was the case only if every condition lined up in his favor to the maximum degree.
Naturally, he could just as easily imagine the worst-case scenario running the opposite way—

"Stop, stop, cut it out. If you go picturing the worst-case pattern, this world's god will gladly duck right under that line and bring you something even worse — that's how it works around here."

Judging from his track record so far, he tried to set up a defense line before getting hit with a brutal comeuppance. Even knowing full well that this world's idea of showing affection was to mercilessly rip that defense line away and gouge at the wound hidden underneath.

"—!"

A sudden sense of wrongness caught in his throat, and his feet stopped.
The feeling of the air itself changing pressed vividly against his skin, and he could tell the temperature of the sweat beading damply on his forehead from exhaustion was plummeting rapidly.

What pricked at his skin like needles was the aftershock of pressure unconsciously radiating from a dense, overwhelming presence. Just by *being there*, it was enough to make his instincts recoil in fear.
As the warmth of the air shifted and the flow of the wind grew turbulent, it carried a foul smell with it. Where until a moment ago only the thick scent of grass and earth had hung over the forest, now a rank, fleshy odor drifted in, sharp enough to make him grimace involuntarily.

Unlike Rem's preternatural sense of smell, even Subaru's ordinary human nose could pick it out clearly. It was the particular rank smell of a living creature — to put it plainly, the stink of a beast.

Unable to shake the bad feeling welling up in him, Subaru held his breath and crept forward.
He carefully peered through the gap between the trees, looking toward the source of the smell drifting toward him — and learned that the thin thread he'd been clinging to had just been snapped clean through.

—It wore a coat of jet-black fur that melted into the deep darkness of the forest.
Its build might rival that of a large dog. Long limbs planted firmly in the earth, supporting a body whose weight looked no less than Subaru's own.
Its developed claws and the fangs too large to fit inside its mouth were, unmistakably, an accumulation of violence evolved purely to pierce and tear flesh.

In the darkness where branches and leaves blocked out the light, only its gleaming, glowing red eyes were impossible to miss. They swept the surroundings restlessly, as if it were waiting, any moment now, for the chance to sink those fangs into its prey.

Seeing that shape floating in the darkness, Subaru understood clearly.
—That grotesque form, in which nothing but pure, undiluted menace had been distilled — that was a witchbeast.

"And on top of that..."

Studying its shape closely, Subaru touched his chin and dug through his memory.
The witchbeast before his eyes — he had seen it before.
It closely resembled the horned one he'd fought to the death with at the end of the previous loop. At first glance, he couldn't confirm a horn on its forehead. But if it were, hypothetically, the same creature—

"So I actually got my revenge last time without even meaning to..."

Not that it was any kind of comfort, but there was some small satisfaction in the thought that he'd landed a blow in return. Though, just as everything from the previous loop existed only within Subaru now, that fact couldn't possibly bring about any change in the situation before him.
If there was anything to gain from it,

"At least I know those claws and fangs aren't for show..."

He hadn't exactly been hoping for the claws to turn out fake and the fangs some kind of fashion statement, but knowing the threat was simply real was, at best, only enough to make him more discouraged.
And now that he'd confirmed the sight of the large witchbeast, Subaru's resolve rapidly began to deflate as well. What little chance of victory he'd had was now completely cut off.
By the self-diagnosis he'd just run through, not being reckless was exactly the policy Subaru's actions should follow.

"Anyway, slowly..."

He was about to decide to withdraw from this spot and wait to rejoin Rem, when—

"—"

Without letting his attention stray from the witchbeast's presence, glaring at that grotesque shape, he stepped backward. And at the edge of his slightly widened field of vision, something caught his attention.
In the depths of the forest where the witchbeast stood, there was a place where fallen trees lay stacked atop each other. Probably, in the gaps between those felled trees, was something like the witchbeast's den.

—Beside those fallen trees, a single girl had been discarded.
Her clothes were in tatters. Her brown pigtails had come undone. Blood seeped here and there across her white skin, and lying face-down as she was, there was no telling from a distance how severe her injuries were.
But even so, that child—

"—uh"

A small, faint voice.
It traveled through the air, passed through the forest, and struck Subaru's eardrum with startling force.

It was a distance the sound shouldn't have reached. Or perhaps it was only an auditory illusion, a trick of the mind that made him think he'd heard it.
Looking closely, the fallen girl wasn't moving even slightly, and for a miracle to only grant the wish of letting a groaning breath from a girl like that reach him — that would be a pretty pathetic miracle.

The only one here was Subaru. Only Natsuki Subaru.
If a miracle were to happen, if the heavens wished to save the girl, then the one standing here ought to be someone more reliable — a strong, valiant hero.

Someone like Roswaal. Someone like Beatrice. Someone like Reinhard.
They — he or she — would surely have broken through a situation like this with ease. They'd never even consider quailing like Subaru, legs trembling, frozen in place.
Effortlessly beating back the threat of the witchbeast, saving the girl from despair, guiding the story to a flawless happy ending. Amazing. Cool. Elegant.

But the one here was Natsuki Subaru.

No strength. No magic. No wisdom, no cleverness sharp enough for quick thinking. If there was a miracle to be clung to, he'd be the one shoving through the crowd to snatch that right for himself before anyone else could. That was the kind of hopeless man he was.
Subaru was the one who *wanted* a miracle. There was no way he could *become* one.

Subaru clenched his back teeth hard to keep them from chattering.
He held his breath, touching his chest as if to check his heartbeat. It was beating. It was definitely beating, but he couldn't tell if it was fast or slow.
The passage of time had grown horribly vague. His vision seemed to warp and bend; he wiped the sweat trickling down his forehead, rubbed his eyelids, and tried to hold to his original resolve — to remain purely a bystander.
And yet—

"Hey, stop..."

A weak murmur. In the direction of his gaze, the witchbeast turned its head in a slow arc, looking toward the fallen girl. Then it scraped the ground with its sharp claws and began moving toward her with a leisurely gait. One step at a time, one step at a time, as if grinding away at Subaru's heart.

He couldn't read the witchbeast's intent. It must have been the one who'd dragged her here in the first place. It could have killed her at any time. And yet it had kept her alive until now. And now that the child it had kept alive had moved, it was trying to stop that. It made no sense. It was unreasonable. Just a few more steps and it would reach her. Wait, this is wrong, it doesn't make sense. What should he do. No chance of winning, no chance at all, nowhere in sight. No thread left to cling to either, nothing, just—

—Emilia surely wouldn't hesitate.

"...Hey, hey. Hey hey hey hey hey hey, what a stupid thing to be thinking, me."

He took a deep breath, steadied his breathing, dug his nails into his trembling knees, and bit down on his lip. He bit it hard enough to draw blood, using the pain to jolt his mind awake.

Natsuki Subaru couldn't perform miracles. All he could do was, in the most graceless way possible, claw and flail and whine "no, no" at the approaching despair, making a nuisance of himself about it.
Subaru didn't overestimate himself. And so he could see the limits of what he was capable of.
The limit was inflicting the tiniest of scratches on fate. And if he could see that limit, that ceiling — then he ought to at least meet it.

He pulled off his jacket, wrapping the tattered butler's uniform tightly around his left arm. He cinched it tight so it wouldn't come loose, wiped away the blood, and faced forward.

"OOOOOOOOH!!"

He roared, kicked aside the underbrush, and threw himself at the spot where the witchbeast crouched, braced.
Startled by the sudden shout, the witchbeast flinched violently and leapt back, lowering its body and glaring at Subaru.
Subaru, too, egged himself on with his own shouting, stomping the ground and snarling to intimidate it.

"AAAH! Over here! C'mon, look at me! What's wrong, scared?!"

Spit flying, he bellowed. His heart sent stabbing pain through his entire body, like it might burst.
Facing it head-on like this, the witchbeast's frame felt larger than he'd imagined. Partly because he'd only seen it from a distance before, but largely because he was being overwhelmed by its sheer hostility.

What he'd learned from his fight with the witchbeast last time was that, however grandly it was called a "witchbeast," its manner of fighting never strayed outside the bounds of the beasts Subaru already knew well.
Of course, back in his old world, Subaru had never had occasion to cross blades with a four-legged beast, but he'd seen humans fighting beasts countless times in manga and anime.
Among those, the most effective tactic against an opponent whose main weapon was biting was, just as Subaru was doing now, wrapping the arm in thick cloth.
Given the poverty of his equipment, it was the only countermeasure Subaru could prepare.

Shouting, yelling, intimidating, he thrust his left arm forward to keep it in check.
Best case, the witchbeast was a coward and would flee, cowed by Subaru's ferocity. But as had been true last time, these things never let go of prey they'd once marked.

Contrary to Subaru's furious shouting shattering the forest's stillness, the witchbeast, crouched low, didn't so much as growl. It simply, silently coiled its limbs, glaring at Subaru as if gauging the moment to unleash them.
Which of them was really the prey — even its bearing alone seemed to make that plain, and Subaru's voltage rose further still, and riding the momentum he took another step forward,

"What's with the attitude?! C'mon!! Come at me! C..."

It vanished.
In an instant, the witchbeast that had been right in front of him melted away into the darkness.

His throat froze in shock, and in the twitch and tremor of his blinking eyelids, Subaru realized his mistake: the instant of his blink had been stolen from him. The witchbeast had been waiting for even that split second, that moment Subaru lost track of himself.
That the wild thing had performed such a high-level tactical gambit — waiting for its opponent's one unguarded instant — sent a chill through Subaru. Even as he did, he swept his gaze around, chasing the vanished witchbeast's whereabouts—

—the instant the image flashed through his mind, Subaru had already sprung, yanking his right foot back where he stood.

"—!"

"Ha! Serves you right!!"

The witchbeast's fangs snapped shut with a shrill crack in the empty space where his right foot had just been. Having dodged a strike that, even a moment slower, would have gouged clean to the nerve, Subaru swung a kick down with all his might into the witchbeast's now wide-open flank.

A front kick thrown from an off-balance stance was far from his full strength. But it was enough to send the witchbeast tumbling with a yelp like a wounded puppy.

Perhaps stung with shame at that weak cry, the witchbeast righted itself and glared at Subaru, finally baring its fangs and letting out a savage snarl.
Its red eyes burned with fury, and inside its drool-flecked jaws, its fangs clacked together again and again.

—That last strike had been luck. Last time, the beast had chosen to attack the leg first, and this time he'd simply gambled that it would do the same, and the gamble paid off — nothing more.

"C'mon, come at me, come on! You're pretty pissed off too, huh — well I'm way more pissed than you are, you hear me?!"

—It's coming!

A certainty with no basis whatsoever — but in the same instant, the witchbeast's shape vanished.
By the time it reappeared, it was already right in front of him, circling around to his left, and its long fangs sank straight into the arm he'd thrust forward.

The sharp fangs tore through the thick cloth and sank deep into the flesh beneath — he could feel it.

"Guh—!!"

A searing pain that nearly reduced him to tears; a sharp agony struck directly at his brain.
But—

"—not gonna—give it to you!!"

He tensed the muscles of his left arm with all his strength. Naturally, the embedded fangs became harder to pull free, and combined with the wrapped jacket, the witchbeast's movement was brought to a complete standstill.
His gaze locked with those red eyes, and even as he nearly drowned in that overwhelming hostility—

"You bit me, you — you brainless idiot—!!"

Cradling the witchbeast's body along with his left arm, Subaru spun with all his strength. Centrifugal force lifted the beast's body off the ground, and the momentum of the spin slammed its back into a large tree behind him.

He thought he heard a yelp of pain, but Subaru didn't stop moving. Refusing to let the opening slip — with those fangs, that weapon, now pinned — he swung his body around and around, slamming the prey in his arms against the tree, against the ground, again and again and again, single-mindedly.
And finally—

"That's the end of you!!"

He brought it down from directly overhead onto a fallen tree, aiming to crush the witchbeast's skull.
A tremendous impact traveled back up his arm, and the body that had been thrashing to break free suddenly went slack, all strength draining out of it.

At that abrupt, anticlimactic response, Subaru let out a ragged breath and sank to his knees on the spot.
Looking down, he saw the witchbeast still latched onto his left arm, its eyes now devoid of life. Blood poured freely from its crushed, mangled head, and a tree branch, driven in during one of the earlier slams, jutted from its back.

Slowly, he pried its jaws open and pulled the fangs free. Its tongue lolled loosely out. Confirming that all signs of life had stopped, Subaru finally caught the breath that had been on the verge of stopping in his own chest.

"I... won, huh."

He murmured that, dazed, and pulled the jacket, soaked with blood and drool, off his left arm. The skin beneath was a horrific sight — the embedded fangs had gouged deep into the flesh, and beneath it, the pale white of bone was faintly visible.
The pain he'd forgotten in the heat of the fight came rushing back the moment he laid eyes on the wound. Grimacing hugely at that burn-like pain, he muttered,

"Anyway, ow, ow. Damn it, that girl..."

He crawled, at a maddeningly slow pace, deeper toward the girl beside the fallen trees.
Even though it had been a fight for his life, he felt not a shred of accomplishment or satisfaction at having won it. All he had was a fatigue that made his whole body suddenly leaden, and a revulsion urging him to hurry up and erase from his mind the exchange of killing intent that had just taken place.
He had no need right now for exhilaration or the afterglow of victory. All that mattered, far more urgently, was the anxious need to confirm whether what he'd fought for at the end of this struggle had actually been saved.

"—Glad it... wasn't for nothing."

Still cradling his left arm, he threw his back against the fallen tree and let out a deep breath of relief.
Because he'd confirmed it — that the girl right beside him, faintly stained with dirt and blood, still carried the breath of life in her. If anything, her color looked better than the other children's had earlier, and aside from a few minor injuries, the damage to her was extremely slight. Subaru himself, with the bone showing through his left arm, was in far worse shape.

"Making me worry like that... well, whatever, it's fine. If a girl's body ends up with a scar on it, I guess I'll just have to take responsibility and marry her, huh. Though Emilia-tan seems pretty strict about the whole multiple-wives thing."

As relief poured over him all at once, what came back was a joke to steady his nerves.
He'd told Rem he was just buying time, but as it turned out, he'd gone and eliminated the threat itself, splendidly. He figured he was allowed a little pride for that, without being punished for it.

"'Buying time's fine, but I guess it's also fine if you just go and beat the thing,' or something like that."

Pretty impressive, honestly — he'd gone and fulfilled, in someone else's stead, a wish that had never been granted in the original.
To distract himself from the pain of his wound, his rambling thoughts kept circling on and on. He wanted to somehow carry the girl to the village, but as things stood, he had no strength left to do it.
Best to sit tight and wait for Rem to catch up. This time, there was no time limit. Worst case, even if he passed out here, things would probably work themselves out.

"Not that I can go around openly dozing off, but..."

He retied the loosened jacket, giving it the role of a tourniquet, and let out a breath.
And then, Subaru's ears caught, faintly, the sound of the underbrush rustling somewhere in the distance.

He raised his head. He thought he'd misheard the sound of the wind, but a moment later, the same sound came again, from directly ahead — from the direction Subaru had come running from.
The direction Subaru had come from was, in turn, the same direction he'd parted ways with Rem. Which meant — was that the rustling of that sharp blue-haired girl making her way toward him?

"Took you long enough... too bad, there's nobody left for you to fight here."

He readied a line he'd always wanted to try saying once, bracing himself for the girl he was waiting to meet to come bursting through.
The undergrowth rustled, and he sensed a presence stop just beyond it. Feeling a gaze studying him from the other side, Subaru gave a wry smile.

He raised his right hand, about to tell her there was no longer any enemy to be wary of, when—
—his whole body caught, once again, the thick, choking stench of beast wafting toward him.

The bushes rustled, and from the other side, a low, four-legged shadow stepped out.
And then—

"Hey, hey, you've gotta be kidding me..."

Red eyes, cutting brilliantly through the night forest — countless points of that light, more than he could bother counting, peered out from beyond the cluster of trees ahead of him.
So many he didn't even want to count them — probably more than all his fingers and toes combined could tally.

Still clutching his left arm, he pushed himself to his feet, swept his gaze across the points of light floating in the darkness, and let out a deep breath.
Almost absently, Subaru touched his right hand to his own cheek and noticed it had twisted out of shape. The corners of his mouth were pulled taut, forming the shape of a smile.
Ah. I see. Right. This — this actually makes me laugh, doesn't it.

"—I'm dead. This is it."

Overwhelming killing intent burst forth in that single line, and the silent forest was swallowed in a frenzy.
All Subaru could do was shield the girl, still unconscious, behind him, spread both arms wide, and stand his ground — if only to show one last shred of his own stubborn pride.



—A scream trailed high, high into the sky above the forest.
