Arc 2 Chapter 36: "A Moment in the Village, Moved Up"

For Subaru, this was the third time all told that he'd visited this village.

The first time he'd gone with Ram, the second time he'd made rounds with Rem — this hamlet, small in scale for one sitting so close to the manor, was home to at most three hundred people or so. Far fewer than the elementary school back in his own world.

Knowing full well how tiny it was, Subaru still found even that modest size to be more than he could get his arms around right now.
Three hundred people, easy enough to say, but checking every single one of them was impossible. And even by the most optimistic reckoning, he had only today and tomorrow.

"Even so, you finished your work awfully fast."

"Barusu was creepily sharp today. Wonder what got into him."

"Heh, guess the sleeping potential inside me finally bloomed. Come on, Ram-chi, no need to get all shy about it — go ahead and praise me straight up. Just don't fall for me!"

Subaru, thoroughly full of himself after his morning's work earned high marks.
It was the result of throwing everything he had into finishing his mandatory chores, all to make sure the afternoon shopping trip would actually happen. In the past, psyching himself up like that had often backfired, spinning his wheels — but strangely enough, this time the tension had drained out of his shoulders, and he'd been able to tackle it naturally.

Naturally, the memory of Emilia's lap surfaced, and his ears went hot.

*—Guess I got relaxed, huh.*

Even contact with the twins, who'd forced him to put up such a front, wasn't weighing on him mentally the way it usually did.
Some tension remained, sure, but it was working in his favor — bolstering his resolve and keeping him focused.

So, as promised, he strolled the village with a flower on each arm, and his state of mind stayed calm enough to keep the shakes at bay.
That applied both to his feelings toward the two of them, and, at the same time, to the tension of operating in the village.

*—Above all, he had to avoid tipping off the person responsible with some odd behavior.*

From last night's Q&A with Beatrice, he'd learned the condition a Curse Arts User needed to cast their curse — physical contact with the target.
Before cursing someone, the Curse Arts User had to make contact with the target at least once, touching them in some way. Viewed purely as an assassination method, that was a fairly risky condition.

By the standards of his old world's knowledge, it was a lot of exposure to risk for something a long-range sniper shot could accomplish just as well from a safe distance.
Though, one could argue that paying that price was precisely what guaranteed the curse's absolute success once triggered.

In any case—

"The suspect pool doesn't change — limited to villagers I ran into both times I visited before."

And if that person had arrived in the village sometime within the last few days, that was practically a lock. Narrowing it down would be easy.
Gazing at the cozy little village scenery, Subaru spun his memory into overdrive, digging through the past. But no matter how hard he tried, he couldn't account for every single person he'd met in the village.
His attention, which up to now had been fixed inside the mansion, had suddenly swung outward. Since that was a line he'd all but written off, digging up old memories of it was quite a chore.

"Well, what I can remember offhand is... 'Rejuvenation Hag' and the young men's group. The 'Ram-Rem Honor Guard' and the 'Village Elder-ish Guy,' plus the kids, I guess."

Those were the faces that stood out most in the village.

The "Rejuvenation Hag" was an old woman with an admirably shameless streak of sexual harassment, who'd groped Subaru's backside with a lecherous grin — a memory still fresh in his mind. Word was, whenever she spotted a young man, she'd grope him and mutter something about "getting younger, getting younger."

The young men's group was exactly what it sounded like — a band formed by the village's young men. Their leader was a crew-cut, loud-voiced jock type, and his habit of slapping Subaru's shoulders hard had been brutal for the strictly indoor Subaru.

The "Ram-Rem Honor Guard" was just a name of convenience — they'd never actually called themselves that. At a glance, its membership closely resembled the young men's group, and it too was led by the crew-cut ringleader. They apparently didn't care for any man getting friendly with Ram and Rem, and they'd slapped Subaru's shoulders hard enough that he'd scowled at the harassment.

And the "Village Elder-ish Guy" was a short old man with a bent back. His head was bald on top, with distinctive white hair sprouting from either side like horns instead. His splendid beard was pure white too, and combined with his sharp eyes, he looked, through and through, like a "capable village elder." He wasn't actually the village elder — he just had the bearing of one. Hence the lukewarm nickname "Village Elder-ish Guy." He tended to grumble things like "kids these days!" while jabbing at Subaru relentlessly, which made him a pain to deal with.

"Thinking it over like this... man, everyone's suspicious as hell. Every single one of them's had contact with me, casual as you please. So it's these four... well, really three, options."

Since options two and three collapsed into one, it naturally settled down to three. Still, that was as far as he could reason it out. To go any further, well—

"Guess I've got no choice but to walk straight into the same spot again this time too."

That was the only conclusion he could reach.
Subaru sighed at himself for never finding any approach besides the brute-force kind. And in response to his gloomy sigh,

"Wha's wroooong, Subaru-!" "Tummy hurt-?" "Tummy hungry-?"

The voices came from above him, one right after another.
Subaru craned his neck around and looked behind him — at his own back, where a small figure clung.

A boy with short brown hair. He couldn't have been ten years old yet, probably a young grade-schooler. He grinned goofily at Subaru's glance. And besides him, more small shadows clung to Subaru's legs and waist.

They were all small children, none of them taller than Subaru's hip. Just counting the ones clinging directly to him made four, and glancing around, there had to be close to ten total.

Shifting the weight on his back — not much of a burden for a body he'd been conditioning — Subaru said "well then" by way of preamble, and,

"You brats follow me across time and space to cling to me..."

"Whaddaya mean-?" "Bumped yer head-?" "Tummy trouble-?"

"Quit fixating on stomach pain. What, you want me to have diarrhea that bad?"

At that, the children all burst into giggles at once. Probably not because it was clever, but simply because they'd latched onto the word "diarrhea."
Apparently, even across worlds, potty talk was the surest way to send kids this age into hysterics.

"And I still get mobbed by kids for no reason, huh. Consistent as ever."

With a child perched on his back tugging at his cheeks, Subaru could only slump his shoulders at the cruelty of his own constitution.
The power that counteracted the absolute loner power born of his social awkwardness — "Absolute Loner Power (Except Against Kids)" — was showing its true colors.

"Man, for some reason I've always been kind of popular with little kids and old folks. Honestly, I'd rather just be popular with the one person in this whole world who matters."

He turned around, jostling the kid on his back to soothe him.
Amid the squeals of laughter and cries of "me next, me next!" flying every which way, Subaru led his procession of children around the village.

Right now, Subaru was on independent free time.
Not literally alone — with kids clinging to him from every direction, "unfree" was more like it — but in the sense that he wasn't accompanying Ram or Rem, it counted as free time.
The maid sisters he'd come to the village with had said,

"Sister, sister. Let's split up and gather just the light things."
"Rem, Rem. Let's leave the heavy, hard-to-carry things to Barusu."

—leaving behind remarks bound to weigh on him later — and hurried off to do the shopping.
Probably out of consideration for Subaru, who'd said he wanted to see the village, but honestly, he wished one of the two had stayed behind. That way he might have avoided being mobbed by children, and,

"I wouldn't have had to do my suspect-greeting rounds while being super nervous."

Feeling like he might break into a cold sweat, Subaru voiced his own decision again just to gauge how difficult it really was.
In his search for the Curse Arts User, the method Subaru had chosen was extremely simple.

*—Use himself as bait to lure out the Curse Arts User.*

Not that he was offering himself up with a "here, go ahead and kill me!" What he intended to step into was the stage before that — the stage where the curse's formula got carved into him.
He was aware this was an extremely risky call. It meant that, if the Curse Arts User felt like it, the instant they triggered the curse, what awaited Subaru would be a countdown to death by wasting away.

Leaping onto the cutting board himself, waiting for the artisan's knife to fall — a carp playing that role was Subaru's aim right now.
Because if he didn't get on the cutting board, he'd never see the unseen artisan's face.

"At least, the way I hear it, Bea-ko can undo the formula itself."

Word was that both Beatrice and Puck were capable of dispelling a curse's formula. He wanted to avoid the latter option since it would naturally drag Emilia into it, but if Beatrice proved uncooperative, that fallback might be unavoidable.

"Subaru-, mean face-" "Scary face-" "Weird face-"

"What are you pointing at people's faces for, you little rascals. And I've been hearing it from you specifically for a while now — number three."

Grumbling back at them, Subaru dragged his train of children around the village.
They wouldn't leave him alone even if he tried to shake them, and as navigation for tracking down suspects, they were pretty useful too.
More than anything, an assassin who couldn't afford to cause a scene in the village would never be able to touch Subaru while he was trailing this many children behind him. Bullet shields, in other words — that calculated angle to it.

"I'm getting pretty rotten myself. Even I'm recoiling from my own thoughts."

"Subaru, what's wrong-" "What happened-" "You zone out-?"

"Nah, it's nothing."

Stroking the heads of the children gripping his legs, Subaru laughed self-deprecatingly, and,

"Well, it's for my own happiness. Help me out just a little, yeah?"

Come to think of it, he'd never really liked kids to begin with.
Loud, overly familiar, self-centered.

*—Maybe it's because they remind me of myself that I feel that way.*

"Figured since free time's almost up, I'd come check on you, and..."

Ram ran a hand through her pink hair, sighing in exasperation.
Subaru, taking in her stare, swung his arms in a big circle, and,

"I'm not doing anything weird, am I? Er, sorry, almost done. — Right, arm swings with knee bends and extensions!"

He moved his body loosely and dynamically, working the stiffness out of his knotted muscles. The children mimicked his movements in kind, and surrounding them, a beat behind, was a crowd of elderly folks and the young men's group too.

Finally, a deep breath, and everyone caught their wind together.
Then, as a finishing touch, they all stretched their arms toward the sky, and in unison,

"—Victory!!"

When it was over, cheers went up, and Subaru found himself trading high-fives with the people around him. He wiped his lightly sweating brow, slipped out of the chattering circle, and headed for Ram. In contrast to the refreshed-looking Subaru, Ram turned a coldly unimpressed gaze his way,

"So what exactly was that little performance?"

"Performance, come on, it's not that grand. I was just handling the little brats all at once, and the adults watching got carried away and joined in."

The demand to play with him, engage with him, notice him, had grown too noisy from the kids, so he'd resorted to Radio Calisthenics Part Two to handle them all at once. Come to think of it, this was the first time he'd ever actually gotten to work as a proper radio calisthenics instructor, so he'd taken the opportunity to go public with it.

"Well, I was surprised myself at how much better it went over than expected. Guess that's the secret to why it's stayed popular for so many years — something everyone from little kids to old folks can enjoy!"

"Don't know, don't care."

"Damn, cut right to the bone!"

Subaru reeled back dramatically at Ram's curt reply, and the watching children, mimicking his reaction, reeled back too,

"Ram-chi's so cold-" "Ram-chi's so mean-" "Ram-chi's scary-"

"...Did you teach these children to call me that?"

"Nah, I mean, not on purpose. But hey, being approachable matters, right? If there's too much distance, you can't even see the other person's face clearly. ...And that's kind of lonely, don't you think? I think so, I really do...!"

"What a mouth you've got on you. Ram doesn't much mind, but Rem might not care for that sort of thing."

"Rem-rin?" "Rem-rin" "Rem-rin-rin"

Watching the children tilt their heads while chanting that over and over, Ram shrugged, as if she'd already given up.
Then she turned back to Subaru,

"So, did you get your fill of the village you wanted so badly?"

"—Yeah, on that front, no complaints."

Answering Ram's question, Subaru twisted his cheek into a grin.

The village stroll — a name that was really just cover for making contact with the suspects — had, ironically, gone off without a hitch, exceeding even his own plans.
Partly because they'd been a conspicuous crowd to begin with, but mostly, he figured, because they themselves had been eager to make contact with him too. He hadn't even needed to go looking; they'd shown their faces on their own, so regardless of how it played out, the physical contact had been just as much as, if not more than, last time.

Granted, he'd stayed conscious of avoiding deliberate touch and paid careful attention to keep anyone besides the main named characters from rising to the level of suspect.

"Wish I could've shown off my slippery-eel technique, dodging by every trick in the book... though against a mob of little brats like that, there's not much you can do."

He sighed at the children still clinging to every part of his body. Unlike the adults, who mostly gave up after one dodge, kids' sheer vitality was something he couldn't hope to match.
Still, now that Ram had come to fetch him, it was time to part ways with that youthful energy.
Subaru let his eyes brim with the reluctance of parting, and,

"Well, I've got work, so scram, you lot. Ahh, what a shame. If only I had more time, I'd have played with you even more. Ha, too bad."

"So cheerful-" "While laughing-" "That happy about it-?"

"Shut it! If you want me to keep hanging out with you, think about how you talk to your elders! Getting talked down to by kids on first meeting makes me painfully aware of how low my objective social standing is!"

And then he found himself yelling at a bunch of kids, painfully aware all over again of just how low his own people skills were. A negative spiral.

Waving off the sulking children, Subaru followed Ram, who signaled with her eyes that they should hurry and rejoin Rem. But just before he started walking, someone tugged his sleeve. He turned only his head to find a girl with brown hair in braids, bashfully holding him back.

"Oh?"

A surprised sound slipped out of Subaru involuntarily.
Unlike the crowd that had been aggressively clinging to him until just now, this girl was one who'd been peeking at him from a step back the whole time.
He crouched down, meeting the gaze of the downcast girl,

"What's up? If you've got something to say, I'm listening."

"Um, well... this way."

Tugging his sleeve, the girl pointed in the opposite direction from where they were supposed to meet Rem.
Reluctant to shake off her weak little pull, Subaru glanced at Ram as if asking permission. She let out a small sigh,

"Fine, do as you like a little longer."

"Yep, much obliged, senpai. So, what's up?"

He let the girl lead him by the hand.
With her in front, trailing along in a line came the same group of children as before. They seemed to know exactly where they were headed, every one of them wearing a mischievous grin, whispering to each other in hushed voices.

"He's gonna be so surprised-" "He's totally gonna love it-" "He's gonna wet himself he'll be so happy-"

"Surprised, happy, and wetting myself — what am I, a badly trained puppy? Get my expectations up that high and my contrarian streak makes me want to not be surprised on principle..."

Pushed along by the children's stifled giggling, they slipped between houses into a sunless corner.
And there, Subaru found the thing the children had wanted to show him,

"Ahh, right, right, this happened too, huh."

A sound of realization slipped out involuntarily, and Subaru clapped his hands, nodding over and over.
The girl let go of his hand and dashed off. A little more reserved than the others, she came back to Subaru slightly out of breath, cradling the thing in her arms.

It was a brown-furred creature that looked like a "puppy."
Still clearly newborn, its body couldn't have stretched to even thirty centimeters even at full extension.
With round, innocent eyes and soft-looking fur, it had more than enough charm to set the fluff-lover's blood in Subaru stirring.

His arm started to reach out on its own, ignoring his intentions, but he managed to restrain himself, letting out a long breath.
Surrounded and doted on by the children, it was a pampered pet living the life of a king. But going by past experience—

"Hisss—!"

"Yeah, figured it'd go like this..."

The instant Subaru stepped closer, every hair on its body stood on end in a threat display.
At the sight of its tiny body bristling with all the wariness it could muster, the children too looked surprised,

"It's usually so calm-" "It's only mad at Subaru-" "What'd you do, Subaru-?"

"That's what I'd like to know myself. Three for three now — guess this is just plain incompatibility between me and this thing."

Responding to the children's booing, Subaru observed the small animal, which showed no sign whatsoever of warming up to him.
Barring some particular reason, this would be his third encounter with this particular little creature. Making contact with the exact same individual and getting the exact same reaction every single time — there was almost something rewarding about that, given how his loops had gone. For whatever reason, the loops he experienced had a stubborn habit of never quite repeating the same pattern as before.

"Still, if it's gonna react the same way every time, I kinda wish it'd at least be a friendly reaction."

Three for three, being hated — that genuinely got him down.
Subaru forced a placating smile, trying to win it over. Then, suddenly, the puppy seemed to let its guard down and relaxed. Seeing it curl up in the braided girl's arms, Subaru thought "now's my chance" and stepped forward eagerly.

"Well then, pardon me."

He tilted the fluff-lifting arm he'd leveled up thanks to Puck, putting everything into it toward the curled-up little creature.
First, a probing touch to the head and back, then a check of the belly's texture, working his way from the tips of the paws to their roots — hitting all the connoisseur's favorite spots. The ears and tail, his recent obsession, he saved for the second-half treat.

"Whoa, this is the fluffy little one I've been dreaming of, all right. Not bad fluffication, if I do say so myself. Though a stray's coat does have a bit rougher texture, admittedly. Daily brushing and love, that's what makes the difference there."

"I don't get what Subaru's even sayin'-" "Fluffy little one? Fluffication?" "Grown-up gettin' all mushy over a puppy, kinda creepy-"

"Number three's been merciless nonstop since a while ago, huh!?"

The chestnut-haired pretty boy assigned to number three smiled innocently at Subaru's retort. Cut that moment out on its own and he'd look like an angel — which no doubt was exactly what had gone to his head.

"How many girls is this kid gonna make cry when he grows up... Anyway, that aside, man, fluff-lifting really is the best. The pinnacle of human culture."

Healed of his frazzled nerves by the soft touch, Subaru found himself captivated by the mysterious charm of fluff-lifting.
No wonder cat cafés were all the rage. Sanitation-wise, who in their right mind would think to combine cats and cafés? Whoever came up with that had to be an outstandingly gifted fluff-lover.

"Guess fluffy little ones and fluff-lovers are just fated to be drawn together. Poetic, aren't I. And you're super lovely. Still, there's a few little things bugging me here. Oh hey, there's a coin-sized... no, a yen-coin-sized bald patch on the top of your head. It's gone all whitish — where'd you even bump yours—OW!"

Maybe it was the sting of having its complex pointed out, but the puppy, docile until then, suddenly bared its fangs.
Chomp — small canines sank into his left hand, and when he yanked it back in a panic, a thin trickle of blood ran down the back of it.
Subaru went "aiyah" looking at the wound, and,

"Man, what an event-completion rate. Even the location of the wound's basically the same. Hey, don't tell me you're time-leaping too?"

At his question, asked while lightly wiping away the welling blood, the small animal, its wariness fully restored, gave nothing back but a low growl.
Watching the renewed rift open up between the one boy and the one puppy, the children looking on said,

"Well, you did get cocky-" "Can't just go touching it like that-" "Also this one's a girl, y'know-"

"Feels like the points you're all raising keep drifting off-target... and not one of you is worried about me at all. It's about time a tsundere started warming up to me!"

He had a word or two to say about the children's attitude, but not one of them listened. If anything, it was probably because Subaru had raised his voice.
Startled, the puppy trembled, wriggled free from the girl's arms, and bolted off toward the bushes.

"Aww man-" "Aww man-" "Aww man-"

"Great, now the whole crowd's in on it! My bad! I didn't mean anything by it! I never do!"

Stomping his feet, apologizing profusely, Subaru waved goodbye to the children as they went off to search for the puppy.
Parting ways with them, who to the very end had left their marks all over various parts of his body, he hurried back to where he'd left Ram waiting.

"Sorry, kept you waiting."

Arms crossed, leaning her back against a wall in a big attitude, the maid cracked one eye open toward Subaru as he called out to her,

"About this junior I sent off thinking she'd be right back — she comes back with her hair a mess, clothes all rumpled, and blood dripping from her left hand."

"Yeah, my bad on that! A lot happened, you can probably tell just by looking."

"Right. I saw most of it, so I've got a general idea."

A shadow of concern crossed her refined features as Ram let out a small sigh.
Subaru furrowed his brow at her uncharacteristic manner, but a moment later Ram looked at him and gave a "hmph" through her nose,

"That wound and that state you're in both look pathetic. Let's get to Rem quickly. She should be able to fix both."

"Oh-ho, so Ram-chi's not the healing magic type?"

"I can perform the drastic treatment of slicing off the affected area, if you like."

"That's way too dangerous a folk remedy!?"

Subaru couldn't hide his shudder. Then, suddenly, Ram walked up to him, tugged his sleeve, and started leading the way.
Rare, for her to initiate contact like this — as Subaru blinked in surprise, Ram glanced back with just her head and said,

"Barusu, you coming?"

"I'm coming, with you."

At his answer, he caught the briefest flicker of Ram's lips loosening into something like a smile.
And so, sleeve still in her grip, he started walking.

He thought that if she were always this candid, she'd be adorable — but then again, maybe it was precisely because she was always so candid with herself that those kinds of remarks kept coming out of her.
Candor, too, depends on the time and the circumstance.

Thinking such things, Subaru turned his steps, alongside Ram, toward the place where Rem was waiting.
For some reason, he found himself noticing that the back leading him along seemed to be walking slower than usual, more unhurried.
