Arc 5 Chapter 29: "Gorgeous Tiger"

—Let us go back in time, to more than half a day before Garfiel came running into the shelter.

"So, right? Hetaro fell down there and looked like he was gonna cry, so Mimi held his hand, 'cause there was nothin' else to do. But then Tivey started lookin' all lonely too, so, well, no helpin' it, Mimi held both their hands!"

"...Ahh, that so."

"Yeah, that's so! An' then, an' then! When we got back like that, Ojou looked so super happy!"

Undeterred by the indifferent reply, the small girl walking beside him laughed and laughed.
Orange fur, round, guileless eyes. A girl who behaved as though innocence itself had been drawn in ink — for some reason, one of the members of the rival camp who had taken to clinging to him. A beastfolk named Mimi.

Ever since arriving in the city of Priestella. No — thinking back further, ever since Mimi had come to Roswaal Manor as an envoy over this whole business, she'd seemed strangely, inexplicably attached to him.

At first he'd suspected it was wariness — keeping an eye on him as the Emilia Camp's greatest fighting strength. But watching the girl's attitude and words up to now, that suspicion had all but vanished. Now he honestly figured, for some reason or other, she'd just taken a liking to him.

The "some reason or other" part he couldn't puzzle out, and Garfiel could only tilt his head over it.

—Right now, the two of them, Garfiel and Mimi, were strolling side by side through Priestella before evening fell.
Though "strolling together" wasn't quite accurate — neither had invited the other along. Garfiel had wandered out of the inn on his own, and Mimi had simply tagged along uninvited.
Garfiel would rather have been alone, if he could help it, but he felt reluctant to say so out loud, and Mimi — apparently remarkably poor at picking up on such subtleties — pressed on regardless, leaving him to be swept along on this empty-headed conversation as they wandered the city.

"Garf, you're makin' a weird face. Somethin' happen? Somethin' funny?"

"If it were somethin' funny I'd have a face full-a good cheer, wouldn't I. Don't wanna talk about it. An' 'sides, ain't got no obligation to tell ya anyway."

"If you go around sayin' hard words like 'obligation' and stuff you'll end up like Joshua, y'know? Mimi thinks it's better to just take it easy and have fun, more or less! Garf, you look cooler when you laugh like an idiot."

"The hell you mean, laugh like an idiot, huh!"

At Mimi's words — outrageous, if he had to say so — Garfiel bared his fangs, and the girl went "kyaa!" and dashed off. She ran a little ways ahead, then stopped, waiting for him with a grin as though she'd already forgotten their exchange — and somehow, strangely, watching her made the smallness of his own irritation feel almost laughable.

Claiming he needed to work off his dinner beforehand, Garfiel had challenged the Sword Saint, Reinhard, to a sparring match just an hour or so ago.
Strongest in the Kingdom — or arguably the strongest currently living in the world, this generation's Sword Saint.

Even before meeting him face to face, Garfiel had already heard from Subaru the extent of his skill.
Reinhard — a friend, a benefactor, and someone Subaru had described as having parted with in a somewhat complicated way. Running into him here, in such an unexpected place, had been a real find.
Subaru, too, seemed to have finally worked through whatever awkwardness he'd been carrying, thanks to their long-overdue conversation. And with that unease gone, Garfiel no longer needed to hold back either.

To Garfiel, the very sound of "strongest" carried a purely special meaning.
To be the strongest. To aim for the strongest. To become the strongest.
That, Garfiel firmly believed, was the towering goal that had stood before him from the very moment he drew his first breath, simply by virtue of having been born a man.

Everyone, at some point on the long road of life, wishes for it — that admiration, that fantasy of being "the strongest" — only to eventually forget it. But Garfiel had never once forgotten it.
He had always pictured it, always kept striving toward it. To Garfiel, the title of "strongest" was the summit he was naturally meant to aim for, having been born a man — and it was also an indispensable condition for protecting everything he wanted to protect.

And so, standing before a man so renowned as that very summit, Garfiel had no way of holding back his throbbing fangs and claws.

With Subaru putting in a word for him, he'd gotten Reinhard's consent to spar.
The Sword Saint had proven to be a gentle-looking man of a mildness that seemed impossible for someone standing at the pinnacle of martial arts — a presence so far from the word "strongest" that Garfiel felt he could probably snap him in half with one hand, if he felt like it.
But Garfiel knew that the stronger someone was, the better they tended to be at hiding their strength. Setting aside his own habit of constantly radiating a prickly, on-edge air, in Garfiel's experience the truly strong rarely looked the part in everyday life. Roswaal was like that. So was Subaru.
And he judged that Reinhard belonged in that same category.

—The sparring match ended up taking place in the gravel-laid courtyard of the inn.

Worried about damage to the surroundings, Garfiel had proposed they take it outside the city, but Reinhard flatly rejected the idea and insisted on fighting within the inn grounds instead — with the added condition that they "not trample the garden."

Nothing short of an insult. Whatever the pinnacle of martial arts he might be, that was underestimating him more than a little too much. He'd make the man regret looking down on him immediately, and drag him out into the open.

Facing off in the courtyard, until Subaru gave the signal to begin, Garfiel had kept his fangs bared, focused on nothing but landing a blow from his powerful arm across the red hero's cheek.

"――――"

That thought vanished the instant the signal was given.

The figure of the man standing before him changed entirely in a moment shorter than a blink.
The air of the mild-mannered man who had been standing there until then vanished into the distance, and what stood there instead was either flame, or a single finely honed blade.

A naturalness so complete that an ordinary person wouldn't even notice the blade's presence at all.
A crushing pressure that would make anyone with some passing knowledge of combat collapse before the hopeless gap in skill.
But Garfiel was neither of those.

Garfiel was, at the very least, someone with the skill to be worthy of sparring with the Sword Saint.
Even as he shuddered, sensing that naturalness, even as the pressure made his stomach shrink, Garfiel killed his hesitation with a roar and leapt at Reinhard.

A first strike meant to gouge the man's throat with sharp claws — forgetting entirely the agreement that neither of them would inflict serious wounds on the other.

The instant that attack magnificently missed and his body went reeling, Garfiel understood the gap between them.

"—I lose."

After that, he tried multi-angled attacks from every conceivable direction, but Reinhard dodged, deflected, and parried every single one of them, evading them all with ease.
On top of that, Reinhard did all of it without moving a single step from the spot where the match had begun.
In other words, Garfiel had poured every ounce of his strength into attacking everything from Reinhard's waist up, and lost.

Caught in an opening left by an overly wide swing, he was thrown, and the moment a fist was thrust in front of his face, Garfiel declared his own defeat.
Against the Sword Saint — without even making him draw his sword — Garfiel had been utterly, completely defeated in a contest of fists alone, his own specialty.

He could barely remember what Reinhard had said afterward, or what words Subaru had offered him.
Still, he was fairly sure he'd at least managed to leave without making a spectacle of himself with sore-loser grumbling or parting shots — managing, somehow, to leave a single word behind before walking out of the inn.

The emotion swirling inside him — he couldn't tell what it was.
Beaten down by feelings he couldn't put a name to, Garfiel had meant to wander the water-gate city alone as evening approached, searching for an answer by himself.

That was the plan, anyway.

"Garf! Garf! Look, look! C'mon, the sunset's reflectin' on the water somethin' fierce, it's super red! This is amazin'! Amazin'! Pretty!"

Yipping and dashing around Garfiel, tugging his sleeve, pulling his hair, leaning her weight onto his shoulder — Mimi, having caught up, showed neither reserve nor mercy.

Thanks to that, despite having deliberately walked out of the inn, he didn't even get a moment alone to wallow.

"Oi, you — you been annoyin' as hell since a while ago. Can't ya be quiet for a bit?"

"Nnn, nope!"

"That's a real quick answer!"

Mimi grabbed Garfiel's arm and tried to spin him around by running in circles, and — pulled along by that surprisingly strong grip — Garfiel found himself spinning right there on the spot.
The thought of shaking her off for real, running fast enough that even Mimi couldn't keep up, and just escaping crossed his mind — but the limits of a beastfolk's physical ability, in Mimi's case, were an unknown quantity. She might well catch up to a fleeing Garfiel with ease.

Standing out too much in the city was also something to think about.
Before leaving for Priestella, both Frederica and Ram had given him pretty stern warnings. It wouldn't do to cause trouble for Emilia or Subaru because of his own antics. The only one allowed to be a bother was Otto — he was the reliable older-brother type who was good at cleaning up messes.

"...Haah."

"Ah-ha, whoop— hm? Whatsa matter, Garf. Wo~rries... wo... wonyin' 'bout somethin'?"

"You tryin' to say 'worryin' about somethin'?"

"Yeah, that! Wonyin' thing! Somethin' up? Tell me, tell me—"

In the end, unable to say anything, Mimi thrust out her fists with little "shuh, shuh" noises, urging him to spill it. Faced with that small, dependably earnest body, Garfiel felt some of the poison drain out of him, and clacked his fangs together in a small laugh.
He turned his gaze to the canal, narrowing his eyes.

"Ahh... damn, that's one hell of a nice view, ain't it."

"Right, right?! This is amazin'! Amazin'!"

He'd only been half-listening to Mimi's words, but looking now, the sight of the sunset reflecting off the canal really was a vivid, beautiful thing. Against the sunset dyeing the whole world orange from above, the red glow on the water, tinged with reflected yellow and white, struck him unbearably in the chest.
Before he knew it, Garfiel had sat down on the edge of the canal, watching the passing crowds and the small boats on the water, letting time drift by.

"Hmm hmm hmmm~"

Sitting down beside Garfiel, legs dangling, Mimi hummed to herself, in high spirits. She didn't seem the type capable of total silence, but for now, sticking to just humming, she was fairly well-behaved. She gripped the shoulder of Garfiel's short sleeve and swayed her head side to side.

Glancing sidelong at her cheerful profile, he belatedly noticed that the color of Mimi's fur was exactly the same orange as the sunset. Without quite meaning to, he reached out and stroked her head, and Mimi leaned into him happily.

"Fluffy? Fluffy—? Mimi, y'know, Ojou touches me a lot too. Says I'm fluffy, says somethin' like 'iyashike~.'"

"Ahh, yeah, you're nice to touch, all right. That healin'-type thing, the boss says that sometimes too — so that's what he means. Kinda get it now."

"Garf, are you gettin' healed?"

"Feels like that changes the meanin' again, somehow!"

"Huh?"

Mimi tilted her head with a face utterly devoid of malice, and Garfiel couldn't help but laugh.
And the fact that doing so made something heavy in his chest feel like it was draining away made him think he really was a cheap sort of guy. It felt like his sense of defeat and humiliation were converting themselves into plain, honest rebelliousness.

"...Ain't like a guy just up an' becomes the strongest outta nowhere. I'm still climbin'."

"Ohh, up this long, long slope to Strongest-ville!"

"Heh, ain't half bad, that. Yeah, that's it — that's the road to the strongest."

To Mimi, who thrust her fist into the air, Garfiel answered by tracing the white scar on his forehead with a finger.
Annoying as it was to admit, he'd been cheered up. Left alone, he might well have kept moping over it forever. He supposed he ought to be grateful Mimi had tagged along.

"Guess I oughta thank ya for it. Oi, shortstuff. Let's stop by a stall or somethin'. I'll treat ya—"

"Garf, look at that!"

"Huh?"

Just as he slapped his backside and stood up, about to invite Mimi to go get something to eat —
Still seated, Mimi pointed across the canal, and, drawn by her voice, Garfiel looked that way too. And his eyes narrowed.

Looking over, on the far bank of the canal, the mooring rope of one of the small boats docked there had come loose, and, caught in the current, it had begun drifting away, unmanned. But that wasn't the real problem.

"Kiddos!"

Mimi shouted loudly toward the direction the drifting boat was heading — toward one of the moored boats where about five children were riding, playing on the water.
The children hadn't noticed the approaching boat. If the drifting boat, carried by the current, collided with theirs, there was a chance it would capsize and throw them into the canal.

At Mimi's voice, the other people around the canal noticed the situation too. One of the boat owners at the mooring dock scrambled to his feet, but he'd noticed too late to make it in time.
At the voices of the people around them, the children finally noticed, their faces going pale, and they nearly panicked at the sight of the boat bearing down on them.

And then —

"Yo, you brats. Thank that little cat sister there — she's the one who noticed first."

"Garf!"

Leaping across to the boat on the far bank in a single bound, landing on the boat on the water with barely a rock — an astonishing sense of balance. Landing in near silence, Garfiel must have seemed, to the children, to have simply dropped out of nowhere.
Bared fangs, a wickedly grinning face with mean eyes, a blond man. At his sudden appearance, the children froze speechless, and — with no one thrashing about, conveniently enough — Garfiel scooped up all five children at once and leapt again.

Clear of the boat, landing on the canal's walkway. The instant he released the children there, behind him the boats collided, and the boat the children had been riding flipped over.
Just as several more boats were about to get swept up in a chain-reaction collision, Garfiel — together with a rope he'd grabbed from the boat he'd carried the children off — skillfully brought them to a stop.

Using the motion of the capsized boat to check the momentum, and stopping the runaway boat that had first slipped its mooring from drifting further downstream, Garfiel brought the whole chain of chaos to a halt with minimal damage.

"An' that's about that!"

Retying the rope firmly, Garfiel declared the incident closed — and from the onlookers who had witnessed it all, a burst of cheers and applause rose up.
He waved off the boat owner, who should have been watching the boats, as the man bowed over and over in gratitude, and Garfiel scratched his head, looking bashful.

And then —

"Um, um, mister. Thank you so much."

"Ohh?"

The children he'd rescued spoke their thanks to him, all together.
Turning to face them, Garfiel saw none of the fear they'd shown on the boat — only eyes shining with something like sparkles, directed at him.
At the sight of the children and Garfiel together like that, the applause grew even louder.
Feeling something oddly pleasant in that sound, Garfiel rubbed his nose lightly with a finger, and said —

"What, ain't nothin' to make a fuss over. Just a coincidence... yeah, coincidence. Coincidence, the sunset an' the damp wind just happened to tell me somethin'. This water-gate city, always surrounded by water... if somebody's tears go an' fall, hell, the canals'll finally overflow, won't they."

"――――"

Garfiel answered proudly, and the sound of applause abruptly thinned.
The cheering grew intermittent too, and voices tinged with something subtly awkward began to mix in. But unlike the crowd around them, the reaction of the children in front of him was dramatic.

"Th-that's amazin'!" "So cool!" "Not carin' about danger, all for someone's tears!" "Never back down! Never flatter! Never look back!"

At the children's rising excitement, Garfiel nodded, satisfied.
And then, as Garfiel laughed, clacking his fangs together, one of the children asked —

"Mister, what's your name?"

"Ain't somethin' worth namin'. But, well, if I gotta say... I'm a tiger. Yeah, a golden tiger. People call me — Gorgeous Tiger!!"

"Gorgeous!" "Tiger!!"

Both arms stretched diagonally toward the sky, body tilted into a pose, Garfiel struck the stance.
At the sight, the children's voices cracked with excitement, their eyes lighting up, and they struck the same pose right along with him.

And then, as Garfiel bonded with the children, Mimi came running around the canal to catch up, eyes sparkling just the same, and —

"—Garf, that's so cool!!"

Saying so, she ran up and joined right in, striking the pose along with them.
The laughter of the children, Mimi, and Garfiel's booming laugh rang out over the canal in the evening light.

—By then, the applause and cheers had long since died away, and only the boat owner, wearing a strained smile, remained behind, alone, watching it all unfold.

Leading the now-bonded children along, having treated them all to snacks from the stalls, Garfiel walked along in high spirits, swaggering along with his shoulders back.

"An' right then, I said it. 'Ain't a single one of you takin' one more step past here, small fry. This here's the boss's belly. You lot already fell right into the trap'!"

"Amazin'!" "So cool!"
"Waah! Gives me chills!"

Beneath a Priestella now thoroughly dyed the color of sunset, Mimi and a blond boy egged on Garfiel as he recounted his heroic exploits.
Incidentally, what he'd just been telling was one of the more memorable episodes from the past year — the "Earth Spider hunt."

That was the incident where, for whatever reason, the trio of Subaru, Garfiel, and Otto had ended up having to exterminate a mass outbreak of a witchbeast called the "Earth Spider" in some village — an incident that had been a real triumph, with Subaru and Otto's scheming and Garfiel's combat prowess both fully on display.

Listening to that reminiscence with delight were Mimi, and one of the children he'd rescued earlier — a boy, maybe about six or seven years old, with golden hair.
He had a charming face and an endearing smile — the makings, one might guess, of quite the heartbreaker in the future. But right now, he was just one boy whose eyes shone at Garfiel's heroics and way of life, well on his way to leading his life astray.

Having finished treating the children to snacks, Garfiel — with a self-appointed sense of guardian responsibility — was in the process of seeing each of the children safely home one by one. Four of the five had already made it home safely, leaving this boy as the last one.

"Still, you kiddos went pretty damn far out just to play, huh, oi. Ain't that dangerous?"

Along the way to the boy's home, Garfiel frowned at just how far they'd walked.
The children had been boating around the border between the First District, where the inn was, and the Second District, where the Muse Company was located. Garfiel himself, who'd been wandering aimlessly, lost in thought, all the way out here was one thing — but he couldn't help thinking it was quite a distance for still-young children to have come out to.
After all, apparently the children's homes were further in still, in the city's Third District.

At a child's pace, heading straight there, it would probably take nearly an hour. At Garfiel's remark, the boy grinned, showing his teeth, and said —

"Sometimes, the songstress comes to that coen in the First District. We went to go see her."

"Songstress... you mean, that. Sure, the boss did say her singin' voice was somethin' else, but I'm a little skeptical about that myself."

At the child's innocent answer, Garfiel rubbed his nose, unconvinced.
The only time Garfiel had seen the songstress Liliana was for a very brief moment at the Muse Company. Still, even in that short time, she'd left more than enough of an impression — there was no doubt she was, well, a rather strong-flavored girl. But that flavor seemed to him utterly at odds with the pure, wholesome image of a "songstress."

"Garf, didn't you listen to the songstress's song? It was, like, super somethin'!"

"You actually listened all the way through, shortstuff?"

"Nnn! Didn't fall asleep till the very end! Mimi's amazin'! Praise me!"

Garfiel gave a perfunctory pat to Mimi's head as she thrust it out at him. Mimi cried "Yay!" and dashed off delighted, and Garfiel turned his gaze from her back to the boy.

"So, did ya get to see this songstress, then?"

"Nuh-uh, no good. Said we were a little too late. But since we came all the way out to the First District..."

"You ended up playin' in the river instead. Well, good thing I made it in time, huh."

"Gorgeous Tiger!"

"Damn right!"

The boy stretched a hand toward the sky, and Garfiel raised his own to match.
Gorgeous Tiger, here and present.

But right after they'd struck their poses together in high spirits, the boy lowered his arm and let out a melancholy sigh. Garfiel tilted his head at the boy's profile.

"What's with the sudden long face? Cut that sighin' out. It'll scare your happiness away."

"It's, um... when I get home, my big sister's gonna yell at me."

"Ahh?"

At the boy's timid, sister-fearing posture, Garfiel overreacted. Seeing the boy's shoulders shake, he hastily backpedaled with a "my bad, my bad," and —

"But hey, why's it gonna turn into you gettin' yelled at like that?"

"...'Cause I snuck out without tellin' her."

"Ahh."

It seemed the boy had gone on today's outing with his friends without telling his sister. As a result, he was apparently terrified of the thunderbolt that awaited him from his surely-worried family right about now.

That feeling wasn't lost on Garfiel either. A sister was the kind of presence a younger brother could never quite get over, no matter how much time passed.
Even after being reunited after ten years, even with a body grown so much bigger, that still held true. For a boy who saw his sister face-to-face every day, and who was now outmatched in build besides, the fear must be even greater.

"Got it. Leave it to me — I'll handle it proper."

"Eh...?"

At Garfiel thumping his chest as he spoke, the boy's face showed surprise.
As if to reassure that expression, Garfiel grinned, baring his sharp fangs.

"I get how scary big sisters can be too, y'know. I'll lend a hand. When your sis shows up, kid, I'll back you up right."

"Mister!"

Overcome with emotion, the boy threw himself at Garfiel, clinging tight. Garfiel hugged him back, and then Mimi latched onto his back the same way.
With small kids clinging to him front and back like that, Garfiel set off toward the boy's home with renewed resolve, trudging along.

Sunset was closing in in earnest, and it looked like he wasn't going to make it back to the inn in time for dinner. But today, of all days, that was probably for the best.
He still wasn't quite recovered enough to sit calmly and eat a meal with Reinhard face to face in the dining hall. But given one night, he figured he'd be steady enough to manage.
And that recovery, all of it, was thanks to the children who'd taken to calling him Gorgeous Tiger, and to Mimi, who somehow gave him energy he couldn't quite explain.

"—Fred!"

Just then, that thought was pierced by a high voice ringing in Garfiel's ears.
At the sudden presence, Garfiel looked up, and there was a girl running toward them from a distance, at a furious pace. Her long blond hair streaming behind her, the girl's eyes were fixed, with single-minded intensity, on the boy clinging to Garfiel.
At that voice, the boy looked up, opening his mouth toward the girl.

And then —

"Sis..."

"Do you have any idea how much you worried people?!"

A dynamic flying kick, launched off both feet, struck the boy dead-on just as he tried to leap toward her, and his small body went bouncing lightly backward.
A perfect flying kick, killing none of its momentum, with a twist added for good measure — Garfiel, having watched it through almost involuntarily, was slow to react as the girl landed with beautiful precision.
In that instant, the girl fixed Garfiel with a sharp, if pretty, glare, and stamped her heel down onto the top of his foot.

"You suspicious creep. What were you planning to do with our Fred?!"

"Doesn't... hurt, but get your foot off, shortstuff."

At Garfiel's calm, quiet retort to her spirited challenge, the girl flinched and stepped back, her first-strike attack having failed to land any damage.
Maybe she thought Garfiel had gotten angry, but he hadn't quite gotten there yet. If anything, he was still mostly just stunned.

He'd never have guessed a sister would show up and land a flying kick on her own little brother without so much as a word of explanation.
Incidentally, the kicked boy — before he could hit the ground unable to break his fall — was caught by Mimi, who leapt in with a "pyaa!" and rolled cleanly across the ground with him.
The two of them were now standing back up, brushing the dirt off each other.

Watching that out of the corner of his eye, Garfiel let out a sigh. At that attitude —

"Wh-what's with that attitude?! I-I'll have you know, I won't let anyone do anything weird to Fred or to me... I-I'm scary when I get angry, y'know!"

"First off, gonna say it's a misunderstandin'. An' second, don't go pullin' some big move like that on a brother who can't even take a fall. He coulda got hurt bad doin' that. You get me?"

"Ugh..."

Crouching down, Garfiel spoke to the girl in that same quiet voice, haltingly.
The boy's sister, this girl too, was still young — maybe around ten years old. The precocious sort, at that age where you want to seem a little grown-up. Her earlier bravado had faded somewhat, and at Garfiel's calm, subdued manner of responding, her eyes had begun to well up.

Given that, in looks alone, Garfiel was pure delinquent through and through, the girl must have summoned up real courage to confront him. In a way, it was an unfortunate outcome for her — being met with something scarier than being shouted back at.

"G-Gorgeous Tiger... please don't be too hard on my sister."

Perhaps unable to bear watching his trembling sister, the boy — dust now fully brushed off, recovered from the shock — peeked out from behind her back and pleaded with Garfiel. His sister, for her part, looked her pride wounded by her brother's behavior, but she still managed to maintain enough dignity to shield him from Garfiel's gaze. The start of things had been rocky, but Garfiel figured it wasn't a bad relationship between the two.

"Not real convinced 'bout me endin' up the villain here, if I'm honest."

"Villain! Villain's no good, Garf. Gorgeous! Tiger!"

Mimi, having pattered back over, jumped up and gave Garfiel a little bonk on the head. It didn't hurt, but he had to admit the blow left him rather unconvinced all the same.
And so, the fruitless staring contest between the siblings and Garfiel and company continued.

Or so it seemed, until —

"Sis. Fred. Where did you two go?"

Once again, it was a voice from elsewhere that broke the stalemate.
At the soft, gentle voice of a woman, the siblings looked at each other as if struck. Then they took off running toward the sound, and Garfiel watched them go, eyes wide.

The siblings headed toward a bend in the road. That was where the voice had come from, and as a woman stepped into view from around the corner, the siblings dove without hesitation into her arms.
The one who caught them was a blond woman — likely their mother.

"Mom!"
"Mom, there's a suspicious person! Some Gorgeous-whatever tried to do somethin' to Fred!"

The little brother, clinging tearfully to his mother, and the sister, trying to make the false accusation stick.
At the two children's words, the figure who'd caught the siblings gave a troubled sort of smile.

Watching the family like that, Garfiel shook his head, exasperated.
It wouldn't do to have the city guards called on him now. Relieved, for the moment, that an adult who could talk things through calmly had shown up, he took a step forward to explain himself, and —

"――――"

Still holding both children, the woman turned toward him a face touched with a smile — and at the sight of her, Garfiel's feet stopped dead.

"Garf?"

At Garfiel's suspicious sudden halt, Mimi turned a puzzled look on him.
But Garfiel couldn't respond to Mimi's words. He had no room left for that at all. His heart, right now, was filled with turmoil and confusion, in an utter mess.

Because — standing there was —

"Um, I'm sorry, it seems our children have been a bother to you. If you don't mind, could I hear the story?"

Gently, with a tone utterly devoid of malice or suspicion, the woman made the offer.
Stepping forward, now standing right in front of him — at her presence, Garfiel's mouth trembled, and, at that, the woman tilted her head, puzzled.

That expression, that manner, that voice, shook Garfiel to his very core.

"—Mother?"

A cracked, murmured voice spilled from his throat.
