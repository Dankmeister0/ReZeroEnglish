Arc 7 Chapter 57: "I Can't Live Wisely"

I am in a dark, dark space.

Adrift. Wandering. Tossed about. Trampled.
As if my own body had been severed at the torso — my four limbs and everything above the neck — each floating apart in its own place, drifting, swaying, toyed with.

What happened, what occurred — my memory of it is terribly hazy.
Everywhere is pitch black, and I find myself wondering why I'd been cast into a space with nothing in it at all.

——.
————.
————————.
————————————.

Nothing comes to mind.
There's no reason at all for me to be trapped here, severed at the neck and limbs, floating.
Then, to begin with, isn't there no reason I have to be in a place like this either?

"—I love you."

Every time that thought crosses my mind, I hear a faint voice holding my thoughts back.
A voice hard to make out — whether hoarse, or simply far away. But an earnest voice that makes me instinctively want to strain my ears, tilt my head toward it, and listen.

"—I love you. I love you. I love you."

Every time I hear that voice, whatever I'd been thinking a moment before resets, back to zero.
Whether I should call that bothersome, or something that can't be helped — I can't even tell which is right.
But as this repeats over and over, a new question arises — whether there's something I can do about the distance of that voice.

"—I love you. I love you. I love you. I love you. I love you."

As though even to cut off that thought, the number of times the faint, pleading voice calls out increases.
But that has the opposite effect. Because I want to hear that voice, want to go to the one it belongs to — that's the very reason this thought began, trying to reach from zero to one.
And so—

"Someone... "

Severed at the torso from my limbs and head, unable to move, there's nothing I can do myself.
So I want to rely on some outside power that might do something even in this situation.

Someone — was there someone?
Someone who might lend me a hand even in a hopeless situation like this.

Was there someone who was kind to me?
If that someone would come along with me, surely, the answer too—

—And so, to reach the voice echoing from far away, I stretch out my hand.

"—!"

"Ohh? That one just now had some real spirit behind it, didn't it."

An especially loud, tremendous roar echoed out, its tremor traveling through the ground to reach the two of them at the inn.
Outside the window, a colossal black shadow rampaged, slowly eating away at the magic city from its center, drawing it into itself, chewing it up, turning it into something that had never existed in this world.

That was, surely, the worst nightmare imaginable, one that every being in this world would fear.
The embodiment of an ending that anyone, at a single glance, could not help but call to mind.
And Al understood that he himself, more than anyone, knew the terror that ending brought — not merely on his skin, but in his very soul.

Abel, Medium, and the others had moved to break the deadlock of the situation.
Far from moving alongside them, here he was, curled up, clutching his trembling head — what a sorry state. Just what face did he think he could show, saying he'd come along and be of use?

"I... I..."

Had he come all this way just to feel, keenly, how powerless he was?
If so, then just what had he come for in the first place—

"Sheesh, here I go hurrying all the way back, and all that's here is one green kid cowering and curled up. Well, if His Excellency were here that'd be its own problem too, mind."

"—"

"Still, if Kafma's out there tangling with that thing, then that means His Excellency ordered him to, I reckon. So does that mean His Excellency doesn't intend to run? You know anything, kid?"

So saying, Olbart came plodding closer, tossing out the question.
The small old man cracked his neck and bent at the waist to peer into the face of the crouching Al. Al's fear-numbed brain barely managed to chew through the content of the question.
The false emperor, absent from this place — Vincent — had reached some sort of agreement with Abel—

"H-he's with... that girl, Tanza..."

"Tanza, huh — that deer girl. More and more, I got no idea what he's thinking... His Excellency's always been a bit short on words. Least he could do is leave a note behind about what he means to do next."

"Y-you..."

"Hm?"

Having heard Al's account, Olbart made a sour face and stood up. Not wanting to let the strange old man slip away as he moved off a little, Al pushed his trembling tongue and throat to their limit, forming words.
Olbart, having come back here — what did he mean to do now?

"Are you... planning to fight that thing?"

"Oi oi, don't talk nonsense."

"Eh..."

At Olbart's breezy answer, Al couldn't help but gape.
And before Al, who stared rigidly wide-eyed, Olbart pointed out the window.

"C'mon, one look and you'd know. That thing's seriously bad news. I already had my right hand taken clean off, and I ain't keen on doing anything else that dangerous."

"—"

"Reason I came back here was in case His Excellency happened to still be around — figured I'd need to haul him out. Since he ain't here, guess I gotta find wherever he is and get him clear... You know where he and the deer girl went off to, kid?"

Asked by the breezy Olbart, Al shook his head where he sat.
It wasn't a lie — it was the truth. Ever since that black shadow had swallowed Koururi Castle and overflowed, he retained almost no memory of the exchange Abel and the others had carried on at this inn.

All he barely retained was that Kafma, given his orders, had leapt outside; that Vincent had gone out together with Tanza; and that Abel had left the useless Al behind, while Medium alone had worried over him until the very end. That was all.

"Well then, that's a real proper mess. Seems like the townsfolk have gotten fired up on that fox girl's say-so too, which just makes finding His Excellency alone all the harder... And I ain't keen on lingering too long in this powder keg myself."

"—! Wh-what's that supposed to mean? Don't tell me you're running away?"

Feeling the flow of his thoughts head toward an unbelievable conclusion, Al's voice cracked.
But faced with Al's stunned stare, Olbart merely shrugged, unfazed. "Can't be helped, can it."

"Don't feel like there's a way to deal with that thing, and nothing matters if you're dead, right. What I hold dear is my own life and my dream. Ain't got a reason to stand my ground."

"B-but the Emperor's out there! The Emperor you're supposed to protect, old man—"

"If His Excellency's thinking and moving on his own, then that's his own version of the best move, ain't it. If he's counting on my help showing up here, then he don't have me figured out either. That'd be a shame for that 'Wise Emperor' title of his."

"Ah...!"

"You too, kid — I ain't saying this to be mean, but you'd best run. Staking your life here won't get you a thing. Living wisely is what wins out, see."

Olbart's philosophy was something bloody and unshakable — the harsh Vollachian code of blood and iron, blended further with the lifetime he'd survived as a Shinobi.
It was something an outsider like Al could never change no matter what he said. —No, likely no one but Olbart himself could ever have a say over Olbart's own thinking.

It could never be allowed for someone else to sway or warp his own life.
That was precisely why the man named Olbart Dunkelkenn reigned as the strongest Shinobi. He was a Shinobi made complete by creating nothing — no treasured possessions, no one to give his heart to.

In that, no weight was placed on the title of General, nor his position as head of the Shinobi, nor any sense of duty that he must protect the Emperor.
He was not free. He was lawless. —The world's foremost "outlaw" — that was his true nature.

That way of thinking could not be moved. Could not be shaken. By anyone.
So arguing back and forth over his beliefs here would gain nothing at all.

"—"

Grinding his back teeth hard, harder, Al lowered the arm he'd had pressed to his ear.
The roaring and the rumbling of the earth, the war cries of the magic city's people standing against the black shadow, rang out as though staging hell on earth, shaking Al's heart.

His heart felt ready to crack. His head felt ready to shatter. His soul felt ready to scatter into mist.
But it hadn't. Not yet. If it hadn't—

"...Old man, let me ask you one thing."

"—? What is it."

"That... that mass of black shadow — it's connected to my brother, isn't it."

Clenching his teeth hard enough to taste blood, Al spoke, his voice managing, for just that moment, to hide its tremor.
Whether he read Al's desperate expression — hidden by his mask and not clearly visible — from the look in his eyes and the tone of his voice, Olbart touched his left hand to his own beard, and—

"Aye, that's right. To my eyes, that shadow looked like it came out from inside the kid."

"—"

"Now that you've heard that, you got your mind made up on what you're gonna do?"

Squeezing his eyes shut, Al chewed over the words Olbart had told him.
It was something he'd already known — beneath that black shadow was Natsuki Subaru. —No, at the very center of it all was Natsuki Subaru.

If so — if so, then Al had to raise his face, prepared for his back teeth to crack.
If Natsuki Subaru was there, then Aldebaran had to go there.

"You said to live wisely, didn't you, old man."

"Aye, that I did. That's what I reckon."

"But I don't think so. Old man, your way of living isn't a wise way to live — it's a cunning one. —I don't want to become a cunning adult."

There came a hard thud.
It wasn't from outside — it was right beside Al. He'd slammed his fist against the floor, and with the pain and momentum of it hauled his body up. Leaning the raised body against the wall, he somehow, someway got to his feet.
His knees trembled, his heart quailed, and though he hadn't even fought yet he was already battered to the point of collapse mentally — even so—

"No time to be sitting around... I couldn't face my parents like this...!"

Bracing a hand against the wall to stand, Al bared his teeth inside his mask.
Seeing his desperate resolve and hearing his blood-spitting cry, Olbart raised his long, bushy eyebrows. He glared out the window, then, closing one eye at the back of the shouting Al—

"Your spirit's fine and all, but you got any chance of beating that thing in a fight?"

"No! There's no way anybody could beat it! I could challenge it ten thousand times and never win! The only one who can beat it... the only one who can beat it is Natsuki Subaru!"

Swinging his arm wide, striking the window frame, Al turned around.
Facing the strange old man head-on, Al stepped forward with both eyes burning fiercely, and—

"Old man!"

"Oho, getting all fired up. I'll tell you now, if you're asking me to help, that's a no—"

"Don't need it! More than that, hurry up and put me back, you damn old geezer."

"—"

"I'll buy you the time to run. So give me back my Authority!"

Stretching his shortened right arm straight out, Al roared this at Olbart.
At Al's cry — a bestial roar, a grieving wail, and a sense of duty blazing like fire, all tangled together — Olbart held his breath.

Then, slowly, the strange old man shook his head, and—

"Well, if you're going out of your way to throw yourself away as a sacrifice, I got no objection. Truth is, that fight was the kid's win from the start, and a promise's gotta be kept."

"My brother's... win?"

"That's right. That kid, in a game of tag, went and caught me fair and square. Thought that was something, I did."

As he spoke, Olbart flicked his left wrist and drew closer; Al met him head-on.
Hearing Olbart's account, Al felt, beyond the bomb-like pounding of his own pulse, a single note of relief and admiration ring out.

While Al had been frozen, cowering even at the sound of the wind, Subaru had carried his original resolve through to the end, forced a head-on confrontation with Olbart, and in the end had even wrested away a victory.
Surely, at the end of a pile of defeats that couldn't have numbered just one—

"Say, I'm just asking outta curiosity, but—"

Suddenly, into that pounding heartbeat, a hoarse voice cut in, and Al looked at the man without a word.
Olbart bared his teeth in a grin at the unwelcoming look and gently pressed his outstretched left hand to Al's chest.
And then—

"How exactly are you planning to challenge an opponent you say you couldn't beat even in ten thousand tries?"

"Shut up, you damn geezer. —I'll go die a million times over if I have to."

"The new 'Stargazer' who has inherited the heavenly mandate to prevent the 'Great Disaster.'"

The instant she was named that, Taritta's world lurched violently.
The rampaging Great Disaster was shaking the magic city, an earth-rumbling roar sounding without pause. But the illusion of the ground crumbling beneath her feet had nothing to do with any of that.

It was because the words of the man before her wearing the demon mask — Abel — had bored into the depths of her heart.

"Star... gazer...?"

Medium, standing close beside the swaying Taritta and gently supporting her, murmured the unfamiliar word under her breath as if to confirm it.
It was a word with no connection to those who didn't know it, one they had no need to know.
In fact, if Taritta herself had been able to go on living without incident, simply as one of the People of Shudrak in her home forest, she would never have needed to have anything to do with it at all.
But—

"I... am..."

"Abel-chin! I don't get any of this at all! What are you even talking about!"

In place of Taritta, whose voice trembled, Medium bit back at Abel.
Though her body had been made small, she hadn't lost her fighting spirit, and she puffed out her now-slight chest, glaring straight at Abel head-on as though shielding Taritta.

"What's this 'Stargazer' thing? Why is Taritta-chan supposed to be one?"

"—. A 'Stargazer' is one who bears the role of foreseeing the future from the movement and guidance of the stars. No — rather than a role, it would be more accurate to call it a karmic burden."

"...Are you making that sound difficult on purpose?"

"That's not my intention."

At Medium's words, her face screwed up in the struggle to understand, Abel let out a small breath.
Then Abel called out "Taritta" once more, past Medium, to Taritta. Taritta's shoulders jumped involuntarily, and Abel fixed his gaze on her straight on, and—

"To begin with, the reason I was driven from the capital is closely tied to the 'Stargazer.' You surely already know the substance of it, Taritta."

"Th-the substance...?"

"Naturally, it is the prophecy foretelling the Empire's ruin. You knew of it. —And that is why you sought my life in the Buddheim Jungle."

"—!"

At Abel's flat, matter-of-fact declaration, arms crossed, Taritta's cheeks stiffened and she looked down.
The shock of being called a "Stargazer" had nearly buried it, but the fact Abel had pointed out just before that was not something she could let pass unheard either.

He had, indeed, said it. —That Taritta was the hunter in the jungle.
Whether she regretted having failed to finish him off.
In other words—

"Since when..."

"—"

"Since when did you realize that I had been targeting you...?"

"Taritta-chan!?"

Head bowed, unable to meet Abel's eyes, Taritta posed that question.
Hearing Taritta's words, Medium let out a stunned voice. Naturally so. Hearing that question made it plain there was no room left to deny Abel's accusation.

It was a confession of the fact that Taritta had, with her bow and arrow, targeted Abel's life.

"The prophecies of the 'Stargazers' — this so-called heavenly mandate — are, by and large, shared among Stargazers themselves. So it stood to reason that, having fled the capital, I would be pursued by someone who knew the mandate. In addition—"

"In addition...?"

"Anyone who understands the workings of the throne could deduce that I would turn to the People of Shudrak for aid. Nine times out of ten, there would be an assassin among the Shudrak."

"—"

"Though, I only obtained confirmation of the assassin's existence when you mistook Natsuki Subaru for me and took aim at him."

Hearing the reasoning Abel laid out, Taritta gripped tightly at the hem of her clothing.
It was true. There was no way for Taritta, living at the edge of the Empire, to have known the face of the Vollachian Emperor. All she knew was the outward feature: black hair, black eyes.
Who could have imagined that two people bearing such a rare feature would appear in that forest on that very day.

"Your reaction, seeing the two of us — myself and him — imprisoned in the village cage, was quite telling. ...You're too honest at heart to serve as a pawn of the Observers."

"—Ah"

"After that, you accompanied us both through the fall of Guaral and the road to the magic city, yet you never once tried to carry out your mandate. Why?"

"—"

"Why did you refuse to obey your mandate? I've heard that a Stargazer's karmic burden is not something one can resist, even knowing it. I cannot believe you possess the will needed to resist such a burden."

If she had possessed such strong will, such self-mastery, Taritta would never have accompanied this journey in the first place.
Abel's assessment was harsh, but correct. Correct enough to draw blood.

Taritta had no strength to cast aside her mandate of her own will.
If that had been possible, she would have thrown off this burden far, far sooner.
If she hadn't known that, without obeying the mandate, the "Great Disaster" would destroy the Empire—

"—Ugh! I don't get any of this at all!"

Taritta, tormented, and Abel, who showed no sign of loosening his pursuit.
Caught between the two of them, and yet left out of the loop, Medium exploded. She spread both arms wide, shielding Taritta behind her, and glared at Abel with her blue eyes.

"I don't know anything about Abel-chin being targeted, or that it was Taritta-chan, none of it! Besides, if Taritta-chan had really meant to, Abel-chin would've been done for ages ago! Right?"

"You could stand to mince your words a little."

"Right!? Abel-chin, could you even get away from Taritta-chan?"

At Medium's forceful words, a bitter look carved itself at the corner of Abel's mouth.
He likely couldn't deny her words. But regarding that very point, Abel had already put the question to Taritta.
Namely — just as Medium said, why hadn't she done it, when she could have if she'd meant to?
That was—

"...Because I couldn't find any meaning in it."

"—. Do you need a reason beyond saving the Empire from ruin?"

"I! I didn't even know you were the Emperor, or the circumstances of you being driven from the capital...! Because — I — the one who's the 'Stargazer' isn't—"

The anguish she had kept locked away deep in her chest all this time — her mouth opened, and it spilled out.
The anguish she had long hesitated to put into words, drawn out by none other than Abel's pursuit.
But before it could become clear words—

"—No, don't!!"

Medium cried out, eyes wide, and in an instant Taritta's and Abel's attention snapped upward.
An instant later, directly above the building where the three of them stood, black darkness seemed to blanket the sky — no, that wasn't it. It wasn't simply spreading black darkness, but a colossal black arm.

Equipped with five fingers, it stretched straight down, swinging from directly above toward the building.
As if to crush the three of them here flat — no, as if to seize them in that gigantic hand.

"—! Medium! Abel!"

The instant she saw it closing in, every extraneous thought vanished from Taritta's mind.
The scene of accusation from just moments ago was wiped away, and Taritta moved as instinct demanded — scooping up Medium before her, slinging Abel over her shoulder, and leaping straight backward.
She tried to escape the range of the colossal black arm as she was. —But it wasn't enough.
Caught by the falling fingertips, they would be stripped utterly from this world with no way to resist.

"I won't let that happen—!!"

But just before the giant arm could reach Taritta and the others, an enormous mass wedged itself, with tremendous force, between the arm and the three of them overhead.
It was a section of castle wall, torn free and bound tightly all over in green thorns.
That colossal mass, carrying tremendous centrifugal force, became a canopy shielding the three of them, moving to catch the falling giant arm.

Of course, it wasn't something that could actually be stopped by that.
The instant the black giant arm touched the castle wall, it unraveled as easily as spun sugar candy, vanishing before their eyes like a leaf devoured by insects.
However—

"I'll not allow any more violence in my city than this, I won't—!!"

A woman in a kimono leapt in directly beneath the vanishing castle wall, the beast ears atop her head standing straight up, and she kicked her long leg straight upward, driving the wall up into the giant arm.
A shockwave tore through, bringing down the building beneath her pivot foot. But whatever the force of that woman's kick had truly been, the single blow — delivered through the wall — bent the giant arm and halted its advance.

Only for an instant, though.
Even so, that one instant was enough.

"—! We're clear!"

Having narrowly escaped the giant arm's range, Taritta let out a breath as her heels tore into the ground.
Freed from her arms, Medium and Abel landed on the ground as well. The three of them raised their heads, watching the building that had been their footing just moments ago being swallowed by the shadow.

"Y-Yorna-chan, is she...!?"

"She's fine. Though she seems to be being chased even more than before."

To Medium, casting her eyes about, Abel answered with a jerk of his chin.
Where his gesture directed her gaze, leaping about while pursued by the raging shadow, was the woman in the kimono who had unleashed that fierce blow moments before — so, she was Yorna Mishigure.

Power befitting one of the "Nine Divine Generals," the strength of one beyond the ordinary.
It was easy to understand why Abel would want to bring someone of her caliber onto his side. A person of her ability could surely accept, with ease, even a fate that might sway the course of the world.
Not hesitating, as Taritta had, when that moment stood right before her.

"You've saved me again, Taritta."

As if seeing through Taritta's hesitation, Abel watched her where she knelt.
For reasons other than the exertion of a moment before, Taritta couldn't calm her breathing. But it seemed Abel had no intention of letting her rest, body or mind.

"Had you not reached out, I might have lost my life without any effort at all on your part. And yet you saved me — was it because you cannot fulfill your mandate without dirtying your own hands?"

"Th-that's not it...!"

"Then why? Your actions lack consistency—"

"More importantly, shouldn't you be saying thank you for saving you!"

With Taritta at a loss for words, blamed for her inconsistent actions, Medium once again came to her rescue.
Her beautiful golden hair disheveled, Medium stood right before Abel, pointing a finger at him. It was a gesture made awkward by their difference in height, but even so Medium wouldn't bend her point, wouldn't stop.

"Both me and Abel-chin would be dead right now if Taritta-chan hadn't saved us, you know! You gotta say thanks first. Taritta-chan, thank yooou~!"

"Eh, eh..."

"After that, then the rest! What is it, Abel-chin? What are you telling Taritta-chan you want her to do?"

Whirling around to say her thanks, then whirling right back to face Abel, Medium.
Her line of questioning trailed somewhat behind the exchange between Abel and Taritta, and Abel sighed, unable to keep pace with her level of comprehension.

"I have already conveyed that to Taritta. There is no need for me to gain your understanding of it."

"If I don't get it, I'll keep getting in the way until I do, you know! Is that fine with you!?"

"—. To quell that 'Great Disaster,' I must lose my life. That is the future the 'Stargazer' foretold, and the mandate laid upon Taritta."

"—? Even hearing that, it doesn't make sense. Why would that huge black shadow calm down just because Abel-chin dies? That's weird!"

At Medium's persistent protest, Taritta bit hard at the inside of her cheek.
Certainly, she understood the prophecy that fulfilling the mandate would result in the Empire escaping ruin. But precisely how that would be brought about — even Taritta didn't know that.
But such a point would only serve to make Medium keenly aware of Abel's coldness all the more—

"—Strange, is it."

But Taritta's speculation was arrested by the sound of Abel's next words.
Medium, baring her teeth as she pressed "It's weird!" — before her, Abel put a hand to his mouth and repeated "Strange" once more.

"Taritta."

"Y-yes, what is it?"

"Your mandate is to take my life in order for the Empire to escape ruin. Is that correct?"

"—!"

At the renewed question, Taritta nodded, though with hesitation.
That was what she had been told. The mandate she had never once managed to carry out, though she knew it was necessary.
But having confirmed that, Abel pressed further, "Then—"

"Is the ruin you know of one and the same as that thing there? Is that correct?"

"Eh..."

"I know there existed a prophecy of a 'Great Disaster' that would bring the Empire's ruin. But do you have certainty that that thing there is it? Is that correct?"

Asked that, Taritta raced her thoughts trying to grasp what meaning it could have.
That black shadow gradually spreading from the center of the magic city, that presence summoning up horror and dread and awe — what could you call it, if not the "Great Disaster"?

Again and again, over and over, the nightmare vision shown to her.
The nightmare that pleaded with her every night, don't forget, had made Taritta dream over and over of the moment of ruin yet to come. That thing was, surely, precisely its manifestation — and yet—

"Whether that thing is truly the 'Great Disaster'..."

"Is not something within your knowledge, is it. Then what of another 'Stargazer'?"

"Another..."

Even told that, all Taritta could do was let her eyes wander uncertainly.
Abel had said that Stargazers were connected to one another, but Taritta was an exception. —No, even the word "exception" didn't quite fit. To begin with, her very foundation was different.
So for Taritta to have any idea who another "Stargazer" might be—

"Ah..."

As for someone she could think of as a "Stargazer" — she had, in fact, only just crossed paths with one moments ago.

"—"

That effete man who never once dropped his breezy manner, that irritating person who hadn't even given his name — he had been thoroughly versed in the circumstances, and had called Taritta the "taint of the Shudrak."
He had known of Taritta's sin. Likely through the connection between Stargazers.

That man, who had been a "Stargazer" — what had he said, upon seeing that "Great Disaster"?
Yes, if she recalled correctly, that man had—

"He said that thing... was outside his jurisdiction."

"—. I see. That is what the 'Stargazer' said. I see."

"Abel-chin?"

Hearing the words Taritta let slip in bewilderment, Abel nodded and then turned his back.
Frowning at that, Medium circled around in front of Abel and peered into his face. Then Medium's round eyes shot open with a start.

The reason for her surprise became clear from Abel's reaction just after.
Abel put his palm to his own demon-masked face, and, fixing his gaze on the "Great Disaster" still rampaging at the center of the magic city — no, on the pitch-black darkness swallowing everything—

"—So it's a different thing from the 'Great Disaster' that brings the Empire's ruin."

Murmuring that, Abel gave a faint sound in his throat and let out a short breath.
To Taritta, who couldn't see his expression, it sounded as though he had laughed.

"Then there is no reason to stumble along the way. —You will be removed from the board, insolent thing."
