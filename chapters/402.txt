Arc 5 Interlude: "The Name of Warmth"

Whatever anyone might say, in the end his true nature hadn't been corrected.
Even knowing a problem lay right in front of him, even knowing what needed to be done to solve it, if he knew it would bring pain, he still shrank away.

It had been the same during the affair of the Sanctuary — facing that same problem now, Garfiel found himself thinking.
That in the end, he hadn't changed one bit from when he used to howl from inside that cramped little den.

"Ah! Gorgeous Tiger!"

"Oh—wait, that's dangerous!"

Seeing Garfiel come to visit, the boy's face lit up in an instant.
Watching him come dashing over with his whole small body, Garfiel felt both alarm and relief at once, and caught him.

"C'mon, watch where you're steppin' when you run, would ya? Ain't it just dumb to go fallin' and hurtin' yourself?"

"Ohh, falling hurts, huh? Mimi used to trip over all the time when she was little too! And every time, Hetaro made this face like it hurt him! But Mimi wasn't hurt that much. Weird!"

Mimi laughed innocently as she said this, but it wasn't strange at all — it was simply that her brother, unable to bear watching, had taken on the pain and injuries for her.
The result of being spoiled rotten: a fully finished, careless older sister who might still go tripping over things even now that she was grown.
In any case—

"So, things settle down a bit round the house since then?"

"Yeah, we're fine. Mom and big sis have calmed down too, y'know?"

"...That so."

Garfiel patted the boy's head — presumably his little brother — and looked up at the large house.
For Garfiel, it was a place that stirred up complicated feelings.

At the Thompson residence, Garek Thompson, the pillar of the household, had long been absent.
If it had simply been that he worked at the city hall and was too busy with reconstruction to come home, there would have at least been some comfort in that. But unfortunately, that wasn't the case.

Garek Thompson had lost his human form, his body now transformed into that of a black dragon.
Garfiel had already confirmed the fact himself, with his own eyes.

Though Garek had become a black dragon, his vocal organs remained intact, so conversation with him was still possible. In that respect, he was far better off than the other officials at the hall.
As for the people turned into flies, they couldn't manage so much as basic communication, let alone words. And yet, not a few believed that was actually a blessing.
After all, if it were possible to speak with those who'd been turned into flies, it wasn't hard to imagine what sort of despairing wish, borne of their transformation, they might voice.

"Hey, Gorgeous Tiger. When do you think Dad's gonna come home...?"

"—"

"He is coming back, right?"

Garfiel roughly ruffled the anxious boy's head with his palm again.
It would have been easy to just offer some baseless, encouraging words here. But even if he did, there'd be no warmth behind them.
And when he thought about what kind of wound such hollow words might leave on the boy, Garfiel couldn't bring himself to say anything so careless.

If the boy had been someone with only the thinnest of connections to him, would he have offered some irresponsible encouragement?
Was it because he wasn't such a stranger that Garfiel found himself unable to encourage him? He didn't know.

"Fred? How long are you gonna keep our guest outside—ah."

"...Yo."

As he'd been talking with the boy, a blonde girl appeared from inside the house.
She was the boy's older sister — and, by Garfiel's estimation, his own younger sister as well.

When the girl noticed Garfiel, her face lit up for a moment, before immediately twisting into a look of awkward embarrassment. Ordinarily, such quick shifts in expression might have been called cute, but right now they only offered a painful glimpse into her complicated feelings.

"Y-you came all the way out here again? Guess Gorgeous Tiger's got a lot of time on his hands."

"Yeah, well, I'm on somethin' of a break right now. Wanted to see your faces. Though if I ain't welcome, I'll clear out quick... hey."

"Garf, you gotta look people in the eye when you talk to 'em!"

At his sister's turning away her uncertain face, Garfiel too grew hesitant. But no sooner had he averted his own gaze, signaling he wouldn't force the matter, than Mimi pinched him on the side from behind.
Turning back to face the girl, sure enough, his sister looked like she was on the verge of tears.
At least, he sensed she wasn't pushing him away, telling him to leave.

"Supportin' your mom, lookin' after your little brother... bein' the big sister's tough work, huh."

"—! Yes, that's right, it is. So, um, I guess it's fine if I keep you company for a bit. One more person around doesn't change much at this point."

"Not one — there's two of us here!"

"Two more doesn't change much either!"

At the girl's red-faced shouting, the boy and Mimi both turned expectant looks on Garfiel. He wasn't cruel enough to brush off three sets of childlike hopes at once.
If anything, it was only his current state of mind holding him back — Garfiel actually loved answering to looks like these. And all the more so when it was his own little brother and sister.

"Well then, guess I'll come on in for a bit. If your mom finds it a bother though, I'll clear right on out."

"That'd never happen..."

"Not with our mom, no way."

The siblings exchanged a glance as they said this, then flashed oddly confident smiles.
And as it turned out, they were right.

* * *

"I'm sorry. You came all this way, and I haven't even properly prepared to receive you... I wish I were a little better at this sort of thing."

"Don't worry about it. It's my fault for showin' up outta nowhere anyway."

"You don't need to look so uneasy — I don't think of it as a bother at all. Having you make time to come like this is a real comfort to me."

"—!"

His carefully hidden feelings laid bare so easily, Garfiel's throat froze.
And yet Reala, who'd done this to him, held no malice whatsoever. Of course not — Reala, his mother, whether toward herself or others, had truly never known malice at all.
Even having lost her memories, that much about her hadn't changed — this was something Garfiel had come to feel more and more, the more time he spent with her.

"...Still, though, the inside of the house looks weirdly empty."

"Hmm, now that you mention it, yeah? Last time we came it was all cluttered up with stuff, but now it's all neat and tidy?"

As Garfiel idly looked around the house, Mimi, bouncing on the sofa, agreed.
Once again invited into the family's living room, Garfiel and Mimi watched as Reala, setting tea out on the table, gave a small laugh at their comments.

"Fufu, you two are quite observant. Maybe it's because I live here every day, but I hardly notice a difference..."

"That can't be right. It feels really strange to me. Mom, you just don't pay enough attention to things!"

"Sis, come on, you say that all the time, it's annoying."

"What did you say!?"

At his pouting little brother's words, his sister flew into a rage and chased after him.
Watching the siblings go clattering noisily around the house out of the corner of his eye, Garfiel silently asked with his eyes what the conversation had really meant.

"—?"

"Ah, no, I mean, what'd that mean just now? There really somethin' goin' on?"

Since asking with his eyes proved useless, he asked properly in words instead.
At that, Reala clapped her hands with an "Ah,"

"It's nothing complicated. It's just that right now, everyone in the city needs to pool their strength and support each other. ...We've given some of our household goods to people in need, let go of a bit of our savings, that sort of thing."

"...So that's why so much stuff's gone missing."

"There was a lot in this house to begin with, honestly. Less that I take good care of things, more that I've just never been able to throw anything away."

Reala stuck out her tongue in a manner rather unbecoming her age, but surely the matter wasn't so simple.
Of course, there was the pretense that everyone needed to help each other. But the Thompson household's circumstances were a bit different. After all, this family was missing its pillar — its father.
In an environment like that, if anything, they were the ones who ought to be receiving help, not giving it.

"My husband — Garek — will be back soon. I believe that. So you don't need to worry about us like that."

"—! But still..."

"I've always thought — the more you let yourself feel anxious, the more happiness slips right out of your hands. Though when I say 'always,' I only mean a little over ten years. I haven't had any memories from before that... oh, did that surprise you?"

"...Might be your go-to line, but sorry, I already heard it from your husband first."

"Ah, I see. ...Honestly, that man."

Apparently her memory loss was her go-to line for surprising people, and Reala looked a touch disappointed that it hadn't worked on him. It struck Garfiel as an awfully upbeat way to use one's amnesia, but if she'd actually caught him off guard with it, it would've been a disaster for him personally.
Even now, he felt he could take it in stride only somewhat — and that, too, was the result of time to think it over, and the help of many people along the way.

And more than anything, what struck him more than the topic of her amnesia itself was that, at its foundation, his mother's way of thinking hadn't changed at all, memory loss or not.
— Tomorrow might bring something good: that had always been the wellspring of his mother's actions.

"Garek is the one who gave these past ten-odd years to me, who had nothing before. He turned my empty self into who I am now, and gave me the chance to have such lovely children besides... so what good would it do if I couldn't believe in Garek?"

"—"

"So really, you don't need to worry about us more than necessary. I'm not putting on a brave face or anything — I truly believe in Garek. ...Honestly, if Garek didn't mind, I wouldn't have minded him coming home just as he was, either."

"Nah, even if the old man himself was fine with it, everybody else woulda stopped him."

"You think so? I thought he looked rather dashing like that, actually..."

Reala offered this bit of support for her husband, now transformed into a black dragon, in a direction entirely beside the point.
That said, only his shape had changed — Garek still retained the intelligence to converse. And yet, after talking it over as husband and wife, deciding on the cryogenic sleep had been a choice the two of them made together.
Garfiel hadn't been so crass as to eavesdrop, nor had he gone to actually witness the freezing itself. So whatever resolve Reala carried in her heart, it was hers alone, built by herself.

"...You're strong, aren't you."

"Yes, of course I am. I'm a mother to two children, after all."

Reala puffed out her chest with a proud little "hmph" and smiled. In truth, she was a mother not of two but of four children — and indeed, she was strong. Almost too strong.
It was a strength on a different plane than anything Garfiel knew, nothing to do with trading blows. The same kind that ran through Subaru and Otto — it existed within his memory-lost mother too.
Surely, right in front of him, was the very strength that Garfiel had never trained.

"Oh, that reminds me, actually there's something I wanted to ask you too, Mr. Gorgeous Tiger."

Reala suddenly clapped her hands at Garfiel, who wore a distant look in his eyes.
At her all-too-usual manner of speaking, Garfiel gave an unthinking nod, "Ah,"

"Ask away. Though honestly, I ain't been involved much in the important parts of the talks, so I don't figure I got much worth sayin'."

"No, it's not that kind of question. It's not about that at all — it's about you, Mr. Gorgeous Tiger."

"Me?"

"Yes. — Why do you look after us so much, Mr. Gorgeous Tiger? That's what I've been wondering."

Caught off guard, an unexpected blow landed square on him.
Garfiel's fangs literally trembled, and whatever resolve he'd built up cracked.

"—"

In front of him, Reala; beside him, Mimi — both waited silently for his answer.
Under both their gazes, Garfiel's thoughts spun in circles.

The moment Mimi had dragged him here, he should have already braced himself to some extent.
But now he was being made to realize that his resolve had, in the end, only ever been a vague thing.

Had he wanted to tell Reala about the memories she'd lost?
Had he at least wanted to declare himself the older brother to her two children?
Or had he only meant to offer his concern over the matter with Garek and leave quickly after?

By now, he could no longer even remember which it had been.

"I-it's just that... I got some connection to you all, and it makes me uneasy, like I can't take my eyes off ya. You're all, y'know, kinda scatterbrained, ain't ya?"

"My, how rude. Though it's true, so I can't say anything back."

"Ohh, scatterbrained? What's scattered? Oh, fur? Y'know, Mimi sheds a ton of fur during the fire season too! But in the ice season she gets all fluffy! Fun fact!"

At his stumbling cover-up, Reala and Mimi each responded in their own characteristic way.
Seeing this, blatant relief took hold of Garfiel's chest. Given the two of their personalities, this should keep any further prying at bay.
Garfiel could now, without letting this indescribable feeling show, at least get away from this spot for the time being. Time — yes, this was a matter that needed time.
A thing like this should be approached calmly, with time set aside to search for the right answer. He'd check with Frederica, with his sister, first, and then, and then—

"—Ah."

"Are you alright, Mr. Gorgeous Tiger?"

"Why..."

"I don't know why, but you suddenly looked like a lonely, helpless little child to me."

As Garfiel stood there stunned, slender white fingers touched his head.
Reala had leaned forward and was stroking Garfiel's head. The softness of her touch was terribly gentle, brimming with the same tenderness one might show a beloved child of one's own.

Memories Reala shouldn't have retained, and memories Garfiel had nearly forgotten, overlapped.
Once, surely, just like this, he had been held in Reala's — in Lisia Tinzel's — arms, his head stroked just so.
That bodily memory, from back then, revived in this very instant, and bound Garfiel's heart fast.
And before he could even think to hold it back, his emotions burst their banks.

"...Mom."

"—"

"Mom... Mom, Mom...!"

With her stroking fingers still against him, Garfiel called out to the Reala before him like that.
His eyes brimmed with tears, his voice shook, and his already small frame seemed to shrink even further — Garfiel looked utterly frail and fragile.

Of course he did.
No matter how tough a front one put up, no matter how one struggled, before a mother, everyone was just a child.
No matter how much bravado one showed a mother, it was nothing more than a child's stubbornness.

"I... I wanted to, Mom, I...!"

There was a mountain of things he wanted to say. Things he wanted to say, countless as the stars.
All those feelings he'd given up on, thinking he could never convey them, still shone brilliantly within Garfiel even now, exulting in this chance finally given to them.
He wanted to be held in his mother's arms, to be forgiven, to cry out from within that peace.

"...It's Garfiel, y'know?"

"—"

Eyes brimming with tears, gaze lowered, voice straining, Garfiel—
—beside him, Mimi suddenly spoke his name aloud. Who it was meant for, Garfiel couldn't tell.
He only heard, right in front of him, the sound of a sharply indrawn breath, and then felt the fingers on his head pull away.

"—Ah."

As the fingers drew back and his mother's warmth was lost, a weak sound escaped Garfiel's throat.
But that feeling was swiftly replaced by another.

"Garfiel, come here."

As Garfiel lifted his face, Reala stood before him, arms spread wide, smiling.
At that gesture, at those words, Garfiel's thoughts ground to a halt.
But even with his mind stopped, his body, his soul, understood exactly what to do.

"M-Mom... Mom...!"

Sobbing like a child, exactly like a child, Garfiel flung himself at Reala — at Lisia — burying his face in her chest and wringing out his voice.
A gentle, soft palm stroked the head of the sobbing Garfiel.

"There, there... Garf, good boy, good boy. You've worked so hard, all this time, haven't you."

"—! That's right! I kept pushin' myself, kept tryin' so hard! But I messed up so much, and still... everyone... !"

Unable to form real words, Garfiel went on stringing together incoherent phrases in Lisia's arms.
What poured out of him was fifteen years of Garfiel's life.

Losing his mother, being parted from his sister, standing firm so as not to lose any more of his family, and then having the following ten years shattered apart by Subaru and the others.
How many times, in all that time, had he nearly been crushed under the weight of what he carried? How many times had he grieved?
Desperate to reclaim the love he'd lost, frantic not to let go of it, how many feelings had he trampled underfoot along the way?

By rights, he should have leaned on and clung to a father and mother much, much earlier in life — but instead he'd left the wall in his heart he needed to overcome standing exactly as it was, all this time.
Knowing full well the wall was there, he'd slipped down side paths, grown used to leaving that high wall behind him unseen, and gone his own selfish way.

"...Mom..."

"It's alright, Garf. Mom's right here beside you."

Spoken to so gently, comforted by such tenderness, he let his heart lean into the maternal love he had craved but never been given.

He remembered being loved by his family.
Garfiel knew that his sister and grandmother had loved him. He knew that his mother had loved him too.
And yet, Garfiel, who had felt his sister's love, his grandmother's love, was only now, here, for the very first time, feeling his mother's love in the flesh.
Having gained what he'd only known of before, Garfiel wept and wept.

He didn't know the answer to the emotion welling up in his chest.
Its name, Garfiel did not yet know.

But the very thing he felt, the thing that shook his heart, rattled his fangs, and stirred his soul even now — that, just as it was, was the answer.
Surely it was an answer everyone else already knew — Garfiel had simply, at last, brushed it with his fingertips.

— This burning feeling, just as it was, was the answer.

* * *

"Ohh, Garf's done cryin'? Cried enough? Geez, Garf's such a crybaby!"

As the passionate fit of sobbing subsided into sniffles and Garfiel grimaced in embarrassment, Mimi opened the door and returned with the younger siblings in tow.
He hadn't even noticed when she'd left the room — apparently she'd given the sobbing Garfiel and Reala some time alone, and that consideration only made him more embarrassed.

"Gorgeous Tiger, you okay?"

"I can't believe a guy would bawl his eyes out in front of people like that. You're just like our Fred."

Even without having witnessed it directly, he'd wailed loudly enough to be heard through the whole house. Mimi had gone to the trouble of taking the siblings out, but that consideration looked like it was about to go to waste.

"...My bad."

"Hmm, for what? More than that, Mimi's more curious whether Garf's satisfied now. And also whether snacks are gonna be something sweet!"

"Ah, that so. Geez."

Laughed at with that carefree, thoughtless face, he genuinely couldn't tell anymore just how much thought had gone into her actions.
Was she really just acting purely on instinct, exactly as she appeared? If so, that instinct of hers was nothing to scoff at.

"Hm? What's up? Fall for me? Fall for me?"

"Not fallin'."

"That so—"

Even told she acted with real thought behind it, he found it hard to believe — but it was a fact that Mimi's consideration had saved him more than once.
His heart, of course, but also his life, once. As it turned out, Mimi hadn't ended up losing her life either, though that too was more a product of chance. The battle that Garfiel should rightly have reclaimed had been handed over to Wilhelm, and he'd had almost nothing to do with saving Mimi.
The debt remained a debt, unpaid, still nothing given back.

"So, how was Garf?"

"Well, why don't you ask him yourself. I'm sure he'll be just fine now... he's the Gorgeous Tiger that Gorgeous Mimi loves so much, after all."

"Well, maybe so. Garf gets it done when it counts."

With the two of them hitting it off over such an embarrassing conversation, there was nothing Garfiel could do. He simply reached out and ruffled both the head of his worried-looking little brother and his sister, who kept sneaking glances his way, to distract himself.
Touching his siblings like this, he felt an even greater fondness for the two of them than before — perhaps because the fact of being siblings, which he hadn't truly felt or accepted emotionally until now, had suddenly taken on a sense of reality.

"—"

The moment he became aware of this, another kind of anxiety sprouted up.
This time the anxiety was easy to understand, genuinely easy to understand: whether he had the qualities, the qualification, to be recognized by these two — his little brother and sister — as their older brother.

"Gorgeous Tiger?"

"H-hey, why'd you freeze up like that? I-it's not some weird illness or something, is it?"

Comparing the gazes of his little brother and sister, Garfiel thought it over.
Probably, he wasn't disliked. His little brother's good will was plain to see, and while his sister's was harder to read, it surely wasn't malice.
Besides, besides, he had, at least nominally, fought powerfully before the two of them as the Gorgeous Tiger. — Honestly, he'd shown some pathetic sides too, and it was hard to deny there'd been a moment he'd have lost without the boost of their cheering, but he'd set that aside for now.

"Garf?"

"Q-quiet. I can handle somethin' this small on my own."

Sensing Mimi was about to throw him a lifeline, Garfiel gnashed his fangs and headed her off preemptively. At his words, Mimi pouted, muttering "geez," and backed off — and once again Garfiel's brain began spinning at high speed, starting to overheat.

Declaring himself — that itself was possible, this very instant. Of course, "possible" here only meant it was achievable as an action; once the condition of his own feelings got tangled up in it, the matter changed somewhat.
No, it wasn't that he was getting cold feet, not at all, but every matter required preparation. When challenging a strong enemy, charging in without any chance of victory wouldn't win you the fight. No matter what sort of formidable foe you might run into, training daily and honing your body so that you could win — that was the effort truly needed for victory.
In other words, this obstacle too required preparation. And honestly, that preparation could hardly be said to be in place right now. So perhaps he ought to fall back for the time being — no, no, what was he saying, going soft like that—

"H-hey, are you really okay...? Your eyes are spinning and your face is bright red..."

"G-Gorgeous Tiger?"

"'Course I'm fine. Grrr."

"That's the first time I've ever heard Garf go 'grrr'!!"

An impossible verbal tic for everyday use had slipped out, and Mimi teased him for it, but he didn't even have the energy to snap back.
With his little brother and sister still worried, Garfiel's confused thoughts kept churning, his brain boiling round and round—

"Honestly, that won't do, Garfiel. Bottling everything up like that is exactly what's landed you here now."

"Ah, Mom..."

Noticing Garfiel's eyes spinning, Reala scolded him gently like that. At her words, Garfiel let the word slip out reflexively — and it was the siblings who looked utterly startled.

"Huh, why'd Gorgeous Tiger call you Mom?"

"N-no, that's not right! She's mine and Fred's mom, not yours—"

"—It's alright. Both of you, okay?"

To her startled little brother and her snapping sister, Reala spoke gently. Perhaps unable to resist their mother's words, the siblings fell reluctantly silent.
And then, looking at Garfiel, who wasn't even ready to prepare his heart, let alone actually prepared, Reala spoke.
Yes, she said this.

"Garfiel here has surely been separated from his own mother. And I suppose I must resemble her somehow. That's probably why he got so lonely just now."

"—Huh?"

"Ohh, Mom and his mom look alike?"

"W-what's that supposed to mean... hmph, so embarrassing."

At Reala's explanation, her three children each reacted in their own way. Garfiel was left dumbfounded, but from Reala's thoroughly confident manner, there was no sign of a lie or a deflection.
In other words—

"Garf, guess you fell way short on explainin', huh?"

"—"

Just as Mimi had summed it up so bluntly, that was probably about the size of it.

Despite crying so wretchedly, so unbecomingly, hurling the full extent of his emotions out into the open, Reala hadn't caught even the slightest hint of the truth behind it.
What a rash, premature move that had been, all things considered.

"'Course, I guess that's only natural."

Having no memories meant having nothing to connect the dots with.
In such a state, there was no way she could have grasped the truth just from listening to Garfiel's incoherent string of words.
All at once, the strength drained out of him — out of his fangs, out of his whole body.

"W-what... haah, what the hell."

Whether the words that spilled out of him that way were relief or disappointment, he couldn't say.
Probably it was half and half — that much, at least, only Garfiel himself was aware of.

* * *

Having had the wind taken out of his sails in various ways, with gains that felt like both something and nothing at once, it was probably time to go.
Judging as much, Garfiel left the Thompson residence together with Mimi.

"Sorry again for not being able to properly look after you."

"It's fiiine! If anything, sorry on Garf's behalf for cryin' up such a storm? That kinda thing!"

"Shut it, don't go bringin' that back up."

Garfiel grabbed Mimi by the scruff of her neck as she answered the departing Reala. Lifting her light body up with a "nya," he glanced, sighing, at Reala and the siblings clinging to her.

"You two don't gotta be so worried, either — I ain't gonna go stealin' your mom or anything."

"I think so too, but..."

"Don't let your guard down, Fred. He was after Mom all along, wasn't he. Just because Dad's away for a while doesn't mean we're handing over Mom while she's wide open like this!"

"So you do realize she's wide open, huh."

If anything, the siblings' wariness had shot up to maximum, and Garfiel put his head in his hands over it. As a result of Reala's odd explanation, the two of them had apparently both gotten the mistaken idea that Garfiel had come to steal their mother away.
He had no such intention — though, well, if his mother had actually remembered him, he'd have liked her to give it at least some thought. Thinking that, he found it hard to deny it outright, straight to their faces.

"Yeah, yeah, guess the bad guy'll make himself scarce."

"Please come again anytime. Whenever you feel like crying, I'll lend you my shoulder, any time at all."

"Ain't gonna go showin' off some pathetic side like that so easy."

"Then, only to me, who resembles your mother...?"

"Nn, guh...!"

Choking on his words, Garfiel decided to flee.
With Mimi still dangling from his grip, Garfiel turned his back on the three of them — a family he still hadn't been able to call his own out loud.
Watching Garfiel about to leave like that, Reala clapped her hands. Then, pushing the children clinging to her forward,

"Now, both of you, say a proper goodbye."

"Gorgeous Tiger, see you."

"—"

At those words, the little brother obediently complied, but the sister stayed silent.
Can't be helped, Garfiel thought. Reala, though, did not think so.

"Now, now, you see a guest off properly, don't you?"

"Mmph."

Told so by an unexpectedly stubborn Reala, the sister still wouldn't nod easily. At her daughter's obstinate stance, Reala looked just a little troubled.

"Nah, ain't nothin' to worry over."

"That won't do. Come now, big sister. Honestly — Rafi! Rafiel!"

"—"

With an exasperated look, Reala finally called her daughter's name.
The instant he heard the sound of that name ring out, Garfiel convulsed as if struck by lightning, and froze in place.

"R-Rafiel?"

"Yes, Rafiel. Come to think of it, have I never introduced her? That's her name. My two children — Rafiel Thompson, and Fred Thompson."

Rafiel and Fred.
He'd already heard the little brother's name a few times before now. That he hadn't paid it much mind — was that because Garfiel simply hadn't cared, or because he'd been afraid of what he might notice?

Rafiel, and Garfiel.
Fred, and Frederica.

Reala's two children, and Lisia's two children.
The closeness of the sounds, and the reason behind it.

"You think it's a weird name, not very girly, don't you. I'm well aware of that much myself, you know!"

Misreading the reason for his silence, the sister — Rafiel — said this with a sulky face. At that, Garfiel, who'd been reeling for a moment, shook his head, "No,"

"I think it's a good name."

"You don't have to flatter me like that..."

"—I mean it. I really, truly think it's a good name."

"Right!"

Spoken to with such heartfelt words, Rafiel was taken aback, just a little. And Reala, cutting in, said happily,

"I'm the one who named them both. For some reason, I just felt I wanted these names for them... and so."

"You named both of 'em?"

"Yes. They're good names, aren't they? — I'd always thought, if I ever had children I loved, I wanted names like these for them."

"—"

It was proof of love beyond any possible doubt.

Having lost her memories, remembering nothing of what came before, and yet never losing her kindness or generosity, this mother had passed on the love meant for the children she should have had, to the children who had actually been born to her.

"—"

Garfiel could have gotten angry about this.
He could have felt revulsion, could have shouted from the heart, could have sunk his fangs in out of sheer unreasonable feeling.
But none of that so much as crossed his mind.

In this moment, Garfiel had been overwhelmed.
By his own mother's love — Lisia Tinzel's love.
By the love of his sister and brother's mother — Reala Thompson's love.

— And so, it was already enough.

"Ha, hah! Hahahaha!!"

Laughter escaped him.
The listlessness that had lingered in his chest until just moments ago vanished.
The pathetic sense of having left things unsaid, of failing to fully convey what he should have, ending everything halfway — that too vanished.

For now, this alone was enough.
Because he could finally feel, truly feel, that the connection had been real.

"See ya then, Rafiel, Fred. I'll come again."

"—! Yeah, Gorgeous Tiger!"

"D-don't go crying again next time, got it!"

He roughly ruffled the heads of his little brother and sister.
This time, unlike before, his palm surely carried real affection in it. It hadn't been left halfway, he felt sure.

And then, last of all, Garfiel raised a hand to Reala — to his mother.

"Thanks, Mom. I'll come bother you again."

— He'd probably drop by a few more times while he remained in the city.
— And even after that was done, once he'd withdrawn back to Roswaal Manor, he'd come again.

Next time, surely, he'd bring his sister and grandmother along too.
That time, at last, he'd tell them about those ten years.

Different from before, this time with a forward-looking heart — but he wouldn't speak of it now.
Family talk was for family — that was how it felt to him now.

"Till then, take care!"

Garfiel raised his hand and was able to say it, strong and clear.

* * *

"Mom, I'm glad Gorgeous Tiger's feeling better."

"Yes, that's right. Truly... I'm glad."


"...Mom, don't you seem kind of lonely? Do you not want to part with him that badly?"

"I wonder. It's not that I don't want to part with him. Parting can be lonely, but it might be a happy thing too."


"When do you think Dad can come home?"

"I don't know. But he'll surely come back to us."


"...Mom, why are you crying?"

"— Maybe it's because I found something I'd forgotten."



"I'm sorry, but thank you. — I love you, Garf."

* * *

Still holding Mimi with one hand, Garfiel entered the general ward of the treatment house.
Among the many beds lined up in rows, Otto's bed had been placed all the way at the back, by the window.
On his way there,

"Uh, whoa—!"

"Ah! Sorry, mister! Hey, Tina, wait up!"

"Doesn't matter what you say, I'm not letting you catch me. Hurry up and come here, Lusbel!"

A boy and girl went dashing noisily through the ward. The girl wore hospital clothes; the boy looked to be visiting.
The girl, too, wore a face full of energy — at that rate, she'd likely be discharged soon. Ordinarily someone should have told them off for making such a racket in the ward, but—

"Sometimes a child's excited voice can be a comfort. So no one can quite bring themselves to scold those two for carrying on like that."

"Guess that's an easygoin' way to look at it, Otto-nii."

Seeing the children off and heading further in, Garfiel was greeted by Otto. Still with his leg painfully wrapped in bandages, Otto raised an eyebrow with an "oh?" at the sight of Garfiel bringing Mimi along.

"You've got a bit of a refreshed look on your face. Did something good happen?"

"Ah... whether it's somethin' good, that's kinda hard to say."

At Otto's question, he couldn't quite bring himself to nod and simply agree.
It had been complicated, tangled, one relentless assault on his sense of values after another. But every word, every encounter, he could now believe had turned out good in the end.

"But it was somethin' that made you happy, right?"

"Mn..."

"Garf's got a good face now! That's proof somethin' happy, somethin' like that, happened! Mimi kinda thinks that's plenty good enough! Yep!"

Still dangling in his grip, Mimi laughed her carefree, foolish laugh.
At the loudness of her voice, the eyes of everyone in the ward turned toward them, but they quickly looked away again.
The reason was the same as with the boy and girl's racket earlier.
No one could bring themselves to cut off the feelings of someone laughing so genuinely, so happily, so joyfully.

"Geez, no helpin' it, I guess."

"Ohh, Garf's laughin' too. Fall for me? Fall for me?"

"Not fallin'."

"That so."

"Not fallin'. ...But still."

An exchange they'd repeated a few times now.
At the end of it, this time, Garfiel added one more line.

Mimi's eyes went wide, and Otto watched the exchange.
To his mother, to his sister and brother, to Subaru and the others who weren't here—

"—Thanks."

Because he felt, just a little, like he'd managed to move forward.

Garfiel bared his fangs and smiled just like that.
