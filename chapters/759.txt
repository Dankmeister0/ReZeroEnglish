Arc 10 Chapter 2: "The Church's Sacred Rite"

—There once was a peculiar young man by the name of Fourier Lugunica.

When we first met he was still a boy, and even now his personal impression has barely changed since then—he was always a childish sort of person.
Bright, innocent, artless—there was no telling what he might do or say a second later, and he kept everyone around him perpetually on edge.
And yet, no matter how much he dragged people around by it, he had a charm that never let it feel unpleasant.

We'd known each other for ten years.
Ten years built up from seconds where you never knew what might happen—and a great many things happened in them.
There must have been countless times when something happened that was far too serious to simply laugh off.

And yet, looking back, even those events can somehow be told with a laugh.
And at the very center of those memories, told with a smile, was his own smile—like the sun itself.

Let me confess it without any pretense.—I loved Fourier Lugunica.

From the day we met until today, that feeling has never once wavered.
No matter what words Fourier spoke to me, what feelings he threw at me, what events we shared, what time we spent together, what future we dreamed of, what painful betrayal I suffered, or what tragedy tore my heart apart, my fondness for him has never once wavered.—Not once.

Surely, whatever the circumstance, he would never lose his smile. He would never lose hope.
In fact, right up to his very last moment, he remained noble, pure, and single-minded.

I respect that from the bottom of my heart. I revere it. I hold it dear.
And so—

"—Your Highness, please, forgive me."

Neither this decision nor this resignation is, in any way, the result of my love for you fading.

—The "Divine Dragon Church," as its name suggests, is a religious order that reveres the "Divine Dragon" Volcanica, who forged the Covenant with the kingdom, and that venerates his power and blessings.

Its founding traces back some four hundred years, to the very era of the Covenant that first gave the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica its title as the Dragon-Friendly Kingdom, so it may fairly be called an institution of proper and venerable history.
In fact, the Divine Dragon Church had always restrained the expansion of its own influence, maintaining a certain distance from the royal family and the royal castle at the kingdom's core. This was, in itself, proof that the Divine Dragon Church sought no more power than necessary, defining itself solely as a wellspring of faith.

That was why, even when the Lugunica royal family was wiped out by the plague and the Royal Selection began to determine the next monarch, the Divine Dragon Church had refrained from actively intervening in the kingdom's affairs.


"—Except that's no longer a situation I can calmly go on saying. Ever since I heard of the Witch Cult's outrage in the water gate city, my patience has been creeping toward its limit, day by day."

"—…."

"Of course, the target this anger ought to point at is the Witch Cult, who dealt so cruelly with the common people. But smoking them out and punishing them thoroughly is a task of the utmost difficulty… or rather, even if we did punish them, it wouldn't save those who were already harmed. In everything, there's such a thing as priorities."

"—…."

"Punishment must never take priority over salvation. The scripture itself says as much: 'Punishment without salvation is but empty thunder and lightning; the Dragon spreads his wings first, and cradles the smallest of lives in tender care.' That is precisely the optimal answer, in matters of humanity, that I believe in...!"

Declaring this loudly and passionately, the woman forcefully snapped shut the thick scripture she held against her chest.
She put so much force into it that one worried the bound pages might come loose. Fortunately, the book, closed with all her might, didn't fall apart, and the disaster of her passionate speech's persuasiveness plummeting straight into the ground was narrowly avoided.

"Ahem, ahem. My apologies. I got a little too worked up there. It's a habit of mine. Whether it's a good habit or a bad one, I suppose, depends on how I conduct myself from here on."

So said the woman who'd just been delivering her speech, clearing her throat with a touch of embarrassment, to the one who had been uselessly fretting over her book.
It was a kind of self-reflection that, even while acknowledging her own conduct, came across with a strangely conspicuous positivity.
There was a certain strength in declaring outright that whether it turned out good or bad depended on her own conduct going forward, and it was a way of thinking one couldn't help but find likable.—Though it was, admittedly, a rather complicated sort of likable.

"—…."

The one he stole a glance at was a beautiful woman clad in a nun's habit.
Having introduced herself as a nun of the Divine Dragon Church, she had first held talks with the Council of Wise Men at the royal castle before coming to visit this Karsten family villa in the royal capital.

The purpose of her visit was a startling one, and the shock of it had yet to fade. But the shock she'd brought was not confined to her stated purpose alone.

A nun with long golden hair and a strong-willed light burning in her red eyes. Her physical features—and, above all, her name, given as Filore—had dealt this old body a shock to rival even the purpose of her visit.

"…I hesitate to say this, since it makes me sound rather self-conscious, but being stared at like that does leave me flustered. …I am flustered. Quite a lot, actually."

"—. My apologies. I was somewhat discourteous."

"No, it's not that I mean to reprimand you. It's only that, as an unworthy youth, I can't help but feel a certain awe... trepidation... a sense of physical peril?—well, in any case, something that sounds a bit better than that, in the face of the gaze of the one they call the 'Sword Devil.'"

To Filore, who so graciously overlooked the rudeness of his stare, the man called by that epithet—the "Sword Devil," Wilhelm—bowed quietly, putting his gratitude into the gesture.
The gratitude carried two intentions: an apology for the stare that had made her shrink back, and one for keeping her waiting so long. After all, more than an hour had already passed since Filore had arrived at this mansion.

In that time, Wilhelm had been unable to find any small talk to fill the silence, leaving Filore—who disliked the quiet—to go hunting for topics as she had at the start. And since she kept refilling her tea to cover her own awkwardness, she was already onto her sixth cup.

That said, the true reason the conversation refused to take off wasn't simply Wilhelm's poor way with words. The greater reason was the double shock Filore's visit had delivered, which had shaken him deeply within and stripped him of his composure.

Indeed, just how was he supposed to treat Filore? As if seeking an answer, Wilhelm glanced past her, toward the figure standing by the wall.
It was the tall, powerfully built man who had accompanied Filore to this mansion—

"—Captain of the Royal Guard, Marcos Gildark."

"Sir."

Called by name by Wilhelm, the large man in silver armor, who had kept his silence, straightened his already-upright spine further as he answered.
The reply was but a single word, yet it carried the sense of a great boulder shifting, and within its weighty tone there was an unmistakable reverence.
At the consideration packed into that tone, Wilhelm shook his head slowly,

"Please, there's no need to be so formal. It's been more than ten years since I served as Captain of the Royal Guard. I'm just an old man now, not fit to be compared with you, who leads the kingdom's knights today."

"An old man? Surely you jest."

So Marcos judged Wilhelm's remark, a faint smile rising on his craggy features.
It seemed to be taken as modesty, but it was, in truth, Wilhelm's honest self-assessment. He trained diligently to reclaim the sharpness of his swordsmanship from days past, but his aged body was slow to yield the results he hoped for.
At this rate, he was nowhere near fulfilling the reason an old soldier refuses to let go of his sword.

"At the very least, I seem to have recovered enough that the current Captain of the Royal Guard takes it for a joke. I suppose that much, at least, could be called good fortune."

Naturally, whoever was appointed Captain of the Royal Guard was among the kingdom's foremost talents.
Wilhelm had no intention of praising himself, but just as he himself had once been, Marcos too possessed skill worthy of that position—one of the finest warriors in its history, in Wilhelm's estimation. Overshadowed as he was by Wilhelm, whose exploits drew far too much attention, and by Reinhard, the strongest Sword Saint in history, it was nonetheless Marcos's own strength that accounted for the current knighthood's formidable might.

For that reason as well, Wilhelm rated Marcos as the most fitting man for the post among all the captains the knighthood had ever had. Though, of course, praise from a man like Wilhelm—who had abandoned his duty, prioritizing private grudge over loyalty—was probably worthless drivel as far as Marcos was concerned.

In any case, what Wilhelm wanted to discuss here and now was not some exchange of hardship stories between captains separated by a generation. It was a matter far more grave and serious.
It was this—

"—Am I to assume the Council of Wise Men already has a full grasp of the situation?"

"Of course. We regard it as an extraordinary situation indeed."

"That is… inevitable, I should think."

Even at Wilhelm's confirmation, its subject left unspoken, Marcos did not feign ignorance and ask what he meant.
Whether that answer was owed more to his character or to his position was unclear, but for Wilhelm, it confirmed that the Council of Wise Men had tasted the same shock he had, and a sigh slipped from him.

"The very same name as Lady Filore Lugunica, daughter of Prince Ford Lugunica, the king's younger brother."

The murmur that spilled out with that sigh carried an emotion that was neither relief nor lament, and Wilhelm found he couldn't sort out his own feelings. And as noted before, this wasn't unique to Wilhelm—it held true for the Council of Wise Men and everyone else who had taken part in the discussions at the royal castle.

For a girl had suddenly appeared bearing the same physical traits of the Lugunica royal family, and even the same name, as Filore Lugunica, who had vanished without a trace fifteen years ago.

"—…."

Stealing glances at Filore, who was taking a sip of her seventh cup of tea as if to cover her own restlessness, Wilhelm searched her profile for some trace of the royal family as he remembered them.
There was only an ambiguous impression—one that could pass either as present, if he said it was there, or absent, if he said it wasn't.
To begin with, the princess had vanished when she was still an infant, so if she had in fact survived, just how she might have grown up was pure speculation. —Still, if her origins were indeed what Wilhelm and the others suspected, it was a grave matter that would send shockwaves through the entire kingdom.

It could threaten the very continuation of the Royal Selection itself, and depending on the Divine Dragon Church's intentions, it might even be called a seed of calamity capable of splitting the kingdom apart.
What that seed might sprout, and what kind of flower it might bloom into, was beyond anyone's power to imagine.

Even so, there was one thing that could already be stated with certainty.
Filore's existence, and the proposal she brought as a representative of the Divine Dragon Church, would deeply affect two of the Royal Selection camps. —And it goes without saying that one of them was the Crusch Camp, to which Wilhelm himself belonged.

"—Lady Crusch."

Closing his eyes, Wilhelm thought of the liege who had extended a saving hand to him.
One of the Royal Selection candidates, and the gifted heir to the ducal House Karsten, unrivaled among the kingdom's talents—she was, to Wilhelm, who had spent his life chasing the White Whale that had slain his wife, an irreplaceable benefactor. Yet the reason he still believed, unwaveringly, that she was the one fit to be queen wasn't owed to that debt of gratitude alone.

Crusch's bearing, her character, her very way of being—values and conduct honed and fierce like a polished blade—those were the true reasons Wilhelm could wish, from the bottom of his heart, to see her ascend to the throne.

Even when the demonic hand of "Gluttony" fell upon her and stole away her memories, it could not strip from within her that earnest nobility, that unwavering sincerity.
That, he could believe, was the very radiance of the soul she had been born with.
That further calamity should strike such a liege was thus a bitter regret to Wilhelm, a bitter experience that thrust upon him a sense of helplessness fit to tear his body apart.

But being able to regret his own mistakes, to lament his errors of judgment and his own lack of strength, was still better than nothing, he supposed.

"—Ferris."

The next name to leave his lips was, as far as Wilhelm knew, that of the valiant knight who had spent the days since the Royal Selection began plagued by the strongest regret and helplessness of anyone.

"—…."

The reason Wilhelm now kept his guests, Filore and Marcos, waiting was, in fact, none other than Ferris. While Wilhelm and the others passed their stagnant time in the parlor, Ferris was spending time alone with Crusch, just the two of them, in her bedroom.
Just how anguished, how full of suffering that time must be, Wilhelm ground his teeth to think.

It was a hardship that made him want to pray, if only he could take Ferris's place, that he would.
But Wilhelm could not serve as his substitute. —No, not just Wilhelm; no one else could take over Ferris's role now, either.
A decision only Ferris could make, a choice permitted to no one but Ferris, lay there waiting.

Wilhelm, Filore, and Marcos alike had no choice but to simply wait, quietly, for that decision and that choice to be made.
There was no need to grow impatient. Everyone sensed it wouldn't take very long.
And in fact, that was exactly how it went.

"—Sorry to keep you waiting."

Saying so in a pain-wracked voice, Ferris stepped into the parlor.
He had surely made the effort. And yet there were tear tracks on his pale cheeks, and his wavering yellow eyes still bore, thick and unmistakable, the traces of a conflict he couldn't wipe away.
Seeing his thin shoulders slumped, his cat ears and tail—marks of his Demi-Human ancestry—drooping and worn out with exhaustion, Wilhelm hesitated over what words to offer him.

He couldn't ask something as hollow as "How did it go?" Then what, "It's not your fault"? Just what face could he possibly wear to say such a thing? And if he tried to say something like "It couldn't be helped," he'd want to cut off his own head.

There were no words he could say. Every word imaginable would become a blade to the Ferris of this moment. Silence—the one thing he could choose from among them—was the shortest, least cruel blade of all.

"I won't make an issue of how much time it took. I believe I understand the weight of the responsibility you must bear. …Though I'm sure it's nothing more than a small comfort."

"…Yeah. But a small comfort helps. Right now, I'm at my limit."

In place of the silent Wilhelm, it was Filore who had spoken, and Ferris answered her.
His usual playful, mischievous banter was nowhere to be heard, and his weak smile had the fragility of some confectioner's sugar-craft ornament, as if it would crumble at the slightest touch.

Surely she felt that keenly, and yet Filore pressed on with her words.
As if trying to postpone the moment when Ferris, looking ready to break at any second, might finally shatter—

"Let me hear your answer. —Your answer."

Gently extending a hand, Filore fixed her red eyes on Ferris and asked. Meeting that gaze head-on, Ferris's thin lips trembled.
A hesitation rose in him, but it had no power to hold back the devoted love of the "Blue"—

"—Please. I'm not enough. Save Crusch-sama."

That was the answer of Felix Argyle, the "Blue," first knight to Crusch Karsten, given to the hand the Divine Dragon Church—Filore—had extended.

"—Is that for real?"

The news—that a countermeasure was being applied to the malevolent poison tormenting Crusch, who had been brought to the Karsten family's villa in the royal capital—reached them just as Emilia was enjoying a brief moment of peace, having exchanged promises of friendship with Felt, who had come to visit the royal residence.

"I can't say anything for certain, but it seems there was a discussion at the royal castle involving the Divine Dragon Church. From what I gather, the Church apparently has some kind of countermeasure for Duke Karsten's body, afflicted as it is by the Sin Archbishop of Lust."

"Some kind of countermeasure… does that mean they can heal Crusch-san's body? Some way other than what happened to Subaru's hand turning all black?"

"Unfortunately, I don't know the details…"

"Then there's no time to sit around dawdling. —Reinhard, go on over to the castle and find out what they were talking about."

"Understood."

While Emilia stood bewildered by the report from Otto, who had returned after brewing tea, Reinhard, following Felt's swiftly decided order, dashed off toward the castle at once.
Watching Reinhard's figure shrink into the distance as he leapt out the window, Emilia murmured under her breath, "The Divine Dragon Church…" and then said,

"They're the people who believe in Volcanica reeeally strongly, right? But I thought they were supposed to stay away from the castle, so as not to get involved in the kingdom's politics."

"And yet they went and broke their own rule to show their faces at the castle. …If it means they can do something about Crusch's condition, that's good news, but if that's the case, they ought to have said so sooner."

"There's no telling what the Divine Dragon Church has in mind. As it stands, this is still unconfirmed information. Still—"

In the midst of a conversation growing thick with confusion, Otto fell silent, as if lost in thought. Seeing this, Emilia tilted her head and said, "Otto-kun?" and he shook his head.

"No, it's not something I should say while nothing's confirmed yet. For now, let's wait for Reinhard-san to come back."

"…Really? All right. If you feel like saying it, be sure to let me hear it."

"Whether Greenie here feels like saying it or not, that Reinhard bastard won't make us wait long. Man, this is aggravating."

Arms crossed, tapping her toes against the floor, Felt muttered that in impatience.
Beside Felt, who was gazing out the window, Emilia gently drew her head close, her thoughts searching not toward the castle where Reinhard had gone, but toward Crusch's mansion in that same noble district.

Crusch, whose safety she hadn't been able to confirm since they'd parted ways in Priestella. A guilty ache throbbed in her chest at not having brought back any immediately useful good news from the Pleiades Watchtower. If, as Otto had overheard, the Divine Dragon Church really could save Crusch—

"It's completely fine if it's not us who does it. So…"

"—There it is!"

Felt's spirited voice drowned out Emilia, who had begun to murmur as though in prayer.
Just as he had leapt off toward the castle, Reinhard returned to the royal residence in a single bound. He landed quietly before Emilia and the others, who rushed to the window, taking care not to disturb the garden lawn.
And then—

"Felt-sama, this is an urgent matter. Would you come with me to the castle?"

"Ah? Why the castle? More importantly, what about what we were just talking about…"

"—Felt-sama."

Cutting off Felt's words, Reinhard called her name, short and sharp. Sensing the edge in his manner, Felt narrowed her red eyes, clicked her tongue with a "tch," and said,

"Fiiine. I'll go to the castle. What about you and the others, Emilia?"

"Otto's report has been confirmed. It seems the Divine Dragon Church did indeed head to Crusch-sama's mansion. As for that—"

"We'll go there. I can't just sit still and do nothing!"

Hearing that Otto's information had been correct, Emilia struck her own chest firmly.
She was terribly worried about Crusch, but if Felt had to go to the castle, then Emilia meant to carry that worry for her too, and go check in on Crusch herself.
Of course, there was a fairly high chance she'd simply be turned away amid all the commotion, but—

"That's only if you go to visit, I'd think. Leave this to us. …It seems like we might need to head to the castle later ourselves as well, hm?"

"Yeah. Better to think of it that way. Felt-sama."

"I get it without you having to call me that a dozen times."

Snorting at Reinhard as he held out his hand, Felt then hopped lightly up onto the windowsill, and before leaping into his arms, turned back and said "Emilia,"

"The night before Priestella got torn up, I promised Crusch I'd talk with her more. So…"

"—! Right, leave it to me! I'll make sure to tell Crusch-san how much you were worrying about her!"

"Heh. I do wonder about turning the whole Royal Selection into one big pile of friends, though."

Shrugging at Emilia's forceful assurance, Felt leapt into Reinhard's arms. Catching her body with reverent care, Reinhard adjusted his hold on her, flashed a glance at Emilia and the others for just a moment—and then, in the next instant, leapt away once more, heading straight for the castle.

"Good grief, he's really beyond the norm. Let's hurry along on foot, like the humans we are."

"If I really tried, maybe I could carry Otto-kun and go boing! off somewhere too…"

"Please, save that for Natsuki-san, not me. —Let's hurry."

"Right!"

Nodding at Otto's urging, Emilia hurried too, dashing out into the streets of the royal capital.
Their destination was the Karsten estate—the same path she had once rushed down before, worried for Crusch and the others attacked and wounded on the road back from the White Whale hunt, now run faster, more impatiently than before.
And then—

"—Ferris!"

Rushing to the spot out of breath, Emilia, the instant she caught sight of Ferris kneeling as if in prayer, found herself calling out to him in a voice like a scream.
At the sound of Emilia's voice, his flaxen cat ears trembled, and, hesitantly, Ferris turned around.

"…Emilia-sama?"

"Yes, that's right. Not everything, but I heard what happened. I'm sorry I'm late getting here."

At Ferris's weak voice, Emilia felt a keen, aching sorrow that made the depths of her chest creak.
Ferris, who was always so cute, so full of lively spirit—and yet, meeting him again like this after so long, he looked so painfully fragile, so ephemeral, as if he might vanish altogether.
Wanting only to hold him back from that, Emilia wrapped her arms around his slender body without hesitation.

"—You're gonna get tears and snot all over you."

"That's nothing at all. I don't mind one bit. Leaving Ferris alone over something like that would hurt so, so much more."

"—!"

Careful not to hold him too tightly, Emilia prayed that the warmth of her body, and her feelings of concern—for the Ferris before her, and for Crusch—would somehow reach him.
Behind Emilia and Ferris, treading over the carpet, came Wilhelm, who had received them. Rather than turn away Emilia and the others, who had come rushing to the mansion in a fluster, Wilhelm had let them inside just like this.

Otto, arriving a little behind Emilia, glanced over at Wilhelm and said,

"It's a bit rich of me to say this after showing up uninvited, but was this all right? In a situation like this…"

"This is my own decision to make, but precisely because of the situation, yes. Right now, having even one more person who can pray from the heart for Crusch-sama's safety is a source of strength."

"…Indeed, on that point, Emilia-sama's power is likely considerable."

Otto and the others exchanged some such words behind her, but none of it reached Emilia's ears. Right now, she wanted to pour her entire being into the Ferris in her arms. Holding his weakly, painfully trembling body, Emilia gently stroked his back and said,

"Ferris, Crusch-san is…"

"—Inside. She's in that room, right now, getting treated…"

"—…."

What Ferris, held tight in her embrace, indicated with his gaze was the door before which he'd been kneeling in prayer—the place which, in Emilia's memory, was the bedroom.
Almost certainly, it was the room where Crusch had been laid to rest, and where her treatment was now underway.
And yet, the reason Ferris was out here, outside the room, was—

"—It's a sacred rite of the Divine Dragon Church, one that must never be shown outside."

Emilia was startled that the huge frame standing silently in the corridor, like some ornament, had been the one to give her that answer. Looking closer, she realized he was the Captain of the Royal Guard, whom she'd seen many times at the royal castle.
Had the Captain of the Royal Guard, too, come rushing here out of worry for Crusch? Perhaps he and Ferris were close, and that was the connection that had brought him here.
In any case—

"A sacred rite… and that can save Crusch-san?"

"I—I don't know… but, but there's no other way… b-because I'm, I'm useless…!"

"That's not true...!"

She wanted to say it loudly—that it wasn't true. In fact, the Emilia of not so long ago would surely have blurted it out without thinking twice.
But now she understood that saying it thoughtlessly, carried along on nothing but momentum, wouldn't truly encourage Ferris, or comfort him, in any real sense.

Ferris was amazing. Truly, remarkably, he possessed a special, deeply gentle power.
But that power, which Emilia respected from the bottom of her heart, was one Ferris couldn't put to use for the sake of Crusch, the person he treasured most. In the face of a reality so truly painful, so agonizing, so utterly beyond help, he was shedding tears and trembling like this—there was no way she could say something without thinking, not now.

"It's all right. It's all right."

So Emilia swallowed the words she'd been about to say, and gently embraced Ferris instead. She kept holding him. She kept holding him, and kept staying close by his side.
Because that was the thing that had made her happiest, back when she'd been freezing with the helplessness of being able to do nothing at all—so now, in the same way, she did it for him.

"—Hang in there, Crusch-san."

Supporting Ferris as he sobbed and shook, Emilia prayed.
Behind Emilia and Ferris, huddled together and praying for Crusch's safety, she could tell that Otto, Wilhelm, and the Captain of the Royal Guard were praying too.

Someone from the Divine Dragon Church, whose face she didn't even know, was fighting hard on the other side of that door.
Fighting to save Crusch, who had fought for the sake of others and ended up so terribly wounded for it.

Praying, praying, praying, that those efforts would bear fruit, and then, at last—

"—You may come in now."

At the voice that suddenly came from beyond the door, Emilia's head snapped up as if jerked by a string.
At the words spoken in that utterly exhausted woman's voice, Ferris flinched in her arms, and looked up at Emilia's face as though unable to believe it.
His single-minded prayer cut off so abruptly, Ferris's mind hadn't yet caught up with reality.

"Can you stand?"

"Y-yes, I ca—I can stand…"

Standing up ahead of him, Emilia lent Ferris her arm and helped him to his feet. His slender knees trembled unsteadily, but with his round eyes fixed on the door, Ferris let out a breath and, step by step, pressed forward.
Supporting Ferris's shoulder as he did so, Emilia too moved toward the door. And, in place of Ferris—whose whole body wouldn't stop trembling—she turned the door's handle.

The door swung open with a sound, and beyond it lay the bedroom. There, on the bed placed in the center of the room, a woman lay resting, and her form was—

"—Ah."

A hoarse breath escaped him, and with the gait of someone walking through a dream, Ferris stepped forward. His footing was so unsteady it seemed it might tangle and pull him down at any moment.
But that didn't happen. Unconsciously shaking off Emilia's supporting hand, Ferris rushed toward the bed—toward Crusch lying there—with a force that looked almost like collapsing forward.

"Crusch-sama… Crusch… sama… Crusch-samaaa…!"

Listening to that strained, tearful voice call out again and again for the one he loved, Emilia too advanced to the middle of the room, and looked at Crusch, at whom Ferris was gazing.
Crusch's beauty, though it had never gone without daily care even through this long stretch of bedrest, looked haggard now, her skin pallid like that of the sick. —And yet the malevolent poison that had spread like roots across her face, her neck, her body, was nowhere to be found on any visible patch of her skin.

"Prayers, they do reach."

"—You are…"

When Emilia, standing beside Ferris as he clung to the bed, turned around, she saw, on the far side of the bed, a golden-haired woman sitting on the floor with her legs stretched out.
She was the one from the Divine Dragon Church who had tried the method to save Crusch. Her breathing was heavy with exhaustion, and sweat poured from her brow, but the woman let her lips curve into a smile all the same, and said,

"The scripture says as much: 'The salvation of one is achieved through the prayers of the many. It is in the sharing that the Dragon's grace becomes greatest of all.'"

"—Thank you."

"Something this small was easy as pie. …It was only natural, of course."

The woman, who had put on a bold smile, immediately tightened her expression and corrected herself. Thanking her from the bottom of her heart for that effort, Emilia crouched down and put an arm around Ferris's shoulders.
Supporting the trembling, weeping Ferris, she rejoiced, with her whole heart, that their prayers had reached.

"Crusch-san, once you wake up, there's sooo much I want to talk to you about. There's a message from Felt-chan, too, and I've got lots to say myself."

With every trace of that dreadful poison erased from her face, Crusch slept on, her eyes closed. Emilia spoke to that sleeping face, longing for the moment she would wake.

So much she wanted to talk about, once Crusch opened her eyes. Among it, there was even a selfish request or two from Emilia, who had gotten a taste for such things thanks to Anastasia and Felt.
And so—

"Thank you, sooo, sooo much, for fighting so hard."

So Emilia said, softening the corners of her eyes, thanking everyone gathered there.

"—…."

Having witnessed with his own eyes the sacred rite of the Divine Dragon Church take effect, Otto watched Ferris clinging to his liege as she lay on the bed, and Emilia supporting him.

"Crusch-sama, thank goodness… thank goodness…"

Wilhelm too, seeing the same sight, was overcome with emotion, his voice trembling.
It was proof that even he—known as the "Sword Devil," famed for his legendary swordsmanship—had been aching over his inability to help his liege through her time of agony, wearing his heart down all the while.
It was nothing to compare with Ferris, the first knight, who had devoted years of loyalty to her—but even so, one could tell that relief and gratitude were soaking deep into Wilhelm's chest.

And that was, to no small degree, true of Otto as well.
Just moments before this, as he'd happened to reflect on with Reinhard, the shock that Priscilla's death had brought had struck Otto too. That being so, he felt a genuine relief at seeing Crusch—who had suffered such deep wounds in that battle in the water gate city—saved as well.

It was good. It was truly, truly good.
And at the same time, he became certain of a possibility he couldn't let Emilia hear.

It was this—

"—Duke Crusch Karsten's Royal Selection ends here."

That, at the very least, was a certainty whispered only within his own mouth, one that needed to be spoken to no one—not here, not now, in this place where Crusch's safety and recovery were being wept over with joy.
