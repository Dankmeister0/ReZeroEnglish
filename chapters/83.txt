Arc 3 Chapter 7: "The Orange-Haired Girl"


Whether to head toward the voice or not — Subaru agonized over it for only a few seconds.

Even Subaru himself wasn't sure what had tipped the scales.

Maybe it was the loneliness of being lost that made him crave human contact, or maybe it was that contrary streak in him that couldn't resist rubbernecking the moment trouble reared its head.

Or perhaps it was because, back in that first loop repeated so many times over, the silver-haired girl had never let a scene like this pass her by.

Grimacing at the bad feeling creeping over him, Subaru stepped forward anyway — and stumbled right into the scene.

"Quit screwing around, woman! Want me to smash that pretty face of yours in, huh!?"

"Now, now, no need to shout so, lowborn cur. A man lacking in breeding picks his quarrels just as gracelessly."

Against the man spraying spittle in his fury, that voice rang out clear and cool as glass — and laced with venom.

Peering carefully around the corner, just his head poking out, Subaru cautiously took in the scene.

Ahead, in the narrow alley, a girl stood surrounded by three men who'd blocked off every avenue of escape.

Subaru nearly sighed at the sheer predictability of yet another thug event, but what seized his attention far more than that was the girl's appearance — one wildly out of place for the setting.

Her vivid orange hair shone like it had captured the sun itself, gathered up with a barrette and let fall down her back. Her gorgeous crimson dress would have shown its full beauty at a ball or some noble's tea party, but here, in a grimy, uneven back alley, it looked utterly out of place.

The jewelry adorning her neck, ears, and fingers was, even to an untrained eye, obviously first-rate, piece after piece of it — an ensemble from head to toe that could have emptied Subaru's purse a hundred times over.

And yet, draped in all that resplendent finery, the girl's own beauty didn't lose to any of it — if anything, it stood leagues above.

Sharp, challenging red eyes tilted upward at the corners. Pale pink lips set against skin as white as untouched snow. A beauty so complete that any artist would snap their brush in despair — and staring at its living proof, Subaru was reminded all over again just how absurd this other world could be.

Surrounding a girl like that, the men bared their violent tempers and roared at her without restraint.

The girl stared back, utterly unruffled. There wasn't a trace of forced bravado in her manner — yet it seemed equally unlikely that such a slight frame hid the strength to fend them off.

Or rather, even if it did, until proven otherwise Subaru should assume it didn't.

Arms crossed, lifting her ample chest as she did, the girl held herself with utter composure. The men, reading that gesture as further proof of her insolence, were visibly working themselves up all on their own —

"—H-hey there! Sorry to keep you waiting!"

— and on impulse, hand raised, Subaru found himself bowing and scraping his way right into the middle of it.

With all four pairs of eyes present — three thugs and the girl — fixed squarely on him, Subaru raised a hand in the classic sideways-chop gesture of apology to the thugs first.

"Looks like there's been a bit of trouble, but do me a favor and let it slide, yeah? I mean, you can probably tell just by looking — this girl's, uh, a little touched in the head. You know how it is, right?"

Strutting through the seedy underbelly of a city whose public safety left much to be desired, dressed like a walking invitation to be robbed blind — if that wasn't the behavior of someone not quite right in the head, what was?

Conveniently forgetting that he himself had lost his mind — and his way — over the chance to pet a beastfolk, Subaru chirped a cheery "Well, that's that!" at the taken-aback men and grabbed the girl's hand from behind.

"Hm."

"C'mon, let's get going before we cause these gentlemen any more trouble. We've got that promise today, remember — feeding each other sweets, saying 'ahh' and everything..."

Spinning out a recast version of his fantasy itinerary with Emilia, Subaru tried to hustle the girl away from the scene at a brisk pace. But —

"How presumptuous."

"—Wha—?"

— the arm he'd taken was seized from above by another hand, and with one twisting motion his whole body's angle was forcibly rearranged. Bent over, spun through what felt like a grinding pivot of the hips—

"Do not touch me."

— the wrist he'd been gripping slipped free, and the very next instant he felt a palm come to rest against the back of his head. A powerful force bore down on it, and helpless to resist, his face was slammed straight into the wall.

"Gerguhh!?"

"Honestly, the moment I set foot outside it's always the same thing. Men come flocking after my body like flies. Truly, one cannot let one's guard down for a single moment."

Looking down at Subaru as he rolled on the ground clutching his throbbing face, the girl spat those words out as though regarding a piece of trash. Half in tears from the pain of his nose grinding against stone, Subaru glared up at her from below, and driven by a surge of fury, hauled himself back to his feet.

"Hey, read the room! That was Textbook Pattern A — 'rescue the girl from the thugs'! Was not getting my intentions recognized part of the template too!?"

"I haven't the faintest idea what you're on about. I merely acted as I willed, exactly as I saw fit."

"A woman who slams a total stranger's face into a wall on first meeting isn't just 'strong-willed,' that's a whole other level!?"

Not only had his intent to rescue her failed entirely to land — he'd been treated like a groper and personally subjected to a punisher's finishing move by the victim herself. Had he not had a constitution resistant to nosebleeds, or had his teeth stuck out even slightly more than average, catastrophe would have been unavoidable.

Face twisted with pain and humiliation, Subaru belatedly noticed the men had gone silent and were watching him. Having just put on a spectacularly pathetic show in front of them, he propped the bag of Appa he'd dropped during the face-smashing back up against the wall, and said—

"What, go on, laugh. I swoop in trying to look cool and save the day, and not only does she not even notice, I get my face crushed for the trouble — that's peak lame, so go on, laugh your asses off, huh!?"

"Nah, man, even we can't laugh at that."

At the man's words, thick with pity, Subaru's shoulders sagged — he'd have honestly felt better if they'd just laughed it off.

Then, letting out a long breath to settle himself, he squared up as if to start over.

"Take two! All right, you villains! Now that I'm here, I won't let you have your way! If you want to get past, you'll have to go through me first. Heh, you know, once this fight's over I'm gonna open a bakery back home. Got a promise to marry my childhood friend and everything!"

Firing himself up with the bravado of his own lines, Subaru laid into the men with a bold declaration.

Shielding the girl behind him, he watched the men warily for their next move. Three against one — if he could land the first surprise strike, he had a chance — or so he'd gotten as far as thinking, when—

"Huh, wait, don't I recognize you guys?"

Tilting his head, he compared the faces of the three men before him against his memory, and then, remembering, clapped his hands together.

"Oh, it's Ton, Chin, and Kan! Wait, seriously? Are you three the only thugs in the whole royal capital?"

Of course they looked familiar — these were the bumbling trio he'd nicknamed Ton, Chin, and Kan, the ones who'd hassled him on the very first day he'd been summoned. Given that he'd once had his life taken by their hands, a flicker of tension gripped him —

"— but honestly the feeling of my strength just draining out of me is stronger. Seriously, what is with you guys, do you do this every single time? Everyday? One day? Summer vacation?"

"He just suddenly butted in, smacked his head, and now he's spouting nonsense."

"Hey, I really don't wanna deal with this guy, you go."

"I don't wanna either. Maybe if we just shove him around a little he'll wander off?"

Whether wary or simply deflated, the men huddled together and started muttering that sort of consultation at the sight of Subaru's spineless, half-hearted face, devoid of any real fighting spirit.

Just as the men seemed on the verge of losing their will to fight entirely, the girl — who'd taken no part whatsoever in the exchange — cut in with an "Oi."

"I find no amusement in watching a conversation dawdle along so hesitantly. Girls, are you? Very well then — if that's so, mind your appearance and show me a form worthy of my admiration. Your figures, all sinew and needless body hair — how positively lovely they'll be. Comical, no doubt."

Hand resting at her lips, the girl hurled an insult steeped without restraint in mockery and contempt.

For a moment, all four of them — the three thugs plus Subaru — didn't quite process what she'd said, but once the meaning sank in a beat later, all of them boiled over at once.

"The hell d'you think you're saying, you damn woman!"

"Who's a girl, huh!? Don't screw with me!"

"Running your mouth all high and mighty, who the hell do you think you are, huh!?"

"Seriously, don't push your luck! Want us to show you exactly whether we're girls or not, using that absurdly sexy body of yours however we please!?"

A chorus of graceless retorts burst out, the four of them — thugs plus one — now indistinguishable from one another.

Of them all, that last remark was steeped in a nastiness bound to stir up trouble later. And the person who'd shouted that final line was, in fact, Subaru — his shoulders heaving with ragged breath.

"Oh no. What am I even doing, lumping myself in with Ton, Chin, and Kan..."

He was honestly startled at his own fence-sitting.

Honestly, the girl's attitude was bad enough that the thugs' anger was more than justified. But that didn't mean it was fine for the men to subject her to that kind of degradation just because her mouth was foul.

"I feel for you guys, I really do, but I can't go back on my principles now. And it's not like I don't have my own grudge against you, either. If you're gonna resent anyone, resent the you from that first day."

"That stuck-up woman's one thing, but what the hell even are you, kid."

Scratching his head, Ton spat that out, clearly finding Subaru's presence hard to deal with. It seemed there was, after all, no memory of Subaru in their minds.

Never mind that they'd been thrashed by Emilia's magic, gotten utterly demolished by Subaru three-on-one, and later been the perpetrators of a random attack against him — after everything that had happened between them, it was awfully cold of them to forget.

"Well, I guess none of those events actually happened in this timeline. If there's anything these guys would actually remember... it'd just be a handsome guy showing up in the nick of time, probably."

That, after all, was the outcome that had wrapped up most peacefully — the one where Subaru's history with them ran shallowest.

Since it amounted to both sides having avoided involving themselves with the other, it was only natural that Subaru had left no impression on them.

So Subaru was musing, half-savoring a faint touch of loneliness at the thought, when—

"Hey, wait, I remember now. This guy, a few days back in an alley on Merchant Street..."

"—! That's the crazy kid from back then! He hasn't changed a bit!"

"It's true! I didn't recognize him 'cause he's dressed different!"

Chin snapped his fingers as it clicked, shouting it out, and Ton and Kan followed suit, digging up the memory one after another. Letting slide the fact that a slightly troubling word had just been dropped in there, Subaru clapped his hands, glad they'd finally remembered.

"Right, so, that's the thing — you and I aren't exactly strangers, see. So how about you let this one slide, for my sake?"

"Are you an idiot? We're not 'not strangers,' we're enemies. All that's changed is three-on-one became three-on-two."

"One more and the balance of power evens right out. Neither of us wants to get hurt here, right?"

"Correction. Not three-on-two — three-against-one-against-one."

"Would you just be quiet for one second!?"

Even as he tried, through pure bluster, bluff, and desperate improvisation, to somehow smooth things over peacefully, the girl ignored his intentions entirely and pressed her own agenda.

If only he could time-leap back five minutes, he'd love to walk up and clap the shoulder of his past self peeking around the corner and tell him, "Seriously, don't even bother." But that, like everything else, was too late now.

After all, Subaru knew from firsthand experience that these were not patient men to begin with.

He could feel their demeanor shifting at last, no longer simple anger but something laced with a cold, sharp edge — the killing intent that had, ever since he'd come to this other world, become all too familiar.

It was only a matter of time before each of them reached into his coat and drew out the blood-scented weapon that fit so familiarly in his hand.

"...Guess it can't be helped. I really didn't want to resort to this."

Judging that a clash was now unavoidable, Subaru shook his head, ashamed of his own immaturity.

Unable to talk his way through the situation alone, forced as always into pulling out his trump card in a knife's-edge crisis — just once, he'd like to breeze through one of these with room to spare.

"Stop right there, all of you. Take this any further and it stops being a joke."

Palm raised toward the three of them, Subaru tried to hold back the men who looked ready to turn violent at any moment. Chin, the most short-tempered of the three, went red with a bulging vein at Subaru's words.

"Ah? Don't go getting the wrong idea. We're on top, you're on the bottom. Don't you go mixing that up and start giving orders."

Not bothering to hide his murderous intent, Chin moved to draw a knife from inside his coat.

Before his hand could clear it, Subaru stepped forward, thrust his face right up in front of Chin's, and declared, in the most menacing voice he could muster—

"You sure you won't regret talking that kind of trash?"

"Ahh?"

"Just so you know, I happen to be acquainted with a certain Reinhard, all right? Me and Reinhard, we're tight, real tight. I call, and he comes zooming in like that."

"—Wha!?"

At what Subaru had said, Chin's face went slack with shock and he stumbled back a step.

Operation "Borrowing the Tiger's Authority" — engaged. And the enemy was thoroughly rattled.

At the mention of Reinhard's name, unease rippled through Ton, Chin, and Kan alike. They'd love to object that it was surely a bluff, but unfortunately, they themselves were the very ones who'd once witnessed Reinhard rushing to Subaru's aid firsthand.

There was no way they didn't know the strength of that slightly unhinged swordsman.

"So, whaddaya say? One word from me right now, and I'll call this whole thing settled. I mean it."

The moment he saw it hit home, Subaru clasped both hands above his head, swayed his hips, and went full throttle on the swaggering, unbothered act to intimidate the men. Frankly, unless Reinhard was the sort of heroic figure who conveniently appeared whenever a citizen cried out for help, it was pretty much a foregone conclusion that Subaru would come out of any actual fight here badly hurt — this was, in its own way, a desperate pantomime.

Unable to see through to the cold sweat beneath his bluster, the men ground their teeth in frustration.

"We'll let this slide, just for now."

"But don't think we lost or nothin', remember that."

"And it's not like we got scared just 'cause of Reinhard's name, either!"

With their obligatory sore-loser remarks and a batch of unnecessary self-justifications only making them look smaller, the men left behind a trail of foul curses and stalked off out of the alley.

Watching their backs disappear completely around the corner of the street, Subaru finally let out every bit of pent-up tension in one long breath, bent over, and braced his hands on his knees.

"Ahh, that was rough. Seriously scary. Guess in a small-fry versus small-fry showdown, my small-fry-ness won out in the end... It's a small world!"

Cracking a joke to somehow claw his way back to his normal composure, Subaru turned to face the girl behind him.

Just as Subaru had bluffed his way through the men to somehow hold on to his mental balance, perhaps the girl, too, had adopted that haughty attitude as a form of self-defense—

"What is it? Looking at me with the eyes of a beggar. I have nothing to give the likes of you."

"No, that's just completely her real personality. Hey, you could at least say thanks for me saving you, you know?"

"Saving me?"

The girl tilted her head, an expression of genuine puzzlement on her face.

She closed her eyes for a moment as if in thought, then, as though something had finally clicked, let out a small "Ah" of a breath.

"So all that chirping you were doing earlier was meant to save me? Hm. I hadn't noticed."

"Pulling off a whole stunt like that and not even getting noticed — that's beyond unrewarded, that's a whole other tier!?"

Clutching his head and twisting at the waist, Subaru expressed the shock with his whole body. But the girl watched his display with a bored gaze, and in the end said nothing more.

"Honestly, what a waste of time. To steal away even a moment of my finite time — how sinful of you."

"So you're just never going to say thank you, huh..."

Wanting nothing but gratitude, thanks upon thanks — it couldn't help but remind him of Rem. The blue-haired girl, probably still making her rounds shopping through the royal capital right about now, had only been apart from him a few hours, and yet he already missed her fiercely. He wanted healing.

Aware his tone might have sounded a little self-righteous, Subaru spoke up anyway — and the girl, with a small "Hmm," turned just her head back toward him.

"Do not misunderstand. Even without you, I would have faced no trouble whatsoever. It was merely a problem that would have resolved itself one way or another, and you happened to be the one who resolved it. To take pride in that as though it were your own achievement is nothing but comical."

"I don't get what that means, is that like — 'even without your help, little old me was gonna be totally fine because I'm super strong,' that kind of thing?"

"No, it's simpler than that. This world is arranged for my convenience. Therefore, nothing detrimental can befall me. That I came through safely is thanks to me, and me alone. Do you not think it shameful, to steal that as though it were your own achievement?"

As if it were only natural, only obvious, as if it were common sense shared by the whole world, the girl thrust out her ample chest with magnificent confidence and asserted her own absoluteness.

A vibrant certainty that declared every event to be on her side, every phenomenon the result of her own doing.

And to top it off, the sheer refreshing nerve to flatly call Subaru's act of stepping in to help nothing but credit-stealing.

Witnessing that sun-like radiance of hers firsthand, Subaru arrived at a firm conclusion.


—This was, without question, the kind of hopeless case you should never, ever get involved with.


"R-right. Sorry for the unnecessary interference, then."

With people like this, the best move was not to provoke with needless words but to simply receive whatever they said with agreement.

Without any hint of pushback, Subaru nodded exaggeratedly, then hurriedly scooped up the bag of Appa he'd left leaning against the wall.

They'd nearly scattered everywhere during the face-crushing incident, but thankfully, none of them looked damaged. Relieved that the greengrocer's kindness had come through unscathed, Subaru said—

"Well, that's that, then. Let's both try walking somewhere a little more out in the sun from now on, yeah?"

Raising a hand, he did an about-face and turned his back on the girl.

For now, he'd put distance between them on foot without ruffling her feathers, and once he reached the corner, break into a full sprint to make his getaway.

Having settled on that course of action, Subaru was counting off the steps to the corner when—

"—Wait."

At the low voice calling from behind him, Subaru froze in place before he could stop himself, and cursed his own lack of discipline.

He should have just pretended not to hear and kept walking, but having stopped, that excuse no longer held any water.

As at least a token act of defiance, Subaru answered without turning around, voice alone—

"W-what is it?"

Subaru failed to notice that the subtly deferential tone of his own reply already betrayed which of them held the psychological upper hand.

The mentally weaker Subaru did his level best to hold onto his pride by refusing to turn around, but to the girl, mentally the stronger of the two, such a clumsy act of defiance meant nothing at all.

"What's in that bag. Show me."

Circling around to face him without a trace of hurry, the girl gestured with a finger for him to lower the bag he was clutching to his chest.

He was under no particular obligation to obey, but not wanting to drag this out any longer by resisting, Subaru grudgingly complied, tilting the bag's opening and showing her what was inside.

At the sight of a heap of ripe red fruit, the girl blinked her red eyes several times.

"I can't tell just by looking. This fruit... what is it?"

"What do you mean, what — it's Appa. Never seen one before?"

Given that they showed up regularly at breakfast at Roswaal Manor, Subaru had assumed it was a common fruit — was that not the case after all? Or perhaps the standard of Roswaal Manor's meals was quite high, and Appa itself was a fairly pricey ingredient — but that possibility had already been ruled out by the two-copper-coin transaction earlier. Which left a genuine question mark floating in Subaru's mind.

And there, before Subaru knitting his brow in suspicion, it was the girl who showed an even more pronounced reaction.

Peering into the bag again, she then looked up at Subaru as though mocking him a little.

"Don't lie to me. Don't make me laugh. Listen well — Appa is a white fruit. It is absolutely not one with a red appearance such as this."

"Peel it and the inside's white, sure."

Wondering what she could possibly mean, only to be told something perfectly obvious, Subaru shot back with a sullen retort. But it was the girl whose expression drained of color at that answer.

Sensing something ominous in that reaction, Subaru asked, hesitantly—

"Wait, seriously — you've genuinely never seen an unpeeled Appa before?"

"Hm. Now that you mention it, I have only ever seen it once it's already on the table. ...I see. Hand it over."

Muttering as though convincing herself, the girl then thrust one hand out toward Subaru, demanding he hand over an Appa.

Subaru tried to object to her sheer high-handedness, but the attempt died the moment he noticed what her other hand — the one not extended toward him — was doing.

Opposite the left hand held out to him, her right hand had slipped into the folds of her dress, and when it emerged from that lavish garment, it held a short-bladed dagger.

The hilt, set with gems, was every bit as obviously fine a piece, but from the way it caught what little light the alley offered and gleamed sharply, its edge looked to be anything but merely decorative.

Having stepped in to save a girl from a shakedown, only to now find himself on the receiving end of a shakedown from that very same girl, Subaru experienced the rare sensation of his head spinning.

To Subaru, struck silent by his overworked brain, the girl repeated her curt demand — "Hand it over" — and added—

"I shall split it in two and see the inside for myself. That way I can confirm whether that mouth of yours is nothing but a hole that leaks lies."

"Having a knife pointed at me in a back alley just... it's giving me a real visceral kind of disgust..."

"Hurry it up."

His resistance in vain, the Appa was seized from him, and split cleanly in two atop the girl's palm.

Gripping the dagger reverse-handed, the girl handled it with a skill more practiced than he'd expected. Watching the halved Appa reveal its white cross-section, she licked up the few drops that had spilled onto her palm, and said—

"Sweet and tart... yes, this is unmistakably the taste of Appa."

"I trust you're satisfied now?"

To the girl, who looked at last convinced, Subaru put on his full customer-service smile. Honestly, he was fully prepared to just gift her that one Appa if it meant getting released already. How long had it even been since he'd gotten separated from Emilia? With no watch, he had no way to know — and honestly, he was afraid to find out.

But the girl, mercilessly, gave not the slightest consideration to Subaru's near-pleading sentiment.

"How amusing. Hand over the rest of them too, every last one. I shall split them all open and confirm each in turn."

"B-A-K-A-I-E!!"

At a remark that had gone past mere high-handedness into outright tyranny, even Subaru finally erupted.

Body flailing with fury, Subaru closed in on the girl.

"I already don't get why you get to split one open, so why the hell would I hand over all of them to you? Absolute no! These Appa, these Appa are... proof of the bond between me and one particular man. They're not just Appa — they're fruits of friendship!!"

"Enough nonsense. Then let us do this instead."

Paying no attention to Subaru's impassioned speech, the girl pressed on, entirely one-sidedly.

Pointing at the bag of Appa in Subaru's arms, she split her lips into a coquettish smile and said—

"How about a wager?"

"—A wager?"

"Yes, a simple wager. Something as easy as calling heads or tails on a tossed coin will do. One Appa staked per round. Well?"

At the proposal of a coin toss, Subaru scoffed at her declaration.

She acted as though she were making some great concession, but the whole premise was absurd to begin with.

"What you're saying makes no sense. For one thing, there's no upside for me in that bet. If all I get out of it is not losing my Appa, I could get the same result just by making a break for it right now."

"Of course, I shall provide you with a reward as well. Let me see..."

The girl touched her lips as though thinking it over. Then she shot Subaru a sultry sidelong glance, crossed her arms, and lifted her ample chest for effect. And then—

"Should you win the wager, I shall permit you to touch my breasts. How does that sound?"

At her offer to stake her own body as the chip in this bet, Subaru let out a long breath and shook his head.

The mindset of casually offering oneself up as gambling stakes, a ruinous way of thinking that gave no thought whatsoever to consequences — this was, without fail, the nature of anyone who ever ruined themselves through gambling.

And the beautiful girl before him surely assumed that any man, upon seeing her, would inevitably be enchanted by her charms. It was a lamentable thing to hear, and a sad way of thinking, too, he felt.

Reading something into his slowness to answer, the girl regarded Subaru with a faint trace of suspicion.

Meeting that gaze head-on, Subaru declared to her, plainly—

"Value yourself a little more. Don't talk nonsense... like I'd ever be enchanted by charms like that!"

—And so,

"That makes seven wins running for me. You've only got three Appa left, you know."

"No way!? Am I about to be stripped clean!?"

And there, in the back alley, was Subaru, losing match after match after match, ruining himself at gambling right before everyone's eyes.
