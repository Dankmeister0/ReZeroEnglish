Arc 4 Chapter 99: "Alone in a Closed Place"

——A situation in which two important figures vanish at the same time.

Naturally, faced with an experience that hadn't occurred even once across all the previous loops until now, Subaru's insides were nothing but scorched with impatience.
He dashed through the Sanctuary and headed for the temporary lodging where Ryuzu had been staying. He'd heard that while she lent her house to Emilia, Ryuzu was using a hut on the outskirts of the village to live in.

"...So you finally showed up."

Garfiel, standing with arms crossed like a guardian statue inside the building, greeted Subaru as he shoved the door open and stormed in. Garfiel glared at him, his nose wrinkled in open irritation.

"Slow. How long d'ya think you can keep a guy waitin', huh?"

"I-I hurried here as fast as I could... more importantly, what's this about Ryuzu-san disappearing?"

"Ain't it plain as you're lookin' at it?"

Garfiel jerked his chin to indicate the cramped room — not that there was much space worth surveying. Ryuzu's temporary residence was little more than a shack, a simple room just barely large enough for the single bed sitting alone in it.
And if Ryuzu's form wasn't to be found on that bed, then there was no doubting she was away.

Catching his breath, Subaru roughly wiped the sweat running down his chin with his sleeve.

"I can see she's not here, but... isn't 'missing' a bit much? Ryuzu-san's a proper adult despite how young she looks. It's not like it's cause for alarm just because she stepped out for a walk or something..."

"Don't screw with me! What the hell do you know?! The old lady ain't ever, not once, skipped breakfast without sayin' why. I ain't never seen her oversleep, never seen her laid up sick, and you're tellin' me a walk? I'll eat you alive."

"Your tone's so violent, but exactly how homey is this relationship between you and Ryuzu-san..."

Given the circumstances, the heartwarming basis for concluding Ryuzu was missing took some of the wind out of Subaru's sails. But Garfiel had no eyes for Subaru's deflation.
He closed in on Subaru, who had finally straightened his back, baring his fangs.

"Somethin' that ain't never happened before happened all of a sudden this mornin'. If somethin' that's never happened before is happenin' in this village right now, even an idiot could figure it's 'cause of you lot showin' up. — Don't tell me you did somethin' stupid to the old lady."

It was a baseless hunch dressed up as an accusation, but this time, of all times, Garfiel's suspicion hit the mark dead center.
Ryuzu — today's rotation should have been θ, and her disappearance was almost certainly Subaru's doing. Like Emilia, it was hard to imagine θ had been carried off by someone. She had left this place on her own two feet, without leaving so much as a word for Garfiel. Like Emilia, she too was barred by the Sanctuary's barrier from fleeing outside. Which meant θ's goal was to hide herself away — and unlike with Emilia, there was an even more pressing time limit on this one.

"In θ-san's case, if I can't find her before the day's out..."

The role would pass on, and tomorrow it would switch to a different Ryuzu — α, β, or Σ. If that happened, holding a proper talk with θ before the two-day deadline would likely become impossible, which in turn would mean the complete clearing of the Sanctuary stalling out entirely.

"Shiii-ta...?"

At Subaru calling Ryuzu that, a question mark rose on Garfiel's face.
Subaru, who'd been about to ask for his help in searching for θ, saw that expression and abruptly hesitated.
A question that had welled up choked off his next words.

"――――"

It was a simple question, and yet one he'd never once confirmed until now.

——Did Garfiel actually know that Ryuzu had four representative personalities?

This was Garfiel, who knew about the abandoned experimental facility deep in the forest, and about the Ryuzu Meyer inside the crystal. He had faced the Trials himself, and had crossed paths with Echidna — he held the qualifications of an Apostle of Greed. As the current holder of command authority over the copies, Garfiel of course knew that Ryuzu was a copy of the original, and that there were many identical beings besides her.
But beyond that? Did Garfiel know that Ryuzu's representative selves numbered four — α, β, Σ, and θ?

"Whadda ya starin' off inta space for all of a sudden? If ya got somethin' on your mind, spit it out quick. 'The honest and obedient Lib-Lib is happy even when he's tricked,' ain't that how it goes?"

"That Lib-Lib guy's tricked right up to the end, isn't he..."

While sketching out a Happy Prince-esque story in his head, Subaru wracked his brain over how to respond to Garfiel's interrogation.
The confrontation with Garfiel was the final barrier to clearing the Sanctuary. Honestly, having to face it before he'd even wormed θ's true feelings out of her was a criminal lack of preparation. And yet, depending on how he answered here, he might unwittingly be forced to broach that very subject.
— How should he respond? After much deliberation, Subaru said,

"Hey, Garfiel. If you really wanted to, couldn't you call Ryuzu-san out yourself?"

"————!!"

The instant he finished speaking, Subaru watched Garfiel's expression change violently. Garfiel's eyes wavered with agitation, and in the next moment he grabbed Subaru by the collar and yanked him in hard.
Close enough that their foreheads nearly touched, Garfiel's golden eyes blazed with a fury so intense it seemed capable of burning the person in front of him to ash with a glance alone.

"I got not one single clue, not a scrap, not a shred, not even a mew-frame's worth of an idea what the hell you're tryin' to say, oi."

"I-I don't know what a 'mew-frame' is, but... that ain't the reaction of a guy who's got 'not one single clue'... let go, idiot."

Blood had rushed to Garfiel's head, grabbing Subaru's collar — but had he then caught himself and regained some composure? His words, as he tried to hide something he mustn't say, came out incoherent.
His grip on Subaru's collar loosened at having his bullseye struck, and Subaru used the opening to twist free of the hold. He straightened his rumpled clothes, put a step of distance between them, and said,

"What I'm trying to say is exactly what it sounds like. You're not dense enough not to get that. There's a way. The quickest, most straightforward solution there is. Why not just use it?"

"Easy for you to say, ain't it...!"

Garfiel's cheek twisted with irritation, glaring at Subaru with something like genuine hatred.
But in his eyes, even as the proportions of fury and hostility deepened, Subaru could clearly make out the shadow of grief cast there.
When Subaru's expression changed at noticing that sorrow, Garfiel clicked his tongue and turned his face away, as if he couldn't bear having that shadow of emotion seen.

"This is why I can't let my guard down around those with qualifications. No tellin' what that rotten witch's been whisperin' in your ear from the inside. Don't screw with me. Don't screw with me."

"――――"

"You know about the old lady's experiment site, and by the look on your face, you know about the original of the old lady in the stone too... this ain't somethin' I can just use willy-nilly."

Garfiel drew his right hand in against his chest, a gesture as if hiding it from Subaru's view. It wasn't literally there, but he was probably indicating that he possessed something invisible — the "command authority."
As Subaru interpreted the gesture that way, Garfiel, still holding his arm to his chest, said,

"I ain't like you or Roswaal. Even knowin' it's there, even knowin' I could use it however I please... I never would. Not ever."

"...Garfiel."

"I'm enough on my own. Long as I'm here, I don't need nothin' else. This power — I ain't usin' it till the last of the last of the last resort comes. — Granny's my granny."

A murmur that slipped out last, almost inaudibly.
He had called Ryuzu "Granny" once before, one other time. This time, on top of that, he hadn't even puffed himself up with "俺様" — his usual boastful "I."
Was that unintended slip the true depth of Garfiel's feelings?

"—Tch."

Realizing he'd said more than he meant to, Garfiel kicked the floor in irritation. The whole ramshackle hut shook from the excess force, dust drifting down onto Subaru's head and neck as he waited to see what Garfiel would do next.
Understanding that he alone had gotten worked up, Garfiel's face twisted with even greater discomfort, and he shoved Subaru — who stood before the door — roughly aside with his arm.

"Move. I got no more business with you. If you can't get a lead on where the old lady went right now, I'll just find her myself first. And after that, you don't get to touch her."

"I'm the cause, I'm the cause — you sure jump to conclusions fast."

"You... until you lot showed up, things here were peaceful, nothin' ever happened. I'm gonna get that time back. I don't need nothin' else, inside or out. ...Don't need it."

Leaving those weak parting words, Garfiel strode out of the hut with long steps. Once outside, he bent his knees slightly and accelerated with beastlike leg strength — even on a single straight path, he vanished from sight within mere seconds.

Brushing away the dust cloud kicked up by Garfiel's departure with his arm, Subaru checked the room once more before heading out after him.

Setting aside Garfiel's hasty conclusion, had Ryuzu really left without leaving a single trace? Maybe, just maybe, she'd left some hint about where she'd gone—

"As if. If there was somethin' like that, someone who's known her way longer than me woulda noticed it ages ago. My nose ain't as good as his, and my eyes are worse besides."

After about five minutes of searching turned up nothing, Subaru let out a self-deprecating sigh. Then he too stepped outside the hut, casting his gaze toward the village as he thought.

"――――"

Emilia and Ryuzu-θ, both had vanished at almost the same time.
Presumably each had left of her own volition, slipping away from Subaru's and everyone else's notice. Was there any chance the two were acting together?

"From what I've seen, I don't remember Emilia and Ryuzu-san being especially close..."

Emilia had constantly been troubled over the Royal Selection and the Trials while in the Sanctuary. Subaru couldn't recall her having peacefully spent time socializing with anyone.
At most she'd had idle conversations with Subaru, Ram, and Otto, and even with the Sanctuary's representatives like Ryuzu or Garfiel, she seemed to have exchanged few words.

Thinking on it now, it was that very lack of outside contact that had likely driven Emilia to shoulder her lonely sense of mission entirely within herself — his own lack of consideration was thrown into sharp relief. If Subaru had handled things better, might Emilia not have found her feelings so unmanageable, might she have found a clearer path to a solution?

"All of that's way too late to matter now..."

Now wasn't the time to be reflecting on the causes and contributing factors behind Emilia's disappearance. Above all, if he traced it all the way back, the reason only led to his own self-denial.
He had no time to indulge in that kind of self-loathing, prone as he was to slipping into negative thinking at the drop of a hat.

"It's a small mercy that Garfiel doesn't know Emilia's missing too... he probably wouldn't drop searching for Ryuzu-san to go looking for her, but there's no telling what he'd say if he found out."

But even that relief would mean nothing if Subaru couldn't secure Emilia soon. And Ryuzu-θ, too, wasn't a matter he could afford to put off.
If he couldn't secure θ before Garfiel did, she would fall under the overprotective Garfiel's protection going forward, making contact all but impossible.
In other words, spelling out what Subaru needed to do:

"Secure Emilia and Ryuzu-θ before Garfiel does. Both within half a day. Then get θ to tell me her reasons for opposing the Sanctuary's liberation, and get Emilia back on her feet enough to face the Trial again... is that it?"

"...Natsuki-san, how much of a thorny path do you intend to keep walking before you're satisfied?"

More than heavy-hearted, Subaru's conclusion was one that could only be called pitch-black with no way forward. Right as he voiced it, Otto — who had just arrived at the hut's entrance — cut in.
Having arrived well behind Subaru's dash over, Otto surveyed the room, thoroughly ransacked by Subaru in his search for clues, and frowned.

"Even for a house search, a little more decorum would be nice. Looks like the talk with Garfiel went off without incident, at least."

"Without incident, sure, but calling it a productive one would be a stretch. For now, I got a reconfirmation of the situation, and the muttering you just heard was the plan of action based on that."

"To me, it sounded less like a problem getting solved and more like a second problem stacking on top of it into total chaos."

"…………"

At Otto's all-too-reasonable observation, Subaru couldn't even manage a wisecrack and just let his shoulders slump.
Even so, he did feel somewhat lighter than before — maybe because Otto's presence reassured him that he didn't have to keep agonizing alone.

"...You know, it's kind of a problem when you look that openly relieved. Really."

"—? What?"

"Never mind, that's my own business. You're probably not even aware of it, huh. I bet you're not. If you were doing it on purpose, I'd be completely played, ugh."

Otto scratched roughly at his gray hair, and Subaru tilted his head in confusion. But Otto didn't answer the question written on Subaru's face, instead raising his voice with an "Anyway!"

"There's no question the deadlock situation just got worse. So what do we do? On top of conditions that already made me want to bolt, now there's even worse ones piled on — how do we break through this? Honestly, right now I still feel like I'd be forgiven for throwing it all away and running."

"Leaving behind everything we've seen and heard and gotten involved in up to now? That's not the kind of irresponsible move either of us has it in us to pull."

"...Emilia-sama might have already done exactly that, though."

Otto looked away, murmuring with a sigh mixed in.
There was no malice in it — he probably just wanted to let out the frustration built up inside him. Subaru made a point of not blaming him for that attitude.
Still, in response to his words, Subaru gave a small shake of his head.

"Emilia's not the type of kid to turn tail and run from a problem riding on her own back..."

"Can you really say that for certain, Natsuki-san? I've been meaning to say this for a while now — aren't you looking a little too exclusively at Emilia-sama's beautiful side?"

"...What's that supposed to mean. Sure, Emilia's gorgeous enough to blind you, but—"

"I'll grant you that without argument, but you know perfectly well that's not what I mean."

His low-grade quip was brushed aside without effort, and Subaru pressed his lips together under Otto's gaze.
Looking at him almost with pity, Otto held up a finger and said, "Listen, okay?"

"She's the woman you're in love with, so I understand wanting to see only her good sides. That happens plenty. There's nothing wrong with projecting an ideal onto someone you love."

"――――"

"But Emilia-sama isn't a perfect person either. If anything, she's someone with plenty of problems — including parts that aren't even within her own control. Her origins, her position, all sorts of entanglements cling to Emilia-sama."

Listening to Otto lay out his words so fluently, Subaru thought he must have been preparing this particular lecture for a while now, waiting for the moment to deliver it.
And indeed, Otto's words were composed entirely of sound reasoning that left no room for rebuttal.

"Of course, those external factors have nothing to do with Emilia-sama's own inner nobility. Her beautiful appearance, too, I'll grant is simply a virtue. But listen, Natsuki-san. Emilia-sama is a human being too... an ordinary woman, just one person. She surely carries her fair share of ordinary womanly worries, weaknesses, and ugly parts as well."

"That kind of thing just doesn't apply to Emilia..."

"That right there — blindly assuming Emilia-sama exists in a separate category — that's exactly what's wrong. You yourself have seen Emilia-sama's shortcomings here in the Sanctuary more than enough, haven't you? And it's not limited to this place — it's something that could happen again and again going forward. The place Emilia-sama is aiming for is set impossibly high, somewhere an ordinary person could never reach."

The throne. That was how Otto characterized the summit Emilia had to aim for.
Subaru knew the faces of the others aiming for that same summit alongside her.

Crusch Karsten — noble and sincere, holding high ideals backed by genuine capability.
Priscilla Barielle — arrogant and vicious, yet holding a self that, precisely because of that, would never waver.
Anastasia Hoshin — greedy and calculating, pouring everything into realizing her own ambitions, who had climbed to her current position through unmatched competitive strength.
Felt — risen in a single leap from a poor, powerless position, whose upward drive would not let her stand still even now, showing a vessel beyond her years.

Every one of these candidates faced the Royal Selection with a strong will and virtues they would yield to no one.
Standing shoulder to shoulder with such formidable rivals — did Emilia have what it took?
Just kindness, kinder-hearted than anyone else. Was that alone not enough?

"As things stand now, Emilia-sama is lacking in all sorts of ways. She isn't complete. Because she's the kind of person she is, there will be times she flinches before hardship, times she wants to run away. Why can't you consider that this might be exactly one of those times when she wants to run, Natsuki-san?"

"...That's. That's because I— Emilia would never—"

Never. Subaru couldn't quite manage to put what came after into words.
He couldn't find it. This feeling for Emilia that undeniably smoldered in his chest — the words that should refute Otto here — what phrase could dress it up enough to explain it?

"――――"

"...Stubborn, aren't you."

Biting his lip, Subaru only stared back with eyes holding nothing but defiance and no words, and it was Otto who gave up trying to draw a response out of him.
Otto shrugged, shook his head as if exasperated, and looked up toward the village.

"We don't have to settle this argument here. Us going back and forth won't move things forward anyway. The bind we're in — needing to find both Emilia-sama and Ryuzu-san — doesn't change either way."

"...Sorry. I really should be talking to you more properly about all this."

"I'll let it slide, since we're friends. — So, what'll it be?"

Gesturing outward with his chin — toward the Sanctuary — Otto left the course of action up to Subaru.
Run, or resist. Of the two missing people, which to go searching for first. Tossing over all these various problems was Otto's trust in Subaru, plain and simple.
Subaru had no intention of treating it lightly, but he laughed inwardly at himself for how fortunate that was.
And then,

"Garfiel doesn't know Emilia's gone missing. And it's not like it matters if Garfiel finds her — nothing bad comes of that. — The worst case is if Garfiel secures Ryuzu-san first. If we lose the chance to sit down and talk with her, you can bet the best ending slips further out of reach."

"...In other words."

"—Let's go find Ryuzu-san. Find her before Garfiel does, and hear her out."

"—Emilia. Everyone's got some important things to talk about. So be a good girl and wait quietly in the usual spot, all right?"

Being shooed off into the hollow of the great tree deep in the forest — the so-called "Princess Room" — was, for young Emilia, one of her greatest sources of dissatisfaction.

In the forest village where the elves lived hidden away, Emilia had been raised, doted on by everyone.
The adults had all treated her kindly without exception, and even her small bouts of selfishness were indulged without a hint of displeasure. She'd felt a little lonely about having so few chances to interact with children her own age, but rules were rules that had to be followed. Fortuna-mama, who stood in as her mother, had impressed upon her sternly that such things were to be obeyed properly.

Fortuna was the woman who had raised Emilia in the elven village, standing in as her mother.
She shared the same physical traits as Emilia — silver hair and dark violet eyes — but she kept her long hair cropped short, finding it a bother, and her sharp gaze was a stark contrast to how Emilia would later turn out.

Emilia couldn't recall when exactly she had started living with Fortuna. She'd only been told that Fortuna wasn't her birth mother, but rather a blood relative — an aunt.

"I'm your father's little sister, you know. My brother — Emilia's papa — and your mama are busy right now and can't be with you... so I'm looking after you in their place."

Fortuna's explanation had struck Emilia with great force. Not that it was a shock in a negative sense, though. Whenever the occasion arose, Fortuna insisted she wasn't Emilia's real mother, but to Emilia there was no doubt whatsoever — Fortuna was her mother.
And on top of having Fortuna-mama, she apparently had a father and a real mother besides — two more of them. Normally, a child was only supposed to have one father and one mother. And yet she had one papa, and two mamas. How lucky could a person be, she'd thought.

"Emilia's silver hair comes from my brother. The color of your eyes, too, seems to be strongly our family's influence. ...But that gentle face of yours is your mama's. Everyone in our family has such sharp-looking eyes."

"...I like Fortuna-mama's eyes, you know?"

That sharp, severe gaze was an everyday fixture. On the rare occasions Emilia broke a rule and made Fortuna angry, that stern gaze would grow even keener, sending Emilia into a great fright.
But apart from such displeased occasions, Fortuna was, to Emilia, the ideal mother. Even that sharp gaze could be met with affection.

Fortuna was strict, but a kind mother.
The discipline she imposed on young Emilia's conduct might have seemed excessive, but even from a young age Emilia understood it was all done out of care for her. There had never been violence involved in her discipline, nor had she ever been scolded or struck unreasonably. Even on the rare occasions she scolded a misbehaving Emilia to tears, on the night they made up, Fortuna would hold her the whole night through in the same bed.

"There's something I regret so, so much. That I should've been kinder to more people, sooner. If I'd realized that earlier, I'm sure my brother wouldn't have ended up relying on me as a last resort."

When she said "so, so much," a shadow of loneliness had crossed Fortuna's profile.
That lingered so strongly, so deeply within Emilia, that she made a point of imitating it herself. Not at sad moments, though — she chose to use it at happy times, when she laughed.
It was a child's shallow wish: that her mother wasn't someone who carried sorrow or loneliness on her shoulders, and to paint over that emblematic phrase of her mother's with good memories instead.

"Mmm... boring."

The story returns to its beginning: Emilia, alone, shut up in the "Princess Room."
She didn't much care for being called a "princess," but since everyone in the village called her that without exception, it was a name she'd grown thoroughly used to by now.
She knew it wasn't mockery but affection, so she'd never asked them to stop. But being dragged off to a room where she was locked away because of it was, for young Emilia, one of her few sources of discontent.

"I wonder what everyone's doing..."

Emilia was shut in the "Princess Room" invariably whenever someone came to visit the village. Without being told by anyone, Emilia sensed, in her own way, that a fair number of people had entered the forest to visit the elves' hidden settlement.
In truth, this sense was the result of Emilia unconsciously interfering with the forest's minor spirits and gathering information from these non-human beings, though at the time she had no idea of it.

She simply spent her time in the cramped little room, hugging her knees, flipping through the pages of a book given to her to pass the time, or playing with the clumsily made dolls Fortuna had sewn.
She'd been told it was a secret discussion only the adults could understand, but that the other children besides herself seemed to be included in it was, of late, one of Emilia's grievances as well.

It was Fortuna-mama who had taught Emilia that lies and secrets were wrong. So wasn't it just as wrong for Fortuna-mama and the other adults to keep secrets from her, to lie to her?

Once every dozen-odd days or so, that was the frequency of these stints in the Princess Room. Until now, no matter how discontented Emilia felt, she hadn't been a bad enough child to show it openly.
But this time — she'd lost count of how many times it had been by now, spent in the Princess Room — combined with a small quarrel she'd had with Fortuna the night before, and above all, forgetting to bring the doll Fortuna had given her, leaving it in her own room, proved the final straw.

"I wanna go outside."

That was all it was — a single murmur, spoken with no one in particular to hear it.
But though what Emilia let slip wasn't heard by anyone she knew, it was heard clearly by "them" — those who knew her.

"—?"

In the hollow, in the room lit by the white glow of Lagmite Ore, a pale blue phosphorescence suddenly mingled in. Blinking, Emilia found her eyes captured by that light that had abruptly welled up.
The phosphorescence danced and swayed before her eyes, tickling the young girl's curiosity and holding her captive, before drifting toward a corner of the Princess Room — and vanishing, as if drawn into the wall.

"――――"

Emilia stood and made her unsteady way toward the spot where the light had vanished. There was a hint of fear, but a greater curiosity burned in the young girl's chest.
Standing before the wall the light had been drawn into, feeling around at the wood with her hands, Emilia found a hole in the wall — just about the right size for a small arm to fit through.
That pale blue light from before had slipped out through here, to the outside.

The front door of the Princess Room was barred from the outside and couldn't be opened from within. Even if Emilia tried to escape, the arrangement was built so she couldn't get out.
Looking back on it now, that treatment could hardly be called ordinary for peacetime — excessive, even — but to Emilia, who had taken it for granted, there had been no room for doubt.
But now, having stumbled upon the possibility of getting outside from a place she was supposed to be unable to leave, Emilia's heart wavered between curiosity and her mother's discipline.

She wanted to know what everyone in the village was doing outside while she was away.
But Fortuna-mama had strictly disciplined her to obey the rules. She had to wait here until Fortuna came back to the Princess Room.
But then again — what if she tried this way of sneaking outside, peeked at what everyone was doing, and then came back before anyone noticed? Surely that wouldn't be found out.

Besides, it was the adults who'd broken the promise first — that lies and secrets were wrong.
If Emilia broke that rule just once too, wouldn't that make them even?

"――――"

It was the small justification a young girl had worked hard to come up with, small as it was.

The hole she'd stuck her arm into was, on closer look, a gap among the tangled tree roots. When she pushed with all her might, she could feel the gap gradually widening, bit by bit.
Relying on that feeling, young Emilia pushed the roots aside, growing increasingly desperate to secure a space she could fit through. Sweat on her brow, her clothes caked with dirt, leaving traces that would give her away the moment she tried claiming "nothing happened" to Fortuna — Emilia nonetheless managed, somehow, to widen the gap among the roots and crawl her way out of the hollow.

"――――ah"

The moment she stepped outside and felt the wind, Emilia's chest swelled with a strange sense of accomplishment.
Even though she'd done something bad that would get her scolded if found out, she was filled with the urge to run straight to Fortuna and boast, "Ta-da, I did it!"
Of course, doing something like that was certain to get her scolded like a house on fire, so right before she took off running, Emilia stopped herself. That had been close.

But Emilia also thought this:

——If only she'd followed through on the foolish conclusion she'd reached in that moment — gone to Fortuna-mama seeking praise, gotten scolded with all her might there, cried, wailed, regretted it, and simply forgotten about the gap in the tree roots.
If it had gone that way, the trigger for the later tragedy would never have been pulled.

——What tragedy, though?

That question went unanswered in young Emilia's mind, and having successfully escaped the Princess Room, she set off at a trot, buoyant with triumph, toward wherever everyone was gathered.
Her awareness that she was doing something bad compelled her to sneak and peek in hiding. Where everyone was, she could vaguely tell, thanks to the minor spirits' cooperation.

Before long, Emilia found everyone gathered in the village square. And there, she also saw a group in black clothing she didn't recognize, gathered together with them.

"――――"

Hiding behind an especially large tree, young Emilia nimbly scrambled up into its branches. Little Emilia had quite the mischievous streak, moving from tree to tree like a small animal, no end of trouble for the adults trying desperately to catch her.
In any case, the acrobatics she'd picked up along the way now allowed her to watch the discussion unfold, unseen, from the adults' blind spot.

The whole elven village numbered about forty people. From adults down to children, everyone but Emilia appeared to be gathered there. And the group in black robes was fewer — around twenty or so.
A few figures were talking in the middle, while the rest exchanged goods, it seemed. The black-robed group appeared to have brought a cart, and the villagers receiving the goods handed down from it wore bright expressions, bowing their heads over and over.

"—Thank you as always, we're always in your debt."

What were they doing, what were they talking about?
Just as Emilia wondered this and started to lean forward, a voice suddenly felt as close as if whispered right beside her ear.
Emilia's back stiffened reflexively, but scanning around, there was no likely source for the voice anywhere. Before that, though — that voice had unmistakably been Fortuna-mama's.
And indeed, the Fortuna in question was right now, below Emilia — at the head of the group, in the middle of exchanging words with one of the black-robed figures.

"You provide us with things we can't get in the forest — everyone's grateful, and it helps us greatly."

"Those words are more than we deserve. If anything, it's we who feel it a shame that support in this form is all we can offer. Fortuna-sama, it's always you who bears the burden."

"That, too, goes both ways, wouldn't you say."

The sound of both of them giving a wry laugh, and the content of their conversation, came through with perfect clarity.
From Fortuna's gestures below, Emilia understood that this exchange was without doubt happening right at this very moment. Somehow, she seemed to have gained impossibly sharp hearing.
In truth, the minor spirits had, sensing Emilia's wish, extended her their consideration, but naturally young Emilia had no way of noticing this gesture from the minor spirits either.

Facing Fortuna, opposite her, was a man in a black robe with a resolute face.
His muscular, tall build stood out markedly among the elven village's generally slender folk. And yet, despite an appearance one would never expect from someone so martial-looking, the man seemed to hold himself humbly before Fortuna.
Seeing such a formidable man show unstinting respect toward Fortuna, Emilia — even as she peeked from hiding — felt a swell of pride.

That her mama was someone splendid enough to make a grown man bow his head so.

"And, as always... how is the seal holding up?"

Just as Emilia was puffing out her chest with that misplaced sense of pride, the moment the man raised his next topic, that sentiment was blown clean away.
That was how strongly the man's single remark carried a heavy, complicated weight of feeling.

"I won't laugh it off as you worrying too much. It's fine, unshaken as ever, solid as bedrock. Not the slightest chance it could ever come undone. — I couldn't face my brother, or my sister-in-law, if it did."

"Your brother and his wife — that was truly regrettable."

"...My brother must have made his peace with it, I think. What my sister-in-law felt, I still don't know, even now. But I understand the weight of the responsibility entrusted to me. I don't want to abandon it, or handle it halfheartedly. You feel the same, don't you?"

"I... for me, it's all I have left. Not quite the same as your sense of duty and responsibility, Fortuna-sama. Attachment, lingering regret... that's closer to it, I think."

The man laughed as if letting out a breath, and Emilia saw Fortuna's eyes take on a wistful cast.
But to Emilia now, none of these details carried any weight.

——What did he mean, that her brother and his wife were "truly regrettable"?

Fortuna-mama's brother was Emilia's father. And his wife would be his spouse — his bride. Emilia's mother, surely.
What did it mean, that the two of them were "regrettable"? Why didn't Fortuna, on hearing that, ask him to explain further?

Clinging to the branch, straining her neck to catch even a little more of the group's conversation up close, Emilia listened intently. Thanks to the minor spirits' blessing, unaware that such efforts were pointless, she gritted her teeth, desperate not to miss a single word, not a single fragment.

"Motive and the nobility of an act have nothing to do with each other. What you're doing is something you could take pride in before the whole world. That we can't shout it from the rooftops is, so, so frustrating."

"Ha, hahaha. Your words of comfort do wound me a little. But indeed, that's difficult. After all, should the world learn the true intent behind our actions, the world's affairs — only just settling down — would sink once more into a sea of chaos. Neither you, nor I, and above all, she herself, would wish for that."

"...Yes. I suppose you're right."

Fortuna drew her chin in, showing her agreement with the man's words.
After that, the topic seemed to veer away from what Emilia wanted to hear, and the conversation that followed was all harmless small talk. As Fortuna and the man exchanged such words, the exchange of goods between the two groups seemed to conclude as well. One of the adults called out to Fortuna, and with a nod in reply, she turned back to face the man.

"Thanks to the spirits' Divine Protection, the changing of the Seasons doesn't affect the forest much... but even so, having clothes and bedding brought to us helps. Thank you."

"By rights, you people ought to be treated with far greater honor for your achievements. That you should be forced to endure inconvenience in a place like this — it shouldn't be allowed."

"Don't call it 'a place like this.' We love this forest, you know."

She said it half-jokingly, a small smile rising on her face. The man's mouth curved into a smile too, and for a moment a gentle atmosphere passed between the two of them.
And then—

"Bishop-sama. The exchange of goods is complete, and preparations for the return journey are ready. Please make haste."

"Yes, I understand."

At the black-robed figure's call, the man cast one last, lingering glance over the village. Then he bowed to Fortuna, and Fortuna and the other adults answered the black-robed group with a salute — a hand over the heart, eyes closed.
Turning their backs, the black-robed group pulled the cart away — and at the very rear, the man stopped, and said,

"Ah, right, there's just this one thing I had to ask."

"…………"

The man turned back, raising a finger, and Fortuna silently urged him to continue.
He closed his eyes once at her manner, then looked toward the depths of the forest as if peering into it.

"—Is Emilia-sama keeping well?"

"—!"

At her own name coming from the man's mouth, Emilia up in the branches nearly choked on her breath. That she managed not to let out even a small cry of surprise was pure luck — she'd just exhaled a moment before.
Fortunately, that faint sound reached no one's ears and went unnoticed, and Fortuna, receiving the question, gave the man a slow nod.

"She's fine. Emilia's healthy, growing into a good child. Too good a child for the likes of us, really. ...But, I'm sorry. I can't let you meet her."

"That's quite all right. I understand. Simply knowing Emilia-sama is well is enough. To wish for more than that would be too great a hope for a sinner such as myself."

"…………"

Whether the man's tone was self-mockery or self-reproach, it was hard to say.
Either way, Fortuna, hearing it, offered no easy words of comfort.
The man, for his part, raised his face looking almost saved by Fortuna's silence. For a moment, the two of them simply gazed at each other without a word, until—

"Bishop-sama, is something the matter? — Bishop Romanée-Conti-sama."

One figure came back from the group that had gone on ahead without the man. At the call, the man spread both hands.

"It's nothing at all. Come, let's be off. Fortuna-sama, until next time."

"Thank you, as always. ...I'm sorry, Juice."

At Fortuna's final words, the man called Juice gave a small smile, and this time he departed the forest for good, taking the group with him.
Fortuna watched until they were entirely out of sight, her shoulders dropping as she let out a breath. Then she clapped her hands, drawing everyone's attention.

"All right, let's get the goods carried in and sorted quickly. Distribution as usual, please. I'm going to go let Emilia out."

"――――!"

At Fortuna's instruction, watching the adults and children shoulder the goods and start moving, Emilia all but tumbled down from the tree and sprinted at full speed back to the Princess Room.
Wedging her small body back through the gap in the roots she'd used to escape, scraping herself in several places along the way, Emilia somehow made it back inside. But the moment she stepped in, she froze in horror, realizing her appearance was nothing like that of a child who'd been quietly waiting.

Until just a moment ago, she'd assumed that even if she were found out and scolded for going outside, it was the kind of mischief that would be forgiven quickly enough once she reflected on it.
But after hearing what she'd just heard, no matter how she tried to think optimistically about it, Emilia couldn't manage to feel that way anymore. If anything, what she'd just overheard felt like precisely the kind of thing she alone was never meant to hear.

"What do I do, what do I do, what do I do, what do I do."

There wasn't much time before Fortuna-mama would come back and unbar the door. If she stepped into the light like this, her state would give away her escape, link by link.
If Fortuna found out she'd overheard what she just had, it felt to young Emilia like it would spell absolute ruin.

"If only I could at least hide the scrapes..."

Having scraped herself in various places, her knees and arms bore several scratches, faintly beaded with blood. Sharp-eyed Fortuna wouldn't miss them, and besides, they'd surely sting terribly at bath time — that scared her too.
She threw herself entirely into figuring out something, anything, to fix this.

"—Eh?"

So when the pale blue phosphorescence appeared once again in the Princess Room, to Emilia it seemed like some kind of saving guidance.
The light wavered gently across her vision, toying with her attention just as before, and this time it began scattering that glow over her own body.

"—Ah, ah."

Just as the light had been drawn into the wall before, the pale blue phosphorescence was now drawn into Emilia's body. It gathered as if attacking each of the young girl's wounds and scrapes in turn, dyeing the injured spots white with a faint glow — and once the light faded, only a faint redness remained where the scrapes had been.

"――――"

Emilia stiffened wordlessly at the strange change that had come over her body.
The wounds that had been aching all over her elbows, knees, everywhere, had vanished without a trace, and now her body was exactly as it had been before her escape.
With this, Emilia thought, and pulled the shirt she'd been wearing off over her head, changing into the spare clothes kept in the Princess Room. And when she found herself at a loss over what to do with the shirt torn through from the roots—

"With this...!"

She overturned the inkpot used for drawing and roughly smeared the shirt she'd been wearing with it. Just as the shirt had become thoroughly stained with a mess that wouldn't wash out—

"—Emilia? Are you awake?"

Fortuna's voice came from outside the door, and Emilia's spine snapped straight.
With her heart pounding at the razor's-edge timing, she tried, somehow, to answer, but the first word simply wouldn't come out.

"Emilia? Did you fall asleep?"

"I-I'm awake, okay? I'm awake, Fortuna-mama. But right now..."

"Oh, you are awake. Sorry, keeping you waiting..."

Sounding relieved at Emilia's reply, Fortuna unbarred the door from outside and entered the room. Her smiling face changed the moment she stepped inside, though — her shapely nose wrinkling.

"...What's this? There's a strong smell of ink."

"Um, I'm sorry. I spilled a lot of the drawing colors... it got all over my clothes too."

Comparing the inkpot rolling in the middle of the room to Emilia standing before it looking at a loss, Fortuna put a hand to her face with an "oh, dear," and said,

"Well, what's done is done, nothing to be helped. Good thing there were spare clothes here. Otherwise I'd have had to carry naked Emilia all the way home."

"Um, Fortuna-mama... I..."

"It's all right, Emilia. No need to be so scared — you didn't do it on purpose, so I'm not angry. More importantly, you're not hurt anywhere, are you?"

Crouching down to meet Emilia's eye level as she shuffled hesitantly closer, Fortuna checked her beloved daughter's body from head to toe. Finding no obvious injuries, she let out a breath of relief and pulled Emilia into a gentle embrace.

"Mama?"

"No, it's nothing. I just... wanted, so, so much to see you. I'm sorry. Just let me stay like this a little while, okay?"

Still holding Emilia close, Fortuna pressed her cheek against her, saying so.
Fortuna usually shied away from such gestures out of embarrassment, so it struck Emilia as unusually rare — and it seemed to her that Fortuna looked, somehow, uncertain and lonely.
So—

"...Cheeky."

Fortuna, her short silver hair being stroked by the Emilia she was embracing, murmured that with one eye open.
But since she hadn't been told to stop, Emilia judged it was all right, and continued slowly, slowly stroking Fortuna's head, filled with tenderness.

There were so many things she wanted to ask.
But to put any of it into words, young Emilia lacked too much — too many things she didn't understand, didn't know — and so she said nothing at all.

"Hey, Emilia."

"...Mm."

Watching Emilia continue quietly stroking her head, Fortuna narrowed her eyes. And then, suddenly, Emilia saw a drop of tears well up in those dark violet eyes, the same color as her own.
In the space of a blink, that tear slid down Fortuna's cheek and fell, and without wiping it away, Fortuna smiled at Emilia and said,

"—I love you."

There had been so many things she wanted to ask, so many things she wanted to know.
——But for now, young Emilia decided, she would let herself be satisfied with just that one word from her mother.


※※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※


Dragging her feet, pale blue phosphorescence drifting around her, Emilia walked through the darkness.

Exhaustion of spirit had stolen the strength to walk from her, dragging her feet along. And yet, her will remaining intact would not let her stop walking, and even now it drove her onward.

Memories from her childhood flickered through her mind, one after another.
Why, now of all times, had she recalled a past like that?

The past Emilia had seen in the Trial was a slightly different span of time than the one she'd just remembered. What she'd just recalled came from slightly earlier than the events of the Trial.
If, hypothetically, she could go back to that point in time — surely, everything could be redone.

"Fortuna... mama..."

Kind, warm, strong — Fortuna remained, even now, Emilia's ideal of what a woman should be.
She had always, always wanted to become like Fortuna-mama, to be like her. And yet, she herself, every single time, agonized and regretted over small things, grew frightened, and brought about irreversible outcomes.

"Uh... hyuu...!"

Thinking of those irreversible outcomes, a pain too great to contain shot through Emilia's chest.
Crushed beneath a tangle of sorrow, regret, and pain all mixed together, Emilia felt near tears from shame at her own foolishness and inadequacy.

It was always like this. It was always, always like this.
Desperately, with all her strength, doing her best — she never meant to hold anything back, and yet her hand never so much as brushed against the thing she truly wanted, let alone reached it.
Even the things that had surely been there, that she was sure she'd held in this very hand, slipped through her fingers like grains of sand, dazzling her with a moment's glimmer before vanishing.

Fortuna, and Puck, and Subaru — all of it was the same.

"It's my fault... because I'm a bad girl who can't even keep her promises... everyone..."

Spilling the words like a sob, Emilia dragged her feet forward.
Through the dense, overgrown green, slowly, slowly, she still kept moving forward.

"So everyone always hides things from me... keeps secrets... but no, that's wrong. It would've been better if I'd just stayed not knowing, never been told... never noticed... but..."

Trees deep in the forest. Pale blue phosphorescence. Men in black robes. Fortuna-mama. A great black serpent. A closed door. Snow. A white world. A silver world. A world ending, ending. Papa, mama.

"So, I..."

An endless whirl of words raced through her mind.
Tossed about by them, Emilia lifted her face, and pressed on.

"――――"

A faint voice. An unsteady gait.
——And yet, in those eyes, not a single tear had risen.
