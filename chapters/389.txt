Arc 5 Chapter 72: "Sword Saint VS the Previous Sword Saint"

—The dragon sword Reid is a blade of many mysteries.

It is certain that it is a treasured sword passed down through House Astrea, which has produced generation after generation of 'Sword Saints,' but where that dragon sword was bestowed from has not been handed down along with it.
It is a treasured sword of unknown provenance, and on top of that, carries the peculiar distinction that none but the 'Sword Saint' can draw it. To add further to that, it is said that even the 'Sword Saint' cannot draw it except when it is truly needed.

The treasured sword with which the first 'Sword Saint,' Reid Astrea, brought down the Divine Dragon Volcanica.
The legendary blade that once cut down every last one of a great horde of dragons that came surging forth.
Or perhaps merely a dull blade that gained its power by continuously drinking the blood of its enemies, an Enchanted Sword.

If one is content with unverified tales, mere fairy stories, there are any number of such anecdotes.
In any case, nothing can be said for certain, and there is no way to confirm it either.
If there is one single thing that can be stated with certainty, it is this—

—The dragon sword Reid surpasses every treasured sword, every legendary blade, every Enchanted Sword that exists.
—It is the ultimate embodiment of steel refined to its furthest extreme as a sword, and there exists no steel that surpasses it.

To witness that unclouded white blade with one's own eyes was, even for Wilhelm—who had been granted the name "Van," the pride of swordsmen—only the third time in his entire life.



"—Reinhard."

He stood with the jet-black, dragon-claw-engraved scabbard in his left hand, and the dragon sword gripped tight in his right.
The one whose red hair swayed in the wind, whose blue eyes surveyed the situation with unwavering directness, was none other than the present-day 'Sword Saint,' Reinhard van Astrea.

Even Wilhelm was overwhelmed by that dignified, valiant bearing.
This was, in fact, the first time Wilhelm had ever laid eyes on the battlefield form of his own grandson—the one who had inherited the title of 'Sword Saint' and taken it upon himself to be the kingdom's sword as a Royal Knight.

After losing Theresia during the Great Conquest, Wilhelm had fled House Astrea. The rift with his son and grandson that resulted from that had gone unmended even now, fifteen years later.
For those fifteen years, Wilhelm had done nothing but chase after his wife, turning his eyes away from his family the whole while. So he had witnessed neither his son's decline nor his grandson's growth and accomplishments—none of it.

—And it was precisely because of that, that he was overwhelmed now by the sight of Reinhard.

What stood there was the 'Sword Saint.'
A being blessed with the undivided favor of the Sword God, granted the honor of drawing the highest of all blades, standing at the summit that every swordsman longs for—nothing more and nothing less than the 'Sword Saint' itself.

At that sight, Wilhelm remembered.
The pain, he had long since forgotten. What he recalled instead was something else entirely—a feeling from long, long ago, from the very first time Wilhelm had seen a 'Sword Saint,' had seen Theresia's sword dance.

Even then, Wilhelm had felt a distance he could never close in this lifetime.
He had grieved the smallness of his own talent with the sword, certain he could never reach that realm.
And yet he had not let himself rot. He had swung his sword, and swung it again, again and again, until at last his hand had brushed the very edge of that realm. He should have been able to prove that no distance was too great to close.

—And yet, how narrow, how small his field of vision had been.

The quality was different. The height was different. The weight was different. The very substance was different. Everything about it was different.
That thing was not an existence one could speak of in terms of reaching or not reaching.
It existed, quite literally, in a different dimension altogether.

"——"

Theresia slowly lowered the longsword she had raised. The blade that, only a moment before, had been on the verge of cutting down Heinkel now turned its point toward the newly arrived enemy.
Theresia van Astrea—a mindless, moving corpse—had already lost every trace of a swordsman's pride, a warrior's code, anything of the sort.

All that remained in her now were the commands of the sorcerer who moved that corpse through forbidden arts.
Only the optimal judgment for carrying out those commands, and the elimination of whatever might obstruct them.

And if those commands gave priority to whichever opponent posed the greater threat, then this was, naturally, the correct judgment.

An old swordsman already stripped of the ability to continue fighting, defeated, left only to wait for death by blood loss.
A vice-captain of the Order of Knights in title alone, who had lost the will to fight and did not even possess the courage to flee.

Neither of the two was, any longer, any kind of threat to Theresia whatsoever.
Thus, for her to turn that longsword, that skill of the former 'Sword Saint,' toward the present 'Sword Saint' was not, in any sense, a mistaken judgment.

"Stop! Theresia! Look this way—look at me, Theresiaaaa!"

Dragging his leg, trailing a thread of blood, Wilhelm screamed.
As though the cry had not even reached her, Theresia did not so much as glance his way. As though the clash of blades until just now had been a lie, he was treated as though he did not exist at all.

It was humiliating. And at the same time, there was a grief that outweighed even that.
But there was no time to drown in sorrow. Such stagnation was not permitted to him now.
He had to cry out now. He had to stop this now—

"——"

Ignoring Wilhelm's desperate heart, Theresia closed the distance in a single bound.
Her long red hair streaming behind her, Theresia danced toward Reinhard standing before her.

The longsword traced a semicircle, carving a trajectory of pure artistry as it moved to cleave Reinhard diagonally in two—but through a gap of an instant that would not have allowed so much as a blink, Reinhard slipped beneath the blade.
As Reinhard tried to slip past her side and circle behind her, the tip of the longsword pursued him like a living thing possessed of its own will. It cut through the very air in its path and still closed in, yet Reinhard's expression did not change in the slightest. He merely shifted his distance by half a step, and it did not even graze him.

"——"

As though sensing the poorness of her position, Theresia leapt forward without a word. To face Reinhard while turned half-open to him would have been nothing short of suicide.
Turning to face him, longsword raised in a level guard, Theresia looked straight at Reinhard.

Just behind Reinhard stood Heinkel. As if shielding his father, Reinhard faced his grandmother. Only then did Wilhelm realize that the brief exchange of attacks just now had all been in service of securing that very position.

"Stop it… what is this, what the hell is this… what did I—what did I even do…!"

Pale-faced, head clutched in his hands, Heinkel did not notice.
That his own son stood before his eyes, shielding him—that meant nothing to him. The truth of what had come before had already overwhelmed the capacity of Heinkel's heart.
There was no hoping he could break through this situation. That had been true from the very start.

Which was exactly why it had to be Wilhelm who raised his voice.

"Stop, Reinhard! Look at me! I am still in the middle of my fight with Theresia! No one is permitted to interrupt a battle between swordsmen!"

"——"

Reinhard glanced sidelong at Wilhelm, who screamed that the fight was not yet over. With those blue eyes, he looked at Wilhelm's right leg, still steadily bleeding.

"…With that leg, you cannot continue to fight."

"What does it matter if my leg cannot move! This hand that grips the sword still lives! If the hand dies, then the mouth! If the mouth cannot speak, then the soul! So long as I have not lost my life, I have not lost!"

"So long as you have not lost your life… then what will you do about the woman standing before you?"

"—!"

At Reinhard's question, Wilhelm's throat closed.
Expressionless, her eyes devoid of any feeling, utterly silent, Theresia kept her gaze fixed on her enemy. Keeping that figure at the edge of his vision, Reinhard demanded an answer of Wilhelm.

"A corpse that moves only in obedience to a sorcerer's will, with no self-awareness of its own—to bring the code of the swordsman to bear on an act that toys with the dead, I cannot see the meaning in that."

"The code of the swordsman, you say…!"

What foolishness, to demand such a thing of a duel against a moving corpse.
Against Reinhard's argument, Wilhelm had no answer. It was, after all, simply true that Theresia had already put distance between herself and Wilhelm, treating the fight as finished.
No matter how much the defeated Wilhelm screamed, a swordsman's true purpose could not be fulfilled.

And Wilhelm himself could not, in good conscience, loudly proclaim himself a swordsman right now.
Rising to his feet leaning on his sword, entrusting himself not to steel but to words, not to the power of the blade but to a plea—in this state, where in Wilhelm van Astrea was there any pride of the sword-demon left?

There was nothing of the sort anywhere. He was empty.

"—The dead do not move. The dead have no future beyond this. I will not forgive that injustice."

Before the now-voiceless Wilhelm, Reinhard declared this.
His eyes no longer looked at his grandfather at all; they were fixed, single-mindedly, on the corpse of his grandmother standing before him.

With a slow, unhurried motion, the dragon sword Reid was raised into a level guard.
By strange coincidence, that stance was, mirror-like, identical to the one Theresia held with her longsword.

"——"

The unclouded blade of the dragon sword seemed to shine with an unusually lustrous gleam.
It was the sword's own applause—moved, delighted, at having gained the chance to be swung once more, the supreme steel carried a voiceless jubilation at the prospect of facing its own former wielder.

"——"
"——"

In silence, the blue eyes of the two swordsmen locked together.
Though both 'Sword Saints' stood with blades raised, neither offered the customary declaration of names that should have come first.

Naturally so. That ritual exists because one asks of one's opponent the worth of a swordsman's pride, a warrior's code.
It is not performed toward one who is not such an opponent—toward one unworthy of being asked for that equal footing.

The air froze. The tension pressed down upon the world with color and weight.
His whole body gripped by an oppressive, suffocating sensation, Wilhelm opened his mouth.

Without knowing what words he should say, driven only by the desperate urgency that he had to say something.
—And, ironically, that became the signal for the two swordsmen.

"Stop it—!"

The voice did not reach.
Leaving even the voice behind, the two swordsmen collided.

"——"

Stepping in, swinging through to the end, Theresia's longsword howled as it tore through the air, and the finest blow she could unleash struck at Reinhard from a perfect angle.
It might have been, perhaps, the single most refined and beautiful stroke of Theresia's swordsmanship that Wilhelm had ever witnessed.

That it was not himself who had drawn out the fullness of the sword-power slumbering within Theresia—had this been an ordinary moment, Wilhelm would have burned with jealousy over that.
But in this instant, the emotion that swelled up within Wilhelm's heart was something else entirely.

And that which surged explosively up through his chest spilled out as words, unmistakable and clear.


"Please, don't kill her…!"


The emotion he had sealed away, the passion he had suppressed, the love he had forbidden himself even to wish for—all of it burst forth from Wilhelm as though a dam had broken.

Theresia, in her youth.
The woman who had set Wilhelm's heart aflame, who had made him aware of a world beyond the sword, the one single woman in his entire life he had thought he would not regret trading everything for—she was standing right there.
The woman he loved most, to whom he had never once, not even a single time, said the words "I love you"—she was right there—.

"That is my Theresia—!!"

Words that should never, under any circumstances, have been spoken.
In a life-or-death confrontation where any wavering could cost one's life, to give priority to one's own feelings was not something to be forgiven.

It was an act that defiled the nobility that ought to belong to a swordsman's pride, a warrior's code, to any battle.
It was, simply, the voice of a man. A man desperate, frantic, not to have the woman he loved taken from him.
And to that desperate, dying cry, the answer came—


"—Grandmother was killed by my own hand, fifteen years ago."


It was a quiet voice, almost a whisper.
So faint one could not tell whether it would even carry.
But it was, unmistakably, nothing other than an answer to Wilhelm's cry.

"——"

Theresia's blow struck home, directly, against Reinhard.
The dragon sword had not even yet entered the arc of a swing.

It should connect. It should cut him down. By any measure, that should have been the outcome.

"What stands here is nothing but a fake."

—The dragon sword Reid traced its arc.

A single stroke, the dragon sword swung without a sound, and the white blade flowed smoothly back into its scabbard.
Only the faint metallic click of the guard meeting the scabbard's mouth could be heard.


And with that alone, the battle ended.
It was over.
