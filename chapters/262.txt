Arc 4 Chapter 94: "Left Behind"

The first thing she felt upon waking was a loneliness coming from her empty right hand.

Half-asleep, her blood still sluggish with sleep, she thought this vaguely, and then, as consciousness surfaced, she realized just how selfish a sentiment that was, and her cheeks flushed rapidly with rage and shame.
Rather than sit up, she chose to curl into a ball inside the bed, drawing the towel blanket around herself to make her body small. Recognizing her own wretchedness the very instant she woke, she'd already come to despise herself, and the day had barely begun.

"—Awful, awful, so awful. I'm... I'm so selfish."

The girl curled up in the bed—Emilia—murmured this and let out a long, long sigh at her own disgraceful state.

In the hand that opened and closed again and again beneath the covers, she still remembered the sensation that had been there until the moment she'd fallen asleep.
Thick, rough fingers, the fingertips just slightly calloused—so different from her own thin, feeble fingers, she thought every single time she'd had the chance to hold that hand.

The feel of that rough yet delicate palm belonged to the boy—Subaru—who worried over Emilia, spoke gentle words to her, and sat beside the bed holding her hand until the very moment she lost consciousness.
That the first half-conscious thought upon waking was a forlorn sense of loss—for the vanished feel of Subaru's palm, for fingers now grasping empty air—showed just how hopeless she was.
Even after leaning on him so much already, did some part of her still wish to burden that boy further? Even though her own weakness and sins had already caused irreparable trouble for those around her.

It was now the fourth day since arriving at the Sanctuary—crossing over the first day, yesterday and the day before, Emilia had thrown herself into the Trials of the Tomb deep within the Sanctuary.

For Emilia, aiming to win the Royal Selection and take the throne of the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica, the power of this Sanctuary was the very first step among the things she needed to obtain.
Roswaal, who governed this land, was Emilia's patron, and the troubled residents here were people whose position, as Half-Elves, was close to her own. If she couldn't prove herself and win their approval in a place with such favorable conditions already arranged, what could she possibly accomplish beyond this?
Emilia's standing in the Royal Selection was, undeniably, at a disadvantage compared to the other candidates. For someone as powerless as Emilia to survive to the end, the cooperation of those around her was indispensable. And the trust needed to secure that cooperation could only be built through Emilia's own actions.

For Emilia, who understood her own position correctly, what she needed to do in the Sanctuary and what she needed to prove were clear. On that point, there was nothing to hesitate over.
And yet, what cast a shadow over her forward-looking gaze was—

"...The 'Trial.'"

The sole, absolute condition for winning the approval of the Sanctuary's residents was breaking through the Trial.
The residents, bound by the barrier stretched out from the tomb, could not leave the forest surrounding the Sanctuary. In order to fight alongside them in the outside world, the barrier had to be dispelled by completing the Trial. And as a matter of sentiment too, unless she could at least manage that much, they would never be willing to accept Emilia.

Both the physical problem and the emotional one could be resolved through clearing the Trial. Since the issue was concentrated into a single point, there was no need for convoluted logic or unnecessary argument.
The only problem was that the content of that Trial happened to be, for Emilia, a deadly poison.

—The inorganic voice that echoed through the tomb had told her to face her own past.

If she closed her eyes, she could vividly recall that white world.
The instant she did, Emilia's body began to shiver with an unstoppable chill, as if she'd been thrown naked into bitter cold.
Was the terror racing through her whole body from remembering the cold of that day, or because she still hadn't forgotten the fear she'd felt then?

Haltingly, in her own words, she had spoken of her past—what had Subaru thought, hearing it?

That unforgettable past, which still bound her body in chains of guilt—Emilia had confessed it to Subaru around midday yesterday.
The night before that, she had challenged the Trial for the first time, and her heart had been, quite literally, broken. She had cried, screamed, fallen to pieces in the arms of Subaru, who had shaken her awake, and only regained her composure at the sound of his voice gently stroking her back—and then she had told everyone waiting outside the tomb that she had failed the challenge.
Emilia didn't remember what expressions everyone wore upon hearing those words.
She hadn't had the composure to check each of their faces one by one. Whether she'd been met with looks of contempt or disappointment didn't matter either way. She had simply put on a brave face, parted from everyone, and stumbled into the house assigned to her—and the instant she realized she was alone, she'd very nearly been swallowed by unbearable fear.
Unable to stay shut up like that, she had burst out of the building and stood trembling in the night wind, and that was when she encountered Subaru again, walking toward her beneath the moonlight.
Afterward, when Subaru confessed his resolve—that he wouldn't spare himself for her sake—Emilia had fled behind a string of idealistic resolutions of her own.
How devastated Subaru had been to hear that, Emilia—cornered by her own words in just the same way—hadn't been able to notice.

After that, Emilia didn't remember how she'd made her way back to their temporary lodgings.

The next thing she knew, she woke to Subaru's voice calling out to her, his face pale, as she lay collapsed on the floor.
And from there, having worried Subaru so, it was an inevitable course that they would talk about the Trial that had caused it—and by extension, about Emilia's past.

There wasn't a single embellishment or lie in the account of her past that she gave Subaru.
Emilia's sin remained exactly as it was—she had only just been forced to witness it vividly all over again. Something impossible to forget, and yet she had peeled back the scab of memory and exposed the raw wound to the open air.
It had also meant confessing just how private and selfish her own motive was for entering the Royal Selection and aiming for the throne.

She couldn't say there hadn't been fear, hadn't been a cowering dread.
As a result of a mistake that her youth could never excuse, Emilia had sacrificed far, far too much. And without ever paying the price for it, she alone had gone on ticking away the years, blithely, even now.
And on top of that, the method she'd chosen for atonement was one that would only be completed by dragging even more people into it.

She thought it only natural to be met with disgust, contempt, to be kept at arm's length.
And yet, even as she thought so, she was aware of another part of herself that held something like certainty that Subaru would never abandon her.
Natsuki Subaru surely couldn't turn his back on her now, no matter how cruel a past she carried, no matter how selfish the wish behind her attempt at atonement—not even after learning all of it.

Emilia had watched, all this time, the results of Subaru's actions—the many things he had been hurt over, grieved over, and still protected to the end.
He was a kind, loyal, deeply caring boy. He took on far too much, never once considering throwing any of it aside, and kept running even as he became covered in wounds.
If her own existence was among the things he carried, then no matter how ugly Emilia's true nature might be, surely he could never let her go.

—That was, in the truest sense, a cruel calculation—one that could even be called wicked.

Even if she shook her head and denied thinking such a thing, it would be a lie to say the thought hadn't crossed her mind, not even once. And if some part of her heart had been hoping for that very outcome, then that was tantamount to her whole being affirming it.

Because she trusted that he was someone who surely wouldn't come to hate her, she had been able to confess a past that might have made him hate her.
Put into words, that was all it really was.

As it turned out, though Subaru couldn't hide his shock and unease at the past Emilia confessed, he never once condemned her for the sin she had committed.
Even as the confession took its mental toll and she pleaded exhaustion, the feel of his hand holding hers had been every bit as full of consideration as always.

Infuriatingly enough, Subaru's feelings had worked out exactly as the ugliest part of Emilia had hoped they would.
Subaru, who usually kept his sharp eyes narrowed, softened them now with worry, caring for Emilia from the bottom of his heart. That kindness of his was, for Emilia, far too sweet a poison.
Sweet enough to melt her heart, melt her resolve, sweet enough that it might have exposed the ugliest of her true feelings.

To entrust him with everything, to have him shoulder in her place the hardship that was wearing her heart down. If she'd let slip even one childish lament, the kind that turns away from anything unpleasant, surely Subaru would spend himself without reserve, exhaust himself utterly, all for Emilia's sake.

—Something like that could never be allowed.

Ever since they'd met, Emilia had been saved by Subaru's hand, again and again.
In the Loot House of the royal capital. In the mansion under threat from Witchbeasts. In the hall of the Royal Selection, under the glares of the other candidates. Amid the shadowy figures who had targeted both the village and the mansion at once.

Emilia had clung to Subaru's hand the whole time. There had even been a time she'd batted that hand away, unable to bear watching him get hurt, telling herself she had no right, no reason, to be treated that way.
And yet Natsuki Subaru had never once tried to abandon Emilia.
More than that—of all things—he had said this to Emilia, who couldn't even understand why she deserved saving:

"Because I like you—that's why I want to be your strength."

Never once, in her whole life, had she experienced anything like that—a declaration of love given with her whole being, without a shred of reasoning behind it.
The only ones who had ever shown Emilia goodwill were, at first, her companions in the Forest of the Elves where she'd once lived together, and after that, for a long stretch of time, only Puck, who had stood in for a parent.

Leaving the forest at Roswaal's invitation, she had come to feel all the more keenly just how poor the standing of Half-Elves truly was, and her understanding had only deepened further after two trips to the royal capital.
Taking up Roswaal's proposal, while pursuing her own goal, she had also nursed the faint hope that she might be able to change, even a little, the deep-rooted contempt this world still held for Half-Elves. And yet it was Emilia herself who, thinking that hope too fragile, had never quite managed to fully believe in it, somewhere deep down.

That was precisely why it meant so much to Emilia—how much it meant that Subaru, that earnest, single-minded boy, had told her he liked her, all of it included: her being a Half-Elf, her being weak and helpless.

Not of the same race, not a being fated from birth to spend her life alongside Emilia, not by anyone's design but through sheer chance—they had met, grown close, and from an exchange of feelings a warm affection had sprouted. How much that had saved Emilia—she could hardly put into words.

And that was exactly why Emilia couldn't rely on Subaru this time.
Every time he shouldered the hardship that Emilia ran up against in her place, more scars that would never fade were added to his body. Not only to his body—wounds to his heart as well.

Emilia didn't think Subaru was someone particularly strong, in heart or in body.
He had a strong will that carried through on his convictions, and a kind heart that cared for those around him—and even so, he wasn't a special kind of person.
Sadly, he got hurt, he wept when in pain, and if he bled too much, he would die.
He was, in that sense, an ordinary person.

And Emilia had no intention of burdening that ordinary boy with any more hardship than he already carried.
If he would simply stay beside her, continuing to support her back as she faced forward, that was all she could ask for—and even that, she felt, was too selfish a wish; she was almost ashamed of herself for wanting even that much.

By having him support her resolve when it threatened to break, Emilia could surely overcome her obstacles without breaking.
But the one who fought the obstacle in front of her—that had to be herself.

"Because, if it isn't..."

If she kept relying on him, leaving everything to him, clinging and leaning and pushing it all onto him, someday even Subaru would come to see her as a burden.
Just the thought that such a day might come was terrifying enough.

It was something she had made herself stop wanting. Something she had given up on, believing that even wanting it would get her nowhere. Something she had tried not to be conscious of, and yet had gone on wanting all along.
And because she had gotten it, because it had been given to her, because she had taken the hand held out to her—Emilia could no longer even imagine losing it.

"————"

Emilia's sin had dyed the forest white, sealing her companions and family beneath snow and ice.
Emilia herself had fallen into a long sleep within the ice, spending nearly a hundred years without even being aware of her own sin, until Puck saved her.

Her own sin, one she wanted to look away from. What made it most sinful of all was that Emilia herself remembered nothing—nothing at all—of the substance of what she had actually done.
The process itself was missing. Even as she recognized that her own actions had plunged everyone into that white stillness, she remembered nothing of what she'd thought, or for what purpose she had done it.

She had thought it only natural to be called the Witch's child.
Woken from the ice by Puck, Emilia had spent seven years in the Elior Forest. Within the frozen woods, she could neither grow nor prepare food, and so for most of her meals she had gone to the villages near the forest.
She could never forget the frightened stares she'd been met with there, or being called "the Witch of the Frozen Forest."

To be reviled as a witch—that suited her just fine.

Haltingly, she had spoken of the resolve needed to overcome the Trial, feeling all the while how hollow her own words sounded. How she was supposed to defeat that "past"—Emilia had no idea. She had simply deflected Subaru's questioning with pleasant-sounding words and chosen to keep shutting herself inside the shell of her own dream.

It was soon after that, while confirming the feel of Subaru's palm, that she had fallen asleep.

—And that time too, she thought, she hadn't dreamed at all.

When she woke, Subaru had been watching over her sleep in the very same position he'd been in when she fell asleep. Feeling an unbearable emotion well up from deep in her chest at that, Emilia let him lead her by the hand out into the Sanctuary at night—and challenged the Trial.

The result, though expected, was that she could not overcome the Trial.

Subaru and Ram had seen her off in front of the tomb. Under the gazes of Garfiel and Ryuzu, residents of the Sanctuary, she had stepped into the tomb—but the Trial gave not even a passing glance to an Emilia who had come without a single clear countermeasure, without so much as one hopeful condition prepared.
The unchanging "past" tormented her heart, ate away at it, cast her aside, and flung her back out.

When consciousness returned to her on the cold, hard floor of the tomb, Emilia realized her cheeks were wet. She hated her own wretchedness so much that she felt even shedding tears was too presumptuous for her.

Unable to overcome the Trial, unable even to grasp a single thread leading toward it, Emilia had left the tomb looking utterly worn down, and was met by Subaru and the others, worried for her.
After that, just as the night before, she had been ordered to sleep soundly within this building, and had lost consciousness the moment she collapsed onto the bed—waking now, this morning.

"In the end, nothing's changed at all... I'm hopeless, aren't I..."

If there was anything she had learned from yesterday, it was only the same unchanging fact: that she was a hopelessly soft-hearted child who kept causing trouble for Subaru and everyone around her, and still couldn't find a single glimmer of hope ahead—a weak, feeble being.

"Puck..."

The pendant hanging at her chest—the green glow adorning its tip was the vessel for Puck, the Spirit under contract with Emilia.
Whenever Emilia called his name in a faint voice, it was practically a promise that he would answer, "What is it?" in that same leisurely voice as always.

That answer had now been silent for nearly two weeks.
At first, she had thought he'd simply entered his dormant period, something that came around once every few months. There had been times before when she couldn't get a response from Puck's side, and each time, Emilia had endured the loneliness and waited for his return, for his awakening.

But his dormant periods had always ended within three or four days before; one lasting this long was a first. More than that, even when Puck had been dormant in the past, if Emilia called out to him in earnest, he would always interrupt his rest and answer her.
Even that response now felt impossibly distant from the Puck of the present.

What had happened to him?
Had something irreversible happened during his dormancy, something that made it impossible for him to appear? And if that were the case, what was she supposed to do?
Despite having spent such a long, long time together with Puck, now that she'd been left alone like this, Emilia couldn't find any way at all to reach out to him.

The Trial. Things with Subaru. Settling accounts with her own past. And now Puck, who wouldn't show his face—every problem that arose showed Emilia not a single ray of hope.

"...I'm such a fool."

Stuck like this, with no one beside her to offer a hand the way he usually would, Emilia nearly let a complaint slip out—but stopped herself just short.
If she let herself do that, her very being would sink to a point beyond redemption. Even now, thinking as poorly of herself as she already did, she didn't want to believe there was a depth lower still than the bottom.

"No, that's no good. Thinking only of the worst like that... Even though he's not showing his face right now, Puck must have his reasons too. And as for the Trial, nothing's been resolved yet either. I have to—I have to pull myself together."

Raising both hands, she slapped her pale cheeks, forcing her spirit to steel itself.
After lifting her face, she ran a comb through the hair her hands had touched to check for tangles.—Even in a thing like this, there was a part of her that struggled. Ordinarily this task, too, had always been left entirely to Puck. Emilia had never once taken the initiative to groom herself.
She checked with her hands whether it was tangled or not. She didn't look in the mirror. The mirror in the room had long since been draped with cloth, left abandoned in a corner where it reflected nothing.

Fiddling with the ends of her hair between her fingers, Emilia judged that the bare minimum of preparation was done. Then, gathering the strands with her fingers, she began braiding her silver hair into a bundle.
Preparing her braids—the decision of Emilia's daily hairstyle belonged to Puck; it was even one of the important clauses included in the contract exchanged between them. For two weeks now, Emilia had received no instructions from Puck on her hairstyle, but she had kept following the instruction given on the last morning he'd been present, continuing it through these two weeks since.
Of course, other things too—exercise after bathing or washing up, conversations with the minor spirits—continued in their small way, far from lively though they were. She kept them up because she was afraid that if she didn't, the connection with Puck, now invisible to her, might vanish entirely.

"—...There."

Parting her hair down the middle and making two braids had been her method until now. But today, she arranged it into a single long braid, let down over her back.
Today too, she would properly keep her contract with Puck, and pray for that contract to continue.
Aware of the sure bond that existed within her—

"...Eh?"

Before Ram appeared with a basin of water, as Emilia moved to prepare her change of clothes, she let out a small sound.
Before her eyes, wide with surprise, in her lowered gaze, was the pendant at her chest.
The pendant, as she had confirmed just now, had the green crystal stone hanging from it—proof, too, that Puck truly existed—but on its surface, a sudden crack had appeared.

"Eh... no, eh...? Wait a minute... w-what is this...?"

Pressing her hand to the crystal that had cracked without warning, Emilia let out a wordless sound.
Her eyes wavered with violent unease as, with trembling fingertips, she timidly stroked the crystal's surface. From the tip of the finger that touched it, the crack spread further, and a small, choked scream escaped her.

"N-no... no, I don't want this... wait, please wait... Puck, please wait..."

Emilia shook her head no, again and again, but the crystal's collapse would not stop.
Trying not to add any stimulus, she poured all her strength into the hand holding the crystal, trying to hold the present state together, but the uncontrollable trembling only hastened the collapse, and within Emilia's hand, the vessel lost its shape.

When this collapse spread through the whole—what would happen then?
Faced with this unprecedented event, a situation she had never even imagined, Emilia's mind went utterly blank.
And yet, there was one thing she understood. It was—

"If this keeps up, Puck's going to—!"

That it meant a parting from a being who was, to Emilia, as good as family.

"————!"

She raised her face. Looked around. No one was there. It was still early morning; she couldn't sense anyone outside yet up and about. Even if she cried out, surely it wouldn't reach anyone. Even if she wanted to run and call for help, she couldn't move, afraid the very tremor of it might pull the trigger on the end.

Holding back her voice, holding even her breath, Emilia stared at the crystal losing its shape in her hand.
There was no solution here either. Even now, at this point, Emilia devoted herself more to delaying the end that was surely, closely approaching than to any effort to actually avert it, too afraid to make a decision.
And then—

"—Ah."

The price for neglecting to avoid it arrived together with the dry sound of the crystal shattering.
On the palm of the stunned, wide-eyed Emilia, the green crystal had completely lost its shape. The crystal had shattered, the fragments losing their color, their circulation of life force gone, their glow dimming.

"H-hey... Puck, this is a lie... isn't it?"

Clinging to hope one last time, Emilia continued to call out to her palm in a hoarse voice.
But the jewel in her hand—now shapeless—was nothing more than green powder and grains. Not a Spirit, not even holding the power to retain a trace amount of Mana—just green stone dust waiting to be scattered by the wind.
To anyone's eyes, Emilia's fragile hope had already been severed.

The only one unable to accept that it hadn't been severed, unable to bear that reality, was Emilia herself.

"No, no... this can't be... it can't be, because... because Puck told me, the very first time, that I was... family... that he'd never leave me alone again..."

As if grasping for that old promise, for the sure bond that had surely been put into words, Emilia repeated it over and over like a delirious child.
—To her plea, the shattered stone answered only with silence.

"...You... liar."

And so, as if unable to bear that silence any longer, her eyes—which had come to understand that this unbearable reality was, nonetheless, fact—turned up toward the ceiling, her violet eyes trembling with tears, and—

"Puck's father, you liar!!"

She dropped to her knees and hurled the shattered fragments at the wall.
There came the faint sound of stone fragments striking the wooden wall, and it seemed to her to hint at just how anticlimactic, how abrupt, this parting between Emilia and Puck truly was.

Emilia covered her face with her hands and went on letting out sobs.
The tears did not come.

Only an utterly empty, hollow feeling of loss weighed down heavily, thickly, upon Emilia's chest.
