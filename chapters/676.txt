Re:Zero EX: "Iris and the King of Thorns" (Part 1)


　—An ancient tale tells.

　It is the story of a lonely king and a single girl.
　A story of a king who was alone until he met a girl and was alone no longer.

　A story where everything was a memory as brief as a blink, as passionate as a fire burning everything to ashes, as vivid as to be worth trading all else for, leading into a future beyond all they had offered up.

　A story of a king and a girl that remains, for long ages, as a burning, burning flame in the hearts of the people.
　This is an ancient tale containing the unwritten historical truths behind the tale that was set down.


　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　△▼△▼△▼△


　—The Nineteenth Prince of the Sacred Vollachia Empire, Eugard Elcanti.

　The infant who would later be called the "Thorn Emperor" was born into House Elcanti as the nineteenth son inheriting the blood of the honorable Emperor Radcain Vollachia.

　House Elcanti, one of the prestigious families serving Vollachia for generations, was a rare house in an empire where the iron-and-blood rule "Citizens of the Empire must be strong" breathed life into all things, recognized for achievements other than prowess on the battlefield.
　In the Vollachia Empire, which was strictly harsh toward those who could not prove their valor on the field of battle, House Elcanti was the only one granted the position of Special High Count, praised for its political acumen and connections in every direction.

　This special treatment of House Elcanti was not something that pleased the other houses.
　Backbiting that they had used low cunning and curried favor with the Imperial Capital was bad enough, but this friction between aristocrats did not subside until blades were drawn.

　—In the end, whose scheme it was remained unknown even to posterity.

　What was certain was that the malice of someone seeking to eliminate House Elcanti directed its spearhead at Eugard Elcanti while he was still a young child.
　And that malice produced results that decisively twisted the life of Eugard Elcanti, the history of the Sacred Vollachia Empire, and even the destiny of the world.


　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　△▼△▼△▼△


　A small, ordinary village on the edge of House Elcanti's domain was attacked.
　That the village, which possessed no notable characteristics, became the target of bandits should be called the result of a double stroke of bad luck for both victims and perpetrators.
　Attacking an impoverished village brought little profit and was dangerous even for bandits.
　That the bandits did so anyway was proof of how driven into a corner they were; it just so happened that the village was within their reach.

　—And the fact that House Elcanti's standing army had been conducting drills near that village diverged the fates of the village and the bandits into stark contrast.


　Banners bearing the coat of arms of House Elcanti were raised throughout the village, and after the corpses of the bandits trampled by regular soldiers were burned all at once, a banquet was held in the village.
　It was a feast welcoming the lord's troops who had offered to help rebuild the village after defeating the bandits. So as not to spurn the villagers' kindness, the soldiers were treated to food, drink, and song.

"..."

　Listening to that clamor from afar, Eugard Elcanti stood alone atop a hill.
　In his attempt to distance himself from the songs and laughter carried on the night wind, letting the stars guide his way, Eugard found himself standing on a small hill before he knew it. His figure, unable to mingle in the merrymaking, looked frail; surely no one would guess he was the young lord of this Elcanti domain.

　However, Eugard, twenty years of age, was undeniably the lord of the Elcanti domain, and the key figure who had prevented in advance the crisis of a village in his domain being raided by bandits.

　Rumors had been circulating about a group of rogues ravaging adjacent territories. Hearing word that a mixed band formed of strays from various lands was drifting toward the Elcanti domain, Eugard had feigned training exercises for the standing army to patrol the vicinity of high-risk villages, arranging the circumstances that led to this successful bandit subjugation.

　Naturally, Eugard made no point of flaunting such facts.
　It was enough that the peace of his subjects had been protected before it could be critically shattered. There was no need to make the villagers conscious of anything beyond that—least of all, the "King of Thorns."

"..."

　Anyone who drew close to Eugard would be tormented by the binding of thorns.
　That was an unchanging truth since Eugard had first gained awareness, an inescapable karma that haunted his life without known cause.
　The binding of thorns, which hurt blood family and servants, domain folk and criminals alike without distinction, caused people to call Eugard the "King of Thorns" and forced him to live alone.
　That was why, even now, Eugard remained far from the joyful commotion of the people, alone atop the hill.

"..."

　He could not join the feast, but Eugard had many duties to perform.
　During the training he had insisted on accompanying, as far as he could see from a distance, every one of his soldiers had applied themselves earnestly. In the subsequent bandit subjugation, too, their performance had been remarkable.
　Since he could not offer them words of praise, he must reward each and every one of them justly with compensation.

　Yes. Even alone, there was much to do.

"Wouldn't counting the stars in the sky soothe your heart more than staring into pitch darkness?"

tIt was while he was lost in such contemplation that a voice called out to him from behind.
　Eugard felt a shock as though struck by lightning and deeply regretted his carelessness. It had been a long time since he had allowed anyone to approach him so defenselessly.
　As a member of Vollachian royalty, as the "King of Thorns," it was an unforgivable blunder.
　And yet—

"Um, you're the leader of those soldiers, right? Isn't it lonely standing all by yourself in a place like this?"

tEugard whipped around, and into his sight came a village girl of plain features with fair, unadorned skin and flaxen hair tied up in a single bundle.
tStrangely, quite unconcernedly, with over a dozen black sheep crowding around her waist.

"Wherefore..."

"Huh?"

"Wherefore are you surrounded by so many sheep?"

tTo the girl who embodied the word pastoral better than anyone could, Eugard, still unable to shake off his shock, asked the question just as he saw it with his eyes.
tIn truth, Eugard should have distanced himself before the thorns bound the girl's heart.
tYet, to the question from Eugard who had failed to do so, the girl stroked one of the sheep pressing its nose against her waist and said, "Well..."

"...Personal charm, maybe? Animals have always taken a liking to me since I was little."

"I see... Personal charm is something that works even on animals, then."

"Oh no, he's taking it seriously...! I mean, no, it's because I always take care of them! Feeding them and taking them for walks is the job I was assigned."

tThe girl stroked a sheep with one hand and waved her other hand back and forth as she answered. Then, shifting her eyes toward the bonfire-lit banquet in the village, she said,

"Um, do you not like animals, mister soldier? Is that why you stayed away from the village and are standing here all spaced out by yourself?"

"...No. I did not distance myself from the village out of dislike for sheep or cattle. Wool and milk must be indispensable sources of income for your village. Continue to raise them in greater numbers and size in the future."

"You're taking our village's finances so seriously..."

tWhen he offered his personal opinion on the village's livestock industry, the girl expressed great surprise.
tAt her reaction he reflected on his response, but found nothing odd. To begin with, Eugard lacked both the knowledge and experience to judge what might be strange.

"If my words offended you, I offer my apologies. I had no intention of insulting you, nor did I mean to dispute whatever annual livestock plans your village may have drawn up."

"Not at all, not at all! It didn't offend me or anything... Besides, I don't even know the results of any discussions about how we're going to market the village's wool from now on!"

"What? But in a small community such as a village, is it not unnatural that such policies are not shared with you, a worker? ...Could it be that you do not get along with the villagers?"

"I-I don't really want to be worried about that by someone moping alone on a hill!"

"..."

"Ah."

tAfter blurting it out, the girl covered her mouth with a look of having said something she shouldn't have. But words once spoken could not be taken back. Not for the girl, and not for Eugard either.

"Fair enough."

tLeaving behind those words, Eugard turned his back on the girl, who seemed to have been displeased after all.
tHe should have done this from the start. Though it had been a precious opportunity to exchange words with another at an unexpected proximity, the "King of Thorns" should not interact with people after all—

"Wait!"

t—or so he concluded at that very moment.
tA hand reached out unexpectedly and grabbed his sleeve, leaving Eugard speechless for real this time.

"—!"

tJust getting close was enough for Eugard's thorns to bind others.
tHow much worse if they actually touched him? What came to mind was the memory of his wet nurse coughing up blood and writhing in agony. The fate of the nurse who suffered under the thorn binding yet concealed the fact to continue attending to Eugard—since then, Eugard had never touched anyone.
tThere was likely no other member of royalty who changed his own clothes and cleaned his own room.

tAnd she had touched Eugard, stopping him in his tracks.
tIntolerable pain would gnaw at the girl, and a scream cursing the world would rise from her slender throat—

"I-Is something wrong?"

t—or so it should have been.
tYet the girl, who ought to have been gnawed by thorns and writhing in pain, was looking at Eugard quizzically. Her hand was unmistakably pulling at the sleeve of Eugard's clothes.

"Why..."

tIt was impossible. The impossible was happening.
tWhy was this girl able to touch Eugard without being tormented by the binding of thorns?

"Ah! Hey, you guys?!"

tAs if in answer to Eugard's question, the black sheep crowding around the girl bleated in unison and fled down the hill, jostling each other to escape.
tEugard's thorn binding made no distinction between humans and animals.
