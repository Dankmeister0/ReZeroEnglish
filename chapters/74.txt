Arc 2 Interlude: "Secret Talks Under the Moon"

The morning Subaru and Emilia sealed their promise — from there, time advances roughly half a day.

The place is the topmost floor of Roswaal Manor, the room of the master at its center.
When secret talks are held there at night, the participants are always, without fail, the same two people.

"First, let's offer thanks for your labors while away. You worked so hard to bring the situation under control. Thanks to you, I daresay the damage was kept to a minimum."

The soft voice carried a distinctly masculine luster to it, but its tone was, as ever, so flippant that it left the listener with a certain bewilderment.
At those words from Roswaal, the master of the room, the maid facing him — apparently well used to it — showed no sign of concern and bowed at the waist.

"You flatter me with such words. And besides, in the end I troubled Roswaal-sama to lend his own hand..."

"Ah, no need to mention that. Incinerating a few vermin from the forest is hardly any trouble or effort at all for me."

Roswaal waved a hand, speaking of it as though it were nothing at all.
Ram — the girl before him — knew that these words were neither exaggeration, nor vanity, nor false modesty, but simply, plainly, the unvarnished truth. And so she interjected nothing.

"Pursuing blame will only bring us down. Let's have a more constructive conversation instead. — For instance, how is Subaru-kun's condition progressing since then?"

Roswaal tossed the question at Ram, who had fallen silent.
Ram gave a small shake of her head at the question.

"His body is nearly healed. Beatrice-sama poured her all into the treatment, complaining all the while."

"Now that really is something, isn't it. I've known her a long while, but I've never once seen that child throw herself behind someone like this. Love at first sight, perhaps?"

"From what Ram observed, it didn't look like that sort of feeling, but..."

She answered while recalling the scene of Beatrice's treatment, then, as if collecting herself, Ram raised a hand and added, "However —"

"Thanks to Beatrice-sama's treatment, he was saved. Had she not been present, whether Barusu's life would still be with us is doubtful."

"I suppose we can chalk that up to Subaru-kun's own good fortune. In truth, there really aren't many healers on par with Beatrice. Both you and I are quite hopeless in that department. — All we're good at is hurting people. It's rather sickening, really."

Shaking his head, Roswaal's expression drooped ever so slightly.
Anyone watching would assume the content of his words bordered on self-loathing, and that his lowered face matched it in kind.
In truth, though, his face twisted into neither anguish nor self-directed disgust — it merely wore its usual transparent smile.
Even so, Roswaal prefaced his next words with "But," glancing up at Ram from beneath his brow.

"Starting with a shake of the head in denial — that must mean this isn't a situation where we can simply be glad his life was saved, hm?"

"Yes. There are several problems."

Nodding at the question, Ram paused a beat before continuing. Once she had organized in her own mind what needed conveying, she began anew.

"Barusu forcibly activated his Gate from a depleted state twice within a short span. On top of that, he had life-threatening wounds healed with Magic... Given how much strain was put on prying the Gate open and overusing it, there's no telling how long it will take before it functions properly again."

"Is that the Great Spirit-sama and Beatrice's assessment?"

"Yes."

Roswaal folded his hands and closed his eyes, letting the information sink in.
Damage to the Gate — in a world where one cannot separate one's life from Mana, that was equivalent to bearing an exceedingly troublesome affliction.
As a court mage, someone who handled Mana more intimately than most, Roswaal could feel all the more keenly just how inconvenient Subaru's current state must be.
He hoped for as swift a recovery as possible, even by a single day —

"Though individual differences exist in Gate repair, this could still take years. We've forced a cruel choice upon him, it seems."

At Roswaal's weighty words, Ram nodded her agreement, and continued further.

"And there is also the matter of the Curse."

"— The danger of it activating should have passed, hasn't it?"

"The caster — in this case, that would be the Ulgarms — has been wiped out entirely, so there is no caster remaining. Therefore the Curse cannot activate... but the spell itself still remains within Barusu's body."

"As for removing it, I'd rather hear a specialist's opinion."

In this case, "specialist" referred to Beatrice, who was well versed in matters of Curse Arts as well. Asked for her opinion, Ram shook her head.

"Even for Beatrice-sama, unraveling threads tangled this intricately is said to be difficult."

The cursed hand gnawing at Subaru's body had rooted itself deeper than anyone had imagined.
The spell bound his flesh so tightly that even the great Beatrice hesitated to attempt it.

That said, the spell itself, having lost its caster, posed no threat worth the name.
Ordinarily, a cast spell cannot activate without the caster's own awareness of it, and this particular curse in question was of the type that transfers Mana from the target to the caster. With no destination for that transfer to go to, the curse's activation lacked even its basic preconditions.
Therefore, the spell remaining in Subaru's body was not something that would cause problems if left alone.
However —

"Given that you're raising it as a problem, that must mean you've confirmed what I asked you to look into."

Roswaal's question lacked a subject, but Ram nodded without hesitation. As he waited for her reply, Ram touched her own forehead.

"Limited to those individuals whose corpses could be confirmed, but every single Ulgarm discovered had been made 'Hornless.'"

Touching the scar beneath her headdress, Ram ignored the faint throb of the old wound and reported to Roswaal flatly, without inflection.
Roswaal let out a breath at her manner, then creaked the back of his chair.

"That matched what I observed as well. Likely the entire pack was the same."

"But is that even possible? Breaking the horns of that many Witchbeasts..."

"Oh, nothing to it. Break them while they're still young, or first break the horns of about ten, then have those ten attack smaller numbers and break theirs in turn. Repeat the same trick, and before long the whole pack simply ends up hornless of its own accord."

Ram showed her understanding at Roswaal's explanation, and watching this, Roswaal closed one eye. The yellow of his odd-colored eyes shut, and his clear blue eye fixed on Ram.

"But then, that changes things somewhat. All the more so given that Subaru-kun has twice now been our benefactor."

At Roswaal's languid voice, Ram voiced her agreement with an "Indeed."

"A Witchbeast whose horn has been broken obeys whoever broke it. — That means there is a fool out there who intentionally set out to ravage this manor, or perhaps Roswaal-sama's domain."

"This is turning into Royal Selection business again, isn't it. Between this and the lure set for Garfiel's side, it seems we're quite the thorn in someone's side."

"Garfiel... What did he say?"

Ram raised her brows at the mention of a familiar name, and asked timidly. Roswaal shrugged with a wry smile in response.

"Of course, he flatly denied ever recalling sending out any summons. We'll switch to new passphrases, and overhaul how we make contact, roughly speaking. Well, whether we could even count on their help to begin with was already fairly uncertain."

Roswaal mused this while touching his chin.
He spoke of it breezily, but Ram understood full well this was not something to be brushed off lightly. To begin with, this was plainly a battle with slim odds of victory. If so, then naturally, the more cards one held in hand to raise those odds, the better.

Ram, well aware that she herself was one such card, could do nothing but watch Roswaal's anguish, and it galled her endlessly.
But her train of thought was interrupted by Roswaal waving a hand and saying, "No, no."

"Let's return to the matter at hand. That would make the 'parent' of the Witchbeasts, I suppose. Do we have any leads?"

"Tentatively. Though the trail has gone completely cold."

"Escaped... or perhaps was disposed of?"

"I cannot say which. What we do know is only that the child Barusu and Rem were supposed to have brought back from the forest has been missing since the following day."

Regarding the "girl with braided hair" that Rem and Subaru had supposedly brought back from the forest, the villagers unanimously stated she was a stranger to them. The children who had spent time with her likewise testified that she was someone who had simply drifted into their group.
The adults had judged her an outsider, much like Subaru; the children, meanwhile, had never even thought to question the girl's identity in the first place. Slipping through the cracks of these two mindsets, the household had noticed her presence too late, and was left playing catch-up.

Thus, the primary suspect was this girl who had now vanished.
In this case, her youthful appearance served as no counterevidence whatsoever. Beings that could only have grown up in such a world — well, there were more than enough of those to go around.

Having heard Ram's report in full, Roswaal toyed with his hair, saying "What a bother."

"A Bowel Hunter in the royal capital, a beastmaster in my own domain. What an odd repertoire of trouble to get tangled up with."

"No matter how many oddities are gathered against you, surely Roswaal-sama is not one to be discouraged by that?"

"My my, aren't you getting cheeky. How pleasing."

Roswaal laughed and beckoned Ram over. Following his gesture, Ram moved from the center of the room toward the back — crossing past the ebony desk — to Roswaal's side.
Then, Roswaal extended an arm, drew her small frame close, and set her upon his knee.
And then —

"I couldn't attend to you for one whole night, due to my own circumstances. That must have been quite hard on you, hm?"

"I understand that Roswaal-sama is busy. Please, put Ram's needs last, even until the very end, that would be more than —"

"Ram, haven't I always told you?"

Sliding a finger along the chin of Ram, whose eyes were downcast, Roswaal tilted her face up slightly and smiled.

"You and Rem are, to me, among my most precious, irreplaceable treasures. Yes — if, hypothetically, something had happened to either of you in this whole affair, I'm not even confident I could have kept myself in check."

He stretched his free hand toward the ceiling in a theatrical gesture as he spoke. Ram gazed at him, her face rapt and intoxicated as though feverish.

"Ram and Rem are, to Roswaal-sama, precious —"

"Yes, you and Rem are, to me, precious, dear, and above all, essential —"

Piling word upon word, feeling upon feeling, Roswaal paused a beat, then smiled at her, radiant —



"— indispensable pieces on my board."



Roswaal cast those words at Ram with utter composure.
In them was contained not a shred of negative feeling such as guilt — only the ring of someone purely, simply enumerating facts as he understood them within himself.

And so, having had her own existence plainly declared to be a mere piece, Ram received these words and —

"— Yes."

— nodded back in dazed rapture, her cheeks flushed.

At her attitude, which could be called either obedience or infatuation, Roswaal nodded with satisfaction.
Drawing Ram closer still upon his lap, enfolding her small frame entirely within his arms, he said,

"Now then, shall we begin? You've pushed yourself rather hard too, haven't you? I told you to hold back, yet you let out quite a bit of Mana, didn't you?"

"My apologies... Please, go ahead."

At Roswaal's words, Ram removed the white headdress adorning her pink hair.
Beneath that ornament, as his fingers parted her pink hair, a faint white scar appeared just above her forehead.
— A remnant of the era when she was once made into an Oni, hailed as a child prodigy.

The scar bore damage from the exercise of Mana, and even now it faintly seeped blood. Roswaal traced the wound with a finger, an ache in his expression.

"— Blessings of the Stars be upon you."

Four colors of light traveled along Roswaal's arm, converging at his fingertips the instant they transformed into white light — crossing over his fingers, that light poured into the scar Ram bore.

— Directly transferring Mana to another person is an art requiring an extraordinarily high degree of skill.

Unless the proportions of Mana across each attribute are perfectly balanced, the Mana converts into raw force and wounds the target's body. This "treatment" was possible only because Roswaal was compatible with all four colors of Mana and could wield them at an elevated level.

For the Oni Tribe, the horn upon the forehead signifies the line through which Mana passes, both in and out.
Functioning much like a Gate, yet possessing physical substance, it is an organ that channels Mana even more densely than a Gate does, and the body itself is built to match.
But Ram had lost that horn to external forces, and neither the amount of Mana she could take in nor the amount she could expel was enough to satisfy her body's needs.
Left untended, her body would only continue to waste away — and to sustain it, these nightly secret meetings with Roswaal were carried out as a daily routine.

As Mana was poured through the scar where her horn once was, Ram gave a small shudder of her shoulders.

"Ahh... nn..."

"No matter how many times I hear it, it's a scene poisonous to both eye and ear."

Roswaal gave a faint wry smile at Ram, her breath quickening, her cheeks flushed with a sensuous color.
Ram cast him a sidelong glance in turn.

"If you find it poisonous, you're welcome to simply dispose of me, you know."

"Of someone practically my own daughter? My my, wouldn't that be a fine display of the depraved, degenerate noble at his finest. Doesn't that excite you?"

Deflecting Ram's temptation smoothly, Roswaal continued the act of pouring Mana.
As vitality filled her body and she felt her Gate swell with warmth, Ram, surrendering herself to the sweet sensation, suddenly spoke up.

"Oh, that's right. There is something Ram must report to Roswaal-sama..."

"What is it?"

"It concerns Rem."

"There's no reprimand coming, if that's your worry. Well, of course, there might be a small scolding, but beyond that I have no intention of punishing her."

Roswaal cut in, pre-empting her. But Ram gave a small "No," and, her lips troubled as though unsure how best to convey it, said,

"Rem has grown attached to Barusu."

"Hmm?"

"Something about Barusu seems to have touched upon Rem's weak point."

It concerned her twin sister.
As her elder sister, Ram understood all too well what state of mind Rem must be in. And she also understood that the reverse — her thoughtless younger sister grasping Ram's own feelings — would never happen.

Roswaal, initially showing a flicker of bewilderment, gradually came to look convinced as Ram explained, and groaned a difficult "Hmm."

"Rem, is it. Well, I suppose it's not so strange, is it. Unlike you, that child doesn't serve me out of loyalty, after all."

Their master's assessment of her sister was, if one could call it that, rather harsh — and yet Ram offered no rebuttal whatsoever, answering only with silence. Which was to say, a silent affirmation.
Unlike Ram, who devoted herself to Roswaal out of unconditional loyalty, Rem's devotion was weighted far more heavily toward a sense of self-preservation.

To Rem, Roswaal was, above all, "the one who protects her elder sister, Ram." It was a way of thinking born from Rem's dependency, one so fixed in her mind that her sister's existence had become her own reason for being, and her actions to protect that small community tended toward the rash and hasty. Left unwatched, she was quite capable of unilaterally "dealing with" anything that seemed even remotely threatening.
In that sense, Ram's honest assessment was that Subaru — having established trust before being attacked rather than after — had rather narrowly escaped with his life.

Of course, even holding such an assessment, to Ram, Rem remained the most adorable little sister in the world, and her place in Ram's priorities ranked higher even than Ram's own self.
— Though, asked whether she ranked first, Ram could not, at present, honestly nod yes to that.

"Whatever Rem's feelings may be, Ram will remain within my grasp. Which means, inevitably, she too has no choice but to remain there as well. See? Nothing has changed from before, nothing at all."

"That's... true, I suppose. Though it could also be said that, with something new now precious to Rem, the chances of that child jumping to rash conclusions have only grown."

"Then let's make sure to give her fair warning beforehand. That, I daresay, is tomorrow's important task."

Even as he spoke half in jest, the light faded from Roswaal's palm.
The treatment was complete. Feeling vitality restored within her, Ram reluctantly climbed down from Roswaal's lap to the floor.
Turning her body around, and as she faced him again, Roswaal rose his tall frame from the chair.

"In any case, things are about to get busy again. It'll be hard on you, but I'll be counting on both you and Rem, understood?"

"As you command. This body has belonged to Roswaal-sama ever since that night of flames."

Pinching the hem of her skirt, she bent her knees in a small bow on the spot.
Receiving her show of loyalty, Roswaal clasped his hands behind his back and walked toward the window. With Ram falling in at his side, he drew open the curtain.

Looking up at the sky — a sky where the shadow of a plump, full moon floated — Roswaal narrowed his mismatched eyes just slightly.

"This Royal Selection, I must win it, no matter what. For the sake of my own goal."

He murmured, and the arm he extended drew Ram close by the shoulder.
Feeling once more the warmth of that tall frame close beside her, Ram quietly closed her eyes and bowed her head.

Listening to the voice of the man beside her — her own master, the one to whom she had entrusted her Soul —


"— For the day I kill the Dragon."


— The night quietly deepens.
Sweeping away even the turbulent days, washing and purifying everything, as though welcoming in a new world.
