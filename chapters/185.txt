Arc 4 Chapter 19: "Homework"

—Having said his farewells to his father, Subaru made his way home, walking slowly through the familiar streets while he put his feelings in order.

As he walked, a swirl of emotions welled up in Subaru's chest.
He hadn't wandered around outside in broad daylight like this since he'd started refusing to go to school. And yet, the scenery he must have walked past countless times looked different now — not only because of the sunlight, surely.

"Well, it's not like a truant kid can go strutting around under the sun looking all proud of himself."

He couldn't stand the thought of becoming neighborhood gossip, or getting picked up by a policeman.
Even Subaru — who, without realizing it himself, harbored a secret wish to be hated by his father and mother — apparently didn't want things to go so far as getting hated over a run-in with the police.

Every street around the house held some memory or another.
Savoring them, confirming them through the soles of his feet, he passed through the quiet — or rather, simply deserted — residential streets, and by the time the dried tear-tracks on his cheeks had finally stopped bothering him, he'd arrived in front of his own gate.

He drew a breath once, and held it.
The countless emotions passing through his closed-eyed heart — he swallowed them all down, and—

"—I'm home."

He opened the door and called the words into the house.
Amused by how tense he felt, he waited for a reply. But the reply that should have come didn't come at all. Frowning in puzzlement, Subaru took off his shoes and stepped up into the house. He let his gaze wander, searching for his mother, who ought to be there—

"...Oh, welcob home."

—Turning around in front of the refrigerator, his mother, mayonnaise tube in her mouth, had called out those words to Subaru as he came home.

"...I'm home."

All the tension from a moment ago vanished without a trace — Subaru's shoulders slumped, and with a wry smile, he somehow managed to respond in kind.

※※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※

"I said 'I'm home' and you didn't answer, so I got worried something happened, you know?"

"Something did happen. See, Mom's personal mayonnaise ran out. So I was secretly sucking down Dad's mayonnaise when... Lately I feel like Subaru's voice and Dad's have started sounding alike. Can't tell you two apart on the phone, you know."

"Your logic's jumping around a bit, but so you were hiding because you couldn't tell me and Dad apart. Though if you were 'hiding,' I'd have liked a bit more actual sneaking around."

Facing off in the living room with his mayo-huffing mother, Subaru squeezed the dented, sucked-hollow mayonnaise bottle back into shape and, for no particular reason, stood it upright on the table.
Watching him, Naoko tilted her head.

"Keep it a secret from your father, okay? When I suck on Dad's mayonnaise, see, I get to taste my favorite mayonnaise flavor and a taste of Dad at the same time — feels like a bargain, doesn't it?"

"I don't know how to respond to hearing something that sounds like testimony from a kid who licks recorders! 'Combine two things you like and it's the best thing ever' — what are you, a grade-schooler?!"

"So, what happened to your father? Did you leave him behind? Subaru, since when did you get fast enough to leave your father in the dust?"

"There's no way I could beat Dad in a footrace to begin—wait, no, actually, maybe there is."

At his mother's question, Subaru had almost blurted out a reflexive objection, but he swallowed it back down.
When had he last done anything like race his father? Back then, Kenichi had left him behind without holding back, ungentlemanly as that was for a grown man to do to his kid, and Subaru had watched his father's retreating back with a chest full of frustration and just a little bit of admiration.
But years had passed since then. If he compared foot speed with his father now, surely he wouldn't be left behind like that anymore — if anything, he probably wouldn't even lose.

Subaru's admiration alone had swollen out of proportion, until Kenichi's presence within him had grown too large. But even so, mistaking the essence of the thing wouldn't save anyone.

"In the end, I guess everything I did was half-assed, huh."

As he spoke, Subaru leaned back, making the chair creak, and stretched hard. Watching him do it, Naoko put a hand to her mouth and laughed.

"What? Something funny happen?"

"I was just thinking that gesture is just like your father. He's always stretched his back against the chair like that too, ever since way back. Sometimes he even tips right over from the momentum."

"So it's not just my voice, my mannerisms are turning into his too, huh. Can't decide right now if that's a good thing or a bad thing."

"I think it's a good thing. —Yep, you really are that man's child."

Thud — his heart gave one hard beat, and Subaru desperately fought back a groan rising in his throat. At his reaction — his expression stiffening, his eyes going wide — Naoko blinked those sharp eyes of hers, so like Subaru's own. Breathing through his nose, he quickly settled the pounding in his chest, and—

"If I stick around too long, I'm gonna end up with nothing but regrets about leaving..."

He said, and rose from his seat. Under his mother's puzzled upward gaze, Subaru scratched his cheek with a finger.

"Hey, so, there's something I wanted to ask you."

"Sure, go ahead."

Utterly unaware of his hesitation, of his feet dragging, Naoko kept her easygoing air and kept glancing at the mayonnaise. She was probably being driven by an urge to get more mayonnaise down her throat that was on the same level as wanting to hear the rest of her son's story.
Completely disarmed by how unchanging she was, Subaru let out a small laugh.

"—Where'd we put my school uniform again?"

※※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※

—He slid his arms into a freshly ironed dress shirt, stepped into a pair of neatly pressed slacks. He fastened his belt, stood before the mirror, and struggled his way into a dark green necktie. Then he pulled on the navy blazer over it all.

"Student Natsuki Subaru, complete... roughly three months since the last time, huh."

Checking his completed reflection in the mirror, Subaru let out a breath, as if he'd just finished a job of work.
Reflected in the full-length mirror was the school uniform he hadn't worn in a long, long while. The blazer-type uniform required tying a necktie every morning, a hassle he'd always hated, feeling like it stole a whole minute of his precious morning sleep.
Two years and a bit of tying it every single day, and he'd never gotten any better at it — the knot still looked somehow misshapen, and thinking that this was the last time gave him a complicated feeling.

"Since it's the last time, should I make it perfect, or just do it like always?"

Even as he said it, though, he already had his answer.
He gave the loosely puffed knot of his tie a light flick with a finger, then left its clumsy shape as it was and turned his back on the mirror. He swept his gaze around the room, and picked up his school bag.
Now, from any angle you looked at him, he was a model high schooler, fully prepared to head off to school.

"Unfortunately it's already past homeroom — hell, it's about time for third period. Heading out after the sun's already fully up like this, there's no 'model' anything about it."

Scratching his head with a wry smile, Subaru stretched right where he stood, then turned to leave the room — but just before he did, he turned back to look around it, as if suddenly remembering something.
For Subaru, who had never once moved house in his entire life, this room was the only place he could call "his own room." Given to him when he'd entered middle school, it was the room he'd slept and woken in for nearly six years now. —And this, too, would likely be the last time.

"――――"

Without a word, Subaru simply bowed his head, quietly.
Into that single gesture, he poured everything from those six years.

When the long, long bow finally ended, Subaru lifted his face, turned brightly on his heel, and put the room behind him. Then he shut the door with a click. He went straight down the stairs to the first floor, and Naoko, waiting in the living room, widened those sharp eyes of hers in surprise.

"Oh my. You asked where your uniform and dress shirt were, so I thought maybe you were going to burn them and got all kinds of things ready... guess that was all for nothing."

"Your son asks where his uniform is and your mind jumps straight to 'burning'? And what kind of prep were you even doing for that — is this potato and these skewered franks part of it...?"

At the array of ingredients lined up on the table, Subaru was rendered speechless by his mother's laid-back-ness, which had exceeded even his own expectations. Naoko, meanwhile, completely oblivious to Subaru's horror, looked him up and down in his changed clothes.

"Mm-hm, it suits you. Dressing all grown-up like that cancels out the look in your eyes a bit, makes you seem a little more composed."

"You are, in real time, right now, stealing my composure away from me!"

"Why are you so on edge? Want some mayonnaise to calm down?"

"I'm not really in the mood right now..."

"Yeah, I figured."

The mayonnaise held out to him — the one with "Su" written on the cap, the named mayonnaise that marked it as Subaru's own property. But when he pushed it back, saying he wasn't in the mood to lick it right now, Naoko nodded as though she'd known exactly how he'd react.

"Because Subaru, you don't actually like mayonnaise all that much, do you."

"――――"

"You just licked it along with us because you love your father and me so much."

Naoko set the Subaru-branded mayonnaise on the table, spinning it round and round as she spoke. Struck by her words, Subaru felt his throat close up in surprise. Flustered, he sucked in a breath, his lips trembling.

"Wh-what are you even..."

"Okay then, Subaru, if you had to choose between the world and mayonnaise, which would you pick?"

"I mean, obviously the world..."

"See?"

"You're terrible at analogies!! What's with that smug 'see?'! Anyone who picks mayonnaise in that scenario doesn't love mayonnaise — they hate the world!"

Having called out his mother's wildly off-target statement, Subaru glared at the mayonnaise on the table, breathing hard. Then he gave a small snort.

"...Since when did you think that, anyway."

"For ages now. Because when your father and I run out of our personal mayonnaise, we get depressed like it's the end of the world, but Subaru never did."

"I just despaired at how high a bar you two were setting."

Subaru went limp at his mother's words. But inside, he was far from calm.
Subaru was, without question, a bona fide mayo-lover. There was no lie in that; ask him to name a condiment and mayonnaise came before anything else. Mayonnaise on karaage, of course. He was hardcore enough to even spread mayonnaise on mayonnaise-flavored snacks.
But the reason he clung to mayonnaise so obsessively was—

"I guess I wanted to taste the same thing you two looked like you were enjoying so much. Man, when you really think about it, I'm a father-complex, mother-complex, Famicom kid, huh..."

"Want me to add 'Super' to that?"

"'Super Family Complex, Super Famicom for short' — shut up."

After exchanging that pointless bit of conversation, Subaru shook his head and let out a long breath. Then, unhurriedly, he picked up the mayonnaise from the table.

"Oh."

"—Ahh. Mm, delicious! Real, homegrown mayonnaise after all this time really is different! The natural-ingredient mayo over there is fine, but the real deal is mayonnaise loaded up with synthetic food coloring and thoroughly unhealthy! What they've got over there is more like... mayonnaisé."

Squeezing it like he was wringing it out, he swallowed down the nearly-full bottle of mayonnaise in one go. A tangy, sour taste passed over his tongue, and a searing heat ran through his throat and chest.
This — this was the mayonnaissage that mayonnaise addicts everywhere couldn't help but love.

Wiping the white smear left around his mouth with the back of his hand, Subaru curled his cheek into a grin at his mother's startled face.

"I can't beat you two when it comes to loving mayonnaise, but I'm still a devoted believer who loves the stuff, no doubt about it. I swear it on every mayonnaise cap I've ever finished off."

Incidentally, stored in his room's closet were all the mayonnaise caps Subaru had personally used up over the years. Swollen to an abnormal quantity, the count stood at seven hundred and seventy-six. And with this one, just now emptied, that made seven hundred and seventy-seven.

"Triple sevens. Go ahead and put it away in my closet later, would you."

"Ooh, three sevens, how auspicious. Just the other day your father was thrilled to hit four sevens too."

"Literally an entire order of magnitude more love than me!"

His mother happily accepted the mayonnaise. His sense of accomplishment thoroughly ruined, Subaru wore an unconvinced expression, but quickly composed his face again.

"Well... guess I'll get going, then."

"Oh, if you're going to the convenience store, I want a cream puff, so pick one up for me."

"Could you look at what I'm wearing and use your imagination before you say things like that?!"

He spread both arms wide to show off his outfit. Naoko laughed, saying, "Just kidding, just kidding."

"Are you off to school now? I'm happy about it, but... won't you stand out in a bad way? Why not leave for tomorrow what you can do tomorrow?"

"Would you stop trying to knock the wind out of your son's motivation like that. I've already got a spineless attitude of being hard on others and soft on myself soaked all the way through."

"If Subaru really were a kid like that, Mom wouldn't have had such a hard time of it, though."

To Subaru's bit of self-deprecating humor, Naoko gave her usual airheaded response as ever. As Subaru narrowed his eyes at her, his mother said "up we go" and stood up.

"Okay, Mom's gonna go grab a jacket, so wait a sec."

"Wait a sec... don't tell me you're coming with me? Breaking out of my shut-in phase only to go to school with a parent in tow isn't just a punishment game, it's on a whole other level!"

"I'm not going all the way to school. Just to the convenience store right there, to buy mayonnaise and a cream puff. You shouldn't get so spoiled, you know."

"Wait, what?! Why does it sound like I was the one who asked you to come along?!"

To Subaru's shout of confusion at the unconvincing turn of events, his mother gave a perfunctory "yes, yes" and headed off toward her room. And so, by default, it became a situation where she'd walk part of the way to school with him.

"No, no... give me a break. Honestly."

Even as he said it, Subaru's cheeks had gone slack with relief.
—That the moment of saying goodbye to his mother had been put off, even a little, was something Subaru was fully aware of, even as he faced up to his own weakness in feeling that relief.

※※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※

"It's been a while since I walked alongside Subaru like this, hasn't it."

"You think? Feels like we've gone shopping together pretty often at night and stuff."

"Oh, honestly. Listen, given the flow of the conversation just now, obviously I meant during the daytime. You need to think about the meaning behind the words when you talk."

"Being told that about reading between the lines, of all people, by you, Mom — that's the one thing I can't accept!"

Natsuki Naoko's obliviousness was second to none, a truly demonic, world-class level of not getting it.
That was the shared assessment of Subaru and Kenichi within the Natsuki household, and in practice, when talking with Naoko, analogies and jokes went almost one hundred percent unexplained and uncomprehended.
And yet she herself had no self-awareness of this, and there was a part of her that didn't understand her own singularly airheaded nature at all, which made talking with her build up no end of stress.
Even knowing all that, though, Subaru loved talking with his mother.

"It's nice and warm today, isn't it. So, what did you and your father talk about?"

"Whoa, there's Beginner-Level Conversation with Mom — the first half and second half not connecting. It's not intentional so it's kind of a lot, but uh, let's see..."

Walking side by side down the road to school, Subaru tilted his head at his mother's question.
If he explained the details of his talk with Kenichi, he'd end up having to talk about his own embarrassing inner self, his complexes, the fact that he'd cried his eyes out. He thought it had been necessary, but that wave of emotion was something he'd only been able to let out fully because he'd thought it would stay confined to that one moment — bringing it out again here seemed like a bad idea.
And so—

"Nothing much... talked about Mr. Ikeda, some old stories, that sort of thing."

"Oh, Ikeda-kun. He hit it big on the horses, moved to Thailand, got conned and stripped of everything by his young local wife, and now he's tanned pitch-black, throwing himself into manual labor, right?"

"That whole tragic second half is news to me!?"

"Easy money never does stick around, does it. He wrote a letter saying his body's having a rough time these days, but his heart is full."

"So experience in an unknown place made Ikeda-san grow as a person... this hits close to home!"

Setting aside the difference between another world and another country, Mr. Ikeda's circumstances weren't all that different from Subaru's own. Feeling like he'd gained an unexpected comrade, Subaru quietly wished the man well.
As he did, his mother tilted her head with an "hmm."

"So, talking about old times made you feel like going to school?"

"Ah, yeah, well, to put it simply. There was this thing that got me looking back and realizing I'd been thinking about all kinds of reckless stuff. And so."

"In other words, you stopped trying to do everything the way your father would."

"――――"

Naoko mercilessly cornered Subaru, who'd tried to dodge with a vague, evasive answer.
The smile on her profile stayed unchanged, and there was no shift at all in her eyes — sharp, yet full of gentleness. So maybe there hadn't been any particular intent behind those words just now. And yet, it had still felt, more than enough, to Subaru, like she'd gotten the jump on him.
In front of Subaru's stunned silence, Naoko walked on, swinging both arms broadly.

"Subaru was always a hard worker, and he could do all sorts of things, which didn't help. And with your father being recklessly into so many hobbies, there were all kinds of opportunities too... you must have gotten tired."

"M-Mom... how much do you... know about me..."

His true feelings — the ones he'd been hiding even from himself. At his mother's way of speaking, as though she'd understood them all along, Subaru couldn't find his next words.
Called by name, he lifted his face. His mother, having circled around in front of him, stopped, and looked Subaru straight in the eye.

"People say it a lot, don't they — that children see their parents far more clearly than the parents realize."

"…………"

"But you know, the reverse is true too. Parents watch their children far more closely than the children realize, too. Mom watches Subaru far more closely than Subaru thinks, you know?"

There was nothing to do but stand there, dumbstruck.
The exhausted feeling that the inner truth he'd been so sure he'd kept hidden had, in fact, been spinning its wheels for nothing all along. And more than anything, the shallowness of his shut-in self, who had postured as some lone, aloof figure certain that no one could possibly understand his suffering.

"When you were little I even gave you suppositories, so I've seen all the way up your backside. About the only part of Subaru's body Mom hasn't seen is your internal organs."

"Um, excuse me. We were having a pretty good moment just now, so I didn't need the airheadedness."

Besides, when it came to internal organs, it wasn't something even he himself got much chance to see, let alone his parents or siblings. Though as it happened, Subaru had been — fortunate, or unfortunate — enough to have had that particular opportunity once or twice.
Anyway—

"The mayonnaise thing, the reason I shut myself in..."

"If Mom could have done something about it, she would have, you know. But it felt like no matter what Mom did, it would surely go wrong. But—"

Giving a small laugh, his mother continued, gazing steadily into Subaru's dark eyes.

"Someone who wasn't Mom or Dad did something about it for you, didn't they. I thought that was a really good thing. You should thank that person."

"...Yeah, that's right. That person told me that I was hopeless, when I was hopeless. That person told me that a hopeless me wasn't just a hopeless me. That's why I can walk like this now."

Having been made to recognize his own foolishness, and having that very self affirmed, Subaru could now stand here like this and face his past — his father and mother.
Ah, honestly.

"They're really great people. Honestly, more than I deserve."

"But you're not going to hand them over to someone else, are you?"

"Of course not. It's not a matter of whether I measure up or not. Rather than hand them off to someone else, I'll make them mine even if I don't measure up. And then I'll build up my own worth from there."

"Mm-hm—yep, you really are that man's child."

How much meaning did those words carry for Subaru?
His mother, who knew his inner heart that he'd never spoken of to anyone, surely knew that too. And if she knew, and was still saying this to him—

"Am I really managing to do that? Am I really managing to be that man's child properly?"

"It's fine. Because half of Subaru is Mom, after all. If you get half as cool as your father, that's quota met, isn't it?"

"So you're aware that the genes making up my mother-half are recessive?!"

"Get half as cool as your father... and be Subaru for the other half — isn't that fine?"

Unfazed by Subaru's yelling, Naoko offered up her answer just like that.
Hearing those words, Subaru was left stunned, dumbfounded.

"You don't have to become exactly like your father in every single way. Because if all of Subaru became just like your father, there'd be two of him, and Mom would end up all flustered, wouldn't she?"

"A woman's heart torn between her husband and her son — is this a smutty novel?!"

"Ugh, stop it, come on!"

"A fist-punch?!"

He got punched, his mother hiding her embarrassment at Subaru's tasteless remark.
Subaru staggered, catching a surprisingly solid right hook to the face. Eyes watering from the pain in his jaw, he watched as Naoko spun around, turning her back to him.

"Anyway, Mom thinks Subaru should just do his best being Subaru."

"You're wrapping that up nicely, but I'm bleeding a little here, you know."

"Speaking of blood, doesn't licking an iron pipe taste kind of similar to blood?"

"That's a pretty extreme situation to have run into often... uh-oh, here comes Intermediate-Level Conversation with Mom — a question that suddenly loses all sense of direction."

There was no point explaining carefully here that it was because the iron content in blood was chemically close to the material that made up an iron pipe. If he responded seriously to this kind of question, he'd get scolded with "Why are you talking about something else?" and end up feeling unreasonably wronged.
So, ignoring the context in between—

"Be myself, huh."

"Yup, yup. Wanting to become like your father, while becoming Subaru."

Naoko looked satisfied with the conclusion she'd arrived at. Then, suddenly, the feet walking ahead came to a stop, and turning around, Naoko pointed to the right at a fork in the road.

"Well, the convenience store's this way for Mom, so this is as far as I go... you gonna be okay on your own?"

"Was I really... that badly hurt, to be worried about like that. Yeah."

It wasn't excessive worry on Naoko's part.
Subaru, in his beaten-down state, must have looked that pathetic, that hopelessly spineless even to his mother's eyes. So, to reassure her, Subaru said—

"I'm fine. What I have to do and what I want to do line up perfectly now. There's no reason left anywhere for me to shut myself away anymore."

"I see. Well, that's good then. Okay, do your best."

Nodding as though reassured by Subaru's answer, Naoko turned right at the fork with a bouncing step. Subaru's path lay to the left; here, he and his mother would part ways.
It would become a parting. And surely, one far, far longer than his mother imagined—

"Mom—!"

Unable to bear watching her back walk away, Subaru called out loudly to stop his mother.
His mother's feet, skipping along in search of mayonnaise, came to a halt, and she turned her whole body back around toward him. He burned the sight of her — that same, unchanging mother of his — into his eyes.

"Ah..."

He tried to speak the words of farewell, tried to say goodbye — and hesitated.
If he didn't throw out the words of parting here and now, his mother would remain unaware of just how long this parting from Subaru would be. And Subaru, too, would lose the chance to witness his mother — whom he would never see again — break down in tears. If he didn't want the last sight of his mother to be a crying face, wouldn't it be better to keep his mouth shut here?
That deception, wearing the mask of consideration for himself and for her—

"—There's something I have to do. So, this is going to be a long goodbye."

Natsuki Subaru's heart would not allow it.

At the words he'd spoken, Naoko fell silent. Before any reaction could form in her, Subaru pressed on—

"It's someplace kind of far, and I don't think I'll be able to keep in touch or anything. I think I'll end up causing all kinds of worry. I won't do anything dangerous... I can't even promise that. If anything, it's more like — I've got to go help out a reckless kid from a place that's nothing but danger."

His words came fast. He rattled off information one after another. The words he wanted to say kept overflowing.

"I think I'll worry you and Dad again. Unlike up until yesterday, when I was somewhere you could keep an eye on me, this time it'll be somewhere out of sight. But no matter where I am, I'll be thinking about you two, and I won't forget you, and..."

"Subaru."

"I won't ever think I don't want to be your kid anymore, and I don't want to do anything that'll make me hate myself. I know it's not really my place to ask you to send me off without worrying, but still, I..."

"Subaru."

His mother's voice, having come back, called a halt to Subaru, who had started to lose track of what he was even saying.
He lifted his face, and his mother was standing right there in front of him. And—

"Subaru. —It's okay."

"...Wh-what do you mean, okay..."

"I understand exactly what Subaru's trying to say. So you don't have to work so hard searching for the words."

"Even if you understand... how, though...!"

"Because Mom is Subaru's mom, that's why."

—What kind of grounds was that, with not a shred of logic to it, and yet absolutely impossible to argue with?

Heat rose behind his eyes. He'd only tasted this exact feeling barely an hour ago.
Just how many more times was Subaru going to end up crying his eyes out like a child like this? How many more times would he have to shed tears like this before he could get himself a heart of steel, unshaken by anything?

"T-this is... so lame... crying like a kid..."

"If it's lame to cry when you want to cry, then every single baby that's ever been born would be lame too."

"That's not... what I meant..."

"Yeah, yeah, I know, I know. In front of your father and me, Subaru's always going to be our child, no matter how old you get... so it's fine to cry when you want to cry."

The world blurred. Tears overflowed. Wiping them away with his sleeve, hiding his face, Subaru wouldn't let his mother see it. Naoko, too, respecting that stubbornness of his, made no move to peer in at his face.
She only, slowly, stood on tiptoe and stroked Subaru's close-cropped head.

"...Sorry, Mom. In the end, I never managed to do anything for you two..."

"It's not that I gave birth to you wanting you to do something for us, you know? I gave birth to you because I wanted to do something for you. Because I wanted to love you — that's why Mom had Subaru."

—If that was the love she meant, Subaru had already received it more times than he could count.

"If you want to do something for your father and me, just give that feeling to someone else instead. If it's someone Subaru loves, and the two of you have a child you want to love all over again... wouldn't that be just perfect?"

"...Yeah. Perfect."

"Right? Mom's not usually wrong about these things, you know."

Smiling in satisfaction, Naoko tickled Subaru's forelock with a finger.
Then she pushed him back a step by the chest, and looked up from below at Subaru's raised face.

"When you cry and your face all scrunches up, it looks even more like Mom's, and even weirder."

"...You've got some nerve, using your own face as the comparison."

"I'm confident it's the face your father loves. So you can be just as confident that you're loved by your father too, Subaru — as confident as I am."

"That's only talking about the face part, though!"

He wiped his face hard with his sleeve, and though the skin around his eyes had gone bright red, he managed to stop the tears.
He found it funny — having cried his eyes out, having bared his true feelings and been comforted, how refreshed he felt now.

"Ugh, geez, all this crying is so pathetic."

"Crying's fine, isn't it. Subaru, you cried like crazy when you were born too, you know. Everyone cries embarrassingly hard at the start. There's all sorts of things, and you cry in all sorts of situations."

"――――"

"And after you've cried plenty, if you can laugh at the end, then everything's all right. What matters isn't the beginning or the middle — it's the end."

"So you mean, as long as the result's good, everything's okay?"

"That's not quite the right way to take it. Here — this is your homework, from Mom."

There would surely never come a chance to check his answer.
The parting greeting offered to him, in the guise of homework. Subaru received it, and tucked it away in his heart. Would a day come, when he'd finally worked out that answer, that he'd simply understand, naturally?

It was a farewell scene that refused to be dashing or clean-cut, no matter how you looked at it.
Both his father and his mother, faced with a son who, after shutting himself away, was now disappearing off to who-knew-where — far from hurling reproaches at him, they'd sent him off with smiles.
Utterly, thoroughly, they were parents too good for him, an environment too good for him, a place he had loved.

"—Well then, I'm off."

"Yeah, you do that."

He shook his head, and at the very end, forced his cheeks to move, making a smile.
Leaving that clumsy smile behind with his mother, Subaru turned his back and started walking.

The walk to school was already nearing its climax — from here, all that was left was to follow this fork to the end, climb the slope beyond, and the school building would be waiting—

"Oh, that's right. Subaru, Subaru, I forgot."

And there, despite having steeled himself with this much resolve, came his mother's airheaded voice calling from behind.
Nearly stumbling in surprise, Subaru turned around, unable to hide his deflation.

Anxious about what on earth could come at this very last moment, Subaru watched as his mother raised a hand.

"—Have a good day."

And with that, she gave a small wave, and said it to him with a smile.

—On his last night before being summoned to another world, when he'd headed out to the convenience store, his mother must have said the same thing to him then, too. But back then, in a foul mood for whatever reason, Subaru had silently pushed open the door—

"――――"

So this was his last chance to wipe away the regret of that day—

Advanced-Level Conversation with Mom — no matter how far it wanders off down side roads, it always, without fail, arrives at the one right answer in the end.
The instant that thought crossed his mind, a genuine, unforced smile rose to his cheeks, and—

"—I'm off!"

His voice rang out loud and clear, echoing down the road to school.

※※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※

An empty school building. From the entrance hall, he made his way to the shoe lockers and opened the sticky, poorly-fitted door that clearly hadn't been opened or closed in a long while. He swapped his outdoor shoes for indoor ones, tapped his toes against the floor to settle them into place, and, letting out a breath, headed for the hallway.
Subaru's year was third-year, in the sixth of eight classes. Class 3-6, mixed-gender attendance number twenty-two — that was Subaru's number.

The third-year classrooms, being the top grade, were on the first floor, and he walked down the hallway, passing the stairs by. Through the silent corridor, with only the sound of Subaru's own footsteps striking the linoleum floor echoing around him, he kept walking, aiming for his own classroom.
And before long, he arrived at his destination. He stood before the door, and took a deep breath.

"――――"

He put a hand to the door, slid it sideways, and threw it open.
The instant he did, a barrage of reproachful stares converged on Subaru, arriving at school so terribly late—

"That was a lot faster than I expected."

That wasn't what happened.
Through the thrown-open door, as far as he could see, the classroom was lined with nothing but empty seats — the only one occupied was a single seat in the very center of the room.
And the figure seated there turned, chair and all, toward Subaru as he entered.

"Welcome. —So, what did your time spent facing your own past bring you, I wonder?"

So asked the Witch of Greed, her white hair swaying, eyes brimming with curiosity, as she posed the question to him.
