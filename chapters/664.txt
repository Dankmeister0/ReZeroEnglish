Arc 8 Chapter 47: "Tanza"

—This was a terribly complicated feeling, but just like Natsuki Schwarz, Tanza hated the Vollachian Empire.

The idea that the empire's people should be strong was praised as though it were something wonderful, and in keeping with that, those who weren't strong were oppressed — and even if it cost them their lives, they were told flatly that being weak was the sin.

The weak had no right to call themselves citizens of the empire. —Well then, fine. She'd rather not be one anyway.

No one gets to choose where they're born.

So neither Tanza nor anyone else had asked to be born in the Vollachian Empire.

Tanza hated the Vollachian Empire.

She hated the empire that had taken her father and mother, that had killed — through torment, for some senseless reason called "the imperial way" — the sister who had raised young, weak Tanza with everything she had; the sister who had finally breathed easy, believing she'd found a place to call home; the sister who should finally have been able to repay all she owed.

That, surely, was why.

"Do you hate this country, little one?"

When she was asked that, the expression that rarely showed any emotion stiffened, and her breath caught in her throat.

She thought she'd been found out — something she must never let anyone notice, a thought she must never let anyone know, a conviction that deserved nothing but rejection.

But the beautiful woman, gazing at Tanza as she trembled all over and hung her head, saw straight through to the heart of her, and smiled — gently laying a hand against Tanza's stiffened cheek.

The warmth of those fingers felt just like her sister's — the sister she had already lost.

Just like the sister who had given everything of herself for Tanza's sake without a thought to her own wellbeing, and who had died without ever knowing a single happiness of her own.

"So do I."

For a moment, Tanza didn't understand what that meant, and she was bewildered.

Only after the bewilderment passed did she understand what sentiment those words had been answering, and she caught her breath.

If a child like Tanza, one who didn't yet understand the way of things, had said such a thing, it would have earned nothing worse than a scolding and some punishment. But the woman before her was no child.

She wasn't merely an adult — she was someone of standing.

One of the Nine who stood as living symbols of strength in this empire that worshipped strength above all else.

"—I, too, despise the way this country is."

So she said, smiling gently and tenderly, as she confided her true feelings to Tanza.

"Please, let me go to Yorna-sama. I want to be by her side."

In a battle that would decide the empire's very survival, she was aware she was being selfish.

So she'd meant to endure whatever harsh words came her way. She told herself it couldn't be helped if she was cursed and scolded for it — that she would still be honest with her own feelings, no matter what.

That was what she'd thought, anyway.

"—Yeah, that's the plan. Go show your face properly, and have a proper talk."

He always selfishly put Tanza's feelings on the back burner, and yet at times like this, he'd nail exactly what she was feeling, as if it had been tailor-made.

That was the most annoying thing about this black-haired boy.



"—Yorna-sama."

The instant she called out, Tanza spread her small body as wide as it would go and took the full brunt of the flash of silver light head-on, saving the precious woman whose neck had nearly been severed.

The impact tore through her entire body, a crackling numbness running clear to the tips of her fingers and toes.

But she clenched her back teeth so it wouldn't show that she was in pain — not for the sake of the one who'd struck the blow, but so the precious woman she'd shielded wouldn't know.

"—!"

She swallowed down her groan and her expression alike, and endured.

Her stony face was a source of frustration more often than not, but at times like this, she was grateful for it. Surely it looked, at least, like she'd weathered the blow without so much as a flinch.

In truth, if not for the ice shield she'd hidden inside her robes, she'd surely have been cut clean in two.

"You all have the same face, but get out of the way, every one of you!"

Following on the heels of Tanza's bravado came a valiant, silver-bell voice.

The reason Tanza hadn't been cut in half — the silver-haired half-elf who'd tucked that ice shield away for her, Emilia — descended gallantly upon the imperial capital and turned her hand toward the row of undead who all shared the same face.

An instant later, a high sound like cracking glass wrapped around the entire area, and clear ice rising up out of the ground trapped the undead one after another, locking them away in icy prisons.

The undead reacted at once — some leaping away, some ducking low to dodge, some trying to slash the ice apart with their blades. —But few of them guessed right.

Those who leapt were frozen mid-air, those who crouched were frozen to the ground, and those who drew their blades were turned, sword and all, into part of a blossoming flower of ice — frozen solid on the spot, quite literally.

It swallowed up nearly all of the close-to-twenty undead; only a handful managed to escape. Such was the overwhelming suppressive power of Emilia, her silver hair dancing.

Tanza wanted to praise it with every word she had, but for now—

"—At last. At last, I've been granted the chance to see you again."

The severed ice shield slipped from inside her robes and dispersed into mana before it hit the ground.

With that hard weight gone from her chest, Tanza wrapped her arms tight around the head of the woman kneeling before her. Because the woman was tall, Tanza had often been the one held like this — but this was the first time she had been the one to do the holding.

Honestly, she'd always found it a little ticklish, being held like that.

She'd thought it set a bad example, too, and it was frustrating to be treated like a child. But she'd never actually disliked it — and her thinking on being treated like a child had changed, besides.

Schwarz and Cecilus both did exactly as they pleased, small or childish as they were, didn't they?

Being a child might get in the way of doing something, but it was nowhere near a reason to give up on it. Tanza gave that lesson back now, with the whole of her small body.

Holding tight to the bleeding woman whose blue eyes had gone wide with shock — Yorna — Tanza turned her black eyes toward the enemy, toward the obstacle standing in her way.

And then—

"Whatever villain would turn a blade upon Yorna-sama — unworthy as I am, I shall be your opponent."

So she declared, clearly, as if staking out exactly where she stood.

"—————"

At Tanza's declaration, neither the undead men nor Yorna, held in her arms, could find words.

Whether it was exasperation at such a foolish thing to say, or simple bewilderment at Tanza's sudden appearance, she didn't care which. Still holding Yorna, Tanza moved to ready herself.

And then—

"Hyah!"

"Whoaaargh!?"

Emilia's charging strike came faster than Tanza could move.

Stepping in on her long legs, Emilia swung down the ice hammers she'd conjured in both hands at the undead man standing frozen in place. The undead man, who'd had his eyes on Tanza, knocked it away in astonishment and turned to face Emilia.

"I'm your opponent!"

"Just now, didn't that girl over there say something about being my opponent herself?"

"Tanza-chan just finally got to see Yorna-san, who she's been wanting to see so badly. I'm not going to let you… you all? You've got the same face, but… I'm not going to let any of you get in her way!"

"Troublesome as it may be, however many of me there are, I am still I — Rowan Segmunt is but one man. There's no need to trouble yourself over that."

"I'm not going to let you get in her way!"

Correcting herself, Emilia conjured a pair of ice swords in both hands, and the undead — the multiple Rowans, all wearing the same cruel smile — faced her, every one of them in the same stance.

Behind her, Tanza called out, "Emilia-sama!" meaning to lend her strength, but—

"It's fine, go talk to Yorna-san first. —Subaru asked me to make sure of it."

"—————"

At the mention of Schwarz's name, Tanza faltered, just slightly.

In that moment, Emilia, a smile still lingering, snapped her expression sharp and took a step toward the Rowans, who stood with their swords sheathed and ready.

"—Icicle Line."

The instant those words left Emilia's lips, the scene around her turned a shade whiter.

Every color took on a tinge of silvery white, and the Rowans wore triumphant grins, ready to meet Emilia's ice-and-snow-wreathed twin blades head-on — and then a storm of icicles came raining down upon them.

"Whoaaaaa—!?"

"Here I come!"

Rowan, who'd apparently expected a clean contest of swordsmanship, was stunned senseless by the barrage of ice, but still stood his ground against Emilia's rushing attack.

Her light, nimble ice weapons clashed against the undead's swinging blades, and the clash of steel — and ice — began.

With that clash at her back, Tanza took Emilia up on her consideration and looked again at Yorna. Her face still hadn't caught up with the situation.

"This is the first time I've ever seen you make such a face, Yorna-sama."

"—Tanza… is that truly you?"

Staring hard at Tanza's face, the corners of her eyes softening ever so slightly, Yorna spoke the first meaningful words since their reunion. Tanza nodded at the question. Nearly two months had passed since she and Yorna had been parted, back when they said their farewells in Chaosflame.

She hadn't even been able to send word, so of course she'd have been assumed dead.

"But I've been safe all this while. So that I could return to your side, Yorna-sama."

She said it with strength, staring straight back into Yorna's eyes.

The words she flung at Yorna, still reeling from the shock of reality, carried a force so great even Tanza barely recognized it as her own, even as she spoke it.

It was probably the influence of people who could say such things without a shred of pretense.

It was proof that a great deal had happened to Tanza, too, in the two months apart from Yorna — though the changes weren't Tanza's alone. Yorna had changed as well.

Yorna stood there in a beautiful blue dress. Not the kimono Tanza was used to seeing her in, her hair let down instead of pinned up — the sight gave Tanza more than just the thrill of novelty; it gave her doubt.

It wasn't that she questioned a change in taste. What troubled her was that Yorna wasn't wearing any of her ornaments — the gifts offered up to her by the people of Chaosflame.

Every single resident of the magic city of Chaosflame loved and revered Yorna without end.

To convey the full depth of that feeling, they had shaved down, made use of, and ingeniously fashioned the very demi-human traits that were so often the reason they'd been cast out, and offered the results up to Yorna as her ornaments.

From her hairpins and earrings down to her obi clasp and even the single needle and thread stitched through her kimono, Yorna had draped herself in the feelings of Chaosflame's people, proclaiming her own existence with dignity and majesty.

Among them was a comb carved from a piece of Tanza's own sister's horn.

"Tanza…"

Called by name from trembling lips, Tanza waited for Yorna's agitation to settle.

There was still the matter of lending her strength to Emilia, fighting Rowan, but right now Tanza wanted to devote everything she had to helping Yorna, right in front of her, sort out her feelings. Yorna's fingers reached tentatively toward Tanza's cheek, as if she meant to confirm the feel of it before she spoke.

But Yorna's fingers never touched Tanza's cheek.

"Yorna-sama?"

At the last moment, Yorna stopped her outstretched fingers, screwed her eyes shut, and instead pushed against the chest of the Tanza embracing her, making her take a step back.

The distance between Tanza and Yorna, who had only just managed to embrace each other, opened up once more, and Tanza blinked. Unable to read Yorna's true intent, Tanza watched as Yorna opened her mouth.

"Whatever did you come to a place like this for?"

"—————"

"This capital is a city of the undead now… a place abandoned not only by the empire's soldiers, but by the Nine Divine Generals and even the emperor himself. What business could a girl like you possibly have coming here?"

Lifting her knee from the ground, Yorna stood and glared sharply down at Tanza. Pinned by those narrow, sharp eyes, Tanza's slight shoulders drew in just a little.

For a moment, she didn't understand what she'd been told. But she quickly realized they were words of rejection.

Yorna — the woman of boundless mercy who had accepted everything and everyone — was rejecting Tanza.

"This is far too harsh and bloodstained a place for the living to linger in. The wise course would be to leave at once. And if you cannot manage that yourself, shall I see to it with my own hand?"

"…If you're going to divide us that way, the living and the dead, then surely you fall under the same condition, Yorna-sama—"

"The same, you say? —I, and you?"

Struck with astonishment, Tanza tried to argue back, but Yorna's hand shot out toward her. This time, though, it wasn't to touch her cheek — it was to seize her by the collar.

Yorna's slender fingers gripped the front of her kimono, and Tanza's small body was lifted clean off her feet.

She had been picked up before. But never handled so roughly.

"You and I do not stand on the same side. I… there is someone I wish to remain beside. At long last, I am with that person again. So…"

"Y-Yorna-sama…!"

"You, and the rest of the children — I no longer need any of you."

Close enough that her breath brushed Tanza's face, Yorna peered into her eyes. What she spoke of, from her own mouth, was the reason Yorna had stayed behind in this capital overrun with the undead.

Not that she had been taken captive — but the plain fact that she had chosen, of her own will, to remain.

"—————"

Just as Yorna peered into her, Tanza peered right back into Yorna's blue eyes.

Deep within those eyes, Tanza could see a fragment of longing that had never once vanished, even as Yorna lived in the magic city, treating its people, and Tanza too, with such gentleness.

Yorna Mishigure had been searching for something, all this time.

It was, Tanza thought, something so vast and so far away that even Tanza couldn't fathom it — something like a star, beyond even Yorna's reach.

A thing she knew shone bright and dazzling, and yet could never touch.

Yorna had kept searching for it, all this time.

That she was the seeker of such a thing — Tanza knew it, and so did everyone else. They prayed her wish would come true, and if it couldn't, they hoped to offer her something in its place.

Not just Tanza — everyone felt the same.

And now, it seemed, it had finally been found — Yorna's wish had finally reached the star.

If that was the reason her hand now gripped Tanza's collar—

"—Then reject me looking a good deal happier about it, Yorna-sama."

So Tanza said, catching hold of the other woman's wrist, speaking to the precious person telling such a clumsy lie.

—Ah, she'd gone and become a bad girl.

Gripping that pale, slender wrist, Tanza lamented how much she had changed.

This wasn't how it was supposed to be. Tanza had an ideal of herself, a vision of who she wanted to become — namely, someone like her late sister, Zoey.

—Zoey had been a woman of deep inner strength and a kind heart.

Tanza's home had been destroyed because of a superstition that had spread — that the horns of Deer Humans, brewed into medicine, could cure anything — and bandits, after the horns that fetched such high prices, had attacked.

Both her father and mother were killed, and Tanza, still a small child, was led by the hand by her sister and barely escaped with her life.

But the sisters' hardships didn't end there. The clutches of those after their horns caught up with them again and again, and even setting that aside, it was the way of the Vollachian Empire that the weak were preyed upon.

Their lives had been endangered more times than Tanza could count, and now, older than she'd been then, she could well imagine just how brutal the ordeals her sister must have endured just to secure a single bowl of soup. At the same time, she hated herself — hated herself enough to want to die — for having been able to do nothing but lean on her sister's protection.

"Tanza, you mustn't curse anyone. Someone who curses others so easily ends up cursed just as easily themselves."

Emaciated, dressed in ragged clothes, sharing a single bowl of soup between them on some night, her sister told her that.

No one was ever kind to them; everyone treated Tanza and Zoey alike with coldness. Whenever Tanza cursed the world for it, her gentle sister always scolded her with exactly those words.

Not out of irritation, but out of something like a parent's love, her sister scolded her. —And yet Zoey herself was still at an age when she should have been leaning on parents of her own, far too young to be one herself.

The superstition surrounding the horns of the Deer Humans refused to die out, and the sisters could never stay in one place.

They ran and ran and kept on running, and by the time even the running had worn them down, word reached the sisters' ears of the "magic city," Chaosflame.

A place, they said, where great numbers of demi-humans lived together, a paradise for the outcast.

The lady who ruled over it was strong, overflowing with compassion, a wonderful person who would never allow the weak to be oppressed. —What nonsense, Tanza had thought, with a child's heart.

There was no way such a person could exist. As if anyone so conveniently perfect could be real.

If such a person really existed, then why had her father and mother died? Why was her sister so ragged and wasted away, still leading Tanza along by the hand?

And so—

"—Well done, the both of you. Zoey. Tanza."

When they finally arrived at the paradise that shouldn't have existed, and the beautiful woman in a kimono welcomed the ragged, grime-streaked sisters into her castle, and embraced the two of them after they'd stammered out their names — that was the first time Tanza ever heard her sister cry out loud.

Not on the day their parents died, not on the day she was made into a spectacle for a single bowl of soup, not even on the day willful Tanza had hurled thoughtless words at her — never once had Zoey cried.

Watching Zoey now sob and weep, clinging to that woman's chest right beside her, Tanza cried out loud too, and from the bottom of her heart, she thought—

This paradise this woman had built — no, this woman herself, Yorna Mishigure — must never, ever be lost. That was what her young heart resolved, fiercely, with all its strength.

—Given food and a bed to sleep in, dressed in a kimono, Zoey was beautiful, and Tanza was proud of her.

Many people adored Yorna and prayed desperately for the chance to serve her. Among them, Zoey was the most earnest and hardworking of all, and Yorna in turn came to rely on her heavily.

Tanza was proud that her sister served the magnificent Yorna and carried out such important work.

Someday, she thought, she would become like her sister, serve Yorna with wholehearted sincerity, and let the countless suffering, weak people of the world know of the paradise that was Chaosflame.

Her sister died two years after that life began.

Outside the magic city, there had been a group of demi-humans seeking Chaosflame's protection.

When they passed near a fortress of imperial soldiers on campaign, her sister, acting as Yorna's representative, had made contact with the group and was in the middle of guiding them to the magic city.

The imperial soldiers cast the group of demi-humans as a mock enemy and attacked them, and her sister was killed without ceremony — tormented to death, this time with nothing to do with any deer horn, and her body left on display.

"Tanza, you mustn't curse anyone. Someone who curses others so easily ends up cursed just as easily themselves."

Her sister's words flashed through her mind, and amid her grief, Tanza's heart was torn to shreds.

Even now, was it still wrong? Even now, must she still not curse anyone? Even with her sister murdered, just when she should finally have found happiness — must she still hold back the curse?

"Do you hate this country, little one?"

Tortured by despair, suffering as though her own horns might snap from the strain, Tanza was spoken to, just like that.

The kimono she'd worn in imitation of her sister, her paltry knowledge of attendance that didn't even amount to mimicry, and a heart so weak and fragile it couldn't even hide what was in her own chest.

Believing herself seen through, exactly as she was, Tanza grieved — and it was then that Yorna touched her cheek.

"So do I."

Even as she touched her, she affirmed Tanza's anger, her curse.

And then, in place of the powerless Tanza, she took action herself, to give that curse a form — never minding that what she'd have to stand against, to do it, was the vast, towering empire itself.

"—I, too, despise the way this country is."

Whenever the "Ever-Vivid" Yorna Mishigure raised a banner of rebellion against the empire, it was always for someone else's sake.

Knowing she was that kind of woman was exactly why Tanza hadn't wanted her to do it.

She'd wanted to be a child who never put Yorna in danger.

She'd wanted to be a child who never made Yorna sad.

Like her sister Zoey, she'd wanted to be a proper attendant, one who never troubled Yorna.

—And yet, Tanza had gone and become a bad girl.


"You saved my sister, Yorna-sama."

Gripping tight the wrist that held her by the collar, Tanza's lips spoke those words.

Whether it was Tanza's reaction, her action, or her words that she hadn't expected, Yorna's blue eyes went wide, and her lips trembled faintly.

Tanza didn't miss the shape those trembling lips formed — her sister's name, "Zoey."

"You granted my selfish wish, Yorna-sama."

After Zoey lost her life, once the "rebellion" that clashed head-on with the imperial capital had reached its conclusion and Yorna had settled the score for her sister's death, Tanza had asked to be allowed to serve in her sister's place.

A wish so brazen, so utterly unfit for someone of her caliber — and Yorna had granted it, gladly.

With all her heart and sincerity, Tanza had served her. Yorna had allowed her that.

"You brought happiness into so many people's lives, Yorna-sama."

Not just for herself and her sister — how many countless others had Yorna been a support to?

How many people had been saved, protected, by their bond with her? Tanza's words and actions were nothing more than a single fragment of that whole mass of gratitude.

If Yorna could leave all of that behind and move forward without a single regret, then so be it.

If she thought of them all as a burden and wanted to lighten her load, to leap up to wherever the star could be reached, then that was fine too.

If Yorna couldn't be happy, then Tanza had no wish for her to stay with them. To ask her to remain by their side while unable to be happy would be nothing but selfishness — no different from a curse.

So at the very least, if she was going to shake off the hand that held hers, Tanza wanted her to do it smiling, wishing for her own happiness.

And if she couldn't do that—

"—You love us, Yorna-sama."

Not in the past tense — refusing to let go of a feeling that still held true right now — Tanza declared it, plainly.

Before her, Yorna's already-wide eyes stretched even further open, past their limit, falling into pure astonishment. It was the face of someone who'd never imagined, not for a moment, that Tanza would talk back to her.

And no wonder. Tanza had wanted to serve Yorna well — to follow her words quietly, obediently, the way Zoey had, striving with all her might to see them fulfilled.

But a hint of how unsuited she was to that must already have shown itself, back at the very moment that led to her parting from Yorna.

When the emperor and the false emperor came to Chaosflame, Tanza had acted on her own, without Yorna's permission, to keep her from being dragged into the chaos of war — and had only ended up needlessly widening the confusion.

As a result, through everything that followed, Tanza had been separated from Yorna, and had seen and heard a great many things.

And now, in this reunion finally realized, Tanza was arguing back against Yorna, head-on.

A bad girl. She had become a bad girl.

She didn't want the person she wished happiness for to settle for anything less in her pursuit of it. And for that, she would even trample over that person's own words, her own prayers.

That was the kind of bad girl she had become.

"—Ah—"

Yorna's eyes were still stretched wide with shock as a thin breath slipped from her lips.

It was a reaction to the pain of her wrist being gripped hard — Tanza, in turn, had seized tight the very wrist Yorna used to hold her by the collar.

If Yorna truly had stopped caring about Tanza and the others, as she'd claimed, and was free to pursue whatever she wanted without a single regret, then this shouldn't have happened.

Yorna's Soul Marriage Technique shared her strength with those she loved.

The logic by which that effect applied to Yorna herself was self-love — which was to say, nothing less than an expression of self-affirmation, a belief that one's own actions were right.

As long as Yorna Mishigure could believe from the bottom of her heart that she was in the right, extraordinary power welled up within her.

But what happened if she couldn't believe that?

One of the Nine Divine Generals, one facet of the pinnacle of martial might in the Vollachian Empire — and yet, unable to affirm her own actions, Yorna would lose her strength at an accelerating rate.

So much so that even the blade of the "Undead Sword Saint," a man who'd relinquished life itself, could surpass her in strength.

Yorna couldn't push forward something she couldn't love, couldn't affirm.

So she couldn't push forward the self she was right now.

Which meant—

"—There is one thing you cannot fake, Yorna-sama: your love."

With blue flame lit in her left eye, Tanza could believe, without a shred of doubt, in Yorna's love.

"—————"

Setting her dangling feet back on the ground, Yorna found herself speechless before Tanza, who watched her with burning eyes.

Cradling her own tightly-gripped wrist, Yorna stared at Tanza's face before her — at the flame dwelling there, proof of a love more absolute than any other — as though she couldn't believe it.

As if she had genuinely believed she could deceive her own heart completely.

"You aren't nearly so clever as all that, Yorna-sama. —Even putting up your hair, even dressing in your kimono, you've always borrowed the hands of myself, my sister, everyone who loves you, haven't you?"

So saying, Tanza rose on her toes right where she stood and reached out to touch Yorna's cheek.

The way it had once been done to her; the way one does to someone one wishes to cherish and love. At the feel of those fingertips, Yorna's blue eyes shook — wavered — greatly.

Just as they had the day her sister first cried aloud; as though a stubborn heart were cracking apart.

And then—

"—! Watch out! Tanza-chan!"

Behind Tanza, facing Yorna, came Emilia's urgent voice, and the sound of her zori sandals kicking hard off the ground, catching against her eardrums.

At the same instant came the ring of a blade leaving its sheath — she could tell at once that one of the Rowans had slipped past Emilia's efforts to stop him and was closing in on her.

"Step aside there, girl. This here's a stage I've longed for even in my dreams!"

With that self-serving declaration, the man came charging like a gale.

Yorna must have seen him over her shoulder. Her hand shot instantly toward Tanza's shoulder, meaning to shove her body aside and protect her.

As if to return the earlier favor, meaning to become Tanza's shield in turn this time.

"But no, you mustn't."

"Tanza…!?"

Bracing herself against the hand trying to push her aside, Tanza refused it. Instead, she turned as if to shield Yorna from behind her, and faced down the hideous golden eyes bearing down on her.

The blow being unleashed aimed to sever Tanza's neck, and beyond her, cleave clean through Yorna below the chest as well. A single strike fierce enough to scorch the cold, chilled air came bearing down on Tanza's throat—

"—!"
"A stage you've longed for, you say?"

Swallowing his astonishment down his throat, Rowan's golden eyes went wide.

And no wonder. He surely never imagined that a strike unleashed with everything he had could be caught, bare-handed, by a child this small.

With her raised hand, Tanza had seized the silver light that scorched the very air, and stopped it dead.

—Yorna's Soul Marriage Technique shared her strength with those she loved.

For anyone able to believe, selfishly and from the depths of their heart, that they themselves were right, that there was no doubting they were loved — for such a person, the effect would be tremendous.

'Tanza's mine too, so basically that puts her on our side, right?'

Ah, how infuriating. She'd caught it from him — that confidence in being loved, that bad-girl habit.

It was almost unbearably itchy with embarrassment. She was truly grateful her face never showed it.

"My apologies. I'm rather busy, so I haven't the time to sit around dreaming happy dreams."

Putting all her strength into her grip, Tanza snapped the sword she'd caught clean in two, and — sandwiched front and back by reactions of gasping shock — drove the fist that had shattered the blade straight into the obstacle's face before her.

She landed a blow hard enough to cave his nose clean into the inside of his skull, and Tanza made her declaration.

The days of merely dreaming of paradise, merely praying for the sake of someone precious, were over.

Neither paradise nor a precious wish could be seized without moving on one's own.

That was the answer Tanza had arrived at — saved by her sister, helped by Yorna, having met Natsuki Schwarz and her comrades of the Pleiades Battalion, and made it all the way here.

"—Because a harsh, painful, beloved reality is waiting for me."

After all, she hadn't a moment to spare standing still — not when she meant to reach out and touch the star.
