Arc 4 Chapter 17: "Love Talk"

"Yo, Ken-san, don't see you out and about like this in the morning much. Finally got fired?"

"Don't talk nonsense, without me nothing anywhere would run smooth. I don't want to hog all the work and take it from everyone else, so this is me letting off steam and pumping everybody else's motivation back up."

The neighborhood bakery owner, riding by on his bicycle, called out to him familiarly, and Kenichi answered while flipping him the middle finger and cursing. They kept up their crude, cackling banter for a while before waving and parting ways.

"Geez, every single one of them, the moment they see my face on my rare day off, it's 'unemployed, unemployed.' I've got a beloved family to support — there's no way I'd screw up like that. Even if I did dip my hands into something that might cost me my job, I'd do it so nobody found out, heh."

"...As someone who's being supported, I'd like to pray, before we even get to whether you'd be found out, that you never dip your hands into that kind of thing to begin with."

Subaru, hands shoved in his tracksuit pockets, had drawn to the side of the road to wait out the conversation, and now shrugged. To his son, who had taken shelter from the sun in the shade, Kenichi shook his head and both hands, saying,

"A man who forgets his sense of adventure doesn't grow, not as a man, not as a person. Course, if it means actually doing something bad, that's a different story, but figuring out exactly where that razor's edge line is — that's half the fun..."

"You're way past the age where that's an excuse, so calm down. Forty-something and still talking like a kid."

"I think there's a part of every man that stays a kid no matter how old he gets. Anyway, it's because you, who's supposed to be the one having this kind of dumb conversation with me, won't join in, that your old man's stuck talking this nonsense by himself. What do you say to that, huh?"

"What do I say — I can't just casually chat with some old guy I don't even know."

"He's not some guy you don't know. That's the owner of the bakery I stop at sometimes on my way home to buy bread. And on top of that, he's a year below me from high school."

Even told that, nothing clicks. Subaru's never so much as looked closely at the bread bag, let alone stopped by the shop himself.
When Subaru wordlessly gave off the air of wanting to end the conversation, Kenichi clicked his tongue and said, "Can't be helped, I guess," and continued,

"Making a face like you just bit into something bitter on a bright, clear morning with the sun blazing down like this — don't you think that's rude to the sun god? You've got the face of a guy who's about to get stopped and questioned by the cops."

"If I get stopped and questioned, that'll be your fault for dragging me out at this hour... I said I didn't want to go, and you made me anyway."

"Your resistance was just for show, and you basically went along with it obediently. Deep down you love your old man, don't you, Subaru. Don't worry, I love you too. Right after your mom, though!"

Kenichi started walking again, laughing good-naturedly, and gave Subaru's back a rough slap. Wincing at the force of it, Subaru felt a flicker of unease at just how high Kenichi's spirits were.

No — it was true that his father was usually like this even on an ordinary day, but Kenichi, who had his moody side, should have sulked much longer after an exchange like the one just now.
And yet, for whatever reason, he seemed unusually at ease this morning.

—In stark contrast to Subaru, whose chest felt fit to collapse just from walking beside him outside like this.

"So..."

"Hm?"

"So, you had something to talk about, that's why you dragged me out, right? You don't usually go this far... what is it. Something you can't talk about inside?"

Subaru asked with the nuance of, something you don't want Mom to hear?
He already had a vague, unpleasant premonition of what kind of talk this would turn into. Probably some lecture about Subaru's current listless way of living, or something like that.
Normally he could pull the blanket over his head and let it wash over him, but that trick wouldn't work outside. Or he could shout loud enough to cut him off. If he embarrassed Kenichi that badly out in public, even Kenichi might change his attitude and — Subaru got that far in the thought and shook his head.

"With my old man, there's a chance he'd find even that embarrassment funny...!"

"I dunno what wild thing you're imagining, but it's nothing that out-there or interesting. I just felt like having an ordinary family chat under the sun for once."

"Yeah right, I don't buy it. ...I'll nod along anyway, though."

"That's the spirit. By the way, Subaru, you... which would you rather have, a little brother or a little sister?"

"Getting asked that question at seventeen is straight-up terrifying!!"

The question came flying in from a completely unexpected angle, and Subaru's voice cracked with alarm. Kenichi, watching Subaru's shoulders heave with ragged breath, bared his teeth and laughed, "Kidding, kidding," and said,

"Sure, your mom and I are still plenty lovey-dovey, but even we're not gonna go and have another one at our age. Which means, you've got Mom's and my love all to yourself. Be grateful."

"Ah, yeah yeah, thrilled, thrilled. ...You're really joking, right?"

"Hey now, don't say it like that. You keep acting like you hate the idea and I might start feeling like you're setting me up for something and get all fired up, you know?"

Since it was starting to seem like this might not stay a joke after all, Subaru silently stared him down until the topic died. Kenichi gave a wry laugh and accepted the message.

—The path Subaru was walking with his father was a stroll of barely ten minutes from home.
It was a neighborhood with a famous river running through it, and the riverbank was lined with cherry trees, a well-known spring sightseeing spot. Though that season had already passed, and instead of pink blossoms, the trees now just held green leaves soaking up the sun.

After breakfast, Subaru had spent the time before school shaved away by guilt and impatience, and it was to this spot that Kenichi had led him after inviting him outside.
At first, the moment they'd left the house, Subaru had worried he might be taken to school, but—

"When you tried walking toward school, your guard went up like a wall, man. That's not my goal here anyway, so let's take the long way to the riverbank."

—Reading Subaru's intentions, Kenichi had said this, and Subaru had let himself be dragged along here.
A strong wind, carrying the scent of grass and trees, blew across the top of the bank, and if you stood on tiptoe, you could see the calmly flowing river beyond the dividing fence.

"There used to be no fence like that, you know. I used to horse around in the river with my buddies all the time. Oh, you remember Ikeda? During a typhoon, we came to see how wild the current had gotten and he got swept away... if some random old guy with a lifeguard certification hadn't happened to pass by right then, he'd have died."

"Don't tell me this fence exists because of you and this 'Ikeda' guy."

"No way... wait, hold on. Actually, the timing kinda lines up, doesn't it?"

Leaning against the fence, gazing at the river, Kenichi tilted his head as he looked back on the memory. Standing idle behind his father, Subaru glanced around at his surroundings.
It was a weekday morning, so naturally there weren't many people around. In fact, aside from Subaru and Kenichi, there wasn't a single person in sight. It wasn't the kind of place people were drawn to in the first place. Anyone wandering around here at this hour was either the caretaker or some kind of oddball.
As Subaru was having that thought, the sound of someone's footsteps crushing grass suddenly reached his ears, followed by—

"Ohh? Who's this, if it isn't Ken-bou? What, what, you come to play in the river again at your age?"

"Who's calling me... oh, it's the old caretaker guy. Hasn't retired yet, huh? That's actually more surprising. And I'm not wearing swim trunks today, so I'm not getting in any river."

"Don't kid yourself. Coming from the guy who used to say boxers look just like swim trunks at a glance and then jump in wearing nothing but his underwear. Man, it's been a while, huh."

Climbing up the bank, exchanging an easy handshake with Kenichi, was an elderly, short old man. He had the air of a kindly old grandfather and wore a worn, well-used green uniform. Judging from the conversation and the logo on his back, he was probably the caretaker of the riverbank or something similar.
And given that he knew Kenichi from back when he used to play in the river, he must have been at this a long time.

The two of them, apparently reuniting after a long while, laughed together, and the old man clapped his hands.

"Right, if you're here, how's Ikeda doing? That guy, thanks to how often he actually got swept away, the number of times I scooped him up with a net was way ahead of anyone else's."

"Old Ikeda hit it big on a long-shot bet about ten years back, took the money and flew off to Thailand, and nobody's heard from him since. Though he does send New Year's cards, midsummer greetings, midwinter greetings, Obon and Christmas cards, and letters on Father's Day and Mother's Day."

"You don't call someone who sends letters that often 'nobody's heard from'..."

Subaru couldn't help muttering the retort under his breath. Catching that small mumble, the old man looked over and, seeming to notice Subaru's presence for the first time, raised his eyebrows.

"Oh, you had company... hm? Could this be—"

"Ah, yeah, my son. No, I should say — my beloved son."

"Ohh, I knew it! Somehow there's a hint of you when you were young in him... no, actually, not much at all. Doesn't look like you. Takes after his mother, huh?"

"Ha ha. I get told that a lot. My eyes especially."

Amid an otherwise perfectly ordinary face, only his sharp, triple-white eyes stood out as distinctive. His mother's sharp glare ran deep in her too, so this feature clearly bore her strong influence.
As Subaru gave that inoffensive answer, the old man stepped closer.

"I see, I see. Still, I'm surprised. So much time's passed that Ken-bou here's got a kid this big. Guess that means I've gotten old too. I don't have the stamina left to swim out and rescue a drowning Ikeda anymore."

"I don't think even Ikeda-san would go play in the river and nearly drown at his age anymore..."

"I sure hope so... but those two never could settle down. Especially your dad — he was always kicking up some kind of ruckus wherever he went. You'd probably notice that, walking around town with him, wouldn't you?"

"...Yeah, I guess."

Subaru's answer came out halting. At that, the old man frowned slightly, puzzled. And a moment later, the furrow between his brows deepened further, as—

"Hm? Ken-bou's kid, sure, but — wasn't today Monday? Why's he out here on the riverbank with his old man at this hour?"

"—!"

Hit with the exact question he didn't want asked, Subaru's expression stiffened in pain.
Next came a sharp, stabbing headache, the same kind he'd had in his room. Squeezing his eyes shut against the pain that nearly made him clutch his head, Subaru said "Sorry" in a strained, urgent voice and turned his back on the old man as if fleeing.

"Ah, hey, hold on, Subaru! Sorry about that, old man. I'll swing by again sometime when we've got time, we'll catch up then."

"Ah, ah... guess I said something I shouldn't have. Apologize to him for me."

The exchange behind him didn't even register in Subaru's ears.
In any case, Subaru fled from the pain grinding at his skull, running away from the riverbank as if searching for somewhere the pounding in his chest might finally settle.
Chasing after him, Kenichi murmured quietly, just to himself,

"Nothing to apologize for. — The rest of it's his problem to deal with."

"Here. Cold, delicious, love-filled cola. I wanted to say I shook it up nice so it'd taste even better, but doesn't look like you're in the mood for that right now."

"...There's nowhere on a vending machine to put love into. Thanks."

Savoring the coldness of the can in his palm, Subaru hooked a finger under the tab. Then, as if thinking something over, he closed his eyes, pointed the can's mouth away from anyone, and pressed down — instantly, the contents came bursting out of the opened top with tremendous force. In the blink of an eye, the can in Subaru's hand had lost about a third of its weight.

"Hey now, come on, you're supposed to fall for that. I said 'I'll make you think I'm gonna shake it and not do it' as a two-layer trick where I actually do it — that whole thing's ruined."

"I can see the pattern by now, you know how many years I've been dealing with you? It's basically a kind of trust that you'd never not do it. Ugh, my hands are sticky."

Shaking off the hand that got splashed with overflowing cola, Subaru tilted back the now-lighter can before even bringing it to his lips. The fizzing taste of carbonation passed through his mouth, and he felt his parched throat finally moisten.
If only whatever unpleasant thing had been welling up in his chest could have been washed away along with it — but unfortunately, the heavy knot remained lodged right where it had been.

"So, calmed down?"

"...Debatable."

Answering the question, Subaru sank further into the bench he was sitting on, let out a long sigh, and slumped his shoulders. Standing right in front of him, Kenichi, also raising his own cola to his lips, had the air of someone lost in thought with one eye closed.

After fleeing the conversation on the riverbank, Subaru and his father had ended up at a children's park not far from the bank. Naturally, there was no one around here either — no dad on an endless summer vacation swinging idly on the swings or anything like that.

"In a way, if I were on that swing right now, swaying back and forth, I feel like I wouldn't even be able to laugh at it. What would you do, Subaru, if you came back from the convenience store and your dad was on the swings?"

"First, take a picture on my phone and spread it on Twitter. Tweet reads: 'my dad's been freed from gravity rn.'"

"Ah, Twitter, huh. Your old man's on it too, you know. I followed and got followed by basically everybody, so now my feed's a total mess."

Glancing sideways at Kenichi talking cheerfully, Subaru let out a listless sigh and searched for a new topic. Somehow, something other than his behavior on the riverbank earlier — and once again, his skull throbbed.
Wary of the headache's intervals growing shorter and shorter, Subaru swallowed down the reaction as though it were some weak-willed thing, and stubbornly ignored it.

"...You only went to buy a drink from the vending machine, but it took forever. Something happen?"

"Hm? Nothing much. When I got up to the vending machine there, there was apparently some high school girl skipping school. Lectured her into going to school, bought her a drink, exchanged emails, and sent her on her way."

"I can't believe you managed to get her email address in that short a time."

Subaru had no words for the sheer skill of pulling off getting a high school girl's email address in about the time it'd take to swing by the bathroom. Kenichi tilted his head at him, saying "Really?"

"You can get someone's email address that easy, no problem. My phone's address book has, all told, close to three digits' worth of high school girls' addresses."

"I don't even have two digits total, and you've got three digits just from high school girls — that's literally a different order of magnitude. ...Dad, you're not, like, having some inappropriate thing with high school girls that's gonna end up in the papers, are you?"

"What are you even talking about."

Kenichi raised both hands at Subaru's concern, shrugging with a gesture that seemed to say he was exasperated.

"I don't get worked up over kids like high schoolers. Where my love's directed has long since been decided — I don't lust after anyone outside my family."

"By that category, even I'm included in the target group!"

"...Well, there is love there, after all. Might just barely be a chance, you know?"

"There's no chance! You're the one who should be asking what I'm even talking about!!"

At Subaru's furious shout, Kenichi cackled with laughter again.
The way that laugh lingered in the ear was crude, and yet it somehow never put anyone off. No — that was true of every single thing Kenichi did.
Every single one of his actions was, without question, the kind of thing that should be considered outlandish, over the top, overproduced, the sort that would make anyone recoil — and yet somehow, everyone took it in stride, even fondly.

Walking with his father again today for the first time in a while, Subaru had come to feel that firsthand.
Just from walking down the street, the number of times people had called out to Kenichi was more than could be counted on one hand. On top of that, everywhere they went there was someone who could share old memories with Kenichi, and he built easy, casual rapport even with people he'd just met. And he made no effort to hide any of it.

A throbbing pain pulsed at Subaru's temples, his breath growing ragged little by little.
The intervals between the sharp headaches were no longer just "shortening" — they'd become nearly continuous.
The pain, like a needle stabbing at the inside of his skull, wasn't the kind of thing that would just heal if left alone. But it wasn't the kind of thing a hospital could do anything about either.

Even without knowing why it hurt, Subaru felt he understood the cause of the pain.
And that it lay in the same place as the emotion pressing on his chest, the same place as that suffocating sensation.

"You don't look so good, Subaru. If you want, I can carry you piggyback, head home?"

"Don't need a piggyback ride, and don't need to go home either. ...Even if we went back, it'd be the same."

If anything, with Mom — Naoko — at home too, Subaru's condition would only get worse.
He was starting to understand what this pain reacted to, what thought or action made it flare up. If his guess was right, then the moment he and Kenichi went back to stand before Naoko, that was exactly when the pain would become unbearable. In other words—

"Even my own body's lecturing me now, huh."

Was this, finally, his body screaming out from the guilt of continuing to run away?

The terror of the hours spent hugging his knees in his room, glaring at the second hand of the clock. Even having gotten past that, the restlessness that refused to subside, and now this sudden, sharp headache.
It was as if, from inside his own skull, someone somewhere was shouting something at him at the top of their lungs, an unpleasant sensation of being accused.
—I don't know who the hell you are, but what do you know about me.

"Hey, so, Subaru. — Is there, like, a girl you like?"

The question came out of nowhere, aimed at Subaru who'd fallen silent.
It was the same not-even-funny throwaway line he'd been asked back in his room. The first time, he'd managed a wry laugh and a comeback; the second time, now, it struck a strangely raw nerve.
With the unrelenting headache adding to it, Subaru was about to give the same answer, packed with pure irritation—

"—Subaru."

Suddenly, from somewhere, he felt as though his heart had been shaken by a voice like the ring of a silver bell.

"—Huh?"

He lifted his head, searching for the source of the voice that seemed to have whispered right by his ear. But scanning around, he found no one — the only person in the park besides Subaru was Kenichi, standing right in front of him.
Kenichi too raised his eyebrows, startled by Subaru's sudden head-whipping.

"What's up? You've got the face of someone who just got called by name out of nowhere by some beautiful girl who shouldn't even be there."

"That's exactly it, and I've got nothing to say back... Did someone just say my name? Dad, you haven't secretly learned how to do a beautiful girl's voice, have you?"

"Your old man's got plenty of little tricks up his sleeve, but that one I haven't mastered. Alright, I'll practice it before the next bit, ask me again in like a month."

"It's not a bit... seriously, what was that."

Deflated by his father's remark, Subaru looked away and replayed the voice in his head. That silver-bell voice had struck him with a tone that was terribly gentle, yet carried warmth — enough that, for that instant, it had made him forget the intermittent headache entirely.

Soothed by that saving voice from somewhere unknown — a goddess's beautiful voice — the edge faded slightly from Subaru's expression. And to Subaru, who was quietly steadying his breath,

"So, that question from before. You got a girl you like or not?"

"...What's with you all of a sudden. What are you gonna do if I tell you? Even if there were someone, telling you a name wouldn't mean anything to you."

"That's exactly what you don't know. Who knows, maybe the address of the girl you like is already in my phone."

"Even if I did like someone, if I gave my dad her email address, that'd kill any romance stone dead."

Subaru cut him off flatly, and Kenichi pouted, whining "Aw, come on." Watching that gesture, so unbecoming of a middle-aged man, Subaru drained the rest of his cola in one go.

"You don't need to go about it so indirectly. Just ask straight out. ...Like, 'why don't you go to school.'"

"A guy's being considerate for once and you don't even read the room, son."

Kenichi gave a wry laugh at Subaru's words, but then continued with, "Well—"

"It's true I wanted to talk about that, so you're not wrong."

"...I know it's bad."

"No need to think it's bad, really. I've got a vague idea you've got your reasons for it, and even if you don't, well, I figure that's more or less fine to overlook too."

Subaru, looking away, offered a small excuse. Kenichi drained his own cola and sat down beside him on the bench. The wooden bench creaked, and a breeze passed between them.
Staying like that, both gazing in the same direction, neither looking at the other's face,

"I dunno how it is for everyone else, but I don't think school's everything. I mean, if we're being honest, I wasn't exactly a model student either. Skipped my own high school graduation ceremony — had my little sister bring me the diploma afterward."

"I've heard that story a hundred times. Your sister, two years below you, was at the same school, so when she graduated, they handed her your diploma along with hers, right? I've heard it till my ears are calloused."

"Then listen till they're squid, too, while you're at it. Point is, being the guy I am, I don't see any problem with not going to school if you don't want to. Now that I'm this age, there's times I think, huh, maybe not taking school seriously back then really was a loss — but you probably won't get that yet."

Kenichi's gaze seemed to drift somewhere far off. Catching a glimpse of that serious profile out of the corner of his eye, Subaru thought once again what a sly man his father was.
Usually he only ever showed his goofy side, which made moments like this — where'd he put that clownishness, all of a sudden — hit all the harder.

"I figure it's fine, y'know? People these days live to eighty on average. With eighty years to work with, wasting a year or two lazing around isn't gonna kill you. While you're young, you can make up for it easy enough. Lucky for you, my paycheck's decent enough."

Kenichi made a ring with his fingers and grinned crudely.
Without so much as glancing at Subaru, who hadn't offered so much as a murmur of agreement this whole time, he crossed his arms and nodded a few times.

"Long as you're alive, you're gonna run into problems that don't have an easy answer. When I hit stuff like that, I always used to just move around till the answer showed up, but I guess there's other ways too, like rolling around your room till you find one. Long as you're still struggling with it, I won't say a word. If you start giving up on it, though, well, I might have something to say then."

"...Why."

"Hm?"

"Why'd you suddenly feel like having this conversation today. ...It's not like today's some special day or anything. Today's just, I dunno, Green Pea Day."

"Piled a whole plate full of 'em, is why."

Subaru's mouth, only just having drained a cola, went dry all over again.
Breathing raggedly, Subaru waited on the answer to his question. Beside him, Kenichi tilted his head with a "hmm,"

"Why, huh. I dunno. I happened to have the day off, and I sorta thought of it in the middle of my morning dry towel rub, and Aquarius was doing great in the morning horoscope, and this morning your face looked... I dunno, just a little bit better than usual, somehow."

"My face, better?"

"I mean your expression, your look. Your face itself's still the same, only your eyes take after your mom, so it's still a villain's mug."

Kenichi pulled up the corners of his own eyes with both hands to demonstrate, then said "No, not that," and pointed the same fingers he'd used for the funny face at Subaru instead.

"I mean whatever happened — you didn't look like some guy who'd just been holed up in his room the whole time. According to your mom you didn't even go out yesterday, so you should've been exactly that, a guy who'd just been holed up in his room."

"...I guess that's — right, I guess. Though I did set sail on the vast sea of the internet."

"If that's what's making you grow, the lost little lambs coming to me on Twitter with their worries should be dwindling, not multiplying by the day..."

"You're doing that too now..."

Marveling at the sheer breadth of his father's connections, Subaru nonetheless found Kenichi's gaze wouldn't let him escape the real subject.
At the same time, it was also true that Kenichi's reasoning didn't click for Subaru at all.
In fact, just as his mother's testimony indicated, yesterday's Subaru had, just like every Subaru before him, simply wallowed in idleness and wasted the day away.
That he'd spent a day like that and yet the atmosphere had suddenly changed today, out of nowhere—

"Maybe it's just your own assumption or misunderstanding, Dad, or maybe it's the theory that you're not actually looking at me properly."

"That last one really stings! I'll have you know my phone's lock screen is still set to your adorable devil-child smile from when you were little!"

"'Adorable' I can live without, but putting 'devil' in there really captures the bad look you've had in your eyes since you were a baby."

Anyway, there was no question that Kenichi's words were off the mark.
Yesterday had been yesterday, and today was today. Subaru would spend his time exactly the same, unchanged.
He thought that was fine, and he meant to keep it that way. If he just kept on like this, surely someday even Kenichi and Naoko would notice.
—What it was Subaru truly wanted.

"—Guh!"

The instant that thought crossed his mind, a pain like sparks bursting before his eyes struck Subaru down.
A shock that made him think he'd genuinely been punched by someone. His vision swam at the grinding of his skull, as if his brain were trying to burst outward, and Subaru's posture crumpled even as he sat.

His heartbeat surged into a racing gallop again, the sound of blood rushing through his veins reaching his ears with startling clarity. His vision blurred, the world hazing and doubling, tripling before his eyes.
A wave of nausea welling up, and some nameless heat asserting its presence from deep in his chest.

Each tormenting Subaru's very existence in its own way, screaming as if trying to convey something.

"Whoa, hey, that looks seriously bad. You okay, Subaru?"

The state of his distress was apparently too much to ignore, and Kenichi, looking worried, put a hand on his shoulder. At that touch, Subaru somehow managed to raise his head, sweat beading on his brow.

"Ah... no, I'm fine. Just got a little dizzy, that's all..."

"—That must've been rough."

"—!?"

Once again, the silver-bell voice trembling in his ear sent a shiver through Subaru's entire body.
A voice full of tenderness and compassion. A voice that seemed to melt his taut, strained heart, intervening directly in Subaru's suffering — the pain, the grinding, the heat, all at once, their screaming weakened.

What was this voice? Why did the pain and suffering retreat before it?
He felt like he knew this voice. Felt like he'd been searching for it all along. Yearning and yearning, chasing and chasing, clinging to it, letting it go, and yet reclaiming it again—

"Thank you, Subaru."

"You're..."

Silver hair dancing in the wind was burned into the back of his eyelids. Amethyst eyes gazed straight into Subaru's face, and every note woven from those lips overflowed with something achingly dear.

"For saving me."

What, what, what, what, what is this.
Who, who, who, who, who, who, who, who, who is it.

—Was this girl the cause of his suffering? Was it her fault that he was being tormented, suffering, feeling so wretched he could throw up?

"—Subaru."

His breath caught. His throat burned. Something was welling up behind his eyes.

"It can't be helped, you know."

His fingertips trembled. His legs wouldn't hold him. His throat seized as though his lungs were convulsing.

"Subaru, you always do that, covering it up like that..."

Covering his face with trembling hands, choking back a sob in his seizing throat, letting the welling heat spill from his eyes, Subaru—

"Why do you keep saving me?"

—The answer to that, now, already lay within himself.

The moment he found it, every last bit of discomfort swirling inside Subaru vanished.
The grinding of his skull, the rising nausea, the dizziness that had blurred the world, the racing heartbeat urging him toward a decision — all of it converged, as if guiding Natsuki Subaru forward.

He lifted his head and wiped away the tears threatening to spill with his sleeve.
Staring at the damp sleeve for a moment, he turned his wrist as if shaking off the last trace of tears left there, and clenched his fist. And then—

"Sorry for worrying you. I'm okay now."

"Yeah? Well, if you've calmed down, that's good, but don't go worrying me like that."

"Yeah, sorry about that. Oh, and — about that question from before."

Freeing his shoulder from his father's hand, Subaru turned to face him.
Sitting side by side on the bench, Subaru looked straight up into his father's face. Come to think of it, though they'd exchanged words so many times today, he realized he hadn't once actually looked his father straight in the face like this.
Even in a moment like that, he'd been running away — with a wry, inward laugh at his own weakness, Subaru turned to his father, who wore a look of confusion, and said,

"—There's a girl I like now. So I'm already fine."

Picturing the silver-haired figure burned into his eyelids, Natsuki Subaru resolved himself to face his past.
