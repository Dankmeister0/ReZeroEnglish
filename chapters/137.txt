Let me tell you about a man named Wilhelm Trias.

Wilhelm was born the third son of House Trias, a provincial noble family of the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica. House Trias was an old and storied house entrusted with a domain near the northern border, close to the northern nation of Gusteko. That said, whatever glory it had once known as a house of the sword was a thing of the distant past; by the time Wilhelm was born, it was little more than a weak barony holding a modest stretch of land and a handful of subjects. Plainly put, it was simply one more example of a noble house in decline.

Both his eldest and second-eldest brothers possessed, modestly but adequately, the capacity befitting a lord of the domain, and thanks to that, House Trias offered its third son Wilhelm an environment blessed enough to keep him from want and free enough to let him choose his own future.

Being so much younger than his brothers, Wilhelm grew up without any of the entanglements that came with inheriting the house. And unlike his brothers, he lacked any gift for scholarship or administration — so it was a single sword he happened to encounter that first showed him something he might call a path forward.

In the manor's great hall hung a sword engraved with a weighty crest — a relic of the days when House Trias had still held some renown as a house of arms, a treasured blade now left behind as mere decoration in the Trias of the present, a splendid weapon reduced to an ornament.

The moment Wilhelm took that neglected treasure in hand and drew it from its sheath, he was utterly captivated by the sword's allure.
Before he knew it, he'd taken to spiriting the naked blade away on his own, swinging it from dawn until night in the hills behind the manor, day after day.
He first touched a sword at the age of eight. By fourteen, once he'd grown accustomed to its weight and length and his limbs had lengthened past their childish clumsiness, there was no one left in the domain who could match Wilhelm with a blade.

"I'm going to the capital. I'll join the Royal Army. I'll become a knight."

It was at fourteen that he left those foolish words behind — the kind of thing every boy thinks at least once — and ran off from home.
It began when his eldest brother, seeing Wilhelm absorbed in nothing but swordplay and running wild with the domain's delinquents, started lecturing him about "what he meant to do with his future."

Wilhelm gave no thought to the future; so long as the present was enjoyable, that was enough. Swinging his sword, feeling with certainty that he was growing stronger — that alone was his bliss.
Against a younger brother with not a shred of vision for the future, his brother's words were harsh. Battered by one plain truth after another and left without a retort, Wilhelm blurted out the dreamy declaration mentioned above.
From there it was tit for tat, ending in the inevitable "Like you'd ever understand how I feel!" — and Wilhelm actually did storm out, taking nothing but some money and his sword.

It was an unplanned departure, but Wilhelm managed his first journey to the royal capital before the money he'd taken ran out. He had, after all, always intended to make his name with a blade in the capital eventually — the only thing "unplanned" about it was that the timing had moved up, and he'd done it without his family's blessing.
Arriving in the royal capital in high spirits, Wilhelm made straight for the royal castle and knocked at its gates, determined to make his mark in history as a common foot soldier of the Royal Army.

In the present day, anyone trying to walk through the castle gates on such flimsy grounds would be taken for a vagrant and, as a matter of course, turned away at the door.
But at the time, the kingdom was embroiled in civil war on its eastern front against the Demi-Human alliance, and volunteers were so desperately needed that no number seemed to be enough.

Into that came a young boy claiming he could handle a sword, at least somewhat. He was welcomed with open arms, and Wilhelm enlisted in the Royal Army without so much as a hitch.

And so Wilhelm stepped onto the battlefield without ever having tasted hardship or setback. His swordsmanship, too, was swiftly checked by the towering wall that was reality; the pride he'd carried so high was thoroughly broken, and he was brought to his knees — a bitter but unforgettable experience, and the baptism every soldier undergoes in their first battle.
But even at this point, the keenness of Wilhelm's blade already surpassed, with ease, what one would expect of a green fifteen-year-old who'd never known real combat.

"Huh. Turns out life's pretty easy after all."

Seeing the young soldier standing atop a mountain of Demi-Human corpses in his first battle, sword driven into the pile, no one could help but sense that his future would be one soaked in blood.

From morning until night, quite literally until his strength gave out, Wilhelm kept swinging his sword — that had been his life for six years, from the age of eight until fourteen.
Even after joining the Royal Army in the capital, he spent every hour of free time allotted him on the same pursuit. His swordsmanship already ranked among the finest even among knights who'd seen real battle, and the name of this country-bred swordsman — who hadn't even received a knighthood — became known throughout the Royal Army with hope, and throughout the Demi-Human alliance as something to be dreaded.

Never broken by reality, and yet never satisfied with himself either, Wilhelm went on swinging his sword on the battlefield, unable to master the dark feelings churning within him.
Cutting through another's flesh with his blade, bathing in blood, proving himself stronger than the one whose life he took — only in that instant did a dark joy stir in him, and he knew it.

Before long, Wilhelm was killing with a smile on his face, and the name "Sword Demon" came to be spoken on the battlefield with dread and hatred alike.

The tally of his battlefield achievements easily surpassed the fingers of both hands, and yet Wilhelm never received a knighthood. He never fraternized with others, devoted himself single-mindedly and ascetically to the sword, rampaged through battle as if he didn't even recognize his own allies, and plunged into enemy lines only to return blooming with a harvest of blood. Such a being could hardly be suited to a title as glorious as "knight." In a kingdom where the old spirit of chivalry still very much lived on, Wilhelm — however much he contributed to it — continued to be treated as a foreign body.

And Wilhelm himself never thought to change that state of affairs.
He could not, like a knight, carry honor into battle, or bring some contest of another's life or the nobility of his own soul onto the field. Fight, and people die; blood flows; lives are ground away.
A man who could take the greatest pleasure in that sensation had no business being a knight — and if he ever stopped being able to enjoy it, he wanted no part of becoming one anyway.
A twisted hunger for battle had been eating away, long and slowly, at the heart of the young man named Wilhelm.

The crack in that began to show when he was eighteen — having counted four years of service in the Royal Army, at a point when there was no one left in the ranks who didn't know the name "Sword Demon."

She was a girl with beautiful long red hair, her profile so lovely it made one tremble.

It happened on a day of leave — with the front lines expanding, he'd been pulled back to the capital, and despite Wilhelm insisting it was unnecessary, a day off had been forced on him anyway.

Abruptly freed from the time he'd spent steeped in blood and death, and with time now on his hands, Wilhelm passed through the castle gate with his beloved sword in one hand.
The Trias family sword he'd snatched up on the day he ran from home, taken as a sort of parting souvenir, had grown quite worn by now, but after ten years together, it was the blade that fit his hand best. It wasn't that he couldn't use other swords, but for immersing himself in a place where lives were taken, this one was still the best.

With his beloved sword in hand, Wilhelm made his way to an abandoned development zone at the edge of the royal capital. "Under construction" made it sound better than it was — he'd heard the work had been halted for quite some time. At the very least, it had already been suspended by the time Wilhelm first came up to the capital, and there was still no telling when it might resume. Word was it would stay that way until the civil war with the Demi-Humans was settled.

"——"

The development zone in the early morning had no one about, and what few people did linger there were the sort who used the place as a hangout for unsavory purposes. A small showing of killing intent was usually enough to send such small fry fleeing without even showing their faces.

Emptying his mind, Wilhelm settled his spirit and drew his sword — a single stroke.
What rose up in his head were the thousands upon thousands of inexhaustible wooden dummies he'd already cut down in imagined duels. Dodge, land the blade, twist the body, take the head.
Ever since childhood, over and over, he had kept up this practice of imagined combat. The strength of his opponents grew fiercer and faster with each passing year, until before he knew it, the one he found himself dueling now was—

"You've got a nasty look in your eye."

—a swordsman with clouded eyes and a mouth slack with madness, a hollow figure standing nowhere near sanity — none other than the reflection he saw in the mirror every morning.

A duel against himself; among the shadows now sunk deep within him, that was the only opponent he could find whose skill matched his own. Rumor had it that among the Royal Army's elite, the Royal Guard in particular, there were plenty of formidable swordsmen — but Wilhelm's battlefield offered no chance to stand shoulder to shoulder with them. Naturally, then, his opponent was limited to himself.

Every time a day off was granted him, killing this self he could never actually kill had become Wilhelm's routine. Before he knew it, this corner of the development zone had been branded, so to speak, as the "Sword Demon's" territory, and no one came near it anymore.

Convenient, he decided, and gave himself over to what might be called his own private world of darkness. Within it played out sword battles reality could never offer him — and only there could he feel any sense of meaning in his life.

"Oh — sorry about that."

The foreign element that suddenly intruded on that world belonging to Wilhelm alone took the form of a beautiful girl.

Meaning to swing his sword, to duel to the death as he always did, he stepped into the development zone and stopped short, sensing that someone was already there.
The corner Wilhelm used had relatively even footing and, among the neglected lots, was the ideal spot in terms of space as well. That foreign element had, of all things, settled right into Wilhelm's place of respite, and now tilted her head slightly toward him.

"So there really are people who come here this early in the morning. In a place like this—"

"——"

The girl gave Wilhelm a faint smile and tossed some words his way — but Wilhelm's answer, simply put, was to slam his killing intent down on her.

An amateur would flee at once, hit by nothing more than that pressure. Even a professional, sensing Wilhelm's skill, would likely take their leave in a hurry.
But this girl, of all things—

"...Is something wrong? You're making a scary face."

—said this next, breezily deflecting Wilhelm's killing intent as though it were nothing at all.

Irritated, Wilhelm clicked his tongue.
An opponent unaffected by killing intent — that meant, in other words, someone with absolutely nothing to do with combat. Anyone with even a little awareness of violence would show some reaction to Wilhelm's pressure. But to someone entirely unconnected to it, it amounted to nothing more than intimidation. Depending on the person, even that intimidation might register as nothing more than a slight narrowing of the eyes.
The person before him now was, without question, the very worst — or best — case of the latter.

"What's a woman doing out here, this early in the morning, in a place like this?"

Wilhelm let out a breath, exasperated that her gaze still hadn't left his face, and responded as much. The girl made a small "hmm" in her throat at that, and,

"I'd like to throw that right back at you, word for word, but that seems a bit too mean, doesn't it? You look like someone who wouldn't get a joke."

"There's a lot of dangerous types around here. A woman walking alone isn't something I'd recommend."

"Oh, are you worried about me?"

"I could be one of those dangerous types myself, you know."

Wilhelm answered the girl's light jab with a sardonic one of his own, rattling the hilt of his sword to assert the weapon's presence. But the girl paid his gesture no mind at all, pointing off to the side and saying, "This."
Seated on the raised ledge, the girl pointed toward the far side of a rise in the lot. It couldn't be seen from where Wilhelm stood; he furrowed his brow, and she beckoned him over.

"I'm not exactly dying to see whatever it is..."

"Come on, come on. This way, this way."

Feeling the corner of his mouth twitch at being coaxed like a child, Wilhelm closed his eyes, steadied his nerves, and went to the girl's side. He set a foot on the rise, leaned over the far side to look, and—

"——"

—there before him lay an entire field of yellow flowers, lit up in the light of the morning sun.

"The construction stopped partway through, right? I figured no one would come, so I planted some seeds. I came to see how they'd turned out."

To Wilhelm, left speechless, the girl spoke as though confiding a secret.
He'd been coming to this place for quite a long time now, yet he'd never once noticed this flower field's existence — one that could be seen just by peering a little further in, just by widening his view a bit.

"Do you like flowers?"

The girl asked this of his still-silent profile.
Turning to face her, Wilhelm stared fixedly at the girl's face as she formed a faint smile. And then—

"No. Can't stand them," he answered, mouth twisted, voice low.

From then on, the girl and Wilhelm's encounters continued, again and again.

On the mornings when leave brought him back to the development lot, she always arrived before him, standing alone and quiet, gazing over the flower field as the wind passed over it.
And whenever she noticed Wilhelm had arrived,

"Have you come to like flowers?"

she would ask.

He would shake his head and, as if forgetting her presence entirely, throw himself into swinging his sword.
Drenched in sweat, sunk into imagined killing, he'd finish and look up to find her still there, and,

"You've got a lot of time on your hands, don't you," he'd say, sardonic, in what became a matter of habit.


Bit by bit, it seems, the time they spent talking gradually grew.
What had once been conversation only after he swung his sword began to happen a little before as well, and the talk afterward, too, stretched on a little longer each time.
Gradually, he began arriving earlier, and sometimes he'd set foot on the leveled ground before she did — enough that when she said, resentfully, "Oh, you're early today," it stirred a feeling in him strong enough to bring a smile.

It must have taken about three months from their first meeting before they exchanged names.

The girl, who gave her name as Theresia, said "A little late for that, isn't it?" with a small stick of her tongue. Wilhelm gave his name in return, adding, "Up till now I've just called you Flower Girl in my head," which earned him a pout.
Once they knew each other's names, it seems they began stepping, just a little, into deeper parts of each other. What had until then been harmless small talk gradually began to change in nature.

One day, Theresia asked him why he swung a sword.
Wilhelm answered without a moment's hesitation: because it was the only thing he had.

As ever, so long as he remained in the army, the blood-soaked days went on.
The war against the Demi-Humans grew ever fiercer, and the work of slipping past their magic to close the distance and cut them open from crotch to jaw was repeated with cold indifference.
He would run across the ground, cut through the wind, plunge into enemy lines, and send a commander's head flying. Returning to his own lines with sword in one hand, the severed head impaled upon it, he would receive praise mixed with dread and reverence, and let out a breath.

One day, at his feet on the battlefield, he noticed a flower swaying in the wind even as it was drenched in blood.
And at some point he also noticed that he'd taken to gently avoiding stepping on it.


"Have you come to like flowers?"

"No. Can't stand them."

"Why do you swing your sword?"

"Because it's all I have."


In their fixed exchange with Theresia — when talking about flowers, Wilhelm could respond, even, with a smile on his face. But when it came to talking about the sword, he found himself, before he knew it, feeling pain in reciting that same set answer.

Why did he swing a sword?
He thought back on the days he'd spent simply telling himself, *it's the only thing I have,* and stopping there. Earnestly beginning to search for an answer to that question, Wilhelm found himself going all the way back to the very day he first held a sword.

Back then, looking up at the sword that had, in his hands, shone with an unclouded blade untouched by blood, its light reflected in his small palms too small for it — what had he been thinking?


One day, still adrift in a whirlpool of thought with no answer in sight, he made his way to the usual place.

His steps were heavy, and he felt a weight of gloom at the thought of facing what awaited him there.
He couldn't recall ever having troubled his head this much before in his life. Wasn't it precisely because he didn't have to think about anything that he'd kept swinging his sword all this time? — he was on the verge of arriving at that too-simple answer when—

"—Wilhelm."

The girl, already there before him, turned to face him and called his name with a smile.

—Something suddenly shook his very soul.

His feet stopped, and whatever had been welling up inside him became impossible to hold back.
A sudden self-awareness assaulted his entire body, threatening to crush it.

By pouring everything into swinging his sword with an empty mind, everything he'd left behind, mind stopped, now came bursting forth.
He didn't know the reason. He wasn't even sure of the trigger. It was as though a dam that had long been straining had finally begun to give way, and, quite suddenly, reached its limit in this very instant.

Why did he swing a sword?
Why had he started swinging a sword in the first place?

He'd been drawn to the blade's brilliance, to its strength, to the cleanness of living as an edge. That much was true.
That was true, but that wasn't where it had started.

"I had to be able to do what my brothers couldn't."

His brothers had been utterly hopeless when it came to swordplay, or anything of that sort.
And yet, in their own way, his brothers had been trying to protect the house — and he had wanted to be of use to them, had gone looking for some other way to protect it.
And so, hadn't he been captivated by the brilliance and strength of the sword?


"Have you come to like flowers?"

"...I don't dislike them."

"Why do you swing your sword?"

"It's the only... the only way I could think of to protect something."



After that, the two of them never repeated that fixed exchange again.
Instead, it seems, he came to be the one bringing up topics more often. Before he knew it, he found himself going to the place more to talk with Theresia than to swing his sword.
The place that was meant to be somewhere he emptied his mind and swung a blade had somehow, by turning his slow head as hard as he could, become a place to bring up conversation instead of a sword.

It was around this time, too, that the Sword Demon's conduct on the battlefield began to change.
The body that had once thought of nothing but how quickly he could close the distance and how many enemy lives he could reap had, before he knew it, shifted toward how he could fight while minimizing losses among his allies.
He came to prioritize disabling enemies over ending their lives, and turned more often to supporting his allies rather than pressing his advantage. Naturally, the way those around him looked at him began to change.

People started speaking to him more, and he began speaking to others more as well.
Talk arose of a knighthood — something entirely foreign to him until then — and he found himself weighing, a little, the calculation of accepting it.
Having some measure of honor, he thought, would lend a certain polish even to an ulterior motive.

"They talked about knighting me. I became a knight."

"Really? Congratulations. You've taken one step closer to your dream."

"Dream?"

"You took up the sword to protect something, didn't you? A knight is someone who protects people."

Somewhere among the things he wished to protect, he felt, that smile of hers had been burned in.

More time passed.
Having gained a knight's standing, he came into contact with more people within the army, and information naturally began to reach him. The civil war against the Demi-Humans only grew more serious, seesawing back and forth across countless fronts. Wilhelm, too, came to know not only victories but a number of defeats.
Every time, he struggled to protect at least whatever lay within his sword's reach, and days went by in which he bit down hard on the frustration of what lay beyond it.

That word reached his ears that the fires of civil war had spread to House Trias's domain was, without doubt, in part due to the broadening web of connections he'd begun to form.

The uprising that had originally begun in the eastern part of the country had expanded, and one edge of its northward spread had reached, if only barely, as far as the Trias domain.

There was no order to do so. If he still remembered the loyalty owed to the Royal Army, to the standing granted him as a knight, acting on his own was not permitted.
But to Wilhelm, who once again carried in his chest the feeling he'd had the day he first held a sword, none of those constraints held any meaning.

By the time he raced to the domain he had once called home, most of it had already been stripped away by the enemy's advance.
Before the reality of a scene he'd left behind more than five years ago — a once-familiar landscape now fading before his eyes — Wilhelm drew his sword, raised his voice, and threw himself into the mist of blood.

He cut down enemies, trampled over corpses, screamed until his throat went raw, and bathed in the blood of others.
A demon's battle unfolded — one that lived by the sword, gave life by the sword, and could find meaning in nothing but the sword.

He was outnumbered, hopelessly so. There was no reinforcement, and his original strength had already been thin to begin with.
And unlike fighting shoulder to shoulder with comrades, Wilhelm was alone, given no way to retreat. Made to realize just how much he had, until now, believed he was fighting purely on his own strength, wound after wound piled up on him — until, at last, he could no longer move.

He fell atop the mountain of corpses he himself had built, his momentum finally broken before an enemy force that showed no sign of running dry, and Wilhelm understood that death was closing in before him.
His beloved sword, his long companion, had fallen at his side, and he lacked even the strength to hook it with his fingers and pull it up. When he closed his eyes, half his life flashed before him, and in it he saw only himself, endlessly swinging a sword.

*A lonely life, an empty one* — he was on the verge of settling on that conclusion, in that one fleeting vision — when, one after another, the faces of people rose up in his mind.
His parents, his two brothers, the delinquent friends he'd caused trouble with back in the domain, the comrades he'd fought beside in the Royal Army, one after another they came to him — and last of all, Theresia, standing before a field of flowers, rose in his mind.

"I don't... want to die..."

He had always believed that living by the sword and dying by the sword was exactly what he wanted.
But now, actually facing the end he'd supposedly wished for, at the far end of a life given wholly over to the blade, what assailed Wilhelm was nothing but an unbearable loneliness.

The enemy soldier, having watched countless comrades cut down, would not forgive him even those faint, final words.
A Demi-Human of extraordinary size, its scales green, brought its great sword down toward Wilhelm without mercy—.

"——"

The beauty of the strike that flashed out burned itself into his eyes; he would never forget it, not for all eternity.

The wind of the blade raged, and with each pass, the Demi-Humans' arms, necks, and torsos were shorn away as if brushed by a caress.
A wave of astonishment surged through the enemy ranks like a tide, but the blade's passage was far, far swifter than that, and death multiplied by the moment.

The scene unfolding before his eyes was like something out of a nightmare.
Blood sprayed, not even a scream could be voiced, and Demi-Human lives were reaped one after another. The strikes were so brilliant that they extinguished the flame of life without even informing the very person whose life was taken.
Whether that was cruelty or mercy, no one could say any longer.
If there was one thing that could be known, it was only this—

*That realm of the sword — I will never, for all my life, reach it.*

He had lived, giving nearly all of a life not so long yet, for the sake of being a swordsman. Precisely because he was that Wilhelm, he could understand just how high the summit was of the swordplay being wielded before him without mercy.
And along with that, the fact that it was a realm his own lack of talent could never, ever reach.

If what Wilhelm had produced was a valley of bloody mist, then what spread out before him now was, quite literally, a sea of blood. Even the scale of the mountains of corpses could not be compared.
Until the Demi-Humans who had invaded the Trias domain were annihilated to the last, that swordplay did not stop.

Having witnessed that overwhelming slaughter, and lifted up by comrades who arrived late to the scene, Wilhelm — even as his wounds were being tended — could not tear his eyes from that figure.
A figure walking away at ease, longsword swaying at her side. Seeing that not a single drop of anyone else's blood had touched her, a shudder ran through Wilhelm's entire body.
He would never, for all eternity, reach that place.

It was after returning to the royal capital that he was told the name "Sword Saint."
And it was around then, too, that the name of the Sword Saint began to echo in place of Wilhelm the Sword Demon's, across the land.

The "Sword Saint" — a legendary being of old, said to have once cut down a Witch.

Only the Divine Protection remained now, alive in the blood, continuing to guard one member of the family as a being beyond mortal reach, generation after generation. The name of the current Sword Saint had never once come to light before now — but that, too, ended here.

It was several days later, once his wounds had healed, that Wilhelm was able to make his way back to that usual place.

Gripping his beloved sword, treading slowly over the ground, Wilhelm made his way there.
He had a certainty that she would probably be there. And just as he'd been certain, Theresia sat in that place, unchanged.

Before she could even turn to face him, he'd already drawn his sword from its sheath and leapt at her.
An instant before the vertical strike split her head in two — it was caught, stopped, between two fingertips. Astonishment caught in his throat, and a smile rose to the corner of his mouth.

"This is humiliating."

"—Is it."

"Were you laughing at me?"

"——"

"Answer me, Theresia... no — Sword Saint!!"

He wrenched his sword free by force and slashed at her again, only to be dodged by a motion that didn't disturb a single hair. His legs were swept out from under him, and he was thrown down mercilessly, unable even to break his fall.
An impossible wall, an unfathomable gap, existed between the two of them.

"I won't be coming here anymore."

He slashed at her again and again, and each time she struck back, and Wilhelm was beaten down.
His beloved sword had, before he knew it, been wrested away into her hands; struck by the flat of the blade, he lost his breath and found himself unable to move a single step.

Distant. Far too weak. Out of reach. Not enough.

"Don't you dare... make that face and still carry a sword."

"It's because I'm the Sword Saint. I couldn't understand the reason for it, but now I do."

"The reason..."

"Swinging a sword to protect someone. I think that's a good reason."

—To a girl who loved to admire flowers and had never found meaning in holding a sword, he had, of all things, given her a reason.

Precisely because she was stronger than anyone, her sword's reach greater than anyone's, it mattered all the more.
And so, to atone for the sin he'd committed by giving her that reason—

"Wait... for me, Theresia..."

"............"

"I'm going to take the sword from you. I don't care what Divine Protection or role you've been given... don't you dare... underestimate what it means to swing a sword, the beauty of a blade — Sword Saint."

To a retreating back — to the Sword Saint beloved of the sword — one foolish demon spoke of swords.
Never again, after that, did the two of them meet in that place.

The Sword Demon vanished from the Royal Army, and in his place, the name of the Sword Saint began to spread through its ranks.

A match for a thousand — as if embodying that very phrase, Theresia's valiant fighting turned the tide of the war before everyone's eyes. Though a single individual, her martial prowess had already left the realm of the individual behind; the resounding name "Sword Saint" was, even to Demi-Humans who knew the old legends, a thing of despair.

The civil war came to its end roughly two years after the Sword Saint took to the battlefield.
The final settlement was carried over to talks between the leaders on both sides, and, at the very least, the fighting among those who bore swords came to a close.
To celebrate the war's end, a modest but splendid ceremony was held in the royal capital.

A ceremony planned around the awarding of medals, among other things, to the beautiful and above all powerful Sword Saint.
People from across the country came to the royal capital, to the royal castle, hoping for just a glimpse of her, and fervor enveloped the single girl who was the hero who had ended the war.

It was then, as if to sever that fervor, that the Sword Demon drifted down out of nowhere.

Before this reckless intruder with a drawn sword, the guards grew agitated. But the one who stepped forward to stop them was, of all people, the Sword Saint herself, on the very eve of her own knighting ceremony.
Everyone held their breath at the sight of the girl drawing her own sword to face the intruder.

The beauty of her stance was so refined it made words themselves seem hesitant.
And yet, how ominous a figure the person facing her was, by contrast.

Wearing a brown coat, whatever skin could be seen caked with dried rain and mud. The sword in his hand, too, was a shabby thing next to the Sword Saint's ceremonial blade — the fittings fine enough, but the blade itself warped, flecked with reddish-brown rust.

The king upon the dais stopped the knights who moved to aid the Sword Saint. Chin drawn in, everyone watched in held silence as the Sword Saint stepped forward and her blade flashed.

The two blades, swung to their fullest, crashed together, and a shrill ring cut through the crowd.
Flashes of light chained one into the next, the wind whipped up, and two shadows began to slide across the ground at a dizzying speed.
Before that sight, the people who had lost their voices found their hearts filled with nothing but overwhelming, boundless emotion.

Attack and defense traded places at a ferocious pace, the two swordsmen exchanging blows with their footing shifting between ground, air, wall, and sky. Watching that, before they knew it, there were even some among the crowd shedding tears.

A person truly could reach a realm such as this.
A sword truly could give another such a profound sense of beauty.

The blades crossed, locked at the hilt, tips flashing, clashing again and again.
And then, at last—

"——"

—the reddish-brown blade snapped in half at its middle, its severed tip spinning off through the air.
And then, the ceremonial sword the Sword Saint held—

"That's mine."

"............"

"That's my win."

The ornate, treasured sword fell to the ground with a clatter, and the broken tip of the other blade came to rest at the very edge of the Sword Saint's throat.
Time stood still, and everyone understood it. — The Sword Saint's defeat.

"You're weaker than me now. You have no reason left to carry a sword."

"If I don't carry a sword... then who will?"

"I'll take up your reason for swinging a sword. All you need to be is my reason to swing mine."

He flipped back the hood of his coat.
Beneath the reddish-brown grime, Wilhelm glared at Theresia with a sullen face.
At that attitude of his, she shook her head slightly, and,

"You're a terrible man. Throwing away all of someone's resolve and determination, just like that."

"I'll take all of that on too. You can forget you ever held a sword and live carefree... yeah, that's it. Grow some flowers, and live safe and easy behind me."

"Protected by your sword?"

"That's right."

"Will you protect me?"

"That's right."

Pressing a hand against the flat of the blade held to her throat, Theresia took a step forward.
The two of them, close enough now that even their breathing reached each other, looked into one another's faces. Tears welled in her glistening eyes and traced down along her smile, and,

"Do you like flowers?"

"I don't dislike them anymore."

"Why do you swing your sword?"

"To protect you."

Their faces drew close, the distance shrinking, and at last vanishing entirely.
Parting their lips after that close contact, Theresia, cheeks flushed, looked up at Wilhelm, and,

"Do you love me?"

"—Figure it out yourself."

He turned his face away and said it gruffly.
The crowd's frozen time broke, and the guards came rushing forward in a great wave. Among them, Wilhelm caught sight of faces he'd once stood shoulder to shoulder with, and shrugged.
At his curt attitude, Theresia puffed out her cheeks — just as she had in one of those days when the two of them stood before a field of flowers, laughing together.

"There are things I'd like to hear you actually say, you know."

"...Ah."

Scratching his head, wincing at his own awkwardness, Wilhelm decided there was nothing else for it, turned back to Theresia, leaned in close to her ear, and,

"Someday. Whenever I feel like it," he said, using words to cover up his embarrassment.

—A gleaming treasured sword sliced through rock-like hide with ease, and the wind tore past.

"AAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAH——!!"

As if chasing after the old swordsman charging forward with a battle cry, blood surged from the wound left by his blade, staining the sky vermilion.

His body was a wreck.
His left arm looked ready to fall away from the shoulder at any moment, and the blood soaking his whole body — his own mixed with that of his enemies — had turned a murky black.
The effect of even the briefest moment of healing magic could offer no more than staunching the bleeding and a small recovery of stamina. He remained, without question, in a critical condition that called for rest.

But looking at Wilhelm now, who could laugh at him as some dying old man?
Seeing the light in his eyes, the strength in each charging stride, the brilliance of every strike of the blade he gripped, hearing the battle cry that split the air, witnessing the blaze of that soul before them — who could call the culmination of that old man's life foolish?

The blade flashed, and the White Whale, shrieking, writhed in agony throughout its enormous body.
The Sword Demon's blade, racing across the back of the Witchbeast pinned beneath the great tree, unable to move, showed no hesitation. The blade entered from the tip of its head, passed clean through the back to the tail, and upon touching down, turned back once more toward the head, slicing open the underbelly along the way.

One stroke — long, long, deep and sharp, a single slash that circled all the way around and cleaved the White Whale in two.

Leaping up, the Sword Demon landed once more before the nose of the now-motionless White Whale.
Shaking the blood from his blade, the Sword Demon gazed steadily at the White Whale's right eye — the only one remaining — his own reflection cast within it, and,

"I have no intention of calling you evil. There's no use preaching good and evil to a beast. Between you and me, there is only, purely and simply, the absolute law of life and death — that the strong reap the weak."

"——"

"Sleep. — Forever."

With one final, small whimper, the light faded from the White Whale's eye.
Naturally, the strength drained all at once from that massive body, and the falling form, along with the blood dripping from it, sent up a tremor through the ground and a torrent of vermilion.

At the sensation of blood running beneath their feet, no one could find words to speak.
Silence fell over the Lifaus Highway, and then—

"It's over, Theresia. Finally..."

Above the now-still head of the White Whale, Wilhelm looked up at the sky.
The treasured sword slipped from his hand, and covering his face with his now-empty palm, the Sword Demon, having lost his sword, spoke in a trembling voice,

"Theresia, I..."

His voice was hoarse, but within it was a boundless love that had never once faded.

"I love you—!!"

Words of love that only Wilhelm knew, words he had never once been able to say.
A surge of feeling he had never once put into words, not until the very day he lost the one he loved most.

The words he should, by rights, have told her back when she once asked him that — Wilhelm finally spoke them aloud, decades later.

Atop the corpse of the White Whale, the Sword Demon, sword fallen from his hand, wept and cried out his love for his late wife.


"Here — the White Whale has fallen."

A clear, dignified voice rang out quietly over the plain in the night.
At that voice, the men who had been left speechless raised their heads, and every one of them saw the girl advancing forward, her earth dragon walking on.

Her long green hair was disheveled, whatever ornaments she'd worn ruined by wounds taken in the midst of battle, her face stained with her own blood — a figure appallingly ragged.
And yet, to their eyes, that girl's form shone brighter in that moment than at any other before.

If the brilliance of one's soul determines a person's worth, then that was only natural.

Her treasured sword had been lent out; Crusch now carried no blade at her side.
And so she raised her empty fist skyward, holding her clenched hand up for all to see, and,

"Having lived four hundred years and threatened this world — the Mist Witchbeast, the White Whale — has been slain by Wilhelm van Astrea!!"

"——Ohhhh!!"

"This battle — is our victory——!!"

Her lord's declaration of victory rang out high and clear, and the surviving knights raised a cheer.
Upon the plain, its mist now cleared, the first signs of night returned once more — moonlight shining down upon all the people of the earth below, as a proper night should be.


—And here, spanning several hundred years of time, the battle against the White Whale came to its end.
