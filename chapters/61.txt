Arc 2 Chapter 37: "The Curse Arts User's True Identity"

"Sister, and Subaru-kun... you two seem awfully close."

When she rejoined the two of them at the meeting spot, that was the first thing Rem said upon seeing them.
Her gaze was fixed on the space between them, on the spot where Ram had Subaru's sleeve pinched between her fingers — and if you cropped the scene down to just that, Subaru, the one actually involved, thought it might well look like some kind of racy little moment.

Laughing off Rem's hasty misunderstanding, Subaru pointed at the scene in question.

"No ulterior motive here, I swear. It's purely a case of me being slow and Ram-chi losing her patience. Sorry for making you wait."

He gave his sleeve a light shake to free it from her fingers, and apologized to Rem for the wait. Even so, Rem kept the temperature of her gaze noticeably lowered — though she dropped the look the moment Ram slid up to stand beside her.

Ah, now that he had the mental breathing room to actually look, he could see just how many cracks there were in Rem's composure, unflappable as she seemed on the surface. Especially where Ram was concerned — her precarious sister-complex tendencies came right into focus.

"Sister..."

"Barusu just said so too, Rem. No ulterior motive. Isn't that right?"

Ram cut off her little sister's clinging tone with a composed face. Rem, staring intently at her sister who shared her own face, suddenly narrowed her eyes as if she'd noticed something.

"Sister, did you happen to look?"

"That's right."

"...Even though I told you to stop, so many times."

"If it's necessary, I'll do it. Saying anything more is pointless, Rem."

"When you two have a conversation in your own private world, I'm the one left behind crying. Let's mix it up, bring me in, let's have a nice chat, yeah?!"

Rem, dead serious; Ram, deflecting her. And Subaru, cutting in to lighten the mood. Both of them — Rem especially — turned a sharp, pointed look on Subaru's attitude.
The reason was murky, but it seemed he'd invited a fair amount of wariness from Rem. Conversely, he had the feeling he'd earned some goodwill from Ram, which only reaffirmed just how tricky a pair of twins these two were to handle.

At this rate, the mood was charging straight toward a BAD END.
He wanted to raise his favorability with Rem somehow, but he hadn't the faintest idea how.
For now—

"Now, now, now, here's the thing. Let's not be so grim about it, let's keep it fun. Original mission: I'll carry bags, do whatever's needed."

He rolled his shoulders, rolled his hips, putting on a show of eager labor.
Some of the smaller purchases were probably already tucked inside the parcels Rem and Ram were carrying. If Ram's account was accurate, Subaru's job was to handle the big stuff — the kind that needed a man's hands.
With that in mind, he glanced behind the twins — and immediately regretted it.

Because there, sitting in stately dignity, was something barrel-shaped and large enough to need both arms to carry.

"...Isn't there supposed to be a limit to this?"

"Seven times."

"Huh?"

To Subaru, who dropped his voice with the mental image of a sweatdrop-line crossing his face, Rem's lips murmured a mysterious number. At Subaru's puzzled tilt of the head, Rem gave him a testing look, then said,

"Along the way, I intend to schedule breaks to match Subaru-kun's pace. Seven times is Rem's prediction."

At that bow, delivered with her familiar brand of courteous rudeness after a while, Subaru thought about the distance to the mansion. Roughly twenty minutes on foot. In a straight line, it wasn't even three kilometers — but Subaru was keenly, painfully aware of his own lack of stamina.
He walked over to the barrel in silence and gently tested its weight. Maybe, just maybe, it was all bark and no bite — a paper tiger. That pathetic hope was flatly denied by the weight that settled heavily into his lower back.

Carry this, for three kilometers, with seven rest stops—

He twisted at the waist, did a squat right there on the spot, stretched his back out wide, and declared,

"I'll do my best not to hit ten."

Rather than defiance against being provoked with "seven breaks," he was hedging his bets by naming an even higher number — and he kind of liked that about himself, just a little.

"For reference, Ram predicts thirteen breaks."

"The way neither of you lets personal feelings sway whether you go harsh or lenient — you two really are scrupulously fair, huh."

Even with the slight difference in favorability, Rem still seemed to be grading him a bit more generously. Which was, in its own way, a situation that made him want to tilt his head in confusion.

It was well past evening, the sunlight leaning heavily westward, by the time the three of them made it back to the mansion.

Beaten down by the weight of his load, Subaru had proposed rest breaks roughly twelve times along the way. His upper arms were already screaming from accumulated lactic acid, and his calves showed no sign of stopping their spasmodic trembling.
He'd lost easily against Rem's prediction — it was only his thin thread of pride, refusing to bow to Ram's malice, that had carried him the rest of the way.

"Made it, we made it... good job, me, GJ! Seriously, GJ."

"Sister, sister, Subaru-kun's sprawled flat on the ground, positively overjoyed."
"Rem, Rem, Barusu's lying on the ground like a dog, all moved to tears."

"Quiet, you two — praise me for pulling through before you start with the insults. I'm naive and delicate, keep whipping me like that and I'll snap right in half. I'm a product of the relaxed education system, not a lot of experience with setbacks!"

Especially since coming to this world, the whip had been coming down way too often. Sure, he was self-aware enough to know he'd also been handed some pretty generous carrots — but even accounting for that, wasn't the whip's power output a little excessive?
In any case—

"Yes, yes, well done."
"Yes, yes, good work."

Offering only the most perfunctory of thanks for Subaru's efforts, Rem lifted the wine barrel off the ground with ease.
Subaru, who could bench press eighty kilograms, wasn't sure he could even get that thing up to shoulder height if he really tried. And Rem hauled an object of that weight up one-handed, while still cradling a bundle of smaller items in the other arm.

At the sheer, almost comical absurdity of the sight, Subaru couldn't help but crack a smile.

"Was I even needed?"

"No? Originally, this is what Ram and Rem always did without Barusu around... as you can see."

Ram, not the least bit inclined to spare the boy inside Subaru's feelings.
Watching Rem's retreating back — her stride practically radiating a *thud, thud* sound effect — Subaru fully, painfully grasped just how meaningless his own manual labor had been.

"Then why'd you make me do it? ...Was this seriously just harassment? Hey now, quit bullying the junior employee, senpai."

"Don't you understand, Barusu? It was consideration, of course."

"I don't get it at all. Consideration for what, exactly?"

At Subaru's question, Ram gave a little "Hah" through her nose, then shrugged her shoulders in a pose of exasperation.

"Well then, would Barusu really feel nothing at all, if Emilia-sama saw him walking back cheerfully with nothing but a little pouch of a few spices in hand, while behind him Rem comes hauling one enormous wine barrel on her back?"

"Truly, senpai's consideration leaves me speechless!"

He dropped to his knees to express his gratitude to Ram's noble countenance.
Making a girl smaller than himself carry a huge load while he swaggered back with just the shopping bag, smug-faced — what kind of hellscape image was that.

Before even worrying about Emilia seeing it, that was surely a sight that could plunge a man into despair just from catching his own reflection in a mirror. Compared to having his thin veneer of boyish dignity worn down to nothing, this was small potatoes.

Just then—

"Sister, Roswaal-sama—"

Rem, who had gone on ahead with the luggage, came back to the two of them still exchanging words in front of the entrance. At the name of the mansion's master on her lips, it was Ram who reacted at lightning speed.
Whatever vitality that name brought Ram, it swept away the languid air she usually carried about her; she moved briskly to fall in step beside her sister, straightening her appearance as she walked. Then—

"What are you doing, Barusu? Do you intend to keep Roswaal-sama waiting?"

"I can't just understand what's going on from some private telepathy between you two, y'know. Uh, what, is this an 'all servants assemble' situation?"

Looked at with the kind of eyes one might use on a slow child, Subaru scratched his head and hurried to follow the two of them. Along the way, he fixed up his own appearance haphazardly in imitation of Ram, then opened the mansion's front door—

"Ahaha, oh, you were all together. That saves some trouble."

There stood Roswaal, master of the mansion, arms spread wide, waiting for the three of them.
Long indigo hair, odd eyes of blue and yellow. A languidly relaxed, effeminate smile on his lips — but right now, the air about him was different from usual.

"Dressed to go out somewhere?"

"Correct answer. Nnn, I don't much care for it myself, you know. But if I dress in my usual style, there are always those who get envious, so, well, I have no choice, no choice at all, but to wear formal attire like this."

The man who, in his everyday clothes, favored an overflowing sense of playfulness — collars absurdly oversized, geometric patterns, colors that seemed to want to blend into the background — was now dressed in proper, respectable clothing, like the first time Subaru had met him.
At Roswaal's attire, Subaru thought of two possibilities.
And then—

"A visitor?"
"Going out?"

The very possibilities Subaru had conjured were voiced simultaneously by the twins.
At the servants' question, Roswaal offered a wry smile.

"Ram's the correct one — going out. A bit of a troublesome message has come in, you see. I'm heading over to Garfiel's place to confirm it. I don't think I'll be long."

An unfamiliar name — Subaru couldn't even tell if it referred to a person or a place.
But following the twins, who nodded as if that alone explained everything, Subaru too nodded along at Roswaal's words without raising any objection.
He gave the three of them a magnanimous nod in turn.

"So, for a little while, I'll be away from the mansion. Either way, I expect I won't be back tonight — Ram, Rem. I'm counting on you."

"Yes, as you command."
"Yes, even at the cost of our lives."

He nodded to the two who agreed without hesitation, with just his eyes, and then those odd eyes turned to look at Subaru. Feeling faintly overwhelmed by their gleam, Subaru shifted in place.

"Sorry, but I haven't sworn any loyalty worth dying for yet, y'know."

"That's perfectly fine. If you suddenly swore something like that, it'd be heavy and honestly a bit unsettling. But well, I'm counting on you too, Subaru-kun."

He clapped Subaru on the shoulder, closed one eye, and let only the yellow one reflect Subaru's face.

"I sense something suspicious in the air. Never mind about me... but please, take good, good care of Emilia-sama, won't you?"

"—Yeah. That one's seriously handled."

No need to even ask.
Subaru had no idea how much Roswaal suspected about the situation. He didn't know — but he answered clearly, without hesitation.

—A development unlike any before.
This was, without question, a development signifying that Subaru's own actions had brought about a change in the world.

Receiving Subaru's nod, Roswaal nodded back, satisfied.
Then, after leaving a word or two more for his loyal twin retainers—

"Well then, I'll be off for a bit. Let's hope nothing happens, shall we."

He murmured that in a somewhat low voice, and Roswaal's back disappeared beyond the entrance.
Subaru saw him off with a bow, and then—

"Huh, now that I think about it, you two aren't going with him? And there's no sign of a carriage or anything waiting outside either... is he walking?"

At Subaru's question, the twins both wore a look of exasperation in their eyes.

"There'd be no point going. We'd only get in the way."

"Loyalty can't make you fly. And besides, it'd show what's under my skirt."

"Hey now, that's a lot of words there I can't just let slide..."

To Subaru's mind, the two of them were a hopelessly formidable fighting force, rivaling even Elsa — and yet here they were, plainly aware of their own insufficiency. On top of that, if he was to believe what Ram said—

"Whoa— seriously?!"

He pushed open the door through which Roswaal had vanished, confirmed his master was nowhere in sight outside. Then, turning his gaze skyward and scanning for a moment, he spotted, far off toward the sunset, a bean-sized silhouette just barely fluttering a cape. Unmistakably Roswaal, in solo flight.

"He can just... fly? ...Can that be done with the 'Shadow' attribute too?"

"If you can handle wind, fire, and earth in proper balance, it's possible."

At Ram's words, Subaru covered his face, thinking of how distant the day he'd achieve that would be.
Already, his lack of talent in magic had been firmly certified by both a royal court mage and a Spirit.
When would the day come that he could fly freely through the sky?

Setting that bit of sentimentality aside—

"Even without Roswaal-sama here, Rem and the others' work doesn't change. If anything, precisely because Roswaal-sama is away, we should do it properly."

"What a model-student opinion. But right, let's get to it."

Nodding along to Rem, who had begun taking charge, Subaru's mind was spinning at dizzying speed.

The situation had shifted once again.
And it was, unmistakably, a shift that made an attack all the more foreseeable. No doubt the twins, too, would redouble their efforts to secure the mansion's defenses — but for Subaru, who knew for certain that an attack was coming, this was no time for such half-measures.

The Curse Arts user's true identity — he needed to grasp it, as soon as possible.
There was no mistaking that the other side had sped up its actions, and today's visit to the village must have been the trigger.
Which meant Subaru's guess was correct: an assassin was, without a doubt, hiding among that group.

"So, with that, it's the long-awaited Bea-ko Time."

He pushed the door open and stepped into the forbidden archive.
At the sheer, brazen confidence of his entrance, Beatrice, seated on the stepladder reading a book, dropped her shoulders theatrically.

"You're the only one who breaks through Betty's 'Door Crossing' this easily. I really do wonder how you manage it, I suppose."

"Instinct, pure instinct. My sixth sense whispers to me, y'know."

He closed the door behind him, stepped further in, and approached Beatrice. She watched Subaru's approach with a face of utter distaste — but perhaps because she caught something serious mixed into his expression, she suddenly narrowed her eyes.

"Your face looks different than it did half a day ago, I suppose. You're a busy one, I suppose."

"Even I want to take it slow sometimes. But apparently the world's too busy a place to let me, or so it seems."

With change after change dropping out of nowhere, an ordinary man could barely keep up.
This and that and everything else, groping his way forward — it wasn't rare for the situation to become a dead end on all sides.
On that point, the current situation had him cornered. But at the same time, he had the confidence that he was, in turn, cornering the enemy.

"There's something I need you to check for me. I finished cleaning the bath at super speed to come here."

"If the bath took priority, this doesn't sound like a big deal, I suppose."

"It's less that the bath was the priority and more that managing Rem's favorability was the priority... nah, never mind that."

Cleaning that vast bathhouse was backbreaking work. Even now that he'd gotten fairly used to it, it was heavy enough labor to leave his whole body sluggish. Which was exactly why Rem's reaction, if he skipped it, was so terrifying to contemplate.
Especially since, even without that, Rem's favorability toward him hadn't been particularly high this time around the loop, given how close he'd been getting with her sister.

Even if he found the Curse Arts user, it would all be for nothing if Rem's low favorability dumped him into a BAD route.
Having to deal with two threats at once — it gave him a tightrope-walking feeling not unlike some two-timing man juggling relationships.
Though, granted, he doubted there were many cases of two-timing where a single flag-management mistake led straight to death.

"And besides, it's not that kind of racy story."

"You're getting off track again. ...So, what business do you have with Betty, I suppose?"

"Ah, right, about that..."

For a moment, in front of Beatrice, who seemed at least willing to listen, Subaru fell into silent thought. Hesitating over how best to bring it up, he gave a single nod, and said,

"I think I might be cursed. Mind checking for me?"

"...What are you even saying, I suppose?"

"I think I might be cursed. Mind checking for me?"

"Who told you to say the same thing twice?! It's only been half a day since we talked in detail about the Curse Arts user! There's a limit to how easily influenced a person can be—"

Perhaps thinking it was just Subaru's persecution complex acting up, Beatrice shouted and closed her book roughly, stepping closer. But partway there, her momentum faltered, and in its place her adorable face colored with suspicion, then conviction.
She came to a stop and looked up at Subaru with wary eyes.

"There's a trace of a spell formula... you really are cursed, I suppose."

"Seriously? Even though I sort of figured that was the case, hearing it actually said out loud still hits pretty hard, huh."

Even having braced himself, resolved to accept the damage of a curse, actually having it flatly confirmed by someone else struck a nerve.
It meant Subaru's suspicions had been affirmed — which was nothing less than confirmation that, among those good-natured villagers, the assassin in question was hiding.

"Do you know what kind of curse it is?"

"Just from looking at the formula, that much I can't tell, I suppose. Betty doesn't have deep knowledge on Curse Arts, and it's not something I particularly wanted to learn about, I suppose. But, nine times out of ten, it's the kind that takes a life."

Not as a threat of any kind, but flatly stating the fact — that was Beatrice.
At her words, Subaru nodded quietly. That a curse could exercise that kind of power was something Subaru had already experienced firsthand, in his own flesh.
Perhaps surprised at the lack of any shaken emotion on Subaru's face, Beatrice blinked her large eyes.

"You've got some nerve, I suppose. Aren't you afraid of dying?"

"Huh? Are you stupid or something? I'm terrified of dying, obviously. There's nothing in this world scarier than dying. Anyone who says stuff like 'better off dead' — I'd love for them to say that after they've actually died."

That much, at least, was a truth Subaru had gained in this other world, one he'd never, ever compromise on.
'Death' is absolute. No one has the right to handle it carelessly. And speaking of it lightly, as something to be compared against — that, too, is nothing but the careless folly of someone who doesn't truly know what it is.

Precisely because he now knew that — precisely because he was someone who had tasted 'Death' so many times — in order to avoid tasting a sensation he never wanted to feel again, worse even than 'Death' itself, he had redone the world this way, and come back.

"So this time, I'm gonna make it through, fate."

He pointed a finger to the sky, declaring war on whatever supernatural being might be watching from who-knew-where.
Then he turned back to face Beatrice once more.

"So, quick, hurry up and lift the curse for me. I don't have much time."

At the very least, he needed to identify the culprit, and strike first, before the person in question could act.
Eager at finally getting the chance to strike first, Subaru grew impatient. Beatrice, facing him, tilted her head.

"Why should Betty have to save your life, I suppose?"

"Figured you'd say something so charmless, so I came prepared with a way to convince you. If I die, Puck's gonna be sad."

"...Nii-cha wouldn't be all that shaken in his heart over the death of one or two humans like you, I suppose."

"No, no, hear me out — if I die, Emilia-tan gets devastated. If Emilia-tan gets devastated, that damages Puck too. And then, what position does that put you in — someone who could've prevented it, but didn't!"

"You — threatening someone in the middle of begging them to save you — that's the mark of someone whose head's gone completely soft, I suppose!"

Stamping her foot on the ground in irritation, but apparently unable to think of an effective counterargument, Beatrice let out a long sigh, using it to talk herself into it, and beckoned Subaru to come closer.

"I suppose I'll go along with your sweet talk. But, from here on out, Betty is done getting involved in this business, I suppose."

"Honestly, can't promise you that either. When I'm in trouble, I'm definitely coming back to borrow your strength. I'll gnaw your leg down to the bone, grind it into powder, and drink it as soup."

"Do you have any self-awareness at all about your position as the one being saved here, I suppose?"

"I've got a reputation for getting my way. Didn't become a shut-in for nothing, y'know."

At Subaru giving a thumbs up with his teeth gleaming, Beatrice shook her head as if giving up.
Then, after a moment, she let a white light gather in her palm, and touched it to Subaru's body.

"Now, I'm going to destroy the curse mark where the spell formula is engraved. It's the spot the Curse Arts user directly touched, so at best treat it as a reference, I suppose."

"Yeah, groundwork's already laid, don't worry."

Watching the light-bearing palm draw near, Subaru once again reviewed his own body.
He'd had each of the suspects touch a different spot. The only exception was the rejuvenated hag, who'd ignored his intentions entirely and grabbed his backside — but if Beatrice's hand headed for his rear, well, so be it, when it happened.
Once he'd confirmed the old hag was the culprit, he'd impeach Beatrice for her sexual harassment too, while he was at it.

But Subaru's little scheme—

"—Huh?"

—was completely derailed by the sheer unexpectedness of where Beatrice's palm touched.

Warm light seeped into his skin, and he felt a slowly building heat. A faint itch suddenly welled up. It seemed to seep out from somewhere deep inside, spreading, and finally—

"Black... mist...?"

It was seized directly, as black mist, in Beatrice's glowing hand.
The itch vanished, and it became clear that whatever had just been pulled out was the cause of that sensation. And then, that mist, within Beatrice's palm—

"How thoroughly loathsome, I suppose."

—vanished with an image like something being crushed, *squelch*.
Beatrice shook her hand, flicking both mist and light from her palm. Noticing Subaru's gaze watching her, she said,

"It's done. You should be fine now, I suppose."

At those words, Subaru let out a deep breath without meaning to.
He realized, without having noticed, just how small and anxious he'd been — holding his breath from tension — but there was something he needed to ask before he could laugh that off.

"Hey, Bea-ko."

"That nickname—"

"That spot your palm just touched — that's the same place the Curse Arts user touched, right?"

Cutting her off, pressing the question again, Subaru got a nod from Beatrice in reply.
With that single nod, Subaru learned the true identity of the Curse Arts user behind it all.

By his original plan, taking action after identifying the culprit was meant to wait until tomorrow.
He'd get word to Ram and Rem, and to Roswaal, the master of the mansion, and only then flush out the village's Curse Arts user and resolve the matter.
That had been the vision in Subaru's mind — but—

"I have to get to the village — now—!"

He bolted out through the door, forgetting even to leave a word of thanks to Beatrice.
Original plans, everything, thrown to the wind — as Subaru ran, his mind was filled with nothing but merciless curses hurled at fate.

"No matter how far, no matter how far... don't you dare screw with me!"

Pouring every ounce of imaginable fury into his snarling voice, Subaru ran.
He ran.
