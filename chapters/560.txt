Arc 7 Chapter 54B: "Covering the Azure Sky"

A sky of piercing blue looks down with magnanimous eyes upon the doings of the people below.

The white sun blazes and glimmers, while great white clouds drift slowly by.
A tepid breeze caresses the back of a neck, stirring pale blue hair, as someone breathes quietly and deeply.

Feeling the world with her whole body, she becomes aware that she herself is a part of it.
It's a rather grandiose way of putting it, as if something terribly difficult were being said. And yet, if she's told this is what she needs right now, she puts it into practice before she doubts it.
She's the one who asked to be taught, of her own will. It hasn't been long yet. She's too short-tempered to give up so soon.
And yet—

"...I can't lie about how impatient I feel."

She gently places a hand on her own chest, waiting for the air she's taken in to exert its power within her lungs.
She has no real sense of whether this feeling is becoming nourishment inside her. That uncertainty is frustrating, and she finds her own nature—always wanting results right away—more than a little irritating.
No, perhaps it isn't her own nature that makes her crave quick results, so much as the situation she's been placed in.
Not for her own sake, but for the sake of someone not here, someone trying to fulfill his role somewhere else—

"Ah! So you were here, were you!"

A voice calls out to the quietly meditating figure, and she remembers to breathe again. Letting out the air she'd held in her chest, she turns around to see a small shadow running up to her.
A young boy with fluffy, pink hair swaying as he runs. His face is adorable, his cheeks a cherry-blossom pink, his bare white legs beneath short trousers dazzlingly bright—a boy who seemed almost too delicate.
The boy, who looked to be about eleven or twelve, arrives before her and smiles.

"Priscilla-sama is calling for you! She wants you to come with me!"

"I understand. Thank you for coming to tell me, Schult-san."

"Not at all! There's no need to thank me for that, no sir!"

Even as he demurs, his cheeks flush happily—and at the sight of the boy, Schult, the corner of her mouth softens.
But the errand he's brought is no laughing matter. She can't just keep grinning.
Thinking that, she firms her loosened cheeks, lets out a small breath, and nods.

"Well then, shall we go together?"

"Yes! Rem-san will come along, too!"

Schult hops in place and raises a hand to his head in a salute. A little startled by his sudden energy, the girl—Rem—nods, and the two set off together toward the one who summoned them.

Toward Priscilla Barielle, Rem's temporary master here in the walled city of Guaral.

About ten days had passed since the fall of the walled city of Guaral—since that utterly absurd plan had been carried out, and the capture of the city had been completed with, as it turned out, minimal loss of life.
Fortunately, chaos within the city had been slight, thanks largely to the skill of Zikr Osman, commander of the Imperial soldiers, and the continued authority of Mizelda, whose influence hadn't waned even after passing the role of chief to her younger sister.
Between the two of them, they had kept those bearing arms from running wild, forestalling chaos and tragedy alike.

Of course, the city wasn't populated solely by fighters.
Rather, far outnumbering them were the ordinary townsfolk who had nothing to do with war. And yet, even without making a living by the sword, the people of Guaral were a hot-blooded bunch.
At first, it was said, there had been strong contempt for Zikr and his Imperial soldiers, who had had the city hall seized and real power stripped from them with barely a fight.
But that discontent among the townsfolk was quickly extinguished.
And the reason for that was—

"It's the Empire's way to follow whoever is stronger. Given that, surely it was plain to anyone's eyes that there was more justice in my actions than in the words of those who talk without acting."

"That's..."

"Or would you rather bow your head and endure the storm, only to shout loudly for someone to fix the broken houses once the wind dies down? Is it words like that which hold value?"

"That's an unfair comparison, taking such an extreme example."

It was a way of speaking that looked down on its opponent, not merely from a different standing but from a level above. Against that method of sealing off any escape, Rem answered quietly, just so.
There was a beat of silence at her reply, and then a faint sound rose in the throat—"heh"—before—

"So you'd call me a coward, and revile me for it. My, my, that's reckless even for the reckless, Rem."

"I think it's reckless when your life is on the line. But I don't think Priscilla-san is quite so short-tempered as that."

"You'd measure me by your own yardstick?"

"I don't have any other yardstick to begin with."

Pinned by those crimson eyes, Rem nonetheless returned the argument with composure.
Having lost her memories, with no past she could feel as real, everything Rem saw was fresh to her, and every action she took an unknown experience.
That might sometimes earn her the other party's displeasure as a result, but if she feared that too much, she wouldn't be able to move a single step. What was certain was this: roughly twenty days had passed since her present self began—and in these most recent few days, Rem had managed to spend her time with the woman before her without dying.
And so—

"Hmph. Impudent little thing to say. What an unlovable girl you are."

Somehow, Rem had come to expect that the other party would sheathe her spear after that.
Priscilla occasionally acted unreasonably for reasons beyond understanding, but at her core, beneath the dangerous remarks, she was a rational woman—that was the impression Rem held of the woman before her, Priscilla.

Priscilla, resting her cheek on her hand in a luxurious chair, a book open on her lap. The sight of her behaving as though she were mistress of a palace or a great manor made for a striking picture, but this place, after all, was borrowed.
At present, staying in the walled city, Priscilla had commandeered the largest mansion in the city and was spending her days there in leisure. Setting aside the city hall, it was the largest building in the city, extravagantly wasting space enough to house twenty or thirty people.
Naturally, there had been resistance from the mansion's original owner regarding the seizure, but that had been settled by the Empire's custom—its barbaric custom—in a manner that spilled a small amount of blood.
In other words, a show of force to see whose claim would prevail—

"..."

Rem glanced toward a corner of the room.
In the room where those in Priscilla's service had been gathered at her summons, besides Rem and the smiling Schult, one more figure had appeared.
That man was none other than the one who had wielded a sword in the "duel" over the mansion's ownership.

"Heinkel-san."

"...What."

"No, it's just that you seemed to be looking at me rather meaningfully, so I wondered if something was wrong."

The man, who had answered Rem's call in a low voice, made a sour face at her follow-up question. He roughly scratched at his own red hair and said,

"It's nothing much. Just thought you were a fearless girl, talking to Priscilla-jō like that."

"Reckless, and now fearless. It's not really either of those, though..."

"That's just how it looked to me. Don't nitpick every little thing."

Clicking his tongue softly, the man waved away the hand he'd been scratching his head with in response to Rem's answer.
This man, with his burning red hair and a body honed to considerable height, his innate ruggedness marred by unkempt stubble, was one of Priscilla's men, named Heinkel.

After Priscilla had unilaterally decided to remain in the walled city of Guaral, it was this Heinkel and Schult who had flown in from their original base. Add to them that helmeted man—Al—and you had the full roster of Priscilla's retainers.

"Of course, Rem-sama is one of our companions now, too!"

"Don't go deciding things on your own, runt. Isn't this woman supposed to be from the camp of Priscilla-jō's enemy? Far from being on our side, she's a potential enemy."

"Eh!? Rem-sama, were you our enemy!?"

"Um, that's still up in the air."

Rem answers the wide-eyed, flustered Schult, whose round eyes have gone wide with shock.
Heinkel's way of putting it is extreme, but the fact that Rem can't deny it outright speaks to her complicated position. With no memories, Rem lacks both the self-awareness and the information needed to determine where her allegiance lies.
Of course, the thing that would most contribute to settling Rem's uncertain standing—

"...that person."

The black-haired boy—Natsuki Subaru—had left the walled city to fulfill a duty entrusted to him, and Rem knew that his presence was what would give her ambiguous position something certain to stand on.
And yet, being unable to simply accept that and listen honestly to his words—that too was Rem's true feeling.

What makes it hard for Rem to easily accept his actions and words isn't only the ghastly miasma that clings to Subaru's entire body—no, that's no longer the only reason.
The miasma was certainly an obstacle to Rem seeing Subaru plainly for what he is, but she has already come to acknowledge that there is no falsehood or malice in his words or deeds.
The reason she cannot honestly accept him lies in the fact that Rem's own footing is uncertain.

Who is she, herself? What relationship did she have with Natsuki Subaru, and with the others?
Until she can face that question, neither Rem's time nor her stalled feet can move forward.

She wants to face Subaru in order to make her own existence certain, and yet, to face Subaru, she must first make her own existence certain.
It has become, in effect, a dilemma like wandering into a maze with no exit.

"You seem to be having quite a hard time of it. The furrow of your brow is fine proof of that."

"That's...true. I believe I'm putting Priscilla-san's advice into practice, but, well..."

"What?"

"Priscilla-san's talk is always difficult."

Lowering her eyes, Rem confesses, swallowing her embarrassment, that her own lack of learning is to blame.
She doesn't say it outright, but Priscilla's choice of words is convoluted and hard to grasp. Priscilla's mind, moreover, moves quickly, and on top of that she rarely bothers to consider others' understanding.
It's a quality much like Abel's—no, rather than "like," it should be called the same quality. If Rem were to voice this aloud, it would probably earn her the displeasure of both Priscilla and Abel, so she has no intention of saying it out loud.

"Advice? What advice? Priscilla-jō's been giving this woman advice?"

"Yes, sir, Heinkel-sama! I know all about it! Rem-sama has been letting me study alongside Priscilla-sama! And she's been taking care of Priscilla-sama's daily needs, just like me!"

"Hey—hey, hey, hey, are you out of your mind, Priscilla-jō!? What do you possibly gain from that? You're just raising up an enemy."

Heinkel, who hadn't been told the details, blinks in bewilderment upon learning of the arrangement between Rem and Priscilla.
He strides heavily up to the dais where Priscilla sits, points at Rem, and speaks.

"Here I figured you were keeping her around as a maid or something... this lets our side's information leak straight through. Going out of your way to put yourself at a disadvantage, this is taking your games too far—"

"Silence, fool. Do you presume to give me orders?"

"Gh—!"

"I've dealt you many a painful lesson, and yet you never learn, do you. My hand that strikes you is not idle for lack of anything else to do, you know—it has pages to turn in a book."

Tracing the cover of the book on her lap, Priscilla glares at Heinkel as she speaks.
There was nothing particularly fierce in Priscilla's tone, and yet Heinkel reacted as though a sword had been leveled at him, sucking in a breath and stepping back once, twice.
To Rem, Heinkel's fear seems far too exaggerated.

Of course, Rem has seen with her own eyes how outstanding Priscilla's skill is, how her swordsmanship drove back one of the Empire's mighty "Nine Divine Generals." Setting that fact aside, that Priscilla possesses matchless strength is something Rem can feel in her very skin.
And yet—

"I don't think Heinkel-san is all that inferior, either, though..."

Insofar as Rem's own eyes can judge, at least—that qualifier is necessary.
But in the seizure of this mansion, against the swordsman who stood in as the original owner's representative, Heinkel had let the man do nothing at all before stripping him of his sword, claiming victory without taking his life.
That clear, sharp sword-spirit and superior swordsmanship—no number of ordinary Imperial soldiers banded together could match it.
And yet, Heinkel's display of fear had seemed the very picture of overreaction.

Then again, Rem is an amateur even at being "herself." Perhaps the world as seen by the strong contains ways of seeing that someone like Rem simply cannot access.
In any case—

"That I favor that girl is nothing more than a whim on my part. But then, that I picked up Schult when he was starving to death on barren land, and that I summoned you when you were bleeding over fruitless efforts—both, too, were whims of mine."

"Yes, ma'am! I was helped because it suited Priscilla-sama's mood! I was very, very lucky!"

"Are you all right with that...?"

Tapping a finger on the book's cover, Priscilla declares breezily that everything hinges on her own mood. At her words, Rem is half exasperated, half overwhelmed, while Schult happily boasts of his own good fortune.
Heinkel, too, seems to have been freed from his earlier fright, though some wariness still lingers in the eyes he turns on Rem.
He is, perhaps, the one with the most sensible view of things among them.

"So, Priscilla-san, what's the reason you called us here?"

"Hm."

"If it's just about my daily duties, Schult-san and I are enough on our own, aren't we? I can't think of a reason for Heinkel-san to be here as well."

In terms of division of labor, Rem and Schult are more than able to split the work of caring for Priscilla's life at the mansion between them. Rem has grown quite accustomed to life with a cane, and has no worries about simple housework.
Perhaps she originally took the lead in doing that sort of task.

Given that kind of division of labor, there's no role left for Heinkel to play.
If she's going to bring that up, then to begin with—

"What exactly does Heinkel-san do, anyway...? I've only ever seen you swinging a sword, drinking, or playing with Schult-san."

"...Let it be known, I don't recall ever playing with this runt."

"That's right! Heinkel-sama looks after me a lot, but that's just me going up to him on my own! Heinkel-sama always looks lonely when he's by himself, so I thought I'd keep him company so he wouldn't be lonely..."

"Ah, is that how it was? Schult-san, you're very kind."

"Ehehehe, ma'am."

Her doubts resolved, Rem gently pats Schult's cowlick. It's a strange charm of Schult's that makes her want to pat him like that.
Within the city, there's also Utakata, who often darts in and out and makes her want to pat him too.
Perhaps it's simply that she likes doting on those younger than her.

"Argh, dammit, I can't keep up with this. Priscilla-jō! Just answer my question already. If there's nothing for me to do, I'm off to the tavern—"

"—It should be about time that those who left the city arrive at the Demon City."

"Ah..."

"If circumstances are going to move, it's likely to happen around this juncture. Keep yourself sharp."

Resting her cheek on the armrest, Priscilla's declaration changed the very moisture of the air.
The dry, taut air was proof enough that everyone's attention had shifted, precisely because of Priscilla's words. Heinkel's expression sharpened, and Rem too felt something like a jolt to the chest.
Only Schult, his hand still resting atop Rem's head, murmured, "Al-sama, thank you for your hard work~," offering encouragement for the long journey of the group that had set out.

"..."

If circumstances were going to move, Priscilla had prefaced. But at the very least, for Subaru and the others who had departed, some certain change—for better or worse—was bound to come.
Negotiations with the one being who might possibly become an ally—whether that succeeded or not would greatly determine the future course of what Abel was trying to do.

"Moving to fit this juncture—does that mean Priscilla-jō has some idea of what's coming?"

"No, nothing specific. It's merely a matter of my own experience."

"Experience, is it? What do you mean by that...?"

"When great things are about to move, the stillness that came before them shatters all at once, as if it had all been a lie. As though by prior arrangement, like a stack of building blocks toppling all together."

There was an indescribable persuasiveness to Priscilla's flat, matter-of-fact way of speaking.
In the end, it was a remark narrowed down to a single point—don't let your guard down—but Rem took a deep breath and looked again at her own feet. If she let her concern for Subaru and the others make her neglect what was close at hand, that would be putting the cart before the horse.
To begin with, she realized anew, upon putting it into words, that being endlessly preoccupied with Subaru was an awfully unpleasant state of affairs.

"Whether he's near or far, he really is a troublesome person..."

"Rem-sama? Are you all right? Somehow, your face looks red."

"I'm fine. Something just welled up in me, that's all. Bubbling up—anger."

"Being an angry-bug isn't good! Rem-sama is lovelier when she smiles~!"

Schult flails his arms and legs, pleading earnestly, and Rem's eyes soften at the corners. Meanwhile, Heinkel, whose face had turned grave at Priscilla's warning, said,

"Priscilla-jō, if that's all, I'll be going."

"Do as you please. Whether you drown in drink or idle your time away is your own affair."

"I'm not in the mood to get plastered on booze. I'll go have a word with that scruffy-headed commander at the city hall."

Answering with a serious face, Heinkel strode out of the room in long strides. Watching his back go, Rem turned back to Priscilla.

"So, Priscilla-san, what will you do?"

"You're curious about my movements?"

"That's... yes, I am. It's not that I think everything Priscilla-san does is correct, but..."

Even so, Priscilla made her judgments with knowledge and insight that Rem simply didn't have.
For Rem, who possessed so little, that was precious material for deciding what to choose. At Rem's remark—rude, in a sense—Priscilla let out a small "heh" of laughter and said,

"You have no wish to be a headless doll, I see. Otherwise, there'd be little point in keeping you at my side."

"Priscilla-san?"

"As for the earlier question—preparations shall be laid by that fool. That 'General,' and the People of Shudrak, are of some use as well. As for me, then..."

"What will you do...?"

Rem holds her breath faintly, waiting for what comes next from Priscilla. Unconsciously, beside Rem, Schult too clenches his fists in anticipation, waiting for his mistress's answer.
At the two pairs of eyes fixed on her, Priscilla narrows her long, sharp eyes and says,

"—A bath, I think. Let flower petals float in it, and burn some incense."

"...Eh?"

"What, must I say it twice? A bath it is. Prepare the bathhouse at once."

Waving a hand languidly, Priscilla hurries the two of them along.
Naturally, Rem cannot hide her confusion, stammering "eh, eh"—but Schult snaps to a crisp salute.

"Understood, ma'am! I'll go draw the bath right away~!"

Answering energetically, Schult darts out of the room at once. Watching his small back go, Rem turns back toward Priscilla.
Priscilla, still resting her cheek on her hand, seems ready to return to her book.

"Are you not going as well? Leave everything to Schult alone, and he's liable to forget to plug the drain and waste a great deal of hot water."

"I'll help Schult-san. But—what are you thinking?"

"That suspicious nature of yours is proof enough of how strong your preconceptions are. Do you take me for some observer of the world, freely manipulating every phenomenon at will?"

"I—I don't think that far, but..."

Argued so logically, the force of Rem's pursuit deflated.
Snorting at Rem's demeanor, Priscilla continued, "Listen well," and said,

"Everything in this world is arranged to my convenience. But that does not mean I bend everything to my own will. Such tedium is not something I could ever wish for."

"Getting everything as you wish is tedious?"

"If everything that could happen went exactly as intended, what meaning would there be in greeting tomorrow? Rem, would you wish for a world in which everything had already ended from the moment you were born?"

"..."

Asked this by Priscilla, Rem fell silent, pressing her lips shut.
Priscilla's words were, once again, difficult to parse, but Rem could just barely grasp the gist of them. To spend one's days exactly as one wished meant never encountering anything new, anything unknown.
And that, for Priscilla, was not something desirable.
And yet—

"For someone like me, who can't manage many things well, that's a luxurious kind of rejection."

Rem, who had seen Subaru off on his journey and remained in the walled city at Priscilla's side—at the root of that was a hunger for the lost existence called "Rem," and a search for some means, some catalyst, to break free from her current, unworthy self.
For Rem, suffering because nothing went as she wished, Priscilla's way of enjoying things not going as she wished was something incomprehensible.
Even if it meant Priscilla might write her off as a girl without any charm for it.

"I've no wish for you to become the same as me, nor to wish for the same things I wish for. Do as you please."

But to Rem, who had spoken even with a certain grim resolve, Priscilla's response was unexpectedly gentle.
To Rem, who blinked in surprise, Priscilla looked on with the eyes one might use to watch a troublesome dog, and said,

"The world is beautiful simply as it is."

In Priscilla's quiet murmur, there seemed to be contained her true feelings, without exaggeration.
Having thought that, Rem dismisses her own notion as foolish. Because, if that really were her unvarnished true feeling, the scale of it would be far too immense.
It would mean that Priscilla loved and cherished something impossibly vast.

"Cherishing something so dearly is the exact opposite of the impression I have of Priscilla-san, though..."

"Did you say something?"

"No, nothing much at all..."

Rem shakes her head slowly, and the murkiness in her chest has thinned somewhat.
Not everything has cleared, but the cloud that had been looming just before has vanished. Just as Rem reaches something of a conclusion in her self-analysis—

"W-w-w-waaah! The water, the water's overflowing! I—I'm going to drown~!!"

An urgent scream from Schult comes from the direction of the mansion's bathhouse. Picturing Schult caught in exactly the situation one would expect, Rem raises her head.
Her eyes meet Priscilla's, who juts her pale chin toward the door, commanding, "Go."

"I won't be bent to Priscilla-san's whims, you know."

"And yet you can hardly ignore Schult's scream. There, I've won."

Rem's small resistance—insisting she won't simply obey—scatters, fleeting.
There was no room to argue further than that, not with Schult's safety so clearly in question.

—Priscilla's warning had driven a wedge deep and true into Rem's chest.

When great things move, they pay no heed to insignificant individual human beings.
Without a care for those caught up in them, they sweep everything away like a great wave.
Rem had believed she knew that from firsthand experience.

Waking with no memory, held in the arms of an unfamiliar person in an unfamiliar place—that was where Rem's memories began.
Fleeing that boy, encountering him again, then being captured by a great many Imperial soldiers, rescued from black smoke and flame, drifting from place to place until arriving at this city to spend her days.
"Turbulent" seemed almost a word coined for Rem's life up to now.

—And yet, she would soon come to realize that even that understanding had been too naive.


"—Priscilla-san?"

Priscilla slipped free of Rem's hand and rose to her feet, and Rem's brow furrowed at the sight.
Amid the warm, rising steam, clad in bathing attire, Rem was in the mansion's bathhouse. The existence of this splendid bathhouse was the greatest reason Priscilla had seized this mansion in the first place.
The bathhouse, with a tub one could fill and stretch one's limbs out in, had apparently been built to suit the original owner's particular tastes, and was something with no equal to be found elsewhere.

Indeed, once she finished tending to Priscilla, Rem too was allowed to bathe there, and she had come to understand firsthand just how much this was something Priscilla would want to seize by force.
In any case, in the midst of bathing there, Priscilla slipped free of Rem's hands as Rem was washing her hair, rose slowly to her feet, and walked toward the bathroom window.

"Priscilla-san, I'm still in the middle of washing you... ah!"

Without lending an ear to Rem's call, wondering what she meant to do, Priscilla threw the bathroom window wide open, her beautiful bare body exposed without the slightest hesitation.
Cold outside air, incomparable to the steam, rushed into the bathhouse, and Rem instinctively shrank back. But Priscilla's sudden action didn't end there.
She leaned out through the open window and stepped straight out onto the terrace beyond the bathhouse.

"Wh-what are you doing! Someone might see you! Even if this is the top floor..."

Hurrying to grab her cane and a towel, Rem catches up to Priscilla, who has gone outside. She drapes the towel over Priscilla's slender shoulders, chiding herself for having thought she'd grown used to this abruptness.
But heedless of Rem's reflection, Priscilla keeps her gaze fixed outward.

"—The sky."

"The sky? ...We're not even that high up. Come now, before you catch a chill, let's go back—"

Rem tries to draw her by the arm to say let's go back inside, but the motion is interrupted.
Turning, Priscilla presses a finger gently against Rem's lips. Before Rem, who widens her eyes in surprise, Priscilla lets her blood-red eyes waver, and says,

"Look to the sky, Rem. It seems the 'vacation' has come to an end."

The words made no sense, but the tone of her voice left no room in Rem for doubt.
As if led, Rem lifts her gaze, turning her eyes toward the same sky Priscilla had been looking up at. She strains her blue eyes, trying to see what Priscilla sees.
But for all her resolve not to miss a thing, it seemed there was little point in the effort.
Because—

"That's..."

Above the terrace, above the heads of Rem and Priscilla as they take the wind, in the vast sky lit by an oversized, shining sun, black dots hang, drawing slowly closer.
One black dot—no, two, then three, gradually increasing in number, their blurred shapes growing steadily clearer, and Rem's throat, unbidden, lets out a gasp.
The identity of those black dots, growing in number, drawing closer, was—

"—Quickly, arm those below. Against a swarm of flying dragons, there is no room to play."

Rem's skin, her very soul, could feel how starkly, how vividly Priscilla's warning had taken shape.

—At that same hour, gazing down upon the city ringed by its high walls, golden eyes narrow.

Wrapping the blue sky about its whole body, advancing through that vast expanse as though it owned the place, cutting the wind shoulder to shoulder with the white clouds—the greatest creatures in this world, a swarm of winged dragons.

"—The walled city. Guaral."

The name of the target city rolls off a tongue, and blazing battle-lust heats the blood through the whole body.
In response to that rising battle-lust, the spirits of the comrades flying all around swell with heat as well. Whinnies mix with the beat of wings striking the sky, and anticipation for the hunt now close at hand swells ever larger.

To scold that as impatience would be nothing less than an insult to dragons, hunters of the azure sky.
Any being whose blood does not stir at this anticipation is a coward unworthy to fly alongside the white clouds.

"—!!"

"I know. Just like the old man says, let's do the job properly."

The appeal rising from below is the anticipation for the hunt, spilling from the one whose back is being borrowed.
On the back of a flying dragon beating its wings, a small figure with short arms folded shows understanding for that excitement.

Sky-blue hair evenly cut at shoulder length, golden eyes gleaming brilliantly. The gorgeous attire wrapping that seemingly delicate body betrays not a hint of that being's ferocity and savagery.
At a glance, some might even call it lovely. But that would only be until they laid eyes on the head of that figure—on the two black horns present there.

Even in the Vollachian Empire, where those of horned races are far from rare, the chance to see those "black horns" is rare indeed—no, it should never be possible at all.
Because that is nothing less than proof of an existence that should not exist.

"Everyone, are you ready?"

At the call of the figure sprouting black horns that should not exist, countless whinnies answer—the swarm of flying dragons, rulers of the sky, a swarm of hundreds that should never exist, responding as one.

Bearing the sun on its back, crossing the blue sky, a manifestation of ruin surging forward like a cloud of locusts.
Once it descends, roars of rage and screams of anguish will echo, every life will be crushed and snuffed out—a calamity.

By strange coincidence, at the very same hour that, far away, the "Great Disaster" was on the verge of destroying a city, a calamity of a different form now closed in upon a city protected by its walls.
Before the opening of an absolute, inescapable, and merciless hunt, that being—Madelyn Eschart—narrows her golden eyes and twists her cheek into a smile.

The "Nine" of the "Nine Divine Generals," the "Dragon General" ordered to annihilate the city, makes her pronouncement.

"Tremble in fear, and flee for your lives. There is no escape for you. —The dragon has come," she says.
