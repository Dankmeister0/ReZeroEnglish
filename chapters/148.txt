Arc 3 Chapter 71: "The Girl of the Forbidden Archive"

"Hey, Subaru. What was that, 'tarara ttararatte'?"

"Don't worry about that one, okay? Right, that was just a greeting long passed down where I'm from. It means, 'That smile of yours is so lovely I could just eat it up.'"

"Then please never say it again."

"That's surprisingly harsh!"

A small exchange after Emilia greeted him with a smile and a "welcome back."

Her smile had bloomed like a flower, cool and clear enough to sweep away every scrap of anxiety and impatience that had been building up like storm clouds in Subaru's heart, all in one gust.

In response, after working himself into a helpless, heart-pounding fluster, he'd tried to muster the softest smile he could manage and answer with "I'm home"—

"Tarara ttararatte."

"...Huh?"

—and had, as it turned out, simply garbled the whole thing.

It was too pathetic and too embarrassing to even consider telling her the truth.

He covered with some empty banter, which she sliced clean through, leaving him a little deflated, and then Subaru and Emilia went off to search the mansion for Ram—though as it happened, the maid they sought turned up only a few minutes into the search.

"Oh my, that was quicker than I expected."

Ram said this while looking up at them from where she sat, quite improperly, on the steps of the entrance hall.

Her attitude—so far from anything resembling deference, more and more like she owned the place—drew a small sigh out of Subaru.

"Your going-my-own-way-ness never ceases to amaze me. I like to think I'm pretty selfish myself, but next to you I lose in a landslide, no contest."

"It's a good feeling to win, no matter the contest. Bow before Ram, Barusu. —So then, did the two of you settle things?"

The first half was aimed at Subaru, the second at Emilia, walking behind him.

At Ram's question, Emilia gave a small nod. "Yes."

"Subaru already told me the broad strokes. Crusch-sama's people and the ones Anastasia-sama hired are apparently coming to the village. And Subaru's hired some traveling merchants too... to help us get away, right?"

"Yeah. The merchants' dragon carriages should be able to carry the whole village between them, and a lot of the goods they're hauling could double as food while we're on the move. Pretty good idea, if I say so myself."

"Not bad, for a Barusu. Ram will honestly praise you for that. Be glad."

"Hard to be glad when you say it like that!"

Setting aside Ram's habit of keeping herself firmly above everyone else, thanks to the earlier talk in the other room, Emilia was taking Subaru's proposal in stride. She'd no doubt wanted to be the one to break the deadlock herself, but—

"If I keep insisting on that and end up missing something important, that's putting the cart before the horse, isn't it? I'm fine, so you don't have to look at me so worriedly."

"I'm not worried or anything. I can feel your trust in me radiating right up through the hand you're holding..."

"Oh—sorry, I've been holding it this whole time. That must've been annoying."

His careless remark reminded her, and the hand that had been linked with his, unimpeded this whole time, slipped free. A whiny "ah—" escaped him, but it was already too late.

Leaving Subaru behind to lift his newly freed hand before his face and mourn his own poor choice of words, Emilia turned back to Ram.

"So, Ram. I want to get the villagers to safety, but... where do you think we should go? I know I'm the one who's supposed to decide, really."

"Emilia-sama has no feel for the local geography, so I won't ask the impossible of you. If Ram may offer an opinion... I'd suggest heading to the Sanctuary, where Roswaal-sama has gone."

"The Sanctuary... would that be safe?"

"I can't promise anything for certain, but Roswaal-sama is there, and while there are complications, there is a defensive force. It would be far more reliable than holing up here."

At Ram's suggestion, Emilia touched a finger to her lips as if in thought. Peeking out from beside her, Subaru prefaced his question with "actually" and addressed Ram.

"I keep hearing about it, but where exactly is this Sanctuary Roz went off to? It keeps coming up with a name I've heard a few times now... but if I don't know that, I honestly can't judge anything."

"The decision is Emilia-sama's to make. It's cruel to say Barusu needn't trouble himself over it... but Ram doesn't have the authority to tell you that either. Roswaal-sama hasn't given his permission, so you'll just have to keep agonizing over it."

"Roz's permission, huh..."

Subaru narrowed his eyes; Ram gave no reaction. Judging that he'd get nothing more out of her, Subaru cast back through everything he'd heard about the Sanctuary so far.

Though, in truth, there wasn't much information on the subject at all. It was where Roswaal had gone, a place where some influential figure in the domain resided, and—as he'd just learned—a place with a defensive force beyond just Roswaal himself.

"You can at least tell me the distance and whether it's friendly toward us, right? That much's gotta be essential as a baseline for making a decision."

"—Roughly eight hours by dragon carriage, I'd say. It's in a cooperative relationship with Roswaal-sama, so hostility is out of the question. Only—"

Ram, who had been answering Subaru's questions smoothly, cut herself off there. Rare for her, she bit her lip lightly, betraying a moment's hesitation. Subaru's brow furrowed at that, puzzled, but Emilia grasped the meaning behind the reaction more clearly.

"Are you worried that half-elves might not be welcome there?"

"I don't wish to add to Emilia-sama's burdens, but... yes, that's it exactly. The Sanctuary is a gathering place for all sorts of people with complicated circumstances, so I can't say for certain how much it would matter that you're a half-elf among them... I can't promise anything."

There it was again—the problem of contempt for half-elves.

Wherever they turned, that problem stood in the way, and Subaru's patience was nearly at its limit. But heedless of the anger churning inside him, Emilia gave a small nod.

"It's something I already know about, so it can't be helped. If you're saying it's not a dealbreaker, then it should be possible to get the villagers to shelter at the Sanctuary?"

"There's no way Roswaal-sama, as lord of this domain, would want his people treated carelessly. On that point at least, Garfiel, the Sanctuary's representative, can certainly be trusted."

Hearing the certainty in that answer, Emilia closed her eyes, seemingly satisfied. Then she glanced quietly toward Subaru, who was holding his tongue and swallowing his anger.

"Does Subaru have any other good ideas?"

"Well... let's see. If it's eight hours to the Sanctuary and it's supposed to be safe, then... I don't think we need another route on the table."

If worse came to worst and they were stuck for somewhere to evacuate to, Subaru had also considered returning to the royal capital as an option. As things stood, with the White Whale subjugated, the Lifaus Highway was passable again.

By dragon carriage at full speed, it was half a day to the capital. With so many women, children, and elderly among them, accounting for that would cost some time, but even so it likely wouldn't take a full day.

It would also mean the chance to link up with Crusch, presumably already back, and Rem, who was traveling with her—and since their countermeasures against the Witch Cult pointed in the same direction, it would be easier to coordinate. There was also the thought that, as allies bound by a pact, Crusch and Emilia really ought to sit down together and talk things through properly.

"But well, this has its share of risks too."

First, it was farther than the place called the Sanctuary.

With so much still unknown about the relative strength of their forces and the enemy's, anxiety remained, and long-distance travel was something to avoid where possible. Between more than half a day and a quarter day, the latter was the better choice.

Second, the greater the distance, the longer it would take to turn around and come back to Mathers territory. With the Witch Cult threat still active, the subjugation force would have no choice but to escort Emilia and the others all the way to safety before doubling back to hunt down the remaining cultists—and the longer that took, the greater the risk the stragglers would simply flee.

And third: was the Lifaus Highway's safety really secured?

Once, in a previous loop, Subaru had encountered the White Whale and survived—barely, but alive. At the time, the traveling merchant accompanying him, Otto, had thrown Subaru off the dragon carriage and made it through the mist to escape—or so it had seemed.

But in fact, afterward, it appeared he'd run into a Witch Cult ambush and lost the life he'd only just managed to save. And the place where that happened had been just before the highway exited into Mathers territory.

Subaru suspected there had been Witch Cultists watching the mist's exit point.

He hadn't run into any this time, but that was probably only because, by the time they'd passed through that spot this time around, their own forces had outmatched the enemy's, and they'd simply been let go.

But if they went back through with a large number of noncombatants in tow, how likely was it they'd be let off so easily again? Especially with Emilia—the very thing the cultists were after—among them.

"When you put it that way, there's not much to think about, is there."

"So you think it's better for us to go to the Sanctuary too, Subaru?"

"That's the best option right now, I think. If I can talk to Roz, I can probably say something more constructive too, and I don't want to put the villagers through more travel than they need."

At the very least, it was hard to move forward without seeing the full picture of the domain's situation. What stance Roswaal even meant to take toward the Royal Selection was still frustratingly vague.

Subaru suspected that much of the reason Emilia had been left agonizing alone over all this was Roswaal's habit of speaking in such maddeningly unspecific terms.

"So basically, to sum up, it comes down to giving that pretty-boy a piece of my mind."

"I thought we were talking about where to evacuate to... how did it end up there?"

"Working through all the little details myself is what got me there. I mean, seriously, if that guy would just talk in plainer terms, wouldn't half these problems just not exist? This is exactly why smart people are the worst..."

Because his own mind worked quickly, he expected the same processing speed from everyone else—a common trait among the genius type. Meanwhile the lapped mediocrities of the world could barely keep up.

Anyway—

"Destination's decided, and Ram knows the place. Once preparations are done, we should leave as soon as possible. Will it take much time?"

"I have nothing in particular to bring with me. Ram is..."

"There's plenty of tea leaves and valuables Ram would like to bring, but those who let greed get the better of them rarely end up well, so I'll give that up. I've already packed the necessary documents and clothing, so I'm ready whenever."

Following where Ram gestured with her hand, Subaru spotted a bag sitting beside the stairs. It seemed her packing was already complete—her foresight left him speechless.

"I always figured a girl's packing took forever, but that's great! Okay, so no forgetting your passbook or your seal, right? If we're good, let's go straight to the village."

"Passbook?"
"Seal?"

Emilia and Ram tilted their heads in unison, question marks practically floating over them, and Subaru enjoyed the moment before turning to look up toward the mansion's upper floor.

The two of them followed his gaze and seemed to realize what he was looking at. They exchanged an awkward glance before speaking.

"I'm sorry, Subaru. We don't know where Beatrice is. I haven't been able to see her even once since we got back from the capital."

"To be precise, no one has laid eyes on her since the day Roswaal-sama and the others departed for the capital. She hasn't answered calls to meals, nor even the Great Spirit's calls."

"Bea-ko ignoring even Puck? That's not normal."

Taking in what the two of them had said, Subaru rubbed his chin, wondering what to do. But recalling events from the previous world, he quickly gave up on worrying about it as pointless.

Slapping both cheeks with a dry crack to clear his head, he said,

"I'll bring Bea-ko myself, so you two go on to the village first. Crusch-san's people—Ferris and Wilhelm-san—and there's some drifter mercenary named Yuri too, so if you link up with them, things should move forward. Leave the drill-loli to me."

"Are you confident you can find her?"

"Not confident exactly, but... I've got this feeling she won't run from me. Probably 'cause she's crazy about me, if I had to guess."

"Well said, considering how difficult she can be."

At Subaru's answer, Emilia gave a small laugh, and Ram let out her usual scornful "hah." Shrugging at the two of them, Subaru spun around on the spot with a "well then" and started up the stairs.

"Watch and learn how a former shut-in gets another shut-in out of her room—that's how it's done."

"Huh, that's weird!?"

Having made such a grand exit, his confident dash to find Beatrice was, in fact, going nowhere fast.

Normally, when Subaru went to fetch Beatrice for meals, the first door he opened would usually turn out to be the one connecting to the Forbidden Archive where she resided. Sometimes it happened so fast that he'd open the dining hall's own door meaning to go call her, only to find the Forbidden Archive on the other side of it.

Beatrice's magic, which she called "Door Crossing," was too advanced for Subaru to really understand, but it seemed to be a spatial-transfer type of spell using shadow magic.

Despite being a little girl, Beatrice was an accomplished practitioner of shadow magic, and she used Door Crossing to randomly relocate her own castle—the Forbidden Archive—throughout the mansion, performing a spiteful little transfer trick where, in principle, only one door ever led to the right place.

The thing that had always managed to break through that trick of hers was something Subaru had privately dubbed "Door Breaking"—the peculiar talent, unique to people who can't read the room, of somehow drawing the correct door on the very first try.

"And of all times for that to stop working, this is not the level of 'unfortunate' I was hoping for. After talking such a big game to Emilia-tan and the others, coming back with 'sorry, couldn't find her'—that's not something I can play off with a cutesy oops, come on, seriously."

Muttering to himself, Subaru pushed open one door after another in the servants' wing. He'd already gone through every room in the central wing, and the time lost in the process was nothing to sneeze at. He'd never had this much trouble finding Beatrice before, and despite the wisecracks, he couldn't hold back the unpleasant sweat beading on his forehead.

Still, Subaru wasn't searching for Beatrice on nothing but blind hope. He had a certainty that she was definitely still somewhere in the mansion.

In the previous world, when Subaru had arrived at the mansion a full day late, Beatrice had certainly still been there. Back then, he'd been too swept up in the chaos of the moment to manage much of a conversation with her, but—

"This time, with time to spare, everyone's feelings have a bit more breathing room too. Bea-ko will probably listen, at least a little..."

It wasn't pure optimism, but if he didn't tell himself something like that, he wouldn't be able to keep up this lonely task in silence. After all, wandering an empty mansion endlessly opening and closing doors only to be disappointed each time—to anyone watching, he'd look like a complete pity case.

"Damn it, can't find her! This is bad! No time! Is this it, should I give up and cut my losses and run! My conscience aches thinking about Emilia-tan's trusting eyes, but maybe that's just something I'll have to live with! I'll tell everyone Bea-ko had a stomachache and wouldn't come out of the bathroom, so I gave up and came back—"

"—Can't you come up with a better excuse than that, you!?"

Mid-mutter, scratching his head as he griped to himself, a sudden retort cut in from beyond the door he'd just opened.

Before his eyes, the room that should have been a bathroom transformed—instead of the kind of paper meant for wiping one's backside, it became a library packed floor to ceiling with books.

The familiar Forbidden Archive, encountered again after so long, and the room's guardian—a girl clad in an ornate dress—stood right where she always did, facing him.

Just past the doorway, a wooden stepladder was set up straight ahead, and there, seated on its footrest, was the girl, a thick book open in her lap.

"Bea-ko, safely located in the bathroom—guess my instincts aren't so bad after all."

"I only feel sorry for how you refused to give up despite missing that many times. For the sake of Betty's honor, I couldn't have you spreading strange rumors about me, is what it was."

"Don't sweat it! Everybody poops, and sometimes you're in the middle of an emergency and don't feel like answering! Sorry for being so thoughtless while you were mid-strain, my bad!"

"Was that just now the single most thoughtless thing anyone has ever said in the history of the world!"

Rising from the stepladder, Beatrice fumed, clearly furious, her ringlet curls bristling as she glared at him. Subaru waved a hand lightly, saying "sorry, sorry,"

"Anyway, it's been a while. You wouldn't open up your heart to me, so I was about to end up searching the whole mansion."

"...This is what Door Crossing looks like when done properly, without letting my guard down. If you must know, I wasn't planning on letting you in here at all."

"Not much persuasive power in that, seeing as you did let me in!"

"I only let you in because you might have gone and spread some slanderous, half-baked scandal about me otherwise, is all it was!"

After shouting, Beatrice immediately looked uncomfortable, as if ashamed of her own outburst. Watching her mood swing back and forth like that, Subaru's mouth curved into a small grin as he walked closer.

"Anyway, glad to see you're okay. Sorry to spring this on you right away, but could you get ready to head out? There's kind of a problem if you stay here at the mansion..."

"Betty isn't going."

"Huh?"

Subaru's feet stopped at Beatrice's words, which shut down his proposal instantly.

Looking up at him, she met his stunned expression with a small exhale.

"I said Betty isn't going. I don't intend to leave this Forbidden Archive, much less leave the mansion. Remember that, and you may go."

"Wait a second, you just don't understand the situation. Staying here isn't an option either—it's dangerous, so come with us. I'll explain from the start!"

"I don't need it explained to understand, more or less. Also, stop treating me like a child."

Glaring sidelong at Subaru, Beatrice reached toward a shelf and pulled out a book, one that looked far too big for her, cradling it as usual as she returned to the stepladder. Sitting down, opening it on her lap—her manner was entirely ordinary, as if she had no intention whatsoever of actually evacuating.

"Hey, hold on, we're not done talking. Don't act like you get to just end this on your own."

"There's nothing more from Betty to say. You're the one who wants to keep going, and even if you do, Betty's conclusion won't change. Weren't you the one who said we can't waste time?"

"Ngh... if you understand that much, then cooperate. I'm taking you. You're being taken by me. Okay?"

"I refuse. It doesn't matter who comes. —Yes, no matter who comes, I won't let so much as a single step into this Forbidden Archive."

Eyes still fixed on her book, Beatrice declared this firmly, quietly but with force.

Scratching his head at her stubbornness, Subaru sighed.

"Listen. It's not just me being noisy, or Ram bringing your meals, who's gonna come here. I don't want to say it, but the Witch Cult is coming. Those guys don't know any restraint at all. You, staying here alone..."

"I've already shown you the power of Door Crossing. If there are those who would still try to force their way in despite that... Betty won't hold back either."

"—!"

For an instant, a dangerous aura poured out from Beatrice, brushing coldly against Subaru's back.

He caught his breath, realizing this was the aftershock of magical power bursting out from her entire body.

That overwhelming torrent of mana—even to Subaru, who'd only had a passing brush with people who dealt in magic—was clearly something extraordinary.

"—Even so, I'm still taking you with me."

"You still don't..."

"This isn't about whether you're strong or not! You're a girl, and you're small, and that's more than enough reason! I don't need any other reason why I don't want to leave you behind somewhere dangerous!"

Even pressed down by the intensity of her presence, Subaru stamped his foot against the archive floor and shouted.

Stepping forward, still pressing his point, he watched Beatrice's eyes widen in surprise, and then she closed them as if enduring some kind of pain.

Seeing that, Subaru frowned, and just as he was about to step closer to take her by force—

"Betty isn't going with you. Please, stop trying to sway me any further."

"I'm not wrong. You're wrong. —That's my answer, and that's final."

"Stubborn, is it. —That's exactly the part I dislike."

Beatrice muttered something under her breath, too quietly to catch. Subaru opened his mouth, about to ask her to repeat it, but before he could, Beatrice rose from the stepladder.

"Very well, I concede. As you say, I'll do it."

"Oh? Oh, good, then that's settled. Glad you understand. For a second there I braced myself, thinking you were gonna stand up and just blast me away with magic."

"With Betty's power, erasing you from this world and leaving nothing but your shadow behind would be easy enough... but I'm not quite cruel enough to go that far."

Saying something vaguely frightening like that, Beatrice returned the book she'd pulled out to the shelf. Following her movements as she did, Subaru suddenly noticed something and raised an eyebrow. Perhaps he'd relaxed a little, relieved that Beatrice had said she'd come along. On a whim, he said,

"Come to think of it, there's a ton of books in here—are you, by any chance, also fluent in scripts other than Iroha?"

"What a sudden thing to bring up... Iroha, meaning things like the I-script? That's the sort of name that would get you scolded by a specialist."

"Yeah yeah, sorry, my bad. So, about the actual thing..."

Grinning sheepishly at Beatrice's narrow-eyed glare, Subaru dug through his coat and pulled out a book. It was bound in black, and its contents were—

"This thing here, the writing inside doesn't look familiar at all. I figured you might know something about it, but..."

"—Why is it that you're holding that right now?"

A stiff voice suddenly cut across his words.

Looking up, he saw Beatrice's already-large eyes widen further as she stared fixedly at the Gospel in Subaru's hands.

The abrupt change in her demeanor startled Subaru, who had brought this up casually.

"Why do you have that right now? Answer me."

"Why do I... I took it, obviously. From some Witch Cult idiot. He was hugging it like it was precious, so I figured there might be some kind of hint written in it."

"You took it? From the Witch Cult? Of all people, you..."

Pressing a hand to her forehead, Beatrice's shoulders trembled faintly as the color drained from her face. Watching her already-pale complexion lose even more color, her eyes wandering, Subaru grew confused.

As if she might collapse right there, he instinctively reached a hand toward her.

"H-hey, you okay? If you're not feeling well, don't push yourself..."

"Betty... can't do this. It's not right. But to let him carry that around... don't tell me Roswaal has, this whole time...?"

"H-e-e-y? Sorry to interrupt while you're making such a serious face, but c-a-n y-o-u h-e-a-r m-e?"

"I'm thinking right now, more or less. Wait a moment."

With a single glare, Beatrice silenced Subaru as he circled worriedly around her. Falling quiet, he closed his mouth and watched Beatrice, eyes shut, her expression shifting before him.

Since she didn't seem likely to respond for a while, he idly opened the Gospel in his hands and flipped through its still-unreadable pages out of boredom.

And then he noticed something.

"Huh, the back half of this book's blank. ...Wait, was there a page like this before?"

The book's contents being written in an unreadable script, and the back half being missing as if the pages had simply dropped out—both were the same as before. But he couldn't shake the feeling that the text on the last printed page had grown since he'd last looked.

It was a book he couldn't read anyway. If he just called it his imagination, that would be the end of it.

"—What do you intend to do with that book?"

Beatrice, who had been silently deliberating, suddenly spoke up.

Hand still at her lips, however her thoughts had settled, she posed the question to Subaru.

In response, Subaru said, "What do I intend... I mean," before continuing,

"Decode what's in it, I guess—not that I care about Witch Cult doctrine or anything, but I just want to know if there's actually useful information in there. If there isn't, I don't wanna be holding onto some book its creepy owner treasured so much."

"...At the very least, Betty cannot read its contents. But if you don't want to keep holding onto it, I could at least take it off your hands."

"Take it off my hands?"

"An unknown book, treasured by an unknown owner. If you're reluctant to hold onto it, Betty will collect it for you."

Beatrice hesitantly held out her hand toward him.

From that gesture, it was clear at least that she didn't actually want the book for herself. This wasn't some scheme to resell it for a fortune.

It was an offer made in good faith. And from the way she'd been speaking a moment ago, she seemed to understand, at least, that this book was in fact a Gospel. Which was why—

"Sorry, but that offer's rejected."

Lightly pressing down on her outstretched hand, Subaru said it flatly.

At his words, Beatrice blinked once, then quickly hardened her lovely features into something sterner.

"W-why? You must sense, at least, that it's not a good thing to have. At the very least, you must know it's not the sort of thing that draws in good things. In that case, wouldn't it be better for Betty to hold it than for you to—"

"Being wanted like that makes me the contrary type who doesn't wanna give away something even if I didn't want it anyway... is what I'd say if I were being coy about it. Truth is, that's exactly why."

This book, this thing called a Gospel, apparently carried great significance for Witch Cult believers. And what's more, this particular Gospel's owner had belonged to none other than Petelgeuse, one of the Witch Cult's own executives.

How much that man had clung to this book was still fresh in Subaru's memory. Even now, with the man in custody and stripped of any means to resist, that wariness hadn't faded.

"This is the kind of book some scary old man might come drooling back to reclaim, you know? Just because it's too dangerous to hold onto, is it really something a guy hands off to a little girl?"

"——!"

"If it's dangerous, I'll hold onto it. I came here to get you somewhere safe, right? Making things more dangerous on purpose would completely defeat the purpose. Don't make me out to be that heartless, please."

Smiling lightly, Subaru tucked the Gospel away inside his coat, hiding it from Beatrice's view. Watching that whole sequence of motions, what was it she was thinking?

She blinked once, then parted her lips as if to say something—

"—...ngh."

In the end, she couldn't put anything into words, and closed her mouth, turning her face away.

Sensing something unnatural in her manner, but unable to press further against a profile that so clearly rejected further questioning, Subaru cracked his neck instead, as if to change the subject.

"Ah, well, whatever. Anyway, now that it's decided we're moving, let's get a move on. Don't wanna have too much luggage, but a couple of important books should be fine to bring. Oh, and probably good to bring a couple spare changes of that needlessly fancy dress too."

"...The archive follows on its own wherever Betty goes. More importantly, did you manage to convince the other two in the mansion?"

"Man, that's a handy little fact to learn. And yeah, those two are all squared away. You're the last one left here. Let's all head off to that Sanctuary place where Roswaal is."

"Roswaal, is it. ...What became of the little maid's younger sister who went with you?"

Unexpectedly, the topic Beatrice raised was Rem.

Surprised that Rem's name would come up from her of all people, Subaru raised an eyebrow to show his mild astonishment. Then, thinking this might be a good sign, he let his mouth relax.

"Rem's holding down the fort in the capital right now. She hooked a huge fish on the way back, actually. It was so ridiculously big she's probably prepping it in style as we speak. Once everything's settled, we should all go have some together."

"You talk about that awfully happily. —Did something happen?"

"Uh."

Overreacting a bit to Rem's name, Subaru couldn't deny he'd spoken a little too fast. Caught out by Beatrice's sharp observation, he choked, then whistled and turned his face away.

"N-nothing happened, nope!"

"For someone to be treated that affectionately and not develop feelings for it would be strange, wouldn't it. Betty won't go spreading it around, so do as you like."

"I-I'm not trying to hide it, okay? It's just, telling Emilia-tan or my actual older sister figure all of a sudden takes some courage, y'know... call it a strategic retreat."

Mumbling weakly, fingers fidgeting together, Subaru turned his back to Beatrice, hunching in embarrassment.

Part of it was certainly the situation itself, but it was also true he lacked the nerve to suddenly announce it. Even Emilia would probably be blindsided if he suddenly declared, "I'm going to have both you and Rem!"—even though that was, in fact, already decided.

"Setting your sights high is a good thing. Gives you something to strive for. I'm not against the whole hard-work thing on principle. I just never had a goal to work toward before—!"

The mouth that had been piling up excuses to talk up his own enthusiasm suddenly stopped short at an unexpected sensation.

Behind him, against his back, he felt warmth—arms wrapped around his waist from behind, embracing him. Small, slender arms circled around toward his stomach, and he knew instinctively who it was.

Beatrice.

"What, Bea-ko? You scared me. Don't do that out of nowhere, seriously."

"That reaction is rather genuinely infuriating. —But it's enough."

"Huh?"

The moment he tilted his head at the unexpected words, a sudden burst of brilliant light seared his eyelids.

Before he could even register that it was the entrance door swinging open on its own—

"Goodbye—"

"Wha, whoa!?"

The arms that had been wrapped around him let go, and a strong pressure shoved hard against his back. Unable to resist, his body pitched forward, stumbling ahead.

And just like that, as if being drawn in, he was sent flying straight through the door—

"Beatrice—!"

"Betty... can't go with you."

Twisting around, he threw one last glance back into the room just before passing through the doorway.

Barely captured in his field of vision, the girl stood with large tears welling in her eyes—

"——!"

Before he could put anything into words, his vision warped. His body was swept up in the distortion of space, his legs carrying him along some path that shouldn't have existed, the connection to the Forbidden Archive severing behind him.

And just like that, Subaru's body was ejected from the space of the Forbidden Archive, vanishing off to somewhere far away.

"—...aa-sama."

Watching him go, Beatrice slowly closed the door herself, which had been left standing open.

There came the sound of air being cut, and a deep, silent stretch of time settled once more over the Forbidden Archive.

"—Mother."

Softly, in a voice on the verge of tears, Beatrice called out that name.

The large tears had already vanished from her eyes, and yet her expression remained unchanged.

"Betty is... just how much longer, I wonder..."

On the verge of collapsing into tears, Beatrice walked over to the stepladder and let her weight fall against it as if she might topple over. Then, reaching out, she drew out the book resting on the opposite footrest of the stepladder—the one across from where she always sat—and clutched it to her chest.

"Mother... Mother... Mother...!"

Clinging to it like a lost child seeking comfort, Beatrice held the book pressed against her chest, her voice continuing to echo through the quiet Forbidden Archive like the sound of quiet sobbing.

The black-bound book cradled in her arms answered her with nothing at all.
