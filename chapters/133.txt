Arc 3 Chapter 56: "The Opening Shot of the Decisive Battle"

With Crusch Karsten at its head, the expedition for this Subjugation of the White Whale gets underway.

Command of the subjugation force she organized rests with Crusch, who leads it, while the post of field commander falls to a scion of the Sword Saint's bloodline — the "Sword Devil," Wilhelm van Astrea.
The subjugation force under Wilhelm is split into some ten squads, each led by one of the veteran soldiers who'd attended the address in the garden.
Each squad numbers around fifteen men, putting the total strength of Crusch's subjugation force at roughly a hundred and fifty.

That, however, wasn't the sum of their total forces. There were also the thirty members of the beastman mercenary band lent by Anastasia and led by Ricardo — apparently known by the moniker "Iron Fang." Ricardo oversaw the whole group, with two vice-captains serving beneath him.

And those vice-captains were—

"Mimi, at your service."
"Hetaro, at your service."

—two small heads bowing politely. Two creatures — no, two people, cat-type beastfolk both.
Their short fur was orange, and their height — barely reaching Subaru's waist — closely matched his mental image of a diminutive, dwarf-type beastfolk.
Add to that their extremely adorable faces, and the pure white robes that covered everything but their heads suited them perfectly. In short—

"They're cute enough to make you want to just scoop them up and run off."

"I get that a lot."
"Sis, don't go saying things like that again..."

At Subaru's remark, the girl who'd introduced herself as Mimi nodded, while the boy who called himself Hetaro chided her, looking faintly flustered. Judging by their identical looks, they seemed to be twins.
Privately wondering whether beastfolk births worked like animals', with several born at once, Subaru gave the two of them a nod and said,

"You said you're the vice-captains... So, are you two any good in a fight?"

"Of course. Put the two of us together and we're the strongest there is."

"Eh...ah, yes. That's right. Sis and I will do our best together."

Perhaps their dynamic was the assertive older sister with the passive, cautious younger brother reining her in. It also raised the question of whether, in this world, twins always came with an older sister who left the follow-up work to the younger sibling.

In any case—

"The boss is the leader and all, but once a fight starts he just charges on ahead and stops looking at anything around him."

"So we're the ones who give everyone their orders in his place."

"Ah, I see... Sounds rough."

Subaru could easily picture a huge figure laughing boisterously and charging headlong, spoiling for a fight, straight into the middle of the battlefield.
It seemed the two of them agreed with Subaru's mental image, as Hetaro let his small shoulders slump with a dejected, "That's exactly it,"

"We'll follow Lady Crusch's command, but we're going to mix in our own way of doing things when we fight the White Whale. On that point, we wanted to make sure it doesn't cause any confusion for you, Subaru-san... Subaru-san?"

"...No, I'm just surprised at how seriously thoughtful you are. Small as you are, you've really got a lot figured out. Impressive."

"Heh-heh, I'm amazing, aren't I~"

"Sis, you get carried away so easily... ...cute."

When Subaru praised Hetaro, who seemed to have come to introduce himself for perfectly sound reasons, Mimi puffed out her chest with inexplicable pride. And Hetaro, even as he scolded Mimi for it, let his true feelings slip out in a mutter at the end. So it seemed her brother bore some responsibility for her getting carried away, too.

—At present, the White Whale subjugation force, numbering roughly a hundred and eighty in all, was marching toward the base of Flugel's Tree, where the decisive battle with the White Whale would take place.

With the preliminary address at Crusch's mansion concluded, Subaru and the others had swiftly linked up with the subjugation force outside the royal capital, and were now advancing, unhurried, along a journey of roughly half a day.
According to the phone, the time remaining until the White Whale's scheduled appearance was just about half a day. Roughly five hours had already passed since leaving the capital, and given the remaining distance, they were now at about the halfway point—there ought to be time enough to catch their breath before the decisive battle with the White Whale.

"Once we reach the tree, we'll have to go over the final details of the plan again, I bet. My part in it is gonna matter a lot too, I think."

"This time, Rem thinks it's better to stay close by Subaru-kun's side rather than go out to the front lines. —Rem absolutely doesn't want to feel the same regret she did with the Juggernaut."

The one burning with quiet resolve at Subaru's murmur was Rem, gripping the reins beside him on the driver's seat. Guiding the Earth Dragon with a flick of the reins, she cast a soft sidelong glance at Subaru.

"Honestly, Rem is against it. She thinks using the Witch's Lingering Scent to draw the White Whale in is far too dangerous... and more than that, the thought of that smell coming from Subaru-kun—"

"I'll use whatever I can use. If it bumps our odds up even a fraction of a percent, that's a win. Someone as lacking as me needs to do that much just to catch up."

"Subaru-kun is wonderful."

Faced with Subaru's resolve, Rem stubbornly refused to yield on that one point, turning her face away. It was a small, sulky gesture, but seeing her emotions overflow like that, Subaru couldn't help the soft, wry smile that rose to his face.

Rem, who had always kept an impassive, composed front, striving to suppress her emotions.
The Witchbeast incident had opened her heart, and she'd begun showing a certain softness of feeling since, yet even then some stiffness had remained. Now, that stiffness was barely felt at all.
Ever since Subaru had confessed his inner weakness, and she in turn had confessed her feelings for him, the richness of her emotional expression was beyond comparison to what it had been before.

She'd once said that he'd set her stopped time moving again—and that was exactly right.
Which was exactly why—

"I want to win."

He voiced that wish, quietly.

He was confident that, as things stood, the situation was far better than in any of the loops before.
He'd gotten a demand through that, no matter how hard he'd pleaded before, no one would ever hear—and his relations with the Crusch Camp, which should have coldly turned him away or abandoned him, were good. His bond with Rem, too—embarrassing as it had been to lay his inner self bare—felt palpably stronger now.

But at the same time, it was also true that he'd stepped onto a more dangerous path than ever before.

The threat of the White Whale was still seared vividly into Subaru's mind, having witnessed it up close.
Even a fighter as strong as Rem hadn't stood a chance against it—a colossal body that could smash a large Dragon Carriage to splinters with a mere flick of its tail, an act that barely counted as an attack. Its open jaws had swallowed an Earth Dragon whole, ground along with the earth beneath it, and the dying scream as its millstone-hard teeth ground the flesh to pulp still wouldn't leave his ears.

Just thinking of facing that thing again, he couldn't stop the trembling that ran through his limbs.
But every time Subaru's heart tilted that way, toward weakness—

"—"

Rem's eyes, right beside him, would peer into him as though seeing straight through to his heart.
That alone was enough to make Subaru's heart blaze up, as if it had forgotten its fear. Right in front of her, with Rem watching, he could not allow himself to remain the weak, hopeless, no-good Natsuki Subaru he'd always been.

If he was going to steady his resolve, hopeful optimism suited him better than pessimism about the situation.
It was true that, as things stood, the path forward was forced down a dangerous route. It was a situation born of necessity, and there hadn't been nearly enough time to prepare everything to perfection.
All Subaru had been able to do, in the limited time available, was call on the best people for the job and leave everything else entirely up to them—the same reliance on others as always.

And yet, none of them had asked more of Subaru than that, nor had they ever treated him carelessly, as some useless, unneeded burden.

He would simply do what he could, as hard as he could, moment by moment.
Since the range of what he could do was hopelessly narrow, the least he could do was grasp the width of that frame properly, and think hard about what was possible within its narrow bounds.

"What's this, kid? —You've got the face of a man who's made up his mind."

Ricardo, suddenly pulling up alongside the dragon carriage, said this with a grin, looking at Subaru.
Subaru glared at the dog-type beastman with the great cleaver slung across his back, and twisted the corner of his mouth into a bold grin.

"You bet. A little late, maybe, but it clicked into place just right. And me with my mind made up? I'm something else. After all, I won't give up on the future even if it kills me."

"Well, ain't that a greedy thing to say! Sounds like something that'd have my lady grinning ear to ear! Yeah, kid, you'd make a perfect friend for my lady, no doubt about it!"

"If our positions weren't what they are, I wouldn't mind shaking on that... ...ah, but getting friendly with Anastasia comes with some troublesome baggage attached."

He suddenly recalled the image of that pretty-boy who was probably standing at Anastasia's side.
Getting utterly beaten down by him and left bloodied in the middle of the training ground felt like ages ago now. Though, by the reckoning of a world that couldn't share his loops, it had only been about five days.

At Subaru's words, Ricardo put a hand to his big mouth, chuckling under his breath in a way rare for him. Judging by the mischievous glint in the eyes he cut sideways at Subaru, he'd apparently heard about the disgraceful mess Subaru had made of things.
A sulky sort of sentiment naturally welled up in his chest, and Subaru turned his face away.

"If you're gonna laugh, go ahead and bust a gut over it. Even I can admit now just how clueless I was back then."

"Nah, nah, what's got me laughing is a whole different matter. Well, you'll figure it out on your own soon enough, and spilling it here would be poor form."

Satisfied with himself, Ricardo flicked the thin whiskers by his nose with a finger, closing the subject.
Subaru was about to protest the coy attitude, but gave up, figuring pressing further wouldn't get him a decent answer, and decided to bring up something else instead.

"Oh yeah, this has been bugging me since we left the capital."

"What is it, ask away. We're pals now, ain't we, you and me. We ain't hardly acquainted, sure, but unless it's something real special, I'll tell you! And if it is something real special, well, that's where the money comes in! Money!"

"You're real particular about that stuff, guess that makes you a proper Kararagi man after all... Anyway, that huge dog you all are riding—it's incredible."

Subaru pointed at the creature beneath the excitedly chattering Ricardo, hesitating over how best to describe it.

What Ricardo's beastman mercenary band rode was an entirely different creature from the Earth Dragons that made up Crusch's subjugation force.
A large dog was probably the closest description. Its build rivaled that of the great Carnivorous Beasts—lions, tigers, and the like, back in his old world—and aside from its low-slung posture, it carried a presence that lost nothing in comparison to an Earth Dragon.

At Subaru's question, Ricardo gave an "ahh," an understanding nod, and gave the dog's back a light pat with his big palm.

"Guess you don't see 'em much round these parts. This here's a creature called a Liger, and it's prized in Kararagi about as much as Earth Dragons are round here. Somethin' to do with territory disputes and the like makes breedin' 'em tough in Lugunica and other countries, so there ain't many about."

"Liger..."

At a glance, going by his own experience, it looked like some subspecies of Ulgarm or another. But there were no horns to be seen on its head, and its face, compared to the Witchbeasts whose ferocity had been so front and center, clearly had a good deal of charm to it.
If the Witchbeast leaned more toward wolf, then the Liger, certainly, leaned toward dog.
Still, the sight of Ricardo, a dog-type beastman, riding atop this super-sized dog, looked to an outside observer like—

"There's something weirdly disorienting about that picture, I gotta say. You don't feel anything off about it yourself?"

"I get told that now and then, but nah, not really. From our own point of view, beastfolk and animals are clearly different things, just lookin' similar. And don't worry, it don't bother me none if people think that."

"No, even as I was saying it I figured it was in poor taste, so let me properly apologize. Sorry."

"Gahaha, ain't you the conscientious one!"

Ricardo laughed, baring his teeth, then gently stroked the Liger's nape. The Liger showed no reaction, but the way it swayed its master on its back did seem to carry, just as its looks suggested, something like canine loyalty. The bond between them, it seemed, held true even across the gap between worlds.

"Ligers lose out to Earth Dragons on raw power, but they make up for it with agility on a whole different level. Once the fight with the White Whale turns into a melee, that's our stage alone, so watch close."

"Horsepower, huh. Even with dragons and dogs being the popular choice, I guess pulling power still gets measured that way... Come to think of it, I haven't seen any horses around."

He'd heard, from Emilia among others, that horses did exist. But given that he'd never once laid eyes on one so far, they seemed to be quite rare.
Then Subaru pointed back toward the rear, at the tail end of the marching subjugation force.

"So they've got a whole pack pulling one wagon like a dogsled? Seems like, worrying about the dogs tiring out before the real thing, you could've just left it to the Earth Dragons instead, no?"

"Gotta manage our own baggage ourselves. And I appreciate the worry, but the Ligers pulling the cart are trained specifically for that job. Can't go spoilin' 'em, and besides, the White Whale ain't necessarily the only enemy out there."

Subaru gave an inward start, catching himself before the flicker of unease could show on his face. Ricardo, meanwhile, seemed not to notice Subaru's alarm at all.

"If bandits or the like come at us on the road, there ain't gonna be time to fumble around handin' off cargo, right? That's why we look after our own affairs as much as we can."

"...Anybody with the guts to attack a fully armed band like this wouldn't be a bandit in the first place. If they tried it anyway, that wouldn't be courage—just a roundabout way of committing suicide."

"Ha, ain't that the truth!!"

Laughing heartily, Ricardo gave the Liger's back a smart smack, and the Liger answered with a low bark, surging forward.
He rode straight on toward the head of the column, apparently heading now for Crusch, who led the march.

Watching his back recede, Subaru let out a breath and set his mind working.
The looming battle with the White Whale—even once he cleared that towering hurdle, there would still be plenty more walls waiting for him to climb.

"Being a hero sure is a rough gig, I tell you."

Even as he let the weary complaint slip out, Subaru's expression had settled into the shape of a smile.

What he had to do and what he wanted to do lined up perfectly, and there were people pushing him on, telling him to go do it. —Could there be a more rewarding position to be in?

Ahead of the coming battle, his fighting spirit stood in perfect readiness—once more, he would take up the challenge against fate.

Fortunately, the march proceeded without trouble, right on schedule, and the subjugation force arrived at Flugel's Tree just as the moon was beginning to rise—the start of a night with roughly six hours left until the White Whale's appearance.

Last time, circumstances being what they were, he hadn't had the chance to take it in properly, but the sheer girth and height of the tree—surpassing, surely, even the thousand-year-old giants of his original world—struck Subaru all the more powerfully with its grandeur the longer he stood at its base, looking up.

"That's... huuuge."

"You seem happy, Subaru-kun."

Rem, beside him, gave a small laugh as she pointed this out, watching Subaru plant a foot on one of the tree's massive roots—which surged up and coiled wildly across the ground—and declare this in an excited voice. At her words, Subaru said, "Well, I mean," and hopped down from the root.

"Guys are just naturally the kind of creature that gets moved by big, powerful-looking things. I was impressed the first time I saw an Earth Dragon too, but Mother Nature's no joke either. Flugel did good work."

He offered honest praise for the great sage's feat of planting a tree. He seemed to be a great figure whose only known accomplishment, as far as anyone could tell, was planting a tree—but if he'd managed even one achievement worthy of a place in history, that was good enough. Subaru could only hope it hadn't been born purely of some vain need for self-display—

"Oh, hey, someone's carved their name into the trunk. Who does that, seriously, it's not like we're on a school trip. No manners at all, none. Rem, lend me a carving knife."

"Rem doesn't have one on her, and even for you, Subaru-kun, that's not allowed."

Rem scolded Subaru as he patted the trunk with his palm, apparently intent on carving in some unnecessary declaration of his own. She gave a small laugh at Subaru's pouting display of discontent, then looked up at the great tree.

"So this is where the White Whale will appear."

"Yeah, it'll show. When the time comes, my phone will... this Magic Vessel, I mean, it'll go off."

He answered Rem, pulling the phone from his pocket and letting it dangle from a finger hooked through its strap. The timer was set, and the alarm was due to sound the moment it hit the White Whale's appearance time—fifteen thirteen.
On this particular point there was no lie or deception, and he'd already gotten Crusch and the others to accept it in the meeting after their arrival. The facts he couldn't speak of did stir up a certain guilt, but for now he'd resolved himself to letting the results speak for themselves and win their understanding.
But keeping the truth even from Rem required bearing a pain sharper still than that—

"So this Magic Vessel tells you when the Witchbeast is coming..."

"Yeah, that's the gist of it. Honestly, without it, my worth this time around would basically be..."

"—That's a lie, isn't it?"

Asked so abruptly by Rem, her eyes narrowed, Subaru's heart genuinely stopped.
A wordless "hah" of breath escaped him, and with it his heart resumed beating. What had Rem just asked him? He raised his face, looked back at hers, and confirmed all over again that the intent he saw there wasn't some mishearing on his part. His lips trembled, and—

"Wh-what're you on about? If this here's a lie, then how'd I ever—"

"You've slipped into Kararagi dialect, and it doesn't suit you, Subaru-kun."

"No, seriously, what kind of suspicion is this, calling it a lie. Crusch and the others were satisfied with—"

Judging that evasion wasn't going to work, Subaru nonetheless tried to push the lie through to the end.
If he were to be pressed on this, it would without question invite disaster. Once before, during a loop at the mansion, the Witch's palm had activated even in a situation where information about Return by Death was forced to slip from Subaru's mouth.
It wasn't because Subaru had tried to speak. Even in a situation he couldn't get through without speaking, that palm tormented his heart. —So then, now that it had evolved into something malicious enough to crush Emilia's heart, what then?

—No matter what, Rem must never learn of that.

"Lady Crusch and the others have simply judged that Subaru-kun has no reason to lie. Or rather than reason, Rem thinks it's more that they've weighed the drawbacks of lying. If you did such a thing, you'd make an enemy not only of Lady Crusch, but of Lady Anastasia and the royal capital's merchant guild as well."

"Th-that's..."

It was an undeniable fact.
Crusch could have refuted Subaru's clumsy line of reasoning any number of times over, if she'd wanted. The same went for Russell and Anastasia, both practiced hands at negotiation.
And yet the reason they turned a blind eye to a number of suspicious points owed less to trust in Subaru than to the weight of the surrounding circumstances.

Subaru himself had set that scene up, and there was no reason for him, of all people, to deceive everyone present and go through with something like this.
Of course, Subaru had understood all this going into the alliance negotiations, and there was no denying that he'd maintained an appearance of equal footing by stacking the presence of others atop his own small, insignificant self.
Which was precisely why one could say that Crusch and the others had no real need to seriously dig into the truth or falsehood of the "lie" Subaru had told. So long as the results followed through, any minor inconsistency in what Subaru had said was, to them, the kind of thing that could simply be forgotten.

But Rem, on the other hand, was different.
Rem's position remained unchanged: she was Subaru's ally. And Subaru knew full well that, in this other world, she was now the one who treated him with the most heartfelt care of anyone.
Continuing to lie to her ate away at Subaru's heart along an entirely different axis than lying to Crusch and the others did. He even felt the urge to lay that feeling bare, to cling to the purity of the affection she offered him.
But that was something he absolutely could not do.

"Rem, I..."

"It's all right, Subaru-kun."

"Eh?"

Subaru had schemed to protect her somehow, with some patched-together words.
But it was rejected by Rem, who raised a smile to her lips and shook her head.
To Subaru, too stunned to close his mouth, Rem turned an earnest gaze,

"Rem can tell when Subaru-kun is lying, at least that much. She's been watching Subaru-kun this whole time, after all."

Rem gave a bashful, embarrassed little laugh, and, in a joking gesture, pressed a finger to her lips. Then she pointed that same finger toward Subaru.

"Rem also understands that you can't tell her the reason for that lie. But that doesn't mean you need to worry about Rem's feelings just because you won't tell her, understand?"

"—"

"Because Rem believes in Subaru-kun, all of him, entirely."

At the base of Flugel's Tree, the wind stroked past between the two of them, standing face to face.
Placing a hand over her chest, Rem made her declaration before the silent Subaru.

"If Subaru-kun says he knows where the White Whale will appear, Rem believes it. If he says the Witch Cult is targeting Lady Emilia and the others, Rem believes that too. Even if Subaru-kun said the moon was going to fall and the kingdom would be destroyed, Rem could believe that as well."

"...I wouldn't go that far."

"Yes, that's true. But that's how serious Rem is."

Her smile fading, Rem then turned a suddenly serious look on Subaru. Then she quietly lowered herself, pinching the hem of her skirt in both hands and bowing,

"This body, this heart—all of it belongs, utterly devoted, to Subaru-kun. —And so Rem, now and forever after, will never once doubt Subaru-kun."

"—"

"So there's no need at all—none whatsoever—for you to drive yourself into a corner trying to make Rem believe you, or to smooth things over with lies."

Subaru caught himself just in time, holding back the hot swell rising in his throat, choking him.
Pressing at the corners of his eyes, he tilted his face upward and opened his trembling mouth wide.

"Ahh, yeah, looking up at a huge tree really does get you pumped up!"

"Yes, it does."

"I think I need to keep looking up at the top of this tree for a while to settle down. No other reason at all, but I just can't look down for a bit."

"Yes, that's right."

To keep his tears from spilling, Subaru kept up the bluff, his face turned upward.
But Rem, gently, wrapped that weak show of bravado in tender affection, and did not expose it.

Right now, once again, Subaru truly understood just how foolish he'd been.

He should have simply told Rem everything, right from the start.
He couldn't tell her absolutely everything, but if he'd at least been able to convey to her the tragedies bound to occur, he surely could have avoided the second and third disasters.

Because he couldn't explain his reasons, because he assumed no one would believe him even if he told them, Subaru had decided he had no choice but to do everything alone, and had repeated every kind of failure because of it.

But Rem was different.
She didn't demand an explanation of reasons. Without a word, she believed in him. Even now, it seemed, she loved and cherished Subaru even as he remained unable to speak the truth.

"'Thank you' beats 'I'm sorry,' at a time like this, huh."

Desperately holding the dam of his tears intact, Subaru somehow lowered his face and turned to face Rem. At his answer, Rem nodded with a beaming smile.

"You're welcome. And besides, Rem is the one who's grateful to Subaru-kun, so much, so much, so much more—so we're even."

"Feels to me like whatever I've done for you, Rem's paying it back way over and above, honestly."

"That's not true at all."

Rem ducked her head slightly, shaking it as she denied Subaru's words.
Subaru raised an eyebrow at her attitude, meaning to pile on more words of denial to change her mind, but—

"If anything, this is just Rem being selfish, even knowing that a conversation like this only causes Subaru-kun pain."

"I don't think of it that way at all. I'm the one in the wrong here, keeping secrets."

"But it's selfish all the same. And even so—I'm sorry."

Her tone held a trace of self-mockery, but the face Rem lifted was bright and clear. Seeming pleased to see Subaru falter at that contradiction, Rem tilted her head slightly and said,

"To be someone Subaru-kun might think to entrust with even just a small part of the burden he carries—not being able to be that kind of presence is, to Rem right now, a sadness she can barely bear."

"I..."

The depth of Rem's resolve, the full measure of her feelings, came through to him even now, just like this.
Leaning his back against the trunk of the great tree, Subaru took a single deep breath.

The courage to put into words, just as it was, the warm feeling welling up in his chest.

"I— I love Emilia."

"Yes."

It was, once again, a rehash of words he'd already exchanged with her.
Subaru knew that these were words that would wound and torment her terribly, and yet he said them again anyway.
And yet—

"But—"

"—"

"But when I look at you, my heart shakes. ...You're welcome to think I'm an awful guy for it."

He thought to himself just how self-servingly convenient these words were.
But it was, without any deceit, exactly how Subaru felt. He knew he couldn't return Rem's feelings, and yet hers were the only words that warmed his heart so thoroughly.

Rem let out a breath, somehow charged with heat,

"Subaru-kun really is an awful person."

"...I know."

"That's a lie. I love you."

"I— I know, I said."

Having her feelings put into such plain words once more, Subaru's face flushed red all at once.
Had it not been a night with the sun long down, that redness would surely have stood out plainly. Subaru started walking, as if to hide the extent of his blush.

"Let's head back, yeah? Gotta get mind and body squared away tight before the White Whale shows."

Before passing by her side, he took hold of her right hand, which had been left dangling.
At the touch of her hand, Rem let slip a small "ah," but quickly matched her pace to Subaru's brisk stride, and gazed with mischievous eyes at the profile of the boy who wouldn't look at her.

"Subaru-kun."

"...What."

"Rem would be fine being the second wife, you know."

It was the kind of remark that nearly made his feet stop dead.
Unable to help himself, he glanced over, to find Rem wearing an expression like a mischievous cat, waiting for his answer with her tail wagging furiously.
Ah, honestly, this girl really knew no bounds—

"If Emilia-tan turns out to be the type who's open to polygamy, sure."

"Well then, once we're back, Rem will just have to work on persuading Lady Emilia. Rem will do her best."

With the hand not being held, Rem made a small fist, put a bit of spirit into it, and smiled.
With his tension melted away by her joking words, Subaru clearly recognized his own weakness—that he really was no match for her.

Emilia was one thing, and Rem another, but in moments like this, a man simply could not win against a woman, no matter what.
That particular weakness, unlike all his others, wasn't one he minded admitting to, though.

—As the appointed hour drew near, a taut, pre-battle tension began to fill the base of the great tree.

Taking turns eating and catching brief naps, the subjugation force gathered there was in perfect condition.
The Earth Dragons and Ligers that had accompanied the long march had also gotten sufficient rest, and now seemed to be waiting, poised, for the command of the riders on their backs.

Holding their breath, steadying their hearts, everyone waited for that moment.
Though there was no need to hold their breath at all, everyone did, as if even the sound of a sword sliding from its sheath might affect something, and so all kept silent, waiting for the time to pass.

In the sky over the Lifaus Highway, the wind was strong tonight, and the clouds moved swiftly across it.
Every time the moonlight was blocked by a passing cloud, there was no shortage of people lifting their eyes, wondering if some massive beast had just shut out the light. That was how much wariness had taken hold.

"Only a few minutes left until the appointed hour."

Murmuring this quietly, Crusch glanced sidelong at Ferris, standing beside her, giving a small nod.
Even Ferris, who had served at Crusch's side for years and ought to have been used to situations like this, showed not a trace of his usual inclination toward joking around.
It wasn't that he'd been swallowed up by the taut tension. He understood that he was, in a sense, this subjugation force's lifeline, and was devoting himself to that role.

Depending on how well Ferris performed, the final count of survivors in this battle would change.
Crusch had no doubt about her camp's victory, but neither was she so conceited as to think they could fell the White Whale without a single sacrifice. Still, she held enough confidence to believe she could reliably keep the number of necessary sacrifices as low as possible.
Though, given that this confidence rested on her trust in her own retainer, Ferris, whether it deserved to be called confidence at all was a bit of an open question.

Wilhelm had already drawn both swords from his waist, holding one in each hand, fully prepared to charge out at any moment.
The quiet killing intent he wore was honed to a razor's edge, refined even in this moment as he stood on the verge of his long-cherished, heartfelt hour.
At the sheer, unadulterated purity of the Sword Devil's being, Crusch couldn't help but feel an admiration bordering on infatuation.

A person, through nothing but sheer, single-minded purity, could refine their soul to such heights.
Someday, she too hoped from the bottom of her heart to reach that same realm.

Standing alongside Wilhelm, the members of the subjugation force, tension etched on each of their faces, nonetheless kept their morale high.
Following Crusch's orders, they had already, silently, finished preparing to launch the first strike. Even they, surely, had their doubts about tonight's sortie. In hammering out the crucial fine details, there simply hadn't been enough time between them and Subaru to build the kind of relationship needed to establish real confidence in his information.
Still, Crusch understood well that the reason none of them voiced any objection was out of respect for her judgment, and that she bore the responsibility—the duty—to answer for it.

She could feel a pleasant battle-fever slowly, steadily searing through her.
As the hour drew close, and the scent of death, fire, and blood pressed nearer, Crusch began to truly feel the sensation of being alive.

As a ruler, she meant to discipline herself, to strive to be someone worthy of respect.
And yet, she also would not deny the unchanging nature rooted deep within her heart.
And that was—

"—!"

Abruptly, it rang out across the Lifaus Highway, sunk deep in the dark of night.

A chain of light, ringing notes, layering over one another, naturally formed itself into music that trembled against their eardrums.
Turning her eyes toward the source of the sound, Crusch could see Subaru holding a glowing Magic Vessel in his hand. And that the music was flowing out from that very Magic Vessel.
Which meant—

"All hands, on alert—"

By Subaru's declaration, the White Whale would appear within one minute of the Magic Vessel sounding.
If his word was to be trusted, it would be no surprise for that colossal body to begin swimming through the sky at this very moment. As for the location, since the Magic Vessel had sounded, here must be correct.
There was room enough for doubt, but no reason for Subaru to have given rise to that doubt. Naturally, Crusch sharpened her senses and braced herself for the creature's appearance.

But—

"—"

In the stillness, not the faintest sign of that mighty Witchbeast's arrival could be felt.

Calling it "anticlimactic" wouldn't be quite right, but the fact that nothing happened even after a full minute had passed left Crusch unable to suppress the beginnings of unease—rare for her.
Surely she hadn't been deceived, she didn't want to think—but.

The stillness settled over the Lifaus Highway remained unchanged, and the surrounding scenery showed no alteration.
Even now, the moonlight was blocked by clouds drifting across the sky, a great dark shadow filling her field of view—but—

"—!"

Looking up, Crusch instantly cursed her own shallow thinking.

The moonlight was blocked, and a shadow was falling.
The cloud-like haze that had cut off that light was slowly, slowly descending, closing in right before her eyes.

It was a Witchbeast, floating in the sky, casting a fish-like silhouette far too vast to be believed.

The instant Crusch caught her breath, nearly every member of the subjugation force grasped the same truth. And once their collective will had aligned, all eyes turned toward Crusch.

—They were waiting for the order: the first strike.

They had succeeded in seizing the initiative, in getting the jump on the White Whale's arrival.
All that remained was to land the surprise attack as planned, and seize control of the battlefield.

"—"

Drawing a breath, Crusch resolved to give the first command.
The White Whale had not yet noticed their own small, insignificant presence. It turned its head slowly, as though trying to confirm just where it was, its movements uncertain and, above all, riddled with openings.

"—Everyone—"

Just as she was about to say the words "all-out attack"—

"—Let 'em have it!!"

"—El Huma!!"

The command rang out, overtaking Crusch's own, and in the same instant, a spell's incantation deployed mana into the world.

Ice pillars, forged to a terrifying density and boasting superlative might, manifested into the world four at once, one after another—launched, they slammed into the White Whale's body, and a beat later, the White Whale's shriek and a spray of blood came pouring down over the earth.

Looking over in alarm, she saw Subaru and Rem, riding double on an Earth Dragon, dashing forward. Subaru, clinging to Rem's waist, pumped a fist in triumph, while Rem, having landed the first strike with her incantation, wore an expression of pure satisfaction, as if to say she'd fulfilled her role perfectly.

The subjugation force wavered at the sight of the two of them jumping the gun—or rather, taking the lead.
Seeing this, Crusch couldn't hold back her mouth twisting into a wide grin.

Not from anger. From laughter.

"Everyone—follow those two fools!!"

Crusch's command rang out, dispelling the wavering, and the subjugation force answered reflexively, launching their attack.

A tremendous cloud of dust billowed up, and beyond it, the White Whale's shriek rose high once more, echoing out across the night sky over the Lifaus Highway.

—The battle to subjugate the White Whale, at long last, had opened fire.
