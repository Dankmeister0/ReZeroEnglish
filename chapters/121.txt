Arc 3 Chapter 44: "Departure from the Royal Capital and a Reunion"

Negotiations with Priscilla Barielle had also ended in collapse, and now, truly, every road had run out.

Curtly refused by both Crusch and Priscilla, with Felt—who kept Reinhard close—nowhere to be found. Bringing his case to Julius's camp, who could only ever be an enemy to him, was out of the question from the start, and Subaru, held in poor regard by the knights, had already thrown away even his qualification to go crawling to the authorities.
In other words—

"So securing fighting strength, the immediate goal, is something I just can't do..."

Crushed under despair and helplessness, Subaru muttered this while touching his own nose.
The nose Priscilla had kicked in had somehow reattached itself, and with the traces of the nosebleed gone, it was more or less back to normal. Still, Rem, who had treated it, had warned Subaru—who'd tried to get moving again immediately—to "please stay still," so for now he was sitting tight.

The location had shifted from the noble quarter to the commercial district in the royal capital's lower tier.
A crowd no less dense than that of Merchant Street came and went even today, and Subaru, watching the passing waves of people, sat inside a snack shop.
Chewing on a meal of bread-like food with something like pork and lettuce-ish vegetables sandwiched inside, Subaru silently filled his empty stomach alone.

The morning hours had been thoroughly squandered at Priscilla's mansion, and now it was probably right around noon. With roughly seven or eight hours left until the deadline he'd set himself for departing the royal capital, his margin of time was dwindling further still.
And yet the situation wasn't even holding steady—it was only retreating, which made no sense at all.
Granted, considering that the reasonable solutions had all been crushed, one could argue it had at least produced the result of narrowing down his options.

"I'm not happy enough to mistake that for progress. Damn it, that arrogant woman... forgetting the debt she owes me for saving her..."

Recalling how, at their first meeting, he'd told her of his feat in rescuing her from a gang of ruffians, Subaru spat out his bitterness with the corner of his mouth twisted.
It was true she'd shown not a shred of gratitude even then, but he hadn't expected her to respond that way even to someone coming to her now, clutching at straws, begging for help.
On top of it all, she'd kicked him aside, hurled the same incomprehensible logic Crusch had used to berate him, and tossed him out without ceremony. And Al—Al was no better. That heartlessness, saying nothing in the face of his mistress's outrageous behavior. What good was a fellow countryman if he was useless like that?

"To hell with every last one of them. They don't know anything, they don't understand anything... they can't protect anyone, and all they do is get in my way—!"

Grinding his teeth, he tore at the bread and meat in irritation.
His mouth, chewing roughly, was so full of anger and humiliation he couldn't taste any of it.

He needed to think of a second plan, a next method—but what filled Subaru's mind was nothing but resentment and curses at the people he'd dealt with so far.
Fools, all of them, throwing up empty rhetoric to block Subaru's path as he tried to protect everyone, tried to save them—practically complicit in Petelgeuse's vile deeds.

"Might as well just let everyone who gets in my way di—"

"Sorry to keep you waiting, Subaru-kun."

The blue-haired girl who cut off Subaru mid-way through his dangerous declaration, the hem of her apron dress swaying, was Rem.
She sat down in the empty chair across from Subaru, and—

"You've got sauce on your face, Subaru-kun."

—reached toward him, gently wiping the corner of his mouth with the cloth in her hand.
Letting it happen, Subaru pressed her for a report with a silent look. There was of course a reason she'd parted from him to act separately.
Namely—

"I reported to the Knights' Garrison that Witch Cult activity is taking place. Since I gave Roswaal-sama's name, they didn't turn me away at the door, but..."

Her voice grew weaker toward the end, and Subaru bit his lip.
Rather than Subaru himself going, who wouldn't be well received by the knights, he'd sent Rem as Roswaal's envoy to the Knights' Garrison. If she could suggest the Witch Cult's presence, he'd thought, perhaps it would be possible to get the knights moving after all, but—

"Not going well, huh."

"...It seems there are already several similar reports filed with the knights. Since the Witch Cult's background is so murky, the moment something looks suspicious, the report immediately gets bumped up the chain."

"Ah, I see. So it's really treated like the witch-hunt era, huh. ...And if that's what's giving them cover, that's not funny at all."

For every person who feared the Witch Cult in secret, another layer of the phantom Witch Cult's supposed activity was built up.
If that fear got siphoned off and pooled at the knights, and as a result even the value of genuine information faded and vanished—what a backwards state of affairs that was.

Rem hung her shoulders apologetically, but he had no intention of blaming her.
This wasn't Rem's failing; the fault lay with the knights' negligence and the Witch Cult's own repulsiveness. The knights ought to properly vet the truth of the reports coming in, and the Witch Cult's very existence was nothing but a blight. Grinding his teeth at these injustices, Subaru made his decision.

"If gathering fighting strength is impossible... it's frustrating, but there's no help for it."

"What will you do?"

"Obviously. We go back to the mansion. Go back and get Emilia and Ram out. The royal capital, or wherever Roswaal's gone off to, either's fine. That place is dangerous, plain and simple."

Petelgeuse's cackling laughter surfaced in his mind, and Subaru's fists trembled with frustration.
But even wanting to smash that skull-like face to pieces, his hands, the ones that mattered, couldn't reach that far. He had a vague idea of where they were hiding. Even so, he lacked the manpower even for an ambush, and if he tried to take them on with his current strength—it would mean putting Rem on the front line.

That alone he refused.
That alone he could no longer bear.
The idea that, because of something Subaru did, some conclusion Subaru reached, Rem might get hurt—might even be driven to a situation where she lost her life—was unthinkable.

If he couldn't secure fighting strength against Petelgeuse beyond Rem herself, then he mustn't even consider picking a fight with them.
His guts still boiled with murderous intent even now, and an endless curse of hatred rang on and on inside his skull. Kept ringing. But—

"There's still a day and a half—if we can leave the royal capital now, we can reach the mansion sometime during the third day. That should leave time to get Emilia and the others out."

Recalling the first loop, Subaru had checked and rechecked the time limit again and again.
Three days wasted at Crusch's mansion. Hearing there was a shadow of unease over Roswaal's territory, a day with Rem, then another half-day after picking up Otto in a village along the way. After that, he'd headed on foot to the site and greeted the dawn of the fifth day with slaughter.
In other words, if he could arrive by the third day, he still had a margin.

"The second loop... damn it! Just how many days was I out of my mind!?"

On the other hand, the second world offered nothing of value for reconstructing the timeline.
Having lost his mind the moment he returned, Subaru remembered nothing of the journey back to the mansion, carried by Rem. How many days had Rem spent at Crusch's mansion, hauling a Subaru gone mad in her arms, and when had she fallen into Petelgeuse's hands?
In any case—

"No time to waste thinking. Rem, let's arrange a dragon carriage. We can't rely on Crusch or anyone this time... in that case, what do we do?"

"For arranging a dragon carriage, we basically need to use a shop specializing in that. Though right now dragon carriage use is quite congested, so whether we can arrange one immediately..."

Receiving Rem's less-than-confident answer, Subaru wolfed down the rest of his meal and stood, urging her with a "Let's just go take a look, anyway."
Ideally they'd rent a fast, long-distance dragon carriage. But in the worst case, they'd have to consider the first loop's method of swapping partway through.

Grabbing their belongings, thoroughly sick of a reality where nothing was going his way, Subaru set off at a brisk pace, as if begrudging even a single second.
Behind him, Rem, her stride not matching his, worked hard to keep pace. Subaru, not sparing her a single glance, never noticed.

"Merchant Street feels awfully on edge today, somehow."

Leaning his back against the wall of a street he'd grown familiar with, Subaru remarked on the difference in the atmosphere of the passing crowds.
It was a view he had only a few days and a few hours of accumulated experience with, and yet even to Subaru, whose attachment to it amounted to that much, the sense of something foreign was thick enough in today's Merchant Street to feel on his skin.
The reason—

"Word about the Royal Selection's spread, and now people are finally gathering in from all over."

Answering Subaru's mutter with a breath mixed into his voice was the scarred man leaning his sturdy arms on the counter, narrowing his eyes in boredom—Kadomon, the fruit shop owner Subaru had long since grown familiar with.
He glanced sidelong at Subaru, who now stopped in front of his shop as a matter of course, and said,

"When the royal capital changes and the people coming in change, what sells changes too. If you're just going to stand there, get lost—my shop's busy season is finally coming and you're scaring it off."

"Even if the royal capital's clientele changes, your ugly mug doesn't, so this shop's sales won't change either. More importantly, people really are increasing, huh?"

Returning Kadomon's complaint with a scathing jab of his own, Subaru pressed the question further. The shopkeeper made a face of particular distaste, but touching the white scar on his face with a finger, he answered "Yeah" and continued,

"A ton of traveling merchants who'd been outside have come flooding in. Well, in business, timing's everything. Join in late and there's not much sweetness left. The ones with a nose for it have already slipped in, laid out their goods, and are probably raking it in by now."

"And in the face of such a big chance, what could a shopkeeper with zero business sense be thinking, still lining up unsold Appas out front? Ah, I just can't imagine."

"You got some kind of grudge against my shop or something? You die if you don't talk trash the second you show up?"

Trading banter with less than his usual edge, Subaru surveyed the street again.
Sure enough, just as Kadomon said, within his field of view, it seemed the ones increasing weren't so much the buying crowd rushing after goods as the selling crowd, searching for places to lay out the loads they carried.
Their feet naturally carried them toward the parts of Merchant Street where street vendors set up shop, while the others pulled their dragon carriages toward the noble quarter.

"I get why traveling merchants would set up street stalls, but what's with the dragon carriages heading to the upper tier?"

"Probably going to wholesale their goods straight to the nobles up top. Do it right and you make more than going through a merchant guild, plus you get your face known to the bigwigs. Screw it up, though, and you get marked—there's even a chance you don't come back down."

Kadomon spoke in a disinterested tone, as if he had no stake in other people's profit talk. Giving him a noncommittal reply, Subaru's eyes stayed fixed on the passing dragon carriages.
If only he could manage to borrow even one of them. Worst case, if he couldn't arrange a carriage, he'd have to consider stealing one as well.
If it was a problem money could solve, then he should solve it that way, since it couldn't be traded for a human life. And if it turned out to be a problem money couldn't solve, well, then he'd just commit a petty crime in the name of saving lives, too.

It was a different matter from stealing a bicycle from a bike rack, but Subaru resolved himself to it as something unavoidable. And then, watching Subaru gaze at the dragon carriages like that, Kadomon apparently assumed Subaru was interested in their cargo, because—

"With the Royal Selection and all, both demand and supply have gone up along with the people. But out of everything, what's especially prized right now is iron goods—swords, spears, weapons, armor. Not just what's being hauled in from outside—apparently they're buying it up from shops that were already established in the royal capital too."

"Iron and weapons... I feel like I've heard that somewhere before."

Where was it, he wondered, touching his chin as he recalled—and remembered it had been a scene from a conversation with Otto in the first loop.
With iron goods selling high in the royal capital, Otto, saddled with a huge stock of oil, had been drowning his sorrows in drink, forced to decide whether to hang himself or not.
Put together with what Kadomon was saying, it seemed Otto's desperate situation had already been set in stone by this point.
Still, more to the point—

"Swords and armor. If they're gathering iron, are they going in that direction too? Whoever's collecting all this, are they planning on starting a war or something?"

"Who knows, maybe reinforcing their defenses... but even so, it's a bit off. If that were the reason, you'd normally do it in your own territory. Piling up too much stuff in a temporary residence like the royal capital just makes it hard to move, and that's no good."

"From the way you're talking, whoever's collecting it—their name's known?"

Casually riding along with Kadomon's conversation, Subaru asked, and the man crossed his arms and nodded broadly, "Yeah,"

"Duke Crusch Karsten. In other words, one of the Royal Selection candidates."

"Crusch...?"

A name closer to him than he'd expected came up, and Subaru's brow knit in suspicion. But thinking back, there were points that lined up.
Day after day, many visitors had come to see Crusch. Subaru had judged that to be part of the diplomacy needed to secure connections with influential figures, but in reality, some of it might have been negotiations with merchants bringing in exactly those kinds of goods.
In fact, as far as Subaru knew, the last person Crusch had met with was a merchant deeply involved in the royal capital's economy—a man named Russell.

For a moment he thought he'd like to hear more details, but a contrary feeling rose up—what good would it do to spend thought on something so beside the point—and he hesitated.
And, while he was at it—

"Hey, isn't that your traveling companion waving over there?"

Following Kadomon's pointing finger and turning to look across the street, he found Rem jogging toward him, waving. Arriving right in front of Subaru, she bounced her shoulders, her expression brightening in a way only someone who knew her well would recognize, and said,

"I found one. It looks like I can manage to rent a dragon carriage for long-distance travel."

"Seriously? That's great news. Given how packed that shop looked earlier, I wasn't holding my breath..."

The rental dragon carriage shop's prosperity had been considerable, and negotiations had gone just as roughly as Rem had feared. Judging that he wouldn't be much help even if he stayed, Subaru had left the negotiating to Rem and come to see Kadomon, sorting through his thoughts along with the conversation.
Rem, who'd apparently managed to steer the later negotiations well, loosened her cheeks in a slightly proud smile.

"Yes. He was quite stubborn, but we agreed on it by paying a bit extra. I'm sorry for using some of our travel funds on my own judgment."

"No, nice call. At this point we shouldn't be stingy with money when it comes to the goal."

Apparently the system was that shop staff would bring the carriage all the way to the royal capital's entrance to meet them. She'd told them the rendezvous time would be soon, so it was a rush, but time to move immediately.

"Well, that's how it is. It was a short while, but thanks for everything, old man."

"Leaving the royal capital, huh. Well, you I don't mind, but I'll miss the young lady."

Kadomon's grudging remark carried genuine regret, probably because Rem had covered his shop a few times, and each time she had, sales had shot up dramatically.
The fact that he never went out of his way to chase off Subaru, arrogant and brusque as he was, owed a lot to Kadomon's good nature and to Rem's contributions. In any case—

"Thank you for everything, Kadomon-sama. Should I visit the royal capital again, I will be sure to stop by without fail."

"Thanks for bein' so polite. ...Say, you're probably some pretty high-ranking person's attendant in truth, aren't you—sorry for talking to you so rudely all this time."

"Not at all. Before being Roswaal-sama's attendant, Rem now belongs to Subaru-kun. Please continue treating her as you have."

"...I hate you more and more, I swear."

Scowling grandly at Rem's answer, his scar crinkling, Kadomon held out a paper bag to Rem with a "Here." Peering into the bag from beside Rem as she took it, Subaru found it full of colorful fruit.

"Gratitude for the sales boost and for your patronage. Watch yourselves on the road."

"...Old man. It's exactly this kind of thing that keeps your sales down. Your wife and daughter are gonna give up on you."

"I didn't give it to you—I gave it to Rem-chan! Now get outta here, already!"

Chased off by his rough kindness, Subaru and Rem hurried away from the spot.
Right to the very last, there had been nothing but goodwill in his exchanges with Kadomon. That alone gave his heart a small measure of relief—and Subaru tightened his cheeks, which had started to loosen into a smile.

Laughing, being at peace—neither was necessary now.

Once everything was settled, he'd laugh so much he wouldn't need to hold back.
For that, he didn't need those feelings now.
He didn't need them. Not now.

The earth dragon pulling the carriage Rem had rented boasted an especially large build even among all the earth dragons Subaru had seen so far, and it tore through the grasslands, its powerful hind legs shaking the ground with every stride.

With leg power strong enough that the dust cloud lingered in view for a good while, their speed rose but their visibility clouded over. Sitting beside Rem on the driver's seat, Subaru narrowed his eyes against the dust and said,

"Fast is... good, but is there nothing we can do about this?"

"This earth dragon is originally meant for hauling heavy freight. So its gait isn't built with passengers in mind, and it's specialized for speed—it can't run quietly, or so I was told."

"Lucky we could even rent it, and it can run without breaks, so I guess I can't complain. ...Even so, this dust is rough. Is there anything...?"

Fumbling teary-eyed through their luggage, he came up with nothing good.
Left with no choice, he wound a thin cloth around his mouth as an improvised dust guard, and did the same for Rem, who was likewise gripping the reins.

"Alright, there. This way we won't get grit in our mouths, and it should be a bit better, right?"

"Yes. As for visibility, we'll just have to bear with it."

With both their mouths covered like this, it had the air of some gang from who-knows-what era, but neither of them had the composure to joke about it now.
Once watching the road ahead required less effort, Subaru's thoughts naturally sank into themselves. All around, grassland stretched as far as the eye could see, and in that world of unbroken green, even change rarely visited. What changed was the position of the clouds drifting across the sky, and the slow but certain shift in the sun's angle—signifying the passage of time, which slowly scorched at Subaru's heart.

His time allocation should be working considerably in his favor.
The day and a half spent in the royal capital had, frankly, been wasted time that yielded no results, but managing to obtain a dragon carriage and depart on the second day counted for a great deal.
Even taking the sun's now-tilting position as the starting point, if they raced along the highway for half a day, they'd reach the mansion sometime tomorrow morning. If that meant the morning of the third day, it would be half a day earlier than when he'd departed Crusch's mansion in the first loop.
There should be ample time to get Emilia and the others out of the mansion and flee.
If there was a problem, it was—

"The chance we run into the Witch Cult on the way, like last time..."

His memory of the second loop was hazy, but if the cave he'd woken up in was their hideout, and it had lain somewhere along the road back to the mansion, then the encounter would have happened during that return journey.
The two of them, racing along the road home by dragon carriage or something similar, attacked by the Witch Cult, resulting in Subaru's capture and Rem, coming to rescue him, being killed—that felt like the natural sequence.
After that, when Subaru broke free of his shackles and escaped the cave, it felt—though he had no clear sense of it—like it must have taken roughly a day.

"Which means they'd have been lurking around the mansion for several days beforehand."

The exact date wasn't clear.
If the second loop's timeline matched the first loop's, then the massacre at the mansion and village happened on the morning of the fifth day. And calculating that the time Subaru had been held captive was around a day and a half, the encounter with them would have fallen somewhere between the third and fourth days. In other words—

"Given that we're set to arrive tomorrow morning, the chance of an encounter doesn't go away...!"

Grinding his teeth yet again—he'd lost count of how many times now—drew a taste of blood.
Had they managed to depart yesterday, this worry would have been considerably reduced. But at their current travel time, they were cutting deep into the danger zone. He couldn't afford optimism.

"——"

Glancing sidelong, Subaru looked at Rem, gripping the reins, focused on driving.
If it did come to a clash with Witch Cult members, he'd have no choice but to rely on Rem again, as a matter of course. Or perhaps, if he told her beforehand about the possibility of encountering the Witch Cult and urged her to be on guard, they might avoid repeating the same failure as before.

But the moment he tried to actually voice it, Subaru realized he couldn't get the words out.
It wasn't that he feared the black mist appearing, the world grinding to a halt, the agony of having his heart crushed in that stillness—no. That fear of the pain was certainly there. Every time he remembered it, every time he tasted it, the pain that tore through his entire body was enough to make him think his hair might turn white. It wasn't something a sane person could endure. Even the one time he'd made use of it, it had only been possible because of some incomprehensible explosion of emotion. Told to do it again, he'd shake his head no.
But it wasn't that pain that made Subaru hesitate now to bring up the Witch Cult.

—Would she, he wondered.

Would Rem actually believe what he told her?

"—!"

Just thinking about it sent a chill down his spine, and Subaru hugged his own shoulders as if unable to bear it.
His heart pounded stupidly fast, and a wave of nausea rose up, pressing on his insides. Stress from an extreme mental state, combined with the fatigue of a body that hadn't slept a wink, were eating away at Subaru's willpower and strength as accumulated debt.

For Subaru now, the most trustworthy presence in this world was none other than Rem.
Abandoned even by Emilia, treated coldly in succession by Crusch and then Priscilla, Subaru's mind had fallen into a world of paranoia, unable to help doubting everything.
He couldn't imagine a scenario where his trust in Crusch and Priscilla was restored, and even with Emilia, he couldn't picture a scene of laughing and talking together openly until he'd killed Petelgeuse and secured her safety.

So, for Subaru now, there was only Rem.
Someone he could entrust everything to completely, and feel at ease—the only one he could call a companion was her.
So what if, hypothetically, he confided in her about the Witch Cult, and her expression clouded with doubt as a result? —Just thinking about it was terrifying.

"This is no time... to be scared..."

He said it aloud, trying to drive away his cowardice, but the sound that came out was closer to breath than voice. A volume more whisper than utterance, swallowed up by the earth dragon's ground-shaking footfalls, not even reaching himself.
And yet, he had to tell her.

As long as there was a chance of clashing with the Witch Cult, not knowing would only mean repeating the last loop. Subaru had lost his life and come back for the sake of seizing the best outcome.
So—

"R-Rem... um, I need to talk to—"
"Subaru-kun—up ahead, there are people gathered."

But his voice, wrung out with resolve, was cut off by Rem's sharp call.
"Huh?" Subaru said, following her gaze, and found several foreign shapes rising up ahead on the highway their earth dragon was running down.

—Could it be a Witch Cult ambush!?

At the sheer suddenness of it, Subaru was startled enough that his heart nearly leapt from his mouth, but as the shapes gradually drew closer, taking on clearer outlines, their true identity became apparent.
It was—

"—Hey there! Could you stop the earth dragon for a bit, let's trade some information!"

Waving both arms in the middle of the highway, calling out loudly for them to stop—there stood the merchant Otto.

"Ah, sorry about that. Thank you for stopping. These days, plenty of people are heading toward the royal capital, but hardly anyone's coming out from it. I've been hoping to hear from someone like that."

Greeting the two who'd stopped their carriage, the young man—Otto—said this with a smile.

He wasn't drunk off his rocker, nor did he look like a man who'd given up on the world. And needless to say, he showed no sign of injury—Otto the merchant, alive and well, right here.
In the first loop, leaving him behind while he desperately tried to stop them had lodged itself in Subaru's chest as a small thorn. Though, considering the state of the village afterward, leaving him behind might well have been a fortunate call for Otto's own sake.
In any case—

"Are all the people gathered here traveling merchants or something?"

"Nothing 'or something' about it—traveling merchants exactly. Everyone here's greedy for a shot at striking it rich in the royal capital, now that it's the talk of the town."

Looking past Otto and asking this, Subaru got a fawning, rubbing-hands smile in return.
Behind Otto—several dragon carriages were parked along the shoulder of the highway, with men who appeared to be their owners gathered in a group. Their number was somewhere shy of ten, and their ages ranged from young like Otto's to around forty. Among them was the gaunt man who, in the first loop, had introduced Subaru to Otto.
Once they judged the greetings between Otto and the newcomers had wrapped up, they crowded around the two of them in a swarm, each introducing himself and starting up conversation.

The content was mainly about the royal capital's current state, the changes before and after the Royal Selection began, and things like currency value fluctuations—naturally, all points a merchant would care about.

Honestly, even the time spent stopping to talk was precious, but the moment he confirmed Otto's presence, the option of ignoring him and passing by evaporated.
Fortunately, he'd confirmed Otto's safety (a given, in the third loop), so Subaru had intended to leave the moment the conversation reached a lull.

"Are you setting off now? It's already night, isn't that dangerous? We're planning to camp here tonight, so if you like, why not join us?"

Just as the information exchange—really a one-sided information extraction—wound to a close, Otto stopped Subaru and Rem as they moved to depart.
Just as he said, even the last traces of sun had nearly vanished from the sky, and beyond a sky dyed orange, night had begun spreading over the grassland.
Before long, the Lifaus Highway would be completely swallowed by night, and their visibility would depend entirely on the light cast by Crystal Lamps.

The gathered merchants had already begun setting up camp, and at the center of the cluster of dragon carriages, preparations for a bonfire were already underway.
It was probably a judgment that wild dogs, bandits, and the like that might appear on the highway would find it hard to make a move with this many people gathered. Impressive as that traveling-merchant wisdom was, even that span of safe time was precious now.

"Talk like that, Otto, and it just sounds like you want to unload even a little of that oil stock you're stuck with out of season, doesn't it? Don't put on that nice-guy act!"

Just as Subaru was about to decline, a voice needled in like that. A man's voice, followed by an eruption of laughter behind him, and Otto, the butt of the joke, twisted his lips and said,

"That's not my intention at all. It's out of pure goodwill. Well—if you happened to bring even a little oil for meals or lanterns... I won't say I have no ulterior motive at all."

"Is something the matter, with the oil?"

Otto, shoulders drooping, spoke as if sore about losing, and Rem asked. At her question, he raised his eyebrows, gave a affectedly casual shrug, and said,

"Oh, well. It seems I fumbled this round's business opportunity somehow. I'm stuck with a huge stock of oil whose value as merchandise is questionable. It was supposed to fetch a fortune up north in Gusteko, but... how much of a loss I can recover here in Lugunica this time of year, I don't know."

His look of helplessness was probably a tactic meant to invite sympathy.
It wasn't that he took them for the type to fall for it, but the calculation peeking through was something like: if it got even a little of the oil sold at a fair price, that'd be a bonus.
Rem probably saw through it too. Though sympathetic, she apparently formed no further impression beyond that, offering only a formal "I see. That must be difficult" by way of consolation.

"Even if I go to the royal capital, I don't know if I can unload all this oil. If I end up having to sell it for next to nothing, I'm ruined. —Ruined."

He seemed to think it important enough to say twice, but that didn't mean Subaru had the grand-hearted generosity to go declaring he'd buy all the oil or anything like that.
Their relationship, born of Otto's help in the first loop, was thin this time around. He'd like to wish good fortune on Otto's path ahead, but his own path ahead came first.

Right now, it was about racing through the night highway and entering Mathers territory as fast as possible.
The chance of encountering the Witch Cult, the measures for getting Emilia and the others out safely—his thoughts were consumed with those, and as he moved to string together words of farewell—Subaru suddenly realized something.

If money could move something, shouldn't he use it?

"Otto, I've got a talk—no, a business proposal."

Suddenly wiping his expression blank, Subaru murmured this in a low voice, and Otto's eyes went wide. But sensing no trace of a joke in that tone, he immediately straightened his posture.

"For a business proposal, I'll hear anything. What can I do for you, customer?"

"That oil loaded on your carriage—I'll buy all of it. In exchange, lend me your legs."

Pointing at Otto's earth dragon—the carriage he recognized well—Subaru then spread both arms wide, raising his voice so it would carry to the merchants busy setting up camp, and declared,

"Every merchant here with a dragon carriage—anyone willing to sell me their legs for money, all of you, get bought by me!"
