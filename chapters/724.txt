Arc 9 Chapter 28: "A Verse of Legend"

---

—This was, without question, a verse straight out of a legend's telling of battle.

"—"

The *Sword Devil's* twin blades danced, and the *Divine Dragon's* claws, rising to meet them, whipped up a raging storm.

Dragons are born as the pinnacle of all life, apex beings above every other creature, and among dragons, the *Divine Dragon* possessed abilities that stood out even further still — its claws, its fangs, its scales, each one a thing that deserved to be called a natural-born divine artifact.

Every time that vast, hidden power was unleashed, the wind screamed its death cries, the sky warped, and the world itself grew hot to the touch. A single blow taken squarely would make it hard for anyone to keep fighting.

The *Sword Devil* had a way to keep that fearsome divine artifact from ever being wielded at full strength.

The reason the *Divine Dragon* had appeared before the *Sword Devil* in the first place was the peril of a girl who shared the same trait as the lineage of the Lion King. That girl was deep within the room where the fight had begun, and without even holding down her hair as it whipped about in the raging wind, she watched the battle intently.

Shielding that girl at his back, positioning himself so that the destruction the *Divine Dragon* wrought would sweep her up along with him—

—if he did that, then the *Divine Dragon*, which for some reason feared the girl being harmed, would be unable to bring its full power to bear, and the footsteps of the death zone bearing down on the *Sword Devil* could be eased, if only a little.

And so—

"—"

The *Sword Devil* abandoned the position shielding the girl at his back, and closed the distance to the death zone himself.

He bared himself to the raging storm of destruction and annihilation, and with the two blades of steel gripped in both hands as his lifeline, he flung himself onto the cliff edge of an ending from which a single misstep meant death.

He could have used the battlefield to his advantage, taken his opponent's feelings as a lever, and crept his way toward victory.

But that would be crass. Worse than crass — utterly discourteous, profitless, worthless. A victory stolen by making up for insufficient swordsmanship not with skill but with schemes — what value could such a thing hold, here and now?

—The *Sword Devil*, too, was a man who had lived on the battlefield.

He knew — knew it only by the weight of the backsplashed blood clinging to his soul — that there were fights fought merely to survive, histories told only by the victors and the living, defeats and deaths in battle for which no excuse would suffice.

But this moment, now, was different. —No: from this point on, in every battle where the *Sword Devil* took up his sword and faced someone with that steel, a victory merely seized would hold no meaning.

To force down, with the strength of his blade, the one opponent he absolutely had to defeat.

On that path, the *Sword Devil* had no time to spend on detours. If this body — one that could do nothing but age and wither — was to be forged back into the same steel it once was, there was no way but to keep steeping his soul in white-hot flame.

"—"

Sword-wind raged, and a dragon claw, torn loose and sent flying, spun through the air. Too cramped. Too cramped a battlefield, whether for swinging a sword or for the *Divine Dragon* to make full use of its tail and fangs. The *Sword Devil's* heels exploded off the ground, his blade-light accelerating past wind and sound alike, driving into the *Divine Dragon's* chest as space itself burst apart.

A dragon's scales — their hardness is determined largely by the total amount of mana each individual possesses.

The more powerful and long-lived a dragon is, the greater its robustness and toughness grows, and when it comes to the dragon-scales of the *Divine Dragon*, the very apex of that hierarchy, their hardness surpassed even diamond.

Against those diamond-hard dragon-scales, the *Sword Devil* swung his steel without mercy, striking, hewing, thrusting, repeating his attacks in an attempt to pierce through. And still, even subjected to that, the steel gripped in his hands held sound — the twin swords *Trias*, which the *Sword Devil* had newly prepared to see his purpose through, were a masterwork from the very same line of swordsmiths who had once forged the famed blade *Astrea*, so deeply entwined with his fate.

The hilts fit his hands, their weight had become one with his body, and the blade-light clashed against diamond and still did not chip—

"—"

Against the *Sword Devil's* fierce onslaught, the *Divine Dragon*, too, was no pushover — it was not one to simply take blow after blow.

Even though the heart hidden within it was not, in truth, its own, by unhesitatingly unleashing the full performance of the Dragonhusk said to be the mightiest on the ground, the *Divine Dragon* could easily bring forth calamities on the scale of natural disaster.

That was, without doubt, the right the *Divine Dragon* had been granted by Od Lagna: to remake the shape of the world however it wished, to paste a convenient outcome onto reality and let it play out.

A roar split the sky; dragon claws tore the earth apart. Its dragon-scales flung back the *Sword Devil's* full-strength blows a hundred times over, and the dragon's aura pouring from its whole body dyed the royal capital's air the color of the *Divine Dragon*.

The tail swung, aimed to strike the aged *Sword Devil* head-on. —No: the *Sword Devil* planted his blade-tip into the tail, dodged aside, and in one motion ran straight up it, closing in on the throat. A roar rose to meet him. Light was cleaved apart from the front, a slash of light racing through. Blocked by the dragon-scale at the shoulder — an instant's pause to reset — and the clash resumed.

"—"

The spread wings became blades, and the sword-storm of gusting wind the *Divine Dragon* whipped up tore through the royal capital. Slipping through that wind, matching the trail the *Sword Devil* left as he trailed a ribbon of blood, the tail — tracing the death zone — was driven home. At the resounding crash of destruction, the *Sword Devil's* body was sent flying violently backward. A direct hit — no: seeing that he could not dodge it, the *Sword Devil*, timing it to the tail's sweep, let the strength drain from his fingertips, his ankles, his knees, his hips, his ribs, his shoulders, his neck, the crown of his head, drawing the fatal impact out through his whole body, and he flew, flew, flew on.

Punching through one building, then a second, then a third, he planted his sword into the ground and raised his head. His will to fight did not waver in the slightest. And there, the *Divine Dragon's* breath came bearing down as white light.

"—"

With one flash of his blade, the *Sword Devil* split the leading edge of the breath with a stroke of light, twisted his body into the sliver of a gap it created, escaped being burned by the light, and leapt into the air. There, wings beating, the *Divine Dragon* lunged, its jaws clamping down on the *Sword Devil* without mercy.

The dragon fangs, boasting even greater strength and sharpness than its claws, moved to tear the *Sword Devil's* flesh apart. Against them, he brought the tips of the swords in both his hands together, precisely, using them as a prop that kept the jaws from fully closing. Had he instead aimed the blade-tips at the upper and lower jaw directly, the closing jaws would have crushed the life out of him.

An instinct that refused to allow that, that rejected "death" — no, a greed for seizing victory — was what kept him alive.

With the *Sword Devil* still caught in its unclosing jaws, the *Divine Dragon* abandoned the attempt to take his life with its fangs — and instead, a fierce click of the dragon's tongue, an explosion of space itself, struck the *Sword Devil's* whole body head-on.

"—"

Blood sprayed from both ears; his ruptured eardrums and the inner ear beyond them reeled. The *Sword Devil* ground his teeth and held his ground, but the dragon's tongue, lashing out, struck him down and flung him — defenseless — out beyond the jaws, into the high sky above the royal capital.

Having been carried up into the sky by the wingbeats of the *Divine Dragon* while caught in its jaws, the *Sword Devil*, now buffeted helplessly by the wind of a free fall he could do nothing about, plunged straight down, all at once, all at once, from a height that would make anyone faint of heart lose consciousness just from looking down.

If he fell like this, even the *Sword Devil* would not come out unscathed. That was the decisive difference between a human who lived on the ground and a dragon permitted, by its massive frame, to rule the sky—

"—"

But even in a situation that seemed like a decisive blow, the *Divine Dragon* would not simply leave the *Sword Devil* to fall.

It drew a great breath, its lungs swelling to prepare a blast — if unleashed on the royal capital spread out directly below, it would cause immense damage, breaking its attempt to avoid killing — no: directly below, in the noble district, people had already been kept away.

Someone had designated this place as the battlefield beforehand, evacuating everyone who might otherwise have been caught up in it, and built the battlefield accordingly. Admiration — and gratitude — for that handiwork.

"—"

The breath released was larger and hotter than before, closing in on the *Sword Devil* as searing heat.

Wingless, with no means of resisting gravity, the *Sword Devil* could do nothing but take the white light head-on, unable even to look straight at it — but in that instant, as he spun his body in midair, something came rushing to meet him from below, to serve as footing.

"—"

It was one of the buildings the *Sword Devil* had punched through with his own body before being taken up into the sky — a tall Time Tower — cutting into the path of the falling old swordsman and functioning as footing for the *Sword Devil* as he twisted his body. Bending his knees, the *Sword Devil* landed on the tower, drew his twin blades back taut, and looked up at the sky. —Across the breath, the gazes of the *Sword Devil* and the *Divine Dragon* met, and in that instant, a beautiful flash of the sword and the white light collided head-on.

"—"

Like water bursting apart, like thin glass shattering in a chain, sounds rang out one after another, and the white light was split open from within.

It was the *Divine Dragon's* breath. It possessed both the power and the right to swallow whatever it collided with, strip away its surface, and tear away whatever lay at its depths, laying it bare. And yet, ignoring all of that presented before it, utterly as it pleased, the blade-strike the *Sword Devil* unleashed split the light in two.

—A flash of the sword, truly pure, with no room left for anything wicked to mingle in, cleaved everything apart with beauty.

"—"

The severed breath spread out across the ground in a radial burst, and the royal capital's noble district was leveled flat, torn apart.

Only the tower bearing the *Sword Devil* at the blast's epicenter, and the crimson mansion under the *Divine Dragon's* protection, escaped direct damage — everything else came to know the full measure of the dragon's tremendous power.

The *Sword Devil*, scorched white by the aftershock of the white light, leapt down from the crumbling tower; the *Divine Dragon*, wings still beating, kept radiating hostility from the vast sky above, and their gazes crossed between the heavens and the earth.

And then—

"—"

The sword-aura, far from running dry but overflowing all the more, and the dragon-pressure that bore down to crush everything, collided once again, and every living thing in the royal capital of Lugunica — animate or inanimate, it made no difference — shuddered in terror.

"Do you like flowers?"

In a world hazed white, he heard the question of the beloved woman, standing before a field of yellow flowers.

As the world warped under the dragon's aura, and he twisted his body into the lethal gale bearing down on him, swinging free the blade-light borne in both hands, countless memories raced through Wilhelm's mind.

"Have you come to like flowers?"

She had been a beloved woman. Drawn to her at first sight, the foolish man had shut a lid over that unfathomable emotion.

And even so, the bond between them was never severed; indulging in that circumstance, afraid to cut in any further, Wilhelm steeped body and mind in a terribly, terribly cowardly and sluggish stretch of time, and let the days go to waste.

"Swinging a sword to protect someone — I think that's a good thing."

His homeland wrapped in burning flame, and the beautiful flash of a blade that had saved Wilhelm when his life was nearly taken.

He denied, with a force that scorched his very soul, the fact that he had given a reason to wield the sword to a woman who held no desire to grip one herself and yet had been loved by the sword regardless — and countless furies filled Wilhelm up.

Fury at his own weakness, at the things he hid, at his own inadequacy — fury, fury, fury—.

By cutting down every last bit of that fury, forcing it under, and devoting himself utterly to housing every violent passion within the length of his blade, he finally earned the right to stand before her and cross swords with her.

And so, he wrested his beloved woman back from the hateful Sword God's side, and made her his own.

"Do you love me?"

—No: had he made her his own, or had he been made hers? He did not know the right answer.

He only knew that he had been happy. Fortunate. The utmost blessing one could obtain had begun the instant he met Theresia, and kept filling Wilhelm up until the very moment it was lost.

—Theresia's "death" was, without question, a tragedy.

Unable even to properly exchange parting words, failing to deliver the words he should have said, Wilhelm was overtaken by grief and lamentation, and in his despair, he overlooked much and committed many mistakes.

But the days he spent with Theresia had been, for Wilhelm, happy ones.

Could it really be concluded that, the instant their last day together ended in tragedy, that happy time — the whole of it — faded away and was ruined, as though none of it had ever been?

Even if it had lost its color for a time, could he possibly help but wish for it to be colored vividly again?

Absolutely not.

To keep Theresia's "death" — her "life" — from ending in tragedy, he had pursued her killer.

And if, by wielding his sword for Theresia's sake, he could bring color back to those long-past days, then he could not stop struggling, could not stop hoping for color to return to this other faded scene before him as well.

The raging wind, the fierce roar unleashed by the *Divine Dragon*.

He drove his blade-tip into the wrath of the *Dragon* that burned the world white, and as it was cut open and his sight cleared, beyond it, still spitting blood, he could see the regret he needed to cut his way into.

—A clouded sky, an empty grave marker, unceasing sobs and voices of mourning, words of prayer wishing for peace.

At the center of the many weeping people who had gathered for the beloved woman, he could not recall what he had spoken, what he had conveyed, what words he had chosen so that the way Theresia had lived would be carved deeply into their hearts.

What he remembered was regret. A regret of Wilhelm's, strong and strong and vast, one that could never be wiped away.

A young child who did not yet understand reason, a child who had not been allowed to be with the father and mother he should have been with, had chosen words meant as kindness and comfort — and he, the fool, had rejected them, had failed to receive them.

The faded sky of that instant, the ground, the faces of the people, himself, and the child—

"—It's all right, Grandfather. When she passed, Grandmother wasn't the 'Sword Saint' anymore. So the 'Sword Saint' hasn't lost."

"...Stop it. Please."

"Because the 'Sword Saint' isn't allowed to lose. That's what Father told me."

"I'm begging you, stop."

"So there's no need for you to grieve, Grandfather — House Astrea is safe."

"Not now... not here... don't say things like that."

Words of powerless refusal, repeated over and over.

Words of comfort, layered over them, from a child who had already settled on what to believe in.

And then—

"I'm the next 'Sword Saint.' —I received it before Grandmother passed away."

"—That's enough! What do you want me to say?! Don't make me say that it's your fault Theresia... that your grandmother died!!"

"—"

"Don't... make me say it...!"

In that faded scene, everything else vanished, leaving only himself and the child, the two of them alone. Driving his blade into that immature, foolish self of that day, in that instant, Wilhelm stepped forward.

Words never spoken cannot reach the other person. Even if feelings are shared, the thousand words, the ten thousand words that ought to have been given cannot be delivered.

In the same way, words that have been spoken will reach the other person, in some form or another, no matter what.

What is drawn from them afterward depends on the vessel of the one who receives them.

But how foolish it would be to pour more water than a still-unfinished vessel can hold, and then blame the vessel — condemn it — for overflowing when it could not take it all in.

In that faded world, he cannot recall the face of the child from that day — Reinhard's face.

That is because, after blurting out what should never have been said, he could not bear to look at that face. It is not that he cannot remember it — he never saw it, and so he does not know it.

And the words once spoken are not always received by only one person.

"...I heard you tore into that boy at Mother's funeral. And now you want to see Reinhard? A father who can't even tell the difference between what should and shouldn't be said — as if you have the right to apologize to him."

"That boy is mine and Louanna's child.... Saying he caused Mother's death, that he stole her Divine Protection! That's some kind of mistake! I'll... I'll prove it myself!"

"It's not you, Father. It's me. —It's something I have to do."

Words once uttered cannot be taken back.

Wilhelm's harsh words to Reinhard spread in an instant, reaching even the ears of Heinkel, who had not attended the funeral — and with that, the chance for apology or explanation was lost.

At the same time, the foolish reprimand from grandfather to grandson spread beyond the household — and beyond even that scandal, word of the succession of the "Sword Saint's Divine Protection" began to spread as well.

Heinkel never once let Wilhelm meet Reinhard.

Reinhard, following what Heinkel told him, avoided Wilhelm, and all contact between them was cut off.

Kept at a distance by both Heinkel and Reinhard, Wilhelm, alone, immersed himself in revenge.

That relationship between the three of them, buttoned up wrong from the start, was tied together again at the Water Gate City amid Theresia's corpse soldiers — and once again gave birth to a decisive rift. —If that were the end of it, it would close as a tragedy.

But Wilhelm, who had chosen not to let Theresia's "death," her "life," her whole "lifetime" end in tragedy, raised his objection to letting this relationship, too, close on a tragic note.

And he knew no way of voicing his objection to an unyielding fate other than by voicing it with his sword.

—Reinhard is the "Sword Saint."

So long as he continues to bear that mission unshaken, the chance to exchange feelings with the Reinhard who stands on the far side of the "Sword Saint," and to bring color back to that faded scene, will never come around.

And so, Wilhelm van Astrea — no, the one who would steal the sword from the "Sword Saint," Wilhelm Trias — must stake his life, blood-bright, upon the flash of his blade.

—Once more: this was, without question, a verse straight out of a legend's telling of battle.

The battle between the *Sword Devil* and the *Divine Dragon*, freighted with all manner of factors, had become a give-and-take struggle, pitting finely honed swordsmanship of steel against the innate power of an apex being born to the top of all life.

The stretch of the noble district in the royal capital that had become the battlefield was leveled to bare ground, and the crumbled buildings and shattered streets told of the sheer ferocity of a collision between beings existing on a plane mere humans could never reach.

Whether in size of body, in the presence or absence of firm footing, or in the struggle for a decisive blow, blade-light and dragon-aura remained locked in balance.

A battle between a human and a dragon — a death-match so rare that in the present age, one might pile up a hundred years and never see its like — and yet, astonishingly, this was the second time the *Sword Devil* had fought a dragon.

It could only be described as a fated destiny — the fact that he kept willingly stepping into the most dangerous battlefields this world had to offer, and that some great power governing fate kept preparing opponents worthy of the *Sword Devil*.

By the terms set from the start, this battle was overwhelmingly disadvantageous for the *Sword Devil*.

The slashes of the twin blades the *Sword Devil* wielded could not pierce the *Divine Dragon's* tough scales, while on the other hand, the *Divine Dragon's* claws and fangs needed only to graze him to easily tear the *Sword Devil's* life from his body.

The *Sword Devil* kept evading them with skill honed to the very limit of his strength, but sooner or later, a limit had to come. —At least, that was so in theory.

But who could possibly picture the *Sword Devil* — smeared in blood, scorched white, his back seared, still swinging his sword — utterly spent, falling at last to the *Divine Dragon's* claws?

The *Sword Devil's* face, twisted with a fearsome intensity; a sword-aura so dense that merely being bathed in it made one feel a blade had already been laid against one's throat; a strength of swordsmanship clawing its way back toward the peak that once brought down the "Sword Saint" — all of it denies plain reality, and makes anyone who witnesses it hold onto hope and expectation that defy common sense.

—The "Sword Saint" is the sword of the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica, and the bearer of its hope.

When that hope was cast down by the *Sword Devil*, the people should have fallen into despair, should have looked to the heavens in dismay. Since the "Sword Saint" had been defeated, that would have been the natural order of things. And yet, reality proved otherwise.

No one mourned that the "Sword Saint" had lost and laid down the sword — instead, they celebrated it. That was because a new hope had sprouted — because a new expectation had come to dwell in the *Sword Devil*, the one who had defeated the "Sword Saint."

The *Sword Devil*, who had pursued nothing but strength, who could do nothing but hone himself as sharp as the steel in his hands.

The *Sword Devil's* way of being — straightforward, yielding to no one — made many people lift their heads, made them hold onto hope, made them look forward to the future.

That no one, even now, in this very moment, could picture a future in which the *Sword Devil* facing the *Divine Dragon* was defeated — that, too, was the same thing. —In the *Sword Devil's* very way of being, people could not help but see hope.

So long as there was a scene worth wishing for on the far side of what that sword cut open, one could not help but believe it would surely be reached.

And so the battle between the *Sword Devil* and the *Divine Dragon* had settled into a stalemate — neither able to find a decisive blow, both locked instead in a life-or-death struggle to determine how each might surpass the other.

—And so the outcome of this contest between two transcendent beings could not be settled by the *Sword Devil* and the *Divine Dragon* alone.

"—"
"—"

At the center of the streets turned battlefield, the gazes of the *Sword Devil* and the *Divine Dragon* crossed, and silence passed between them. But even though both ended in silence, the quality of that silence differed.

One was endurance; the other, astonishment. Both were emotions worthy of silence — but what followed after them was not the same.

"Ngh—"

A faint groan, accompanied by a great gush of blood, spilled from the *Sword Devil's* mouth.

But still gripping his twin swords, the *Sword Devil* made no move to stem the flow of blood, and looked down at his own body — and saw a blood-smeared blade-tip jutting out from his abdomen.

A blade had entered from behind and pierced clean through the *Sword Devil's* torso, blood pouring from the wound. He knew that sword well — the one that had done this. —None other than his own beloved sword of days past.

The blade bore the name *Astrea*, and its current owner was—

"—Heinkel."

Choked with the froth of rising blood, he was not certain his murmur had even come out as sound.

But he could feel the trembling of the sword that had pierced his body, and the intent behind that act, coming through to him regardless. To turn around would risk his torso being torn apart entirely, so the *Sword Devil* clenched his back teeth and held his ground.

Behind him, gripping with both hands the hilt of the sword that had run the *Sword Devil* through—

"...Your way won't bring Louanna back."

"—"

"It's not you... it's not you, Father. It's me. —It's something I have to do."

It was a voice wrung out, mixed with sobs, as though clawing at his own chest.

Hearing it, the pierced *Sword Devil* let out one long, deep breath, and remembered.

The sight of his own child, in the middle of practice, holding back tears to beg for more, after being scolded harshly by his wife every time he made the boy cry.

Now that he thought of it, from back then, whenever the boy cried, he never stood a chance.


—That, it turned out, was the verse of legend: the decisive stroke of the summit battle between man and dragon, a technique known as *the interception from the side*.
