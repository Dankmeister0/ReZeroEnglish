Arc 7 Chapter 36: "The Chaotic Magic City"

"Aah! Uuh!"

Pointing at the cityscape ahead, Rui bounced excitedly on the driver's seat.

Subaru wanted to warn her that she'd get in the way of handling the Galewind Horse, but Medium, the one actually holding the reins, just pulled Rui close by the shoulder and said, "I know, I know!" so cheerfully that pointing it out would have been graceless.

For that matter, Subaru, looking at the same sight, was fairly overwhelmed himself.

"So that's the Magic City, Chaosflame..."

He swallowed audibly, his body betraying his astonishment before he could stop it.

In Lugunica, he'd been struck by the picturesque scenery of the water-gated city of Priestella; in Vollachia, he'd been impressed by the differences in architectural style. But the sight of the Magic City looming before him now struck Subaru with a shock unlike either of those.

As Subaru understood it, a city was a collective built for many people to live in, and that meant it should have some unified style or rules governing its construction.

But Chaosflame's streets had no such sense of unity at all.

It was, true to its name of "Chaos," a melting pot of disorderly, haphazard convictions.

At the city's center, the most eye-catching feature was a castle painted in glossy red, with the streets spreading outward from it in concentric rings. At first glance, the layout resembled Lugunica's royal capital, but there, the noble quarter, the commoners' quarter, and the slums were each clearly distinct by class.

But Chaosflame had none of that.

A dazzling building stood beside a crumbling ruin; a spire more than twice the height of its neighbors rose abruptly along a street lined with low buildings; a lush green park sat right next to a barren stretch of wasted sand.

Countless beams and scaffolds, patched together with no apparent plan, ran over the tops of these buildings, so that from a distance the whole city looked as if it were draped in spiderwebs.

A city with no connection whatsoever to discipline, order, or "normalcy" in general — it was no wonder they called it the Magic City.

One glance was enough to tell that this was, without question, a place where chaos ran rampant.

"...Looks like the kind of place a 'Giant's Poke' could knock flat without much trouble."

Al spoke up beside Subaru, who was squinting at the disorder of the approaching city. He held a hand up like a visor against his helmet's brow as he muttered the remark at the sight of the Magic City.

Not that it mattered, but Subaru wondered if making a visor with your hand over a helmet actually did anything.

"I've lived in Vollachia a long time myself, but this is my first time coming east. Feels fresh."

"So Chaosflame's a first for you too, Al? Then you've been in the west of Vollachia this whole time?"

"West, or more like one spot. There's this thing called Sword Slave Island — a colosseum floating in the middle of a lake. I was a sword slave there the whole time."

"A colosseum sword slave..."

Subaru had heard a bit of Al's background before, and its sheer brutality left him at a loss for words.

Losing his arm was one thing, but a lot of what Al mentioned so casually carried real weight. Probably he spoke lightly about it precisely because it was heavy, so as not to burden the listener — but even so—

"—it's not exactly lightening the load on my end, y'know..."

"Hm? You say something, brother?"

"No, nothing. ...Anyway, what's a 'Giant's Poke'?"

"Huh? They not say that where you're from, brother? It's just, like, earthquakes and stuff."

When Subaru asked about the unfamiliar expression, Al tilted his head and asked back.

Al and Subaru were technically from the same "hometown," but in this case that just meant the "original world" in the broadest sense, so of course their actual hometowns would be different in the real sense.

Still, while there were plenty of proverbs involving oni, Subaru couldn't recall many involving giants.

"Is it maybe a pun on some baseball team? I don't really know much about that stuff."

"Ahh, nah, dunno. I don't even remember where I picked the phrase up, and I don't recall being especially into baseball either."

"Huh. Figured you for the type who'd fit right in drinking beer and watching a game on TV."

"I wasn't even old enough to drink that stuff in the first place."

At Al's wry remark, Subaru remembered the age Al had been when he was summoned to this other world, and privately conceded, *Fair enough.*

Anyway, while they traded such small talk, the carriage carrying the group approached the front of the Magic City — the entrance to the city of chaos.

Speaking of entering and leaving cities, the most recent point of comparison was the checkpoint at Guaral.

At the great gates through which people could enter and exit the city, set at the north and south respectively, inspections were carried out, questioning the identity of those coming and going and the purpose of their stay.

In the end, since they'd let Subaru's group sneak in regardless, whether the checkpoint had actually functioned properly was up for debate — but borrowing Zikr's comment, "The soldiers' eyes were probably dazzled by the sheer loveliness of Natsumi-dono and her companions," Subaru chose to file it away as their side having simply outdone the guards.

In any case, the existence of such checkpoint-like measures made no distinction between Kingdom and Empire.

Each city in Lugunica had similar checks as well. So, thinking this would be one point where things could get tricky, Subaru once again reconstructed "Natsumi Schwartz" from the ground up — and yet—

"—Go on in."

"Roger that! Thanks much!"

"Uuh!"

The one who glanced around the inside of the carriage and granted passage was, presumably serving as a city guard, a member of the Cyclops Tribe — a man of the race with a single large eye in the center of his face.

Permission to enter was granted after nothing more than a quick glance, which left Subaru feeling rather deflated. Or perhaps that distinctive single eye held some special power of sight.

"Maybe that's exactly why they picked him for gate duty."

"You seem to be reading too much into it, but the Cyclops Tribe has no such power. I've heard they can see somewhat farther than those with two eyes, but on the whole there's no difference in how their sight functions."

"I'll bet! Seeing as they just waved through a carriage this suspicious!"

Subaru, hand on chin, trying to reach some understanding of her own, snapped back at the blunt remark.

Hearing Subaru's shrill protest, the other party gave a small snort, adjusted the angle of the mask covering his face — a red oni mask — and settled deeper into his seat.

The moment they'd arrived at the Magic City, Abel had put the mask he'd brought along back on. Subaru had repeatedly protested that this would make the gate guards and sentries suspicious.

But Abel's mask had stayed on without incident, and the gate guard's reaction had been exactly as he'd predicted — leaving Subaru with a bitter taste in more ways than one.

Of course, that Abel hadn't drawn suspicion was good news in itself.

"Honestly, this is a grave situation. If the gate guards — the city's very first line of defense — are like that, what on earth passes for law around here?"

"I, too, am of the same opinion as Natsumi. Even wearing a robe with sleeves — and still, not a word was said."

"Hmm... that surprise of yours is a bit different in kind from mine..."

Taritta, her cheeks stiff, agreed with him, but unfortunately the culture shock she felt sprang from a different root than Subaru's.

Subaru's stemmed from the city's general attitude; hers, perhaps, from her self-awareness as one of the People of Shudrak.

"—————"

Subaru recalled the conversation he'd had with Taritta around the campfire before arriving at the Magic City.

Since then, she hadn't voiced the same anxieties or troubles again, but that didn't mean she'd been freed of them either. Even if she managed to accomplish the decisive act of succeeding as chieftain, it might be an anxiety that never truly went away.

If at all possible, Subaru earnestly hoped things could reach a resolution that would set her heart at ease — but—

"That aside, we've got the Magic City to deal with."

Having cleared the slapdash checkpoint, the carriage was swallowed up by the disorderly, chaotic city.

From the driver's seat came the shrill, delighted cheers of Medium and Rui, impressed by every little thing, and Subaru too found his eyes widening at the view of the Magic City from within — a sight the exterior alone hadn't revealed.

The first thing that caught the eye upon entering the city was a diversity of races unlike anything he'd seen before.

The Cyclops gate guard had made quite an impact, but once inside the city, Subaru was made to realize his presence hadn't been anything especially eye-catching after all.

Among the more common sights were various Beastfolk — not just Cat Humans and Dog Humans, but races like Rabbit Humans and Lion Humans, clearly divided between small and large builds, passing back and forth through the streets.

A group of reptilian Lizard Humans ran a shop; there were members of the many-armed Multi-Arm Tribe; and a cluster of people with unnaturally long hair, whether as fashion or racial trait, Subaru couldn't even tell.

Then there were races that looked like walking chunks of stone, and chimera-like beings whose bodies seemed part-mixed with another race entirely.

"—————"

At the reckless mixing of races, Subaru remained speechless.

Of course, even in Lugunica's royal capital, there had been sights striking enough to make him instantly realize he'd been summoned to another world. But since then, in the course of learning what he needed to survive in this world, he'd also come to know something of the complicated circumstances surrounding Demi-Humans.

Starting with Emilia, who'd been ostracized for being a Half-Elf, the circumstances of Demi-Humans in this world could hardly be called fortunate.

For better or worse, the custom of keeping what's different at arm's length didn't change even across worlds.

So Subaru had heard that many Demi-Humans whose racial traits showed strongly in their appearance left human settlements and kept themselves small and unobtrusive to avoid trouble.

The Sanctuary, the homeland of Garfiel and the others, had likely been one form of that.

But what of the Chaosflame before Subaru's eyes now?

That such a wide variety of races stood shoulder to shoulder, living without reservation, was one thing — but what struck Subaru most was the way they carried themselves.

Backs straight, every one of them proudly asserted their own kind.

Beastfolk didn't file down their claws or fangs; Lizard Humans didn't polish away their scales; those deemed grotesque or strange didn't wrap their faces or bodies in cloth to hide. — To Subaru's eyes, that looked utterly novel.

"You asked what sort of law passes for law here, did you not."

The one who suddenly spoke up was Abel, seated alone in his oni mask.

While not only Medium and the others on the driver's seat but even Subaru, Al, and Taritta were gazing around with interest, the mask-hidden emperor drew Subaru's attention to his words and continued.

"As you can see, what exists here is lawlessness. If you ask what law passes for law, it is a formless law that passes. One might call it a city of vice, mocking the very essence of order."

"Vice... Way to throw cold water on someone who was busy being moved."

"Moved? You mean impressed. I suppose that's only natural for an outsider such as yourself."

At Subaru's twisted-lip retort, Abel gave a slight shrug of his thin shoulders.

The word "outsider" made Subaru feel treated as an outsider yet again, and he was reminded of their argument in the carriage a few days back. Though, given that he'd been the only one to get emotional, calling it an "argument" would probably just earn him a scoff from the other party.

"But isn't the whole 'disorder itself acting like order' thing basically this city's consistent concept? What d'you say to that, Abel-chan?"

"Question the essence of order, and you'll know whether or not this city is truly disordered. — Where do you suppose the essence of order lies, to begin with?"

"He's hitting me with some Zen riddle stuff... Brother, I pass!"

Subaru shut one eye at Al, who'd given up thinking about it almost immediately.

Still, unlike the pride-less Al, Subaru had some resistance to raising the white flag so easily. Crossing her arms, tilting her head, letting her long hair spill over her shoulder—

"Isn't the essence of order, you know — everyone getting along! Peace!"

"—The essence of order is sameness."

Abel stated this without reacting to Subaru's grade-school-level opinion.

When Subaru frowned at the remark, Abel went on to elaborate.

"That the many share the same values. Doctrine, conviction, purpose, or greed — any will do. It is that identity, in which none among a group deviate from one another, that is called order — not within the individual, but within the collective. And it is upon that foundation of order that the pipe dream you spoke of is built."

"A pipe dream... Y-you think peace is that ridiculous?"

"Conflict is an inescapable human instinct. Even if the weapon for it becomes words or nations rather than swords, its essence doesn't change. But order is an excellent mechanism for shaping an environment far removed from collapse. — Look."

Jutting his chin, Abel drew Subaru and the others' attention out the window.

Staying seated himself, what he pointed to was something one could be certain of without even bothering to look outside — the red castle, symbol of this Magic City.

"As the fool said, this city is laid out on an order called disorder. And even as it becomes a melting pot of many races, the reason the city hasn't collapsed into ruin is that."

"That castle... No, someone inside the castle..."

"—Yorna Mishigure."

Abel's voice struck hard against Subaru's eardrums as he gave a faint shudder.

Abel's expression as he spoke that name was hidden behind his mask. But how did he himself regard her — the most troublesome figure among the Nine Divine Generals, and yet someone indispensable to reclaiming his throne?

The Magic City, where disparate things stood side by side in every direction, and people without unity passed each other by.

The castle at the city's center, its glossy outer walls gleaming with an eerie light, seemed almost to be looking down upon the emperor's party that had come marching right up to its feet.


△▼△▼△▼△


—There was little information Subaru had been given about Yorna Mishigure beforehand.

One of the mightiest in the Vollachia Empire, a member of the Nine Divine Generals, a woman granted the rank of "Seven."

Though she held a general's rank in the Empire and was counted among the emperor's own blades, she was a dangerous figure who had raised rebellion time and again, bringing needless chaos upon the nation.

In place of the emperor, who couldn't be bothered to keep anyone informed, it was Zikr who'd brought Subaru information on the Nine Divine Generals — and the only two whose characters he'd been evasive about were Yorna and Cecilus Segmunt, ranked "One."

Which, in short, meant—

"So basically she's the female half of the empire's two craziest people...?"

Saying it aloud and thinking of what lay ahead, Subaru's false chest grew heavy.

Negotiating with the woman was the immediate trial, but eventually he'd have to face the man too. And in both cases, if he couldn't win them over, defeat was all but guaranteed.

Once he started thinking in terms of "the same," Abel's talk of order came back to mind, but—

"...The one I have to deal with is the one who's about as far from order as it gets, though."

Feeling a vague headache coming on, Subaru muttered this while brushing the hair from her forehead.

Abel's argument had been interesting, every bit the emperor's reasoning, but as for whether it would actually help with the immediate task of winning Yorna over, it wasn't especially useful.

Still, that talk hadn't simply been Abel wanting to sound clever and lord it over Subaru — there was something else he'd meant to say. The order of the Magic City.

Chaosflame looked like it overflowed with disorderly people and things, yet even within that simmering pot of chaos, an "order" existed that kept it from collapsing.

Abel had said that that very order was Yorna Mishigure herself.

In other words, Yorna possessed the ability and brilliance to hold this chaos together as one, keeping it from boiling over the edge of the pot.

That was precisely why Abel, despite her being a dangerous, repeat rebel, had never stripped Yorna of her place among the Nine Divine Generals nor had her executed.

"Still, she's gotta be a piece of work, and you drew the short straw here, brother."

Al laughed, sitting cross-legged at his ease beside Subaru, who was lost in thought like that.

Glancing sidelong at Al's slovenly posture on the wooden floor, Subaru let out a small sigh. His composure was a comfort sometimes, but it depended on the case.

"Al, sit up straight. You never know when, where, or by whom you're being watched. And stop calling me 'brother,' too."

"I'll grant you the attitude and posture thing, but the nickname, huh... What else am I supposed to call you besides 'brother'? Write it 'sisters,' read it 'brothers'?"

"Doesn't that joke only land if the kanji actually gets across?"

There was something heart-stirring in the rich expressiveness of Japanese, but under the circumstances, piling up exchanges that only made sense between Subaru and Al wasn't going to help anything.

What was needed, rather, was a name to use in front of others.

"You call Medium-san and Taritta-san and even Abel with '-chan,' so wouldn't it be natural for you to do the same with me?"

"So, Natsumi-chan? Whoa, goosebumps!"

"Please control yourself! Honestly, no composure at all..."

Subaru scolded Al, showing off the gooseflesh on his right arm, in her role as chaperone.

It was a strange thing to be doing, but seniority didn't count for much in how this group had been picked, so it couldn't be helped. Since Subaru had been given the heavier role, it made sense for her to be the one keeping everyone in line.

After all—

"—We're already inside the Crimson Ruri Castle, after all."

Muttering it just under her breath, Subaru worked to steel her own resolve.

Yes — Subaru's group had already been welcomed into the crimson-gleaming castle at the heart of the Magic City, and had already requested an audience with Yorna Mishigure, the city's ruler.


——


—Once entry into the Magic City was achieved, what followed happened quickly.

They soon found an inn that could stable the Galewind Horse and carriage together, took rooms, and set to work on their objective: winning over Yorna.

That said, what needed doing had already been settled on the road, so there was no hesitation in their actions.

And that action was—

"Deliver this to the castle's lord, Yorna Mishigure. There should be some response from her."

So saying, Abel handed Subaru a letter he'd written.

The envelope holding it was sealed with beeswax, rendering it impossible to open without detection. For important documents, it was customary to press a signet ring or the like into the wax before it hardened, as proof of origin.

However, no such mark had been pressed into the seal of the letter Subaru had been entrusted with.

"Both proofs of my imperial authority that I brought out with me have already been shattered. One by you, the other at Guaral's city hall."

"Ah... the Lifeblood Ritual and the 'Arakiya Panic,' you mean. But with nothing to show where it came from, will she even read it? And even if she does, won't she doubt it?"

"Needless worry is unnecessary. I don't intend to reveal the contents, but I've arranged it so that reading the letter will make clear it's from me."

"I see. ...By the way, wouldn't it be more certain if you went yourself?"

Tucking the received letter into her robes, Subaru asked plainly.

The reason she'd been tasked with delivering the letter was that Abel wasn't coming along to the castle. In terms of proving their identity and circumstances too, having him there seemed like it would settle the whole matter in one stroke.

Wasn't the whole reason Abel had come along in the first place to negotiate with Yorna directly?

Taken to the extreme, if he wasn't going to do that, he might as well have stayed behind in Guaral.

"If anything, I think that would've spared us all the worrying over checkpoints along the way, and made for a much steadier journey..."

"Your insolence knows no bounds."

"Well, you're the one person throwing our whole group out of balance, after all..."

Strictly speaking, Abel wasn't the only one — Rui's presence was also a destabilizing factor.

But this was a moment for taking a jab at Abel specifically, so Subaru set aside, for the time being, the matter of Rui, whose full-time profession was disturbing Subaru's peace of mind.

"I intend to speak with Yorna Mishigure directly whether you say so or not. But it's inconvenient for me to show my face too soon. Use your head."

"'Use my head,' he says... Ah, well, fair enough."

At the displeasure radiating even through Abel's oni mask, Subaru quietly accepted his point.

Now that she thought about it, this was someone who'd raised rebellion against him time and again. From Yorna's perspective, those rebellions must have stemmed from dissatisfaction with Abel's rule.

Naturally, the relationship between Abel and Yorna was like oil and water — depending on circumstances, more like a powder keg with a smoldering fuse. It was a wonder he'd even bothered making the trip to the Magic City at all.

"For now, your task is to deliver the letter to Yorna Mishigure. However, keep hidden the fact that it's a personal letter from me — from the emperor."

"Eh, why? If I don't say that, won't I just get turned away at the door?"

"As a precaution. Once she reads the letter, I doubt she'll treat you poorly, but I can't predict a change of heart before it's delivered. So, use some ingenuity."

"Ingenuity..."

Faced with this unexpected difficulty, Subaru looked at Abel in surprise.

He kept his arms crossed, his expression hidden behind the oni mask, as he said—

"Look at this city, and you'll get some sense of her temperament, and what tips her scales of like and dislike. Put the full extent of whatever cunning you possess to work, and pique her interest."

"There's malice in the way you phrase that!"

"There's a second-best plan too, but I'd rather avoid relying on it. It would leave us without a path forward. Given my objective, the road ahead is a long one. Understand?"

"Honestly, what an arrogant man..."

At Abel's lecturing tone, Subaru twisted her lips, plainly displeased.

At this point, she wouldn't mind teaming up with Yorna to raise a rebellion against Abel herself. Thinking that, it started to seem like she and Yorna could get along just fine.

If the two of them could just trade complaints about His Imperial Majesty, they might become best friends.

"...Well then, I'll proceed along those lines."

"Something seems to have struck you, though it doesn't look like it's anything worthwhile."

Coming from the very instigator of the not-worthwhile thing in question, that carried no persuasive weight at all.


——


—And so, through that chain of events, Subaru's group found themselves inside the Crimson Ruri Castle, Yorna Mishigure's residence at the heart of the Magic City.

Following Abel's instructions, they'd been admitted to the castle without ever mentioning his name.

It had been a somewhat irregular approach, but Yorna's private guard, showing about as little wariness as the gate guard had, let Subaru's group into the castle and told them to wait for an audience with Yorna.

So, inside the castle, Subaru's group was spending time in a spacious wood-floored waiting room until Yorna, the castle's mistress, was ready.

There were no watchful guards stationed in the waiting room; they could have walked out at any time they wished.

The laxness and carelessness of the security had Subaru, an outsider, on edge on their behalf. In fact, things had gone so smoothly it left even Subaru a little bewildered.

"Convenient for us, sure... but if they let visitors into the castle like this all the time, wouldn't assassinating her be child's play, general or not?"

"Yeah, they didn't even take our weapons off us. I about fell over when they said we could bring 'em in as-is."

"Well, we're not planning on causing trouble, so it's fine, we won't get in trouble!"

Subaru and Al were less impressed by the other side's generosity than baffled by their sheer lack of caution. But at the two of them talking like that, Medium, sitting demurely to one side, laughed loudly.

The ones who'd gone in as envoys to the Crimson Ruri Castle were Subaru, Al, and Medium — three in all. The remaining three, Abel, Taritta, and Rui, were waiting back at the inn.

Since Abel couldn't come to the castle, someone necessarily had to stay behind as his guard.

And since they'd wanted to avoid tying up someone's hands babysitting Rui, who was bound to be useless anyway, this was how the split had ended up working out.

"I did consider the angle that the castle's mistress might have a taste for unusual things."

If that were the angle, then the People of Shudrak — who supposedly rarely left their forest — might well have caught Yorna's interest, and Taritta joining the group had been a real possibility.

But since leaving just Abel and Rui alone at the inn simply wasn't an option, they'd ended up saddling Taritta with quite a burden instead.

"Taste for unusual things or not, with that many races already crawling all over the city, I doubt one or two Taritta-chans are gonna register as anything special at this point."

"Yes, I thought so too. Which is why we went with this plan."

"Natsumi-chan, you're really bold! Even me and Al-chin were surprised!"

"Yeah, Al-chin here about jumped outta his skin."

At Al brazenly riding along with Medium's remark, Subaru had a few thoughts about his attitude, but still answered with a bit of a proud "Right?"

Tasked as envoy with delivering Abel's letter, yet told she mustn't mention Abel's name — thoroughly stumped, Subaru had finally found a ray of light.

In fact, it was precisely because that idea had worked in their favor that they'd been granted entry into the castle like this.

"The key to winning her over: 'Abel is infuriating.'"

"Ahaha, Natsumi-chan, you're always snapping at Abel-chin, huh~ I think it's amazing."

"Amazing? Me? Or Abel's rotten personality?"

"Both!"

"Medium-chan's got some nerve herself."

Raising her hand energetically, Medium remarked on the relationship between Subaru and Abel.

The relationship between Subaru and Abel wasn't quite oil and water, but since the two of them had no choice but to walk in the same direction, they moved forward bumping shoulders the whole way.

In terms of similar relationships, it was probably closest to how things had once been between Subaru and Julius.

Though, counting what had happened at the Pleiades Watchtower, Subaru did place trust in Julius. — Not that he'd ever say so to the man's face, or let it show.

"But that's not something that comes easily, either."

Put simply, the friction between them had faded, and one could say they'd opened up to each other.

But that, too, was the result of a long road traveled, of climbing over a fair share of peaks and valleys.

Improving a relationship was never a simple thing.

At the very least, unless both sides wanted to make it better, a rough road wouldn't smooth itself out.

No matter how much one side tried to level it, so long as the other kept trampling it back down, it never would.

"—Sorry to keep you waiting. Honored envoys, this way, please."

That bittersweet train of thought was cut short by the words of a servant who appeared in the waiting room.

The one who appeared was the same girl who had guided them this far — a Deer Human with a large pair of antlers growing from her head, a half-beast whose features were otherwise mostly human aside from the horns.

Still only around her mid-teens, the girl wrapped her slender frame in a kimono-like garment, its long hem trailing along the floor, as she led Subaru's group onward.

Something in her quiet, measured bearing tugged faintly at a memory of something similar in Subaru's knowledge.

Unable to pin down exactly what it was, Subaru let the guide girl lead them up through the Crimson Ruri Castle — up to its very top floor, in fact.

The Crimson Ruri Castle's structure gave an impression closer to the old-fashioned Japanese castles Subaru knew than to the Western-style castles he'd grown used to seeing. Of course, the materials and architectural style used were different, but the way each floor opened broadly to the outside, and the interior design, gave that impression.

That the guide girl, along with a scattering of other staff dressed in kimono-like clothing, could be glimpsed here and there was probably part of the reason too.

In any case, Subaru's group followed the guidance politely and were brought out to the place of audience with Yorna.

"Please wait here. —Lady Yorna will be along shortly."

"Yes, thank you. ...Oh?"

Thanking the guide girl, Subaru, shown into the great hall, tilted her head.

The reason was the presence of other visitors already in the hall. — Besides the three of them who'd been shown in, there were other figures in the room, their backs turned toward the group entering from the rear of the chamber.

That these figures weren't the castle's mistress, Yorna, was obvious from the empty chair placed at the very back of the room.

"And those people over there are...?"

"Others who, like all of you, have requested an audience with Lady Yorna. Lady Yorna is a capricious sort, so she prefers to see everyone at once."

"...I see."

At the girl's matter-of-fact explanation, Subaru gave a small groan, finding it hard to simply nod along.

It was high-handed, or rather, the kind of idea that flagrantly broke with convention. In the first place, even if such an arrangement were set up, wouldn't it just be terribly awkward between total strangers?

The other parties, too, might well have matters they didn't want overheard by strangers.

"Well, if we're gonna say that, we're exactly one of those strangers too, Natsumi-chan."

"...Being called that gives me goosebumps too, you know."

Subaru replied to Al, who'd read her thoughts and addressed her that way.

In truth, though the detailed contents of the letter she carried remained unknown to her, it surely laid out the circumstances of Abel's ouster from the throne and his request for Yorna's cooperation as a foothold for reclaiming it.

Since Yorna's character was unknown, her reaction to the letter was an unknown quantity too. At least, Abel had said that if they could just get to the point of her reading it, she wouldn't treat them badly.

"Please wait inside. Lady Yorna will be along."

Ignoring Subaru and Al's whispered exchange, the guide girl bowed and withdrew.

Of course, stopping her wouldn't have changed the situation. If it was her mistress Yorna who'd ordered things this way, there was no point blaming the girl for it.

"So, what do we do?"

Medium tilted her head, her carelessly-tied hair swaying, hand resting on the twin swords at the small of her back.

For all that she went along obediently with Subaru's group's plans, she was surprisingly reasonable about it. Medium gave the impression of being freewheeling and emotional, and yet she was obedient to whoever gave the instructions.

That was likely the fruit of how she and Flop had divided roles during their travels together as a pair.

At Medium's straightforward, honest question, Subaru rubbed at her eyes, shook her head with a "Well, it can't be helped," and stepped into the room.

"For now, let's try asking for everyone else to be cleared out before we hand over the letter. She might refuse, but given what's at stake, there's no harm in asking."

"If it's the boss lady, there's a chance she just doesn't like it and has your head lopped off, y'know?"

"Please, do not bring up examples like that right now..."

Being handed such an extreme example only raised her wariness toward Yorna, presumably an equally extreme figure.

Subaru hesitated a little before stepping fully into the hall, and Al clapped her on the shoulder.

"Don't worry. Told you, didn't I? I've got your back. Bro— Natsumi-chan's not gonna get put through anything awful, not on my watch."

"Al... You're the one who just made me anxious in the first place."

So saying, Subaru glared at Al's hand resting on her shoulder. Al hastily pulled it back, but for better or worse, the tension had gone out of her shoulders.

Taking Al's sentiment gratefully as it was meant, the three of them stepped into the hall.

Aside from the fact that it wasn't floored with tatami, the atmosphere of the great hall resembled the kind of chamber seen in period dramas, where a lord holds council with his retainers.

The way envoys were seated in the lower seats, waiting for the lord to appear in the seat of honor, was the same too.

"Let's stay over here as much as possible, shall we."

Advancing into the hall, Subaru settled into a spot opposite from where the earlier visitors sat.

It felt odd to position themselves directly in front of or behind the other visitors, so, keeping some distance, each group ended up taking the left and right sides of the hall. Oddly enough, in period dramas, you often saw retainers spread out to the left and right, leaving the space directly facing the lord open — and this ended up matching that same pattern.

Finding the strange coincidence amusing, Subaru glanced over to check on the other visitors.

There were four of them, armed just as Subaru's group was. Among them, one carried no weapon and sat slightly ahead of the other three.

That person was likely their representative, with the remaining three serving as guards, perhaps.

"All of them look more reliable than Al..."

Subaru, glancing sideways at the three guards, kicked Al's earlier concern to the curb without a second thought.

Even so, despite knowing it was rude, she turned her attention past the guards to the person they were protecting.

Wondering just who this person was, and for what purpose they sought an audience with Yorna—

"—!"

In that instant, shock closed Subaru's throat, her cheeks and neck seizing up completely.

A soundless sound escaped her, and in a reflexive response she ducked her face down — at which the sound caught the person's attention, and their gaze flicked briefly toward her.

But seeing Subaru with her face lowered, her body turned toward the seat of honor ahead, the person's gaze slid away, uninterested, their attention scattering elsewhere.

Sensing this, Subaru let out a quiet breath against the explosive pounding of her heart.

Seeing Subaru like this, Al and Medium behind her seemed to find it strange, but soon enough the two of them would taste the same shock Subaru had just tasted.

Because—

"...You have got to be kidding me."

Subaru muttered bitterly — and there, barely five meters ahead, among the visitors waiting for Yorna Mishigure to appear—

—was a man with the exact same face as Abel, who had no business being here at all.
