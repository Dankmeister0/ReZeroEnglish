Arc 5 Chapter 62: "A Warrior's Praise"

"—Gorgeous Taiger?"

The moment that voice reached him, Garfiel's consciousness was violently shaken.
He coughed, choking on the water he'd swallowed in great quantities, spewing it out from every part of his body. Shaking his whole frame to fling off the water, Garfiel forced visual information into a head rattled loose from lack of oxygen.

A dim, cold underground space.
Water was now flooding in great volume across the hard stone floor. From a hole in the wall behind him, a muddy torrent poured into the room, its echo resounding through the otherwise clear air.

Gazes bore down on him. Unease, wariness, fear, defiance—eyes of every mingled color.
From the number of people gathered here and the shades of their stares, some corner of Garfiel's mind concluded that this was one of the city's evacuation shelters.
The waterway he'd fallen into ran adjacent to this shelter, and with the wall smashed open, the two spaces had been connected. As a result, he'd been swept into this shelter along with the water.

"—!"

Garfiel had gotten that far in his thinking when he struck his own hazy consciousness like a blow.
He snapped his head up with a start, and the hair on his whole body stood on end at the memory of how he'd come to be washed in here. Whipping his head around in a panic, he immediately scanned the room for the massive frame that had tumbled with him, tangled together, into the waterway—

"...Ah"

His eyes met a young boy's, golden-haired, his emerald eyes brimming with tears.
A face he recognized. A face bound up with memories that tightened his chest and made his heart creak.

The boy connected to Garfiel's mother, with whom he'd had a one-sided reunion.
His little brother—settled into the place Garfiel himself should have occupied, receiving their mother's love—

"—!?"

The consciousness he'd just beaten awake was once again entangled in needless sentiment.
An instant later, a violent splash erupted right beside him, and out of the shallow water, exploding to his feet, rose the misshapen giant. The huge frame swung up an arm and brought it down without mercy on Garfiel, standing stock-still.

Garfiel reacted to the falling blow a fraction of a second too late.
Too slow. Fatally slow.

A single instant of carelessness hands the opponent a whole exchange's advantage.
And this time, the exchange Garfiel faced from this war god was no half-hearted onslaught.

In total, eight blows rained down on Garfiel.
One, two—he managed to block. But the remaining six struck him true.

His cheek was knocked aside; two blows lifted his torso off its feet. His body, airborne, was hammered down by a doubled hammer-fist, and as his head hit the water's surface, a fist slammed straight down from above.
His face, submerged, was crushed between the fist and the hard floor beneath, his nose and fangs taking devastating damage. Blood gushed out, staining the water red, and as he sprang back up, blood from his nose and blood coughed up from within trailed through the air like threads.

"Bwah—kaff... you baaastard!!"

He roared through the gaps of his chipped teeth, driving away the ringing that still shook his skull from the blow. His fierce battle-cry cracked the air of the underground space, and the war god across from him stepped forward as if welcoming it.
Their fists crossed. Tilting his head, Garfiel let a fist slide past the side of his face and sank his fangs into it, tearing from wrist to elbow in a single motion. At the same time, his right arm shot out toward the huge frame's neck, and his claws gouged a line down to below the belly.

Bright blood sprayed from the sharp gash, dealing no shallow damage to the war god's flesh.
But from here, the war god's attack continued for seven more blows. Against every one of them, Garfiel had to use his entire body to evade.

One exchange—eight hands against his one.
Overwhelming disadvantage, overwhelming disparity in numbers, overwhelming difference in raw power—that was what lit the fire.

"O—OOOOH!!"

Closing in, closing in, closing in, closing in, closing in, closing in, closing in—
Block, parry, dodge, deflect, duck, knock aside, strike back—!

Fist collided with fist, and the resulting shockwave singed the fine hairs of both combatants with fighting spirit.
The head-on collision of brute arm against brute arm produced a roar unbelievable for mere flesh striking flesh, and both bodies, unable to withstand it, were blown backward.

Scattering spray, the great tiger and the huge frame tumbled and rolled.
Kurgan slammed into the wall behind him; Garfiel once again met the water face to face. He snapped his head up at once, and his eyes met Kurgan's glare.

Not a word passed between them. Yet understanding was instantaneous.

Garfiel rose to his feet and stamped down on the stone floor, submerged to the ankles in water.
The power of the *Divine Protection of the Earth Spirits*, felt through the soles of his feet, activated—the floor beneath him was cut into a square section and lifted upward. He kicked the rising slab of floor aside, and the water that had been filling the underground space poured all at once into the resulting hole. The water level dropped rapidly.

While Garfiel worked at this drainage, Kurgan moved toward the hole where the water was pouring in.
The hole that had delivered the two of them underground was large, and the volume of water flowing through it was great. Left alone, the underground space would be completely submerged within mere minutes.
In that setting, Kurgan drew one of his Demon Cleavers. Of those Garfiel had bitten and shattered, several were gone; three remained in total. Raising one of them overhead, Kurgan aimed—directly above the hole in the wall, at the ceiling, where the crude mass of iron plunged in and shattered stone.
The soldier's eye judged the extent of the collapse precisely, and the falling rubble roughly sealed the hole in the wall. Of course, it wasn't sealed completely—water still seeped in—but not enough to fill the space all at once.

The hole sealed, the drainage complete, the water that had reached ankle-height receded away.
Without a word, the two warriors secured their footing and returned to their original positions, facing each other once more. A fist braced with a shield, and three drawn Demon Cleavers.

The hero "Eight Arms" Kurgan, and the challenger "Golden Tiger" Garfiel.
Crushing one's opponent at full strength—that was, by now, an unspoken code between warriors.

"——"

He knew full well this was no time to be doing this.
The role expected of Garfiel was to return to the city hall, which had likely come under attack, and rescue the noncombatants there.
But the question of whether to turn his back or not—he had faced that question long ago already.

—Garfiel, out of place though the sentiment was, felt glad.

Beaten mercilessly by Reinhard, his trust in the mother he'd reunited with sealed away along with her memories, unable to avenge the kind-hearted girl who had shielded him, manipulated by the enemy's schemes into putting his allies in danger—
He had felt helplessness and loss stripping away so much from his own hands.

Leaving the *Sanctuary*, learning of the world, Garfiel had learned his own weakness.
He must surely have been far stronger when he was still in the *Sanctuary*. Of course he was. Back then he'd had nothing to compare himself against, and had held no doubts about the martial arts he'd honed to perfection.

Leaving the *Sanctuary*, learning of the world, Garfiel had learned of many kinds of strength.
He didn't think his own power had diminished compared to his time in the *Sanctuary*. But because the thing he measured himself against was no longer his own mirror image, he had become relatively weaker.

That shift in awareness, and its result, had been driven home to him in vivid, undeniable fashion over these past two days.
Helplessness and loss had stripped Garfiel's heart bare, forcing him to recognize himself as nothing but a mouthy kid. Confusion, regret, and doubt pierced his chest, and his heart wavered, wore thin, and weakened.

—And it was Kurgan who had put heat back into that worn-down, shriveling soul.

The hero "Eight Arms" Kurgan. A hero of the Vollachian Empire. The strongest man of the Multi-Arm Tribe.
That this man had raised his Demon Cleavers and faced him, acknowledging Garfiel as a warrior in his own right—how immense that had been to Garfiel, who had lost sight of his own worth.

Tumbling together into the waterway, unaccustomed to fighting underwater, Garfiel's consciousness had grown hazy. Kurgan, who had returned from death itself through some secret art, had no need to breathe—if he'd wanted nothing more than to settle the matter, he could simply have waited for Garfiel to drown.
But instead, the war god had smashed through the waterway's wall, connecting a path to the shelter, and let Garfiel live.
To what end?

"At first... I figured you were takin' pity on me again."

"——"

At the outset, Kurgan hadn't acknowledged the uncommitted Garfiel as a warrior.
Swatting away a child who came swinging, kicking at one who broke down in tears—that was no act of a warrior. And so Kurgan had simply kept his distance from Garfiel, who gave himself over to tantrums.

But that had changed.
Because he had risen to his feet, raised his shield, and fixed his eyes on him—that Garfiel he had acknowledged as a warrior.
The legendary Demon Cleavers, raised and readied—Garfiel had witnessed with his own eyes the war god preparing to meet a warrior in battle.

After seeing that stance, there was no way Kurgan's conduct toward him could be pity or mercy.
Kurgan wanted something. A conclusion befitting his battle with Garfiel.

—A battle between warrior and warrior has no ending but the one decided by each other's blows.


"Oi, all of you... how much longer are you gonna just stand there?"

Checking the feel of the shields fastened to both his arms, Garfiel addressed not Kurgan before him, but the owners of the scattered gazes around the room.
Ever since the two of them had washed in together with the muddy flood, these people—the refugees—had simply watched this warrior-on-warrior battle from a distance in silence.

A ragtag mix, differing in dress, age, even race. What they shared was a lack of any power to fight—a gathering of feeble noncombatants who'd be swept away by the faintest breeze.
If Garfiel were to fall here, there was no one among them who could possibly stand against Kurgan. It seemed unlikely Kurgan would go so far as to slaughter noncombatants outright, but that was something only Garfiel, standing here facing him, could know for certain.
So—

"You can see for yerselves, cantcha. Standin' back there gawkin' like that, there ain't a damn thing any of you can do. Get yerselves outta here while you still ca—"

"—Gorgeous Taiger!"

"Ahh...?"

A high, piercing voice cut off Garfiel's words urging them to leave.
Garfiel's brow furrowed in confusion at whoever had shouted—and there was only one person present who called Garfiel by that name.

The boy had tears welling in his eyes, his face flushed red, his hands clenched tight around the hem of his own clothes.
And he stared right back at the bewildered Garfiel with those brimming eyes, a look so charged with will that it made Garfiel catch his breath.

"Oi, squirt... what're you—"

"Gorgeous Taiger!"

"——"

"G-Gorgeous Taiger!"

To Garfiel, who had fallen silent, the boy called out that name again, his voice trembling.
As if he knew no other way to express what he felt, he simply screamed that name.

The name of the golden tiger. The name of the strongest tiger, the one Garfiel Tinzel admired.
Why was he shouting that name now? What was he trying to tell him?

Tears trailed down the flushed boy's cheeks.
Everyone underground had heard the boy's cry. And so everyone shared in the wordless, burning emotion carried in that voice.

"I said cut it out and run, didn't I?"

"Gorgeous Taiger!"

Garfiel's breath was drowned out by the voice calling the golden tiger's name.
From behind the shouting boy, a girl with the same golden hair wrapped him in an embrace—his older sister. Holding him as if to shield him, her trembling gaze fixed on Garfiel.
Her lips quivered. A voice that made no sound was calling the golden tiger's name all the same.

"Win!"

Not the boy. Not the girl. And certainly not Garfiel.
A single man among those underground had raised his voice, fist clenched tight.

"No, I said cut it out and ru—"

"Fight, and win!"
"Don't lose!"
"A-all we can do is watch... but still!"

Garfiel was stunned into silence.
Every attempt he made to urge them to leave was drowned out, one after another, by other voices.

Before he knew it, the fervor that had begun with the boy's cry had spread to everyone underground, and not a single person watching the duel between Garfiel and Kurgan moved to leave.
By any sensible, level-headed reckoning, who could possibly think staying here was the right choice? Every one of them was feverish, drunk on it. For the sake of some pride or conviction that meant nothing at all, they'd surrendered themselves to a conclusion that might well cost them their own lives.

"——"

*What the hell is this*, Garfiel thought.
What good could there be in staying here? What could they possibly hope to gain by raising their voices, cheering him on?

It would be so much better if they'd just run. There'd be no risk of getting caught in the crossfire, and the chance of casualties after he fell would drop too—it was, by every measure, the more rational choice.
And yet no one was fleeing. Why was that?

"Boss man... guess the speech worked *too* well after all..."

He remembered the words Natsuki Subaru had called out, words that had reached the entire city.
Drawing in the hearts of everyone across the city, the strength found in Subaru's weakness had firmly lifted up people trembling with unease and fear, lighting a faint, final spark of hope.
That smoldering ember had lingered, turning to heat within their chests, ready to blaze up given the chance.

Just as this place, this very moment, had become that chance for them.
Just as this moment, right now, had become that chance for Garfiel.

"Gorgeous Taiger!"

The cheering did not stop.
Leading the charge in calling the golden tiger's name was the little brother Garfiel hadn't even known he had, born without his knowledge. Holding that youngest brother as if to protect him was the sister Garfiel likewise hadn't known he had.

A brother. A sister. Watching Garfiel.
The city that had taken in the mother who'd lost her memories—its people, watching Garfiel.

"A warrior's duel... bit noisy for what oughta be a proper one-on-one fight, ain't it."

"——"

"Really, my bad. I've been nothin' but trouble for you. 'Specially the two loudest ones there—those are my little brother and sister. I'll set 'em straight later, I promise."

"——"

"So—"

The silent war god's stance took on fighting spirit.
No words needed—that posture alone spoke more eloquently than anything.

He drew his clenched fists together, striking the two shields fastened to them against each other.
The clash of steel on steel struck sparks, and Garfiel bared his fangs and drew a breath.

"'Shield of the Sanctuary'... nah."

"——"

"'Gorgeous Taiger,' Garfiel Tinzel."

The name declared, marking the opening of the duel between warriors.
To Garfiel's declaration, Kurgan gave no voice in reply. The war god only ground his Demon Cleavers together in silence, expressing his utmost fighting will toward his adversary.

That alone was enough.


"Gaaaaah!!"

The stone floor cracked beneath his launching stride, and Garfiel's body shot forward.
Kurgan, likewise closing in to meet him, vanished the distance between them in an instant.

Sharp for a strike, dull for a slash, an assault honed to perfection somewhere between the two, was unleashed.
The air swept aside by the Demon Cleavers was neither crushed nor cut but simply killed, and a warrior's instinct caught the scent of lethal intent in the approaching blade.

Eight hands to one exchange, one hand to eight.
The gap in numbers between Garfiel and Kurgan was equal to aiming for some far, distant peak.
And yet, without laying hand to it, it could never be reached. And so he challenged it, staking everything he had.

"——"

A thrust aimed at his torso—a direct hit would cleave him in two even without a cutting edge. Garfiel didn't hesitate; he raised his foot and stamped the charging Demon Cleaver straight down from above.
His heel bit into the flat of the blade, the thick steel driving into the stone floor, the drilling roar so tremendous it felt as though the whole city shook, or so the illusion went.

First exchange down—but there was no time to breathe easy.

The instant the stamped-down Demon Cleaver embedded in the floor, a second blade struck in an arc from his left shoulder. The instant his right ear caught the whistle of the cutting steel, Garfiel guarded his head with both arm-shields. With not a fraction of an inch to spare, the strike landed the moment he raised his arms, and his consciousness went white.
From the impact, his right arm snapped at the elbow, his upper arm and wrist shattering as well. He clenched his back teeth, cracking them under the strain, and endured. That made two.

The third and fourth blows came bare-handed, both at once.

When Kurgan's huge frame clenched a fist, that fist became roughly the size of an infant's head. Artillery-shell force delivered in an artillery-shell package produced a result that could only be described as dreadnought-class.
Fists that looked capable of piercing stone walls, even sheet steel, bore down on Garfiel, his thoughts still blanked from the blow to his head. Aimed at his torso and his head—either one, a direct hit meant obliteration.

The fist driving into his torso scorched the surface of his abdominal muscles.
So hot was the fist, so overwhelming its force, that his brain half-believed he'd been seared by flame.

He twisted his body against that fist, limiting the damage to a mere scraping of muscle. Three.
His nerves still burning with the sensation of half his body being carried off, he brought his right arm to bear against the fourth blow, aimed at his face. His already-broken, shattered right arm exploded completely under the blow's superhuman force.

From fingertips to wrist to elbow, it was crushed flat, mangled beyond recognition as an arm. The shield fastened at his wrist went flying—but the fist, its force spent, fell far short of a killing blow. Arching his back, he brought his forehead to meet the fist instead. A headbutt crushed Kurgan's knuckles, and the fourth blow was evaded.

Remaining: five, six, seven, eight. Far. Too far. A grin broke out. His fangs trembled.

"—Ohh, OOOOHHH!!"

The fifth and sixth blows likewise came bare-handed. One Demon Cleaver remained—the finishing move, held in reserve.
Two left arms—one from below the shoulder, one from the flank—struck at once. His right arm, which could have been used to defend, was dead. His left arm couldn't keep up. Without hesitating, he stamped forward with his right foot.

The sole of his boot raised a faint spray of water, and at the same moment, his will passed into the earth.
Sometimes drawing power up, sometimes bending it to his will, and now once more, he borrowed its strength—

The footing warped, and Kurgan's heel lifted.
But the war god stamped down on that warp without a moment's hesitation. No wavering in that motion at all. Not the slightest tremor. But the focus of all his nerves had, for that instant, frayed.

In the split second Kurgan's attention split toward his footing, Garfiel dove into that gap.
Raising a foot, twisting his body, he thrust his head into the narrow sliver of space between one sideways blow and the next. Weaving through that storm, he slipped past a strike grazing his torso and another grazing his back.

The instant his feet touched down, Garfiel shuddered at his own judgment.
He didn't even know what had told him he could make it—a decision made in less than the gap between thought and action, in a fraction of a fraction of a second. His brain was on fire. His heart was burning. His life itself felt like it was exploding.

The fifth and sixth blows, neutralized. And then, the seventh and eighth—

"——"

A chill ran through Garfiel, every hair on his body standing on end.
Having had six blows evaded, Kurgan sought to finish Garfiel off with the remaining two. —A killing blow was coming.

—The seventh blow would be skipped; the final, eighth blow was coming.

Forfeiting one exchange, Kurgan hefted his Demon Cleaver.
His right arm gripped the hilt, the arm at his right shoulder clamping the blade itself in a vice grip. The same overwhelming slash he'd used to intercept Garfiel above ground was readied once more.

An aura of total dominance, one that made it certain that wherever he stood, death would find him.
This was the final phase, one so overwhelming it made the six blows he'd risked his life to block until now seem to fade into insignificance.

No vision of evasion came to him.
Step back, leap sideways, press forward—any of it, and the blow would still catch him.
He could vividly, terrifyingly picture himself reduced to a scrap of meat before that single strike.

Evasion, impossible. Intercepting it, likewise reckless. —There was only one choice left: to take it head-on.

Raising his unbroken left arm overhead, Garfiel lowered his stance.
Even now, in this frozen moment of the world, he could still hear the voices. His brother, his sister, so many others, raising their voices.

The judgment took an instant, the action a heartbeat, the result immediate.

"——"

The moment the Demon Cleaver was unleashed, Garfiel was cut off completely from the world.
Sound vanished, color drained away, every extraneous sight was blasted from existence. In that extreme of concentration, the only thing that remained in Garfiel's awareness was Kurgan's presence.

With abnormal slowness, the tip of the Demon Cleaver came down toward him.
He looked up at it, and his own motion, bracing to receive it, was equally slow. In that maddeningly stagnant world, all Garfiel could do was clench his back teeth.
No—there was time enough to lose himself in memory, too.

He saw Subaru. He saw Ram. He saw Mimi, saw Frederica, saw Ryuzu rise to mind, saw Emilia there, saw Otto appear, saw that bastard Roswaal come to mind, saw Beatrice and Petra and everyone from the *Sanctuary*—and then he saw his mother Rishia, and his little brother, and his little sister.

In the battle at the *Sanctuary*, Garfiel had become aware of his own weakness.
Learning the vastness of the world, defeated by Reinhard, Garfiel had labored under the illusion that he'd grown weaker than he'd been before leaving the *Sanctuary*.

—There was no way that could be true.

If a man grew weaker for every thing he came to hold dear, then what was the point of living at all?
He need only resolve to be strong enough to protect all that he carried.

"Ahh—that clears it up."

Something—a seed of worry—dropped free from his chest, just like that.
At that instant, the Demon Cleaver's blow struck the shield on his raised left arm directly, and lightning tore through his entire body.

"—!!"

His left arm's defense shattered in an instant before the Demon Cleaver's charge.
Just as with the destruction of his right arm, his wrist, elbow, upper arm, and now even his shoulder were wrenched apart all at once in a chain.
His two familiar arms were wrecked like something out of a nightmare, and searing pain scorched his vision red and his thoughts white. His mouth fell open, and a scream tore out of him.
His clenched jaw sprang open, and every injury he'd sustained until now joined together in a chorus of despair.

The Demon Cleaver's momentum did not stop there.
Having shattered his left arm, the remaining force bore down on Garfiel's neck. It carried more than enough power to crush his small frame outright, to reduce his entire body, every last part, to scraps of flesh.
To the young warrior letting out a cry near a death rattle, what did the war god feel?

Did he harbor mercy? Did he feel pity? —Surely neither.
Right up until the moment he stopped the breath from a body, there was no reason for a warrior to pity another warrior.

"—AAAAHHH"

A scream of agony, Garfiel's head sagging. The anguished cry trailed on, and then—

"—Ah, ga"

The scream cut off, his jaw snapping shut. His teeth clenched once more, and there, a silver gleam.
The silver shield, fallen from his destroyed right arm, was clamped between Garfiel's jaws.

"Gaaaaahh—!"

His neck snapped upward, and his face, shield gripped in his teeth, cut across the Demon Cleaver's path.
The second block by shield—the instant it took the hit, the shield was crushed flat against his face, blood bursting from the bridge of Garfiel's nose. Fangs flew loose. But his knees did not buckle.

The unyielding strength of his neck and jaw held the Demon Cleaver's blow at bay.
Steel struck steel, sparks flying—flame bursting forth—and Garfiel's consciousness was blasted somewhere far away.

"——"

Half his eyes rolled white, and still, some will had made him tilt his neck. What had it been?
Combat instinct, or perhaps a beast's fighting spirit, or its will to survive.

Suddenly, blood sprayed out. A great burst of bright red blood bloomed across the underground space like a flower.
Its source was Kurgan's right arm—the very arm that had gripped the Demon Cleaver, his last remaining right arm.
There, a wound Garfiel had inflicted in an earlier clash had torn the arm open from wrist to upper arm, deep enough to show bone. That wound had now split open completely in this final blow.

There was no surprise on Kurgan's face. No sign of groaning in pain.
Of course not. He was a corpse. Pain was a faculty granted to the living, a lifeline to confirm the flame of life still burned within—the dead had no need of such a function.

And so Kurgan had overlooked the effect of that compromised right arm.
If he had truly wanted to be flawless, that final blow should have been delivered by his still-sound left arm instead.
Whether that was what decided the outcome—that couldn't be said for certain.
But—

"—Ah"

Having withstood the eighth blow, Garfiel let out a breath through his blood-caked face.
The shield he'd been holding in his teeth fell away. Before him stood Kurgan, every arm spent, fully exposed. Garfiel's right and left arms were both completely destroyed, and both his legs had endured so many repeated shocks that muscle had torn in place after place. Even so—he could still leap, just one more step's worth.

Leap, and do what. His arms were useless. His claws couldn't be used. Then what remained was—

"Aah, aaaah, AAAAAAAHHHHH—!"

Screaming, mouth stretched wide, he lunged at Kurgan.
Into the base of the neck of the standing war god, Garfiel's fangs sank deep. His fangs pierced the taut, hardened skin with ease, tearing out the vital organs necessary to sustain life, root and all.
Still latched on, twisting his body, his fangs caught muscle fiber, dragged it along, gouging away roughly half the neck, devoured whole in the beast's jaws.

"Ga—hah"

Falling to the floor without any grace, Garfiel spat out the flesh he'd torn free. Retching, he turned to look back, and saw Kurgan's back, blood gushing in great quantity from his neck.

Both arms crushed, several fangs chipped away, Garfiel drenched in blood.
And yet, having suffered a fatal wound at the hands of that Garfiel, Kurgan stood there, upright, undaunted—the sheer valor of that standing figure. It was, enough to send a shiver through him, the very embodiment of a hero.

"——"

At last, slowly, Kurgan turned to face Garfiel.
Lying flat on the ground, looking up at him, Garfiel watched as the war god quietly folded his arms.
And then—

"—Well done."

A single word, low and heavy, praised the victor.

"Ah..."

There wasn't even time to respond.
Before Garfiel's widened eyes, Kurgan's form crumbled away in an instant.

The frame, huge enough to look up at, collapsed like sand; the misshapen countenance turned to a mass of ash.
Too abrupt an end, almost anticlimactic—returning the dead to death once more. That was the result.

"...Ain't much of a graceful way to go, is it."

Watching the war god vanish, reduced to ash, Garfiel muttered bitterly.
He didn't go so far as to wish the man had clung shamefully to life. In a struggle over life and death, an abrupt ending was only natural.
So this was nothing more than Garfiel's own useless sentimentality.

"Ahh, damn it... this is bad, I'm gonna die..."

He'd lost too much blood.
Lying flat on the floor, he drew power from the *Divine Protection of the Earth Spirits* with his whole body, gathering mana and channeling it into healing magic to repair himself all over. His arms especially, and his face, were in bad shape.
Even the damage he'd taken above ground hadn't fully healed before he'd gone on to take that much punishment. It would be no surprise if serious damage remained.

"Gorgeous Taiger!"

That crying voice called out to Garfiel as he poured all his effort into treatment.
Splashing through the puddles, running toward him, were his little brother and sister. Others were rushing over too, but to Garfiel, only the two of them were visible.

Both of them looked on the verge of tears—no, they were already crying.
It couldn't be helped. Even to an untrained eye, Garfiel's current state was far from normal. Even to a professional's eye, it was a wonder he was alive at all. Any healer would go pale and declare an emergency treatment regimen necessary.

The border between life and death—proof he'd crossed that fine line.
Of course, there was a part of him that took pride in that—but—

"Still alive, though... can't let this stop me here."

Having felled "Eight Arms" Kurgan, having claimed that victory, he still couldn't afford to stop.
This was Garfiel's battle, but it wasn't his alone. Even while he'd been held up here, there might be comrades caught in dire straits elsewhere.

He had to get back to the city hall—Garfiel forced his body upright.
Hearing that action and those words, the younger siblings who'd run up to him went pale with alarm. His sister especially, her face contorted with fury,

"A-are you an idiot!? Just lie down already! I'll—right, I'll go get someone, a doctor, right away...!"

"There's sure to be others who need a doctor more'n me. I got other things I gotta do, squirt."

Face flushing, Garfiel nodded at his sister as she pressed the point. With his blood-caked face, he must have looked truly wretched. His sister wept, tears spilling in frustration.

Meanwhile, the shattered bones of his two arms were knitting back together. The flesh hadn't fully healed, but he judged it unlikely the impact of standing would send him spiraling out of consciousness. Judging so, he rose to his feet.

"W-wait... a-are you really going?"

"...You heard the broadcast too, didn't you?"

"Eh... y-yeah."

Blood dripping from his fingertips, Garfiel murmured, and received a nod in reply.
That voice had given his siblings courage, and had given him the final push he needed here. So Garfiel needed to answer that voice in kind.
Subaru had said everything would be all right—so he had to make it be all right.

"So, I gotta..."

"Wait a sec!"

His blood-starved body swayed, and his knees buckled beneath him. His panicked sister caught his collapsing body, and Garfiel clicked his tongue in frustration.
Then, this time, his brother stepped up in front of him.

"Gorgeous Taiger."

"...What. Sorry, but if you're gonna try and stop me too, I ain't got time to listen."

"No, that's not it. Gorgeous Taiger, your clothes are glowing."

At his brother's remark, Garfiel looked down and noticed it.
Near the waist of his tattered, ragged clothing, a faint light illuminated the fabric from within.
What he'd tucked away there was the dialogue mirror. A tool he'd stowed away as useless, unable to reach the city hall through it. And if it was glowing faintly now, that meant—

"Thought that thing had gotten busted..."

"I-I'll get it out for you!"

Garfiel let out a ragged breath. His sister thrust a hand into his robes and, before he could stop her, pulled out the dialogue mirror. Light spilling from the mirror's surface—from what he'd been told of its use, that only happened when there was a call from its paired mirror.
Which meant someone was calling from the city hall, or from the other team.

"W-what do I do...?"

"Bring it closer. —Who's this?"

His sister, hesitant, brought the glowing dialogue mirror closer to Garfiel. Peering into its surface, Garfiel called out.
The dialogue mirror began, slowly, to flicker.
