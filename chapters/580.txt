Arc 7 Chapter 75: "I know"

That cheerful smile and voice were painfully out of place against the scene of utter, hopeless ruin.

His kimono was burned to a pitiful wreck, the left side of his face charred black — and yet Cecilus's manner as he peered down at Subaru was exactly as it always was. It was abnormal, past the point of abnormal.

And not just him. Everyone still alive on this island right now was some kind of freak.

The decent, ordinary people were surely all dead by now.
Cecilus in front of him, Todd chasing behind, Arakiya on her killing spree, and Subaru — a mortal sinner.
Every single one of them was so far from normal that they could go on living, shamelessly, like this.
And so—

"Se... shi..."

"By the look of things, you've had quite a time of it yourselves. The Lizard Human gentleman there is dead too — at this rate, I'd guess the Deer Human young lady and the rest of your 'Combatants' friends are wiped out as well?"

"—!"

"Oh? Don't tell me you still haven't accepted that he's dead?"

From the trembling of Subaru's shaking eyeballs, Cecilus read straight through to his feelings.
Anger surged up instantly. How could someone capable of reading minds like that be so utterly incapable of understanding what Subaru was feeling right now?
Hiain was dead — to be told that so bluntly, how did Cecilus imagine that would make Subaru feel?
How could he say something like *dead* so lightly?

"He's dead, thoroughly and completely. ...Though it's not a bad face to die with, I'll say that much."

"He's, dead... it..."

"Are you thinking there's no such thing as a good or bad way to go? I'd say that's a difference of opinion. If there's such a thing as a good way to live, there's such a thing as a good way to die. Let's not be so uncouth as to dictate how the one left behind should feel about it. The Lizard Human gentleman died well. This face is the proof."

"—"

"A face that died having fulfilled its role — I'd like to go out that way myself."

Cecilus rattled all this off, and Subaru had nothing to say back.
Not because he'd been talked into it — it just felt pointless. He didn't want to look at Hiain's dead face, and he couldn't imagine ever seeing eye to eye with Cecilus.
So there wasn't a shred of reason to indulge his reasoning.

"Well now, running into you here of all places — the thing is, I'm rather occupied myself at the moment. I'm in the middle of a duel, much celebrated, with a half-naked woman rampaging around the island."

Cecilus straightened his bent back with a stretch and carried right on talking, uninvited.
Subaru didn't even need to think about who he meant. It could only be Arakiya.

Arakiya, who had begun her massacre at Todd's beck and call. The one saving grace about Todd was his lack of any standout combat strength — but teaming up with her erased even that, leaving nothing but flawless, complete evil.
Subaru had noticed she'd been absent since she started turning the sword-slaves' heads into water balloons — apparently it had been Cecilus holding her back.
Rotten to the core or not, I suppose it takes one Nine Divine Generals to stop another.

"Though as you can see, I'm having quite the tan-colored struggle of it. Ahh, what a thing. Who'd have guessed — someone out there who can actually go toe to toe with me. Here I'd been thinking the road to the Heavenly Sword could only be climbed alone."

"...Why."

"Hm? What's that?"

"Why do you... look like you're having fun..."

It was, literally, a complaint that came squeezed out from the last dregs of his soul.
Yes — it was nothing but venting. In this hellish situation, on this island where Weitz and Tanza and Idra and Hiain, where everyone, everyone had died, Cecilus's laughter was infuriating.
How could he laugh. What in this situation was even remotely funny.

"Why...!"

"Ah, that's cause and effect reversed, you see. It's not that I'm laughing because it's fun — I'm staging a situation where laughing is what makes it fun."

"—Ah?"

"In a world as cruel as this one tends to be, everyone chases after their own ideal of happiness. The method varies from person to person — a thousand different philosophies for a thousand different people — but whatever it is, one must hold to a creed worthy of it. And the creed I hold to is exactly this way of being."

As he spoke, Cecilus touched his own scorched left cheek with his right hand. Charred, crumbling skin flaked away, but even amid what had to be unimaginable pain, his smile didn't waver.
He wasn't relying on some cheap trick of feeling no pain — Cecilus simply kept smiling through it.

"To be the leading man of this world! Rather than bow to the script, let's make the script bow to me. Ask me why I laugh, and that's my answer."

"—"

"Who does one laugh for? For oneself. So that whenever, however, the Observers above may be watching, there's nothing to be ashamed of."

"—"

"Now then, Bassy — what creed do *you* hold to?"

Half his body burned, and still Cecilus's smile never guttered — his philosophy.
It was something entirely beyond Subaru's understanding, and yet he felt something that stayed his hand from kicking it away out of sheer emotion — some strange, faith-like thing.
*Observers* — Cecilus had said that same word before.

As if to say someone, or something, not of this world was watching them.
Even in a situation like this, to be able to say such a thing so casually took bedrock conviction — the kind nobody could ever bend.
And so—

"Oh dear, looks like break time's over."

Even as he said this, watching Arakiya land in the lower-level passage, his smile didn't fade.
Arakiya, wreathed head to toe in flame, a woman surely none of the sword-slaves could have matched — the branch of wood in her hand was snapped, and the eyepatch that had covered one eye was gone.
Hating for anyone to see beneath it, Arakiya covered her left eye with her free hand, twisting a face that was usually so devoid of expression into something wrenched with very complicated emotion.

"Cecilus...!"

Glaring at him with clenched teeth, her eyes wavered with anger and confusion.
Wings made of fire fluttered at her back, their intensity flickering in time with her feelings — swelling, burning, filling the passage with ferocious heat.

The smell of scorched air rose as everything around her caught fire.
The passage's walls and floor, the candlestands and doors, and — Hiain's fallen body, too.

"—!"

"Would burning to death here together somehow make up for that dying face? Now that wasn't like me at all. Stay or go, suit yourself."

Watching Hiain's body begin to crackle and catch fire, Subaru's face went white, and Cecilus's voice dropped down to him. Subaru pressed his arms against the floor and, dragging his body off ground that was already beginning to grow hot, pulled himself to his feet.

His head throbbed. Not enough blood. A sense of helplessness crushing his chest.
And yet, why was he still standing? Why not burn to death here, why not bite down on the *medicine* tucked behind his back molar?

"Well then. Until we meet again on this shore."

Not even knowing why he was running, Subaru turned his back on the burning passage.
He ran, dragging his feet, running at a pace so agonizingly slow.

*If only I could move faster than this — could I have kept Idra and Hiain, Weitz and Tanza, from dying* — even as that regret gnawed at him.

Subaru kept running, kept fleeing.

"—Well now, Bassy's gone off as well."

"—"

"My my, seems I'm rather disliked."

Feeling the presence of Subaru's clumsy, fleeing footsteps fade into the distance, Cecilus gave a wry smile and shut one eye.
The flame-wreathed woman glaring ferociously at him — her anger was clearly aimed at him, though he couldn't quite make out where that anger was springing from.
It didn't feel like she was angry simply because they were enemies, or because he'd gotten in the way of her killing the sword-slaves.
Probably there was some other reason.
Except—

"I have a certain talent for infuriating most anyone I come into contact with, and true to that pattern... I haven't the faintest idea what it could be this time!"

"—! How far are you going to keep clowning around...!"

"Hmm, so you think I'm clowning around. Just for reference, might I ask which part strikes you that way?"

"That! That — what is with your appearance...!"

Glared at with red eyes, his appearance was what she pointed to.
*What is with it*, she asked — though it was she, of all people, who'd given him this nice half-roasting.

"But that's probably not what you mean, is it? Unless I'm much mistaken, you and I had some sort of relationship before?"

"Clowning..."

"I'm not, but since I can't produce proof that would convince you even if I said so, I suppose I've no choice but to insist. Though, thinking about it that way, quite a lot starts to line up rather neatly."

As he spoke, Cecilus nodded, spinning the eyepatch he'd taken from her in battle round and round on an upraised finger.
His memories before he'd started raising hell on the sword-slave island remained stubbornly hazy. Just as Subaru had asked him about before — that much was true. Also true that he hadn't much troubled himself over it. Still, he'd assumed there was probably something to it, and then along came this woman with a face that said she knew exactly what.

If she'd been somewhere in those lost memories, that felt a bit of a shame. An opponent who could fight like this — there'd been some hope in the back of his mind. On the road toward the Heavenly Sword—

"That way... nobody, nothing, ever tells me anything...!"

"Oh?"

But before the pondering Cecilus, she let her voice shake with frustration, mixing a sharp bitterness into her anger and confusion.
The flame at her back shifted from red to blue. Good taste.
Bringing about that tastefully-hued change, she let out a pained breath.

"The princess, too, his lordship, too... they hide things... I'm always..."

"Left behind and it hurts?"

"—!"

Whatever offhand remark he'd made must have struck a nerve, because her expression stiffened.
Looking into those wavering red eyes, something distant and blurred stirred faintly — or maybe it didn't. In the end, he couldn't make out their relationship at all.

"Well, it would be odd for someone in a good relationship to try to kill me, so I suppose it's safe to guess we were on rather hostile terms. Which fits the current situation perfectly!"

"Cecilus...!"

"Long conversations bore the audience. Let's get the stage moving again, shall we. Regrettably I can't offer you any real answers, but maybe this'll ease that knot in your chest a little."

Hiding the half of his body that throbbed with pain, gripping the eyepatch he'd been spinning, he squared himself against her.
Ending the conversation, expressing his wish to converse in the realm words couldn't reach — she went quiet for a moment at his stance, then slowly lowered her left hand.
Beyond it, met by lightless red eyes, Cecilus gave a wry smile.

"Go on, in earnest. — You only ever half-fry your eggs."

"—!"

"Oh? Something just came out, didn't it?"

A single line, reflexively slipped from his mouth — Cecilus tilted his head, puzzled at himself over it.
Except it only seemed to further stoke her fury.

"—"

Fiercely, blue flame surged up and began scorching the island's lower level.
With no time to spare a thought for his surroundings, Cecilus himself became the "Blue Lightning" and dove into the flames.

"Haah, haah..."

Feeling air scorched by fire at his back, Subaru fled desperately from the lower level.
Fleeing, yes, he was fleeing. Not resisting, not struggling — fleeing.

He couldn't call it strategic retreat, couldn't call it a brave flight to fight another day.
Because there was nothing ahead of him this would lead to.

Everyone was dead, and there was no getting anyone back.
Weitz and the others had risked their lives to let him escape, and he couldn't do anything with it.

He'd thought he could handle everything, anything, all of it well.
Really, after pulling through the "Sparka" twice, after rescuing his fellow "Combatants" and Hiain's friends both — he'd thought he could overcome any hardship.

What an absurd delusion. What arrogance.
To begin with, Subaru had died over and over. It had only ever worked out because dying gave him a chance to try again — and now that that chance had stopped working, here he was.

If he were really capable, he ought to be able to pull everything off without ever dying even once.
Just like that terrifying man, Todd Fang.

No Authority to die and redo things, and yet the ability to push through every situation on his own strength alone.
No standout combat power either, same as Subaru — so why couldn't Subaru do what Todd did?
Because he couldn't, Subaru had ended up getting so many people killed—

"Damn it..."

The moment he burst out of the island's lower level and hit open air, the frustration he'd been holding down swelled up unbearably. Subaru slammed a fist against the outer wall he'd slumped against, cursing himself for his own weakness.
Small fists, a feeble body, a head that wouldn't turn, and an Authority that was useless. The reasons he'd lost came to mind so readily there was no end to counting them.

If anything, what *was* there to this rotten little brat with nothing but a bad glare—

"—!"

Stopping in his regret, cursing himself, Subaru's throat choked back a scream at a sudden noise.
A sound, here, now — his whole body shuddered at the worst possible imagining. That Idra, who'd desperately held Todd off, had finally been caught.
But—

"Schu... barz... sama..."

"—Tanza?"

The rasping voice that followed the noise brought Subaru not fear, but astonishment.
The one who called out to him weakly, breath ragged and failing, was Tanza — thrown from the passage above by Todd's own hand, her fate unknown ever since.
She dragged her feet, soaked through from head to toe, having shed her kimono for a white under-robe. One of the two horns on her head was broken, and she was in an utterly battered state.
And yet—

"Schubarz-sama, thank goodness... you're safe..."

Her round eyes wavered with relief, and Subaru tasted a bitterness that went all the way to the bottom of his heart.
Seeing her soaked like this told him she'd been thrown from above, down into the lake — the lake said to house savage aquatic Witchbeasts. Her broken horn was proof it had been no small thing.

It would have been only natural to weep, to wail *it hurts, help me*, and cling to an adult.
And yet Tanza found Subaru and let her eyes crease with relief. "Thank goodness," she said.

Finding this — this Natsuki Subaru who could do nothing—

"—Ah"

That was the moment it happened.
The strength drained from his legs, and Subaru sank right down where he stood. The thread that had been stretched taut inside him snapped, and the frantic noise in his head went suddenly, abruptly quiet.

It wasn't relief at finding Tanza alive.
It wasn't the satisfaction of finding some way to break the deadlock, wasn't fear of Todd finally releasing its grip.

It was only that — he had utterly run out of patience with himself.

"Schubarz-sama...!?"

Seeing Subaru drop to his knees, Tanza's face went pale and she ran to him.
Her gait was strange — she might have injured her leg. Looking closer, the side of her wet white robe was slowly staining red as well.
Wounded so badly, so painfully, and still she worried about Subaru.

The moment he realized he couldn't answer that expectation of Tanza's either — couldn't answer anything — the strength went out of him.
Resignation took over, dominating his chest.

"—"

It wasn't pain. It wasn't fear.
What plunged Natsuki Subaru into despair wasn't a hardship that easy to name.

Being unable to meet the expectations placed on him, the wishes entrusted to him.
That, precisely, was the poison that despaired Natsuki Subaru most of all.

"Schubarz-sama, please, stay with me... The others, Idra-sama and Hiain-sama, are..."

Reaching his side, touching Subaru's shoulder, Tanza asked after the two who weren't there.
She didn't say Weitz's name. Of course she wouldn't. Tanza had watched Weitz be killed right in front of her. And she surely had a bad feeling about the two who weren't here either.
That feeling was unbearably, exactly right.

"Both of them, are dead..."

"—!"

"Idra, and Hiain both, protecting me..."

They'd all died. Not simply that he'd failed to save them — he'd been saved by them.
Weitz, Idra, Hiain — all three had died shielding Subaru. Tanza too, had nearly died trying to protect an immobile Subaru.

Everyone died because of Subaru.
Everyone was killed because of Subaru.

This entire catastrophe on the sword-slave island — all of it — was Natsuki Subaru's doing.

"Schubarz-sama... let's get away from here. We need to hide somewhere, right now."

"Even if we hide, it's pointless. ...We'll just be found again soon anyway."

"Even so! Are we simply going to sit here and wait to be killed without doing anything? What would that make the deaths of all our fellow Combatants mean?"

"—"

Shaking her head, Tanza cried out desperately at Subaru, who couldn't move.
Her voice had gone tearful, but her appeal didn't warm Subaru's heart at all — it only produced a cold, sunken acceptance.

Yes, the three who had thrown away their lives to protect Subaru had protected someone who was useless.
Which meant he'd let their deaths be wasted. All because they'd tried to shield him.

He hadn't lived up to their expectations. He'd even lied to them and deceived them.
He shouldn't have told that lie at all. If he couldn't keep the deception up to the end, if he had nothing to bring back to show for lying at all, he never should have lied in the first place.

If he hadn't, the three of them wouldn't have tried to die in his place.
Not for the emperor's son, not for Natsuki Schubarz — for Natsuki Subaru.

Come to think of it — there was one more person he'd deceived with that same lie.

"Schubarz-sama! You mustn't collapse here—"

"...It's all a lie, Tanza."

"—"

"That thing about being the emperor's hidden child — that's a total fabrication. I'm... my father's an amazing, incredibly cool guy, but... I'm not the emperor's child. Not at all."

Head down, Subaru confessed on his own the lie he'd tried so easily to lean on, the false hope he'd manufactured.
*If only I'd done this sooner* — no, from the very start, he should never have told that lie at all.

"—"

Her round eyes going even rounder, Tanza was struck speechless by Subaru's confession.
Naturally so. In a situation like this, of all the lies to have told — Subaru wanted to curse himself for it. It would have been perfectly reasonable for her to give herself over to fury at having been deceived.
For that matter, it would have been only fitting if Tanza killed him with her own hands.

Everyone had been the same.
By deceiving them, Subaru had stolen from the deceived their rightful chance, their chance to mete out punishment.
So if Tanza were to do it now—

"...Schubarz-sama."

"—"

"Let's move from here, quickly. I'll carry you on my back."

"Eh..."

Eyes shut, braced to accept whatever might be said to him, Subaru looked up at Tanza's words.
Tanza pressed her thin lips together and reached to pull at Subaru's arm. In dead earnest, she was about to carry Subaru away on her back — and at that motion, a "wait" slipped out of him.
He'd told her everything, properly. He'd said it was a lie.

"Weren't you... listening to me!"

"That you are not His Majesty's illegitimate child, I heard that clearly. As for that, later—"

"There's no later or anything else about it!? I have nothing to do with the emperor! Which means there's no longer any reason for you to save me—"

Nothing at all. It was Subaru's crime, dressing up something that never existed as though it did.
And that had cost three people their lives—

"—Schubarz-sama, do you truly believe that?"

The hand touching his shoulder, small — Subaru's motion to shake it off stopped.
Before him, crouched down, Tanza looked straight into Subaru's eyes. He couldn't look away from those eyes, and he couldn't quite make sense of what she'd said.
*Do you believe that*, she asked — but it was all, exactly, simply true.

"Weitz-sama, Idra-sama, Hiain-sama, all of them protected Schubarz-sama at the cost of their lives — do you truly believe the reason for that was nothing but the lie you told?"

"B-because... because I said I was the emperor's child—"

"I believed it too. Everyone did. But do you truly think those three — cowardly, underhanded, lying men that they were — would stake their lives on loyalty to the Empire, on patriotism?"

If Subaru had truly been the emperor's hidden child, that would make him an imperial prince.
So of course three citizens of the Empire would want to protect him. But Subaru chewed over Tanza's words again, slowly, carefully, thinking it through.

Hiain — cowardly, always trying to flee to save his own skin.
Weitz — underhanded, scheming to outmaneuver the others and survive.
Idra — a liar, trying to control everyone by puffing himself up larger than he was.

None of them had seemed like people he could ever get along with. Surely the three of them had felt the same way about him.
And yet, in the end, all three had shielded Subaru with their lives. Protected him. Let him escape.

Why? Because the three of them believed Subaru was the emperor's hidden child.
But what if — what if that hadn't been the reason at all?

"Wh-why...?"

"Because Schubarz-sama is someone who can properly stand alongside those three gentlemen."

"—"

"Talking with them properly, answering them sincerely, truly trying to understand them... Because that's how Schubarz-sama stood beside all of you."

To Subaru, who'd voiced his confusion at not understanding, Tanza said this, still looking him in the eye.
She was trying to explain what Subaru had done, why the three of them had tried to save him — trying to give him the answer.
And yet Subaru couldn't grasp what Tanza meant.
Because—

"That's nothing. That's just normal. There was nothing special about any of that."

Talking with people you cooperate with, trying to understand them — that was just ordinary.
There'd been things he'd failed to do too, of course. Things he'd tried and couldn't see through to the end. But everyone did all of that as a matter of course — standing alongside someone wasn't anything special.

"I understand how they felt. I too was saved by Yorna-sama. I too was stood beside. I'm sure the same was true of all three of them."

"—"

"And that alone is reason enough to stake a life on. ...There are times, I think, when one is made to feel that way."

It was an unfair, unfair thing to say.
If this had only been about Subaru, he could have told Tanza flatly there was no way that was true. But Tanza had shared her own experience — what she had received from Yorna.
Yorna Mishigure, mistress of a demon city home to countless races — as far as Subaru knew, the kindest woman in the whole Empire.

To be compared to Yorna — what an outrageous notion. Far too much of an honor.
Tanza was overestimating him, far too much.
And yet—

"—"

And yet, what about those three — Hiain and Weitz and Idra? What of them?
Those three — Subaru probably knew more about them than they themselves realized.

Hiain's self-loathing. Weitz's false accusation. Idra's fall from grace. Subaru knew all of it.
He knew they'd each had hardships, sorrows, no one to lean on, and that was why they'd all ended up on this island.
He'd thought it was too much. He'd thought those three deserved a chance too.
To die here, and have that be the end of it — even as he'd thought exactly that.

"I wanted to save everyone."

"You did save them. Enough that they'd protect Schubarz-sama with their lives."

"No, that's not — that's not it..."

Maybe Tanza was right, after all.
Maybe, more than Subaru had realized, the three of them had considered him a comrade.
Maybe Subaru had managed to save their hearts.

But hearts alone weren't enough.
Saving only hearts, and calling that seeing it through — he couldn't think of it that way. He wanted to save everything. Subaru wanted to save his allies, the people who were good to him, everyone — body and heart both.

If he couldn't do that, was he even Natsuki Kenichi's son — no.

"—I'm the one who can't stand it."

Wailing that nothing could be done, everything around him dead and gone, a world coming to its end.
Before he knew it, the sword-slave island had wrapped itself in black smoke — an island floating on a lake, and yet burning, burning, spreading, as if trying to bring an end to every single life it had held within it.

"Something like this..."

He wouldn't accept it. He didn't want to accept it. He refused to bow his knee to an ending where no one could be saved.
Making everyone throw their lives away, and having that be the end of it — he couldn't accept that.
He couldn't accept it. So—

"—I don't want to lose to you."

"Winning or losing, none of that matters to me."

Black smoke rising behind him, the man who'd tapped his shoulder with the axe in his hand tilted his head and answered so.

Shouldering a blood-drenched axe, the man appeared with a slow, swaying gait — and for a moment, Subaru nearly mistook him for someone else.
His trademark bandana was gone, his orange hair hanging loose, and the impression it gave was so different from the man he knew.
But those eyes — green eyes that couldn't quite hide their malice — let nothing about his true self be disguised.

"Todd..."

"You've got some stubborn luck on you. The passage caught fire, so it took me a while to catch up. I told Arakiya to use water, too."

"—"

"Seems Arakiya's got her hands full with something too. Useless, when it actually matters."

Todd said this with weary indifference, and with his hair down, there was none of his usual cheer about him. The good-natured-young-man air that had fooled Subaru at their first meeting was gone, leaving only a chilly, parched hostility.
Even that hostility might, perhaps, be a misapprehension on Subaru's part — a fabrication too.
A man who told whatever lies were needed, however many were needed, to use the people around him — a deception fitting of Todd—

"Don't come any closer than that..."

Glaring at Todd as he took a step forward, Tanza snatched up a nearby stone.
With Tanza's strength, unbelievable for a child, even a thrown stone was a perfectly sufficient weapon. Still, against an opponent like Todd, a rock didn't feel like it amounted to much more than a rock.
Sure enough, Todd showed no fear, narrowing his eyes.

"Tenacious little missy, for someone I dropped in the lake. You from the same bunch as those two just now?"

"...If you mean my fellow Combatants."

"Nasty look in your eye. I'm not about to underestimate someone desperate enough to be dangerous, myself. Which means we're both stuck unable to move — a real pain."

As he spoke, Todd raked a hand back through his hair. The gesture made clear that it had been Idra's doing, what remained of him, that had cost Todd his bandana.
Not defeated, not even wounded — just relieved of a bandana. And yet, because Idra had landed that one blow, Todd was wary of Subaru and the others.

"What... what is it you want?"

"—? Just to go home, is all."

That was Todd's answer to Tanza's question, as he stood there having stopped.
Without a trace of mischief or malice showing, Todd stated his own wish plainly. Just wanting to go home — that alone, he said, was his desire.
And probably, that wasn't a lie at all.

"—!"

"Tanza, you don't need to talk anymore. I'll talk to him."

Holding back Tanza, whose eyes had hardened, Subaru fixed a quiet gaze on Todd.
Faced with two battered children, Todd shrugged.

"I've got nothing to talk about, kid."

"Keep me company while you look for an opening. Or are you scared of a half-dead kid?"

"Scared, yeah. A brat who's still provoking me in a situation like this — that's plenty scary."

He didn't bite on the provocation at all, didn't let his guard down one bit, didn't go easy just because the opponent was a child.
Truly, an opponent that was trouble in every way — it started to seem only natural that they'd been driven into this corner. But Subaru thought something else, too. Being this cautious, this calculating, never overestimating himself — a way of being like that—

"You try to do everything all by yourself, don't you."

"—. Can't do everything. I know my own limits well enough."

"You came here with Arakiya, with your allies — and you don't trust any of that at all either."

Nothing in Todd's attitude showed the slightest regard for Arakiya.
It wasn't that he trusted her strength and believed she couldn't lose — it was that, strong or weak, he simply didn't care about her at all.
Not just Arakiya. Todd felt that way about everything around him.

So his actions, his thinking, his ideals — all of it was complete within himself alone.

"I don't want to lose to someone like you."

"This isn't about winning or losing. I don't mind if you win, so long as you're willing to just die."

"My losing isn't just my loss. It'd be Tanza's loss, Hiain's, Weitz's, Idra's — everyone in our 'Combatants'."

That, he absolutely could not allow.
Subaru and the others had fought together, resisted together — the "Combatants," aiming for victory as one.
So they would win. — All of them together, they would go and win.

"—"

His body half-dead, Subaru knelt and pressed a hand to his own chest.
Todd's brow furrowed at the words, but wary of Tanza, he couldn't move carelessly. Of course, if Subaru made any visible move, Todd would have moved to stop it.
But Subaru stayed kneeling right where he was. Didn't move. Couldn't move.

"Todd."

And there was no need to move. — All he had to do was say it.


"—I'm doing 'Return by Death.'"


Stepping over a slight hesitation, Subaru said it.
Fully aware of what would happen once he said it, hand still on his own chest, Subaru put it into words, clearly.
And then—

"...What?"

There was a moment's blank pause, and Todd's brow furrowed deeper at Subaru's words.
Not understanding the meaning of what he'd said, a similar note of suspicion came back from Tanza too. Watching the two of them react, and feeling his own chest, painless — Subaru understood the cause of the malfunction.

Even with his body shrunken, the memory of that agony was one he could never forget.
Speaking those words was meant to bring calamity crashing down on him without fail. A black shadow that ruled over a world with time stopped, an unbearable pain given as punishment for breaking taboo.

Kicking his feet where he stood, laughing uproariously, Cecilus laughed himself thoroughly out, then wiped the tears welling at the corners of his eyes with a finger.

"As if! As if, as if — me, of all people! Me, the 'Blue Lightning' Cecilus Segmunt! The leading man of this entire world! You'd have me court myself and beg to come along, is that it! Well then — please, do let me come along! Now that's — that's simply outrageous, Bassy!"

"...So, what'll it be?"

"Of course I'll come along! I'd like to say it galls me to be taken for such a ride, but the incredible thing is being played for a fool doesn't gall me one bit! Ah, incredible! Delightful!"

Clapping both hands together loudly, Cecilus threw himself with full momentum into Subaru's scheme. Relieved at his readiness, Subaru put a hand to his chin.
In fact, there was something he'd been thinking about since the very start—

"That 'Bassy' thing — it just doesn't sit right with me at all."

"Muoh, a headwind! Having said I'll follow you, it'd hardly do to break down over this, but might I ask for an alternative, some proposal for what to call you instead?"

"Let's see..."

At Cecilus leaning eagerly forward, Subaru thought about it a moment.
*Bassy*, he mouthed a few times, getting the feel of it, and then a thought occurred to him — one that, oddly enough, didn't seem half bad. Subaru grinned a wicked grin.

"—Boss."

"—"

"From now on, call me Boss."

He hadn't meant to sound cool about it, but going along with Cecilus's storybook sensibilities turned out to be surprisingly not so bad.
At Subaru's answer, delivered in just that tone, Cecilus let out a "hoo" of a sigh and said,

"Boss, Boss, Boss-Boss-Boss... now that has quite the ring to it! I haven't the faintest idea what it means, but I don't dislike it — in fact, I rather like it."

"It means the head of the gang. Fitting, right?"

"Yes indeed, Boss! Well then, shall we get started!"

Laughing brightly, Cecilus spoke with real vigor, and Subaru nodded, "Yeah."
Then Subaru looked around at Cecilus in the lead, at his fellow "Combatants" Hiain and Weitz, Idra and the other sword-slaves, meeting the eyes of Gustav and the warders he led as well.
After such a fierce, fierce ordeal, standing united at last with his comrades—

"—Let's go, everyone! Off to the capital, to punch that bastard old man of mine right in the face!!"

"「「Ohh——!!」」"

At Subaru's rallying cry, the sword-slave island itself roared back its answer.
Feeling the whole lake the island floated upon shudder with the force of it, Subaru suddenly noticed Tanza's gaze fixed steadily on him.

"Don't worry, Tanza."

To her — the one person who truly knew Subaru wasn't the emperor's hidden child — Subaru gave a firm nod, and steeled his resolve to make straight for the capital from these western lands, all in one push.
He would push forward, as Natsuki Kenichi's son, as the self his comrades believed in.

And so, from this moment on, Natsuki Subaru—

"—This time, we're the ones who win."


—Having been summoned to another world, he would go on to trample the Vollachian Empire underfoot as its strongest being.
