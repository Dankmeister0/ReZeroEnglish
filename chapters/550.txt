Arc 7 Chapter 52: "This Love Cannot Be Yielded"

—She was using the Soul Marriage Technique on the castle itself, running Od through even inanimate objects.

That, surely, was the truth behind the outrageous phenomenon of Yorna standing on a spiraling, swirling foothold of roof tiles.

Abel had said it was already abnormal enough that her Soul Marriage Technique was cast on at least the hundred people who'd come chasing after Subaru and the others — but this was on a whole different level.

If she was sharing a portion of her Od with even her own castle and her pipe...

"That has to be terrible for her body...!"

To begin with, the very act of handing mana over to someone else was exhausting enough on its own.

In fact, Subaru — who regularly exchanged mana with Beatrice under their Contract — knew all too well, down to his bones, just how draining that could be.

Crossing the sea of sand to reach the watchtower, for instance — necessary as it had been — had left him utterly wrung out after Beatrice drew off a considerable amount of his mana.

And that trouble had come from being connected by Od to just one person, Beatrice. By simple arithmetic, the risk in Yorna's case was over a hundred times greater.

On top of that, she still had the raw ability to wage an absurd battle entirely on her own.

Told that she was secretly worn out from sharing her power with everyone, one might have believed it — if she hadn't just split a castle open with an axe kick.

"Hah, quite the strange technique yer usin'. Tch, no matter how long a man lives, there's always some new trick he's never seen before poppin' up — gives an old man a real headache, it does."

"Such is the soil we live in, I dare say — no use lamentin' it. From where I stand, Old Man Olbart's own arts are troublesome enough... best I snuff you out here and now, I should think."

"Ka-ha-ha-hah! Not so easy to snuff out as all that— whoa!?"

Olbart, laughing with his mouth wide open, reacted to the sudden hostility and leapt backward.

In that instant, the roof beneath Olbart's feet burst apart, the tiles blasted upward by a shock from directly below — as if in retaliation for the very attack that had launched Yorna skyward moments before.

And unlike Olbart's single blow, Yorna's came in a relentless barrage, chasing after him.

Boom, boom, boom — the shockwaves chased after Olbart as he tumbled backward through the air. And Yorna achieved all of it with nothing more than a graceful flick of her fingers, standing atop her tile foothold fixed in midair.

"Hup! Hoh! Hah! Now yer really doin' it!"

"Old Man Olbart is a persistent one, I dare say."

"That's the kinda thing you say about a gnat, girlie — you don't know a thing about dealin' with old men. But still—"

He cut himself off there, twisting his body as he tumbled backward. Still upside down, Olbart turned to look behind him, only to be met by the roof rising up in a rolling wave.

Shockwaves pursuing from the front, a wave of roof tiles crashing in from behind — caught in a pincer, the sight of the castle keep rippling like the open sea was enough to make Subaru, watching it head-on, doubt his own eyes.

But what happened next was just as hard to believe.

"——"

Seeing the roof tiles surging toward him like a great wave, Olbart kicked off the air and dove headfirst straight into them.

For all it looked like a wave, it hadn't lost the hardness or weight of the tiles that made it up.

Diving in headfirst should have meant a full-body beating, broken bones, a crushed skull — a bloody disaster. Without thinking, Subaru covered the face of Rui, who was clinging to him, and heard her voice of protest: "Uu!"

But that gruesome outcome never came.

"Just a little twist on my earth-divin' technique — pretty impressive I could pull it off on the fly, ain't I?"

At the moment of impact, Olbart slipped soundlessly into the wave, as if diving into water, and shot straight out the far end of the surging roof-wave. Scraping his heels along the bare, tile-stripped roof, Olbart bared his white teeth in a crafty grin, acting as though he had plenty of room to spare.

In truth, Olbart hadn't shown the bottom of his abilities.

Yorna, for her part, may have revealed the terror of her Soul Marriage Technique, but—

"While you go on fleeing about like that, tell me — will Old Man Olbart's aim ever be achieved?"

Looking down serenely from the sky upon the strange old man, she herself hadn't taken so much as a scratch either.

Neither of them had shown more than a fragment of their true strength. Even so, that alone was more than enough to prove just how terrifying the power of the Nine Divine Generals, the Empire's greatest fighting force, truly was.

Still, there was a sense that if the fight went on like this, neither side would yield, and the deadlock would simply continue.

And it seemed Subaru and the others watching from the sidelines weren't the only ones with that impression.

"Yare yare, that's a painful thing to hear. But then, if 'Three' gets bogged down strugglin' against 'Seven,' can't say I'd blame His Excellency for bein' sore about it."

"Then what shall you do about it, I wonder?"

"Hmm, now that's the question. Well, it ain't like I've just been runnin' around aimlessly this whole time either..."

Yorna asked with her fine brows furrowed, and Olbart stroked his own chin. As he stroked it, the strange old man twisted his cheek in a grin and let his gaze slide briefly toward another direction.

Those glinting, gleaming yellow eyes turned toward the two children watching the battle from the sidelines—

"—!"

"Old man!!"

Olbart's gaze sharpened in an instant, and Subaru flinched, shrinking back.

An instant later, Yorna's brows shot up as she kicked off a tile and hurtled at Olbart with furious speed. Just as in her opening move, a ferocious blow from Yorna came raining down on him once more.

But—

"That's exactly why, for all yer rebellin', you've never once laid a hand on His Excellency."

Olbart spat the words out, his leg — laughably short next to Yorna's — snapping upward. But the sole of his shoe collided head-on with the thick heel of Yorna's sandal, and the resulting shockwave sent both their long hair whipping wildly.

Yorna's teeth ground together, Olbart clenched his jaw, and a second later both their bodies were sent flying in opposite directions — Yorna and Olbart, forward and back respectively.

The moment he saw the distance between them widen, Olbart plunged both arms into his own sleeves and pulled them back out — and between the fingers of each hand were four small, round black lumps.

"Ration pills!?"

"Sure looks that way, don't it? But eat one o' these and you're in for a real bad time. See, they're packed solid with crushed fire mist-stones."

Hearing Subaru's scream, Olbart smirked and swung both arms out wide.

If his words were to be believed, those black orbs were bombs. Flung by the force of his swing, they arced toward Yorna, who'd left herself wide open— no, that wasn't it at all.

"Last time there was a rebellion, the folks I tangled with were who they were, right? Wouldn't have pulled somethin' like this on them — makes it feel kinda fresh, don't it?"

With his cheek twisted into that grin, Olbart hurled the bombs from atop the roof out into the wide-open sky.

It wasn't a throw that seemed to be aimed at anything in particular — and that was exactly the point. To Olbart, it was enough for the bombs to simply land wherever people happened to be.

That alone was enough—

"From what I hear, the folks in your city are supposed to be pretty tough... but are they tougher than the folks back in my village, I wonder?"

"You wretch!"

The bombs were small, but there was no telling what power they held within.

Nor was there any telling how many people in the city were bound by Yorna's Soul Marriage Technique. In short, leaving those bombs alone would be far too reckless.

Yorna reached the same conclusion in an instant. And so—

"Haaaah, aaaaaah!!"

Stamping her foot down hard enough to shatter the roof, Yorna reared back her arm in a great swing. In her hand was the pipe she'd already used to strike at Olbart.

The arm holding the pipe followed through in a full swing, and at the same time, the smoke spilling from the pipe's tip traced the very same arc — the purple smoke, given mass and weight, flashed across the sky of Chaosflame like a horizontal slash of a blade.

—That mass-laden purple smoke caught every single one of the scattered bombs, without exception.

An instant later, a chain of tremendous explosions went off, the roar and the shockwaves painting the sky above the castle. One of the blasts went off right beside Subaru and the others, and it left him blinded, unable to stay on his feet.

"Guh—"

When an explosion happens, you're supposed to cover your ears and open your mouth — Subaru thought he remembered seeing that on TV or in a book somewhere.

Having failed to do either, his body took heavy damage as a result. His head rocked, and his vision flooded red. That was how far outside the norm Olbart's bombs were.

Carrying around that many of those things — there was something seriously wrong with Olbart.

And the fact that he'd tried to scatter them mercilessly across the whole city, without hesitation, made that all the more obvious—

"Uwau!"

A cry rang out, and Subaru's shoulder was shaken by a small hand.

Looking over, what came into his blurred, blood-red vision was Rui, her face drained of color. Her mouth trembled, and tears welled up in her blue eyes.

Witnessing that, Subaru tried to answer, "I'm okay," but—

"—Ah."

—At the edge of his red-tinted vision, he caught sight of Olbart appearing behind Yorna.

"——"

Occupied with dealing with the thrown bombs, Yorna was still frozen in the follow-through of her pipe swing. Olbart, who had appeared right behind her, was drawing back his arm to strike with a knife-hand blow.

His target was the dead center of Yorna's back — the kind of spot that, in a comic book, would pierce straight through the heart in a single blow.

Whether something that comic-book-like would really happen, Subaru didn't know. But he'd seen more than enough in this fight so far to know that both Yorna and Olbart were superhuman monsters with comic-book-level power.

At this rate, Yorna would die. So—

"Rui—!"

Grabbing the hand of Rui perched on his shoulder, Subaru pointed toward Yorna and the others.

The procedure he'd drilled into her thoroughly before entering the castle went into motion — Rui's big round eyes went wide, and the next instant, Subaru's vision flashed white, and a teleport occurred.

And then—

"Ah."

Without even a moment to catch his breath, Subaru let out a stunned sound.

The reason was simple: even his blurred red vision was swallowed up by a sudden lukewarm sensation. He understood on instinct that it was because some kind of liquid had suddenly been splashed across his face.

He tried to wipe the sensation away with his arm, and that was when he noticed something wrong.

The arm of Rui he held was far, far too light.

"What was that just now? You two were supposed to be collapsed over there, weren't ya?"

Olbart's voice, puzzled, reached him, and Subaru lifted his dumbstruck face. In his line of sight was the strange old man, head tilted, in the middle of shaking his hand vigorously.

In his red-tinted vision, he shook an arm that was somehow even redder still.

"Rui...?"

Watching that, Subaru called out Rui's name and gripped the hand he held all the tighter.

He thought they needed to get away from here right now. It might take two jumps, maybe more. He'd probably end up throwing up, but that was something to just bear through.

He'd bear it, so they needed to get away from here right now.

So—

"Rui! We have to hurry—"

"Nah, that'd be askin' too much of the poor thing. She ain't got a head above the neck no more, y'know?"

"—Eh?"

Faced with those words, said so blithely, Subaru couldn't grasp their meaning.

Having shrunk down, his brain grown young along with his body, maybe that was why he couldn't understand. Told something incomprehensible, his small brain refused to understand it, and so.

And so, even being told there was nothing above the neck—

"How dare you... how dare you do such a thing!!"

An instant later, along with that furious voice, something tore straight through the air above Subaru's head, and Olbart leapt hard to the side to dodge it. A shockwave shot in a straight line right through the spot where Olbart had just been standing, and the castle keep — only just repaired — was wrecked all over again.

But Olbart didn't so much as flinch at a blow of such terrible force,

"Ooh, scary, scary. But you oughta be thankin' me, fox girl."

"What did you say!?"

"If that boy and the little girl hadn't butted in, it'd be you lyin' dead right now. Though, at this rate, it's just a matter of time anyway."

"——"

Wagging a raised finger, Olbart went on in that same easygoing tone.

Yorna held her breath, and Subaru could sense her attention slip away from the strange old man, turning toward him instead. But Subaru had collapsed right where he sat, and pulling the too-light arm of Rui toward himself was all he could manage.

"R-Rui..."

His vision was red, and he couldn't make out Rui's form properly.

Whether this was some abnormality in Subaru's own eyes, or because Rui's form itself had turned entirely red, he couldn't even tell the difference.

Only one thing could be said for certain. — Rui had died.

Even though Subaru hadn't come to any answer at all about what to do with her, what he should do.

She had acted exactly as Subaru told her to, and as a result, she had died.

Subaru had let her die.

"Child! Child, look at me, I say!"

With a firm tug, Subaru's downturned face was lifted, both cheeks caught between a pair of hands.

Right in front of him, in his red-tinted vision, was the face of a beautiful woman. That he hadn't recognized her as Yorna at first glance was because the expression on that face was one of anguish.

It was unlike the confident face she'd shown Subaru and the others, and the fake emperor's group, the day before.

It was unlike the gentle face she'd worn today, trying to treat Subaru and the others with courtesy.

Right now, Yorna's eyes wavered, gazing at Subaru with obvious pain.

The reason for that finally became clear to him, reflected in Yorna's eyes right before him.

"—Ah."

Subaru, down on his knees, had a face in a terrible state.

His left eyeball had burst a deep red, and his right eyeball had spilled out of its socket, trailing strands. Because he hadn't been able to absorb even a fraction of the blast's force, his eyes had been forced right out of their sockets.

It looked almost like the cheap special effects of some bad zombie movie — almost funny, in a way.

"Hih."

Understanding his own situation, Subaru let out a breath, and the vision from his one remaining left eye swam. But Yorna's hands wouldn't let him collapse.

Yorna, her lips trembling, murmured "Child..." as she stared into Subaru's face, and then—

"—Love me. Right now, this instant."

With that, she made an unreasonable demand, her eyes utterly earnest.

Love her, and right this instant, no less — it was a completely unreasonable thing to ask. To begin with, he had no idea what exactly he was supposed to do, and loving someone wasn't something simple.

Yorna, he thought, was a good person, and beautiful too, but even so.

"Child! If you love me, that wound of yours will—"

"Ka-ha-ha-hah! Oi, oi, that's a real outrageous thing to be sayin', girlie, truly. Dunno much about it myself, but ain't love somethin' that don't just spring up so easy?"

"Silence!"

She snapped at Olbart, who kept butting in with jibes from behind, and Yorna's eyes took on a deeper hue.

Watching that up close, Subaru kept breathing in hoarse gasps, beginning to brace himself for the slowly approaching wave of pain.

Even with one eyeball burst and the other popped out of its socket, it wasn't an injury bad enough to kill him.

Unlike Rui, he still had everything above the neck. Which meant that what was coming next would hurt like nothing else. He'd surely end up howling and screaming, causing Yorna a great deal of trouble.

He couldn't cause anyone any more trouble than this—

"Child."

Called by that single word, Subaru's consciousness flickered back up for a moment.

As if seizing that one instant of opening, Yorna's earnest face drew closer.

"——"

Yorna's lips met Subaru's.

A soft sensation pressed against his face, and he felt her breath right up close. It took him a while to understand that he'd been kissed.

Partly it was because he'd shrunk small, and to begin with, he had little experience with this. What scant memory he had of it lay far, far away, and he remembered being deeply moved by it — but this, this was—

"My lips do not come cheap. Now, with this..."

The kiss over, Yorna pulled her face back, and her words trailed off as if clinging to something. But even so, Subaru had a vague sense of what the rest of that sentence would have been.

What she'd wanted to say was this: now, could he love her?

If Subaru loved Yorna, the situation would change.

That, surely, was the source of Yorna's power — something like the very cause of her strength.

But even so—

"There's... a girl I love, already..."

Beyond his blood-red vision, far away, the face of the girl he loved rose up in his mind.

Seeing her smile made his heart race. And that was why Subaru couldn't love Yorna.

And because he couldn't, hearing that made Yorna's face tighten with a look of such terrible pain that it hurt to see.

"—Well, matters between a man and a woman never do run so smoothly, do they."

In that instant, along with a voice that sounded almost disappointed, Subaru's vision changed.

"——"

Yorna's face, which had been right in front of him, vanished, and all he could see was a sky stained red.

His vision shifted abruptly, just as it had with Rui's teleportation — no, but it was different from hers.

With Rui's teleportation, the world would shift into something entirely different, vividly, in a single instant.

But what was happening to Subaru now was much rougher, cruder than that. His vision spun round and round, without the clean break of a teleport — what he saw remained properly, continuously connected.

Just below him were Yorna kneeling and Olbart standing before her. Beside Yorna lay the collapsed body of a girl — presumably Rui.

And there was one more thing — between Yorna and Olbart, something, someone, stood.

It was—

"—Ah."

—it was the body of a child with nothing above the neck: the small body of Subaru himself.

"Yare yare, killin' a kid sure takes it outta you mentally. Though physically, it's easy enough, ain't it?"

"Old man—!!"

With a scream that seemed to tear blood from her throat, Yorna's body slammed into Olbart. He met her head-on, their bodies colliding, and Kōruri Castle shook violently.

It shook, cracked, crumbled, and once again the destruction spread and spread — what became of it after that,

Subaru didn't know.

Nothing at all, anymore. And not knowing, Subaru's head bounced along the rooftop—

—bounced, and—

—He was dragged down into a dark, dark space.

"——"

Nothing there, floating, no limbs, incomprehensible, no sky and no ground, drifting — a space with no shaking, no resisting, no tasting, no trembling in fear.

As if seen countless times before, as if seen for the very first time,

as if slowly drawing away, as if endlessly drawing near.

In that space ruled by a blackness so complete it could almost be called the Everlasting Darkness, something echoed.

Echoing — a sob, someone's crying voice, a voice that struck at a chest that did not exist.

'I love you.'

It was a word of love.

A voice that pleaded for love, begged for love, demanded love, surrendered love, loved love itself.

'I love you. I love you. I love you.'

Love, repeated over and over — words that should, by nature, carry all manner of emotions within them.

Joy, or anger, or sorrow, or delight — a whirlpool of feelings like that.

And yet, the love that filled this place, this Everlasting Darkness, to overflowing, was only ever one single thing.

'I love you. I love you. I love you. I love you. I love you. I love you. I love you. I love you. I love you. I love you. I love you. I love you. I love you. I love you. I love you—'

Into every part of that emotion, every part of that swirling love, only 'sorrow' had been packed.

Sadness, sorrow, grief — grieving so sadly, sadly, it made the love itself weep.

The sobbing wouldn't stop.

A sobbing that made you want to die just from listening to it — and it wouldn't stop.

Grieving over love, love itself sorrowful, love itself sorrow, love above all grieving so sadly.

Sobbing all the while, the love that loved love grieved over its own grief, and in the end, it came together as one:

'—Where are you?'

"From what I hear, the folks in your city are supposed to be pretty tough... but are they tougher than the folks back in my village, I wonder?"

"You wretch!"

His spinning, whirling red vision flipped over, and what struck his eardrums was a voice tight with urgency.

One was an old man's voice, the other a woman's — Olbart and Yorna, he could tell.

That much he understood. That was as far as his understanding went.

"Eh..."

"Haaaah, aaaaaah!!"

Right after he let out that stunned sound, a heavy boom rang out, and Yorna's foot smashed hard into the castle roof. At the same instant, she reared back with the golden pipe held in her hand.

The pipe swept out in a great horizontal arc, and with tremendous speed and destructive force behind it, spread purple smoke wide across the sky of Chaosflame.

Subaru already knew what this was for, and what was about to happen.

He knew — but it happened before that knowledge could catch up with reality.

—The bombs, struck by Yorna's purple smoke, exploded, and the shockwave swallowed everything around them.

"Guh—"

His whole body bathed in the exact same impact as barely a minute before, Subaru's vision flooded red.

He thought he heard a small cracking sound, and it connected instantly with the redness flooding his sight. His eyeball had burst with an audible pop. That was why his vision had turned red.

So then, why had the other half of his vision, the other side, gone dark—

"Ah, guh, gyaaaaaaaah—!!"

Covering his face with both hands, Subaru screamed, struck at once by pain and by "understanding."

He'd "understood" it. That one of his eyes had burst, and the other had popped out. As long as his brain had refused to understand, he'd been able to ignore the pain.

But once he already knew from the start what had happened, there was no ignoring that pain.

"Aaagah! Agi, gigigiaaah!!"

"Uwau! Uwauu!"

Falling backward onto the spot, Subaru wailed, and a small body clung on to him.

His name was being called. He couldn't tell. His ears were ringing, and his own voice was too loud. The pain, the sound of blood pulsing, thudding — he couldn't even tell whether that blood was flowing inside or outside his body. It hurt, it just hurt. Hurt hurt hurt hurt, it hurt.

Somebody, help. He didn't want the pain. He hated the pain. The pain, he hated it.

It hurt, it hurt so much, it hurt hurt because it hurt so it hurt, hurt hurt hurt hurt hurt—.

"Chi—"

"Oi, oi, that's a bad move."

"—!"

The pain spun round and round inside his skull, and his red vision glared, blazing.

Close beside him, a hand held down Subaru as he tried to thrash and roll, and it was in the way. He wanted to scream louder, thrash around more, let the pain escape out of his body.

No, it was his face that hurt. His eyes and ears. So if somewhere else hurt instead, if this pain would just go somewhere else—

"Uwau! Awa—!"

He slammed his head against the ground, again and again. A strong force hauled him up. Pinned from behind in a full nelson, he couldn't move. It hurt, and the pain was terrifying. No, scary, he was going to die.

"Guh..."

"Ha—for real, fox girl? Not dyin' from that — what the hell kind of body is that. Don't tell me that's the same reason Cecilus said he couldn't cut off her head."

"...I hardly see why I should reveal a maiden's secrets to some brute who lays hands on children."

Somewhere far away, someone was arguing. But sandwiched between his own voice and the voice trying to hold him back, he couldn't hear any of it. He couldn't understand. The pain was to blame for all of it.

Yes, all of it was to blame. The red, the pain — all of it was to blame. All of it, because of that.

"It's because of that...! That thing, that, that's what's wrong! It's not me! It's not me, but, ugh, giiiii!"

"Uu—!!"

Thrashing and struggling, Subaru tried to push it all off onto something else. Push it off, and if he could escape from there, what then?

That was something to think about after escaping. He'd think about it after escaping, so, so.

"All that wailin' — can't stand the sound of a kid cryin', y'know. Shut it already."

"Wait—!"

A woman's desperate voice, and a careless, hoarse voice.

After hearing that, something drew nearer with a rasping sound. From behind his head, whoever was holding him in that full nelson pulled him into a tight embrace.

But an embrace alone could do nothing.

Because once again, a red-hot burst of light swelled up and swallowed Subaru and the other along with it.

Swallowed whole, the pain vanished, and then—




"From what I hear, the folks in your city are supposed to be pretty tough... but are they tougher than the folks back in my village, I wonder?"

"You wretch!"

In an instant, the voice he'd just heard moments before sounded again, and his consciousness went white.

"Eh...?"

Everything cut off abruptly, all at once, and the moment after it snapped off, playback began again.

Color returned to his all-red vision, the sound of wind reached ears that had gone silent, he felt the soft warmth of a girl's body right beside him, and then—

"Haaaah, aaaaaah!!"

A woman's valiant voice rang out high and clear, and in that instant, an arm swung with tremendous force.

The result traced out by that swinging arm was already something he knew all too well.

—A chain of thundering explosions, and the impact that painted his vision red.

"Gi, aaaaaah—!!"

Covering his face, tasting the pain of one burst eyeball and one popped-out eyeball, Subaru reeled backward and collapsed onto the roof, screaming.

"Uwau! Uwaauu!"

Onto his fallen body, a voice on the verge of tears leapt, clinging to him.

Feeling that, hearing a world drawn taut with a ringing sound, lamenting his pain and suffering, Subaru's mind was painted over entirely with tears, fear, and a single "why."

Probably, he'd died. He had died.

He could tell he'd died. It hurt so much, it was so awful, and then he'd been pulled away from it. But right away he'd come back to this same place again, and died again, and yet, it hurt, the explosion was red.

Even knowing all that, Subaru kept repeating "why" over and over in his head.

—This is wrong.

Vaguely, that feeling echoed inside his head, on the verge of being shoved aside by the pain and the redness. But this was a feeling he mustn't let go of — an absolute certainty.

His eyeball burst, his eyeball popped out, his eardrum ruptured, it hurt so bad he wanted to cry, and right after this he'd die a horrible death all over again — he almost wished he could just be carried away from the pain like that instead — and streaming tears, snot, drool, and urine all at once, with a desperate mind, he thought this:

"Ah'b... wong...!"

"Uwau! Awa, uu!"

"'Iss... nod mine...!"

Wrong.
Wrong wrong wrong.
Wrong wrong wrong wrong wrong.
Wrong wrong wrong wrong wrong wrong wrong wrong wrong wrong.
Wrong wrong wrong wrong wrong wrong wrong wrong wrong wrong wrong wrong wrong wrong wrong wrong wrong wrong wrong wrong wrong wrong wrong wrong wrong wrong wrong wrong wrong wrong wrong wrong wrong wrong wrong wrong wrong wrong wrong wrong wrong wrong wrong wrong wrong wrong wrong wrong wrong wrong wrong wrong wrong wrong wrong wrong wrong wrong——.

"Ah'b—"

Before the pain could fill his mind completely, he thought of the one thing he must never forget.

Over and over, and over again, this thing that kept hurling Subaru back into this world of red and pain — this, this—

"It's wrong...!"

—wasn't Natsuki Subaru's Return by Death. It was something else entirely.
