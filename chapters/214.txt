Arc 4 Chapter 48: "The Price of the Tea Party"

A roar echoed out.

Subaru, stretching his tongue toward the blood dripping from his right hand out of hunger, heard that voice from somewhere far away. Anger, it was anger. Fury. Someone was angry. Someone was raging. Someone's voice, howling in fury for the sake of something.

—It didn't matter. Right now, all he wanted was to satisfy this hunger, endlessly.

He chewed, and chewed, and chewed, but it wasn't enough.
What could two fingers possibly satisfy? How much could the flowing blood quench this thirst?
Not enough. Not enough. Nowhere near enough.

Even if he devoured his right hand whole, even if he devoured the left hand that remained, even if he devoured his entire body, it still wouldn't be enough. Appetite knew no limit. He simply wanted what he wanted. And for that—

"—Hnnngaaah!!"

"——Do— *don't*!?"

A blow struck straight down onto the head of the sprawled figure, and Subaru's body sank into the ground amid a shockwave. The force was so great the earth buckled violently, a crater forming on the hill centered on the Subaru embedded within it.
And the one who'd delivered that blow, fist still pressed into the back of Subaru's skull, drew ragged breaths and said,

"I hate this, why does everyone have to fight...? Using violence is the worst... the *worst*..."

The half-crying voice fell from the sky, and Subaru's consciousness, tasting dirt, was dragged back to reality. At the same moment, a water droplet struck the back of his head, and as if drawn by it, he lifted his face.
The outline of the blonde girl blurred, and the tearful figure was already vanishing.

—He realized what had happened to himself only when he pressed his right hand to the ground to push himself up and found it whole again.

So, reflexively, Subaru called out to the vanishing girl,

"H-hey, thanks for healing me...!"

"...Hmph."

Turning her face away, the witch vanished with a sulky expression.
Right before she disappeared, unable to hide that the face she wasn't showing him had gone red, her cheeks loosening with something like happiness.

And when the blonde girl—the Witch of Wrath—vanished from the spot, in her place there appeared once more the crude coffin, and,

"Honestly, Nell-Nell is such a busybody. It was just a little lesson, wasn't it? Riiight, Subaru-run?"

Spoken to as though nothing at all had happened, from within the coffin, Subaru sprang up as if leaping, immediately backing away from the crater, his expression openly wary toward Daphne.
The unseen Daphne snorted at his reaction.

"Yes, yes, that's the level of caution you should be showing. Because, you seeee, this world is nothing but eat or be eaten."

"I don't want to believe it's that cutthroat...! What was that just now! It was only a short while, but... I was definitely losing my mind."

"That's what maddening hunger does. Extreme hunger turns people into something less than beasts. If you look at my right eye too, it'll get even more interesting, you knooow?"

"...Don't screw with me."

An Evil Eye, or something akin to one, Subaru judged.
Ram's clairvoyance corresponded to the ability that operated through the eyes Subaru knew of, but where hers was utterly devoid of any offensive capacity, Daphne's was viciously malevolent.
Call it the "Evil Eye of Hunger" or the "Evil Eye of Starvation," perhaps. The moment he'd met her left eye, Subaru had, in unconscious hunger, been devouring the fingers of his own right hand.
A hunger that left even pain behind was bait to satisfy a hunger that could never be satisfied by anything visible. In other words—

"Don't tell me... that's the view the Great Rabbit sees?"

"Those children were born modeling themselves especially after Daphne's rumbly-tumbly stomach, so... you can understand them wanting to eat each other, can't you?"

"You say that so breezily... doesn't it pain you at all, giving birth to creatures like that? Since you lectured me all high and mighty about it, that means you know that hunger yourself too. Your own children... ah, making your own children feel like that..."

"—? Even if the Great Rabbit gets hungry, Daphne's stomach doesn't get hungry along with it, you knooow?"

"...I was a fool to even ask."

Parallel lines. No matter how far he went, he'd never see eye to eye with this witch.
To her, even the Witchbeasts she'd given birth to were nothing more than emergency rations, meant to be nibbled on a little whenever she herself felt hungry.
Birthed from her own body and devoured by herself—the ultimate self-sufficiency.

"If you'd just holed up in some other dimension and been self-sufficient there without troubling anyone, nobody would've had to deal with any of this."

"In the eeend, what did Subaru-run even want to ask Daphne? If all you wanted was to badmouth Daphne like that, being awake is just making me hungrier, so I'd like to excuse myself."

Turning her face from Subaru inside the coffin, Daphne went limp all over, settling into a sleeping posture.
He finally understood why she stayed cooped up in that mobile coffin, why she sealed her whole body in restraints. It wasn't a cage to keep her danger contained from the outside world—no.
It was simply fashion, done to reduce, even slightly, the calories she'd burn by moving or freeing her limbs, a way to distract herself from her hunger.
The eyepatches over both eyes might be the one exception, but even those were most likely there just to avoid the physical exhaustion that came from using her Evil Eyes.

Through and through, her existence was complete within herself alone.
A mass composed of nothing but "appetite"—the Witch of Gluttony. Apt, indeed, a fitting name.

"Honestly, I figure asking is pointless, but... how do I go about destroying the Great Rabbit?"

"Ehh, you want to destroy the Great Rabbit? That kid's weak, easy to eat, and multiplies well—it's one of Daphne's masterpieces, you knooow?"

"If you can accept the idea of eat or be eaten, then you should accept killing your opponent in order to live. I'd like you to recognize the survival instinct too, not just appetite."

Subaru flung his extended sophistry at the reluctant Daphne.
Truthfully, he'd already half given up on drawing useful information out of her. Both because she seemed unlikely to offer anything useful, and because there was no sign a proper conversation with her was even possible to begin with.
What made it worse was that, at a glance, it looked like they were having a proper back-and-forth. In reality, it was Subaru throwing the ball, her chewing it up, and instead of throwing it back, just wanting want want the next one.

But—

"Since you eat to live, if you don't recognize killing to live too, then... hmm, that's, yeah, that's, certainly, right, isn't it."

"—Wait, that actually landed?"

"If I think something's right, then even Daphne will accept it. What do you even think Daphne is, Subaru-run?"

*The biggest witch I've ever seen, by a landslide*, was Subaru's honest opinion, but starting down that road seemed like it would never end, so he kept silent.
Daphne, lips pursed, waved off the silence with a casual "hmmph," and,

"If you want to destroy little Great Rabbit, Metometo or Donadona, or Miramira would be easy, you knooow."

"Wait, your nicknames are hard to follow. Donadona... that's Echidna, right? Metometo is... Sekmet-san? And Miramira is—"

"Carmilla... 'Lust,' you knooow. Though it seems she doesn't want to meet you, Subaru-run."

"It's kind of sad being disliked by the one in charge of 'Lust' of all things, but your plan doesn't work. The witches can't leave the Tomb. I can't borrow their power."

"...Hmmph, is that no good?"

The idea of enlisting help was, for Subaru too, a proposal too good to hope for. If they could come out and wield their power solely against the Great Rabbit and Elsa.

"Say hypothetically you could get out... would destroying the Great Rabbit—I mean, would just eating the Great Rabbit satisfy you enough to go back?"

"Daphne's never once, in her whole life, felt full."

"So even hypothetically, we can't let you out. Any of you."

Subaru stuck out his tongue at the all-too-predictable answer and rejected Daphne's proposal. She hummed in her throat from inside the coffin, an "Mmm," and,

"If that's no good, then I think the only way is for a lot of you to work hard together and eat every last one of them, without leaving a single one. Those kids can't multiply from zero either, so... haah, haah..."

"Eating them aside, wiping them all out, huh... but if even one's left, it revives, right? You said they act in a swarm—are they all always together?"

"They aaare. There's a lot of them in number, but there's just one consciousness. It's like the swarm shares one mind. They don't have the wits to split apart or anything like that, you knooow."

"I see... then there's no scenario where, after we exterminate them, some leftover ones multiply back... that kind of panic-horror ending, huh."

Even after slaying the monster causing all the damage, some remnant survives underground and breeds, and "the crisis isn't over!"—that's the standard cliché ending of every monster panic story.
As far as the Great Rabbit went, at least, that sort of cunning seemed to be entirely absent.

"By the way, how many Great Rabbits are there on average? Since they cannibalize each other, I figured there must be some system keeping the numbers in check."

"...Who knooows? Subaru-run, are you the type who counts water droplets when fog rolls in?"

"That bad, huh... well, I get it, you're not a reliable source. I'll confirm it with my own eyes."

Given the number that had swarmed Subaru's body, that scene alone involved over a hundred. Considering they aimed to devour the whole of the Sanctuary, the total number was likely close to tens of thousands.
Wiping them all out would require carefully considering both method and means.

While Subaru mulled over how to exterminate them, Daphne let out a huge yawn, declaring her boredom. Moving her mouth in a mumble at the now-silent Subaru, she said,

"If Subaru-run's going to sit there pondering riddles like that, can Daphne go ahead and disappear now? Existing makes me hungry, you knooow."

"That's a hell of a thing to say, that existing makes you not want to exist because it makes you hungry. Ah, well, regardless of how it went, this was useful for reference. Thanks. —And, one more thing."

Tossing out a perfunctory word of thanks, Subaru then added one last thing.
To Daphne's puzzled expression, he spoke in the most spiteful voice he could manage,

"I'm the one who's going to destroy the Great Rabbit bastards. The White Whale, too—already dead. Don't you dare complain, Mother dearest."

"............"

"Four hundred years—that's how long the things you birthed for whatever reason you thought was good have been running wild. That's enough. —I'll erase them, without a trace."

"Such a little thing—a mere human."

At Subaru's declaration of war, Daphne showed a reaction unlike anything before.
Her mouth split wide open sideways, and for the first time she showed a clear emotion other than "appetite," as she said,

"If you think you can do it, then go ahead and try, you knooow."

From a mouth lined with too-sharp teeth, the Witch of Gluttony bared her red tongue and laughed.

A strong wind blew through, and Subaru instinctively raised an arm to shield his eyes.
The gust whipped the grassland at his feet, green leaves swept up and scattered by the wind. He reflexively followed them with his eyes, watched them get sucked into the sky, then brought his gaze back down.

"Sorry for the unreasonable request, Echidna."

"For my part, I stopped you because I could tell it would end up like that."

"Couldn't be helped. I released the restraints myself, took off the eyepatch myself. You could at least praise me for the part where I didn't touch her body."

"True enough. If you'd touched Daphne, that alone wouldn't have been the extent of it. The left eye alone isn't that much of a threat from Daphne. Daphne's true terror is the right eye, and what comes after being 'eaten'—that's when the real show starts."

An unsettlingly ominous remark came flying out, and Subaru could only recoil.
Honestly, that "not that much of a threat" part alone had already been more than enough abnormality for Subaru to stomach.

"...I don't need a status rundown on an enemy I've already finished off. If there's a plan for me to fight some monster similar to her, that I should hear."

"A monster, is it."

His negative impression of Daphne still unshaken, the words slipped out of Subaru, and at Echidna's oddly parched murmur in response, he realized his slip.
To her, Daphne apparently counted as something like a friend. The fact that Echidna could maintain a friendship with something like that only confirmed, once again, that she truly was a witch through and through—but that was that, this was this. Especially calling another witch a monster in front of a witch was—

"Ah, yeah, that was thoughtless of me, even for me. Sorry. I'm a little on edge. As for your friend—no comment from me. That's all I can say."

"Fufufu, you don't need to be that considerate toward a witch. It's a rejection I'm well used to hearing."

"...I can't really use cheap words about Daphne, but I don't think you're a monster. I'll say that much properly, and correct myself."

At Subaru's words, Echidna blinked, her eyes going round in surprise.
Averting his gaze from that reaction, Subaru grew disgusted with the calculating thought in his own chest.

What he'd just said was, transparently, an attempt to curry favor. No doubt, some half of it was genuine, but he couldn't help being conscious of not wanting to leave a bad impression on a witch who was being cooperative.
Of course, a witch who'd lived through countless battles had probably already seen right through something that obvious.

"No, no, that won't do. Even if you try to smooth things over with sweet-sounding words like that, I won't let myself be smoothed over. Would you like a refill of tea and cookies?"

"You saying it while looking that pleased with yourself kills all the persuasiveness! What is with your chorotic-heroine vibes? This is beyond just being lonely for company."

The future of a woman who fell for such a transparent bit of flattery worried him.
Even knowing full well that she had no future left—a dull ache stirred in his chest.

"I'll pass on cookies made with your bodily fluids, and whatever else might be mixed in there without me knowing, thanks."

"I didn't put hair in them or anything, you know?"

"I can't help but doubt every single thing you say now!"

He resolved, from here on, never to eat or drink anything in this place again.
Echidna gave a wry smile at Subaru's suspicious glare. Then she turned a gaze on him, one that seemed to be studying him closely. That look, which she sometimes gave, was, for some reason, uncomfortable to be under.

"I don't much care for that look, like you're trying to see straight through my insides."

"If just peering at someone let me see through all of them, I could stare at you until you were scorched black, and it still wouldn't be enough... but more to the point, I wonder—are you aware of it, or not?"

"Aware of what? What's this about being aware or unaware?"

"Speaking within the limits of my own poor, narrow imagination—the state you were just in should have been a fairly shocking scene, for a human. Losing to hunger and gnawing on your own flesh isn't something that's supposed to happen easily."

At Echidna's flat, matter-of-fact remark, Subaru reconfirmed just how terrible his own condition had been moments earlier. That his lifted right hand properly had all five fingers was thanks to the witch who'd healed him with her characteristically bold method, mending him like some back-alley healer instead of a back-alley killer.
When Subaru asked her to pass along his thanks, Echidna closed one eye and said,

"As for Minerva, she just leapt out ignoring my call. Rushing out heedless of everything the moment she spots a wound... she was never the type built to live long. In fact, she was the first of us to be killed."

"A witch's end... huh. I heard all of you were eaten by the Witch of Envy—is that a topic I'm even allowed to ask about?"

"Whether it's tactless to ask the dead their cause of death is a difficult question, I'd say. There isn't much precedent for it. As for my own personal feelings... yes, well. I don't intend to tell the whole of it. It concerns the honor of the other five as well."

Echidna kept her lips sealed regarding the cause of death, and Subaru accepted that this, too, couldn't be helped.
Even Subaru himself, who had already died so many times, couldn't exactly laugh and casually talk about the circumstances of each death. "Death" was a thing too deep and too heavy for that.

"Thinking about it that way, I guess you all might be one of the few who could share this feeling of mine, huh."

"...No, I wonder about that. It's true we've died once ourselves, but whether we can think the same way you do—that, I don't know."

Subaru's words, hoping for at least some measure of empathy, were denied.
At the curt response, Subaru wanted to say something back to Echidna, but it dissolved before her grave expression. She looked toward him, brow knitting with something like sorrow, and,

"This ties into what I said about awareness earlier, too... are you aware that you are, right now, in a distorted state?"

"Distorted...?"

"There's no doubt that the peculiar environment called 'Return by Death' has made you that way. So to me, who understands the cause, it looks nothing but painfully tragic—and all the more so, I think, if you're not even aware of it."

"I don't follow. What exactly do you—"

"Do you consider it a normal state, that you can converse as though it's only natural with the very person who made you eat your own finger, just because the wound has healed?"

"————"

For a moment, Subaru's breath caught.
Echidna, watching Subaru go still, studied him closely with her eyes. Then, to Subaru, who resumed the breath he'd forgotten, she said,

"So it seems you weren't unaware, after all."

"...I think it's a matter of how you look at it. It's true, honestly, that I'm aware my current thinking is pretty far from sane. It feels like, for the sake of the one most important thing, I'm cutting away everything else."

"'Everything else,' meaning?"

"Right now, for me, breaking through this deadlock is the top priority. That goes for the manor, for the Sanctuary, and Emilia most of all. I'm still boxed in on every side, not knowing where to even start, in a real mess..."

Breathing in through his nose, Subaru looked up at the sky.
Blue sky, white clouds drifting by, a somehow leisurely air. Taking that in, so as to slowly, even in reality, indulge in the idleness of gazing up at such a view later, he said,

"But I've made up my mind to use everything I can use for that, to use it to the last drop."

"...Are you affirming 'Return by Death,' then?"

"I'm not affirming it. It's just that, for someone like me who never had many usable tools to begin with, that's about the only thing I've got going for me. ...I don't want you to get the wrong idea, though. It's not that I want to do it."

To Echidna, who seemed to be reading his true intentions, Subaru pressed the point again, knowing full well it was pointless.

"If spending my life gets me within reach of the future I want, then that's what I'll do. I've at least got a guarantee that the limit on how many times I can 'Return by Death' is, for now, as far as my sanity holds out. That being the case, all that's left is for someone with no guts, like me, to wring out what little guts I have and stretch that limit further."

"Piling up 'deaths,' to stretch your fingertips that much further. That's a resolve an ordinary person couldn't manage."

"I've died too many times, that's all. ...Somewhere along the line, I guess my head's started going a little crazy too."

It wasn't that he took "death" lightly. If anything, through accumulating his own experiences of "death," Subaru had only strengthened his conviction that it was an irrecoverable, terrifying thing. That much was certain. His fear of "death" had only grown, compared to before.
And yet, that Subaru could still bring himself to use his own "death" as a tool was, in fact, simple.

From piling up his own "deaths" and witnessing the worlds that could only end the way they did, Subaru had come to find the "deaths" of the people he knew even harder to bear than his own.
If he could save them—her, or them—from the inescapable fate of that "death," then he'd spend his own "life," something that could always be recovered, as many times as it took.

Swallow down the pain, the suffering, the fear, all of it, and die, and live on through it.
—That, right now, was the root of the resolve people called Natsuki Subaru's distortion.

"If the price to pay is only my own heart breaking, then I'll pay it. 'Return by Death'—it really is the perfect power for someone as powerless, ignorant, and dependent on others as me, isn't it?"

"............"

"Were you expecting some kind of comfort there, like 'you don't need to put yourself down that much'?"

"Thinking of the obstacles standing in your way, I can't say that so lightly. In fact, if you truly wish to do something about your current situation, there's no path forward that doesn't make use of it. Personally, all I feel is dissatisfaction that you have to rely on 'Envy' for it."

The fact that she didn't offer some easy comfort told him Echidna understood the situation.
It wasn't exactly reassuring, but having someone who'd kick him like that was still something to be grateful for.
Even walking a road he knew would leave him covered in wounds felt a little more bearable for it.

"In the end, I guess there's nothing to do but keep repeating 'Return by Death' and sweep through every option. At this rate, it's scary to think how much more pain is waiting."

"............"

"One of the mysteries I need to solve—the Sanctuary being an experiment site, and what kind of experiment you were running there... you're still not planning to tell me that, are you?"

"...No, I'm not. I told you before, didn't I? I don't want you to hold me in contempt."

Shaking her head at the question, Echidna refused Subaru's request.
Taking that in, Subaru cracked his neck with a "well, can't be helped, then," and,

"If you won't tell me, then fine. I'll just move around on my own and dig up the secret you're trying to hide, myself. You won't get in the way of that, will you?"

"...If it's to be uncovered, then that can't be helped, I suppose. If you're going to pin down a secret of mine that I'd rather you didn't, and drag it forcibly out into the light of day, then I'll simply accept it, quietly."

"Can you phrase that differently! You're making it sound like I'm about to do something indecent!"

Faintly flushing, Echidna averted her gaze from Subaru.
Whether she was deliberately teasing him or it was just her natural disposition, he couldn't tell, and it troubled him. As far as he'd seen during this tea party, she seemed genuinely, innocently naive underneath it all—witches were scary.
And, just as they were talking like that—

"Nn—"

Suddenly, Subaru felt a dizziness in his head, even while still seated in the chair. Something like a head-rush shook his consciousness for a while, continuously. That was—

"It seems your body is nearing its waking, it would appear."

"This tea party's over too, huh... if I had to call it meaningful, I guess it was meaningful."

"Last time was, unexpectedly, a case of 'nothing I wanted to ask,' after all. For my part, I suppose I got to show a bit of face as the Witch of Greed, wouldn't you say?"

For a witch who loved to talk, loved to teach, loved to chatter, this tea party must have been thoroughly satisfying. He couldn't help but feel a small tug watching her barely concealed joy, and the trace of lingering reluctance she showed as Subaru prepared to leave, but he shook his head to sever the sentimentality.
She was a witch who struck an oddly resonant chord in him, but getting too attached wasn't good. She was a witch, and on top of that, a dead person. Which of those was the "on top of that" part, he couldn't quite say.

"When I want to come here, what should I do?"

"The conditions for the tea party, is it? No, no, absolutely not, that won't do—you shouldn't rely on me too much like that. I understand your unease, truly, since you have no one out there you can confide 'Return by Death' to, and for now I'm the only one you can bare your heart to, but I am, after all, one of the dead, and you are among the living, so... no, that won't do at all."

"You saying that while unexpectedly wriggling around all pleased with yourself kills all the persuasiveness!"

How to judge the current situation, where her favorability seemed to skyrocket just from having a visitor after so long.
While Subaru, hand to his cheek, hesitated over how to respond to Echidna, who kept glancing at him, she hid her mouth and laughed, "Fufufu," and,

"Don't make such a troubled face. I'm a girl too, you know—there are times I want to have a slightly giddy conversation like this, too. That's all it is. I understand the gulf between witch and human well enough."

"...Echidna."

"As for the conditions of the tea party—it's to cry out, from the bottom of your heart, a desire to 'know,' within the Tomb. The first time, I could summon you unconditionally, but the second time onward won't be so simple. A third time, too... I suspect, would be difficult. A surface-level cry won't reach me."

At the words spoken in a quick rush, Subaru recalled the moment just before he'd been summoned.
Having completed a 'Return by Death' after being torn apart by the Great Rabbit, his body still dragging the sensation of "death" and writhing in agony, his consciousness had kept crying out, wondering what on earth was happening.
This time, it seemed Echidna had caught that cry and invited him to the tea party. If he wanted to come here again, the implication was he'd need an equal or greater degree of desperation.

"Yeah, I think I'll pass on that, honestly..."

"I imagine so. Which is why this might be the last time you and I get to meet face to face. Though, that won't apply if you go on to face the 'Trials.'"

Just as she had been present at the first 'Trial,' it seemed she would be present at the second and third 'Trials' as well. If Subaru was to take Emilia's place and face the 'Trials,' then that reunion was more or less guaranteed.
Which meant—

"So I guess I'll see you again during the 'Trials.' No tea then, though, probably."

"If you really insist on drinking some, I wouldn't be averse to brewing it there on the spot, but..."

"Nah, watching the manufacturing process would probably kill any desire to drink it even more, so I'll pass."

Holding out a palm in refusal, Subaru drew from Echidna the most disappointed expression he'd seen yet.
He had no idea what about her made her so eager to press her own bodily fluids on him. Some kind of fetish, deriving pleasure from part of herself becoming part of someone else? Her karma ran deep.

"Guess it's about time I disappear... well, Echidna, thanks for everything. See you again..."

"Before that, may I?"

Feeling the sensation of his own body growing steadily hazier, Subaru moved to say his farewell to Echidna. But it was Echidna herself who stopped him.
She rose from her seat and, the hem of her mourning-dress skirt swaying, approached Subaru, saying,

"I attended the tea party, and I passed a portion of my knowledge on to you... but is there something you've forgotten, I wonder?"

"Something I forgot?"

"The price."

Narrowing her eyes, Echidna showed her red tongue at Subaru, who tilted his head.
At those words, Subaru's eyes went wide, and "the price..." he murmured, just under his breath. At that murmur, Echidna nodded, "Yes, the price," and,

"I imposed one last time too, but it's an inseparable part of any bargain with a witch. Last time's price is last time's business—so what shall I take as this time's price?"

"C-couldn't we do like, pay-when-you-can? For me right now, with so few cards to play, having things taken from me or conditions imposed just raises the difficulty."

"You'll need a bit more skill in negotiation than that, to bargain with a witch."

Backing Subaru into a corner, chair and all, Echidna's cute face took on a sadistic tint. She looked him over, up and down, deliberating over what to take.
The witch's price—last time, it had been the forgetting, upon returning to reality, of Echidna's very existence. If the same were done this time, the very content of this tea party seemed likely to vanish along with it, pushing his progress further out of reach. Still, whether he could comply even if some other heavy condition were imposed was uncertain, and—

"Alright, I've decided."

Bracing himself for whatever might come, Subaru watched as Echidna bent at the waist and brought her face close. Just as her lips seemed about to brush past his, and Subaru flinched in alarm, her body continued on, further down past him—into his chest.
White hair fluttering softly, the witch shifting at such close range gave off a faint scent of flowers, and Subaru, unused to it after so long, felt flustered at his own weak resistance to a beautiful girl.
Then, ignoring Subaru's inner turmoil entirely, Echidna touched his chest, and,

"I'll take this."

"...O-oh?"

From Subaru's bewildered chest, Echidna withdrew her hand—and clasped between her white fingers was a handkerchief, fluttering lightly in the wind.
White cloth trimmed in gold, with a gray Great Spirit embroidered on its lining, it was—

"That's... the handkerchief Petra gave me before we left for the Sanctuary...?"

"You should be grateful to the person who gave you this present. It's imbued strongly with a pure feeling of worry for your safety. Every stitch of thread passed through it carries magic infused through that medium. The power that dwells in things like this is quite fascinating."

"...Petra, huh."

"You're well thought of, aren't you. I feel a bit sorry for the girl who cared for you, but I'll take this."

Softening at the embroidery of Puck on the lining, Echidna then tucked the handkerchief away inside her robes. And, stepping back from Subaru's body, she said,

"The price of the tea party, duly collected. I look forward, from the bottom of my heart, to your next visit."

With a teasing air, Echidna pinched the hem of her skirt and gave an elegant bow.
At that gesture of hers—at least trying to send him off lightly, so unlike her—Subaru rose from his chair, and,

"Thanks for everything. Really. —Farewell, then."

He pinched the edge of his own clothes the same way and bowed, drawing a wry laugh out of Echidna.
And then the scenery was wrapped in white light—and Subaru withdrew from the tea party.

—What Subaru's consciousness felt, returning from the tea party, was the abrupt cold and hardness of the floor, and the foul taste of dirt lodged in his mouth.

"Ugh! Ptooey, ptoo, ptooey! Is this the default every single time...!?"

Spitting out the foreign matter in his mouth, Subaru pushed himself up and shook his head to spur his consciousness fully awake.
First, checking the state of his own body, he replayed the events before he'd woken. That he'd been eaten to death by the Great Rabbit and come back; that immediately after, he'd been invited to Echidna's tea party. That, having been put through a terrible ordeal by Daphne, he'd firmed his resolve, and been saved by Petra's youthful feelings, and returned.
None of it had been erased from his memory. Relieved at that, he said,

"Echidna kept her word. This time's tea party isn't erased from my memory either."

The white-haired witch, too, was now burned clearly and vividly into his memory.
She was someone who somehow lacked that certain sense of "witch-ness," but the fact that she'd kept her promise like this, combined with her genuinely warm treatment of him, meant he could count her among his precious few allies.
It was a shame there didn't seem to be many chances to rely on her, but—

"This time's harvest, whatever else you want to call it, was worth it, at least—there's that."

Placing a hand to his chest, Subaru once again recalled the events of the tea party—namely, having been able to confess "Return by Death"—and savored the feeling of having been saved.
In that place, limited though it was to Echidna and the other witches, being able to reveal it and rack his brain over the matter together with someone else was, above all else, a real gain.
Having heard the reflections of someone far more versed than Subaru in the affairs of this other world, on the subject of "Return by Death," was one part of that.

Though it also meant that the Witch of Envy was the root cause of everything, and that a time would eventually come when he'd have to face her—he'd come back carrying that headache along with him, in that sense too.

"With that feeling renewed, it's Natsuki Subaru, reborn. For now, I'll let myself rely on that witch's power. However many times it takes, I'll spend my life for it."

If that got him closer to an answer, then that was exactly what he wanted.

"Tea party messes with my sense of time, but right now should be right after the 'Trial,' right?"

That the scene of "Return by Death" hadn't changed was Echidna's claim, but even by Subaru's own perception, shaking his head and looking around, there was no mistaking that this was the Tomb.
Confirming that her words had been correct, Subaru first set about getting Emilia out of this place, and began searching for her figure.

"Thinking about right after 'Return by Death'—this being the third time, I need to think about how to handle Garfiel too. ...I doubt he'd suddenly try to kill me, but..."

Still, the man acted on impulse. There was no reading ahead what he'd do.
Thinking of him brought back the most recent "death"—the direct cause being torn apart and devoured by the Great Rabbit, and before that, the scene of the villagers' massacre—and dark emotion welled up unbidden.

Defeating the Great Rabbit, defeating Elsa, the mystery of the Sanctuary, and settling the score with Garfiel.
Even knowing it was an event from a world that hadn't happened, Subaru still couldn't bring himself to forgive it. Somehow, some way, Garfiel would have to pay for it too.
While mulling that over inwardly, Subaru's ill feeling toward the Garfiel who was presumably outside kept rising—but his train of thought was suddenly cut off. Because—

"...Emilia, where are you?"

—Her figure was nowhere to be found.

Brow furrowing at the wrongness of it, Subaru strained his eyes in the dim Tomb. But no matter where he looked in the not-so-large chamber of the 'Trial,' he couldn't find Emilia's slender body lying anywhere.
It was an anomaly unlike anything before.

"If I'm right after the 'Trial,' Emilia should still be in the middle of hers, but..."

By rights, Emilia, unable to overcome the first 'Trial' and struggling against her past, should have been right there beside him.
And yet now, her figure was nowhere in the Tomb. And yet the timeline had, without question, returned Subaru to the Tomb right after the 'Trial.'

"............"

He had a bad feeling.
Emilia's absence from this place, and the difference from every situation before now.
A change that shouldn't have existed—that could only be because Subaru's actions had changed the future. But what influence could Subaru's actions possibly have had, when he'd been unconscious the whole time?

Driven by rising panic, Subaru bolted out of the 'Trial' chamber into the passage. And then, his hard footsteps echoing down the corridor, he passed through it, out of the Tomb.

And what Subaru saw, stepping out of the Tomb under the moonlight, was—
