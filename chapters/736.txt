Arc 9 Chapter 40: "Flash Remnade"

—At the hands of Roy Alphard of "Gluttony," Otto Suwen was struck down and helplessly forced from the battle.

As the bearer of the Divine Protection of Spirit Words, the man who had wielded it more masterfully than anyone in history fought true to his declaration — branding Aldebaran's group as enemies of the world and making them suffer greatly for it.
Which was exactly why the conclusion to his lone battle came about through a merciless, thoroughgoing suppression that left him utterly without recourse, without so much as a sliver of an opening to counterattack.

His words were sealed, his movements were sealed, his tricks were sealed, his quick wit was sealed, every weapon he possessed was sealed away, and in the end even the "memories" that made him who he was were stolen from him — Otto Suwen was defeated, utterly and completely.

Even Otto had no means to counter it when Aldebaran's group came at him in earnest, throwing everything they had into sealing him away with a speed that left no time to plan a response.
Unable to predict whether his opponents would attempt a counterattack, much less where they would spring it, he couldn't even form the trap zones his Divine Protection excelled at.
So there was nothing Otto Suwen, once attacked, could do — his last resort amounted to nothing more than a desperate, futile struggle prepared in case exactly this happened.

—All he'd done was leave instructions for the Zodda Bugs to take flight all at once the moment he was taken down, so the others would know where it had happened.


　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　△▼△▼△▼△


"That Al guy — even before he and I got acquainted in the Empire, even before 'Gluttony' ate my memories, he already knew Rem. There's no mistaking it."

That was the opinion Imaginary Subaru offered while they were hashing out the plan for the surprise attack.
Looking at the sour expression on "Subaru's" face as he sat cross-legged in midair, arms folded, Petra recalled — from within his memories — the loop in the royal capital where Al and Rem had first crossed paths.

That was back when Subaru had committed a terrible blunder at the castle and was desperately trying to make up for it.
Petra thought his subsequent meltdown was pretty awful, though not quite enough to make her disillusioned with him — but Subaru himself remembered it as an embarrassment so mortifying that just recalling it made him writhe. In truth, everyone present at the time must have frowned at his antics, so aside from Petra, the only one who could watch that display without losing any respect for him was probably Rem, blindly devoted as she'd already been back then.

In any case, unable to break free of that loop of negative emotion, Subaru had once gone to visit Priscilla's mansion, searching for some way to save Emilia from the Witch Cult's attack.
There, he'd received a coldness from Priscilla so scathing it beggared imagination, but the crucial connection between Al and Rem came to light in that very moment, as he was being chased off from the mansion.
The specific reason for it, Subaru didn't know, but—

"Al called out the wrong name, mixing up Ram and Rem — and he seemed genuinely shocked at himself for doing it. When I asked Rem about it, she said it was the first time she'd ever met him."

"So it was one-sided — Al-san knew about the two of them, but they didn't know him? Maybe because they're rival camps in the Royal Selection, he'd done his research on them properly...?"

"You mean something as sensible as proper Royal Selection prep being Al's job in the Priscilla Camp? I guess it's not impossible, but that's beside the point either way. What matters is just the one fact that Al knows Rem. Which means—"

"Just like the rest of us — if Al-san sees Rem-neesama, his 'memories' get thrown into confusion too."

"That's it. If I had to put a name on it — a Flash Remnade."

With an inaudible snap of his fingers and a wink, "Subaru" got a thumbs-up from Petra in return.
Honestly, she felt a bit conflicted about treating Rem like a flash grenade, but Petra and the others had already confirmed firsthand that there was no countermeasure against this rebound of "memory."
As with Petra-in-Subaru or Ram, the deeper the relationship and the feelings involved, the bigger the shock tended to be — but even a small opening was still an opening.
All that remained was to seize it—

"Let's show Al-san — just how angry we all are."


　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　△▼△▼△▼△


—Here, let's offer a word in Roy Alphard's defense, the Sin Archbishop of "Gluttony."

Using his nature as an omnivorous glutton and the Authority of "Gluttony" that had birthed so many tragedies, Roy had completely neutralized Otto Suwen, who'd been giving Aldebaran's group such trouble. His "Corrosion" didn't merely disable Otto — it chewed through the whole twenty-two years of the man's life, joy and anger and sorrow and everything else lumped together, and licked the plate clean in a single breath.

Roy, letting out a satisfied gulp as he finished his intangible meal, tasted Otto Suwen's life with a precision and freshness beyond anything a reading of the Book of the Dead could offer, and rendered his verdict: a man who'd weathered a fair share of hardship and sampled a decent mix of joy and sorrow, seasoned somewhat rich — he'd give it two stars.

The very fact that he rated what he devoured this way already carried a whiff of the "gourmet" spirit inherited from his late brother Lye Batenkaitos, but setting that aside — to devour someone's "memories" meant gaining access to that person's thoughts and philosophy, and, if it happened mid-battle, even the chance to learn what schemes and plans they'd been laying.

So naturally, even amid the afterglow of victory and sense of accomplishment from defeating Otto, sensing the ambush that had been planned to follow immediately after should have come easily.
But that wasn't what happened. Because Otto had never shared with his comrades exactly what they were to do after he himself went down.

Otto Suwen had simply believed.
That if he just gave a signal marking the instant he was taken down, his comrades wouldn't let it go to waste.

Because everything had been staked on that — not a plan at all, just plain, unbreakable trust—



"—It's an honor to meet you for the first time, Aldebaran-sama."

It happened, quite literally, in the space between one blink and the next.

Eyes closed, then open. In that instant, a blue-haired girl slipped into the gap — and neither Aldebaran, nor Yae, nor Roy, nor even "Aldebaran" could react.
An intruder who had no business being there had appeared suddenly, without so much as a trace of presence.
That fact alone was enough to steal an instant away from even these battle-hardened warriors, but the one for whom it proved most fatal in this moment was Aldebaran — for whom it didn't end at just an instant.

"—"

It was a phenomenon that didn't occur for Yae, who had no prior acquaintance with the girl, nor for Roy, who fell outside the scope of his own Authority, nor even for "Aldebaran" — who shared the "memories" but possessed an entirely different soul.
The blackened "memories" etched into brain and soul were stirred, and with a sensation like something once removed being forcibly crammed back in, Aldebaran's consciousness was washed white.

Someone, somewhere, had called it a flash grenade of "memory."

"—Al-sama!!"

Called by a shrill, desperate voice, he felt a hand reach through the whiteness of his blanked mind and catch hold of him. But it did nothing to rescue Aldebaran from his predicament.

"—"

The soles of his feet, which should have been planted firmly on the ground, cut through empty air, and Aldebaran realized his body was being battered by a furious wind, losing all sense of up and down, left and right, and beginning to spin.
With his body utterly out of his control, and his black eyes scorched by the dazzling sun still hanging in the blue sky overhead, Aldebaran registered something more.

—That by some means, he had been transported into the sky, hurled outside the bounds of his "Territory."

"—! Territory Deployment, Matrix Redefinition."

As he re-established the "Territory" that had been forcibly severed, Aldebaran ground his teeth.
He'd been played right into his opponent's hand and made to update his matrix exactly as intended — which meant there was no going back now to before this ambush had struck.
But if he'd hesitated even a moment longer in deciding to update it, Aldebaran would have lost with no recourse at all.
His Authority was invincible, but only within the "Territory" he'd set — die outside of it, and, just like any of his countless other lives, he'd get no chance to try again.

"—"

Swallowing the new terms he'd been placed under, Aldebaran's awareness converged fully on the present.
Having been forced to update the matrix, everything that happened from here on was, for Aldebaran, both treasure and calamity, both tailwind and headwind, both good and evil, all at once.

"The sky...!"

The roaring howl of wind, the violent, buffeting sense of weightlessness pounding his whole body — falling from a great height with no memory of having been launched meant some proper procedure had been skipped entirely.
For an instant, the Authority of the strongest "Witch" flickered through Aldebaran's mind, but no — that wasn't it. If his opponent were that "Witch," the phenomenon would be something else entirely. He wouldn't be thrown out into the sky like this, and more importantly, he'd have been killed a hundred times over in the span of a single blink.

So he crossed that possibility off his list of worst-case scenarios.
And as he whittled down the possibilities, he simultaneously gathered information on his surroundings. Right now, amid the wind battering him, the anomaly closest at hand was—

"—Al-sama."

—Yae, clinging tightly to Aldebaran's one remaining arm, as if embracing it.
Just before being flung into the sky, she'd leapt onto Aldebaran as he froze from the flash grenade of "memory," and so she too had been sent flying from the forest up into the sky along with him.
Whether to call this good luck or bad, Aldebaran sorted through what he could and couldn't do with Yae there beside him, and settled on what needed doing first.
Which was—

"I'll stabilize our posture! Al-sama, please don't let go of me—"

"Ogh."

Yae, clinging to him, moving to do something about their fall, and Aldebaran cracking open a medicine packet in his mouth and swallowing the poison — both happened at almost the same moment.
He could tell Yae's crimson eyes had gone wide, her startled expression turned toward him. But what was done was done — he'd already downed a lethal dose, and there was nothing for it now.

"I'll hear about it later."

Bloody foam spilled from the corner of his mouth, and as he felt it mercilessly staining the inside of his helmet, tasting of iron rust, Aldebaran's consciousness was tossed and tumbled by the wind—


　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　×　　×　　×


"—!"

He'd known it was coming, but reality greeted him with the wind's howl, seamless and unbroken.
Opening his closed eyelids again, there before him, unchanged, was the hateful glare of the sun — after glaring right back at it in earnest, Aldebaran took stock of his surroundings and confirmed he was hundreds of meters up in the sky.

"You've gotta be kidding me."

Transference, instantaneous movement, teleportation — whatever term you used, the effect was the same, but as something that acted on space itself, falling under the field of Yin magic, it was a super-tier magic requiring every conceivable condition to be met.
If one built a precise formula and firmly established one's own field carved with it, a limited warp connecting door to door within that field was achievable. Or, even without such preparation, if the caster could picture the destination's coordinates and constructed a formula meant to burn through their Gate entirely to send the target off, they might manage to issue a one-way, one-time-only ticket.


　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　×　　×　　×


In short, whatever phenomenon had sent Aldebaran and the others flying into the sky was, even by magic, at a level of impossibility — meaning some other rule-breaking trick was involved.
He'd never heard of a Divine Protection capable of interfering with another person this physically.
Of course, there were exceptions like Reinhard, who could take a Divine Protection everyone dismissed as trivial and wield it in some abnormal way, turning it into a terrifying combat weapon — but—

"—No, this ain't that. But if it's an Authority..."

As far as Aldebaran knew, of the currently existing Authorities, "Wrath," "Gluttony," and "Lust" each belonged to a Sin Archbishop, while "Sloth" and "Greed" both belonged to Natsuki Subaru.
Setting aside Aldebaran's own Authority — which ought to be called some kind of missing number, an outlier, or a stolen thing — the remaining possibilities were "Pride," and then "Vanity" and "Melancholy." Since no one should be able to wield those last two, the only possibility left was "Pride."


　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　×　　×　　×


"Has 'Pride' shown up? Sprouted? Been added to the roster? —No, never mind that part."

Whether someone Aldebaran knew had been chosen for "Pride," or someone unknown had joined the ranks as "Pride," what he had to consider was the possibility that a bearer of an Authority had turned against him.
Setting aside the one exception of Natsuki Subaru and Reinhard, if some unknown quantity of power were threatening Aldebaran, it could only be a bearer of an Authority — none other than one of those destroyers of concepts, permitted to twist the world's rules and trample it as they pleased.


　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　×　　×　　×


"—Alright. I'm settled."

Is your time spent flopping around in a panic, played for a fool by your enemy's scheme, finally over? It felt as though the roaring wind itself had asked him that, and Aldebaran answered.
That he could answer at all meant he'd managed to get the present situation sorted out, at least that much, in his own mind.
Of course, none of the reality had actually changed — not being thrown out into the sky, not the bitter regret of dragging Yae along with him, not the sensation of his consciousness being shoved aside into whiteness as his "memories" were restored.

But as long as he didn't actually die, it was cheap. That was Aldebaran's life for you.
If he could trade that bargain-bin life for a chance to let his head, body, and heart catch their breath, it was a cheap price to pay.

"—Al-sama."

Watching Yae, clinging to his one arm, her tied-back hair whipping about violently like the wagging tail of a happy dog, Aldebaran's thoughts moved on to the next phase.
He had to reach the ground with both himself and Yae still alive.
And for that—

"I'll stabilize our posture! Al-sama, please don't let go of me—"

"No, that's my line."

Fired up, Yae made to act on her own judgment there in the open air, but Aldebaran forcibly pulled her against his chest to stop her. Having used his right arm for that, he rapidly generated a stone prosthetic arm he'd grown fairly practiced at making by now, and raised it overhead — the very next instant, a violent impact struck the prosthetic arm from directly above, and their fall accelerated.

It was a blow from a mass of iron so dreadfully savage it reverberated with a heavy weight even through the arm he'd constructed. Mingled with the tremendous roar of wind, the threat lay at the end of a chain that had come dancing in like a writhing serpent.
A spiked iron ball, shaped — contrary to its name — to put a person to eternal sleep—

"—Prepare yourself!"

Beyond the iron ball she'd hurled at him, a blue-haired girl fell as if bearing the sun on her back. The one who had stripped away so much of Aldebaran's advantage and lit the beacon of war — Rem — roared.
Slowly, a single horn pushed out from her forehead. Proof of the Oni Tribe, proof that she was one of the decisive races created to sweep every trace of the "Witch" from this world.
But more than that, to Aldebaran, who had now recalled her existence, it carried a different meaning entirely.
Which was—

"In the first place, the fact that little missy Rem is even alive already doesn't add up!!"


　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　△▼△▼△▼△


Having steeled herself, Rem threw herself into the air she'd been braced for, her maid uniform's hem fluttering as she gripped the handle of her Morningstar tight and glared down at Al and the others below.

—So far, the ambush had gone off exactly as planned, functioning perfectly.
Exposing herself before Al, then, while he was frozen and stripped of his judgment, scooping him up with "Compression." That had succeeded in cutting Al off from his allies while carrying him somewhere his instincts couldn't help him — a place they'd all quickly agreed upon: the sky.
As for the crucial matter of timing, it had helped that Otto, who'd kept up his guerrilla attacks and pursuit of Al's group, had prepared nothing but a signal for the moment he himself went down.
Because that signal was, in effect, synonymous with the moment Al's group finally brought down the formidable Otto — the exact instant their tension would slacken, even if only slightly.

"Priscilla-san has done a great deal for me. And so, as one of those who holds Priscilla-san dear, I cannot go on overlooking Al-san's outrages any longer."

That was what Rem had said, declaring her own intent, back when objections were raised against her going to confront Al directly.
It was, strictly speaking, only a matter of mindset laying the foundation for her resolve, but it was, without question, Rem's true feeling — the unshakable core of her motivation for taking on this fight.
Of course, right beside that core sat the existence of Subaru, captured by Al. In fact, maybe that was the real core. —No, well, Priscilla-san was surely a core too, so there would just have to be two cores. Rem would apologize to Beatrice-sama about that later.

In any case, fired up with that resolve, Rem's participation in the unit set to confront Al directly had been decided.
Part of it was that no one could envision Rem being especially useful on any other battlefield strategically, but beyond that initial jolt to his "memories," there was every possibility Rem held some special effectiveness against Al.
And that very possibility was—

"In the first place, the fact that little missy Rem is even alive already doesn't add up!!"

The instant she'd driven the iron ball down at him from above, Al — blocking the fierce blow with his stone prosthetic arm — bellowed that at her, his eyes fixed on Rem.
Even tossed about amid the roaring wind, his words reached her clearly enough that Rem became certain it had worked — that unknown connection to Al which Petra had told her about, one Rem herself had no memory of.

"Rem, being no good at these things, can't recall any connection with Al-san, but..."

She'd checked with Ram too, but even Ram's connection with Al apparently amounted to nothing more than exchanging a few words in the royal capital once, before the Royal Selection had gotten properly underway. There hadn't been anything substantial enough to stick in her memory, and even what they'd talked about was hazy.
Most likely, whatever connection she and her sister had with Al was one-sided — existing only from his end.
But—

"That kind of talk hurts my feelings! When Arakiya-san attacked us in Guaral, the ones who saved us were Al-san and—"

There, she nearly broke off, but Rem forced down her hesitation and pressed on,

"—wasn't it Priscilla-san!!"

Deliberately invoking Priscilla's name, Rem schemed to provoke Al into losing his composure.
She loathed herself for it, having displayed about the worst kind of underhandedness she could imagine. But telling herself this too was necessary to stop Al's outrages, Rem steeled both body and heart and hardened her gaze.

"—"

In response to Rem's provocation, Al chose not to raise his voice but rather a suppressive silence.
Behind the jet-black iron helmet, hidden by the face guard, Al's expression couldn't be seen, nor was there any telling the light in his eyes — no way to know at all how much pain her words had just inflicted on him.

"—Here I come!"

But without waiting for Al's reaction, Rem resumed her attack, giving him no chance to catch his breath.
Rem had already settled her priorities. To begin with, compared to Ram — excellent and gifted, a step above every other being in this world — Rem was clumsy, mediocre, a girl who fell one step short in everything.
If a girl like that wanted something more than ordinary, all she could do was pursue the best possible means to that end, relentlessly.
For now, Rem was good at closing her eyes to lower priorities for the sake of higher ones.

"Huma!"

An incantation, and with it Mana interfered with the world, and magic manifested as an impossible phenomenon.
Unlike Emilia's Huma, which manipulated heat to freeze moisture already present in the air into ice, what Rem excelled at was a type of Huma that conjured water into empty space through magic and output that newly created water as ice from the very start.
Neither type of Huma was inherently superior, but Emilia's type, which made use of the existing moisture in the air, needed less Mana and had the advantage of remaining in place afterward, available for reuse once it had served its purpose. Rem's type, on the other hand, needed more Mana since it created water anew, and after use it was more often returned entirely back into Mana, leaving nothing behind.

Going by this explanation alone, Emilia's type of Huma would seem overwhelmingly superior. But Rem's type had its own powerful advantage, in its own right.
Namely, that it could produce ice in whatever location and whatever shape she wanted, unaffected by the amount or quality of moisture in the air—

"—Haaaaah!!"

Kicking off an icy foothold conjured in midair, Rem hurtled ferociously at Al and the others.
By creating a Huma fixed to specific coordinates, she could form an improvised platform sturdy enough not to break even under her full-strength stance. A Morningstar swing launched after gaining even the briefest instant of solid footing packed a power incomparable to one delivered by twisting her whole body in open air.

The iron ball, spinning with a thunderous roar as it hurtled toward Al, held nothing back.
A direct hit would easily shatter a human body — and as if answering the loving care Subaru had poured into polishing it every single day, it surged from Rem's hand, brimming with joy, closing in on Al, closing in, closing in—

"I won't allow it."

That ball of affection was seized head-on, right at the moment of impact, by countless threads.
Looking closer, it was the crimson-clad shinobi in Al's arms who'd done it — twisting her body around to glare at Rem, the peerlessly powerful thread-user who served at Al's side.
Held from behind by Al, the shinobi had spread threads released from both outstretched hands into a net, and used it to catch the iron ball softly, like a cushion.

That skill alone was impressive enough, but it wasn't what Rem found most notable.
To begin with, this ambush that flung Al into the sky had been calculated to isolate him completely from his allies. That she had managed to interpose herself there at all was, in itself, unthinkable. —No, "unthinkable" was the wrong word. Rem knew exactly why she had been able to do it.

Despite everyone's minds having gone blank at Rem's sudden appearance, the shinobi had kept enough room within that blankness to reach out — and had actually acted on it.
What had made that possible—

"—That would be love!"

"Wrong!!"
"That's not it!!"

At Rem's confident shout, Al and the shinobi both yelled back their denial in nearly the same breath.
In that opening, Rem forcibly wrenched back her Morningstar, which had nearly been seized from her, retrieving the iron ball from the shinobi, who'd been unwilling to let her own stance be broken. For her next attack, Rem conjured several new footholds and kicked off them, plunging down to catch up with the pair.
But—

"It's frightening, having you catch up to us like this, but every strength has its weakness, doesn't it?"

Her once-tense expression regaining its composure, the shinobi smiled a bewitching smile as she sent out her threads. The threads she loosed from her hand made straight for the icy footholds Rem had created in the air.
Suddenly thrown out into the sky, with nothing to do but be tossed about in freefall, Al and his companions were surely planning to use Rem's own footholds as a handhold to right themselves.
From what she could tell, the shinobi's skill outstripped even Rem's own. If she let her opponent make up for the handicap Rem had worked so hard to impose, whatever gap in ability had been closed, however slightly, would reverse itself in an instant.

"I know just what you mean. Rem's weaknesses are about all she has, after all."

So Rem wouldn't let her opponent make up for that handicap at all.

"—!"

On hearing Rem's reply — no, on feeling the grip she'd once secured vanish like mist, the shinobi's expression was once again stripped of its composure.
The icy foothold she'd meant to use as a handhold had vanished as though it were nothing but a dream or an illusion. Of course, it was neither a dream nor an illusion. It was Mana. Rem had simply chosen, at will, to return it to Mana.
This was the great difference — even between the same Huma — between Emilia's type, which stayed frozen once frozen, and Rem's type, which froze only when she wanted it frozen and could be cleaned up just as easily.

"Put simply, Rem's the more aggressive one here."

Right before Rem's eyes, the shinobi, having lost the destination for the force she'd meant to exert, lost her own balance.
Al, holding the shinobi, nearly lost his own balance in the process, but even so, he reinforced his cracked prosthetic arm with additional armor and caught the iron ball as Rem sent it again and again, a second time, a third.
And into that—

"—Teeyah!!"

—Emilia's long leg came flying in from diagonally below, driving the ice-armored toe of her boot mercilessly into Al's undefended back.


　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　△▼△▼△▼△


—Splitting apart Aldebaran's group.

That was a precondition they could never afford to let slip, when facing a group in open rebellion against the world that rivaled the Witch Cult — or, judged by sheer momentary force, even surpassed it.
Of the ambush plan devised to satisfy that condition, some ninety percent could be said to have gone exactly as intended. The remaining ten percent of deviation — how the shinobi, who had chosen of her own will to be dragged along, would factor in — had marred the plan as an uncertain element, but excessive worry would only poison things further.

Each of them would carry out their own assigned role to the fullest.
Without that trust and resolve, no strategy to stop Aldebaran's group could ever hold together.

"—Man, would you look at that — everyone's got such an irresistible look in their eyes, it's almost enough to make me shiver."

As he spoke, Roy Alphard, the Sin Archbishop of "Gluttony," swung both arms loosely at his sides and looked around with a sadistic grin.
Around him, figures had begun appearing one after another the moment Aldebaran and the others were sent flying, as if taking their place — warriors gathered to bring down Aldebaran's group.
Every one of them met the Sin Archbishop's gaze without so much as flinching, and Roy couldn't help but lick his lips.

"Nice, real nice, might even be great. But hey, gathering up this many all at once — doesn't that mean whatever forces were gonna try and take us down might just get scooped up in one net themselves?"

"—Ha. You're one to talk. Why don't you show a little more fear, cower a bit more sweetly? Unless you want to die the same way your brother did, in the Tower of Sand."

"And besides, I wonder if you two are into Witchbeasts too, hm~? Because if not... I do think I might just be your natural predator, hm~?"

Among the warriors, the figure hugging her own elbows and the figure smoothing down her braid answered him in turn. Even in the face of their unmistakable hostility, Roy made no effort to hide the shiver of anticipation for a feast, plastered across his grinning face.

"Y-you gotta be kidding... Can't you idiots see the 'Divine Dragon' standing right there!? Do you have any idea how badly it tore up the royal capital...! You don't stand a chance!"

In stark contrast to Roy's elation, it was Heinkel whose face had gone pale, his voice shaking.
True to his words, relying entirely on the might of the "Divine Dragon," he stood right at its feet, straining his voice with all his might, his face twisted with resentment, against the hostility glaring down at them.
The sight of him was, quite literally, a case of "a man borrowing a dragon's authority" — but as a matter of fact, Heinkel's claim wasn't wrong. If one stood against the absolute power of the "Divine Dragon" without any countermeasure, what awaited them was ruin and an ending with no mercy at all.

"That is, assuming one has no countermeasure."

"—Tch, you old Giant."

"This old Giant remembers the 'Demi-Human War' well enough. I show no mercy twice to those who turn their hostility on me. —Now then, I'll be taking Felt back."

Facing Heinkel, whose breath caught and whose eyes darted about, the massive figure stroked his bald head and declared as much. At that declaration, Heinkel's cheeks stiffened — but there was another shadow that stirred as well.
None other than the "Dragon" itself, the very one being called out.

"Not that I wanna admit it, but gettin' pushed around this hard's kinda scared me straight. I'm feelin' real hard, right about now, just what it means to make an enemy of the whole world."

The "Dragon," for all its imposing majesty, raised a hand to its head with a gesture strangely, uncannily human.
Even as it radiated a presence so overwhelming that many who lived in the Dragon-worshipping Kingdom would prostrate themselves simply from seeing or approaching it, the way it carried itself could only be described as something else entirely.
At least, to anyone who knew the real "Divine Dragon," the wrongness was plain to see.

"Mind you, unlike the dignitaries of the Kingdom, I don't go flying into a panic over the 'Divine Dragon's' i-i-image crumbling, or its brand value taking a hit. —But sa-a-ay, what about the fellow beside me, hm-m-m? I'd imagine he has one or two thoughts on the matter, wouldn't you say-y-y?"

"No, Master, there's no need for concern. Needless worry. To go on stringing together words about a past I've already cast off would be nothing short of laughable. Weakness."

Standing before a Dragonhusk that fit not his physical stature but rather ill-suited the stature of his heart, "Aldebaran" narrowed his golden eyes, looking down on those who showed no fear.
Among the assembled figures blocking his path, he focused his attention on a small shadow slowly stepping forward. Naturally, Roy and Heinkel's attention was drawn to that same figure as well.
He didn't know how she had come to stand in such a position, but somehow, without a word needing to be said, he understood — that she was the representative will of everyone gathered here.

Drawing every eye upon her, she — Petra Leyte — thrust a finger up toward the sky and declared,

"—This is a total war. So go ahead and just lie down and take a good, thorough beating, why don't you."
