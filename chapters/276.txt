Arc 4 Chapter 107: "And the Last Trap"

"Alright, I'm counting on you to stall for time just like we planned."

That was what Subaru said to Otto after successfully finishing the search for Ryuzu and rejoining him.

Having had the whole situation explained to him by Subaru the day before, and the day before that, Otto immediately understood this as the signal to set into motion the plan they'd spent two days preparing.

"That's fine by me, but do you actually have a lead on where Emilia-sama went? If you and Emilia-sama can't manage to meet up while we're stalling, then everything we do here is going to end up meaningless, no matter what it is..."

"Can't really say I've got that covered without a hitch. It's exactly because I dropped the ball there that we're in this mess now. But well, no need to worry."

Scratching his head with a pathetic look on his face, Subaru's expression suddenly turned serious.

When a man whose eyes were less "sharp" than simply bad-tempered-looking put on a serious face, it alone made him look as though he were suppressing anger at something.

Even though their acquaintance was short, Otto knew Subaru's character well enough not to mistake it for that — though he couldn't help thinking the man was cursed with an unfortunate face. Subaru, for his part, thought something similar about Otto; and the fact that neither of them was aware of it was one of the things this odd pair of friends had in common.

"I've got a hunch where Emilia is. Honestly, when I heard she'd disappeared I pathetically panicked for a bit... but once I calmed down, there was really only one place it could be."

"Is that so. Out of curiosity, where—no, I'll refrain from asking."

"Yeah? You could take on the role of praising my deduction, you know, if you wanted."

"I'll pass. I've no interest in playing yes-man to you, Natsuki-san — and besides, if I end up caught by Garfiel after all this stalling and start blabbing everything I know, that would be a problem for you too, wouldn't it?"

Shrugging to make his point about the danger of information leaking, Otto got a nod and an "I see" out of Subaru.

In truth, it really was a concern worth having. Otto didn't think of himself as especially tolerant of pain, and he had no memory of ever having endured pain past his limits.

There was no telling whether, pushed to the brink and hurt by a desperate Garfiel, he might not let something slip. Being a drag on the plan in that particular way was the one thing he wanted to avoid.

"Well, if it does end up slipping out of your mouth, I guess there's nothing to do but figure that's just how it goes."

"――――"

Without any particular weight behind it, Subaru could just casually say something like "take it easy, alright?"

His face showed not the slightest understanding of how it might feel to be on the receiving end of that.

Trusted this unconsciously, this thoroughly — who could betray it?

And since he wasn't even doing it on purpose, he really was an outrageous friend to have.

"In any case, I'll do my utmost to pull this off well. After all, whether or not you succeed, Natsuki-san, is going to make a huge difference to my future."

"Yeah. If I go and fail spectacularly, your future's in the gutter. ...If it looks bad, just run, no arguments. That guy's probably not in the mood for jokes today."

"...Yes, I'll keep that engraved in my mind."

At those words of concern, Otto's lips loosened faintly in response.

In their planning session with Subaru, Otto — following the groundwork and preparations he'd already laid — had instructed that the evacuees from Arlam Village be loaded onto the dragon carriages, with the escape to begin a short while after he himself set off as the decoy.

To fool Garfiel's nose into thinking they were carrying a great many villagers, two of the dragon carriages had been stuffed with villagers' clothing, and the plan was for the decoy to take the single, obvious road leading outward.

The escape route for the evacuees had been thoroughly researched by Otto through a sleepless night beforehand, and painstakingly explained and drilled into each of the earth dragons.

There should be no gaps in it. That, at least, was the plan.

All that remained was for Otto's decoy scheme to be seen through, purely and simply, while the evacuees made their escape outside — that alone would suffice.

It was a plan that would get the villagers away from the looming threat of the Great Rabbit two days hence, while also buying Subaru and Emilia time to talk. After that, though, it was easy enough to picture how things would go: Garfiel would return to the village, find Subaru, and a bloodbath would follow—

"――――"

That was something Otto had decided shouldn't be allowed to happen.

To begin with, Otto Suwen was not a man blessed with physical prowess.

As a peddler by trade, he'd picked up a fair amount of self-defense to avoid dangers along the road, but compared to those who made actual combat their livelihood, he fell not just one step behind, but ten.

When transporting valuable cargo, an escort was indispensable — and there had been a time when, trying to save time by cutting through a mountain path, he was attacked by bandits and had to tearfully abandon his cargo and flee.

That he wasn't suited to violence — to solving things head-on with force — was something both he and everyone around him agreed on.

"And yet here I am, doing this — going up against something like that. What on earth am I even doing..."

Lightly wiping his cold-sweat-beaded brow with his sleeve, Otto forced his stiffening cheeks to move into a smile. A merchant's first principle: always face the other party with a smile.

Born into a merchant family, Otto had grown up with that lesson drilled into him — though admittedly it amounted to only about half a lifetime's worth of experience, starting from around age ten.

Still, this habit was nothing to scoff at.

By putting on a smile like this and convincing his body first that "this is just another ordinary tight spot," his body could gradually come to accept the tension as something almost pleasant.

His hands moved. His legs moved. He could still keep running.

It struck even him as strange that, after running this much over such uneven ground, his breath wasn't ragged. The lightness of a mind that had thrown caution to the wind was drawing out untapped strength from every corner of Otto's body.

"That said, even if I've had some kind of awakening, it's only so much. No room for conceit — carelessness is the enemy."

Slipping between the trees, Otto reminded himself to be wary — he was never lucky at the moments that mattered most.

Far behind, the Garfiel he'd left in his wake had not yet spotted him. But of course he couldn't simply keep running away like this forever. Otto's role now was to keep drawing Garfiel's attention and prevent him from returning to the Sanctuary.

The key was to keep Garfiel from ever realizing that he didn't actually need to bother with Otto at all.

Hiding in the forest, Otto unveiled one device after another to draw Garfiel in. That earnest thought was what occupied his mind, through and through.

Yes. In truth, Garfiel had no need to deal with Otto at all.

To achieve his goal of keeping the Sanctuary from being released, the crucial thing was to seize the key figures — Subaru and Emilia. Otto was nothing but an afterthought.

That fact — having paid Otto no mind at all up to now, and thus having his feet swept out from under him like this — was surely something Garfiel himself understood best of all.

Whirling fallen leaves and mana-ore. And the swarm of winged insects packed into the dragon carriage.

With these two ostentatious, harmless traps, Otto had completely succeeded in sending the blood rushing to Garfiel's head.

Garfiel had now lost his composure, his focus narrowed to seeing Otto as an opponent who had to be beaten down — even though, properly speaking, there was no need for that at all.

"That said, if I give it too much time, he'll catch on right away."

And so, against a Garfiel he knew full well it would be most dangerous to approach, Otto had no choice but to keep provoking him while holding a careful, ambiguous distance — never too close, never too far.

Since he'd neutralized Garfiel's sense of smell, as long as he stayed out of sight things wouldn't turn fatal — but the instant he was spotted, the sheer gulf in ability meant Garfiel could close the distance between them in an instant.

It truly demanded the tension and caution of walking a tightrope.

"――――"

Still hidden in the underbrush, Otto peered at the scene ahead.

About twenty meters off, there was Garfiel, glaring around at his surroundings. His sense of smell seemed completely dead, thanks to the effect of an irritant-smelling oil called kisnis doused onto the dragon carriage's wheels. The sight of him searching for Otto irritably, relying only on his eyes, carried the unmistakable air of a wounded beast — not to be underestimated.

To keep drawing his attention, poking at that thing was, quite literally, like sticking a hand into a raging fire — a foolish act guaranteed to leave a burn.

"Alright, I'm counting on you!"
"Aye-aye-o~"

Otto's voice came out as a thin sound, and the answering cry passed through his eardrums and converted itself into meaning.

Following Otto's signal, the forest gave a faint stir.

"Ahh...?"

At the rustle of the wind-swayed trees, Garfiel lifted his face as though sensing something off.

Aimed straight at that face, launched all at once from the trees surrounding him, came mud balls made of rolled-up dirt and droppings.

It was the threat display of small creatures known as forest rats that made their homes in the tall trees.

Naturally, the mud balls themselves had no lethal power, but pelted from all eight directions at once, Garfiel flinched back in a panic to dodge. Still, he couldn't avoid them all, taking several hits to the legs, and clicked his tongue at the stink and the mud sticking to him.

"Gah, what the hell is this! Damn it! Why the— this is that bastard's doing too..."

Wiping the filthy stuff off on a nearby tree, Garfiel suspected Otto's hand in this too. But even as he voiced that suspicion, he noticed something and wrinkled his nose.

—The forest rats' mud balls had no lethal power. They only soiled clothes and left a lingering smell.

But the smell of their droppings had a way of drawing in a certain insect that lived in the forest, and—

"――――ッ！"

Beneath Garfiel's feet, something writhing under the soil closed in and leapt onto his leg as though coiling around it. Crawling up the leg of a Garfiel who choked in surprise was a black, long-bodied, centipede-like insect.

The centipede, as long as a man's arm, crawled all the way up past the knee of Garfiel's left leg where the forest rat droppings had stuck, pressing its grotesque mouth against the traces of the mud ball and biting down.

"Gah! Disgusting!"

Garfiel flipped his claws and knocked the centipede's body away. But more of the same centipedes kept crawling out of the ground one after another, swarming over not just Garfiel's legs but flinging themselves around fighting over the mud balls that had missed him, until the whole area took on a sort of hellish look.

Those centipedes were creatures that favored the tree-fruit seeds found within the forest rats' droppings.

Having had to wander ceaselessly through the forest setting traps, Otto had exchanged words with many creatures and thrown in every "usable thing" he'd come across, one after another.

Contrary to their hideous appearance, the centipedes apparently weren't carnivorous or venomous types — but surrounded by that many of them was threat enough on its own.

And indeed, even Garfiel—

"—! Argh! To hell with this!!"

Fed up with the swarm of clinging centipedes, Garfiel shouted, spit flying.

And then he raised his right foot high and drove it into the ground with all his might, as if to stamp it flat.

An instant later, the earth around him bounced upward in a square shape, as though it had been cut clean out.

"――――"

Witnessing that outlandish sight, Otto couldn't help but gasp.

On the cut-out earth, Garfiel ran his claws and feet through the centipedes left immobile by the shockwave, hunting them down one after another. By the time the raised earth crashed back down with a violent noise, every centipede that had been crawling around him had been wiped out, and the survivors, as if struck with fear, burrowed back down into the ground.

The trees that had served as the forest rats' homes were caught up in the sudden upheaval of earth and toppled as well, and the residents who'd cooperated with Otto seemed to have fled in a mad scramble.

It seemed they'd paid a fairly steep price in exchange for a bit of sugar water.

"Well, I suppose that's business too... whether you can turn an opportunity into a win depends on your own capabilities, so I'd rather they didn't hold it against me."

Faced with a glimpse of Garfiel's true power, Otto held a brief little post-mortem to steady his nerves. Then, silencing his movements once more, he fell back, keeping his distance from the now-walking Garfiel while guiding him toward the location of the next trap.

He hadn't gone two and a half days without sleep, tearing all over the forest, for nothing.

—Because the moment this was all over, he intended to sleep so deeply he wouldn't even dream.

"Something big's coming."

—I know, yes, I know that already.

"Behind. Big. Coming soon. Coming now."

—I told you, I know. I've already accounted for that.

"Gonna die. Gonna die, huh. Poor thing."

—Would you mind not being so pessimistic!?

With the Divine Protection of the Soul of Language unleashed, a ceaseless stream of noise poured into the ears of the running Otto.

It was the voices of insects, small animals, every sentient creature living throughout the forest — and sorting through it all for what was relevant to him was a Herculean task.

About twenty years since the Divine Protection of the Soul of Language had first manifested in him, and about ten since he'd learned to make use of it. Even across that whole span, he'd never attempted anything this reckless.

Even when he'd used the Protection before, to clear himself of a false accusation, the setting had been a city — provincial, but still a city — so the creatures living there had been limited in number.

But here, in the middle of a vast forest, the sheer number of them far exceeded what Otto could handle.

In the air, in the trees, on the leaves, in the soil, under stones — insects and small animals had endless places to live. Listening to the voices of every creature hidden there was something close to having the words of over a hundred people crammed into his brain all at once.

And it wasn't just something he heard.

The Divine Protection of the Soul of Language forced comprehension on Otto. Which meant his brain's activity was consumed processing everything the Protection picked up.

"Guh...!"

A sharp pain shot through his head, and Otto, his upper body swaying, instinctively leaned against a tree. When he raised his sleeve to wipe his face of sweat, it came away stained with dripping fresh blood.

A nosebleed. The blood trickling down his face was surely proof his brain was operating past its limits. His brain creaked intermittently, and the ringing in his ears kept going without let-up.

"Ahh, I didn't know that. So this is what happens if you keep using it — my Protection. Really is a pain to handle, honestly... it's not just convenient, it's a headache too."

Wiping his nosebleed roughly, he rubbed at the space between his brows and set off running again, unsteady on his feet.

The ringing in his ears was still going, but he hadn't the slightest intention of cutting off the Protection. To begin with, Otto alone couldn't even properly sustain this chase.

There were voices — halting as they were — that reported Garfiel's movements to him, as before. There were eyes that kept watch on Garfiel in place of Otto, who couldn't afford to look back.

Securing the cooperation of insects and animals with wills so different from a human's was not an easy thing, whatever others might think of it.

After all, their way of thinking was fundamentally different from a human's.

What pleased them, what displeased them — their common sense was his nonsense, and vice versa. Under such conditions, even trying to negotiate, there was no way of knowing what leverage to use.

Moreover, even among the same species of insect or animal, the higher their intelligence, the more individual variation there was. There were regional differences too — it was common for the same species living in different areas to hold entirely different likes and dislikes.

That Otto had managed, after a fashion, to keep evading a threat like Garfiel this way was thanks to nothing but the fact that he'd been given a preparation period, brief as it was, and had spent every scrap of time and strength he had within it.

—By now, had Subaru found Emilia, and were they managing to talk properly?

It was solely to extend, even a little, the time he needed to talk with Emilia, that Otto was putting himself through this ordeal.

If Subaru's hunch had missed, or things didn't go as planned and no progress was made with Emilia, then it was all a fragile attempt that would come to nothing.

Why was he throwing himself so completely behind Subaru, of all people?

Amid thoughts meant to distract himself from the pain, Otto suddenly found himself wondering that.

That Subaru had saved his life, and that he was cooperating to repay that debt — that much was true.

That Subaru had acknowledged him as a friend, and asked for his help, and that he was cooperating because it couldn't be helped — that too was true.

But was he really the kind of man who, over something that simple, would throw himself so desperately into doing more than was asked of him?

"...Ah, I see."

At that moment, a sensation flickered through his mind, and it triggered his realization of the reason.

And Otto couldn't help but laugh.

Once he'd realized it, it was a simple thing.

The reason Otto trusted Subaru, and lent him his strength, was almost absurdly simple.

"Giving up on being understood, holding your head like that... I should know that better than anyone, shouldn't I?"

The power of the Divine Protection of the Soul of Language — hearing voices no one else could hear.

Otto, who heard the voices of other creatures and knew things he had no business knowing, had been shunned by many people. Having lost those who'd once been his friends, and now unable even to see his family, the Divine Protection of the Soul of Language had become, for Otto, an unwanted tool with no use beyond breaking through emergencies.

But the experience of having carried that Protection was another matter.

Being kept at a distance because of the Divine Protection of the Soul of Language had taught Otto the pain of not being understood by others. He'd come to know the frustration of being unable to fully convey to others what he himself knew, no matter how he explained it. It had given him the bitterness of resigning himself to the thought that he'd never be understood anyway.

All of that was the very same thing Subaru had carried before he'd confided what was inside him.

That was why Otto trusted Subaru — because he saw his own past self overlaid on him — and set off running.

No other reason.

It wasn't only Natsuki Subaru that Otto wanted to save. Through him, Otto had wanted to save his past self — Otto Suwen.

"Fooound... yaaa!!"

"—!?"

The instant he became aware of that other truth within himself, Otto heard another's voice from an angle quite apart from the Divine Protection of the Soul of Language, and was sent rolling across the ground by an impact striking his shoulder.

He was thrown down sideways as though yanked over, and rolled along, caught by the soft earth.

"Bwah, ptooey! Wh-what... guh!"

"Toooo baaad!"

He spat out the fallen leaves that had gotten into his mouth, and the instant he pushed himself up, a claw-tipped foot slammed into his torso. The air was wrung out of his lungs, and he was violently kicked flat against the ground again.

Up and down swapped places in disarray, his head spun, and his thoughts wouldn't hold together. Oxygen failed to reach his brain, and a pain like all the blood in his body had congealed tore through his veins and battered him from head to toe.

"Even with my nose outta commission, I've still got ears. Whatever trick it was, those bugs were makin' one hell of a racket, chirpin' away... but this is the end of it."

"W-well, who knows... just because you caught up doesn't mean the match is decided—guhff!"

"Don't go runnin' your mouth. Ya did real good, but... I can't afford to burn any more time on this either."

Garfiel's foot came down on Otto's belly as he lay on his back, grinding his full weight down onto him.

His ribs creaked; a pressure far beyond what Garfiel's supposedly small frame should weigh bore straight down from above, and Otto flailed his limbs, letting out a pained groan.

"If I stomp down for real, that alone'll blow you to splinters. Ya must've been watchin' from somewhere when I busted up the ground earlier, huh. Same thing'll happen to that body of yours. Wanna try it out?"

"—Sorry, but I'd rather not be on the receiving end of that."

Against the threatening face glaring down at him, Otto answered with a smile like sour grapes. Garfiel was faintly taken aback by Otto's attitude, but—

"Ya got some serious guts written on that face, don'tcha. If I'd noticed before we started tanglin', wouldn'ta had to take this roundabout route."

"…………"

There was something close to admiration in the ring of Garfiel's words.

Listening to it, Otto turned his head and let out a thin breath. At the sight of Otto continuing his faint, rasping breaths, Garfiel narrowed his eyes.

"If it'd been any other time, I probably would've overlooked that of yours as nothin' much..."

"――――"

"But the second ya got serious, the whole damn forest turned against me. Even that first cloud of leaves — that was 'cause the bugs hidin' underneath 'em all took off at once. The ones in the carriage, the field mice's shit-balls, the centipedes, the snakes that came outta the fallen tree, gettin' birds to herd me into a patch of poison flowers — there's gotta be some kinda trick behind every last one."

Garfiel counted off on his fingers the traps that had been laid, the traps he'd fallen for, all the way up to this point.

Listening to his words, Otto went on breathing thinly, endlessly.

Just as Garfiel had seen through it, every one of those traps was something Otto had set while tearing all over the forest — means to sap his mobility and buy time.

Not one of them had missed its mark; each had done its job of drawing Garfiel toward him.

But the sheer number of natural wonders — too many to pass off as coincidence — had made Garfiel realize that Otto had engineered all of it, and that there was a reason he'd been able to.

"Thinkin' ain't exactly my strong suit, but not givin' up on thinkin' anyway — that's what livin' is. So I thought about it. Thought and thought, and figured this: most of the shit in this world that don't make sense has got some Protection's power behind it. —You've got one too, don'tcha."

"…………Heh."

"Dunno if it's a Protection of the forest, or of the earth, or what — but if that's the case, there ain't nothin' you could pull that'd surprise me. Means I gotta finish you off, no carelessness, no mercy, none of that. ...So."

Throwing his words at the unresponsive Otto, Garfiel, still with his foot planted on the finely trembling body, turned to look back. And narrowing his sharp eyes as though in pity, he said—

"Lookin' at somethin' with eyes that stubborn about not givin' up — there's no way I wouldn't notice."

"――――"

Before the turning Garfiel's eyes lay an open clearing, and there, right now, white light was gathering.

It was a coloration entirely different from sunlight filtering through the gaps in the trees — meaning this was a place where mana had pooled so densely it had become visible.

At the sight of a swell of mana so dense that carelessly stepping into it might make one sick, Garfiel scowled and looked down at Otto.

"That's your trump card, ain't it. Different story from all the empty scare tactics up to now. That's got somethin' in it that could actually flip me over. ...If ya could've kept me stringin' along right to the end, maybe you coulda shoved me into that too."

"...Ah... uh..."

Crouching down, Garfiel grabbed the groaning Otto by the collar and hauled him up.

Blood was flowing from Otto's nostrils again, from his brain's overactivity, and at the gruesome sight of the lower half of his face stained red, Garfiel shook his head.

"Admirable, but ya fell one step short of me. Shoulda known your place and stayed quiet."

"My... place, is it..."

"That's right. There's no way you could beat me. —Dunno what kinda trap it was, but you can taste its power yourself."

As though offering a parting gift, Garfiel gently tossed the body of Otto he'd been holding.

Thrown, Otto experienced a brief moment of weightlessness before tumbling across the ground, unable even to break his fall, rolling straight into the dense white mana zone.

Amid the thick, drifting mana, the contents of Otto's already-blurring mind became further contaminated.

His vision spun, his tongue went numb, and his nosebleed kept flowing without end.

A trap. The last trap. Thrown into it, and then — what would happen, how would it go?

"I'll see it through to the end."

Crossing his arms, Garfiel waited for the fallen Otto's end.

Lying face-down, catching that sight at the edge of his vision, Otto gathered together his scattering thoughts and understood where he was, and what he had to do.

—That the last trap had borne fruit.

"...Mind if I ask you something?"

"Ahh?"

Otto planted his hands on the ground and hauled himself up with all his strength.

Not having expected he could still move, Garfiel's eyes went wide with plain surprise. Otto found that satisfying. Ah, so this was what Subaru meant, he thought, understanding now.

Pulling off something you're thought incapable of — that's what's fun about it. It was true. His personality might be a bit twisted for it, but this thrill really did seem impossible to give up.

"Before you got here, Garfiel-san... how many trees did you knock down, how much ground did you tear up, I wonder?"

"I don't get what the hell you're tryin' to say."

"The fact that this much mana has piled up right where I'm standing... means you've made the forest that angry. That's all it is."

As he spoke, a sense of accomplishment let Otto forget his exhaustion and pain.

His shaky speech gained steadiness, and sitting on the bare ground, Otto looked at Garfiel.

Garfiel unfolded his arms and, with a look that showed he'd finally realized he'd walked right into Otto's scheme, moved to act.

But it was too late.

"—Al Dorna."

The mana filling the air circulated through Otto's whole body, and in answer to the incantation, the world changed shape.

—A torrent of earth and rock surged up with tremendous speed and force, slamming into the whole body of the too-slow Garfiel and blasting him away into the far reaches of the forest.


※※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※


"Haah... haah... haah..."

His raised arms trembling, Otto went on breathing with an exhaustion so thick it felt like blood might mix into it.

All the mana that had been drifting around had been poured into the spell and vanished entirely.

The sick, dizzy feeling from the mana had receded, but in its place his whole body, steeped in fatigue, was agonizing.

—The last trap Otto had laid was a simple one, connected to all the many traps that had come before it.

Namely: as a precondition for securing the cooperation of the insects and small animals living in the forest, Otto had proposed that they band together and punish the enemy who was wreaking mischief on the forest surrounding the village.

It seemed that, in the course of his day-to-day life in the Sanctuary, Garfiel had gone tramping through the forest, and from the animals' point of view had behaved with real, thoroughgoing high-handedness.

Sharpening his claws, mowing down trees in place of training, probably even gathering firewood for daily life — seen over the long term, all of it amounted to acts of sabotage as far as the animals were concerned.

Those countless small offenses had piled up, and sadly, Garfiel had come to be regarded by most of the forest's creatures as a big, powerful bad guy.

Through negotiation, Otto had offered to help the forest's creatures teach Garfiel a lesson. And so, while he laid trap after trap and kept them running, in the event Garfiel repeated further destruction of the forest — the forest's residents had promised to concentrate their own mana into a single location, lending Otto the greatest possible strength.

That visible, massive quantity of mana was, in other words, "a trap so obvious it looked like a trap."

Garfiel, having grown wary after falling for so many traps already, avoided it — and, as a final act of spite, tossed the seemingly powerless Otto right into that very spot.

Which meant Garfiel had, with his own hands, helped Otto borrow the power of the whole forest to cast magic far beyond what he could manage alone.

As a result, the resulting torrent of earth and rock had battered Garfiel's entire body, dealing him damage severe enough to be decisive — to a man Otto had barely managed to scratch until now.

Garfiel's complete confidence that Otto had no strength left to fight back had helped push it through.

It was all exactly the result Otto had schemed for.

In other words—

"This time, surely, this'll..."

"—So you're outta tricks too, huh."

Otto, his rasping breath carrying disappointment, was glared at by Garfiel, whose body now emerged into view from among the trees.

His clothes were in tatters, and where his skin showed through, numerous cuts from sharp stones had opened up. Still, he seemed to have managed to protect vital areas like his head, and his gait didn't look to be affected much.

The sheer gap in raw strength had spread even wider than Otto had imagined.

"Honestly, I'm impressed."

"...Is that so."

"Didn't really think you had it in ya to pull off this much. Worse, I went and assumed ya'd given up, and looked down on ya for it. —Forgive me. I did somethin' cheap to a man."

At Garfiel's solemn-faced words, Otto shook his head — he didn't need that kind of apology.

What he'd wanted was one single word: "I give up." But Otto's every last effort, though it had fulfilled its role perfectly, still hadn't been enough to bring Garfiel down.

And so, Otto's resistance ended here.

Checking the feel of his hand, Garfiel flashed his sharp beast-claws — this time, he would show Otto no mercy.

Those blade-sharp claws would, just like the sincerity he'd shown toward Otto, cut straight through his body and take his life.

—Had he done everything he could?

He had the sense that he'd given everything he had.

Whether it was the Divine Protection of the Soul of Language, or the matter of friendship and debts — he'd poured it all out.

And if even that hadn't been enough to reach the goal, then there was nothing more to be done about it.

What Otto himself could do ended here.

And so—

"See ya. —When ya wake up, everything'll be settled."

"Let's just say my one-man battle ends here, then..."

"――――"

Muttering as though the breath were leaking out of him, Otto went limp and closed his eyes.

That attitude seemed, for something so far from resignation, strangely far from resolve as well—

"No way..."

Wondering if there was still something else, Garfiel shuddered again, the fine hairs all over his body standing on end as he swung his head around, wary.

All around, in every direction, there was no sign of anything. If there was something, then it was—

"――――ッ！"

Baring his fangs, Garfiel readied his claws and snapped his gaze upward.

He drew breath, swelling his lungs for a roar. But that motion faltered. His eyes went wide at the sight before him, and from his open mouth came something other than a battle cry.

It was neither killing intent nor hostility — it was a name.

"Garf—!!"

Overhead, a shadow leapt from atop the trees, falling toward him.

A short skirt flashed, and the tip of the wand gripped in her hand took aim squarely at Garfiel's head.

Watching mana converge at the wand's tip and light burst forth from it, Garfiel screamed.


"Why the hell are you here... RAAAAAAAAAM!!"


The next instant, a blade of wind exploded, and a blast of wind tore through the forest of the Sanctuary.
