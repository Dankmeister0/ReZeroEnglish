Arc 4 Chapter 49: "Love Love Love Love Love Love You"

—Hearing the dry sound of footsteps against his eardrums, Subaru felt a wrongness prickling against his skin.

The cool breeze flowing into the tomb carried with it a faint, clinging discomfort. His running feet felt as though they were sticking to the ground, each step stealing away a little more of his will.
The sharp sensation prickling his exposed skin struck his whole body, as if the air itself had sprouted barbs. All told, it was a feeling that made him hesitate to press forward.

—Subaru already knew a sensation very much like this one.

Driven on by a bad premonition, Subaru consciously shook off the clinging discomfort and pushed toward the tomb's entrance.
Down the passage where moonlight faintly filtered in, through the ivy-choked doorway, he dashed as if flying, feeling the illusion of breaking through a membrane of air as he emerged from the tomb.
And then, he saw.

"...You've gotta be kidding me."

Slamming on the brakes, Subaru's body gouged into the dirt underfoot and skidded to a halt.
Even as he stumbled forward gracelessly, nearly pitching onto his face, the eyes fixed ahead of him held something like resignation, a kind of detached clarity.
That was how far beyond common sense the sight before him had strayed.

"A shadow... it's a shadow."

The murmur that slipped from Subaru's lips—it summed up everything in a single word.
A shadow—there was truly no other way to describe the scene before his eyes.

From the tomb's entrance, the view of the Sanctuary that should have spread out before him was nowhere to be found. It was true the tomb stood apart from the cluster of villagers' homes, but as far as Subaru knew, there was no way that not a single building should be visible from this spot.
More than that, even with the round moon hanging in the sky and its pale light pouring down onto the earth, the world before him was far too dark. As if it had fallen into darkness itself.

"—"

Holding his breath, Subaru steeled himself and stepped from the tomb's entrance into the darkened Sanctuary. The soles of his feet left the stone floor and reached the ground of earth and grass. —Or so it should have been.
The sensation underfoot was that of treading on grass, but the ground below was swallowed in thick darkness, impossible to make out. The clinging feeling against his skin hadn't changed, either.

"E-Emilia—!"

Unable to bear the uncertainty of a world that should have been solid, Subaru couldn't help but call out the name that sprang to mind. And once he'd called the name of the girl he knew for certain existed, his mind began to turn, and one after another, faces and names flickered across his thoughts.

"Ram! Ryuzu-san! And Otto too, while I'm at it! You're out there, aren't you?! Come out!"

If this was right after taking the Trial, then Ram and the others, who'd been waiting outside for word of Emilia's result, should have been standing right there before the tomb. That was how it had always gone—Subaru shaking off their attempts to stop him and diving in, getting swept up into facing the Trial himself.
And afterward, when he came out with Emilia, that same group had always been there together to greet the two of them. This time should have played out much the same, without any major difference.

"They're not just missing... this is way beyond that. What's with this murkiness? It's nothing like how dark a country rice-paddy road gets at night."

On a night along an unlit paddy road, on days when you couldn't even rely on starlight, a genuine, honest darkness would fall.
But the Sanctuary's current state was different from that instant darkness. Overhead, the moon shone, and its light reached at least as far as Subaru's own body.
And yet, before that light could reach the ground, it scattered away, producing a vague, unstable kind of night. —It was something like the sensation of a spotlight shining down on him and him alone.
In the darkness, the only thing visible, floating there, was his own form. When he glanced back on impulse, even the tomb's entrance he'd just come out of was swallowed by the dark, invisible.

The sensation of wandering through the night mist the White Whale had conjured came flooding back.
The memory of losing sight of anyone he could rely on, thrown from the Dragon Carriage, not knowing when the White Whale's jaws might close in from behind, walking on and on while even his sense of direction—even his reason for living—grew hazy.
Back then, in the end, he'd simply kept walking, walking, until he broke through the mist and was picked up by Frufoo, Otto's beloved dragon.
So would blindly pressing on this time bring salvation as well?

"Am I an idiot... no, I am an idiot. What's with this defeatist, loser mindset? Not knowing what's happened means not knowing what's going to happen, right? Everyone else's fate is still unknown, and here I am worrying about my own skin—what an idiot."

Hadn't he just resolved himself, in the tea party with Echidna inside the tomb?
Whatever might happen, however much hardship might assail him, if the price he paid for it was only his own life, that was, if anything, a bargain.
Compared to a future where someone precious to him was hurt beyond repair, how fortunate was his current situation, where he could pay with his own life and simply try again.

So what Subaru needed wasn't the ignominy of cowering before an inexplicable situation, being flung about without ever properly grasping what was happening, and losing his life for nothing.
It was to boldly challenge the incomprehensible, and even if he failed to reach the right answer, to seize some foothold toward it—to meet a meaningful death that would let him strike back next time.

"Anyway, what I need to confirm right now is..."

...to find out where Emilia, Ram, and everyone else had gone.

When he hadn't found Emilia's figure inside the tomb, Subaru had thought for a moment that she might have overcome the Trial, woken up on her own, and left. But he quickly dismissed the thought. If Emilia had truly overcome the Trial by herself and woken safely, there would be no reason for her not to rouse him too.

That being touched or called out to during the Trial would interrupt it—Subaru knew this from his own experience of doing exactly that to Emilia.
Strictly speaking, at that point his own consciousness had been in the tea party with Echidna rather than the Trial proper, so there was a chance this premise didn't quite apply.

"Even so, leaving me behind and walking out is way too unlike Emilia."

Carrying an unwaking Subaru outside, or at the very least laying him down against a wall—she'd have done something like that in her own way. It was hard to imagine her walking out without doing anything of the sort.
And this was, if he were honest, a rather unkind conclusion to draw—but Subaru didn't think Emilia could clear the First Trial on her very first attempt.
Having seen her struggle with the same Trial, starting from the very first day and continuing on afterward, he was skeptical from the outset of the theory that she'd cleared the Trial on her own and left the tomb.

So, in Subaru's thinking, it was highly likely that Emilia's disappearance from the tomb had happened without her own will involved. Either someone had carried her out, or else—

"Coming back from the Trial in a daze, with so little composure left that she wouldn't even have noticed me, and walking out of the tomb like that... that's not entirely impossible to imagine, I guess."

But even that didn't explain the abnormality of the outside world sinking into darkness like this.
He could force himself to accept that explanation for Emilia's vanishing from the tomb. But it offered no progress toward understanding the reason, the cause, or what lay ahead for this scene.
At the very least, in Subaru's experience, the Sanctuary had never once been struck by an abnormality like this while the Trial was underway.

As for why the people who should have been waiting weren't there, what stirred unease in Subaru's chest was the threat of the ferocious white rabbit. But he shook his head, denying such a hasty conclusion.
By Subaru's reckoning, the Great Rabbit's attack came on the night of the sixth day spent in the Sanctuary—that is, five days from now. However much that might be moved up, he wanted to believe it wouldn't be pushed forward all the way to the very first night of the Trial.

—He deliberately averted his eyes from the mystery of why Elsa's attack on the mansion had shifted in timing.
That, too, was one of the mysteries still unanswered for Subaru. But if the Great Rabbit's attack, like Elsa's, was also subject to random shifts in timing, that would mean the situation had become utterly unmanageable.
There was nothing left for Subaru to do but believe—simply believe—that no environment so hostile it couldn't be avoided even by wielding Return by Death actually existed.

"What I can probably do right now is... call out while searching for Emilia and the others, and head over to the cathedral area to check on the Arlam villagers' safety, I guess?"

Straining his eyes ahead, Subaru fretted over how low the feasibility of what he'd just said actually was. He had a rough map of the Sanctuary in his head, but it wasn't refined enough that he could walk around it with his eyes closed.
And right now, what he needed to wander this place was exactly that level of memory.
Even reaching a destination safely was, for Subaru right now, an extremely difficult task. And yet the plan of calling out while searching for people wasn't something he could adopt unconditionally either.

"If this situation, this pitch darkness, is somebody's doing... odds are pretty high they're not friendly."

Scorched by a slow-burning impatience, Subaru kept thinking over what the best course of action would be.
If he wanted to hurry and regroup, he should call out. If he was worried for Emilia and the others' safety, that was the best course. But he'd already learned the folly of acting blindly, over and over, driven into his very bones. How many times had that cost him his life in this world?

"...Damn it. If I don't at least confirm what's going on, even getting caught in something terrifying won't sit right with me."

After agonizing over it, Subaru decided to take the cautious path.
Silencing his voice, holding his breath, straining his eyes through the dark, he headed—following the map as best he remembered it—toward the area where the villagers' homes should have been clustered.
The certainty of the ground beneath his feet, and the certainty of the tomb he'd emerged from—these were the only things he could rely on in a world dyed in darkness. It was only fallen into darkness; the Sanctuary should still be right there before him, unchanged—or so he told himself.

"—Uh?"

Slowly, testing each step as he trod upon the grass, Subaru advanced. But before he'd gone even a few steps, his feet stopped.
The reason was the wind.

"—?"

Raising his face, Subaru swept his gaze through the invisible darkness, half aware it was pointless, and sent his thoughts chasing after the wind that had just carried that sense of wrongness to him.

He'd felt it. In the wind that had just blown past him, a distinctive sensation.
Not the cool breeze crossing a grassy plain, not the dust-scented wind blowing into the tomb, not the wind flowing through a blood-soaked scene of grief—but the distinctive rawness that only comes from wind that has touched a living thing.

"Wha—"

Not knowing where the wind had blown from, Subaru turned to seek the answer.
Behind him, going straight should have led back to the tomb, but after only a few steps its outline was no longer within his sight at all.
—No, the reason the tomb wasn't visible had nothing to do with the darkness.

"—Ah?"

"—"

Close enough to feel breath on his skin, right there before him in the pitch-black world, someone was standing.
That figure was blocking his view—that was why he could no longer make out the tomb's entrance.

And the fact that someone had drawn this close without him noticing their approach, and the question of why, having come this near, the figure hadn't spoken to him—in that instant, a storm of questions raged through Subaru's mind.
But that storm of questions vanished almost at once, when a plain, unmistakable answer was given.

In a form more unambiguous than anything could be.

"—I love you."

That was what the shadow said to Subaru, standing right before it, its voice suffused with an affection so thick it seemed almost to melt.

It was a muffled voice.
So indistinct it was impossible to even tell whether it belonged to a man or a woman.
It was unlike a voice run through a voice changer, unlike one spoken through thick cloth—something more opaque, as if some invisible force were at work, an artificial vagueness acting directly upon his own perception.

And yet, the moment he heard the words spoken to him—that whispered declaration of love—Subaru understood intuitively the true identity of the shadow standing before him. And he was struck with horror.

Come to think of it, Subaru had faintly sensed that presence from the moment just before he'd left the tomb.
The dense Miasma prickling against his skin. The Sanctuary sunk into shadow. A stifling pressure filling the air all around, and a world stripped of every last trace of life.
All of it was, in fact, reproducing the exact circumstances of the meetings with the Witch that Subaru was drawn into whenever he spoke a forbidden word—those trysts in a place where time stood still.
Which meant that standing before him now was—

"Wh-why...!?"

"—"

There was no answer. But the presence was, without question, still right there before him.
Moving his fingers, checking his own breathing, Subaru confirmed that time hadn't stopped. The world was still marking time correctly. And yet the Witch stood before him.

Faced with a threat he had never imagined, Subaru's mind went blank, dyed pure white.
It was a shock severe enough to leave behind everything he'd just sworn to himself only moments ago—that whatever happened, he'd scrutinize every detail, that he wouldn't waste a single second.
That was how unexpected it was for Subaru—contact with the Witch, here, at this stage.

His mouth went dry all at once, and Subaru, forgetting even to gasp, went rigid all over. An overwhelming pressure wound itself around his entire body, and he froze like a frog fixed under a snake's gaze.
Being unable to move right now would, without question, make things worse. Even knowing that, his limbs showed not the slightest sign of heeding that warning.

It was a problem on an entirely different dimension from Subaru's heart or his thoughts.
While Subaru's heart refused to yield and his mind burned hotter, insisting he had to do something, his body—and the deeper part of him governing it—looked upon the situation with cold, objective eyes.
In other words: whether he moved or didn't, the result wouldn't change one bit.

"—"

No hostility radiated from the shadow before him. He felt no malice.
But that didn't mean it was indifferent to Subaru.
Quite the opposite, in fact.

"—"

The presence before him poured out an attention so intense it chilled him to the bone.
Blind, obsessive, the sort that made a person wonder what could possibly warrant such devotion—an overwhelming passion that bound Subaru so tightly there could be no escaping it.
—Right now, the shadow had no interest in anything but Subaru.

Inside the shadow, there was only Subaru. Only Subaru. Only Subaru only Subaru only Subaru only Subaru only Subaru only Subaru only Subaru only Subaru only Subaru only Subaru only Subaru only Subaru Subaru Subaru Subaru Subaru Subaru Subaru Subaru Subaru Subaru Subaru Subaru Subaru Subaru Subaru Subaru Subaru Subaru Subaru Subaru Subaru—.

"I love you I love you I love you."

Round and round, the voice echoed inside his head.
His thoughts scattered into chaos, and he could no longer recognize what stood before him. Whether he was standing or sitting, whether he could breathe, whether he was conscious, whether he was alive or dead—he didn't know. He didn't know. He was losing the ability to know.

Fingers reached toward him.
The surrounding shadows rose up, spreading to engulf Subaru's body from every direction.
He had no will to resist. No reason to resist. Resistance, surrender, being swallowed whole, what would happen after—even thinking about it felt like too much effort, and then—

"I love you I love you I lo—"

"—The hell d'you think you're doin', huh—!!"

—In the next instant, a devastating force plummeted down between Subaru and the shadow before him with tremendous force, cutting between them.
Right in front of him, closer still, the shadow and the impact collided, shattering the unseen earth, tearing through the shadow, and Subaru, caught in the shockwave from so close, was sent flying backward.

"Whoaaa—!?"

Tumbling, striking something hard again and again, his whole body plunging into the shadow, Subaru somehow managed to stop his momentum. He shook his head, loosening both his frozen body and mind at once.
His noise-clouded thoughts cleared somewhat; a weight like packed sand still lingered in his skull, but it was some improvement over the sluggishness of just moments ago.
Spitting dirt from his mouth, he raised his face and looked back the way he'd come tumbling from, and Subaru's eyes went wide with surprise.

"Situation's the worst, ain't it. Can you move, huh, you?"

There, edging forward, a figure stood facing the shadow with its back turned to him.
Short for a man, with cropped blond hair and a blunt, rough way of speaking. His combat stance was low, one foot—having just shattered the ground—drawn back, baring his fangs in open wariness.

"Why... you, saving me, Garfiel...!"

"Hah? Don't make me laugh. Can't you see the damn situation?"

Garfiel answered, sounding annoyed, to Subaru whose voice trembled with shock. Keeping his wariness fixed on the shadow before him, he slid his feet, little by little, toward Subaru.

"I'm grabbin' you by the scruff and jumpin'. Might snap your neck, but grit your teeth and take it."

"I don't have some freaky physique where gritting my teeth makes my neck tougher—!?"

Mid-objection, Garfiel's body shot backward at high speed, and in the process, he literally snatched Subaru's body up. Just as declared, he was seized by the scruff of the neck and hauled up; Subaru let out a strangled "Gwuh!" of pain as the breath was choked out of him, but before he could complain about it—

"—!"

—The ground swelled up, and the shadow exploded.
The exploding black shadow generated a wave that surged toward Subaru and Garfiel—who were leaping back—with terrific force, as if to crush them flat. In an instant, the surrounding darkness merged with the wave of shadow, and Garfiel, who'd planted his foot to push off backward, clicked his tongue as the tip of that foot began sinking into the shadowed ground.

"Aah, damn it all! With the ground like this, my Divine Protection of the Earth Spirits ain't workin'—!"

"Garfiel, my feet are starting to sink too!"

"The whole damn area's like this! 'Do bad things and the Witch'll come get you'—ah, ain't that just exactly it, huh!!"

Subaru's limbs, dragged along as he was, also had the parts touching the ground start to be swallowed by the shadow. It was an unfamiliar sensation, different from sinking in water, or in mud, or in a swamp.
The shadow was warm, trying to entangle his body as though wrapping it in soft, smooth cloth. In a moment of peace, he might almost have thought it wouldn't be so bad to let himself be embraced by that sensation.
But under these desperate, harrowing circumstances, that was a judgment he wanted no part of.

"—Tch, don't you dare bite your tongue!!"

Garfiel's shout came with a snort, as he swept his gaze around in a full circle.
He bent his knees and, with his body already beginning to sink, sprang lightly into the air. With his feet caught by the shadow, the distance he covered fell short of even a few meters, but at the point where he landed, he swiftly extended his legs again and leapt, leapt, and leapt once more, over and over.

"Hup—hah—raaah—!"

In a world hidden and invisible beneath night, Garfiel nevertheless reached a place with a building, magnificently. He literally drove his toes into a wall, using it as a foothold to leap again. Landing atop a roof, he tossed down Subaru, whom he'd been hauling along all this way, and let out a breath.
Thrown down, Subaru grabbed hold of something to keep from sliding off, and glared at Garfiel's profile—his face heaving with each breath, dimly visible in the dark—

"Th-thanks for saving me...!"

"The hell? That ain't the face of someone sayin' thanks. You got a complaint or somethin', huh?"

"It just doesn't sit right. ...I never in a million years thought you'd be the one to save me."

"Sounds like I'm bein' treated as some heartless bastard. If you're that unhappy about me savin' your ass, you're welcome to go dive right back into that shadow, y'know?"

To the never-quiet Garfiel, Subaru answered briefly, "I'll pass on that," and sighed.
From behind Garfiel, who made no move to look at him, Subaru studied his manner, his own feelings a tangled mess.

The bafflement of the situation aside, the biggest reason was that he'd just been saved by Garfiel of all people. Right up until the moment they'd come face-to-face again like this, Garfiel had been, in Subaru's mind, the greatest obstacle in the Sanctuary—a man competing with Roswaal for the target of his fury.
He understood perfectly well that the circumstances were different now, which was why Garfiel's manner toward him had changed—but faced with an attitude this contradictory, Subaru found himself unable to settle on how to react.

Oblivious to Subaru's inner confusion, Garfiel glared downward with a bitter expression. Gnashing his sharp canines together, he muttered under his breath, "This is bad..."

"Obviously, don't look like it's got the slightest intention of lettin' us go."

Sliding in beside him as he looked down at the shadow below, Subaru fearfully peered down as well.
The sight drew an involuntary groan of "Ugh..." from him. The Sanctuary, transformed into a sea of shadow, had most of it swallowed in pitch black, its perspective and elevation impossible to properly judge.
But even amid that, he could make out a black deeper still than the surrounding black, writhing, swirling the shadows around it, advancing toward them at a crawling pace.

That was the very culprit that had just tried to swallow Subaru and the others in shadow, the one who'd filled the Sanctuary with a sea of darkness. And its true identity was—

"Garfiel. Do you know what that thing is?"

"Part of me's just goin' with the obvious conclusion that it's somethin' bad, part of me's considerin' the 'no way' possibility, and part of me's optimistically tellin' myself there's no way that could be true. Which one you wanna go with?"

"Forget pickin' one, I think all three are damn close to the right answer. You're... calmer than I expected, facing that thing—"

Subaru stopped mid-sentence as he looked at Garfiel's profile.
Honestly, even while harboring complicated feelings about Garfiel having saved him, Subaru had begun sorting through the situation with total composure.
In the course of that, it struck him as strange that Garfiel—who showed such intense revulsion at the Witch's Scent, who'd turned so hostile just from sensing the Witch's Lingering Scent on Subaru's own body—wasn't flying into a bloodthirsty rage in front of what seemed to be the very source of that scent.
That was exactly why those words had slipped out—but seeing Garfiel's profile wiped the thought away completely.

"You say somethin' just now?"

So he said, turning bloodshot eyes back down below, his fangs seeming, if only slightly, to have begun growing longer.
Anger. Wrath. Fury. Passion run wild. Seeing the whirlpool of crimson emotion swirling in his narrowing pupils, how could anyone claim he was calm?
And at the same time, Subaru remembered there was something he had to ask.

"—Garfiel. The others... Ram and the rest, what happened to them?"

"......"

"By the time I got out of the tomb, the Sanctuary was already sunk in shadow. You're standing here fine and healthy, but what about everyone else...?"

"...In the shadow."

To Subaru, who kept piling on words in the hope of being contradicted, the answer given was a cruel one instead.
As Subaru caught his breath, Garfiel let out a resentful growl from his throat.

"By the time I noticed somethin' was wrong, the ground had just turned into a sea of shadow. If Ram hadn't blown me clear with her wind, I'd have gotten swallowed right along with the rest."

"...And Ram herself was swallowed, just like that? Ryuzu-san and Otto too?"

"Yeah, that's right. The old hag and that noisy bastard, both at once."

Looking down at the sinister, writhing wave of shadow, Subaru found his thoughts turning pessimistic at the word "swallowed," wondering just how much chance the others had of still being alive.
If that thing simply confined whatever it swallowed somewhere in a separate space, there'd be room for hope. But going by the sensation of actually having brushed against it himself, that possibility felt far too optimistic.

"Wh-what the hell even is that thing, seriously... why would something like that suddenly...!"

Elsa. The Great Rabbit. Garfiel.
Subaru had come out here having steeled his resolve to face down the threats bearing down on the Sanctuary and the mansion. He'd intended to boldly confront each and every obstacle, sparing no effort to seize the right answer.
And that resolve, only just hardened, was being swept away by something this incomprehensible.
Just why had this welled up so suddenly, out of nowhere?

"Garfiel... what happened to Emilia?"

"—"

"There was no sign of Emilia inside the tomb. ...Was she swallowed too?"

"—"

Emilia, waking and sensing that something was wrong, dashing out of the tomb.
Knowing her, upon seeing the Sanctuary sinking into shadow, there was no way she'd have simply stood by, wringing her hands. She'd have thrown herself in without a thought for her own safety, trying to save someone, and then—

"Into the shadow... then she's...!"

"After Ram and the others got swallowed, she went into the Sanctuary and that shadow gulped down everything left and right. I chased after it, kept slammin' attacks into it, but it didn't so much as flinch. Then all of a sudden it turned back, so I hauled ass after it, and—"

—and ran straight into the scene from just a moment ago, it seemed.
Even after sweeping through the entire Sanctuary, the shadow had turned right back around the instant it sensed Subaru emerge from the tomb. So the shadow's target was Subaru after all.

A shadow that devoured everything. A whispered declaration of love. And this overwhelming power.
Its true identity went without saying. But—

"Why the hell are you here... the Witch of Envy!!"

"Ain't the time for talkin', this thing."

Beside Subaru, who'd forced the words out as though squeezing them from his throat, Garfiel stood atop the roof with a combative grin. Standing next to him, mindful of his own balance, Subaru too looked down at what Garfiel was watching, and clenched his teeth to the roots.

A vast, swirling quantity of shadow encircled the building beneath Subaru and Garfiel's feet.
And once the whirlpool swallowed the building within its range, it tore it—along with the very earth beneath it—clean away, forcibly dragging it into the orbit of its swirl.

"Wh-whoaaah—!"

It felt like being swept away, house and all, by a great tsunami or a massive flood.
Experiencing that alien sensation—being done in like this by a shadow that shouldn't have had any mass at all—Subaru held on, straining not to be thrown from the shuddering roof.
He held on, but that was no fundamental solution.

"Tch, we're jumpin' again, hold on!"

"—!"

Subaru scrambled to cling to Garfiel's crouched body, and together with his leap, they escaped the roof as it was swept away. Their two bodies, shot diagonally through the air like bullets, misjudged the distance and plunged straight into a cluster of trees, snapping through several branches before slamming into a thick trunk.

"Guhaaah—!"

Garfiel's arm drove itself into the trunk, and through that violent braking he avoided falling into the shadow. Subaru, clinging to his clothes, managed to stretch out his own arm, grab hold of a branch, and shift his body over to hold his posture.
And just as he managed to catch his breath, behind him came the tremendous sound of timber crumpling and shattering apart.
Whirling around in alarm, he saw with vivid clarity the building he and Garfiel had just been standing on, being dragged into the center of the whirlpool and pulverized, smaller and smaller, into fragments.
As it collapsed the building's original shape, the whirlpool of shadow poured what it had swallowed into the main body of the shadow—into that writhing mass—swelling its bulk even further.

"—"

Faced with destruction and devastation like this, Subaru and Garfiel found themselves unable to speak.
And in the brief silence that fell, the shadow's outline wavered and blurred, and in the next instant—Subaru became certain that he'd locked eyes with the shadow's own, even though its overall shape remained indistinct.
