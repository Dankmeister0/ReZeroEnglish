Arc 8 Chapter 12: "Natsuki Subaru and Vincent Abelux"

The instant he swung that fist to its full extent was the instant he most bitterly regretted having become small again.

More than the tragedy of the Isle of the Sword-Slaves that they'd all overcome together as one, more than the "Sparka" fought to bring Gustav to their side, more than the exchange under the moon with Cecilus, in this instant Subaru bitterly regretted that a bigger body would have let him hit even a little harder than his small one could.

"You bastard—!"

The emperor's face, smeared with blood, took the young Subaru's fist on his left cheek, and his eyes went wide.
His gaze dropped to the floor, to Subaru, who'd botched his landing and gone rolling across it. But though there was shock in his expression, there was no anger. Astonishment outweighed humiliation.
The emperor pressed a hand to the cheek Subaru's fist had struck—the fist that had given him surprise rather than pain—and said,

"Do you not value your life? Lay a hand on me, and the flame of the 'Sunlight Sword' shall burn you where you—"

"Yeah, I don't value it! Compared to letting you walk out of here without punching your lights out, getting burned to death doesn't scare me one bit!"

"Wha—"

Under the emperor's glare, Subaru sprang back up onto his feet and spat the words out. The instant he did, his exhausted body wobbled and his back hit the door—which was just as well. Leaning his back against it, Subaru spread both arms and planted himself in the emperor's way.

Saying his life didn't matter was a total lie.
But tit for tat—he didn't want to show even a shred of weakness here. Not after everything this man had said to him.
Whether with words or with fists, he had to hit back at this emperor who'd said whatever he pleased. He couldn't let it go unanswered.

"You're right, all of you. I probably don't make any sense. Even I don't really understand what standard I'm operating on myself."

"...'All of you,' you say?"

The emperor's brow furrowed at Subaru, who bit his lip and glared back at him.
He lowered the hand he'd held to his cheek, his face smeared now with the blood dripping from Subaru's hand, and puzzled over just who—besides himself—Subaru's plural target could possibly be aimed at.
But no matter how much he thought about it, there was no way he could know. The man before him was the emperor; the other man had been nothing more than a foot soldier with no rank at all.
And yet the wound the two of them had dealt Subaru, the scar they'd both pointed to, was one and the same.

"I want to protect the people I love, the people who matter to me, first. That's my honest feeling. But even someone I dislike, even a stranger—if they're in danger right in front of me, I..."

Todd had said Subaru chose who to save based on subjective likes and dislikes.
Abel had said Subaru chose who to save with no rhyme or reason at all, not even likes and dislikes.

Both of them were right, and because both were right, both were wrong.
Because—

"But is that really so bad...?"

"What?"

"Is it bad? Helping the person in front of me on impulse, without shouldering some lifelong resolve, without some grand and noble reason—is that so wrong!?"

Raising his voice, stamping his foot, Subaru made the emperor's black eyes go wide.
The string of out-of-character expressions and reactions was still going, but Subaru pressed on regardless.

"You're right! I think about things within the range I can see, and I reach out within the range my hands can reach, and that's how I've gotten by up to now. What's wrong with that!"

"You dare to brazen it out!? What's wrong with it, you ask? It's obvious! Why do you refuse to look at the bigger picture, moving instead entirely on impulse and emotion? Why do you fail to make use of the Authority granted to you!"

"I'm using all of it! I'm using it, and that's how I'm standing here! Don't tell me not to get emotional!? Don't talk nonsense! It's my business how I use my own feelings!"

"Then at the very least, follow those feelings faithfully! If you would decide who lives and dies by like and dislike, do not try to bend that method to suit you. Your very way of being is riddled with distortion."

"If living straight and true gets you to that conclusion, then give me a twisted, crooked spirit any day!"

Still berating Subaru's stance, the emperor got kicked for it, Subaru talking back all the while. He kicked the emperor's shins over and over, letting the anger he could no longer hold in explode in full.
At that petulant kick of Subaru's, the emperor's face twisted, and—

"This is pointless."

—he tried to cut the conversation off, one-sidedly. Again, one-sidedly.
And so, trying to leave the chasm between their values behind unresolved, the emperor grabbed Subaru's shoulder. He meant to shove him aside just like that, and against the strength of an adult's arm, a child's had no hope.
So Subaru bit that hand as hard as he possibly could.

"—Ngh, you wretch!"

"Ahfnahmnah, I hon't hare!"

Biting down with no mercy at all, he carved tooth marks and a wound into the emperor's right hand. Wrenching it free by force drew more blood, so between that and the knife wound, the emperor was now bleeding from both hands.

Making the exalted pinnacle of the Empire bleed—there was no way Subaru would escape execution for this.

"That's assuming there's a country left to execute me in, you jackass!"

"What are you— guh!"

The instant his hand was wrenched free, Subaru's small body lunged straight at the emperor from the front, pressing his head into the man's stomach and taking his knees out from under him—a brawling technique Weitz had taught him.
Then, advancing to an application of Weitz's lesson, he took the strongest position in a street fight: the mount.
He pressed down onto the chest of the emperor, now flat on his back, grabbed his collar with both hands, and slammed him against the floor. Slam the back of someone's head down enough times and anyone gets knocked out in a hurry.
But—

"Do not, get carried away!"

The first slam succeeded through surprise, the second through confusion, but the third was stopped by sheer difference in strength, and Subaru's hair was seized and he was hauled bodily off from atop the man. He let out a yelp and was sent rolling across the floor, and while Subaru lay there, the emperor got to his feet and turned once more toward the door.

"Not a chance!"

Subaru lunged from behind at the emperor's knee, forcibly stopping the body that had turned toward the door. But he'd put too much force into it, and the result was that he shoved the man forward, sending him sprawling.
The result—

"Bmph."

—came with a dull thud as the emperor's face slammed straight into the room's door.

"——"

There was a sound of a hand slamming hard against the door his face had been pressed to, and the emperor, who'd pitched forward into it, straightened up. Then, turning around, he seized Subaru's collar with both hands and lifted him.
Subaru let out a pained "ugh" as his body spun around, his back slammed against the door and pinned there.
Right in front of him, the emperor, his nose and forehead reddened, glared at Subaru from up close, his feet dangling off the floor.

"What is it you want. What is your aim. What is it you desire!"

"I want to punch you! Punching you is my aim! Punching you is what I want!"

"You...!"

"What about you—what is it you want? What's your aim? What do you actually want!"

"——"

Digging his nails into the hands gripping his collar, Subaru spat right back, shouting, spittle flying. He twisted his neck, opening his mouth to try biting the emperor's hand again, but no matter how hard he strained, he couldn't reach. So instead, he pooled saliva and spat it onto the emperor's hand.
As his hand was fouled with Subaru's spit, the emperor's cheek stiffened for reasons other than the humiliation of it, and he murmured.

"My... desire...?"

"That's right. Forget the grand pretenses—there was something you wanted to do, wasn't there? Otherwise you wouldn't have bothered coming all the way to see me when you're this busy."

"——"

"You're the one who doesn't even understand yourself, aren't you. Out of everything you talked about just now, whichever one you put the most force into, the most feeling into—that's the one that matters most."

The talk of the evacuation status from the capital, the suspicion that Subaru was a "Stargazer." The matter of how to handle Emilia and the others who'd come from the Kingdom, the rupture over how he couldn't understand Subaru's way of being.

Come to think of it, this bastard hadn't even offered a word of concern for Subaru after he'd come back from unconsciousness, and hadn't said a single thing about their reunion since the Mad City to begin with.

But setting all of that aside, the topic that had carried the most heat was obvious.

"—Chisha."

"...Why, Natsuki Subaru?"

The heat that had built up in that shoving, grappling, grabbing match a moment ago was gone now, but in its place a different, colder heat lit the question posed once more.
If the violence a moment ago had been a red flame, this question was a blue one.
With eyes that held that blue flame, burning Subaru standing right before him, the emperor asked.

"I was the one who trained that one. Being what he was, he could perform the same duties as I, without the slightest discrepancy. If anything, one might say he was superior in matters of martial prowess."

"——"

"There was no difference in ability between myself and Chisha. Whichever of us survived, he would carry on the fight against the 'Great Disaster' and see it through to the fullest. —Why."

The emperor's black eyes—no, Abel's black eyes, fixed on Subaru's own, trembled.
The emperor's mask, which he had surely donned with the resolve never to remove it again, was stripped away almost as soon as that resolve had been made, and Abel's face showed through.
And then, not as this nation's emperor but as a single human being who had lost someone precious to him, Abel could not help but ask Subaru.

"Why did you let me, whom you loathed, live, and let Chisha die, Natsuki Subaru?"

Having realized for himself just what it was he wanted to ask Subaru, Abel threw the same question at him that he had before the two of them had come to blows.
The anguish and delicacy carried in that voice were beyond comparison to anything that had come before.

The emperor named Vincent Vollachia had poured everything he had into the Empire.
Called one of the Four Great Nations of this world, ruling over the largest and greatest of them, an emperor who saw and understood all things without exception—that was how Vincent Vollachia had reigned.
For that emperor, Vincent Vollachia, to pose a question not for the sake of the Empire but as a single human being alone meant that this one question carried a weight equal to the Empire itself.

Feeling that in his skin, in his blood, in his mind, in his soul, Subaru's heart trembled.
And at the same time, in contrast to Abel, whose emotions were swelling to overflowing, Subaru's own feelings were quiet.

"——"

Why had a person named Chisha had to die?
Without Subaru's knowledge, Abel and Chisha had each been placed on one pan of a scale, caught in a situation where only one of the two could be saved.
And Abel had intended that it be he himself who shared his fate with the crumbling scale.
That had been overwritten. —Most likely, by Chisha himself, in death.

—Since when had Abel resolved himself to his own death?

Everyone born will someday, inevitably, die. That was nothing like the sort of fact everyone knows and everyone unconsciously ignores.
Bracing for a foretold death was no different from facing head-on the cause of Subaru's own Return by Death.

Abel had, all this while, confronted his own coming death.
With the same resolve that drove Subaru to fight on, believing that the "death" that would come could be changed, and had to be changed, Abel had kept fighting too.

If Subaru didn't die, Emilia would die, Rem would die, Beatrice would die.
Otto and the others, Tanza and the others, everyone in the Shudrak, Flop and the others—countless people would die. If, in exchange for his own life, he could keep everyone else alive.
If Abel had spent every last ounce of his resolve toward that end—then what was Abel now?

"I finished all my preparations to end. And yet—"

Emilia, Rem, Beatrice—they had been kept alive in Subaru's place.
Otto and the others, Tanza and the others, everyone in the Shudrak, Flop and the others—countless people had, someone among them, sacrificed themselves so that Subaru might live, and survived because of it.

That despair—even if no one else in the world could understand it, Natsuki Subaru alone had to.
Only Subaru, who had overturned that very possibility and rewritten reality through his Authority, must not deny outright the despair Abel was tasting now.

He had to understand it, swallow it down, and only then speak.

"—I'm not a 'Stargazer,' Abel."

"——"

The words he'd delivered must have felt, to Abel, like a betrayal—that even now, at this juncture, Subaru would withhold the answer he wanted rather than give it. But no matter how Abel doubted him, the fact remained unmoved.

Perhaps Subaru could do the same things a "Stargazer" could.
Perhaps Subaru held even greater means of intervening in fate than a "Stargazer" did.

Even so, he wasn't all-powerful. He couldn't possibly be all-powerful.
It would be nice if he could be all-powerful, but being all-powerful still wouldn't be enough.

"So I can't save the person you're talking about, this Chisha. I don't even know how I'd do it. But there's one thing I can say."

"...What."

"...If I really did have the kind of power you're talking about, I'd still choose to save you—someone I dislike—over some stranger I don't even know. Not because I'm looking ahead, not for some reason like that. I'd just do it. That's just... what I'd end up doing."

Honestly, he thought it himself—what a hopeless nature to have.
But that was the honest, unvarnished result Natsuki Subaru got when he consulted his own abilities within the vessel he understood himself to be. That was the result, and nothing more.

"——"

Slowly, the strength drained from Abel's hands gripping his collar, and Subaru's body was released.
Scraping his back down along the door, Subaru's body slid down to the floor. Abel's hands still hung on Subaru's collar even now, but the force that had whitened his knuckles had gone out of them.
Subaru could have shaken loose those hands easily if he'd wanted to. But he didn't.
Without shaking them off, he simply called the man's name—"Abel"—and—

"If you say you are not a 'Stargazer'..."

Weakly, Abel's lips made that sound.
But even Abel himself seemed unconvinced by the sound he'd made, and the words wouldn't continue. Abel's black eyes began to wander, searching for the words he truly wanted.

Putting his own feelings into words.
Something even a small child could do, perhaps, that this emperor of the Empire had never once done until now.

"If you would save me regardless of how..."

"——"

"When our very survival as an empire hangs in the balance, to fuss over such trivial matters..."

"——"

"I... I! From the moment I first laid eyes on your way of being..."

"——"

Every one of them trailed off partway through.
None of the words, none of the feelings, were enough to express what Abel truly felt. He stumbled again and again, correcting himself, trying to say it over. Subaru didn't rush him.

"You dislike me, you see me as an enemy..."

Haltingly, sensing with his own sharp mind that the answer he sought didn't lie down the path the words he'd spoken were leading, Abel tried, again and again, to redo what he meant to say.
The man called the wisest emperor since the founding of the Vollachia Empire, whose intellect everyone revered—
And then, repeating it, and repeating it again, at long last, he arrived.

"I..."

"——"

"I had resolved myself to die. ...I never resolved myself to lose."

He had trained only the resolve of one who leaves others behind, and never honed the resolve of one who is left behind.
Perhaps he had even thought as far as the words he would leave at the end. If one searched, there might even be places where his last testament could be found, scattered here and there.
All of it had been meant to be entrusted to those who would carry on after him—with a resolve that never once imagined he himself might end up on the receiving end of that entrustment.

"Why."

A trembling voice said that.
Saying it in that trembling voice, Abel took his hand off Subaru's collar. He brought that hand—his own hand, stained with blood—up to his face, and covered it.

The emperor who never, even in front of others, so much as closed both eyes—covered his face with both hands.

"Why did you die and leave me behind, Chisha...!"

Both hands still covering his face, his voice trembling, perhaps even tears welling in those black eyes, Vincent Vollachia sank to his knees.
A man who had surely never had even a moment to grieve for the loss of someone precious, someone so very precious to him, knelt there before someone he despised, someone in whose presence he need not wear the emperor's mask, with all the impropriety and disrespect that implied.
Watching him, Subaru too let out a long, long, deep, deep breath, and—

"—I'm sorry."

He said that—to a fellow soldier who had fought on desperately against an unreasonable fate and been wounded for it, as one comrade to another still fighting that same fate, though the words could never truly comfort him.

When he'd swung his fist at Abel as the man tried to leave, what had risen in Subaru's mind was the memory of Otto, who had once punched him flying in just the same way.

"You're amazing, you know that? I could never be as smart about it as you. ...I'm definitely never telling him that, though."

Between the irritation and the embarrassment, there was no way he'd ever say it to the man's face directly.
The most he'd consider is, someday when Otto's dying of old age, leaning over his bedside and saying, "Actually, I always thought you were amazing." And Otto—Subaru trusted him enough to know that even on his deathbed, he'd shoot back, "Then why on earth did you keep quiet about it all this time!?" if Subaru actually went through with it.
Anyway—

"——"

Abel, having plopped himself down onto the floor, leaned his back against the wall of the room, hugging one knee, his gaze drifting over the dim interior.
Beside him, Subaru sat cross-legged too, savoring the quiet in a daze.

That opening bit of gratitude and teasing aimed at Otto had been Subaru's genuine feeling, no mistake about it.
Once, when Subaru had tried to shoulder everything alone and was about to break under it, Otto had punched him across the face and thrown at him the words he needed to hear.

Told him not to put on some pointless act in front of a friend. —That experience was what had given Subaru the courage to make the bed springs bounce again.
That said—

"You and I aren't friends, though."

"—. Naturally. Do not speak such an appalling thing. Being friends with you... no, I have no need of friends. I must not desire them."

"Is that the logic of 'I'm not unable to make friends, I just choose not to'? I used to pull that one myself back when I was playing at being a lone wolf, but everyone around could totally tell, you know."

"Your impertinence knows no bounds."

Sitting side by side, neither facing the other, Subaru and Abel exchanged words.
The heat of the grappling and shoving was long gone by now, but neither of them was polished enough to simply cut the conversation short and walk away, either.

Subaru's face, clothes, hands—everything was smeared with blood.
Naturally, Abel's face, hair, and clothes were smeared with blood in various places too.

Inside the room, the sheets were stained red, and a knife lay on the floor. Splattered blood had left spots of mess here and there, and there were signs of the struggle and bloodstains scattered throughout.
There wasn't quite enough blood to call it a murder scene, but it certainly looked the part of a crime scene.

"But even going wild, we couldn't even break a single room. That's the limit of what you and I are, as individuals."

No matter how hard Subaru and Abel thrashed around without holding back, at most they'd manage to break a few fixtures.
If the truly dangerous people of this world went on a rampage, this room would be reduced to rubble in an instant, without a trace. How long would it take Emilia, with her cute face and cute hands, to cutely smash this room to pieces, he wondered.

"You and I going at it isn't cute in the slightest."

"...Just what is it you are even taking issue with."

"You're the one to talk—what are you taking issue with? I'll tell you, your story's so scattered I can't even tell by feel which part you actually want to talk about."

"——"

Abel fell silent, deep in thought.
The reason he'd shut his mouth like that was probably that he was aware of just how scattered his own topics had been. Having shut his ears to what he truly wanted to say and chosen only topics that were comfortable to voice, easy to get out, the result was that his attention had wandered and none of it amounted to anything substantial.
That had been the essence of what Abel had been saying up to now, and a failing he himself was now aware of.
As Subaru waited to see what kind of comeback he'd manage from there—

"Natsuki Subaru, is it true that you are not a 'Stargazer'?"

"...It's true. I'm not any 'Stargazer.' Predicting the future and all that—that's more the Witch Cult's territory than mine. Me and those guys don't get along, you know."

"I have heard word that two Archbishops of Sin were brought down in Lugunica."

"It's not public, but it's three. 'Gluttony' too... no, whether we beat 'Gluttony' or not is complicated, forget I said that. And about Rui too... ah!"

"What."

"You'd better not have done anything to Rui. I haven't forgiven you for what you said about killing her."

Back in the Mad City of Chaosflame, the reason Subaru had ended up parting ways with Abel and the others had been Abel's uncompromising attitude toward Rui.
When it was revealed that she was an Archbishop of Sin, Abel had said he would not forgive her.
That was why Subaru had split off from Abel's group, heading to Yorna's castle alone with Rui, the two of them isolated with no help to call on—

"All kinds of stuff happened and now here we are, but I still haven't forgiven you for it."

"Foolishness. You yourself despised that girl thoroughly at the time, did you not."

"That was then, this is now. Besides, if I let first impressions stick forever, you'd doubt my sanity if you found out who I'm on good terms with these days."

"I doubted your sanity from the moment I met you at the Shudrak settlement. —There is no need for concern."

"Huh?"

"I did no harm to that girl. To begin with, even in that moment, what I intended to say was not that a suspected Archbishop of Sin should be executed on the spot."

"Wha—!"

"You jumped to conclusions. You would do well to reflect."

Told that his was needless wariness, Subaru was left speechless. But even if that were true, the reason Subaru had jumped to that conclusion lay in how curt and cold Abel's manner had been.
It was the accumulation of Abel's attitude up to that point that had led to the distrust in that moment.

"That's on you—your attitude was the problem there—"

"It was necessary. So that you would not, even for a moment, wish to keep me alive."

"——"

"And that, too, was all based on the supposition that you were a 'Stargazer.' That you, who kept achieving unprecedented results in the Kingdom, were accomplishing what no mere mortal could."

"That's..."

"Though, I need not have troubled myself to worsen your impression of me deliberately. Even without acting the part, you are precisely the sort of person I despise most. My contempt, my disdain—they overflowed from genuine feeling."

"You... bas... tard...!"

Whether it was lies or truth, good or ill, a vein bulged on Subaru's forehead at Abel's words.
But just as Subaru had been feeling out his relationship with Abel while walking on thin ice, Abel too, it seemed, had been dealing with Subaru in a state of mind not unlike walking a tightrope.

Subaru on the ice, Abel on the tightrope—an association like that was never going to go well.
Neither of them could see anything but their own footing; neither had even looked at the other's face.

"...I don't know this person Chisha. He drove you off the throne, disguised himself as you, and played at being emperor. I only ran into him in Chaosflame, as the emperor."

"——"

"I don't know what he was thinking, what he was scheming when he drove you out of the castle. But I do get that you're this torn up over him dying, and that he died in the place of you—who by rights should've been the one to die. So..."

Haltingly, saying whatever came to mind exactly as he thought it, Subaru spoke.
Since not facing each other had become the unspoken agreement between them, Subaru couldn't see Abel's expression as he said this. That was frightening, in its own way.
This was a man—someone whom even Abel, of all people, couldn't keep his composure over losing.
Subaru, who knew nothing at all about that person, thought that what he was about to say might be stepping far too deep into another man's heart.

But he thought it anyway.
Even if it hadn't been all that much time, Subaru—who'd been dragged around endlessly by this man calling himself Abel, and swept up into something as huge as the survival of an entire nation—thought it.

"That Chisha guy—he's amazing."

Looking up, Subaru's voice came out bright, maybe out of place given the moment.
He wasn't forcing himself to sound cheerful; it was praise that had simply overflowed from Subaru's heart, genuine and unguarded.
Really, how many people were there, in fact? People capable of betraying the schemes of this man—called the wisest emperor since the founding of the Vollachia Empire, who shaped every single thing inside his own head and considered it right that things proceed exactly as he willed—and of magnificently accomplishing their own aim instead?

Just that alone, Subaru couldn't help but think, was more than worthy of praise.
On top of that—whether it had also been intended that the Abel left behind would be moping and sulking like this, who could say.

"You said it wouldn't have made much difference whether it was you or him who survived. If that's true, then there's only one reason he set up this situation."

"...You."

To Subaru, chattering on as things occurred to him, Abel spoke in a low voice, still not turning to face him.
That tone—one couldn't quite call it anger, nor quite say it wasn't—wasn't meant to condemn Subaru for talking as if he knew, but rather—

"Do you claim to understand? Why it was that Chisha deceived me?"

—was meant to urge him on, to tell him to say the conclusion he'd arrived at.

"——"

Abel wanted an answer.
An answer that even his own clever mind couldn't work out. That it might come from the mouth of Subaru, who knew nothing of this man named Chisha—he didn't expect it, and yet he did, all the same.
That expectation weighed on Subaru somewhat as pressure, but—

"At this point, it doesn't matter what you think of me anymore."

Once he'd resolved himself to that, there was no reason left to hesitate about saying whatever he thought.
The reason, as Subaru saw it, that Chisha had left behind Abel instead of himself.
It was—

"It pissed him off—the fate trying to kill you, and you giving up in the face of it."

"—Ha."

"There's also the pattern where he just wanted you to live even if it cost him his life. But I don't think that's it. Because you don't exactly live in a way that makes people care about you that much."

Even if he himself dies, wanting the other person to go on living—Subaru could understand feeling that way.
But he thought that, to hold such a feeling, you'd need to hold that person dear enough, and have reason enough to be made to feel that way, about them.

For Abel—who'd resolved himself to his own coming death and been desperately tamping down the ground for those who'd come after—to be cared for like that seemed hard to believe.
So, Subaru's answer was this.

"Chisha was pissed off at you. So he proved it."

"Proved it, you say? What... that he could deceive me? That I am a fool?"

"That you're a smart idiot—Flop-san and Mizelda-san have probably already figured that out, I bet. That's not it... it's about fate. How long its reach is."

"Fate..."

As if saying that word for the very first time, Abel murmured it, rolling the unfamiliar thing over his tongue.
In truth, for Abel, who had confronted every matter head-on and kept resisting purely through his own scheming, every obstacle had been something that could be put into words—he might never have been conscious that he was up against something as shapeless and boundless as "fate."

But without a doubt, the enemy Abel had unconsciously admitted defeat to was fate.
And at the same time, Chisha had used his own life to prove something, by keeping Abel alive.

"You can fight fate. —There's not a single reason to give up on it."

"——"

Faintly catching his breath, Abel fell silent, as if struck through the heart by the words delivered to him.
If he'd never even thought his enemy was "fate," he could never have considered fighting it, resisting it, either. He would never even have entertained the thought.
But—if that "enemy" were made clear, who exactly it was—what then?

The wisest emperor since the founding of the Vollachia Empire—once he knew the identity of his "enemy," what would he do?

"—Abel, I'm not a 'Stargazer.'"

"——"

"But—"

Beside the silent Abel, Subaru spoke, resting a hand on the knee of his crossed legs.
Asked, denied, asked again. Denied, asked, denied again.
Even without being able to explain or prove his own Authority, his Return by Death, there was something Subaru had settled in his heart through this back-and-forth.

Perhaps it was also an answer to the man he'd parted from without ever managing a clear counterattack, having only ever been outmatched and cornered—Todd Fang.

"Within the reach of my own hands, I'll save as many as I possibly can. But even so, if there's really, truly a life I can't scoop up in time, that's..."

"...Isn't that defeat, against fate?"

"—. I can't win every single time. Because I'm not the only one fighting fate."

He hadn't been able to save everyone.
And going forward, Subaru couldn't declare that he would save everyone either.
Scoffing "bring it on" at fate, swearing to keep fighting it, and yet, for the lives lost even so, Natsuki Subaru would go on grieving, mourning, and saying so, again and again.
And so, to reduce the chances for regret, and the chances for tears—

"Lend me your strength, Abel. Then I'll lend you mine."

"——"

"From here on, I'll cooperate with you fully! ...Well, I can't just decide that on my own without talking to Emilia and the others, but that's the gist of it. So—"

Saying that, Subaru slapped his knee hard and stood up from where he sat.
Then, without looking at Abel's face, he walked slowly across the room, over to the bedside. Glancing sidelong at the bloodied, disheveled sheets, Subaru reached for the fruit basket.
There sat the Appa that Abel had split in two and left. Its white flesh was starting to turn brown from exposure to the air, but Subaru picked it up regardless.
And then—

"That Appa you split in two—I'll help you finish eating it."

He tossed one half of the split Appa to Abel and bit into the half remaining in his hand.
A satisfying crunch, and a rush of sweet juice spreading through his mouth—Subaru looked at Abel's face as he sat there on the floor.
Abel, catching the Appa thrown to him, looked down at it, and after a slight hesitation, bit into it.
The same satisfying sound rang out, and the same sweet-tart flavor must have spread through his mouth as well.

Liking or disliking, hating or loving—none of it had anything to do with that taste.
Between two living beings, there was no difference in the taste of the Appa they shared.

"Natsuki Subaru."

"What."

"I do not like you."

"Gnuh."

"—But I have need of your strength."

Saying that, Abel slowly rose to his feet from where he sat. Still holding the half-eaten Appa in hand, he fixed Subaru with those black eyes.
All the uncharacteristic, emotional coloring he'd shown inside this room, he hid once more behind the self-awareness of being emperor—but with just a hint of it still lingering, faintly, in his eyes and voice, he said,

"I apologize for my many discourtesies. Knight of the Kingdom."

Watching the emperor of the Sacred Vollachia Empire bow his head like that, Subaru laughed.
He laughed, and biting into the rest of his Appa, said,

"I'm not apologizing for any of my discourtesies, Emperor of the Empire."

And so, in a relationship that had gone wrong from the very start, he took one step toward setting right what had been mismatched from the beginning.
