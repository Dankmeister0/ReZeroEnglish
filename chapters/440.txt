Arc 6 Chapter 36: "A Place to Put One's Relief"

—What did "isekai" mean?

"——"

At Echidna's question, Subaru held his breath and fell silent.
In that moment, every gaze in the room converged on him. The emotions behind those gazes varied, but if there was one thing they held in common, it was probably something best called *suspicion*.
Regarding the word "isekai," everyone present treated it as something wholly unknown.
Which meant that Natsuki Subaru, before he lost his memories, had never once explained it to any of them.

"Isekai... now that you mention it, you did say something like that from time to time before."

"I recall hearing it. Given your usual conduct, I dismissed it as the sort of nonsense not worth minding."

"Emilia-chan I get, but Ram's level of indifference toward me is something else..."

Emilia, finger to her lips, wore an expression of adorable, earnest thought—a stance entirely opposite to Ram's.
At Subaru's sour face in response, Ram gave a cold "hmph" through her nose and brushed it off. But thanks to that cushion she'd provided, the initial shock was softened somewhat.

Emilia, Ram—even the women he'd supposedly been traveling alongside all this time—the earlier Subaru had never confided in them about the "different world."
What that meant, he couldn't say for certain, but—

"Was there some kind of restriction? When I got sent to this world, did some god or goddess make me swear not to talk about it, or something... For that matter, what mission do I even have, being here?"

He tilted his head, but not even the faintest hint of a "mission" crossed his mind.

Thinking it over, there was surely someone's design at work behind Subaru's summoning to another world.
At present, based on Subaru's knowledge, that someone was most likely something in the category of a "god."
That "god"—whom his memory retained no trace of—had presumably imposed some role on him before sending him off to another world. That, at least, was the standard convention for this sort of isekai story.
So if losing his memory was inconvenient for said "god," he'd like to hope for some kind of follow-up support to improve the situation. He wanted to believe he wasn't dealing with some cruel "god" who'd toss him into an isekai fantasy world without generous benefits.
Or else—

"—Actually, I should probably check whether this is some kind of forbidden topic or not."

"Subaru?"

Wetting his dry lips with his tongue, Subaru cast about for a counter to the situation he found himself in.
Catching his mutter, Beatrice looked at him uneasily; he winked at her, and resolved to address the question that had been thrown at him—"isekai."

Even granting that this statement might, in the worst case, be forbidden by some "god"-like entity, it seemed unlikely that touching the taboo would bring some irreversible penalty.
Surely there wasn't some malicious being who'd strike him with unbearable pain the instant he brushed against the taboo.
With a touch of anxiety and tension in his chest, Subaru steadied his breath lightly.
And then—

"So, um, isekai is written with the characters for 'different' and 'world'—isekai... in other words, it means a world separate from the one we're standing in right now."

"A separate world, 'isekai'..."

"Right. And so, that's what isekai means..."

Following Subaru's explanation, Emilia repeated the word, as if testing the feel of its sound in her mouth. That reaction wasn't hers alone—everyone present was likewise testing the word out.
Just as the pronunciation "isekai" suggested, they all received the word itself as something unknown.

Given how little grasp they had of the word "isekai," it seemed the very notion of a different world wasn't a common one in this world.
At the same time, Subaru patted himself down here and there.

"...Doesn't seem like anything in particular happened."

Saying it aloud, Subaru confirmed that nothing had happened to his body.
So far, there was no sign of any abnormality within the range he could perceive. No pain, no numbness. No strange voice sorrowfully intoning, "You've broken the rule, haven't you," nor did everyone around him transform into cranes while lamenting, "I told you not to do that."
The anxiety he'd worried over had resolved itself without a hitch.

"Well, this is unexpected in its own way..."

He'd been convinced there was some reason he hadn't spoken of the other world, but by the looks of things, nothing in particular had tripped him up.
As Subaru tilted his head at that anticlimactic feeling, Julius raised a hand and said, "If I may."

"I understand the meaning of the word 'isekai.' It could be taken as a coinage typical of you, but I find myself with some thoughts on its implications. Why do you invoke this 'other world' so often?"

"Ah? Ah, oh, right, yeah. Just explaining the word isekai doesn't really tell the whole story, does it. Um, that is to say..."

"To say?"

Pressed by Julius, Subaru admitted his own explanation had been insufficient.
And having admitted as much, he cleared his throat once, and—

"I'm from that isekai thing. I wasn't born and raised in this world—I'm an outsider who got mixed in partway through. So, to me, this is the other world."

—Thus he plainly declared his own position and circumstances.


※※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※


—He himself was a person who had come to this world from another world. Pleased to meet you.

Broadly speaking, that was the gist of what Subaru had confessed.
The bewilderment on the side of Emilia and the others, hearing such a bold confession, was not hard to imagine. After all, this was a bombshell dropped on people who hadn't even known the existence of "other worlds" to begin with.
It wouldn't be strange for them to feel their whole common sense overturned, as though the world itself had flipped upside down.

Only after finishing his confession did Subaru realize this concern, and he cursed his own carelessness.
If this led to Emilia or Beatrice being swallowed up in a whirl of confusion and grief, then as the perpetrator who'd caused it, Subaru bore an obligation to take responsibility.
How, exactly, was he supposed to comfort a beautiful girl reduced to tears? He had no experience with that.

So, in the span of a few seconds, Subaru harbored such worries, and glanced at the faces of those who'd received his confession.
He expected them all to have gone pale, thoroughly thrown into confusion. And yet—

"...Um, so, is that basically saying it's beyond the Great Waterfall?"

"Huh? The Great Waterfall?"

Subaru's eyes went wide at Emilia, who furrowed her brow thoughtfully and hesitantly broached the question.
An unfamiliar word—*bakufu*, waterfall or something, he'd guessed, but "Great Waterfall" would be a bigger version of that. Was he to take it as meaning some enormous waterfall?
—No, wait, what did a giant waterfall have to do with another world in the first place?

"No, that's totally different. Nothing to do with waterfalls. Not a waterfall—I'm talking about a world that's completely different from here, in terms of common sense and everything..."

"But that's exactly what lies beyond the Great Waterfall, isn't it. However they phrase it where Barusu is from... no, perhaps it's the reverse from Barusu's homeland's perspective. Across the Great Waterfall, these nations here would be the 'isekai.' That would explain his eccentric speech and conduct too."

"Indeed. The reason no amount of investigating Natsuki-kun's background ever turned anything up is that he hails from beyond the Great Waterfall."

"Wait, hold on, hold on, you're all just accepting that so easily!?"

Not just Emilia—even the seemingly rational Ram and Echidna nodded along to that conclusion.
To Subaru, it was a bewildering degree of trust placed in some mystery waterfall. The idea that there was another world beyond the Great Waterfall—on the far side of a waterfall—just what wonderland-through-the-rabbit-hole logic was that?

"Subaru, who's forgotten so much, might not have any recollection of it, I suppose. But the Great Waterfall... the water's edge at the end of the world—there are a few records left behind of people who came from beyond it, I suppose."

"The water's edge at the end of the world... I don't hate the sound of that, but..."

"The Great Waterfall is said to be the boundary between this side and that, which none but dragons can cross. No one knows what lies beyond. ...So if, on rare occasion, someone did cross that boundary, no one would have grounds to deny what they said as lies or falsehood, I suppose."

Beatrice explained the groundwork behind the audience's ready acceptance.
The content was somewhat hard for Subaru to picture, unfamiliar as he was with the particulars of this world, but taking the word "Great Waterfall" together with the explanation just given, what he could infer was—

"So this is like some floating continent kind of place, and only dragons can cross over to other continents?"

"Hah."

"Don't sigh like that, so obviously disappointed. It hurts, you know."

Ram regarded Subaru's attempt at an explanation with a look of profound disappointment.
Still, to greater or lesser degrees, the rest of the group seemed to share the same reaction to what he'd said. In short: the floating-continent theory was off the mark.

"Fine, if it's off the mark, it's off the mark, understood. ...Though if that theory had been right, I was kind of looking forward to riding on a dragon's back for a nice, refreshing epiphany."

"Talk like that, and your dear Earth Dragon might get jealous, wouldn't he-e-e."

"Oh, right. That black Earth Dragon—that's mine, isn't it. I'll have him give me a ride later."

"Me too! Me too, ah'm gonna get jealous too, y'know! Master, ah think you oughta cherish and dote on and love me more, y'know~. Now that you've lost your memory, it's the perfect time for a relationship restart, y'know! From zero, y'know!"

"You're noisy, and quit clinging to me. Also, don't go poking at the circumstances of my memory loss so casually just 'cause it's none of your business."

Shaula, hugging her knees, joined the exchange with Meili, chiming in raucously.
Shaula's remarks pressed in without much mercy, but for Subaru right now, that actually felt like a relief. —Being fussed over too carefully was its own kind of exhausting.

"——"

Subaru cast a glance at what might be called the mood in the room.
Back in his original world, he'd had quite a reputation for "not reading the room," but even that depended on the time, the place, the circumstances. However low his aptitude, if the flavor or the color was strong enough, he could still catch it, still see it.

Not to mention Emilia and Beatrice, but even Ram, Julius, and Anastasia-Echidna—their attitudes betrayed consideration toward Subaru.
For better or worse, the Subaru before he lost his memories must have gotten along well with them. Envious. He'd honestly like to be told what kind of magic that had taken.
Though even if he were told, he doubted the current Subaru could ever put it into practice.

"Anyway, to sum up what we've got... the edge of this world is a waterfall called the Great Waterfall, and every so often someone comes from beyond it. And apparently I'm one of them."

"Listening to what you've said, that's the only conclusion I can draw... is that wrong?"

"Hmm."

At Emilia's dubious glance, Subaru crossed his arms and groaned in difficulty.
To be precise—no, in fact quite significantly—this understanding diverged from what Subaru had actually meant. Still, even if he were to earnestly expound at length here on the difference between "isekai" and the Great Waterfall, it seemed unlikely to improve the situation.

"In short..."

This world had no concept of "isekai" as such.
Whether what Emilia and the others understood as "beyond the Great Waterfall" actually pointed toward the world Subaru had come from, or the existence of some other world, he couldn't say. It was entirely possible that there really was some other continent-like thing on the far side of the waterfall, and that records of some Columbus-like figure who'd come from there had simply been preserved as an "eccentric."
If that were the case, then a correct understanding of "isekai" was, at this point, simply beyond reach.

"By the way, before I lost my memory, I never talked about the other world, right?"

"Now that I think back on it, you did say 'isekai' every once in a while... but this is the first time you've ever properly talked about it like this."

"Same as Emilia, I suppose."

Emilia and Beatrice answered in words; the others affirmed the question through gesture.
The previous Subaru hadn't spoken to them of the "isekai." Was that because he'd judged, as the current Subaru now had, that talking about it wouldn't earn him any understanding?
If not that, then—

"—Because he'd locked it away in his heart, telling himself he mustn't speak of it, huh."

"Subaru?"

"Nah, nothing. Anyway, if there are other so-called isekai people besides me, do you happen to know any? Could be people from beyond the Great Waterfall, or drifters, whatever you want to call them."

Perhaps there were a great many drifters from other worlds, and perhaps there even existed some sort of self-governing community made up of only such people.
But at that hope of Subaru's, Julius slowly shook his head.

"Regrettably, the existence of what you'd call a drifter is exceedingly rare. To begin with, those who go around claiming to have come from beyond the Great Waterfall are, in the vast majority of cases, either liars or people prone to flights of fancy. The sort who fabricate fairy tales to court people's attention or money."

"Ah, yeah, well, I guess that makes sense..."

Precisely because it was a tale no one could verify, fabrications could sprout as many fins and tails as anyone liked. With both dreamy stories and melancholy ones free to be imagined and invented, that made for fertile ground for delusion.
Even supposing there were genuine isekai people like Subaru among them, their words, their deeds, whatever they left behind would have been buried under a mountain of falsehoods, impossible to ever find.

"—Honestly, my curiosity about what lies beyond the Great Waterfall knows no end. However, it seems this matter has no bearing on our present circumstances. I think it wisest to close this topic here, placing the emphasis on the single point that this accounts for Natsuki-kun's knowledge and unclear origins."

Just as an awkward silence threatened to settle in, it was Echidna who cut the topic short like that.
Given that it was originally she who'd steered the conversation toward Subaru in the first place, there was something less than satisfying about it, but regardless, Subaru had no objection either.

—Besides, the more he talked about his original world, the more he'd have to face the problems he'd abandoned and cast aside back there. That weighed on his heart, and hurt.

He wanted, for now, to forget all that by focusing on the emergency right in front of him.

"That's about it for me. I get that everyone's worried and, y'know, loves me and all, but sitting around chatting isn't going to get us anywhere. So, why don't we—"

Whether or not it was the direct cause of his memory loss, he didn't know.
But he wanted to confirm what had happened at the place the memory-intact Subaru was believed to have last headed.

"Shall we go confirm what happened in the archive of 'Taygeta,' the one in question, I suppose."

At Beatrice picking up where he left off, Subaru nodded back deeply.


※※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※


And so, with that settled, the group set off toward the archive called "Taygeta."

Subaru was tense wondering just what awaited them, but even before reaching their destination, the journey there was already quite the ordeal in its own right.
And that was because—

"Um, Emilia-chan? Why exactly are you gripping my hand so tight?"

"Eh? Well, because I'd worry if I let go of you. I need you where I can reach you... and if that's the case, I figured we might as well hold hands from the start."

"I do like how that conclusion cuts straight to the point, but this is, like, seriously more embarrassing than 'quite a bit'!"

Aware of the heat in his cheeks, Subaru lifted the hand Emilia held.
His hand was gripped, without any ill intent, by slender, lovely fingers with a soft grasp, and Subaru grew honestly worried about whether his palm was sweating.
At Subaru's words, Emilia raised her eyebrows as if annoyed, saying "Honestly."

"Saying that, and yet you're holding hands with Beatrice too, so it's the same thing, isn't it."

"Between holding hands with a little girl and holding hands with a beautiful girl, there's like, a cold rain falling every day, or a gulf as wide as clouds and mud, or the difference between the moon and a soft-shell turtle but for little girls and beautiful girls, y'know."

"Sorry, I have no idea what you're saying."

Though he flustered and lined up excuse after excuse, Emilia showed no mercy toward a Subaru with a prior record of losing his memory as a result of being left unattended. Curse you, memory. And curse the self who dropped it.

"I can't let that last comment slide, but Emilia's right, I suppose. Subaru ought to behave and let Betty and Emilia protect him thoroughly, I suppose."

"Even so. If this were the young-married-couple style, with Beatrice in the middle and me and Emilia-chan flanking her on either side, I'd welcome it, but isn't this just uncool?"

"Subaru wouldn't be Subaru if he weren't uncool, I suppose."

"What kind of evaluation is that!?"

He caught a thoroughly unflattering assessment from Beatrice too, whose hand he held on the opposite side from Emilia's.
Chief among the concerns of these two, it was the constant string of small considerate gestures that nagged at him.

Julius, walking at the head of the group, kept glancing back repeatedly; Echidna, following behind Julius, took the trouble to explain what lay around every corner in turn.
Ram was sharp-tongued whenever she spoke, but Subaru—and Subaru alone—knew that she, straining to keep up appearances, was suffering more than she let on. He wondered if he ought to consult someone about it, but he couldn't judge who it would be appropriate to confide Ram's circumstances to.

Given all that, the only ones offering Subaru any spiritual comfort right now were Meili and Shaula, chatting easily at the very back of the group.

"Well, it's not like it's useful for anything at all, but..."

"...Are you thinking something really rude right now? Mister."

"Master's head is always like that. Ain't worth worrying about it, y'know. Take a peek inside just once and it's solid pink, honestly makes ya recoil. Peach-colored brain, y'know. Indecent academy, y'know."

"It's neither indecent nor an academy. Where do you even get that stuff from."

"From Master, of course. All my knowledge, every bit of it, comes straight from Master."

Just talking with her was enough to give him a headache. But the fact that even that felt comforting was itself a serious symptom.

Anyway, trading such exchanges along the way, the group climbed the stairs and arrived at the space known as the third floor, "Taygeta."
It had been a journey more tiring, in time and distance both, than expected, but—

"So this is 'Taygeta'... just like I heard, it's nothing but books."

Subaru swept his gaze over the vast collection of books housed in Taygeta and let out a sigh of admiration.
Indeed, this was a sight beyond his imagining.
Subaru had been quite the reader himself (of light novels, manga, strategy guides), but he'd never once experienced being surrounded by this many books at once. Of course, compared to something like a national library, that might win out—but that victory would owe itself purely to a difference in space.
In any case, this spectacular sight didn't just represent an enormous collection—

"I was told what's kept here are 'Books of the Dead'... which means, basically, there are as many dead as there are books here. ...If they've been recording this without pause since the days of Homo sapiens, that's a literally mind-boggling volume of records."

He didn't know the scale of this world, but if every record of the dead had been made into a book without exception, then the quantity would be uncountable. Even if he wanted to search out the target book—the name of some dead person he'd known—it seemed utterly hopeless to ever find it.

"Modern libraries pretty much need a search function these days—is there anything like that here?"

"That, whatever a 'search function' is—I wouldn't know, I suppose. But there's no librarian like Betty here. We'll just have to check by feel, I suppose."

"Sounds like searching for a hundred-yen coin dropped in a desert..."

Deflated, Subaru reached for the nearest book at hand.
The volume he pulled from the shelf slipped easily into his hand, and looking it over, he sensed nothing particularly special about it—an entirely ordinary book. Aside from the title, there was no design to speak of; it could be called an extremely simple piece of work. If there was a problem, it was—

"Oh no. I can't read the title."

The presumed title, characters that squirmed like earthworms, was utterly indecipherable.
From what Emilia and the others had said, a book's title was the name of the "deceased." If it was someone he'd had some connection to in life, he could apparently read back their memories—but with the characters like this to begin with, it didn't seem like Subaru would be able to make sense of any of it. —Though with his memory in its current shaky state, it was questionable whether that connection would even mean anything.

"Hmm, this might be tough... wait—"

"Subaru!"

Turning around in a mood of helplessness, Subaru was startled badly by the sudden voice.
In the next instant, the book in his hand was wrenched away as if snatched, and Emilia, having done the snatching, clutched the book to her chest and stepped back sharply.

"Eh, um, Emilia-chan?"

"You can't do something so careless! Subaru, do you understand? You might well have lost your memory right here, you know!"

"No, that's overblown..."

At Emilia's words, her face drained of color, Subaru gave a wry laugh and scratched his cheek.
But it seemed Subaru was the only one taking the matter lightly.

"Subaru, Emilia's right, I suppose. You should refrain from acting on impulse, I suppose."

"I agree as well. Rash action ought to be avoided."

"Mgh..."

Hit with follow-up warnings from both Beatrice and Julius, Subaru's throat tightened.
What welled up in his chest wasn't resentment, but the shame that comes from embarrassment. Admittedly, perhaps his action had been a bit too careless.

"Pfft! Master's getting scolded, y'know! And now he's all mopey about it, y'know! But but, ah'm totally fine with Master even when he's pathetic and acting like a naughty kid, y'know!"

"Shut up. Don't make fun of someone who's honestly reflecting on themselves."

"Now, now. It would be a problem if the conversation got derailed again. Indeed, Natsuki-kun's action was somewhat rash... but something has come to light because of it."

Shaula, hand over her mouth, laughed uproariously at Subaru's blunder. Echidna cut in between her and Subaru, who was about to snap back at her.
Then, gently smoothing her scarf with her hand, she said,

"Natsuki-kun found himself unable to read the book's title. Correct?"

"—? Yeah, that's right."

"What we can infer from this is that when someone loses their memory as he did, it impairs their literacy—or perhaps their cognitive faculties more broadly. His references to another world, too, might stem from something of the sort..."

"Sorry to disappoint, but Subaru only started learning to read and write after coming to Roswaal's mansion, I suppose."

"——"

Echidna, who'd been speaking with such confidence, fell silent under Beatrice's interjection. Paying no mind to Echidna's reaction, Beatrice crossed her short arms and continued.

"Before he came to the mansion, Subaru couldn't even read or write the I-script, I suppose. The elder of the sisters who served as his tutor knows that too, I suppose."

"Yes, just as Beatrice-sama says. Originally, Barusu could barely read or write at all, so Ram devotedly tutored him. ...Given his current sorry state, it seems that effort went to waste."

"My bad! I didn't know there was an episode like that!"

At Ram picking up the explanation with a note of grievance, Subaru apologized in a raised voice. Off to the side of their heated exchange, Echidna cleared her throat.

"Ahem. It seems I was off the mark. Please forget my opinion."

"No, it's not so far off the mark as all that. At the very least, this has revealed that Subaru's memory loss dates from sometime after his arrival in Lugunica."

"In other words, it's a fact that Barusu is nothing but useless baggage in this archive."

Ram tacked on that scathing addendum to Julius's confirmation. Since anything he said now would only dig him in deeper, Subaru held his tongue, while Julius dipped his chin and said, "Lady Ram's words are correct."

"However one puts it, in any case, we couldn't very well have Subaru reenact last night's events. It would be best for the rest of us, apart from him, to search for clues here."

"Yes, that would be the safer course."

"I have no objection. Emilia-sama, Beatrice-sama, are you both agreeable as well?"

At Ram, arms folded, posing the question, Beatrice nodded with an "Understood, I suppose." And Emilia turned her earnest eyes toward Subaru.

"Subaru, please, just wait quietly, okay? I promise, I'll... we'll find the memories Subaru lost."

"O-okay, got it. It's frustrating not being able to do anything, but I'll trust you guys and wait quietly here."

"Can you really wait quietly? Not go wandering off on your own?"

"You're really driving that point home! I'll be fine! I'm not a five-year-old, I can manage to wait quietly. I'll even promise, if you want."

"...Now I feel like you're actually planning not to behave..."

"How does that follow!?"

He'd brought up a promise to set her at ease, only to end up arousing even more suspicion.
Wondering what was going on, he looked around, and while Julius and Echidna seemed unaffected, both Beatrice and Ram were shrugging as if to say, "well, of course."
In any case—

"Subaru, wait quietly over there in the corner, okay?"

At Emilia's earnest plea, Subaru reluctantly took up position at the corner of the archive to wait.
Hugging his knees and idly watching the proceedings, he saw Emilia and the others split into two groups to search different parts of the archive separately. The groupings were Emilia-Beatrice-Ram, and Julius-Echidna.
He didn't quite understand it, but it seemed Emilia and Echidna each functioned as the respective heads of these two mismatched teams.

"Searching in pairs looks like some kind of insurance against ending up like you, Mister. You really do have a gift for bringing along trouble, don't you-u-u."

"I don't have my memories, but the fact that not having my memories is itself the problem means I can't really argue back... no wait, I still don't wanna hear that from an assassin."

"You worry about the silliest things, don't you-u-u."

"I don't think it's silly at all!? I mean, honestly..."

Clicking his tongue after retorting loudly, Subaru turned his gaze sideways.
By the wall, standing apart from the bookshelves where Subaru waited, leaning against the wall in the same manner beside him, was the braided girl, Meili.
She sent Subaru a coquettish sidelong glance unbefitting her age and tilted her head, saying "What?"

"I've been wondering for a while now—aren't you joining the search?"

"Nope, not joining in. 'Cause I'm not really a proper companion of you guys, Mister and the ladies, or anything like that."

"What do you mean, not a proper companion..."

"I told you, didn't I? An assassin... well, I failed, so maybe an ex-assassin. Originally, I was only here to lend a little hand. I don't know anything beyond that."

"So basically, like the terms of your parole. ...Ex or not, deciding to bring an assassin along for the adventure was a pretty bold call, huh."

"—. Yeah, really, it was. I really do think it's crazy."

Hand at her lips, Meili gave a small laugh and let the conversation trail off there.
At her attitude, Subaru let out a breath through his nose. And then—

"Got Meili's opinion, but what about you?"

"Ah, me, y'know? Ah can't read or write, so ah'm no use for anything. Just like Master, books and stuff are all Greek and Latin to me, y'know."

Shaula, leaning against the wall on the opposite side of Subaru from Meili, laughed openly. Then she reached out and pulled Subaru's left arm in against her ample body.
A soft, warm sensation. Reflexively, he shook it off.

"Whoa!"

"Aww, Master, you're so mean, y'know~."

"I-it's not mean, quit it. A girl acting so shameless... something like that, save for a guy you like, or... no, even a guy you like would recoil if you came at him like that out of nowhere, so don't do it at all. That move of yours is all downside, no upside."

"Boo, y'know. Talkin' about proper lady conduct again? Master, you seriously haven't changed a bit, y'know."

Lips pursed, Shaula voiced her displeasure at Subaru's attitude. But at her words, Subaru's face fell, and he lowered his eyes.

"Master?"

"Do you think I haven't changed either?"

At Shaula tilting her head, Subaru found himself asking that.
The phrase "hasn't changed" was one he'd been told several times in these past few hours since waking. To Subaru, it felt like both a salvation and a curse.

—Being glad that he hadn't changed, and being lamented for not having changed.

"Hmm, dunno, not really sure, y'know."

But heedless of Subaru's inner conflict, Shaula tossed that off so simply.
Even Subaru could do nothing but find himself at a loss for words at that reply. Perhaps it had been a mistake to let her in on even a sliver of such a delicate worry.

"You, seriously... no, I was an idiot to ask you."

"Mmh, Master's not an idiot, y'know. Even if it's Master sayin' it, ah'll get mad at that kinda talk, y'know. Master's an amazing person, so ah want you to hold your head up high, y'know."

"I still don't really get your stance on this."

Just when she'd been pointing and laughing at Subaru's dejection, the moment he turned self-deprecating she got angry at that instead. The one consistent thing was that it always concerned Subaru.

At Subaru's puzzlement, Shaula thrust out her ample chest, hands on her hips, and laughed.

"Whether you changed or didn't change, Master's Master, so who cares, y'know. As long as Master does what he wants, ah'll just follow along, y'know."

"—. Even if that ends up somewhere strange?"

"Well! Even if we end up somewhere weird from that, ah'll just force it open by hand, y'know. Master might've forgotten, but that's the relationship between Master and me, y'know."

"——"

Hit again and again by the piled-up bluntness of Shaula's words, words utterly without any hidden face behind them, Subaru caught his breath faintly.
Then he turned his face away so Shaula wouldn't see it.

"Mister?"

"—!"

Turning the other way, he found Meili there, so he turned back around again.

"Master, what's wrong, y'know?"

"Argh, forget it!"

In the end, with no escape to either side, Subaru crouched down right where he stood and glared at the floor. He hugged his own head with both arms, hiding his face from anyone's view.
He sensed Meili and Shaula, with him sandwiched between them, exchanging glances.
They wouldn't understand. —No, he didn't want them to understand.

That such empty, thoughtless, gap-riddled words could make him feel saved.
That, more eloquently than any words, an attitude seemed to be telling him he didn't need to strain himself so hard.

"Mister really is strange, isn't he."

"Master's been strange from the start. But ah love that about him too, y'know."

At the exchange passing right over his head, Subaru had nothing to say.
Only, ever so slightly, he felt the urgent tightness pressing on his heart ease, just a little.

—It was only a few minutes before Emilia and the others came back empty-handed that Subaru finally managed to shrug off the impulse and stand back up.
