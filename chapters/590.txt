Arc 7 Chapter 85: "Ally"

"Even here in the mansion, these past few days, a tense atmosphere has been spreading, hasn't it."

Rem murmured this softly, feeling the air draw taut and prickling around her.
The dry air and cold wind blowing in from outside seemed, faintly, to carry with them threads of human unease and irritation — the kind of thing that wells up on a battlefield.

Rem had experienced something like a battlefield herself, if only for brief stretches, a handful of times now.
Going by that feeling, the tension she sensed on her skin was mounting day by day, as if bracing for the moment it would finally burst. Even the soldiers guarding the mansion seemed to be losing their composure, little by little.
No doubt this state of affairs at large was to blame.

"The rebel army has the advantage... I hear uprisings are breaking out in all sorts of places across the Empire now. The Imperial Army is so busy dealing with them that it can't move as aggressively as it might like."

Ironically, what had triggered this simultaneous uprising of rebels was the very siege of the walled city of Guaral that had led to Rem and the others being dragged off to the capital — the Imperial Army's full-scale offensive, one that had even mobilized the Nine Divine Generals, and which the ragtag rebel army had only barely managed to weather.

The rebel army — led, in truth, by none other than Abel, the real Emperor, a fact that made Rem's own way of phrasing things rather complicated — was said to be steadily expanding, or so she'd heard.
Word had it that the merciless assault carried out by the "Flying Dragon General," so gallantly repelled by the city's iron defenses, meant there was nothing to fear from an Imperial Army that had been sent packing in such humiliating fashion.

"I suppose you could say it isn't a lie, exactly..."

From where Rem stood, one of the people actually involved, Madelyn's withdrawal with her flying dragons hadn't been for strategic reasons at all, and she found it questionable to call the city's side the victor after everything it had suffered.
Of course, from the rebel army's perspective, a win was a win, and there was no reason to pass up such a perfect chance to spread the word however they pleased. In fact, it was thanks to that very propaganda that rebels elsewhere had been emboldened to ride the wave of revolt.
There was nothing strange about the strategy involved. — No, nothing strange there at all.
It was the other factor behind the rebels' excitement that gave her pause.

"――――"

Behind the unprecedented surge of rebel activity lay another reason besides the narrow victory at the walled city: the defection of Yorna Mishigure, ruler of the demon capital Chaosflame and one of the Nine Divine Generals, who had thrown in her lot with them.
The defection of a general known as much for her fickleness as for her formidable strength had lit a fire under the rebels' momentum.
And that, in turn, had been Abel's plan from the start — as well as the policy of Natsuki Subaru, who had gone along with him.

"...Though it's entirely possible he just tagged along and wasn't actually any help."

Just how much of what had happened was owed to Subaru's own efforts, Rem couldn't say.
Even as she voiced the possibility that he'd been of no use whatsoever, Rem had to admit, even to herself, that the words didn't carry much conviction. For him to have been entirely useless, Subaru would have had to keep every word and action of his sealed away completely.
And she couldn't picture him staying silent and doing nothing, not even a little — so he must have contributed something. — Perhaps even turning the general to their side had been his doing.
Though that, she supposed, was probably giving him too much credit.

"...Really, though?"

A premonition she couldn't quite dismiss made her ask herself that very question.
The rebels had risen up all at once, and the Empire's foundations were shaking more violently than ever before. Even the capital was swept by winds carrying the scent of blood and steel, strong enough to reach even Rem and the others, held captive as they were.
Just how far had Abel foreseen all of this?

"Did his calculations even account for the hostility that was originally aimed at him?"

In all likelihood, he had calculated that as the rebellion spread and rebels raised their voices one after another, it would gather the very forces needed to contend with the false Emperor in the capital.
Subaru, too, was being used as a piece on the board to bring Abel's plan to fruition.
Though surely even Subaru hadn't been told the full scope of the plan. In the end, Subaru was nothing more than a pawn used toward Abel's own ends. — She wouldn't say she didn't understand why.
But she wasn't shameless enough to look away by deceiving herself like that.

"――――"

What had begun as a modest little uprising in the settlement of the People of Shudrak — would it, as things stood, go on to swallow the entire Empire whole, becoming a coup remembered for generations to come?
And if it did, how would Abel and Subaru, who had stood at its center, be remembered?
And in the midst of it all, what exactly was she supposed to accomplish?

"—Hey."

"Ah..."

"What are you spacing out for? You're the one who said you wanted to do this, aren't you? So do it properly — see it through to the end, responsibly."

At that, her mind, deep in thought, was called back, and Rem blinked her round eyes.
Looking down, she found her own hands full of dark brown hair, the woman it belonged to being toyed with — Katya. The woman was staring at Rem through the mirror of the vanity in front of her, her eyes sharp.
Katya's wavy hair was tangled around Rem's fingers, and no wonder — Rem had, in fact, been in the middle of styling it.

"I'm sorry. I was thinking about something."

"You don't need to tell me that — everyone's thinking about something, all the time, obviously. You don't have to report it like some kind of excuse every single time."

"――――"

"—Ah, I mean, it's not like I'm telling you not to say it, or anything."

Perhaps feeling she'd been too harsh on the apologetic Rem, Katya faltered and corrected herself.
Seeing her look down, acting like some small, skittish animal, Rem — who had genuinely meant her apology — couldn't help but find something endearing in it.

Katya Aurélie — a woman held captive in the mansion of Chancellor Berstetz Fondalfon, placed, much like Rem, under a kind of house arrest.

Unlike Rem, who had been brought here as Flop's healer after he was injured and had remained confined ever since because of Berstetz's interest in her, Katya's reasons for being here seemed to be something else entirely.
They hadn't exactly exchanged the details of their circumstances, but the fact that Katya talked to her like this at all marked a considerable thaw compared to how things had started. Though if Rem ever dared say so out loud, she'd likely be scolded with a fury fierce enough to double the very distance they'd worked so hard to close — that was the kind of person Katya was.

"...What's with that face? Your face right now, it's irritating."

"I'm sorry. Even I still haven't quite gotten used to it. It's as if it belongs to someone else."

"D-don't say something that creepy! When you talk about not having your memories and all that, I can't tell if you're being serious, and it scares me."

Scowling sullenly at the mirror, Katya bit down on the finger she'd brought to her mouth.
Biting her nails whenever something was bothering her was a habit of Katya's. They hadn't known each other long, but for better or worse, she tended to do it whenever her feelings got frayed at the edges.
Biting her nails, Katya's resentful gaze flicked between her own reflection in the mirror and Rem standing behind her. Behind Katya, seated in her wheelchair, Rem too gazed at her own reflection in the vanity mirror.

Of course, what she'd told Katya was something of an exaggeration.
Even without her memories, Rem had long since accepted that this was her own face, not some stranger's. She couldn't afford to bite at everything she saw and touched.
If she doubted even the good will shown to her by Rui, the People of Shudrak, Medium, Flop, Priscilla, Schult, and the others, what would be left of her empty self?
Knowing that much, surely it was about time she stopped being so hesitant about the person who had first reached out a hand to her, of all people—

"...You're pretty good at doing hair, you know."

"Huh?"

"I said you're good at styling hair. Did you maybe do that kind of work before, back when you still remembered everything...? No, that can't be it. There's no job that's just doing hair. Ugh, that was a stupid thing to say. Forget it. Forget I said it, forget it!"

Looking away from her own reflection in the mirror, Katya's face reddened as she looked at Rem, who had been absorbed in styling her hair without a thought in her head. It seemed that, without realizing it, Rem had finished, and Katya had been complimenting her skill.
With both hands gently pressed to the braids hanging down on either side of her head, Katya glared at Rem, her lips trembling.

"Spacing out again... I-I bet spending time with me is boring for you. Then fine! Don't bother hanging around me like this — go, go find someone else to bother instead."

"No, everyone else in the mansion is busy working right now."

"So I'm the only one with nothing to do? Is that why you keep coming to me...?"

"That's not it at all. Please don't put words in my mouth."

"W-which one of us is doing that...!"

Spinning the wheels of her chair, Katya retreated deeper into the room. Biting her nails and glaring up at Rem from there, she looked every bit as fierce as a cat whose territory had been invaded.
Rem's vague, noncommittal manner had only made Katya anxious.

"I'm sorry for giving you the wrong idea, Katya-san. The reason I come to see you isn't because you're the only one in the mansion with free time."

"Then, then what is it? Why do you keep coming to me...?"

"That's..."

Pressed for a proper reason, Rem fell into thought for a moment.
Just as she'd told Katya, Rem didn't think so little of her own position that she'd go looking for someone to kill time with in a situation like this. But from what little contact she'd had with her, she'd come to understand that Katya wasn't anyone particularly important, nor did she hold any of the Empire's secrets.
For someone like Rem, who felt driven by an urgency to do something, anything, there was no question that spending time with Katya offered little in the way of practical gain.
And yet, Rem still found herself wanting to actively reach out to Katya, and the reason was—

"W-why, then? Tell me! If you can't say it, then..."

"That would be because you're my ally, I think, Katya-san."

"――――"

"Katya-san?"

She had genuinely searched her own heart for a proper answer, but all that had come out was that rough, vague notion.
Rem didn't have any particular calculating motive where Katya was concerned. So she couldn't give Katya the one definitive reason she was looking for.
Realizing that wouldn't be enough to satisfy Katya, Rem found herself at a loss too.

"Ally... who's Ally!?"

"Huh? Ah, it's not a person's name. It means 'ally' — a friend, a companion."

"Al— wait, a... friend...?"

Katya's eyes went wide with shock, as though she'd heard something unbelievable. Seeing her reaction, Rem wondered for a moment if calling her an ally so suddenly had been too familiar.
To begin with, this relationship with Katya was one Rem had more or less forced her way into.
Both of them held against their will in Berstetz's mansion — calling the bond that had formed between two people in that position "friendship" might have been a bit thoughtless.

"I'm sorry, that was presumptuous of me. Maybe 'fellow captives' would be more fitting..."

"A-Ally!"

"Sorry?"

"You said 'ally.' You said it, didn't you. ...Whatever, fine. That's fine too."

Pressing both hands to her face, Katya said this while looking away.
Rem blinked at the words, and Katya let out an "Ah," breathing out, and said,

"But, um, if you ever decide you don't want to, you can just, you know, stop anytime you want."

"I understand. Then..."

"Y-you're stopping?"

"I'm not stopping. I only meant — you and I, Katya-san, are allies."

Unexpectedly having gained the other party's consent, Rem nodded. Katya's eyes went round at that, and then she pulled at her own braid, bit her nail, and murmured, "I see."
Rem had reasoned that nail-biting was something Katya did when her feelings got frayed, but she wondered now whether what she'd just witnessed meant she'd made Katya angry, or anxious.
Still, Katya's expression hadn't looked entirely displeased, so perhaps her theory about the nail-biting needed reconsidering.
Including all these hard-to-read parts of her, Rem felt she couldn't just leave Katya be. She wasn't well-versed in these things herself. But surely this was more than enough to qualify as friendship.

"...Still, come to think of it, you're kind of, you know."

"Kind of what?"

"Yeah, that. You know. ...You're weirdly well-informed about what's going on outside, about the rebellion."

Katya, who had been biting her nails with her eyes closed, suddenly changed the subject as though something had just occurred to her.
For a moment Rem blinked in confusion, not sure what she meant, before realizing Katya was referring to how she'd been lost in thought earlier while doing her hair.

"I don't know if I'd call it 'well-informed,' exactly, but I was originally brought here from the middle of a battle, so I do take an interest in it. Is it different for you, Katya-san?"

"...It's not that I don't care. But I don't really want to think about it much. My brother... my idiot brother died, and I hate anything to do with war."

"—Ah."

Lowering her eyes, Katya murmured this while lacing her fingers together in her lap.
This wasn't the first Rem had heard of Katya's brother's death. He must have meant a great deal to her — she brought him up often, in conversations like this.
The recent uprising set off by Abel had, in part, led to Katya's brother's death. It was possible that death wasn't unrelated to the very fighting Rem herself had been involved in.
If someone close to her had lost their life, Rem too would probably come to hate fighting. Even now, she wished there were no fighting at all.

"Even so, it isn't the sort of thing that goes away just because you cover your ears. Katya-san, didn't you say your fiancé has also gone off to the battlefield? You must be worried."

"That guy...! H-he doesn't seem like the type to die no matter what happens. But..."

"Wasn't that true of your brother too, though?"

"――――"

Was it simply unavoidable, being born into a noble family in the Empire?
Her brother had died in battle, and now her fiancé had gone off to war as well. Taking up arms as an Imperial soldier — that was a complicated thing for Rem to think about. — Abel wouldn't show any mercy to his enemies.
Even if those enemies were soldiers who had, until recently, technically been his own subjects, it would make no difference.

"How strange..."

To begin with, Rem hadn't been moved by Abel's ideals, nor did she agree with them.
Originally, she had been held captive at an Imperial Army encampment, and Subaru had needed the help of Abel and the People of Shudrak to get her out. To repay that debt, Subaru had gone on to cooperate with them — and Rem, more or less swept along, had ended up acting alongside them too, though she'd never owed them anything herself.
Yes — she'd never owed them anything. That, she realized, was already a thing of the past.

"Rui-chan, Priscilla-san, Mizelda-san and the others, Flop-san..."

People Rem had interacted with, people who'd looked after her and been looked after by her in turn — people she could call, in a sense, companions.
And those very people now walked the same path as Abel. Before she knew it, Rem's own heart had become unable to easily walk away from that path either. But none of that mattered to the enemy Imperial soldiers, or to Katya.
If Katya knew the true nature of Rem's position, would she still forgive her?

"――――"

She didn't have the courage to confess it to someone grieving the loss of a beloved brother.

"...You?"

"Ah, no, it's nothing. If it seems like I know a lot about what's going on outside, I think it's because of the people who've recently been gathered in the annex."

"The annex... ah, those people."

Hearing this, Katya's voice dropped a register, and her eyes sharpened.
It was no wonder Katya reacted with such displeasure. To begin with, Katya had a shy, distrustful streak when it came to people, and it had taken a fair amount of effort for Rem to close the distance between them at all.
From her perspective, having more and more people brought into the mansion one after another wasn't something to welcome — especially when those people were rallying banners for rebellion, gathered from all across the land.

"There are so many of them, but do you really think there's someone in there? Among them, one of His Imperial Majesty's..."

"—Hidden children."

"...Well, they're not exactly hidden anymore, since it's already out in the open."

Cutting in on Rem's reply, Katya made an awkward face.
She seemed to think she'd said something unnecessary again, but Rem paid it no mind. More than that, the meaning behind those words was what held her attention and wouldn't let go.

In the annex of Berstetz Fondalfon's mansion — the same mansion where Rem and the others were held captive — a great many boys had been gathered from battlefields across the Empire.
— All of them teenage boys sharing a certain set of features: the "Black-Haired Crown Princes."

"It's weird that His Majesty doesn't have any children — well, that kind of talk was already going around, you know..."

At Katya's hoarse murmur, Rem was reminded of the moment, right after she'd been brought to the mansion, when she'd met with Berstetz one-on-one.
Berstetz, who sided with the false Emperor who had driven Abel from the throne and seized his position, had said that the reason he'd raised his rebellion was Abel's abandonment of his duties as Emperor.
And the duty he'd abandoned, specifically, was the matter of the imperial succession.

"His Majesty never took an Empress... and, well, every Emperor before him had lots of consorts, and children too... that's how you're supposed to pick the next Emperor."

"That's how it's meant to be done. And yet Abel-san... no, Emperor Vincent, never kept to that. And that's where the rumors of the 'Black-Haired Crown Prince' came in."

"They're apparently saying you can't leave the Empire in His Majesty's hands or something like that. ...It's ridiculous."

"Ridiculous, you think?"

Rem raised an eyebrow at Katya, who murmured this with her head bowed, sounding truly resentful.
Katya's rebellious remark seemed to carry more anger directed at the person who'd started the war than irritation at the cause of the war itself.

"Do you think highly of Emperor Vincent, Katya-san?"

"I-I wouldn't be so full of myself as to go around 'evaluating' His Imperial Majesty! ...But, in an Empire where the strong throw their weight around, someone like me doesn't really have a place. ...When there's no fighting, that gap doesn't really show, does it. So it was easier."

"Easier, hm..."

Rem lowered her eyes as Katya haltingly, stumblingly bared what was in her heart.
Apparently Abel's rule hadn't actually been bad, all things considered — even Berstetz had said that if not for the matter of succession, he never would have considered rebellion. In fact, the Empire had enjoyed a long stretch of peace, and there were probably no small number of people who shared feelings like Katya's.

While there was no fighting, fewer people had to put their lives at risk.
The moment the fighting started, Katya's brother had died, and now her fiancé had been dragged off to the battlefield too. It would be hard to expect Katya to hold anything but a poor opinion of war.
That said—

"I don't think there's a real 'Crown Prince' in that annex."

Whether or not Abel truly had a child somewhere, Rem had reached that conclusion regardless.
Hearing Rem's answer, Katya asked "why" in a low voice. Ducking her chin at that upward glance, Rem looked off toward the annex and said,

"The people held in the annex are 'Crown Princes' who joined the uprisings in various parts of the Empire — or at least, people who called themselves that."

"T-that much I've heard too... s-so what?"

"Claiming to be His Imperial Majesty's child, stirring up rebellion, and then getting captured so quickly — that seems like an awfully thoughtless way to go about it, don't you think?"

It was hard for Rem to state things to Katya with real conviction — there was too much she couldn't say, too much information involved.
The "Crown Princes" — taken prisoner on battlefields across the Empire, captured alive so their claims could be verified — had been gathered in the annex, waiting for their sentence to be decided.

Because Rem knew Abel, the real Emperor, as a person, she found herself holding any child of his to a higher standard. At the very least, she didn't think such a child would be a fool.
To begin with, if such a child had joined the rebel side, they would surely be coordinating with Abel, who led it.
If parent and child were working together to bring down the false Emperor, it seemed unlikely that any of the "Crown Princes" who'd been captured this easily were the genuine article.

"Of course, I don't have proof..."

Rem wouldn't claim her own thoughts and impressions were absolute, either.
Abel wasn't omnipotent. It was possible he too had, through some mistake, been captured by an enemy and had his movements sealed at some point. But losing a fight and being taken prisoner was a different matter entirely.
If being captured had somehow been factored into his calculations from the start, she'd have to reconsider — but was it really reasonable to hope that one of the "Crown Princes" in the annex was pulling off something like that on purpose?

"Y-you sound awfully confident. ...What are you to His Imperial Majesty, anyway?"

"—. I've never even met him. And I imagine he himself would insist the same. It's just that, when it comes to the 'Crown Princes' in the annex, I don't think I'm the only one who thinks this way."

"They're just being used, claiming to be someone they're not? Why would anyone do something so presumptuous...?"

"...It's a convenient excuse for gathering manpower, I'd imagine."

Being a genuine child of the Emperor would make for an unbeatable selling point when it came to justifying a claim on the throne.
From what she'd heard, there had never been a case in the Vollachia Empire of the throne changing hands through usurpation. And yet the Empire's own logic didn't forbid seizing the throne by force.
Take the capital, seize the throne, cut off the Emperor's head, and whoever did it became the next Emperor.

As an excuse for gathering the manpower needed to carry that out, raising up a candidate for the next Emperor was an extremely convenient tool.

"Especially since the 'Crown Prince' has never been publicly presented. All anyone knows is black hair and black eyes — nothing more. Whoever claims it first, wins."

"...And the people who started that claim?"

"They're probably fellow rebels, I'd imagine..."

At Katya's question, Rem thought not of the captured "Crown Princes" themselves, but of the people who must have surrounded them. If "fellow" was the right word, then those very companions must have had the "Crown Prince" torn from among them — which surely hadn't gone smoothly.
There was only one path awaiting those who defied the Emperor.

"Used, and used up, and then some of them died or got captured... idiots, all of them."

"Katya-san..."

"W-what? Did I say something wrong? Or is it that you, a hostage yourself, think you're too good to hear it? That they're somehow better than me!?"

Her voice shaking as though she were on the verge of a tantrum, Katya's eyes welled with tears.
Insisting, almost too forcefully, that she was different from those people in the annex, might have been a sign that she'd found some point of connection between herself and them after all.
The reason Katya so often cursed herself as worthless was her awareness of her own status as a captive, and the guilt she felt over being a burden to those close to her — her fiancé, most of all.

"――――"

Understanding that feeling was exactly why Rem found herself at a loss for words.
If she said "that's not true," she'd be accused of lying. If she said "I understand," she'd be scolded for arrogance. She didn't feel the distance between them had closed enough to simply talk Katya's heart open by force.
Frustrated, not knowing what to say, Rem gripped her cane tightly.
And then—

"—Squawking like chicks, cha. Girl."

"――――"

A voice, bitterly cold, rang through the room, and both Rem and Katya froze. — No, for Rem, that alone was enough, but for Katya, it wasn't.
Katya's eyes went wide with shock, her gaze fixed past Rem, toward the window facing the courtyard. The voice had come from that direction, which meant its owner was there.
Katya locked eyes with the speaker head-on and simply froze solid.

"Ah, uh..."

"Don't make such an irritating noise. Don't make such an eyesore of yourself. It's disrespectful, in front of a dragon."

A cold voice struck Katya, whose breath came out ragged, her eyes wide. As if her whole body had been seized in a fist, Katya's throat couldn't manage a proper reply.
Seeing Katya trembling like that, Rem bit her lip and turned around.
There stood—

"—Madelyn-san."

"Healer girl, what are you doing here? You have your duties, don't you. Getting careless while the dragon's away?"

"That wasn't my intention."

With the sharp edge of that voice now turned toward her, it was Rem's turn to feel the pressure of it. But shielding Katya behind her from that gaze, Rem faced the other head-on.
Outside the window, standing in the courtyard, was a girl of small stature in an adorable outfit, distinguished by two black horns growing from her head — Madelyn Eschart.

One of the Nine Divine Generals, and the very person who had brought Rem to this mansion. She often left the estate at Berstetz's or Vincent's request, and Rem was startled by her return now — not by her appearance itself, but by its sheer, harrowing state.
Standing proudly in the courtyard, Madelyn was drenched, head to toe, in black blood.

"That blood — are you injured?"

"Don't change the subject. The dragon told you to heal that man's wounds, didn't she."

"I'm not changing the subject. Flop-san's wounds are being treated properly, step by step. But please answer me first. That blood..."

"—. It isn't the dragon's blood. It's someone else's."

Frowning, seemingly annoyed, Madelyn answered Rem's question by tugging at her clothes. The crackling sound came from the blood, already dried, sticking her clothes to her skin.
Seeing the sheer volume of blood, called nothing more than "someone else's," Rem held her breath. Just what had to be done to another person — or to how many people — to be drenched in that much blood?

"Have you been fighting?"

"Fighting is something done against an opponent recognized as an equal. Do you think there's anything out there that stands beside a dragon? What the dragon has been doing is hunting. A tiresome hunt, with tiresome restrictions."

"Tiresome..."

"Black hair, they live. Everything else, dies."

Faced with Madelyn's blunt way of putting it, Rem couldn't bring herself to offer any careless reply.
Still, she understood now — it was Madelyn's duty to bring back the "Crown Princes" held in the annex from battlefields across the land.

The one ordering the "Crown Princes" to be captured was, no doubt, Berstetz himself.
Given the very purpose behind his rebellion, if Abel really did have a hidden child somewhere, the root cause behind that rebellion would simply cease to exist.
Whether Berstetz feared that outcome, or welcomed it, Rem couldn't say.
She couldn't say, but—

"If the order is to capture rather than kill..."

If a genuine "Crown Prince" were ever found, Rem had a feeling the old man might die satisfied.
That was the thought that chilled her to the core.
In any case—

"So, have you come back to bring yet another 'Crown Prince' to the annex? Or to check whether I've been neglecting my duty as Flop-san's healer?"

"Does the dragon have any reason to chatter and gossip with you? Don't get carried away. Even without you, there's no shortage of healers in the capital..."

"Is that other healer discreet? If the Chancellor hasn't kept one on hand, that probably means finding someone like that is very difficult."

"—Don't get carried away, cha."

Rem hadn't meant to come on so strong, but her reply came out with unintended force. Whether it was that answer that irritated her, Madelyn stepped up to the window, the pupils of her golden eyes narrowing.
Feeling a bone-chilling, ferocious presence bear down on her, Rem felt herself shrink back just slightly.

"Y-you idiot! Don't say anything unnecessary! I-it's not like that at all!"

At that, wheels creaking, Katya rushed forward in a panic.
Her already pale face turning even paler, Katya faced Madelyn through the window, her throat trembling under that gaze, and still she pushed on,

"D-don't take anything this girl says seriously... just don't! Sh-she doesn't understand anything, she's forgotten everything, she's an idiot!"

"K-Katya-san..."

"She's an idiot, but she's better to have around than not, so stop — just stop... I'll, I'll make sure! Make sure she does her job properly. I'll make her heal that blond one too...!"

Rem fell silent, holding her breath at Katya's desperate choice of words. At Katya's sudden vigor, Madelyn narrowed her eyes as well, staring intently at her.
Bracing herself to shield Katya bodily should any terribly dangerous glint pass across that gaze, Rem waited for Madelyn's next move.
And then—

"—A weak thing like you doesn't get to defy a dragon. There won't be a next time."

"Eep."

Resting a hand on the window frame, Madelyn spoke flatly as she crushed it in her grip. With a violent crack, the stonework was effortlessly pulverized, and Katya's throat let out a strangled sound.
Still, though she seemed to find Katya's attitude unforgivable, Madelyn appeared to have decided to let it go this time. She'd decided that much, but—

"Please retract calling Katya-san a 'weak thing.' For someone weak to speak up to you like this..."

"S-stop it! Stop, you idiot! Die! Idiot! Stop it!"

"Katya-san! But..."

"No buts, stop it! Die! Stop it!"

Leaning on her cane, about to turn back to face Madelyn, Rem was struck by Katya barreling into her. It was a weak, feeble collision, one Rem could easily have held off — but when Katya, with a look of sheer desperation on her face, then reached out and clung to her arm, that was another matter, and Rem couldn't shake her off.

Rem had wanted to make Madelyn take back her far too condescending remark about Katya, but with Katya herself so frantically trying to stop her, there was nothing to be done.
Giving up her direct appeal, Rem watched as Madelyn snorted through her nose and turned her back.

"Watch how you speak, girl. The dragon really will arrange a replacement healer."

"Wait, please. Where are you going?"

"To that man. The dragon has business to discuss with him."

"If you're going to see Flop-san, please wash off the blood and change your clothes first. He's an injured man — you need to be considerate of his feelings too. Please think about it."

"You..."

Rem spoke up without hesitation to that retreating back. At that, Madelyn's face twisted with displeasure once again, and Katya, tugging at Rem's sleeve, cried "Die!"
But Rem couldn't die, and she wasn't about to let Flop die either.
Showing up covered head to toe in blood was the height of uncleanliness and poor hygiene. Whatever the customs of the Dragonkin or the Empire dictated, that was one point she wasn't willing to concede.

"Please bathe first."

"...Fine."

"Please change your clothes too. There are plenty of cute outfits prepared for you, after all..."

"I said fine! You're persistent, cha!"

Baring her fangs and raising her voice, Madelyn's fierce, blistering pressure struck Rem — and Katya besides — like a gust of wind, stealing the breath from both of them.
Even so, Madelyn apparently understood well enough that Rem's instructions weren't something to be brushed aside. That, in no small part, was thanks to Flop's considerable feat of having tamed her so well.
Someday, would that same touch of his manage to win Madelyn fully over to their side?

"Don't look at the dragon with such impudent eyes, girl. — Whatever you're scheming, it's pointless."

"I don't think I can say it's pointless. Whatever it may be."

"That's not what the dragon means, cha. There's no time to do anything, is what the dragon's saying, cha."

"No time?"

Tilting her head, Rem narrowed her eyes, trying to divine the true meaning behind Madelyn's words.
But there was no need to search for it. Madelyn surely disliked Rem — and yet, whether it was Dragonkin pride or simply how she lived her life, she also disliked lying or being evasive.
So she made sure to properly break down and explain the meaning behind whatever she'd started to say.
She said:

"The chance to settle things with those defying the Emperor is drawing near, cha. That's why the dragon was called back. — And that's as far as your role goes too, cha."


　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　△▼△▼△▼△


And with those words, ones that felt almost like a pronouncement of despair, Madelyn left the courtyard.
She hadn't headed straight for Flop's room, so presumably she'd bathe and change as instructed before paying him a visit.
That much, at least, Flop would welcome, and so long as she meant him no harm, Rem had no reason to stop her.
And yet—

"—A decisive battle, with the rebel army."

"I-it didn't say where or anything, but..."

"――――"

Biting her nails, still watching the courtyard outside the window where Madelyn had gone, Katya was harboring the very same fear that gripped Rem.
The anxiety Katya carried was the same — the timing and location of the coming showdown between the Imperial Army and the rebel army.
Madelyn's words had hinted that it wasn't far off. But where would it take place? Was there some location out there suited to hosting a full, decisive confrontation?
With rebels rising up across the Empire and battlefields scattered in all directions, if there was a place where preparations could be made to wipe out such scattered opposition in one stroke, then that would be—

"...Honestly, you're all such idiots. You too, you're a huge idiot too."

"Katya-san..."

"Y-your opponent is one of the Nine Divine Generals, a Dragonkin who doesn't listen to reason! And yet you go and pull something like that — die! If you want to do something that stupid, go ahead and die on your own time! Die, you idiot!"

Trading the anxiety of what was to come for irritation at what stood right in front of her, Katya lashed out at Rem, her voice thick with tears.
Unlike Rem, who had, in some sense, already resigned herself to what might come, she'd forced Katya into an awful position just now. In fact, if Katya hadn't intervened when she had, it was entirely possible that even if Rem's life hadn't been taken, Madelyn's anger might well have left her hurt.

"I'm sorry, and thank you. But I couldn't just stay quiet when she spoke so badly of you, Katya-san..."

"H-how should I know! I'm used to people saying things like that about me! And yet you go and pull something so stupid..."

"Getting used to it is ridiculous. That's why, no matter how many times it's said, I'll speak up the same way, in the same situation, every time."

Hearing Katya put herself down wasn't something Rem could feel good about. Still, that self-deprecation came with its own reasons, its own weight of consideration behind it. But watching someone else turn a blade on another person was something she simply couldn't bear to witness.
— Which, considering her own circumstances, was a rather self-serving thing to think, she knew.

"That's exactly why I..."

Knowing full well how self-serving it was, she still didn't want to let that voice go silent.
At Rem's answer, Katya blinked over and over, her mouth opening and closing.
Then, glaring at Rem through teary eyes, she bit her nail.

"F-fine, whatever... I don't care about you anymore, I don't! I-I'm quitting. I'm ending this. We're not allies. I'm the one ending it, not you...!"

"No, whether we stop or not should be my choice too. You can't do that."

"How is that fair, one-sided like that!"

Her throat catching, Katya shot back at her, and Rem gave a faint, wry smile.
And as she did, feeling the tight knot in her chest ease, if only slightly, Rem found herself thinking—

—If the decisive battle Madelyn had spoken of really was close at hand.

"...What can I even do?"

What could she, empty as she was, with nothing inside her, possibly make resound in the world?
That question alone kept stirring, again and again, deep within Rem's heart, without end.
