Arc 3 Chapter 37: "Sloth"

Raucous laughter echoed off the dim, cold walls of the cave.

Petelgeuse cackled — kehkehkeh — whatever he found so amusing, he kept on laughing, baring teeth mottled red with blood.

Left unchecked, he looked ready to keep at it forever. Before this bizarre display, Subaru — the target of that laughter — kept his head down, glaring at the ground.

Subaru's body had been hauled deep into the chamber by the black-robed figure, dumped unceremoniously, and shackled to the wall. The iron manacles bit into his hands and feet hard enough to change their color, and a slow numbness was beginning to spread as his blood vessels were choked off.

"Fuheh... hihiheh..."

At the sensation of his limbs going numb, Subaru wore a twisted grin, as if he knew no other way to express himself. Seeing this, Petelgeuse clapped his hands together, all the more delighted.

"Ahh, how comical, Dess! Quite, quite, quite, quite, quite the sight to lift one's spirits, I'd say. Truly, truly truly truly truuuly, my brain trembles..."

Blood dripped from the wound on his left hand; he wiped it with the fingers of his right and smeared it across his own forehead. Drawing mottled patterns on his brow while wearing a malevolent grin, Petelgeuse was utterly beyond the pale.

Subaru, grinning vacantly at some place other than reality, and Petelgeuse, purely steeped in a world of madness. Into the space where their mad laughter overlapped, faintly eroding one's grip on reality, a shadow suddenly welled up. It was a different figure from the one that had carried Subaru into the cave. The tall shadow moved with a gliding motion, making not even a sound, and drew close beside the laughing Petelgeuse,

"—"

It murmured something in a voice pitched to reach him alone. Hearing it, Petelgeuse suddenly wiped the malicious grin from his twisted cheeks, and the comical antics he'd been putting on stopped dead.

"I see, Dess, I see! Ahh, that is... ahh, my brain trembles, doesn't it!"

With the same nuance that had opened his earlier malicious laughter, but now wearing an ominous, malevolent look that could send chills down one's spine, Petelgeuse bit at the nail of his left finger. He bit, gnawed it off, the nail peeling back and blood flowing, and heedless of that, gnawed into the flesh beneath,

"...Ahh, it hurts. Hurts hurts hurts hurts hurts. My brain trembles. Do it, it says. Advance, it says! There's no time to stand still, it says! It shouts! It calls! My brain trembles, Dess!!"

He swung his arm, and blood dripped from his nail-shorn left hand onto the cold floor of the cave. The black shadow watched this without a flicker of emotion, and, bending slightly at the waist as if in a gesture of respect, whispered again to Petelgeuse, who continued to display his madness.

"The left ring finger, annihilated!? Ahh, what a sweet trial that is, Dess! And here I've been challenging things so diligently... the world isn't kind to me today either, Dess!"

"—"

"Ahh, that's fine, Dess. Whatever remains of the left ring finger, have it merge with the neighboring finger. Ah well, still, still, still, still, still, still, still — there are nine fingers left. No need to worry, Dess!"

"—"

"That's... right, Dess! A trial! A trial! This is a trial! Everything is a trial for us to align with His will, Dess! Shine, blaze brighter... ahh, my brain trembles!"

Petelgeuse cackled, spittle flying in his elation, clutching his head and spinning round and round on the spot. The black-robed figure's voice was muffled, impossible to make out even in the enclosed space of the cave. Because of this, the exchange between Petelgeuse and the black shadow carried an almost comic quality, as though Petelgeuse were performing a one-man play, and combined with his bizarre behavior, it only heightened the sense of eeriness.

"Ahh, Dess, Dess, Dess, Dess, DessDessDessDessDessDessDessDess!!"

"—"

"Dess... ahh, him, Dess? Does he concern you, Dess? No, no, no-no-no-no-no-no-no, o-o-of cou-u-urse I understaaand... Dess!"

Bending at the waist, lowering himself, twisting his body further, Petelgeuse drew close to Subaru. His face thrust near, breathing out something rank and fishy, Subaru, grinning vacantly, looked up at his madness with unfeeling eyes. Facing those black eyes, Petelgeuse strained his own grayish eyes until they bulged, rolling them about,

"Certainly, certainly certainly certainly-tainly-tainly, strange, ominous, inexplicable... at this juncture, with the trial right before us, why would a being like you exist here?"

"—"

"The Dragon Carriage! Ahh, Dragon Carriages are wonderful, Dess! Earth Dragons are endearing and very fine indeed, Dess! Unlike humans, they carry no excess emotion. Above all, the way they diligently obey commands, diligently work, and strive diligently just to be — it's splendid, Dess!"

"—"

"You killed it! Ahh, that too is fine, Dess! Unavoidable, to bring him down! Ahh, you too are diligent, Dess! Good, that's a good thing, Dess! At my side, as the fingers of my two hands, diligence is what matters most, Dess! Goo-ood! Goo-ood! So so so so-o-o-o-o good, goo-oo-oo-oo-oo-ood, Dess!!"

Petelgeuse arched his body back with such flexibility that his head nearly touched the ground. Wearing an expression of ecstasy the whole while, he then sprang upright with the recoil of a drawn bow,

"The diligence of my fingers has brought down a creature that is itself a lump of diligence — the Earth Dragon! Ahh, my brain trembles. Trembles trembles trembles-trembles-trembles-trembles-trembles-emmmbles!!"

His face flushed red with an excitement no ordinary person could comprehend, and suddenly a thin trail of blood ran from Petelgeuse's nostril. He licked it up with his tongue just before it reached his mouth, and Petelgeuse, his face dyed crimson, relaxed his cheeks into a rapturous, dazed expression,

"Ahh... that dead Earth Dragon truly was 'Sloth,' wasn't it, Dess."

He murmured this with heat in his eyes, and Petelgeuse shuddered in the throes of climax. Then he wiped the blood from his nose roughly with the sleeve of his vestments, and, with a coldness that made one wonder where all his earlier excitement had gone,

"See to the immediate cleanup of the site where the Dragon Carriage was destroyed. Before the coming day of the trial, we must avoid our presence being discovered. The area should already have been cleared of people, so there should be no witnesses to worry about... but the passenger? Did you kill them properly, Dess?"

"—"

"A report on that matter? Ahh, let's hear it. Generously, grandly, with a vast, boundless love. I treat my fingers with mercy, after all, Dess."

Spreading both arms wide, the hem of his vestments swaying, Petelgeuse nodded solemnly. Seeing this behavior befitting the position he claimed — that of Bishop — the black shadow drew in its chin and, still stubbornly keeping its voice low,

"—"

"One passenger... a girl with blue hair. The tip of the left ring finger was involved, destroyed the Dragon Carriage. A battle broke out while securing him... the ring finger's rout was caused by that girl... status unknown, alive or dead."

Taking in the shadow's report, Petelgeuse rolled his head side to side, cracking the bones in his neck. He kept at it as if lost in thought, swinging his head like a pendulum, twisting it, wrenching it, rotating it, rocking it, and finally letting it drop forward with a jerk,

"A-live-or-dead... unknown, Dess, is it."

Having said it, he then slowly, slowly lifted his trembling head, and turned a hollow, gray gaze toward the black-robed figure.

"You... are 'Sloth,' aren't you, Dess?"

As he said this, Petelgeuse raised his left hand and took its ring finger into his mouth. And immediately after, without a shred of hesitation, he bit down on the tip of that ring finger and crushed it.

Flesh was ground between his molars, and a sound rose that provoked a violent, wrenching revulsion. Petelgeuse gnawed the nail, chewed the flesh, pooled the welling blood in his mouth, spat it all out in a single lump, and grabbed the black shadow's face with his blood-soaked left hand.

"With the trial before us, a situation on the verge of exposure! That! That! That-that-that-that-hat-hat-hat-hat! Is that your sincere way of repaying the Gospel!? Ahh, this is sloth! Sloth! Sloth-sloth-sloth-slo-o-oth!"

Where such strength could come from a body of nothing but skin and bone was a mystery, but Petelgeuse, gripping the face of one strong enough to carry Subaru at a run, swung him left and right. That the black-robed figure offered no resistance surely played a part, but as Petelgeuse violently shook him about, he turned his gaze up to the ceiling,

"And! The sloth of my finger is my own sloth! Ahh, please forgive my body's sloth, unable to repay the grace shown me! With all of this body, with all the diligence of my whole soul, to live in accordance with the Gospel, to exist as it demands! I beg forgiveness for it, Dess!"

Releasing the shadow, Petelgeuse knelt, tears streaming down in floods, and clasped his blood-stained hands as if in prayer, as if clinging to something, as if he could see something there, and pleaded. Even the black-robed figure, roughly handled as he'd been, followed Petelgeuse's prayer, likewise turning his gaze to the ceiling behind him, wordlessly confessing his own lack of virtue, his sin.

— It was an abnormal sight.

Petelgeuse switched between joy, anger, sorrow, and delight indiscriminately, without warning, round and round and round and round. This wasn't on the level of mere emotional instability. It was emotion gone missing entirely.

That the black-robed figure took this as a matter of course, showing not the slightest reaction even to the violence done to himself, was equally bizarre. Seeing him obey Petelgeuse, who called himself Bishop, perhaps he should be called a believer. Or more precisely, a fanatic.

"It's love! I must repay that love, Dess! Sloth cannot be forgiven! I must obey the Gospel! I must return the love I've been given, with love!"

"—"

"Find the girl whose life or death is unknown, Dess! If she's alive, end her breath! If she's dead, sever the corpse's head! Bring it here, Dess! I must see to it with my own hands, and apologize for having been slothful, Dess!"

The black shadow answered the shrieked command, and melted away, vanishing into the darkness of the cave. As its presence receded, Petelgeuse remained kneeling there for a while, dazed, breathing hard,

"Now then, now then, now-now-now-now-now-nowwww..."

Still kneeling, Petelgeuse shuffled closer to Subaru on his knees. The motion of dragging his knees across ground where hard, sharp rock jutted through was nothing but self-harm, needlessly wounding the skin beneath his vestments. Disregarding that pain, those wounds, he peered into Subaru's face,

"Just what, in the end, are you, Dess?"

"Uuu... auu..."

"You don't seem to have been led here by the Gospel, Dess, and yet such a dense, concentrated favor drifts from your body. Truly, truly truly truly tru-u-u-u-ly, most interesting... Dess!"

Bringing his face close, tongue out at a distance where he might well lick him, Petelgeuse studied Subaru. But Subaru was in no state to answer his question. Still gazing at somewhere that wasn't here, Subaru drew from Petelgeuse a rapturous murmur of "Ahh, my brain trembles," and,

"I should know every face save that of 'Pride,' Dess, and yet I can't believe one so favored has nothing to do with the Gospel, Dess."

He murmured this, and reached inside his own vestments — pulling out a single book. It was bound in black, about the size of a dictionary, with a thickness to match. At a glance, it looked like nothing more than some beloved book he'd brought out, but—

"Ahh... I feel the Gospel. My brain... trembles..."

The way he stroked the black spine lovingly with his finger, breathing out hot breaths, made it more than clear that it was no such ordinary thing. Holding the book, which bore no title or any other marking, Petelgeuse savored its texture for a while before slowly, solemnly beginning to turn its pages.

"You are not recorded in the Gospel. Nor, of course, is the matter that arose before this great trial, nor anything of the present situation — not a single thing! That! Means, in other words!"

He shut the book with a snap, and, holding the closed book aloft, spraying spittle,

"It means that you are of no consequence, Dess! It means your existence, unrecorded in the Gospel, has been entrusted to me, Dess! And yet, receiving such deep, deep, deep, deep, dee-ee-ee-ee-ee-eep favor... what a contradiction you are! Ahh, my brain, my brain, my braaaain—"

He jabbed a finger to his temple, working his nail with enough force to gouge it out. The skin split, and blood began to seep. Even seeing this, Subaru still showed no reaction, only grinning vacantly, literally overlooking Petelgeuse's self-harm.

"A-a-a-a-a-ah... ahh, being ignored is lonely, Dess! Even though, even though! I've been treating you so kindly, so kind-kind-kind-kind-kindly, Dess!"

Petelgeuse's outstretched hand seized Subaru's face in a claw-like grip, fixing his unfocused gaze in place, forcing it to meet his own eyes. Even Subaru, lost in his stupor, grimaced and tried to resist such rough treatment, but—

"— Look into my eyes, Dess."

Quietly — of all his utterances so far, this carried the least emotional fluctuation, yet a forceful power that permitted no refusal was packed into that voice, those eyes. Subaru's body flinched, and though dazed, he looked, as told, into Petelgeuse's gray eyes, shining with a mad brilliance. And then,

"Answer me, Dess. Respond, Dess. To my question, my demand. Why are you in a place like this, and why are you so favored? Do you not possess a Gospel? Then have you heard the Gospel directly, with your own ears?"

"Ahh, euh... hihaheh."

"This isn't getting anywhere, Dess... then, shall we change the order of things?"

His string of questions deflected, Petelgeuse tilted his head ninety degrees to the right. With his face tilted sideways like that, he brought it even closer. And then,

"If I may ask, Dess—"

"— Auuu!"

Having drawn his face close, Petelgeuse extended his tongue and licked Subaru's left eye, right over the pupil. At this touch so far removed from anything ordinary — having one's eye licked by another — Subaru showed a reflexive revulsion, his body shaking, the chains of his manacles rattling. But that lasted only until he heard the next words.

"— Why, I wonder, are you pretending to be mad, Dess?"


* * *


The gray was frightening.

The eyes staring at him, the eyes he could not look away from — he was unbearably afraid of them. He shook, turned his face away, tried to flee those eyes, but the hands gripping his face were so hard, the chains binding his body so cold, that there was no escape.

His body ached everywhere, unbearably. Thrown from the overturned Dragon Carriage, the scrapes covering his whole body had received no treatment at all. Beneath his torn clothes, the raw, red-scraped wounds seemed to intensify their pain just from being looked at, and the seeping blood felt somehow directly bound up with some tremendously unpleasant memory.

Where had blue gone?

Blue, who was always by his side. Blue, who touched him gently. Blue, who fed him, who held his hand when he was anxious — blue, who did all that for him. Without blue... if only blue were here, none of this would be so frightening.

Green wasn't here either. Nor was brown, nor white. All that was here was a black so deep nothing could be seen, and the gray right in front of him.

The black, which said nothing, answered nothing, and simply existed, was frightening too. But the gray was far, far, far more terrifying than that.

"Have you heard the Gospel directly, with your own ears, Dess?"

He didn't understand what was being said. His mind wouldn't work to comprehend it. All he had was an overwhelming desire to reject this awful thing, and so he turned his body, his face, his heart away in refusal. But the other wouldn't allow it, and the gray brought its face even closer,

"This isn't getting anywhere, Dess... then, shall we change the order of things?"

So it said, the gray drawing its face nearer. The tongue extending from its mouth, an unnaturally long tongue, a red tongue that had just licked up dripping blood, closed in mercilessly,

"If I may ask, Dess—"

— across the surface of his left eye, directly over the pupil, the tongue came licking, as if to torment him.

A welling revulsion, an overwhelming horror, rattled the chains as it laid his rejection bare. But the gray showed no sign of stopping. If anything, all the more delighted, it curled its extended tongue around behind his eye, toying with the eyeball as though rolling it atop its tongue.

Disgusting, no, please stop, help me, scary scary scary scary.

"— Why, I wonder, are you pretending to be mad, Dess?"
Scary scary scary scary scary scary scary scary scary scary scary scary scary scary scary scary scary scary scary scary scary scary scary scary scary scary scary scary scary scary sca—?

He didn't understand what had just been said to him.

At the discomfort of having his eye licked, the revulsion of being stared at so intently, the rejection born of fearing the visible madness before him — his body, which should have been trembling, went suddenly, perfectly still. Dazed, stunned, mouth hanging open in blank astonishment, his wide-open eye still being licked,

"Why, I wonder, are you pretending to be mad, Dess?"

He heard the gray's question, repeated.

"Ahh-uuh-aah-uuh-aah!!"

"No, no, no-no-no-no-no, it's a genuine question, Dess. Why, for what reason, for what purpose, are you pretending to be steeped in madness, Dess?"

He mustn't listen. He mustn't let it into his ears. He wasn't allowed to know. He shook his head, rattled his handcuffs and shackles again and again, tried to sever his own awareness. He tried to push the man's words away from his ears. He must not listen, must not notice, must not know.

"I won't offer you the convenient escape route of calling it 'unconscious,' Dess. You are aware, you understand yourself, and it is on top of that understanding that you're feigning madness, Dess."

"Ahh! Nnngh! Uuh, auu-ah!"

"Your display of madness is far too rational. No one goes mad in a way so clever, so docile, so calculated to earn sympathy, to earn love, Dess."

He raised his voice, screaming until his throat felt fit to tear, trying to drown out the man's voice. But the man, as if mocking that very effort, slipped his words smoothly through the gaps in his awareness.

"It's a failed performance of a madman, Dess. If you were to truly go mad, if you were to steep yourself in a world of madness in the real sense, you mustn't be conscious of others' eyes at all. The world must be complete unto yourself alone — because to be mad is to have come unmoored, Dess!"

"— Baaah! Baaaah! Bababa-baaah!"

"Ahh, how comical, how comical, Dess! Why are you pretending to be a madman!? To one who has truly come unmoored, such a thin veneer peels away in an instant, plain to see — and it's so funny I can hardly stand it!"

Words he didn't want to hear, things he didn't even want to know, came pouring in, beating against his eardrums. It hurt. It felt sickening. Something was swelling up inside his chest. Something murky and black was screaming to get out. He couldn't let it out. If that thing were to get out, if that were to happen,

"I pity you, I pity you! You who are wretched, ugly, base, small, and sinful — ahh, I shall pity you, Dess! Loved as much as you are, what could there possibly be for you to reject!? Do you wish only to weather away in stagnation, neither drowning in the love given to you, nor repaying that favor, nor even refusing it?"

The gray man shook his head and flung away the face he'd been gripping. The thrown head crashed into the wall behind, sparks scattering behind his eyes, a groan of pain escaping at the sharp impact. But the man paid him not the slightest attention, instead twisting another finger into the wound of the finger he'd bitten and crushed earlier, drawing out still more blood as he did,

"Ahh, ahh, ahh, you... are 'Sloth,' aren't you, Dess!"

With a sharp crack, something in his head felt as though it had split apart.

He hadn't heard anything. Nothing had reached his ears. It was all the ravings of a madman. None of it had hit the mark, none of it had arrived anywhere near the truth. He understood nothing, comprehended nothing. That was how it should be. That was how it must have been. That was how it had to be.

If it weren't so, then he—

"Ahh, that's enough, Dess."

Just as the murky black thing swelling in his chest was on the verge of bursting, the man suddenly let all his earlier momentum drain away and murmured, quietly. At once, the sense of danger that had been raising gooseflesh on his skin, the mad light that had been bearing down before his eyes, drew back, and the strength drained from his whole body, tense without his knowing it.

"It wouldn't do, after all, to press you too, too, too, too, to-o-o-o much, Dess. Wouldn't it be good to spend a while, taking your time, thinking it over slo-o-owly? If you face your favor with sincerity, the answer should come of its own accord, Dess."

"Ahh... uhngh..."

Just what was this man talking about, anyway? From beginning to end, not a single word out of the man's mouth reached his understanding. And yet, despite that, the man behaved as though he had complete grasp of everything about him, as though he understood him even hidden away in some unseen darkness — like a kind adult leading a small child by the hand, like a demon leading astray a lost traveler crossing a rope bridge.

He mustn't understand. He mustn't try to understand. Much less could he, as he was now, allow himself to think in such a way. He mustn't be able to. Because if he ever came to understand it, there truly would be no turning back.

"Ahh, if I might hope... that you are not sloth, but diligence."
