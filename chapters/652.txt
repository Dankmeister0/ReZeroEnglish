Arc 8 Chapter 36: "The Undead City of Lupugana"

—The imperial capital of Lupugana, occupied by the Undead.

In Lupugana—a place that by now deserved the name "undead city" far more than "imperial capital"—Al was waging a one-man battle to find the missing Priscilla.

That said, the search for his lady could hardly be called smooth sailing.
After all, the basics of finding a missing person meant asking around for eyewitness accounts, but here there wasn't a soul who could offer so much as a decisive lead—let alone a useless tall tale.
The only people around were either Undead who, spotting a living person, closed in with violent intent, or survivors who'd made it this far by curling up, holding their breath, and taking no action whatsoever.
Either way, neither was any use to Al's purpose.
Still—

"You know, it really is one of life's absurdities that meeting a living person doesn't necessarily mean Al-san's objective moves forward in any positive way, doesn't it?"

So declared Cecilus, without a trace of shame, he himself being the very embodiment of life's absurdities.
Al agreed with the sentiment completely, but he had no room right now to hash out the finer points of the remark with the man who'd made it—not while dangling, as Al currently was, from the edge of a five-story building's rooftop.

"...Shit."

Inside his iron helmet, Al spat the curse and pulled his eyes away from Cecilus's smirking face.
There was no point saying anything to Cecilus in this situation. To begin with, the reason Al now found himself hanging here was that Cecilus himself had kicked him clean off the roof. And not just that—he'd specifically lured Al to the roof's edge, made him think there was something worth looking at in the distance, and then kicked him from behind.
If that wasn't malicious intent, what was?

"Call it a test, or a check, either way the goal was to gauge things. The fact that you didn't just plummet straight down suggests your reflexes might be a cut above the boss's."

"Yeah? I got no idea what that means."

"No need to! I know my own thoughts and wishes even if you don't. And I should mention—I won't be helping pull you back up."

Cecilus answered Al's question with an answer he hadn't wanted, then clapped his hands, breezy as ever.
Al hadn't understood the reason for this outrage before asking, and hearing the answer hadn't helped either. To begin with, if they were talking hostility or murderous intent, Al hadn't exchanged much of anything with Cecilus that would warrant it.
He'd been saved from a powerful Undead enemy, they'd traded brief introductions, Al had explained what he'd been doing and what he meant to do next—and then he'd been kicked off the roof.

Truly, running into this man could only be called bad luck incarnate, but even so, Al wasn't especially crushed by his answer.
Because—

"...I've heard that line enough times not to expect anything."

With that, Al let go of the ledge himself and gave his body over to free fall.

"Oho," came Cecilus's voice, sounding genuinely surprised, growing distant as Al's mind raced through the sensation of weightlessness.
The drop to the ground was roughly fifteen meters—a height superhuman types could land from without a scratch, but Al, being an ordinary man, could die from it just fine. A perfectly squashed Tometo, right there for the making.
The ground rushing up to meet him was wet cobblestone—not nearly enough water to cushion the fall, and no muddy give to soften the blow either. A brutally unforgiving setup.
So to survive, he needed some action here besides simply falling.

"Ohhh-AAH!"

Al forced the sound from deep in his throat, straightened his bent knees, and kicked off the wall with everything he had.
His body, which should have dropped straight down, gained diagonal momentum instead, closing the distance to the building across from the one he'd been clinging to—same height, opposite side of the street. The window for that kick was between one and two seconds into the fall; too early or too late and he'd have slammed into the wall, snapped his neck, and become an unavoidably squashed Tometo.
But at this timing—in an instant, Al's body smashed through a window.

"Guoooh!"

Dragging shattered glass along with him, Al's body tumbled back-first into the wrecked interior. Cut shoulders and back from the glass, a hard slam against the floor—significant damage.

"But hey, I survived...!"

Rolling backward as he slid across the floor, Al sprang back up and confirmed he was alive. Next he checked for fatal wounds or broken limbs, and only after clearing that could he properly declare his survival.
If there was an irreparable injury at this point, it would need to be repaired.

"Whoa, my left hand's gone!? Oh wait, that's been the case for almost twenty years now..."

After a beat for the obligatory small joke, Al immediately ducked beside the door facing the hallway and swiftly drew the dao from behind his hip.
He'd avoided dying in the fall. But in doing so, he'd crashed into the building with a very loud noise.
Naturally, that noise drew attention from all around—

"It's here!"

"Yeah, it is!!"

With a clatter of hurried footsteps, Undead burst through the door into the room.
The lead Undead charged in with real momentum, but as its pale profile passed in front of him, Al swung the dao and sent its head flying.
He dropped the vanguard instantly, then used its headless body as a shield to gauge the number of enemies who'd arrived. Beyond the broken door were two more, plus one set of footsteps coming up the stairs—three in total. Beyond the first corpse, the second one, recovering fast from its surprise, thrust forward with the short spear in its hands.

Fast recovery. How irritating.
Thinking that, Al interposed the headless corpse in the path of the spear—

"Oh, screwed that up."

The Undead body, having taken the fatal blow, immediately shattered like a piece of pottery that had lost all motivation.
So the tactic of using a familiar corpse as a shield didn't work here after all—Al remembered this only after tasting the enemy's short spear driving fatally into his own chest.

　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　× × ×

"It's here!"

"Yeah, it is!!"

With a clatter of hurried footsteps, Al sent flying the head of the Undead that had kicked down the door.
Kicking the now-obstructive headless corpse aside, Al leapt backward, deeper into the room. Chasing him, the second attacker charged in, thrusting a short spear ahead of it.

"Dona!"

The tip of that spear was swallowed and forcibly seized by a wall of earth thrust up from the floor.
Seeing that this had caught the enemy's attention, Al planted the sole of his foot against that earthen wall—

"Dona! Dona! Dona!"

From the earthen wall his foot touched, a pillar of earth shot out sideways, striking the Undead beyond it square in the front and blasting it backward.
The pillar shoved back the second attacker along with the middle-ranker who'd been coming up behind it, and from the tip of that pillar another earthen pillar launched, and from its tip yet another—stamping both Undead in a multi-stage-rocket burst of force, crushing them against the corridor wall.

"One more...!"

Amid the sound of pottery shattering loudly between pillar and wall, having dispatched three in one go, Al kept his foot on the earthen wall and stayed alert to the enemy's next move.
He'd taken the vanguard by surprise, felled the second and middle-ranker with small tricks. If the vice-commander or commander now decided the tide had turned and retreated, Al would be in the clear—but—

"Well, it's never that easy!!"

The earthen pillar blocking the entrance was smashed to pieces along with the wall, and the last Undead forced its way into the room by sheer brute strength.
It wielded a massive battle-axe and bore a single golden giant eye at the center of its face—a Cyclops Undead. One glance told Al this thing was on a whole different level of dangerous than the previous three. He turned and, driven by pure desperate instinct, made for the room's window—

"Will I make it!?"

Whether he'd have made it or not, the momentum of a horizontal sweep of that battle-axe caught him full in the back mid-shout, and Al ended up redecorating the building's interior with a grotesque splash of his own spilled innards.

　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　× × ×

"It's here!"

"Yeah, it is!!"

With a clatter of hurried footsteps, Al sent flying the head of the vanguard Undead that had kicked down the door.
Next: two more in the corridor, and one worst-case enemy coming up the stairs—he already knew that. Knowing it, Al leapt back hard—

"Dona!"

He had to deal with the second and the middle-ranker in front of him now, and carve out some path—any path—to a strategy against that last monster.

　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　△▼△▼△▼△

"Wow, amazing, amazing! I really thought that Cyclops fellow who charged in last was someone Al-san absolutely could not beat, and yet here you are!"

"You absolute piece of shit..."

"Now, now, I'll grant he was a formidable foe, but running down an opponent you fought a fierce battle against like that isn't exactly an attitude to be admired. Sure, foul language and abuse tend to leave an impression, but I'm not fond of the idea that infamy beats obscurity!"

"I was talking to you, you know..."

Covered in a staggering number of wounds, breathing hard through his shoulders as he stepped out of the building, Al was greeted on his way out—and promptly slid down the wall to slump to the ground right there.
Cecilus, who'd greeted him and been cursed at in the same breath, widened his eyes in surprise.

"I honestly can't tell how you're managing a shocked face right now, hearing that from me. You're the guy who's tried to kill me twice over now. Even a psychopath ought to have some idea when he's given somebody reason to hold a grudge..."

"Oho, so there are still words I haven't picked up, even though it hasn't been that long since I started learning everything the boss says. By the way, what's this 'psychopath'?"

"Basically means someone messed up in the head. Somebody who can go around half-killing people and not even blink."

"I see. But if that's the definition, I don't think it applies to me. —After all, Al-san doesn't die, does he? No matter what."

Grinning, Cecilus offered up a self-justification with a twisted sense of logic.
At that answer, Al, still crouched where he'd fallen, focused on catching his breath. While Al stayed silent, Cecilus raised one finger and continued.

"That Cyclops fellow just now had quite the skill, you know. Sadly nowhere near my level, but against Al-san, he'd win a hundred times out of a hundred. That's a truth I saw with my own eyes!"

"...But I'm alive, aren't I? Or are you saying I'm dead?"

"No, no, you're alive, I'm sure. I did wonder for a moment if maybe an Undead was disguising itself as a living person, but that doesn't seem to be the case either. Which leaves one possibility. —Al-san drew the hundred-and-first try."

He raised one finger on the hand he already had up, then a finger on the other hand, showing Al both.
Adding those two together only made two, and even generously it only made eleven, but it wasn't the gesture's arithmetic that mattered—it was what Cecilus was saying that made Al catch his breath.

"I'm not fixed on the number a hundred and one specifically. A hundred and two, a hundred and three, two hundred, all fine by me! Al-san loses to that Cyclops a hundred times out of a hundred. But what if you tried it a thousand times, ten thousand times, a hundred thousand times? You might just pull off a literal one-in-ten-thousand fluke. And you pulled it the very first time. Am I wrong?"

"—!"

Staying silent and pretending to just be catching his breath had reached its limit.
Inside his iron helmet, Al twisted his face into an expression the other man couldn't see, and felt a fear for Cecilus rise from the pit of his stomach. That insight, that way of speaking—Al himself couldn't understand just what this boy was.
What Cecilus was saying right now came astonishingly close to naming the Authority Al possessed. And not through logic, either—if anything, it was an utterly wild guess.

The boy who called himself Cecilus Segmunt bore the same name as this Vollachian Empire's mightiest warrior, the "Blue Lightning"—but he was, unmistakably, something alien to this world in his own right.

"His height might be for the same reason as me and my brother, maybe..."

Just as he and Subaru had been subjected to "Infantilization" by Olbart's hand, it was entirely plausible Cecilus had been shrunk by Olbart as well.
The man himself didn't identify with the "First" of the "Nine Divine Generals," but when "Infantilization" grew severe enough, Al knew from experience that one's mind could be dragged along by one's appearance. The odds were high the same applied to Cecilus.
In Al's own case, his mind had been forcibly optimized back to normal—but that drastic remedy was something only Al and Subaru could perform. Which meant Cecilus's mind had likely shrunk with no way to counteract it.
If that was what had turned Cecilus into this childlike monster, then the fallout from whatever Olbart had done ran deep.

"Is your silence because you can't answer, or because you don't want to...? Which is it?"

At Cecilus's quiet question, Al pushed himself up off the wall he'd been leaning against.
The dangerous edge lurking beneath Cecilus's quiet tone was proof that the pendulum inside him was swinging hard from side to side—the pendulum deciding whether he'd stand as Al's enemy or ally.
And this, even in an undead city struck by unprecedented calamity, was a trivial obsession that had nothing to do with any of that.

"I keep thinking about it. I really do. If Al-san happens to possess something that lets you draw the one-in-ten-thousand chance first try... I wonder if you could draw it against me, too."

In that case, it wouldn't be one-in-ten-thousand—it might well be one-in-a-hundred-million, or one-in-a-trillion.
But if it wasn't zero, then—

"Just so you know... you'd best stop digging into my charms right about there. Otherwise you're gonna end up glaring at somebody you really shouldn't, and regretting it something awful."

"Well now! Saying that to me, of all people. I don't dislike it—if anything, I like it."

Al sighed at Cecilus's reaction, which seemed only to have found this all the more delightful.
He wanted, somehow, to find words that would dull Cecilus's interest, but by the look of things, there wasn't time. He'd only just managed to survive a fall and an Undead onslaught.
The toll was heavy, but he needed to move the stage forward.

"—Domain release. Subsequent redeployment. Thought experiment, restart."

Before Cecilus could make any unnecessary move, Al carried out the redefinition of the matrix he'd once set.
The frequency and scale meant the backlash would be far too severe, but Al had decided—at least until he was out of the Vollachian Empire—to disregard the cost entirely. Getting Priscilla back was the top priority.

"For that, I'll do it as many times as it—dowaaah!?"

Just as he was about to speak those resolved words, before he could finish—
Cecilus's figure blurred in front of him, and an instant later Al took the sole of a zōri sandal in his side, flipping him clean sideways.
At the sheer outrageousness of it, Al screamed as he slid across the ground.

"That's— that's messed up! After all that talk about looking good on stage, you're attacking a guy mid-sentence!?"

"No, no, no, that's not it! There's no way I'd do something so uncouth! That would be an act deserving of ritual disembowelment, honestly. That's not it—look, over there."

"Look at what..."

As Al finished sliding and pushed himself up, Cecilus, in the middle of his defense, pointed at a wall with his hand. Looking, Al saw a fist-sized hole punched through at chest height, right where he'd been standing a moment before.
It hadn't been there before. But now, abruptly, there it was.
Which meant—

"A sniper...gyowh!"
"Pardon the intrusion!"

The instant his eyes went wide, Cecilus's figure blurred again, and immediately Al felt the G-force of acceleration slam through his entire body, his back being hauled backward at speed.
Cecilus was sprinting furiously, having hooked a hand around Al's waist and taken off running with him. And as the two of them became the wind—chasing right after them—

"Hey, hey, hey, hey, hey, what, what, what is this!?"

Amid the illusion of being flung backward, Al watched street after street, wall after wall of buildings get punched through in his field of view, something terrifying dancing wildly as it pursued them.
If his first impression was right, this was sniper fire. Sniper fire, chasing Al and Cecilus.

"If it's a sniper, the golden rule is that snipers don't move!"

Lie in wait, get the target lined up in the sights, and land one decisive killing shot.
That was supposed to be the basic principle, the golden rule, of sniping. And yet this sniper was precisely tracking Al and the others as they fled, even getting ahead of them to aim at cover before they could reach it.
It was as if they were surrounded by a hundred snipers—or being hunted by a single sniper who could move at will, at high speed.

"Oh! Oh-oh! Oh-oh-ohh!"
"Buwah! Ogh! Bit my tongue!"

Under fire from a sniper with terrifying precision and freedom of movement, Cecilus, hauling Al along as he fled, weaved right and left, up and down, continuing to dodge the attacks.
But he wasn't looking around at all. Al realized it—pure gut instinct. Cecilus was dodging a top-tier sniper on nothing but instinct.

"It's not quite that much, honestly. It's just, don't you get a bit of a tingle when the other guy thinks 'now's my chance'? That's what I'm going on."

"Never mind my questions! Just focus, would you!"

"Sure thing. But this opponent's quite the skilled one, you know. —I've run into them a few times before, and I've never once managed to land a hit."

The ground gave off explosive booms that seemed impossible for a mere zōri-sandal kick, propelling Cecilus to greater speed. As Al, tagging along, felt the invisible shooter and the sniper fire right up close, Cecilus's remark hit him with a double shock.

One: Cecilus had already run into this enemy several times before, and kept surviving.
Two: this enemy was an abnormally formidable foe that even Cecilus couldn't strike down.

"I think it's flying, but I can't see it for reasons beyond just it being fast."

"Can't see it, the enemy...!"

From Cecilus's alarmingly nonchalant remark, Al picked out the key phrase: an invisible enemy.
If he could expose the trick to it, or even without exposing it find some countermeasure—

"Oh, this is—"

Once again, in that same casual, danger-oblivious tone, Cecilus's voice came, and along with it a tremendous crashing sound reached Al from behind—that is, from their direction of travel—and he swung his head around.
In his field of view, three buildings came crashing down, collapsing directly into their path.

Buildings pressing in from the front and both sides of the street, all three having had their foundational ground gouged out by sniper fire, tilted at an angle and came toppling down.
If crushed, there'd be no avoiding being pulverized. The instant he saw it, Al clenched his back teeth—

"Domain, redefine—!"

"Charging straight through the front!"

The instant Al managed to finish redefining the domain, Cecilus, kicking off the ground with Al still held tight, smashed through the window of the collapsing building directly ahead and plunged inside.
Tilting at an angle, listening to the light chain-reaction shatter of window glass breaking with the flex of the walls, Cecilus kicked through the collapsing building's floors, kicked through ceilings, forcibly carving a direct route from the lower floors to the upper ones, and charged upward.

"Teiteiteiteiteiteiteiteitei!"

Since the building was in the process of falling, it was true that the crushing impact with the street would begin from the lower floors.
But escaping being crushed in a manner like this—by kicking straight up through a collapsing building—was a feat of madness by any measure. Not something one should even attempt. Not something one should even say aloud.
And yet, through this deranged act of judgment, Al and Cecilus, faster than the building could finish falling, kicked through the topmost ceiling and broke free into the open—

"—No, this won't do."

Bursting through the ceiling, Al and Cecilus shot straight up into the sky above.
Below them, the three collapsing buildings crashed into one another, their enormous mass causing a tremendous earthquake as they headed toward total ruin. One of the fallen buildings must have had flammable liquor or magic stones stored inside, because Al could see an explosive blaze spreading across the street below.
But—

"Now this, this is a formidable opponent!"

Cecilus's delighted cheer and the shudder of impact through Al's body happened at the exact same instant.
But Al wasn't the one who'd taken the impact. It was Cecilus, who had leapt into the sky along with Al—he'd taken a sniper shot, and the shock was from one of his legs being blown clean off.
The small left leg went flying from below the knee, scattering blood through the air, and the boy clenched his back teeth. Al knew from experience—the pain would hit soon, and his throat would let loose a scream—

"—Over there!"

Instead of a scream, Cecilus shouted that, apparently having pinpointed the enemy somewhere out in the far reaches of the sky.
Then, pressing his one remaining leg against Al's body, which he still held, he used Al's body as a foothold and leapt toward whatever he'd spotted.
The thrust from a single leg was ferocious—at this rate, he'd surely catch up to the enemy.
But he'd be doing it with one leg gone.

"—That won't do."

Even missing a leg, Cecilus was probably still a hundred times stronger than Al.
But it wouldn't do. He needed Cecilus at full strength. So Al drew his dao, pressed it against his own neck.
And without hesitation, swung the blade forcibly through—

　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　× × ×

"Charging straight through the—"

"No, the building on the right!!"

Just as Cecilus was about to launch himself straight toward the building collapsing dead ahead, Al bellowed with a volume that overpowered even the roar of collapsing structures, forcing a change of course.
The instant Cecilus heard Al's cry, Al could tell—he grinned and licked his lips.

In that instant, Cecilus forcibly redirected the angle of his leap and, still gripping Al, plunged into the collapsing building on the right.
Listening to the chain-reaction crash of shattering window glass, Al pointed toward the back of the room they'd landed in.

"That room over there! Secure the box inside!"

"Aye-aye, sir!"

At Al's confident instruction, Cecilus didn't so much as raise a single question.
Forcibly kicking through the wall into the adjacent room, he found the iron box that had been placed there—part of the property this building's owner had kept ready to grab in case of emergency.
Unfortunately, it seemed all that preparation had come to nothing, and the owner never got the chance to carry it out—but thanks to that, Al and Cecilus were able to secure it themselves.

"Up!"

The moment the box was secured, escaping the tilting building shifted back to relying on Cecilus's deranged leg strength and imagination.
Carrying Al, who was now carrying the box as well, Cecilus's sprint punched through the ceiling of the floor below, the floor of the one above, and repeated it again and again, driving all the way up to the topmost floor in one go.
And the instant he kicked through that top ceiling and burst outside, Al—having lost count of how many times this had happened now—dropped the box he was holding right where he stood.
Right before the sniper fire from the distance could blow off one of Cecilus's limbs—

"—Gore."

Inside the dropped box, Al's crudely cast spell served as the spark, and the high-purity magic stones that had been part of some Imperial citizen's fortune ignited, a violently swelling blaze of fire enveloping the two of them from directly below.

"——"

Al tasted the sensation of his skin roasting in the scorching heat, but the curtain of flame that had been born hid him and Cecilus from view all around. Still, even if the fire hid the two of them for this instant, a sniper of this caliber would surely still find and shoot them—no, would still find and shoot Cecilus—without losing track of him.
So the goal wasn't to blind the sniper's eye.

"—Over there."

Muttering that, Cecilus grinned, baring his teeth.
Blood sprayed from Cecilus's cheek, grazed by the shot, his hair tie blown apart and his long blue hair spreading loose. And yet every one of Cecilus's limbs remained intact.

The curtain of flame wasn't meant to hide Cecilus from the enemy—it was meant to let Cecilus see the bullet closing in on him before it could arrive.
Before it reached Cecilus, the bullet had to tear through the curtain of flame to get there.
It was a ridiculous gamble, but Al had bet everything on the belief that Cecilus, of all people, wouldn't miss the change in the flame in that split second before the bullet reached him. And the gamble had paid off.

—And so, across the curtain of flame, the gazes of Cecilus and the sniper crossed.

"——"

In that instant, the pupils of Cecilus's eyes narrowed to points, and the sole of his zōri pressed against Al's waist.
As before, he clearly meant to leap straight to wherever he'd pinpointed the shooter. What was different from before was that all of Cecilus's limbs were once again attached to his small frame.
From here, all that remained was—

"—I won't let my guard down."

Muttering that, Al steadied himself to prepare for the next redeployment of the domain.
Each time, he felt something inside his head tear away, a sense of loss—but Cecilus was right. A one-in-ten-thousand chance was, by definition, an opportunity that only came once in ten thousand tries.
If he wanted to invoke it a second time, it couldn't come cheap.
So Al began the next redefinition of the matrix—

"—Cloud Cutting."

Just before that, an odd change struck Al's field of view.
Beyond the curtain of flame—in the direction where Cecilus had judged the sniper to be, in the sky where Al still couldn't see any enemy at all—something had shifted.

Put simply: the clouds had been sliced apart.
The thick black clouds blanketing the sky over the undead city of Lupugana had suddenly been torn open. And the wound the cumulus clouds bore wasn't just one—two, three cuts followed in succession.
As if chasing some invisible enemy in the sky—no, not "as if." It was exactly that.
A slashing attack was chasing the enemy, and its aftershock was slicing through the black clouds.

"Aaaahh!! It's getting away, it's getting away! It's escaping!!"

The near-shriek came from Cecilus, still with his foot planted on Al's back.
The reason for his wide-eyed shriek was surely the sniper, fleeing from the slash that was cutting through the clouds—abandoning the fight and disengaging.
From Al's perspective, this was nothing but a stroke of pure luck—but not for Cecilus. His eyes glinted with frustration as he glared down at the scene below.
And there—

"Ha ha ha ha! Run, run, cower before my swordsmanship!"

Away from the site where Al and Cecilus had come flying out of the three collapsing buildings, atop another building's rooftop, a single figure was visible.
It was someone holding a sword, watching the retreating enemy go, raising the blade that had cut through the clouds toward the heavens, laughing in high spirits.

An unfamiliar figure—Al frowned, wondering who it could be.
Behind him, watching the sudden intruder, Cecilus let out an "Oh" and said,

"Somehow you've aged in the short while I haven't seen you, Father."

"Your... old man?"

At Cecilus's blithe remark, Al could only sit there dumbfounded.
That figure visible in the distance, the one Cecilus had just called his father—that they'd be joining up with him was fine and all, but the problem was—

"...There's no way I'm gonna die again right after this, right?"

With the formidable sniper gone, still watching the three buildings below him, their collapse still violently ongoing, Al—savoring the sensation of weightlessness—muttered the rest of that thought, leaving it entirely up to Cecilus to answer.
