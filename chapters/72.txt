Arc 2 Chapter 48: "All In"

The moment they'd spat their parting words and gone their separate ways, Subaru's bravado had already peeled away.

He didn't understand himself.
He'd spent his idle hours, day after day, piling up self-analysis on top of self-analysis. Looking back over the results of all that self-diagnosis, he should have known exactly what kind of small, petty choice he'd make in a situation like this.
And yet here he was, running. He couldn't make sense of it.

Since when had he become the kind of fool who dug his heels in like that?
Raising his face against tears, biting down on the pain, all so those sisters wouldn't have to carry the guilt of it — that was completely, utterly unlike him.

They'd turned their backs on each other, and by the sound of footsteps he could tell the distance was widening — and the instant it did, Subaru's composure gave way.
Sharp pain and dull pain, both extremes, came at him without pause. Face twisted into a grand grimace, teeth bared, tongue lolling out, he groaned.

"Ow, owww, it hurts. It hurts, Mom, Dad, Emilia-tan...!"

He called out the names of the three great objects of his life's dependency, and cast a sidelong glance at his dangling right arm.
The pain had been pulsing intermittently since a while ago. Though the arm wouldn't respond to his will, every time it swayed a jolt shot through his brain like an electrode had been jammed in. Dislocated, probably — he wanted to believe that, anyway.
It was the injury from his botched landing after delivering that blow to Rem's horn. The luck of landing on soil, and the luck of instinctively taking the fall on his right side — between those two strokes of fortune, he'd gotten off with only this much damage.
One misstep and he could easily have landed on his head and cracked it open.

He was spinning vertically, and yet whenever he landed it was always head-first — he was genuinely curious what his Luck stat must read like on his status.

"This phenomenon where you always fall head-first from a height — in respectful tribute to Murphy's Law, I'll call it 'the True Murphy's Law.'"

In other words, the reason Subaru kept landing on his head was entirely the fault of some Murphy fellow he'd never even met.
Cursing Murphy, thinking such foolish thoughts — it seemed to distract him a little from the pain. If only it could banish the fear of the obstacle right in front of him too, that would be perfect.

Straight ahead, blocking the running Subaru's path, was the puppy witchbeast — he'd lost count of how many times their paths had now crossed, and counting felt like more trouble than it was worth. Its persistence had gone beyond mere doggedness; it was almost enough to make him suspect the thing held some personal grudge against him.

"I'd kinda like this to be the last time for you and me..."

Running, Subaru braced himself for the mudslide the witchbeast would no doubt unleash.
If he took that head-on and unguarded again, there was no way he'd get off with just a dislocated shoulder. Knocked out cold, then eaten alive — that would be the least painful route. He didn't even want to imagine the alternative.

Shaking his head to throw off the ugly thought, he ran a quick simulation of the footing on the beast trail and the sidestep he'd need. The instant the mudslide triggered, he'd dash sideways, form be damned.
Come on then, he thought, half out of pure desperation, turning back to face the Ulgarm anew—

"Fwuh?"

An idiotic, air-leaking noise escaped him before he could stop it.
And no wonder — what spread before him was a sight that made him doubt his own eyes.

The puppy witchbeast gave a small growl, its tiny body scrunching even tighter, every muscle coiling as if gathering all its strength. Subaru narrowed his eyes, wondering what it meant to do next, when—

"—!"

The ball of fur swelled with explosive force.
The indoor-dog-sized creature that could have fit in his palm transformed, in the space of a blink, into something big enough to be called super-dreadnought-class among the heads of large dogs. Subaru's jaw dropped open.

"That's a classic manga move, but where does all that extra mass even come from?"

The answer to his question was a roar that shook the forest.
It reared, striking the ground with enough force to bound upright, supporting its bulk on its two hind legs. Then the Ulgarm clashed the claws of its now-free forelegs together, flaunting talons that looked like they could strip flesh to the bone with a single swipe.

Maybe it was galled that all its lethal blows had been dodged so far through magic — whatever the reason, the last confrontation it had chosen with Subaru seemed to be gouging out his throat directly.
But—

"I've got no reason to play along with that!"

If his memory served, that puppy — former puppy's — fangs had never actually reached Subaru's body. Which meant the thing had no reason to risk breaking the curse by attacking him.
That being the case, he'd shift course immediately, steer clear of contact with it. But—

"—Hueh."

Subaru veered sideways, aiming to break off from the fight. But blocking that escape route, closing in and running alongside him, was the fang-baring shadow of another witchbeast.
He shot a glance the opposite way on reflex — and there, too, were several more shadows. And from behind came the sound of countless footsteps trampling grass, ceaseless and uncountable.

"Aw, come on, you're all just melting for my pheromones — you bastards!"

His role as decoy was flawless. For the first time since being summoned to this other world, Subaru had stumbled onto the one calling only he could fulfill. Not that he wouldn't spit and immediately resign from this honor the moment he got the chance.

Once he noticed it, the sheer presence surrounding him was enough to make his legs falter. Probably every Ulgarm in this entire forest had converged here all at once.
And the fact that every single one of these witchbeasts had the same goal — crushing Subaru's throat in its jaws — was enough to make his heart shudder.

Ah, honestly. Really, honestly.

"Going after me instead of the pretty sisters — your taste is demonically bad, all of you."

He was too scared, shaking too hard, to even spare a moment to wet himself.
His escape routes blocked, the encircling pack of witchbeasts was silently forcing him to run straight ahead. Which meant, in effect, a one-on-one duel with that massive witchbeast.

Ahead of him, the Ulgarm's vicious face twisted into something like a malevolent grin.
A one-on-one that was really a beatdown waiting to happen — there was no avoiding it, Subaru judged, half resigned.

—The instant he reached that conclusion, Subaru made the decision to play his trump card.

He shoved a hand into his pocket, fumbling around. The feel of a stone. The crumbly texture of broken candy. The unpleasant stickiness of some cloth. And then—

"...The rest is up to Puck, I guess!"

He pulled it out and flung it into his mouth, offering up a full-hearted prayer to the little gray cat.
Pinning the round thing rolling in his mouth between his back teeth, he opened his eyes wide and looked ahead.

The distance between Subaru and the witchbeast had almost nothing left in it.
In less than ten seconds, they'd be within reach of each other's claws and blade.
That was when it happened.

"—Subaru-kun!!"

He heard it.
He heard, just now, a voice calling his name.

That voice was so achingly sorrowful, so full of inescapable despair, so weak — as if witnessing the end of the world itself — that he could tell just how much it was breaking her heart, worrying over whether he lived or died. And, careless of himself, Subaru felt glad.

He was awful. A pervert. A traitor to himself.
He wasn't incapable of imagining what she must be feeling right now, screaming his name like that — and yet he could still laugh about it. He was completely out of his mind.

He laughed at the realization of just how abnormal that thought was, and with that laugh, the last of his smile vanished.

With his working left hand, he leveled the sword at his hip — the broken one-handed blade — low against his waist.

The witchbeast's roar came from straight ahead.
As if to drive it back, Subaru raised his own furious shout, refusing to be outdone.
His voice cracked, an ugly, grating scream tearing out of him. And yet — a fierce, bone-deep resolve behind it. They glared at each other, soul crashing against soul.

Just before contact, with only the barest instant left, Subaru drew a deep breath.

He pictured it: the very center of his own body.
Between chest and hips, in that dantian-like place, he focused on the 'Gate' connecting inside and outside, and he shouted.

"—Shamak!!"

An instant later, black mist erupted as if exploding outward from Subaru at its center.
With Subaru — its point of origin — at the middle, it swallowed up every witchbeast around him, sealing the forest's decisive battle away into darkness.

—Inside the black haze spread a world of non-comprehension.

The world's shape, its color, its smell — none of it could be grasped within that darkness.
The only thing that conveyed anything solid and certain was the soles of his feet, where they met the ground. Without that, he surely would have lost even his sense of up and down within the blackness.

Seeing nothing. Hearing nothing. Understanding nothing.
That was what it meant for the world to end.

Understanding that the only certain thing within himself was what traveled up through the soles of his shoes, Subaru's brain labored desperately at a single task.

That task was remembering.
The contents of his mind, wholly carried off by the black haze — he had to somehow recover them, and carry out whatever it was he had needed to do.

His foot left the ground to step forward, and in that instant he lost everything.
But then his understanding returned as the other foot touched down again. Truth existed only in the soles of his shoes. Even so, he could no longer tell whether he was truly moving forward.

Within the non-comprehension, on the verge of clinging to the one thing he could actually feel, Subaru's brain pushed back against the part of his consciousness that craved rest, demanding instead that it fight.
Something lost had to be recovered. If he failed to do that, something irreversible would surely happen.

Again a foot lifted, non-comprehension arrived, and the other foot bit into the ground, and understanding returned. But this time, somehow, it felt unsteady — loose.
What, what, what — something was missing.

Remember. What lay before this world of non-comprehension — the world where understanding existed.

What had happened, and how had it come to this. Who had done what, and how.
For what purpose, for whose sake, and how could it be broken open? Hadn't he prepared the conditions for that?
Remember, remember, remember—

Sparks of thought began to fly as his brain commanded it.
But they didn't reach. He couldn't quite grasp it, no matter what. One more step, something more was needed.

The step he took forward — the next one didn't come. His feet had stopped. What was the reason they'd stopped? He could tell only a wretched, powerless strength remained in his legs.
Why, how — had he not foreseen this?

If he had, then he must have prepared some means. Remember. Carry it out.
If the outside of his body was no good, then ask within. Even if the outside, wrapped in non-comprehension, was hopeless, call out to every organ inside him that kept moving on its own, unconsciously.

Fulfill your role. Whatever parts can move, move. Do that, and finally—

"————!!"

Suddenly, a burning sensation welled up inside his body.
A restless, feverish passion raged through him, and Subaru's throat let out a wordless, beastlike roar. Or he thought he did. He couldn't even be sure of that.

He didn't know. He didn't know, but his legs had started moving.
His legs regained the strength to move again. Forward. He put a foot out in the direction he believed was forward.

Understanding, non-comprehension, understanding, non-comprehension, understanding, non-comprehension — it repeated, arriving, vanishing, returning, vanishing, returning — and at last—

The instant he burst through the black haze and out into the open, Subaru felt a tremendous impact of contact.

A shock strong enough to nearly wrench the sword clutched in his one hand right out of his grip.
Reflexively sweeping his gaze around to see the result of that impact, Subaru bit down on his astonishment.

Within the black haze, its head still buried inside it, was the massive witchbeast — and driven deep into its torso was the one-handed sword Subaru had been holding.

That unexpected strike sent a sensation back through Subaru's hand unlike anything he'd felt before.
A dull blade, slipping its edge into the body of a living animal — that sensation delivered a mental shock far greater than he'd imagined.

Until now, Subaru had faced witchbeasts and, as a result, taken life twice. Once by accident, once by intent. But this was the first time he'd done it by a method that left such an unmistakable sensation lingering in his hand.

And that sensation brought him a wrongness, a discomfort strangely out of place given the situation.
The feeling that he was, right now, killing something warm and living.

The witchbeast, still sunk in that world of non-comprehension, couldn't even perceive the death coming for it.
Looking around, the black cloud Subaru had run through spanned a vast area, and not a single witchbeast shadow was breaking free of it.

Unlike Subaru, they'd had no chance at all to prepare themselves mentally for this.
Once you sank into that world of non-comprehension, how could anyone laugh at the cowardice of freezing up in that helplessness?

Staggering, Subaru began a slow, lurching run, trying to put distance between himself and the scene while he still could.
His head was heavy, his whole body sluggish. That was the debt incurred from excessive mana expenditure — the result of Subaru's incomplete Gate opening, something Puck had pointed out too.

Casting that spell should, by rights, have drained every last drop of mana from his body, leaving him collapsed on the spot, unable to rise. Subaru had broken through that and kept moving, thanks to his trump card.

"...I guess I owe you brats some thanks."

He spat out the bit of fruit peel still clinging inside his mouth and muttered that quietly.
The fruit he'd spat out — this was the third time in his life he'd tasted it.

Bokko Fruit. A doping item that activated the mana within the body, restoring a small measure of strength to an exhausted body.
Wherever they'd scrounged it up from, it had been among the useless odds and ends the children had pressed on Subaru when he set out to rescue Rem.

From the moment he'd confirmed its presence on him, this plan had been hovering vaguely in the back of his mind.
The instant he cast the spell, biting into the fruit might just let him keep moving.
It had been a gamble staked on his very life, but which way the scales had tipped — the fact that Subaru's life remained, even now, was proof enough.

He turned his steps toward the direction Ram had declared held the barrier.

He had no idea how long his half-formed, mana-starved Shamak would hold.
He couldn't buy any more time than this. Just — somehow, even a little closer to the barrier—

"—Ah?"

But that plan of Subaru's—

"You... bastard..."

—was cut short instantly by a claw that suddenly grazed his right side.

A sharp pain announced the bleeding, and Subaru let out a groan of agony, his knee buckling. But the one who'd done this wouldn't allow him to kneel.
Extending a thick arm, maneuvering that limb with its blade-like claws with unnerving dexterity, it hoisted Subaru's body effortlessly into the air.

"Damn it all."

A gaping maw opened before his eyes, blood-slicked fangs and a rank, fetid breath filling his face.
At the sheer obsessive tenacity of the massive witchbeast, Subaru laughed, half in awe and half out of desperation.

"You're the one who's gonna drop dead—!"

Wrenching the sword still buried in its torso free by brute force, he drove it with all his strength straight into that gaping mouth.

"————!!"

At the fatal blow struck within its mouth, the witchbeast hurled Subaru's body away.
In that moment, refusing to let go of the sword, Subaru went on ravaging the inside of its mouth with the blade. Having shredded it to ribbons, he was thrown to the ground, rolling before rising into a forward crouch.

"Come on! What's wrong! Come get me, you piece of shit!!"

"—!!　—ッ!!"

Face twisted, hair wild, the witchbeast turned a look of pure frenzy on him.
Facing it, Subaru too shook his blood-soaked body, leveling his blade — now with its edge shattered — before him, spitting out foul-mouthed provocations.

Blood had rushed to both their heads; neither could see anything but the other.
His plans crushed again and again, Subaru's own composure had long since fled somewhere. He couldn't move forward now without finishing off the vermin in front of him.
And it was the same for the beast. It, too, couldn't process the ugly, festering emotion pooled inside it unless it killed Subaru.

Both had entered a state of battle-readiness; a single spark would set it off.
That confrontation between one nearly-exploding human and one beast — no, two beasts —

"—Ulgore-ah"

—was permanently interrupted by a direct hit from a fireball raining down from the sky.


"Uwoaah!?"

Covering his face, swallowed by the shockwave, Subaru's body was blown backward.
Without warning, the ground before him had burst apart. The impact carried searing heat, adding burn damage on top of a body already covered in wounds crying out in pain.

Cuts, bite wounds, scrapes, bruises, burns, and wounds of the heart — he was an absolute wreck.

"What in the world..."

Rolling across the ground, Subaru shook his head, groaning in pain as he lifted his face.
And at the sight before him, he felt astonishment for what must have been the umpteenth time today — he'd lost count.

Right in front of Subaru, the witchbeast's massive body was engulfed in flame.

Its black fur wholly wrapped in fire, the Ulgarm thrashed its whole body, agony plain to see. But the more it writhed, the more it screamed, the fiercer the flames grew, until at last the huge body collapsed to the earth with a heavy, weighty sound.
Even then it convulsed a while longer in its suffering, but that too gradually subsided, until at last all that remained was a blackened lump of flesh lying still.

The witchbeast's unexpected end wasn't the only thing that startled Subaru.
The fireball that had burned the Ulgarm alive — the one raining down from the sky — didn't stop at just the one shot. More came flying in, one after another, striking down into the black haze.

The darkness spread out there; what power the flames landing within it unleashed, he couldn't see from outside.
But he could imagine the outcome well enough.

Within that unbroken darkness, the pack of witchbeasts, still sunk in non-comprehension, remained trapped.
They wouldn't even perceive the flames raining down on them. They wouldn't even notice their own bodies burning. They would die without ever knowing their lives had been taken.

Whether that was mercy or mercilessness, Subaru could no longer say.
He couldn't say — but—

"Well now, my my, but that was clever thinking. Using Shamak, normally meant as a smokescreen, deliberately as a marker instead."

The one whose arrival had produced this scene, part comedy and part tragedy, appeared with an easy, breezy laugh — of that much, at least, there was no doubt.

Indigo hair streaming long in the wind, mismatched eyes of blue and yellow, a gaunt, tall frame wrapped in the familiar, outlandish garb — he descended into the witchbeast-haunted forest with effortless composure.
The master of the mansion. The court mage. The useless one who was never around when it actually mattered — Roswaal had arrived.

He brushed the hem of his robe where he'd landed, swept his long hair back over his shoulders, and looked down at Subaru.

"Ahaha. Wha-a-at a dreadful state you're in."

"You're insanely late, Roz-cchi. You have any idea how many times I thought I was gonna die?"

Probably more times than he had fingers on one hand, he thought.
Recalling it made his mood sink a little, but Subaru, out of sheer relief, let himself sink down right where he sat.

"Still though — how'd you even know that was me?"

"Emilia-sama gave me a ve-e-ery thorough warning back at the village. 'No matter how reckless or foolhardy it looks, if he's cornered he'll definitely use magic, so don't overlook him if you see something in the sky' — that's what she said."

"Damn it, Beako — she went and spilled it to Emilia-tan just like that..."

It seemed she hadn't had the discretion to keep it from Emilia after all.
Given that Roswaal's miraculous intervention had come of it, though — well, maybe he should just call it a happy ending.

As Subaru sat mulling over the situation like that—

"Roswaal-sama—!"

Skirting around the black haze, shaking the underbrush as she emerged, was Ram. Supporting Rem on her shoulder, the moment she caught sight of Roswaal her composed expression melted away in an instant.

"I'm terribly sorry to have troubled you."

"No, no, it's quite all right. This happened on my own domain, after all — seeing it settled is my responsibility. If anything, you've done well."

At his words of comfort, Ram's cheeks flushed, and she bowed her head solemnly, pressing a hand to her chest.
Watching that exchange between the two of them out of the corner of his eye, Subaru let out a deep sigh—

"—Subaru-kun!"

—only to be abruptly tackled by Rem flinging herself into his arms, forcing an "Gweh!" of a scream out of him.
Right in front of him, blue hair swayed just beside his face. In other words, this was an embrace at extreme close range — under normal circumstances, a situation he'd have every reason to celebrate as a stroke of luck, but—

"Rem, right now, my whole body, everywhere... ah, my consciousness—"

Her emotional control seemingly gone, the embrace came with every ounce of her strength behind it.
His whole injured body screamed out in pain without exception, and Subaru, desperate, used his free left hand to pound at Rem's back, trying to signal his suffering.
But—

"You're alive. You're alive, thank goodness. Subaru-kun, Subaru-kun."

Overcome with emotion, Rem didn't notice his reaction at all.
Her face pressed against his chest, a warm dampness gradually soaking through it — ticklishness and a jumble of other feelings arrived all at once, overwhelming what remained of Subaru's brain's capacity to process.
In short—

"Not this... pattern again..."

Even as he said it, Subaru could no longer hold his own neck up, and his head drooped forward.
His consciousness receding. Voices fading out of hearing. And, last of all—

"You should sleep now. When you wake, I shall see to it that you're properly thanked for what you've done. — At the very least, I promise you this: whatever threatens you will be dealt with."

Some voice, stripped of all its usual clownish air and utterly in earnest, shook against his eardrum.

Feeling a certain reassurance from it, Subaru slowly let go of his consciousness.
Right up until the moment he sank into sleep, feeling the warm sensation of the embrace still holding his body, savoring at last the thing he had struggled so hard to reclaim —

Subaru's consciousness sank down into the pool of unconsciousness.
