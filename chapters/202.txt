Arc 4 Chapter 36: "At the End of Incomprehension"

---

—The instant he was dragged out through the door, what Subaru felt was a floating sensation of heaven and earth flipping upside down.

"—Oguah!?"

He fell onto the hard ground back-first, throat gasping in agony as the air was wrung out of his lungs. Carried by the momentum, he rolled across the floor and only came to rest when he slammed into a wall. Shaking his head against the sensation of being flung about, he lifted his face, and Subaru opened his pain-dazed eyes wide,

"Beatrice...!"

He gave voice to the name of the girl he hadn't even been able to call out at the moment of their parting, but it no longer reached her. The "Door Crossing" had already taken effect, and a divide neither of them could cross had opened between them. Her rejection was high and deep, and Subaru's voice would never reach her.

"Why is it that I... always...!"

Was it that he could only recognize the mistakes he'd made after he'd already failed, only after he'd chosen the worst, most abysmal option available? He wished for nothing more than to reel in the best possible means, the best possible future—so why was he always lacking, always weak, always foolish?

"Why do you have the Gospel...? Just what are you...!?"

The black-bound tome she'd held in her hands—the existence of the Gospel—had decisively widened the distance between them.
Until now, Subaru had firmly believed, at least to some degree, that in the short time they'd known each other, he and Beatrice had built something real between them.
Even if they traded barbed words, even if they scowled at each other on sight, Subaru alone had believed there was something more than that, something that didn't simply end there.

It had been a delusion. A conceit. The very height of a misguided, mistaken assumption.
What Subaru had stubbornly believed must exist was nothing but the product of his own self-satisfaction; Beatrice had held toward him only the feelings her words literally conveyed. She had merely gone along with Subaru because the Gospel commanded it, purely to see her mission through to the end—in her true heart, she had held him at a distance, despised him.

"...Is that... really how it is?"

Told that the bond he'd believed existed was a sham, Subaru had wanted nothing more than for it to be denied—but Beatrice had cut him off with an angry shout instead.
Affirming his imaginings, confirming that the bond had been a fake, that his existence had never once stirred her heart in the slightest, that everything up to now had existed for no reason but a sense of obligation.

"That laughter, back then, that anger, the way she tried to protect me... all of it, every last bit, was just a scripted lie...? Something like that..."

It couldn't be, Subaru's weak heart still wanted to deny. In that final moment, at the very brink of their parting, the tears in Beatrice's voice clouded the truth of her words.
It was still far too soon to draw a conclusion on any of it, based on himself alone.

"Whatever the book says, whatever it is—I remember being saved by you... That's a fact that doesn't change, a debt only I know about."

Throughout the loops that began at the manor, Subaru had been saved by Beatrice more times than he could count.
He'd made frequent trips to her Forbidden Archive to gather his thoughts after Returning by Death, and when he'd been struck by a Witchbeast's curse, she had, in the most literal sense, saved his life by pleading for the curse to be lifted. In a loop-world now erased from existence, when he'd let Rem die and found himself hunted by Ram and Roswaal, she had come to protect him even by twisting the meaning of a Contract that had been nothing but empty words.
That greatest debt of gratitude no longer remained in this world—it existed only within Subaru's own heart now.

"Back then... I was happy."

She had saved Subaru when he was convinced he had no allies left at all.
When he'd come to see both Rem and Ram as enemies, when he couldn't read through to Roswaal's true intentions, when he was worn ragged with doubt, unable even to fully trust Emilia—Beatrice alone had saved him.
Just how much that fleeting Contract, in that one moment, had saved Subaru was a debt of gratitude words could never fully express, one he could never repay.

"I'll pay back the debt. Whether you lent it to me of your own will, or whether you were just respecting whatever the book's will is—I don't even know that much yet... so I'll go find out."

He could no longer put that question to her, not now that she had so clearly hardened her resolve to reject him. And so Subaru's resolve held no meaning in this world—it would have to carry over into the next.

He raised his right hand. A hand missing three fingers. Shoulders and hips seizing up. A head that had taken blows. A tongue shortened just a little. Every one of these was a pain he mustn't forget.
Behind his closed eyelids, Rem appeared. Then Petra, then Frederica rose up, then Beatrice turned her back to him, and last of all, Emilia.

—Everything Subaru had let slip through his fingers in this world, everything he had failed to grasp.

To take it all back, he would commit to the one act still within his power. He would carry out again what Beatrice had interrupted, and dive once more into the spiral.

"—————"

Extending his already-shortened tongue, Subaru steeled himself to bite through it once more.
Thinking of the self-destruction he'd already botched once, the agony came flooding back, raising gooseflesh across his skin. Cowardice welled up, his legs trembled. Resolve was nothing but wordplay—worth nothing at all in the face of the end.
He forced down those backward-leaning negative feelings, pushed through the greatest negative feeling of all, the one that came with facing death. And as if praying that he could rewind time far enough to redo everything, Subaru moved to close his eyes for his final moment—.

"...Where... the hell is this?"

He finally noticed that the place he'd arrived at after passing through the "Door Crossing" was somewhere he'd never seen before.

* * *

To Subaru, it was an unfamiliar space that didn't seem at all like the interior of Roswaal Manor he knew so well.
Damp, clammy flagstones; grimy walls thick with creeping ivy. Desks arranged in disarray, rusted metal—things that looked like tools—scattered about, the sheer visual clutter stirring up discomfort.
And above all,

"Ugh—!?"

An overpoweringly thick stench that, once noticed, could not be ignored.
Different from the rot of kitchen garbage, yet a smell he could only describe as putrid, it stirred up nausea, and Subaru clapped a hand over his mouth, wringing stomach acid out of his empty gut.
He spilled a yellowish mess of vomit onto the floor and, breathing hard, surveyed his surroundings with narrowed eyes. The more he looked, the less familiar the space became—more than merely unknown, it stood out as fundamentally alien.

The space was a dim room floored in flagstones, roughly the size of two of Roswaal Manor's guest rooms combined. Not quite spacious, but not cramped either.
In one corner of the room were the scattered desks and mysterious implements; and opposite them—occupying what looked like the greater part of the room's space—was

"A broken desk, and... crystals? Some kind of crystal, or fragments of magic ore? And this hole..."

Looking down before him, the wreckage of a destroyed desk lay scattered together with drained fragments of magic ore, and beyond it yawned a hole perhaps four meters across. Even accounting for the poor light, it was a hole deep enough that its bottom couldn't be seen.

Glancing up at the wall, he saw it was moss, glowing with a faint blue light, that lit the surface. The kind that grows in clusters in forests, it apparently had the property of absorbing ambient mana and glowing—in the woods around the manor, too, relying on starlight and the light of this luminous moss meant one could avoid total darkness.
Conscious of the moss's glow, he crawled along the ground, feeling his trousers grow damp and a slime staining his palms with distaste, and stared fixedly down into the hole.

A quiet, cold wind rose from the depths, and it seemed to carry with it a stench sharp enough to twist one's nose—no, sharp enough to crush it outright.

"Ugh... blech. Guess it's for the best I don't have the guts to look inside... what even is that smell?"

If this had been the distinctive raw stench of something that had once held life, Subaru's imagination might have leapt to a worst case one rung above the worst. But the stench flooding in was different from the rot of flesh or water—if he had to compare it to anything, it was closer to what chemicals gave off.
The kind of unbearably painful stinging in the nostrils one felt when smelling a pungent chemical. What reached him from the bottom of the dim hole was that sort of smell, one distinctly non-biological in nature.

"—What else, then."

Giving up, both physically and mentally, on checking the bottom of the hole, Subaru wiped his nose and, consciously breathing through his mouth, looked around the room.
What stood out were the wreckage of the desk lying at his feet and the spent husks of the magic ore. The iron desk had been crushed under some tremendous impact, and the ore fragments looked to have been piled on top of it. Turning the crumpled desk over, he noticed a pattern carved, as if engraved, into the bent-up top surface of the desk.

"Looks kind of like... a magic circle, maybe..."

That sort of magic circle was practically a staple in other-world fantasy, but Subaru couldn't recall ever coming across one since arriving in this world. In this world, magic fundamentally worked by intervening in the outside world through a living creature's body, and aside from the occasional exception like magic lamps or Magic Vessels, he'd never run into anything resembling one.
All the more reason his surprise at discovering a magic circle now was considerable—

"Does it actually work, though? And even if it does, why put one here of all places... what reason would there even be to place a magic circle..."

Maybe it hadn't been able to trigger magic directly on the spot, or maybe it had some function for receiving remote magical effects. Or perhaps it was some kind of system meant to carry out a spell continuously without a caster present—

"If that's it, then it'd explain why there's a drained-out chunk of ore sitting right next to it."

The magic ore, attached externally as an energy tank, had run dry, and as a result the magic circle lost its effect—that theory made the most sense. What remained unclear even with that conclusion in hand was the true nature of the hole and the destroyed desk. The possibility that the whole room had been set to blow apart if the spell was ever interrupted couldn't be ruled out either.
Even so,

"In the end, none of this answers the question of just where the hell this place even is."

A dark hole whose depths he couldn't even guess at. A magic circle and magic ore that seemed to have once carried out some kind of spell. Observing the room, thick with its rot-like stench, he picked up a rust-red piece of metal hardware from another desk and set of tools lying in the corner.
Something like pliers or wire cutters, it resembled the tools one might use to build a plastic model kit. It, too, was coated in the same slime that fouled the floor, and more than that, whatever time had passed since it had last been used, it was so weathered that it crumbled to dust the moment Subaru's fingers touched its edges.

Not just the tools but the desk was in the same state; its legs broken from years of decay, it had been exposed to the elements so long it was on the verge of becoming dust, and the instant he set his foot on it lightly, it lost its shape and turned to scrap.
No information was to be had here either. If there was one thing that nagged at him, though, it was this:

"The way this stuff broke, and when it broke, is pretty different from the desk by the hole..."

Unlike these things here, which had grown brittle over time and finally collapsed, the desk by the hole had clearly been twisted and mangled by some destructive force. And judging from the state of the broken flooring around it, he could tell that destruction had happened very recently—within just the past few days, if that.

"A broken room... who did this, and why..."

The moment he voiced the question, Subaru suddenly found his own train of thought ridiculous.
What did he even intend to do with the question he'd raised? It didn't seem like the kind of thing thinking it over would answer, and more importantly, the problems Subaru already carried were more than his two arms could hold as it was.
On top of that, he had to avoid anything like cramming another small item into the gaps of an already-overloaded burden, anything that would hasten its collapse. Above all, the very act of turning his attention elsewhere like this felt unbearable, like nothing more than a pathetic struggle to put off the moment of self-destruction bearing down on him.

But even understanding that irresistible feeling of "shame," the sheer strangeness of this room still had its grip on Subaru and wouldn't let go. He was witnessing something tremendously important right now—.

"—————"

Guided by that unanswerable conviction, Subaru swung his head around, searching for a way out of the room. Since it had been the "Door Crossing" that flung him in here, it couldn't be that he'd been thrown into a room with no openable door.
Sure enough, Subaru found the door that had roughly dropped him into this room—a small door, apparently for ventilation, fitted high up on the wall.

Other than that, no door that might serve as an entrance or exit was to be found. The proper door was probably beyond the collapsed hole—on the far side of the room, impossible to reach.
The instant he swallowed that fact, Subaru gave up on choosing any proper means of escape. Wiping the sweat and unidentifiable slime from his palms onto his trousers, he held his breath for a moment and reached his fingers toward the ventilation door.

The door was set at a height he could only just reach on tiptoe, and its size was closest, perhaps, to a garbage chute. It wasn't so narrow a person couldn't pass through, but Subaru wasn't slight enough to move through it with any ease.
Struggling with the grip of a right hand missing fingers, he pried the rusted door open with a creak, forced his body into the narrow passage, and began moving. It was a cramped space, like an air duct. He'd braced himself for the worst-case scenario of it being a breeding ground for insects or rats, but it was surprisingly clean—well, not clean exactly, but a dust-choked passage that at least, mercifully, showed no sign of any living creature at all.

It took about three minutes to get through the narrow passage. By the latter half he'd grown used to crawling on his belly and his movements had begun to smooth out, right as he reached his goal. Dropping down by the same means into the room connected to the previous one by this vent, he gave the surroundings a quick once-over, paying utmost attention to whether a similar hole was gaping open here too, but—

"Different vibe from the last room. Compared to that place, which looked like some kind of lab, this one's more like... a waiting room, I guess."

It was about a quarter the size of the previous room. Whether it was designed purely as a room to pass through, aside from two doors of the walk-through variety there was nothing here that could be called furnishing. Truly, it looked like a waiting room.
If it had had magazines and chairs, it would have been the perfect setup—.

"Given the direction I've been coming from, beyond this door should be... yeah, figures."

Twisting the creaking doorknob and pushing it open, he found a crumbling foothold and, right in front of him, the hole spread open again. Letting out a small breath at this ridiculous deathtrap of a setup, Subaru closed the door—which had barely served any purpose to begin with—for the sake of his own peace of mind. The stench had already crept into this room too, and he regretted having been slow to realize it.
Shaking his head lightly, he turned instead to the opposite door. Whatever lay beyond this one should be, for Subaru, still-uncharted territory—.

"...!"

Naturally, sweat he thought he'd already wiped away returned to his palms, and he couldn't stop a cold sweat trickling down his back.
He had no idea what lay beyond this door, but at the very least, he needed to imagine what, in the worst case, might be waiting there.

"If this is... inside the manor, then..."

It was a room he'd never seen before, but it wouldn't be strange at all if Elsa were standing right there the instant he opened the door and stepped out. And Subaru had no confidence he could keep his composure faced with that murderer.
The fierce urge driving him to die as quickly as possible transformed into a hatred that brought back the memory of grinding his own teeth to bits.

Just the thought that the lunatic who had killed Petra, Frederica—Rem—with that blade might be right there was enough to torment Subaru with a fury hot enough to boil his brain.
A cursing wish that she be there, and a shameful thirst for survival hoping she wasn't. Swaying between these two extremes of feeling, Subaru carved a vicious smile onto his own lips, and

"—————"

Whether she was there or not, he couldn't betray that maddened feeling.
Facing the world he arrived at, at the end of that thinking gone beyond sanity,

"—————ah"

Witnessing a sight that should never have existed, Subaru, in this instant, lost himself.

* * *

—In truth, an answer to the question of where this unfamiliar space was had already surfaced somewhere in Subaru's mind.

To begin with, there was hardly any place in Roswaal Manor Subaru hadn't set foot in—he'd even been able, in a sense, to freely come and go from the Forbidden Archive—so if any unfamiliar possibility remained for him, it could only be beyond the door of the "evacuation route," the one he'd twice laid a hand on and twice failed to see beyond.
The one place blocked once by a gray-furred spirit, and a second time forced him to retreat when confronted by the murderer during this loop. As a result, Subaru had never once laid eyes on what lay beyond that door.

For that very reason, even as he grew suspicious of this string of rooms whose purpose he couldn't fathom, he had never once doubted that the place was part of Roswaal Manor—

"Where... the hell is this?"

Still dazed, he raised the question in a plain, dumbstruck voice, to no one in particular.
What spread out before him, beyond the open door, was not a cold, dark underground passage—but nature, thick with lush green trees, and stranger still,

"M-morning...?"

Looking up through the gaps in the trees, he could see the rising sun in the sky. From the angle of the sunlight and the feel of the wind, he sensed with his own skin that this was an early-morning breeze, and further doubt churned through his mind.
Subaru had made it back to the manor just before evening, and accounting for the time that had passed since—his conversation with Petra and Frederica, and then Elsa's attack—his injuries had come just before night began to deepen. And now, nearly half a day had passed since then.

"While I was unconscious, huh...!?"

The one time he had gone through with self-destruction, biting through his tongue, Subaru had lost consciousness. When he woke again, his regret at having his wound healed and at failing to die outright had rushed ahead of everything else, and he'd forgotten to even consider it—but just how long had Subaru actually been unconscious in the Forbidden Archive?
He recalled Beatrice boasting that that place was "cut off from the passage of time." He had no idea how much credence to give that, but—

"If this just gets saved right over, overwriting everything...!"

It would be an unthinkable disaster.
Before the situation he'd feared so many times over got repainted into reality, Subaru needed to end his own life as soon as possible. He needed to—and yet there was another feeling asserting itself, butting heads with that sense of urgency. That feeling was screaming this:

—He had to confirm just where this place was.

He had no idea what meaning there was in doing that. Looking back over everything that had happened, and considering the sheer viciousness of Return by Death, he ought to kill himself right now.
But some coldly rational part of Subaru was proposing otherwise, even having fully grasped the circumstances he now found himself in.

"—Dammit!"

Kicking off the ground and spitting to the side, Subaru stepped forward into the forest before him. Running at a dash through the gaps between the trees, what rose in his mind was his conversation with Petra.
The evacuation route, she'd said, led into the mountains behind the manor, connecting to a mountain cabin there, and in the event of an evacuation, people were meant to escape from there along with the emergency supply packs.
If he believed her words, then this place must be that mountain cabin, and the ground he was now running across must be the mountain out back that he'd set foot in a few times before. But—

"That was the mountain cabin just now? Then where were the supply packs? And in the first place... what kind of emergency measures would even be stored in a place that looked like it had been abandoned for that long...!?"

All Subaru had seen in that place was an inscrutable space and a heap of physical evidence backing up his bad feeling about it. Far too little of it matched what Petra had described. Above all, if Frederica or Rem had been maintaining it regularly, there was no explaining the state of decay he'd found there. He knew the two of them's work as maids well enough to say that with confidence.

Dashing through the forest, he grew suspicious of the lack of any slope, too. Was a cabin supposedly deep in the mountains really built somewhere with not so much as a slope for a hundred-odd meters around? On the verge of losing track of where he even was, irritation boiled up at the fact that he wasn't seeing things correctly to begin with.
And the breaking point of that mounting irritation and distrust arrived far sooner than he expected.

The trees opened up, his view suddenly clearing, and Subaru skidded to a stop. The ground, paved in a manner too rough to really call paving, was evidence that people came and went here, and more than that, though seen from a distance, the row of houses standing there plainly declared this to be inhabited land.
Taking that in, Subaru's thoughts were finally, truly seized by shock.
Because this scenery was—

"The—the 'Sanctuary'!?"

It was the place he'd said goodbye to half a day ago, the place he'd calculated would take a full day to get back to.
Following the chill running through him, he raised his right hand. Fingers were missing. Lost. Return by Death hadn't triggered. And yet here he was now, standing in a place he had no business being.

"Why... am I here. Is it because of the 'Door Crossing'...?"

There was no other answer.
It meant that Subaru had been sent, by Beatrice's hand, from the manor's Forbidden Archive to a corner of the Sanctuary via the "Door Crossing." But how had that even happened?

"Distance doesn't matter...? No, well, there was that one time I got teleported from the manor to the village's stable, but..."

That had been, distance-wise, still within a range one might consider acceptable for teleportation. But given the distance between the Sanctuary and the manor, a long-distance move of this scale was plainly beyond both imagination and common sense.
Swallowing the fact that he'd just witnessed a power so preternaturally supernatural, Subaru clawed at his own head, forcibly stuffing the thought down into his gut, and

"Anyway! If I'm in the 'Sanctuary' right now, then... Roswaal!!"

He would race back to that clown and make him spit out every last one of his schemes.
Roswaal, who had treated Beatrice with such care back at the manor. That Warlock surely knew everything—her true identity, even the reason she carried the Gospel.
If he knew it all and was toying with an ignorant Subaru in the palm of his hand, then fine. Subaru would smash him in the face, and even if he got burned, even if he got sliced open, he would sink his teeth into that man's throat and make him spit out everything.

"—————!"

In that instant, Subaru forgot the constraint that he had to take his own life and took off running, his thoughts dyed a blazing red with rage, toward the edge of the village. He sprinted for the building where Roswaal slept.
At the speed of a Subaru turned into the very embodiment of fury, his body, tearing furiously through the Sanctuary, forgot fatigue and pain, guiding itself to its destination.
Flinging the door open with a force nearly enough to kick it down, Subaru forced his way into the house, baring his teeth,

"Roswaal! Get your face out here! I've got a mountain of questions for you!"

He burst in with enough ferocity that his own maid back home might well have laid into him for it, opening with a rough, blistering outburst. There was no answer from inside the house, and Subaru, footsteps loud, stormed toward the bedroom and flung the last remaining door open,

"I'm not letting you play innocent, and no lies either. Spit out everything you've been hiding, every last bit of it..."

—so hand it over, was how Subaru had meant to finish, but his voice broke off.
Because the target of this tirade, condensing all his built-up grievances, was nowhere to be found in the room.

The fact that he'd vanished only brought Subaru's fury to a fresh boil. He kicked the bed with all his might, piling further anger onto his throbbing toes as he burst back out of the building.
If Roswaal was anywhere, it would be Ryuzu's house—meeting with Emilia, or else he'd gone off to see Ryuzu or Garfiel. Either way, the man had some nerve, springing into action the instant Subaru was out of the picture. The timing was suspiciously good, good enough to make Subaru wonder whether Roswaal's supposedly serious injury had actually been faked.

A spiral of negative feeling, endless once he started doubting. With his thoughts wholly dominated by it, he glared sharply out over the Sanctuary—and once again, Subaru arrived at an understanding far too late.

"...Huh?"

It was still early morning; as far as Subaru knew, in the Sanctuary's usual daily schedule, this should be around the time its residents began preparing breakfast and doing laundry. With the evacuees gone, there was no longer a need for communal soup kitchens, but each household still needed to cook its own meals.
And yet there was no trace at all of that daily routine in motion. No, before even that—

"It's not just Roswaal and the others... where the hell did everyone go?"

Look right, look left—not a single soul in sight.
Now that he thought of it, from the moment he'd come out of the forest back into the Sanctuary up until now, he had no memory of passing anyone, no memory of even glimpsing a single person.
Even accounting for the fact that the Sanctuary's total population wasn't especially large, cutting straight through the middle of the village without seeing a single face was a possibility so slim it beggared belief.

"There's no way..."

Shaking his head as if to shake off the bad feeling, Subaru knocked on the door of a nearby house. He knocked, confirmed there was still no answer, and opened the door to peer inside. —Empty.
Two beastfolk women, sisters, were supposed to live in this house.

And so, going by whatever he could recall, Subaru peered into house after house that caught his eye, and each time his hopes were betrayed, disappointment piling up.
No one anywhere. Not a soul, anywhere. Every trace of human presence had vanished from the Sanctuary.

"Somebody! There's gotta be somebody here!? Where did everyone go!?"

The bad feeling kept piling up.
This anxiety, this inexplicable sense of loss—he recognized it.

It was the memory of the massacre that had greeted Subaru when he'd returned late to Arlam Village, utterly unprepared, in the midst of the confrontation with the Witch Cult—bodies piled atop each other, face after face frozen in agony and despair. Familiar people drained of all color, and Petra, collapsed and never to move again.

"—————ahh!"

Terror shot up Subaru's spine, and he broke into a run as though pushed forward by an unstoppable dread. Letting out something like a scream, Subaru made for one place, and one place only.
A particularly grand building at the edge of the village. It was one of the few tokens of respect shown to the chieftain who bound this Sanctuary together, and it now served as the sleeping quarters for one girl who had borrowed the place for herself.

"—Emilia!!"

Bursting in, shouting the name of his beloved girl, Subaru swept his eyes across the room.
The silver-haired girl would turn to look at him with sleep-heavy eyes, blink a few times, look surprised, and then say, "Morning, Subaru," with a smile sweet enough to ache in his chest—

"—————"

But the place that was supposed to turn to greet him remained, after all, empty.

He rushed to the bed and reached out to touch the rumpled sheets. There was no warmth left in them; whoever had been sleeping here had already left some considerable time ago.
Confirming just that much, Subaru dashed back out of the house, this time heading for his last resort—the one place that might tell him the answer to this situation, this incomprehensible, senseless nightmare.

"Hah... hah...!"

His breath ran ragged. Running with the taste of blood in the back of his throat, Subaru raced to the deepest part of the Sanctuary, the tomb where the Witch of Greed, Echidna, slept.
Along the way, there was no sign at all of Garfiel, who should have been sitting there to bar the way, and Subaru found himself arriving right in front of the tomb unopposed. Whether that was a relief, or whether it would have been more of one to see Garfiel's familiar face even as an obstacle—.

"No... what face would I even show him..."

Having failed to save the one and only sister Garfiel had, how could he ever show his face without shame?
Some part of him felt relief that Garfiel wasn't there, even while another part felt an unbearable anxiety at finding no one at all—and the fact that he wasn't even reflecting on his own weakness in feeling that way struck him as hideously, wretchedly ugly.

Shaking his head to cast off the sentiment, Subaru turned his feet toward the tomb while nothing stood in his way. It wasn't the hour for the Trial to begin, but perhaps some action might come from the Witch's side regardless. Hoping for that, clinging to that hope, Subaru sought out the figure of the Witch who might give him an answer to his question—.

"—ka, fuh"

The instant he took a step forward, Subaru felt something pass clean through his body.
He looked down slowly. Below his chest, above his lower abdomen, dead center in his torso—there, a perfectly round hole, fist-sized, had opened up.

"Huh... eh?"

He reached out a hand, pressed it to the hole. And with a wet sound, a torrent of blood came spilling out from it. He instinctively tried to stop it with his palm, but the hole went straight through his body, opening out his back as well. He couldn't stop both, and his body, already having lost far too much blood, could no longer hold itself up and collapsed.

—No pain. No understanding. What had happened.

Death. Dying. He was going to die. Death, that thing he knew—he understood it was right in front of him now.
Why, how, had it come to this. Elsa? Had she chased Subaru all the way here? How much distance was there between the manor and the "Sanctuary"? Beatrice—no, could it be. The Gospel. Why would she. Rem. Who did this. Dying. Scared. What. Who. Emilia. Witch. Witch. Wi—.

"—————ah"

His vision began to blur. The end began to draw near.
The death he'd expected was arriving in a form he hadn't expected at all. Subaru felt no relief at finally having managed to die. Only, purely, an overwhelming fear of death, right now.
No matter how much he'd claimed to have resolved himself to die, this was what happened when death came by a road other than the one his resolve had chosen. His heart was in disarray, his shameful thirst for life screaming, his soul refusing to be torn away from the world—and even so, "death" slowly ate away at Subaru, and

"—so... weak"

With nothing but tears for his own helplessness running down his cheeks, Subaru's heartbeat came to a stop.
The death he had longed for, obtained in a form he never wanted, left his dead face twisted in pain and terror, and the pitifulness of that death went unwitnessed by anyone at all,

"—————!"

With an audible sound, he was chewed.
