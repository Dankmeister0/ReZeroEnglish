Arc 4 Chapter 105: "The Peddler's Trap"

"――――"

Wrinkling his nose, Garfiel came to a stop, an unfamiliar wrongness bringing his feet to a halt.

He'd been tearing across the ground like a burst of wind, and now he cut that motion short in an instant. Driving his right foot into the soft earth like a stake, kicking up a cloud of dust, Garfiel crouched low and turned his head.

Snorting, sifting the air through his nose, he stood in the forest surrounding the village of the Sanctuary. He'd been racing through the barrier, searching for Ryuzu, who was nowhere to be found within her usual haunts.

He'd already checked every place he could think of, moved his feet back and forth so many times he'd lost count, searching for that small, familiar frame.

His heart was pounding fast — the cause was a mounting impatience born of a bad feeling.

He could sense a situation developing that was extremely inconvenient for him. Outsiders were throwing their weight around inside the Sanctuary, and now even Ryuzu was acting differently than usual — worse, she'd gone and made a decision without so much as consulting him.

"Damn it… just what the hell is goin' on here?"

Clawing at his short blond hair, Garfiel traced the white scar on his forehead with a finger.

When confused, when flustered, when his heart threatened to lose its composure — touching that wound was Garfiel's habit, a kind of trigger to steady his mind.

Tracing the scar with his finger let him recall the moment he'd received it. By remembering that wound — a mark he'd carry for life — and the time he'd been at his most foolish, he could pull himself back to a state of calm.

Most people who knew Garfiel as a person tended to judge him as short-tempered and coarse in character. That impression came from a tangle of factors — his usual words and manner, his attitude, his appearance — but as a matter of fact, it was mistaken.

Garfiel Tinzel was, surprisingly enough, calm and shrewd, and made it a discipline to always keep his head working.

That was because Garfiel knew — had felt keenly, in fact — that brute strength alone wasn't enough to see his convictions through to where they were meant to lead. He knew what he needed to learn and what he needed to do to make his wishes come true, and he never let up thinking about such things. It was through that very effort that his unshakeable convictions had been cultivated.

And yet—

"But even so… why the hell is everybody runnin' around doin' whatever they damn well please…!"

Bitter, pained, baring his sharp canines, Garfiel let out a growl.

Just as he'd said, the situation kept slipping past what he'd anticipated. Because his firm convictions fixed the conclusion first, Garfiel was terribly poor at flexible thinking.

As far as his stance on the Sanctuary went, his answer was always one and the same.

Even if the process for arriving at that answer branched off in countless directions, on that point alone he would never yield. Which was precisely why he believed he'd paid attention to every conceivable process along the way.

But among those, he had never accounted for Ryuzu acting on her own authority, or the kind of covert scheming the outsiders were currently pulling off.

Sadly, though he was a man who never stopped thinking, he wasn't particularly experienced, nor was he exceptionally clever compared to others. He simply threw himself at things, desperately, with everything he had. That was all.

"――――ッ"

Snorting hard through his nose, Garfiel narrowed the pupils of his golden eyes.

The fine hairs on his back stood on end with the certainty of a scent caught. Bending his knees slightly, he leapt back into motion. Using the springing branches of the trees as footholds, higher and faster, he bounded freely through the green, chasing after the trail he'd caught.

"Buncha 'em, all in a row… the hell're they thinkin', huh!?"

Gnashing his teeth, he let his irritation loose in a roar.

His voice came out close to the snarl of a raging feline, and fury took root in Garfiel's twin eyes.

What his nose had caught was the scent of far too many living things. The smell of sweat, of grass and dirt being trampled underfoot, the sharp, unsettled odor seeping from anxious human bodies, smell, smell, smell—

"――――!!"

It was unmistakably the scent of a great mass of humans on the move.

The number was no fewer than ten — a massive scent closing in on fifty. In the Sanctuary as it stood now, there was only one group that could possibly be moving in such numbers, at this particular timing.

—The refugees who had fled here from the village right by Roswaal's mansion.

People who had taken shelter here to escape danger. To forget the debt of gratitude owed for having been taken in, and to make a move like this, at a time like this — what were they thinking?

"That bastard… messin' around with me…!"

What rose in Garfiel's mind was a short black-haired boy he found unbearably irritating. Someone whose sharp eyes didn't match his glib, lightweight attitude at all. And yet, at times, he was also a man whose gaze turned strangely clear, as if he could see straight through you.

That look, as though he were staring at somewhere that wasn't here — it reminded Garfiel of another man he despised, and it made his skin crawl every single time.

There was no doubt that this man — someone Garfiel already couldn't stand — was behind all of this.

It was obvious the refugees' sudden action had been stirred up by that man's incitement. For whatever reason, the refugees who'd been talked into it placed enormous trust in that boy.

No strength, no notable ability to speak of, just a man skilled with nothing but his mouth. To Garfiel, he was the most despicable kind of creature in this world — weak, foolish, and self-serving.

He couldn't help but think that he really should have taken care of things with his own hands, sooner.

He'd had plenty of chances. But the boy had been so utterly full of openings, and yet, when it came to matters requiring the resolve to bear pain, he had something genuinely fearsome about him — and so Garfiel had held back. The price of that hesitation was the position he found himself in now.

He'd lost track of Ryuzu — family, who ought to take priority above all else — and now those who threatened the Sanctuary's peace were doing as they pleased. Whatever else happened, he could not let the refugees out through the barrier.

A few days ago, maybe it would've been fine. But the situation had changed.

"That bastard knows the old hag ain't normal, that's why."

In the place Garfiel called the testing grounds, and Ryuzu called the Chamber of the Origin, there lay a crystal in which slept a girl named Ryuzu Meyer — the source from which Garfiel's family member Ryuzu had come.

Honestly, Garfiel held little interest in that crystal.

The girl sleeping within it was the spitting image of his family, but if what was inside her was different, then she was an entirely different being. If beings that shared only the shell while differing inside counted, then as far as Garfiel knew, there were more than twenty "Ryuzus" in existence.

It was impossible — and Garfiel had no intention of trying — to hold the same affection and attachment for each and every one of them that he held for his Ryuzu.

Garfiel held the authority to freely command the duplicate bearing his grandmother's face. And he had no particular aversion to using that authority. Still, the fact that he tried, as much as possible, not to exercise it, came down to his own temperament.

He disliked palling around with others, disliked giving orders to people. And something in him bristled, inexpressibly, at the sight of a being that obeyed his instructions as obediently as a puppet.

That was the full extent of what Garfiel felt toward the girl in the crystal. Nothing more than that.

To begin with, Garfiel didn't think of himself as someone with a deep well of feeling, or wide enough arms to embrace many things. What he had to divide among others had always been small to begin with. Two arms, two legs, one body. What he could offer was limited, and he had to be selective about who he offered it to. So Garfiel gave it — only to those few he genuinely cared for.

"So that's whyyy… if you thought I'd go easy on the likes of you, that's a complete misunderstanding, a real green-behind-the-ears kinda mistake, y'know that?"

Kicking hard off a tree trunk, he leapt clear over the height of the forest.

Curling his knees in, spinning through the air, Garfiel's body landed with a force that sent fallen leaves swirling upward. The ground caved in with the shape of his boot soles, and the whinny of Earth Dragons rippled through the gaps between the trees.

Letting the shock of the landing bleed into the ground, Garfiel slowly straightened his back.

The wrinkle at his nose now wasn't from relying on his sense of smell — it was from anger. Cracking the bones of his neck, clacking his sharp teeth together, eyes narrowed with fury, he glared straight ahead.

Before him stood two Dragon Carriages, lined up one behind the other.

The Earth Dragons pulling the carriages, startled by Garfiel's murderous presence, had grown agitated, and the coachman was desperately calling out to calm them.

That coachman was someone Garfiel recognized as well.

"Well, if it ain't the beanpole. Ha! Not that it's much of a surprise, but I take it you're the ringleader of the bunch that got talked into this by that bastard."

"That kind of language… well, I'm well aware of how you rate me, so I can't argue much."

To Garfiel, hands shoved in his pockets, the figure on the driver's seat — Otto, gray hair grown out a little unevenly, wearing the face of a man perpetually burdened with troubles — gave a wry smile.

He skillfully worked the reins, calming the heavily breathing Earth Dragon beautifully, then let out a breath.

"Heh, ain't that somethin'. If I'd really put the scare on 'em, wouldn't be strange for a spooked Earth Dragon to bolt clean out of control."

"I worked hard to make sure that wouldn't happen. Besides, I'd been told in advance that you'd probably show up."

"Haaah—?"

Something in Otto's remark was too much to let slide, and Garfiel's ears twitched.

Unconsciously reaching a finger toward the scar on his forehead, Garfiel stepped forward to press him on what he really meant.

"What's that supposed to mean? You lot figured you'd use the chaos to make a run for it outta here, and I caught you at it and shut it down pathetically. Ain't that right?"

"Yes, that's right. Piggybacking on a sudden bit of trouble to grab the maximum profit — that's standard practice for us merchants. We'd even been promised a hefty reward, and I really thought we'd pull it off cleanly…"

"…………"

Otto covered his face with his palm, playing at having his scheme foiled. But something about both his words and his gesture carried a whiff of composure that left Garfiel puzzled.

It wasn't at all the attitude of someone whose trump card had just been snatched away. If anything, his manner right now was exactly like Roswaal's — the way he acted when he had someone dancing on the palm of his hand.

"That face, that attitude… you look just like the guy I hate most in this whole world."

"That's quite the evaluation. For reference's sake, mind telling me who that might be? I'd like to keep it in mind, for the sake of building a good relationship with you going forward."

"Ha! Me and you? Don't make me laugh, dumbass. — The guy I hate most is probably bein' fussed over in the finest room in the whole Sanctuary right about now."

"I see, I see. It must be quite hard, when the person you've set your heart on is so devoted to your love rival. My condolences."

"Keep runnin' that mouth and I'll shut it for you by force, y'hear."

At Otto teasing him about his feelings for Ram, Garfiel clicked his tongue in irritation.

In truth, Garfiel could turn this whole situation around by force if he wanted. The refugees' mass escape had already failed the moment Garfiel caught wind of it.

He wouldn't let them out. But so long as they weren't trying to force their way out, he figured there was at least no need to actually harm them.

Right now, what mattered most was settling this quickly and getting back to the Sanctuary. What Garfiel had to prioritize, above all, was maintaining the Sanctuary — this was nothing but an unnecessary complication.

"Anyway, this escape plan's a failure. If that bastard's around, get him out here. Gonna make him apologize for pullin' this pointless stunt, and dependin' on how things go, he might have to eat a little pain too."

He needed some outlet for the frustration built up in his gut, or the scales wouldn't balance. And beyond that, Garfiel needed to know what that boy — Natsuki Subaru — had been thinking, pulling a stunt like this.

It had only been a few days since he'd boasted, loudly, that he'd break the Trial and free the Sanctuary.

Even if his spirit had broken, this was far too soon. Frankly speaking — the guy had no guts at all.

Once, Garfiel was going to knock some sense into that misguided thinking of his—

"Ahh, unfortunately, I don't think I can grant that request."

"Haaah?"

"Don't you notice? To you, I'm supposedly wearing the same face as the person you hate most in this world, right now. So then, shouldn't you consider that I might be wearing the face of someone about to do what that hated person would do?"

"――――"

Roundabout, evasive — the true meaning behind Otto's words refused to come into focus.

But Garfiel judged this wasn't the kind of remark to let pass unheeded. Above all, for Otto to wear the same face as the man Garfiel hated — Roswaal — meant nothing less than that he was scheming something, trying to roll someone around on the palm of his hand.

"…What're you plottin', huh."

"Right. Using a face we both recognize — scheming, maybe? Villainy?"

Saying this with an expression like something had poisoned him, Otto rubbed his own nose with a finger. Watching him, Garfiel narrowed his eyes, and only then noticed the wrongness of it all.

Two Dragon Carriages before him, two Earth Dragons, and Otto. — And yet, the driver's seat of the carriage behind had no coachman where one ought to be.

No — it was worse than that.

"Why'd I scare the Earth Dragons enough to shake the whole carriage, and nobody inside so much as showed their face?"

"Now, why indeed."

Shrugging, feigning ignorance, Otto made no move to stop Garfiel as his expression changed and he approached the carriage. Garfiel leapt lightly onto the carriage body and threw open the passenger door.

And upon seeing the sight within, he gnashed his teeth loudly.

"—There's no one inside, you know?"

"I can damn well see that…! What the hell's goin' on! My nose caught the scent of a whole crowd in this movin' carriage, plain as day…!"

Spitting as he spoke, Garfiel stepped into the cabin — and cut himself off mid-sentence.

At his feet, inside the cabin he'd stepped into, clothing lay scattered about. Multiple garments — men's, women's, adult-sized and child-sized, all thrown together in a heap. Seeing it, Garfiel's face twisted at how simple, how childish, the trick used to fool his nose had been.

"A trick this damn stupid…!"

"Right about now, other carriages that departed later, from a different location than these two, should be making their attempt to escape the Sanctuary. Even your legs won't make it in time."

"From a different location, you say? What the hell are you talkin' about. Escapin' without usin' this road, through the beast trails of this forest — you think that's somethin' you can just pull off easy!? They'll get lost halfway and end up caught by my nose, that's how it'll end. You're not tellin' me you don't know I can get through the barrier."

"That's right, I don't know much about just how much you're capable of. But—"

Stepping down out of the cabin, Garfiel and Otto faced each other before the carriage. As Otto trailed off, Garfiel closed the distance, his chest burning with impatience—

"You don't know much about me either, do you."

"――――"

"I'm the type you don't even register, aren't I. You're the type who despises people like me — like Natsuki-san — the smooth-talking, all-mouth-no-substance sort, more than anyone. That's why you've never paid attention to what I've been doing all this while, and you haven't noticed what I've done here, either."

"What're you gettin' at…?"

"It means these past few days, I wasn't wandering the forest pointlessly, or spending late nights in the stables with the Earth Dragons for no reason. I found a way to escape that doesn't rely on this road — and I made sure it was thoroughly taught."

Otto raised both hands with an air of supreme confidence.

At his words, Garfiel's sharp eyes went round, his mouth hanging open.

Taught — taught to whom? The coachmen driving the other carriages? Even so, the meaning of what he'd just said didn't connect. Was he saying he'd told the Earth Dragons themselves, in the stables?

If that were true, then—

"So you're one of that bastard's crew after all…"

"Wha—!? That's how you land on it? I gotta say, I'm not exactly satisfied with that conclusion myself!?"

At Garfiel's sympathetic look, Otto raised his voice in a clamor. It was a familiar bit of behavior — Otto acted this way every time Garfiel saw him in the Sanctuary.

His usual self, in this scene of all places, felt strangely out of place.

"Either way, I'm gettin' you back inside first. I'll find the rest of 'em and drag 'em back too."

"To keep that from happening, I had them scatter in four directions. Catching them will be impossible. And if you still try to run off after them anyway, I've been told to tell you this: — the people fleeing know nothing about the truth of the Sanctuary, nor anything about Ryuzu-san. So letting them go won't put you at any disadvantage. …How about it?"

"That's some thorough groundwork, ain't it."

The message was no doubt from Subaru — there was no mistaking that.

It meant they'd seen right through Garfiel's reasons for not wanting to let the people inside out. It only stoked his irritation further, this sense of being entangled in someone else's scheme — but the reason for chasing frantically after the refugees had, admittedly, disappeared.

"If that's really true, mind you."

"Suspicious as ever. I'll have you know — Natsuki-san included, none of us want to sour our relationship with the people of the Sanctuary. If anything, we'd rather keep the friendship intact while still choosing liberation… If anyone's getting in the way of friendly relations between the two sides, I'd say it's you."

"…It ain't like I've got the slightest interest in gettin' in the way of gettin' along. Not as long as it stays inside, anyway."

"Stubborn about that, aren't you."

"That's the one thing I absolutely won't budge on, no matter what."

Otto made an exasperated face, and Garfiel let out a heavy breath.

Still, despite showing a skeptical stance, Garfiel judged that the content of the message was probably true. Not just because Otto had gone out of his way to warn him, but because if Subaru had picked up on the reason Garfiel didn't want anyone leaving the Sanctuary, it made sense he wouldn't hand the refugees any information that could become an obstacle to their escape.

"Still, though. If that's the case… I don't get why they'd need to pull a stunt like this monkey business and cheap tricks just to get 'em outside the Sanctuary. If the reason's that they might come to harm stayin' inside, that's a hell of a thing to say about people you claim you wanna get along with — not trustin' 'em."

"If that were the case, I have to say, it's rich hearing that from the very person whose name would probably top that list. I had my doubts too, but according to Natsuki-san, it's meant as a solid insurance policy. And, I suppose, there's also the matter of buying time."

"――――"

Hearing the phrase "buying time," Garfiel's expression tightened.

Wetting his lips with his tongue, wondering what exactly "buying time" meant in this context—

"What're you plottin'…"

"Insect repellent, I suppose — to make sure a man and a woman can be left alone together, undisturbed, somewhere no one will interrupt."

With a tired face, yet one that carried some sense of accomplishment, Otto shook his head.

At first, Garfiel was about to snap back — what kind of nonsense are you spouting — but seeing that face, his mood shifted. That expression didn't look like the face of someone telling a lie.

If that was so, then the words were true. And a man and a woman — the two people that could only mean were Natsuki Subaru and Emilia.

"――――"

Instinctively, Garfiel understood he could not let those two meet.

The beastkin blood flowing within him sensed something on a purely instinctual level.

Snapping his head up as if struck, Garfiel turned to look back toward the direction of the village.

If he let Subaru and Emilia's meeting go unchecked as it was, it would invite the worst possible outcome for Garfiel. The Sanctuary's liberation — it would come to pass—.

"――――"

*There's no way that could happen*, reason argued with Garfiel.

He'd seen it with his own eyes — Emilia, crushed by the brutality of the Trial, her spirit broken. And more than that, the girl had lost something that had been supporting her heart since the day before, growing weaker still.

Could a girl who'd worn her heart down that far, who'd fallen to her knees before a nightmare, really stand back up in a day or two?

And yet, instinct still screamed at Garfiel that he should run to stop this regardless.

The "past" the Trial revealed. Even knowing it was, strictly speaking, a different past for each person, what all the pasts shown had in common was the way they pried open the lid on the most detestable memories buried within each challenger's heart.

Garfiel, in his ignorance, had stepped into the tomb of his own accord and been shown his own "past."

Recalling that moment now, he couldn't help but feel the blood drain from his whole body, an emptiness pierce his heart.

Ever since then, he'd lived thinking of nothing but not betraying the thing he'd sworn, fiercely, to his heart. That was how deeply, how sharply, the Trial carved into the hearts of those who challenged it.

"Situation's changed. I'm headin' back to the Sanctuary, right now. Gotta find the two of you're talkin' about and change their minds before—"

"And you think I'm just going to stand by and let that happen?"

"――――"

As Garfiel turned to head toward the village, Otto called out from behind, stopping him.

But Garfiel's response to that was ferocious.

"—Oh, guh."

"Too noisy. Sleep it off. I don't have time to keep playin' around with you anymore."

Closing the distance in a single step, his clenched fist buried itself in Otto's stomach.

Avoiding the bone, driving in as if to gouge his organs, the punch sent Otto's body lifting off the ground; drooling, his body was flung to the earth.

Garfiel had held back. He'd put in more force than needed just to knock someone out cold, but as payback for being so cleanly set up, it was, if anything, on the gentle side.

Clicking his tongue at Otto lying collapsed, Garfiel lightly scraped the ground with the sole of his foot—

"—Where do you think you're goin', hm?"

"—!?"

Just as he was about to break into a run, his feet stopped.

Garfiel turned back, his face blank with shock. There stood Otto, upright.

A hand pressed to his stomach, coughing, spitting saliva onto the ground — yet still conscious.

"The hell? Wasn't tryin' to kill you, but I don't recall punchin' soft enough that you wouldn't even black out, hey."

"Is that so…? Then I suppose your imagination was outmatched by my endurance. Ahh… all that quiet daily training was worth it, then. …A traveling merchant's body is his capital, after all — better sturdy than sorry, hey…"

At Otto's pained laugh, Garfiel felt a bad premonition and turned to face him squarely once more.

One more strike, and he'd cut his consciousness short.

This time, without holding back like before, he'd aim for the head. The risk of lasting damage went up, but it would knock him out for certain.

"Gonna hurt a lot more this time, so clench your teeth good and tight—"

"Still thinking you can get away with going easy on me, huh… that condescending attitude — that's your undoing!"

Crouching forward, settling into a lunging stance, Garfiel heard Otto shout.

Bloodshot eyes glaring at Garfiel, Otto swung up the hand that had been hanging limp at his side with all his might.

In the next instant, a burst of tree leaves swept up and blotted out Garfiel's vision, and a single moment's opening was born.

"Wha—!?"

Caught off guard, Garfiel froze, his body stiffening, motion cut short.

And then—

"Take this—!!"

Simultaneous with Otto's piercing cry, breaking through the curtain of swirling leaves, something thrown glowed—

—and a crimson radiance swallowed Garfiel's entire body.
