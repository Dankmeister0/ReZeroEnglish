Arc 4 Chapter 12: "A Souvenir from the Tea Party"

"It seems the space I so carefully prepared has been broken. Someone this rough... would be Minerva, wouldn't it? That girl's always a bit quick with her hands."

"A bit...? I'd say it was instantaneous. A whole new brand of violent tsundere healer loli with a big bust. You crammed in so many elements I'm stuffed just from it."

Swinging his now-healed right arm from the shoulder, Subaru hurled the insult at Echidna, who stood before him.
The quality of pressure he felt from the white-haired girl hadn't changed. And yet, he had to admit her little scheme had had at least some effect.

"Well, I'm in enough of a mood now to sit across from you and talk. Compared to those witches from before, you're at least somewhat more rational... though the Witch of Sloth seemed like the one who'd actually be reasonable to talk to."

"Sekhmet, if I do say so myself, was the eldest and most rational of the witches. Though when angered, she was anything but ordinary."

"Anything but ordinary, meaning... scary when mad?"

"No, meaning the rest of us together couldn't match her. I'd wager the other five witches banding together still couldn't have beaten her."

Settling back into her chair as she said this, Echidna drew a suspicious look from Subaru.
What crossed his mind was that languid woman with the long reddish-purple hair. That someone whose every gesture radiated a listless, lifeless indifference could be, by far, the strongest of the witches...

"Anyway, listening to you just now, I get the feeling you're leaving the 'Witch of Envy' out on purpose."

"—Here's a piece of advice, for the sake of our conversation."

When Subaru asked, recalling the one witch whose name had never once come up, Echidna raised a smile to her lips and held up a single finger.
Watching Subaru's eyes fix on that fingertip, she tilted her head slightly and said,

"I consider the other witches my friends, and worthy of my respect. I am, myself, a character riddled with flaws in many ways. The presence of those I spent so long alongside was, to me, a refuge and a salvation. That is why I have collected every single one of their souls, without exception."

"...That sounds like something I shouldn't just let pass, but go on."

"The one who destroyed those witches was the 'Witch of Envy.' —Could you speak of the being who slaughtered your friends in the most horrific way imaginable, and still smile while doing it?"

Her smile didn't change. But its quality did.
A chill ran up Subaru's spine, and before he realized it, he found himself nodding again and again in agreement with her. Seeing this through, she dipped her chin—"I thought as much"—and said,

"The air's grown a bit heavy, hasn't it. Shall I pour you another cup of tea, to clear the mood?"

"...I don't have the courage to drink Donna-tea again. If you can't bring out anything decent, I've got no intention of eating or drinking here."

"Being invited to a witch's tea party should have been the envy of everyone in my day... My, my, how times and people change."

Echidna, seeming to have intended to prepare a cup for Subaru too, looked disappointed as she topped off only her own cup and brought it to her lips.
If her words were to be believed, the true nature of that tea was her own bodily fluid. Which meant she was drinking down her own self-produced fluid, by her own hand—

"You know, rabbits and stuff—there's that story about them eating their own droppings, like some kind of perpetual motion machine."

"Being lumped in with that is somewhat humiliating... hm? Or was that, perhaps, an indirect way of saying you'd like to hear about the Great Rabbit?"

"Great Rabbit?"

Subaru tilted his head. It was a word he'd heard somewhere before.
Turning it over in his memory, he recalled where he'd heard it. Right, that had been on Patrasche's back, running down the Lifaus Highway.

"A witchbeast on par with the White Whale... was that it? The Great Rabbit, and the Black Serpent?"

"Daphne's negative legacy. Every one of them was a problem child even she had trouble handling. Never mind the Black Serpent—I hear the White Whale and the Great Rabbit have been causing trouble in various places."

"Speaking of which, the White Whale's already been taken down, thanks to my efforts the other day. Thanks to my efforts."

Thumb pointed at himself, Subaru flared his nostrils with a boastful look. Hearing this, Echidna, for the first time, widened her black eyes in surprise.

"Oh? Is that so. Impressive, that. From what I can see, you show no particular skill with a sword, nor any hint of talent as a mage... you must have maneuvered those around you quite skillfully."

"It's a little sad how instantly you figured out it wasn't a solo kill...! Maybe I took it down myself, single-handedly, in one clean stroke, who knows!"

"Whether it's the White Whale or the Great Rabbit, it's hard to imagine offhand that there's a human who could kill either one alone. Even in my day, the only one who might have managed it was Reid, I'd think."

Subaru raised an eyebrow at yet another unfamiliar name coming up. Seeing this, she said "Hm," pressing a finger to her thin lips.

"Has it not been passed down to this era? I'd thought his achievements were rather considerable. At the very least, cutting down twelve dragons single-handedly, adults among them included—that's a feat only he could manage, I'd think."

"Nah, I'm just a little— how do I put it, shallow on common knowledge, or general knowledge, that kind of thing. Anyway, wow, that guy sounds amazing. How amazing are we talking?"

"—Reid Astrea. He should have been granted the title of 'Sword Saint,' though I wonder if it's still in use now?"

At Echidna's words, the pieces clicked firmly into place in Subaru's mind.
Astrea—that was the family name shared by Reinhard and Wilhelm, a lineage beloved by the god of swords through and through, boasting both a current Sword Saint and a Sword Devil in their ranks.
Presumably, the founder of that line was none other than Reid Astrea.

"Okay, got it. It's still around, the Sword Saint title. Dunno what generation we're on now, but the current Sword Saint's a buddy of mine. Probably as much of a monster as that ancestor of yours you're talking about."

"That's quite a way to speak of a friend... is what I'd like to say, but knowing how far outside common sense Reid was, I can't laugh at you for it. Now then, were we talking about the Great Rabbit?"

"Ah, no. I mean, I'm plenty curious about the Great Rabbit and the Black Serpent too, but..."

Sensing the telltale signs of a talker eager to show off her knowledge, Subaru put a stop to it and thought it over. There was a mountain of things he wanted to ask, but if he tried to digest all of it at once, his head wouldn't keep up.
Better to pick out only what he truly needed to know and pursue those in depth.
First, the most immediate thing—

"Um, so you're Echidna. The 'Witch of Greed,' already deceased. That much's right, yeah?"

"Just as we confirmed at the start. That's correct. This is within my dream, and whenever you want to leave, just say the word."

"Thanks for the consideration. So, for my first question..."

Hand to his chin, Subaru looked the white-haired girl up and down. Bathed in his rude stare, she pressed a hand to her translucent white cheek and closed one eye. "What is it?"

"I feel like I should've confirmed this first, but... what exactly about you makes you 'deceased'? You're living it up, day after day, aren't you."

"...Ah, I see. Indeed, I hadn't explained that point at all. Though you're one to talk, never having brought it up until now—but so am I."

Clapping her hands, Echidna nodded as if something had clicked into place. Nodding along in agreement at her gesture, Subaru scratched his head, thinking he'd been out of it somehow.
Between the impact of the witches' sudden appearance and the shock of the ensuing prank-filled witch reunion, even a question that basic had been slow to occur to him.

"If you're a graveyard ghost, that'd be fine and all, but with this much interference going on, I can't exactly write it off as my imagination anymore."

"I won't deny 'ghost.' It's a fact that I'm a spiritual body that has lost its flesh. Now, as for why I exist like this... well. 'Deterrent' would be the most accurate answer."

"Deterrent...? Against what... no, 'for what purpose' is more correct, isn't it?"

"Sharp of you."

Nodding, satisfied, at Subaru's answer, Echidna gave a small clap. Then she gestured at the sky—the artificial blue sky—with her hand.

"What binds me here to this place is Volcanica—the Divine Dragon Volcanica. You've heard of it at least, haven't you?"

"...That's the dragon that has a covenant with the king of the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica or whatever, right? I heard that name in the Royal Selection hall."

"Volcanica, correct. I am sealed to this graveyard by that dragon's power. And the reason Volcanica did so, as you've guessed, is as a deterrent against the 'Witch of Envy.'"

Though Echidna's gaze remained calm and intelligent, every time the words 'Witch of Envy' left her lips, a flash of harsh emotion crossed her eyes for just an instant.
That alone spoke to how great the rift was between her and the 'Witch of Envy.'

"The 'Witch of Envy' is still sealed within the Evil Sealing Stone even now, but her seal isn't unshakable. Volcanica's own lifespan isn't eternal either, and there's no telling if the seal might be undone by some chance occurrence. There are no small number of beings who worship her, and one can't rule out some natural disaster damaging just part of the Evil Sealing Stone. —Which is why Volcanica left my existence intact."

"So that if the 'Witch of Envy' were ever revived, I'd be a fighting force to counter her, huh?"

"Though I doubt that leaving me behind, of all the witches, will live up to Volcanica's expectations. If it wanted to leave someone, it should have left Sekhmet. The trouble is, Volcanica itself had friction with Sekhmet. Apparently it developed an aversion to her after getting beaten black and blue."

Echidna laughed off the grudge between dragon and witch lightly, but Subaru, hearing it, couldn't laugh along.
The rift between a dragon and a witch didn't seem like something on the scale of a petty falling-out between people, and to begin with, he had no idea how much to believe the claim that the 'Witch of Sloth' had beaten a dragon black and blue.

Subaru fell silent, unsure what to say. In front of him, Echidna continued, "In any case—"

"Myself, a witch, and the Divine Dragon Volcanica. Add to that the 'Sword Saint' and... the Sage, perhaps. If just those were gathered, then even should the 'Witch of Envy' be revived, there might be a chance to oppose her. That, I suppose, is the extent of Volcanica's faint hope. There's the background behind why I'm still airing my posthumous shame like this, even now."

"So basically, it's the dragon that's keeping you here as a bound spirit, that about right?"

"More precisely, it's Volcanica's will, sustained by a Mathers formula. Since you've made your way here, you must at least know the name Mathers? Or perhaps even that family name no longer exists..."

"Nah, Mathers is still around. Roswaal L. Mathers is the lord of this region, graveyard included. And he's my employer, or my guardian, or a pervert, or..."

Exasperated at how deep the witches' connections ran, Subaru racked his brain over how to explain Roswaal. But apart from Subaru's hesitation, Echidna's finely shaped brows twitched, and she murmured, "Roswaal?"

"Forgive me, but did you just say Roswaal?"

"Hm? Ah, yeah, that's right, Roswaal. What, you know him?"

"If I knew him, that would be a strange thing indeed. After all, I'm someone from roughly four hundred years ago. If that person existed in the same era as me, the story gets a little strange."

Subaru agreed with her point too. Pursing his lips as he recalled the clown-like face, Echidna said "Right," pressing a finger to her lips, then—

"This Roswaal you speak of—would he be someone with long, deep gray hair? And his eyes were... yellow, I believe."

"—No, then that's not him. The Roswaal I know has hair that's more indigo, kind of the same color as the jeans I used to wear. And the eye color's different too. That guy's got one blue eye and one yellow eye."

Relieved at the difference in features, Subaru let out a breath, then paused to think.
Roswaal had told him that the stewardship of this land, the 'Sanctuary,' had been passed down through his family for generations. Which meant the covenant with Volcanica that sealed Echidna here in this graveyard had also been passed down.
A role the family had inherited generation after generation. If that was the case, then—

"Maybe 'Roswaal' is like, a title passed down, a name you inherit taking over the role. Sometimes you see manga where a girl gets stuck with a super masculine name, and there's always some setup like that."

"One who inherits the name Roswaal, is it. If so, that's a bit of a nightmare."

Nodding as if satisfied with Subaru's theory, Echidna then shrugged, a faint weariness showing through. Subaru frowned at the uncharacteristic reaction, and she said, "No, it's just—"

"The Roswaal I knew had a personality that ran a bit too single-minded, a bit too devoted. There was a precariousness to him, like he might dedicate his entire life to some one goal. If, after my death, he remained unchanged even still..."

"You mean, not satisfied with dedicating just his own life to it, he might've dedicated the whole family's time to it too?"

"Exactly that. My, my, it's a frightening thing just to imagine."

Despite what she said, a faint smile had risen to the corners of Echidna's mouth.
Was it just Subaru's imagination that it looked like the smile of a parent watching over a poorly-behaved child? In any case—

"Alright, I get why you're here in this graveyard, and who's responsible for it. As for how it actually stands now, I'll grill the present-day Roswaal about it once I wake from this dream."

"Feel free. ...Now then, any other questions?"

"Course there are. Next thing I want to ask about is the Trial. The one I hear takes place in this graveyard. I want to hear what it involves. And if there's a model answer, tell me that too while you're at it."

"Asking the one who set the question for the answer along with the question—how merciless of you."

"I want the easy way out, cheat if I can, don't mind admitting it. If I can shortcut it, there's no reason not to. I'm the type who plays games while peeking at a walkthrough site."

That was because it was such a pain to lose an event and have to do a second playthrough.
Setting aside such a slapdash player philosophy, Echidna closed her eyes as if in thought, and precisely five seconds later, opened them again.

"The Trial, was it."

"Yeah, that's right. I'm gonna be tested on something, apparently. If I can't clear it, an important girl of mine is gonna be in trouble. If she gets homesick, she still won't be able to leave the 'Sanctuary.' And of course, leaving her behind and heading home alone isn't an option for me either."

Something like a barrier surrounded the 'Sanctuary.' If it rejected her passage, then Subaru, who wasn't rejected by it, had no intention of passing through it either.
She would break through the Trial, and the two of them would pass through that wall together.
He'd do anything he could to make that happen. Even if it meant—

"Cheating, even!"

"I hate to dampen your enthusiasm, but I don't know anything about the Trial. I had no hand in it. Which means I don't know its contents."

"Why not!"

His momentum instantly dammed up, Subaru let out a scream. Hearing this, Echidna shook her head. "Can't be helped, can it," she said.

"Do you know where this place is? It's my graveyard, isn't it? Meaning, this graveyard was made after my death. And this Trial you speak of takes place within this graveyard, doesn't it? Which means the Trial of the graveyard was created after my death. There's no way I, already deceased, could have had any hand in it, is there?"

"After talking with you this much, all lively and everything, I can't accept that explanation now!"

"In any case, I am not the one who set the questions. So I cannot answer regarding the Trial. If anything, I'm the one who'd like to hear about the Trial. Its content, the tendency of the questions, how the test-takers are selected, and of course the answers to the questions—my curiosity knows no bounds."

Eyes sparkling, sparkling with a thirst for knowledge—the 'Witch of Greed.'
Sighing at her sight, so honest about her desires, Subaru judged there was no point dragging out the conversation about the 'Trial' any further.
Which left—

"Guess there's not really much I wanna ask you after all."

"...Eh? You're joking, right? That can't be. I mean, I'm the 'Witch of Greed,' aren't I? People from all across the world once traveled here seeking my knowledge. And now, standing before me, given free rein to ask whatever you like, and you've got nothing you want to ask...?"

"Well, you're deceased, right, so you don't really have much knowledge about anything after your own death? Most of what I want to know is current, ongoing stuff, so there's not much point asking someone who's ignorant on all that..."

"No, no, no, hold on a moment. It's true I'm out of touch with today's world, but in exchange, there's hardly anything from the past I don't know. Countless bits of history, worn away over four hundred years, that no one remembers anymore. Isn't this your chance to learn them? The same goes for the story of the witches earlier. There probably isn't so much as a record of it left anywhere in the world now."

"But I'm not really that interested in witches, and anyway you said they're all dead, and I've got a lot on my mind already, so hearing about all that doesn't really..."

"Ehhh..."

Watching Subaru drift firmly toward wanting to take his leave, Echidna's face twisted with dissatisfaction. The two of them had completely swapped positions.
Still, it was the truth as far as Subaru was concerned. As for how great the past witches' misdeeds or great achievements had been—he was interested, in a sense, and yet not, in another sense. Given that hearing about it wouldn't change anything, he really wasn't all that interested.
That said, when asked if there was anything else useful he could draw out of Echidna, nothing sprang immediately to mind either.

"Oh, actually, I just remembered one thing."

"Yes, yes! Good, that's it. Yes, there must be something, of course there is. Ask whatever you like. If it's something I can answer, I'll answer it, go on!"

After being kept waiting, Echidna's eagerness for Subaru's question was nothing short of extraordinary.
Thinking that, witch or not, at her core she couldn't shake off a certain human-like quality, Subaru recalled something about the 'Sanctuary' and said,

"So, there's a resident of the 'Sanctuary' where this graveyard is, and he called this place a testing ground. Apparently it means the 'Witch of Greed's testing ground, and given there's a barrier that won't let halfbloods leave, what kind of experiments were being run here..."

"I can't say."

"If you could just tell me..."

But the question was cut down flat, by an Echidna whose expression had gone blank.
Faced with her utterly unapproachable manner, Subaru fell silent despite himself. Seeing his reaction, Echidna, perhaps realizing how sharp her own words had cut, looked awkward.

"Forgive the way I put that. But there are things I cannot say. I am unable to answer that question. Not that I can't say it—that I don't want to say it."

"...That's not exactly a phrase that leaves a good impression, 'testing ground.' But you're not denying it either."

"I'd like you to stop right there. I don't wish to be held in contempt."

Eyes downcast, Echidna refused any further pursuit of the topic.
An overwhelming being, a witch, hunching her shoulders and pleading with Subaru like that. Hearing it, he had no choice but to give up pressing further.
In its place, what crossed Subaru's mind instead was—

"Come to think of it, your name... I heard it before, even before coming here."

"............"

Echidna kept silent. Before her, Subaru touched his forehead, casting his mind back. The name Echidna, the 'Witch of Greed.' It was a name that had brushed past Subaru's ears a few times before, even before he'd come to the 'Sanctuary.'
Among them, the very first time he'd heard it was—

"Puck."

Within the loop centered on the royal capital, Subaru had been killed by that great spirit no fewer than three times. It was the most detestable and bitterest memory of all—the time he'd been killed as if toyed with, by an enormous Puck.
That time, in the conversation between the freezing Petelgeuse and Puck, her name had come flying out. Subaru's consciousness, in the throes of near-death, hadn't been clear at the time, and he hadn't been able to recall it until just now.

Catching the murmur of Subaru, who'd dug up that memory, Echidna raised her face.

"Puck...? Don't tell me, that's the cat spirit...?"

"—!? Yeah, that's right. The cat spirit. Puck. You know him?"

"Know him, you say... is he here? If so, just how much has he remembered?"

Surprised at the unexpected name coming up, Echidna's reaction, in turn, caught Subaru off guard with its unpredictability. She rattled off her words in a rush, then fell abruptly silent.
As Subaru found himself unable to say anything before her intense, urgent manner, she closed her eyes again as if in deep thought, pondering.

How should he pick up the thread, Subaru wondered, lifting his gaze—

"—Ugh, ah!?"

Suddenly, his attention was seized by something hot asserting its presence deep in his belly.

"...O, ah?"

A sensation like his stomach being scorched by a tremendous heat. Groaning, Subaru pressed a hand to his abdomen, his legs wandering unsteadily on the spot.
The pain that had suddenly welled up was nothing ordinary. Some inexplicable agony beyond any comparison to a mere stomachache had drool trailing from the corner of his mouth. Unable to stay standing, he dropped to his knees on the spot, and immediately toppled sideways.

Looking down at Subaru like that—

"Ah, it seems to have finally taken effect."

—was Echidna, her eyes now carrying a cold light.
She walked slowly toward the writhing Subaru, bent her knees, and brought her face close to him as he lay collapsed, brushing the bangs from his forehead as his mouth worked wordlessly.

"When invited to a witch's tea party, one shouldn't carelessly put whatever's offered into one's mouth. —I daresay you've learned something."

"You... don't tell me... poison, or...?"

"That would indeed be quite something, wouldn't it. I told you, didn't I? What you drank was my bodily fluid. Fundamentally different in existence from something like a human—part of a witch. That's what you took into yourself."

At the word "bodily fluid" and the exchange that followed, Subaru finally grasped just how lightly he'd treated the matter. The result of which was this present state, writhing in agony.
Eyes wide, Subaru glared at Echidna. Wherever that friendly manner from before had gone. Just what was her aim in doing something like this—

"I don't want you to misunderstand—I'm doing none of this out of hostility or malice toward you. If anything, I regard your existence quite favorably. That's precisely why I had you drink part of me."

"S-speak... so I can... understand..."

"To explain it simply: I've made an adjustment so the Witch Factor sleeping within you settles in more easily... something like that."

"Witch... Factor...?"

Still scorched by the unending heat, Subaru repeated the word desperately.

—Witch Factor.
That word, too, was one he'd heard several times before.
Once from Petelgeuse's lips. And another time, from Beatrice's.

"You killed an apostle of the 'Witch of Envy,' didn't you? At that apostle's death, a Witch Factor slipped its way into you. ...Though it seems there's something else entirely different lurking within you as well."

"That... settling in... does what?"

"Now, what will happen, I wonder? To be honest, even I don't know. But think of it as a kind of consideration—setting off a bomb whose fuse you can't predict, before the damage it could do grows too large. If it can be gotten out of the way here, in a dream, wouldn't that leave you more room to spare, not having it go off out there?"

Spoken so breezily, Subaru's vision flickered in and out with agony threatening to steal his consciousness away entirely. But even so, he slowly raised an arm and pointed at Echidna.

"Just occurred to me..."

"Hm?"

"That way you talk... it's just like Puck's. That cat spirit guy talks the exact same way, all laid back, not reading the room at all..."

Hearing Subaru's words, squeezed out through gritted teeth, Echidna blinked her eyes once in surprise.
And then, almost immediately, her face crumpled, and she burst into laughter, holding her stomach as if she'd just heard the most delightful joke.

"Ha! Hahaha! Oh, how wonderful, how truly wonderful! You're incredible. I mean that sincerely. Yes, yes, ah, ahahahaha! I see, so I'm like Puck. Yes, yes, I suppose that's true. Of course it would be. From his perspective, I was likely the only model he ever had to go by."

"What... are you—"

—talking about, he tried to continue, but Subaru couldn't get the rest out.
It wasn't that the agony scorching his entire body had stolen his consciousness away. The suffering that had seemed like it would never end had eased somewhat, its finish beginning to come into view.
But it seemed that, before he could overcome this pain—

"It seems our little rendezvous has come to an end."

Little by little, the outline of the world began to blur within Subaru's field of vision.
The lush blue sky, the grassy hill covered in green. The white table and chairs the two of them had sat around began to lose their form.

"You're the one ending it, so it's not gonna end unless you say so, is it..."

"The time on the real side has reached its limit. That 'Trial' you spoke of seems about to begin. Once it begins, all the graveyard's functions will be directed there. It won't have time to spare for a single ghost."

Saying this in a light tone, Echidna traced a finger along the forehead of the collapsed Subaru.
Unable to resist or react, Subaru merely lay there as she smiled down at him.

"Now then, it's time to return from the witch's tea party. Let's have you hand something over, shall we?"

"...I'll have you know, I'm a broke bum without a single coin to my name."

"Not money. The price will be... let's see. Keeping this space confidential, how about that? You've made a similar sort of Contract before, haven't you? Cheap, don't you think?"

There wasn't even time to ask what that meant.
She kept her finger to Subaru's forehead as she murmured something in a small voice. And as that touching fingertip took on warmth, that gentle heat spread through his entire body in an instant, granting Subaru an uncanny understanding.
The content of the Contract, and the one-sided pact that must not be violated—the binding between them.

"Doing whatever you... want...!"

"For a tale of the past, and the settling of a Witch Factor—I think, even by my own reckoning, that's cheap payment for these. And, since we're at it, allow me to add one more thing, as a bonus."

Laughing at the indignant Subaru, she conveyed yet another new warmth through her still-touching fingertip.
And the result of that warmth was—

"I grant you the qualification to undergo the graveyard's Trial."

"—!?"

"With this, you too can undergo the graveyard's nighttime Trial. Whether you take it is up to you. You could choose not to. But you've gained the option of taking the Trial in place of your precious girl. —What you do with it is your own choice."

The Collapse of the world had begun, the ground beneath his feet dissolving bit by bit into darkness.
This time, the end of the world truly did seem to be drawing near.

And in that world drawing to its close, Subaru lay sprawled, looking up at Echidna.
A girl who had forged an unwanted Contract, forced an unrequested price upon him, and smiled down at Subaru without so much as a hint of shame—ah, that was, without question—

"—You really are a witch, after all."

"—Ah, that I am. I'm a wicked mage, aren't I?"

With that customary exchange as their last words, Subaru's consciousness was cast out of the dream.
Falling, falling. Fading, fading.

Cast out of the dream, rising up.
And then, and then Subaru's consciousness—was set free from the witch's dream.
