Arc 8 Chapter 39: "The Laughing Anomaly"

—Cecilus Segmunt is a "Stargazer."

That is not the "Stargazer" as an official post within the Vollachia Empire — the one Vincent Vollachia first came to rely on when he took Ubilk, one of their number, into his service — but the term in its original sense: a being who has been granted a Heavenly Mandate, and who casts aside all else in order to fulfill that supreme commandment.

The compulsion exerted upon those who become "Stargazers" and receive a Heavenly Mandate is powerful.

It gave a mere male prostitute enough influence to move freely in and out of the castle and speak his mind to the Emperor; it could make a man forget his love for the daughter his frail mother had risked her life to bear; it possessed power enough over a life to make someone readily let go of a purpose he had devoted his whole existence to — one for which he had even considered ending his own life.

Most "Stargazers" have the lives they'd led up to that point twisted out of shape by their Heavenly Mandate, forced into a fundamental change of course. And yet they do not consider this a tragedy.

No matter how abnormal or pitiable it might look to the people around them, that is simply how it is.

Among such "Stargazers," Rowan Segmunt was the sole exception.

The Heavenly Mandate he had received overlapped with the great ambition he had already been pursuing, so that — whatever he himself might have felt — to those around him it never looked as though his way of life had been twisted out of shape.

How he reconciled his own long-cherished wish with the Heavenly Mandate he was granted, and how he arrived at the code of conduct he holds today, has already been told elsewhere, so it will be omitted here.

What should be told here is this: that Cecilus Segmunt, who was born into this world as the culmination of the Heavenly Mandate granted to Rowan Segmunt, is himself also a "Stargazer" —

— and that, among all "Stargazers," Cecilus Segmunt is the sole anomaly, standing outside the rule entirely.


△▼△▼△▼△


With the wind gusting and howling, whipping the hem of his hakama about, Cecilus stood shielding his eyes with a hand-made visor, gazing out at the distant scenery.

Al's proposed operation: bringing down the five points of the star-shaped wall encircling the capital.

To let reinforcements in from outside, they needed to punch holes in the Undead's solid defenses. For Cecilus, more actors to make the stage shine and more spectators to witness his own exploits were more than welcome.

And if he was told this was also foreshadowing for some exciting development down the road, well, diligently grinding through the unglamorous legwork wasn't so bad either.

"That said! Don't forget that's only because a proper reward is promised later on!"

In theater as in war, there is a right person for every role.

Wherever you go in this world, everywhere is a stage — but launching into Cecilus's philosophy would take forever, so suffice it to say: people have things they're suited for and things they're not, and when it comes to fine, careful work or patient, steady accumulation, Cecilus is, if anything, a case of severe miscasting.

Taking the star performer — the very heart of the stage who draws the audience's eye — and draping him in a stagehand's black cloak to make him do grunt work is, as far as stage direction goes, about as backwards as it gets.

"I'm sure you in the audience agree on that point, don't you?"

Tilting his head back to look up at the sky, Cecilus spoke to an unseen listener.

Naturally, under normal circumstances, no matter how one carried on like that, a sky hung with black clouds could never answer a person standing on the ground. — Yes, under normal circumstances.

But Cecilus was no ordinary man — he was this world's star performer.

— A voice from the audience, one no one else could hear, answered back to Cecilus and Cecilus alone.

It was a voice loud like a whisper, flippant to the point of being far too solemn, divine as though it had never known what manners were — a voice that spoke to Cecilus.

And what's more—

『■■■■』『■●■●■●■』『――■■』『●●●●●！！』『■■■■●●■■』『●●■■●■■●●』『■■！！』『●●●■■■』『■■■●■●●■■●●』『●●■●■●●●■■●――』『●●……■』『●■●■●■●■』

With an almost excessive force and volume, it poured down upon Cecilus.

An answer from an audience driven wild with enthusiasm by the star performer Cecilus's address to them, desperately called back — no, that's not it. This wasn't, in fact, an answer prepared in response to what Cecilus had just said.

These were voices that rained down on Cecilus's every single move, on every act he performed.

For Cecilus, it was something he had gone on hearing endlessly for as long as he could remember, something that tried to force every one of his words and deeds this way and that — and—

"Ha ha ha, quite the excitement today, how delightful for you all! I understand, I understand. After all, every last twitch of mine is enough to enchant someone without fail! There's more to come where you won't be able to tear your eyes from my exploits for even a second, so look forward to it!"

To Cecilus, who never lent an ear to a word of what was said to him no matter what, it was simply the passionate cheering of his fans.

— As stated before, Cecilus Segmunt is a "Stargazer."

And a "Stargazer" is one whose way of life is warped in service of fulfilling the Heavenly Mandate bestowed upon them — true of the vast majority of them, no, even true of Rowan Segmunt, who stood in the position of an exception.

The Heavenly Mandate carries that much compulsion, and a "Stargazer's" life is ruled by it.

Excepting, alone, Cecilus Segmunt, the anomalous "Stargazer" who stands outside it entirely.

"Much obliged, thank you all for your cheers. As ever I haven't the faintest idea what you're saying and I've no intention of listening, but rest assured! Defy expectations! Answer expectations! That's the way I, the star performer, choose to live!"

"—Oi, you damn fool. Quit making a racket, it'll be a hell of a pain if we're spotted."

"Oops, oops."

As usual, having taken in only the audience's fervor and let the rest pass him by, Cecilus spun around at the voice calling out from behind him — the bearded Dog Human, Groovy.

Groovy stood with a hand on his hip, his gaze wary, and Cecilus found himself quite taken with it.

This beastman warrior, not so different in height from himself — Groovy was really quite good.

For one thing, he had charm and flair in his looks. His skill, too, was likely among the top five of everything Cecilus had seen since leaving the remote island. There was a promise about him, a sense that his martial prowess couldn't be measured by sword skill alone, that he'd fight in some interesting way.

"The one regrettable point would be his manner of speech, perhaps — a bit lacking. Reel off too much foul language and your dignity drops! And once it drops, even a man skilled with his blade can't maintain his standing. If we're to walk the road of cutting and being cut together, I'd like my partner to bring a standing to match!"

"Don't go spouting damn nonsense, you damn fool! And in the first place, who the hell's gonna cut and be cut with you! That bastard Chisha, pulling some damn unnecessary stunt..."

"Well, well, that's a name I just caught. Your cold reply has me curious enough as it is, but what could that person, coming up in conversation here, possibly have to do with me?"

"It's got everything to do with you, seeing as you shrank and forgot. — Because the one who shrank you was that damn egghead Chisha."

At Groovy's answer, Cecilus let out a low "hmmm" from his throat.

He'd been told this shrinking business over and over, but it still didn't click for him at all. Shrunk or not, as far as Cecilus was concerned, the self standing right here was the whole of him.

Of course, he wasn't entirely unmoved by the persuasive weight of things like Rowan having aged in the short time they'd been apart, or the fact that the moniker "Blue Lightning" — which was supposed to shake the world sometime in the future — was already common knowledge.

"It's just that the idea of this Chisha person shrinking me strikes me as awfully fishy."

"Hah? You gonna spout some damn nonsense about how a person shrinking is impossible?"

"No, no, not at all. Human imagination knows no limits, and what you can picture, you can make real. That kind of fairness is one of the reasons I'm so fond of this world as a stage, so sure, a person shrinking might well be one of those mystifying wonders that happen from time to time. What I find hard to believe is something perfectly simple and clear. — Who could possibly find an opening in me?"

"――――"

At Cecilus's question, hands laced behind his head, Groovy's face turned solemn.

Even the way his muzzle wrinkled in that expression had a certain charm to it — the kind of lively presence you'd want at least one of on any given stage — but it seemed even Groovy felt the weight of Cecilus's question.

Not that Cecilus thought himself invincible or immortal, either.

Gouge out his heart, or sever this slender neck, or drain away close to half the blood flowing through this body, and he would lose his life. People die.

But setting that aside, who could possibly lay a hand to such a feat?

On that one point, surely even Groovy would notice the contradiction in his own thinking. No matter who the opponent might be, no one could possibly shrink Cecilus —

"—Either that Chisha bastard racked his brains for it, or you let your damn guard down."

"Whaaat!? You look at me and that's the opinion you still come up with!?"

"It comes up. Comes right the hell up."

"You're saying it's only natural! Ahaha, that's crude, but you got me there!"

Cecilus clapped his hands and laughed at the way Groovy put it, then shook his head — no, no, wait.

What an assessment — the kind that made him want to bury his face in his hands. No, really — even a strong man like Groovy, who understood Cecilus's ability, couldn't quite shake that suspicion.

"So the bigger me really isn't much of a me after all! I'll never let myself become the sort of man who earns such a sorry state and such an assessment from the people around him! Just watch — ten years from now!"

"Who's gonna stick around ten damn years watching you grow up nice and proper! Quit your damn talk and get back to your original size already!"

"You say get back already, but I don't much want to go back... and besides, do you even know how, Groovy-san?"

"I don't, but that Chisha bastard who set it up probably does. These Shinobi tricks come with a damn time limit or a condition attached. If it's Chisha, it's probably a condition."

"Ho ho, so this Chisha fellow was a Shinobi, was he. Is he an acquaintance, or a friend, or maybe kin of that lively old man?"

"Don't know, and Chisha's not a Shinobi. It's just the trick that's Shinobi-style... ah, damn, this is a pain."

Groovy scratched at his head, grumbling like someone with all sorts of knowledge to share.

So he seemed to know a fair amount about all kinds of things, Cecilus judged — though what mattered more than the true identity of this Chisha fellow was probably clearing whatever condition he'd set up.

If he asked about that, Cecilus had a feeling Groovy would get angry and snap "how the hell should I know."

"So, what do you think the condition for me to go back is?"

"How the hell should I know!"

"See."

Cecilus crowed, delighted that it went just as he'd thought, and Groovy gave a sullen growl of "Hah?"

And then, just as the two of them were talking—

"Hate to interrupt when you two pals are having such a lively chat, but could you wrap up playtime for now?"

"Oh, Al-san."

The owner of that weary, exasperated voice was Al, who had come up to the rooftop where Cecilus and the others stood.

Appearing from the stairwell of the top floor, he tapped his foot against the steps, once, twice, and said—

"Come on, I mean it. When you go hopping up to the roof just to take a quick look around from up top, it's rough on me since I gotta take the stairs. You too, Groovy."

"Damn, my bad. Got dragged along by this damn fool's talk."

To Al, left waiting below, Groovy — brow furrowed — apologized honestly.

In the midst of the three of them moving together, Cecilus had climbed the building for a quick look because Groovy had said he was curious about what lay across the street, but he'd ended up letting his attention wander a bit too far.

In any case—

"Now, now, Al-san, don't be so cross. Groovy-san has his ears drooping in remorse and all, surely you're starting to feel like forgiving him?"

"The fact that you're a thoroughly go-my-own-way kind of character isn't something I'm gonna get hung up on at this point. More importantly, did you find out anything from up there?"

"Let's see. — Point Three and Point Four, and Point Five while we're at it, look to have solid defenses. Given the direction the boss and his people probably retreated in, it seems like a natural precaution."

"Those three spots, huh..."

With his one remaining right hand, Al fingered the fittings of his helmet, mulling over Cecilus's answer.

The numbers Cecilus had rattled off were the names given to the points of the star-shaped wall encircling the capital.

With the glittering castle set at the northern end, the entrances to this capital were numbered like so: the southernmost point was One, the southeast Two, the southwest Three, then the northeast Four and the northwest Five.

The three points corresponding to the north and west sides had defenses solid enough to call ironclad, which, conversely, meant the remaining two spots could be called prime targets — but—

"A hole that easy to spot on purpose — that stinks."

"Can your nose smell that kind of scheming too, Groovy-san?"

"It ain't built that damn ridiculous! It's just a judgment call as a 'general'! And don't you go making excuses about shrinking either... ah, no, never mind, that's pointless."

"What. Cutting yourself off partway just makes me curious."

"Point is, this damn fool never did a general's job even before he shrank."

At Groovy's words, delivered with a sour face, Cecilus and Al both went "ahh" in understanding.

To Cecilus, it didn't even feel like an insult — just a plain fact. Even supposing he really had shrunk as the result of dropping his guard, he couldn't imagine his pre-shrinking self ever leading anyone or standing at their side.

So he had no particular objection to what Groovy was saying.

"Still, I can nod along to the opinion that it's too obvious. But then, that makes this rather interesting, doesn't it. Unexpected, or rather, surprisingly enough, aren't you quite the schemer yourself, Al-san?"

"...What do you mean by that?"

"Well, Points One and Two look easy to bring down, but they're bait laid out as a trap... and you sent my father and his friend there, just the two of them. That makes you a schemer."

"――――"

Bringing up the two who weren't present, Rowan and Heinkel, off on their own, Cecilus smiled over at Al, who had proposed splitting them into groups.

Faced with Cecilus's smile, Al quietly held his breath and fell silent.

At present, the five of them had split into a team of three and a team of two, each carrying out an operation to bring down the five points.

And it was Al who had assigned Rowan and Heinkel to take Points One and Two — south and southeast — and his intentions were plain.

"If it's a relationship and a fighting strength you wouldn't mind losing much, you use them as expendable pieces to draw the enemy's eye. You might be modest about it, Al-san, but you really are a schemer!"

"...You got a complaint about how I'm using your old man?"

"No? As a performer I'm grateful it's better than all five of us clumping together and cluttering up the highlights, and as tactics go, isn't it a thoroughly sound way of thinking? It's a bit much coming from me, but there's no point expecting cooperation out of my father anyway!"

"Yeah, that's definitely a case of look who's talking."

As for cooperativeness, it could only be chalked up as an inborn flaw shared by father and son Segmunt.

In any case, Al had racked his brains to make effective use of their limited forces. If one had a head for thinking or working tactical instincts, one could probably guess what role was being asked of him.

"We can't afford the damn luxury of five of us bringing down one point at a time. If those two middle-aged guys, the red one and the blue one, can't figure that out themselves, then expendable pieces or not, they'll just have to see their damn job through."

"Yeah. Can't let them make one big commotion in one spot and draw all their numbers there."

"Hence the scattered attack... still, it was a bit unexpected. I mean, the way you talk about Heinkel-san, it sounded like you two were comrades."

"I'm no hero. I can set my priorities straight just fine."

There was something self-mocking about it, and Cecilus felt it wasn't really about how he was using Heinkel, but about how he used himself.

Not that this particular answer changed his impression of Al in any real way — but—

"—I see. So you're different from the boss."

"――――"

Al turned to face Cecilus, who had voiced the thought more or less on a whim.

Al's expression was hidden behind his iron helmet, but the sharpness and heat of a gaze, the feeling poured into it, can sometimes speak of a person's heart more eloquently than words or a look on the face.

"Well, that's only sometimes true, it doesn't necessarily hold now."

"...I don't know what you're on about, but fine. It's true, I'm not like my brother."

"Hm? Being different is only natural in itself, isn't it fine as it is? Personally, I think you're the tougher one, Al-san, more than the boss."

Since Al's tone had carried some nuance of himself being lesser, for whatever reason, Cecilus tilted his head at that.

Strong and weak, clean and dirty, liked and disliked — these are like the same moon waxing and waning. They're worth a moment's joy or sorrow, but they don't carry weight enough to tip the scales of a life.

Even right and wrong aren't absolute.

"The boss struggles for his life, clawing through the mud, and yet he still chooses the clean method, the one that shines on stage. You, on the other hand, Al-san, are different. You struggle for your life, clawing through the mud, and then on top of that you can take the filthy path too, the one that makes everyone want to look away. It's not a matter of better or worse. That's just the difference between the boss and you."

"――――"

"Ah, setting aside better-or-worse or differences, if we're talking about how one wants to be, that's another matter entirely. How you want to appear in others' eyes comes down to how far each individual takes it... defecation is a filthy thing, but I intend to make even that look beautiful!"

"Just now I was starting to reconsider my opinion of you a little...!"

"Sorry, Groovy-san's verbal habit just stuck in my ear a bit."

Though the way the topic went leaping about might have been Groovy's influence, there was no lie in the statement itself.

Attaching a reason compelling enough that you can't look away, even to a deed so filthy and unwatchable that people want to turn their faces from it — that, in Cecilus's own theory, is what makes a true star performer.

"So? You done with the damn rambling small talk? Life counseling's a load of crap. Once this is over, I'll hold you two a damn reflection session over your graves."

"...Guess if you get told that much, it'd stick in your ear too."

"Right?"

At Groovy's ceaseless stream of foul language, Al's shoulders slumped, and Cecilus laughed.

He'd gone on kneading out all sorts of theorizing he wasn't suited for, but as far as Cecilus was concerned, he had no objection to Al's plan, and he didn't mind if Rowan and Heinkel had a rough time of it as planned.

There was just one point where Cecilus thought, in his own way, Al's plan might run into trouble.

"Will that supremely self-centered father of mine actually move the way you want him to, Al-san, I wonder?"

That Cecilus, for once, didn't blurt out the thought was, itself unusually, because Cecilus had — again unusually for him — completely given up on his father Rowan's temperament.

Rowan never did what he didn't want to do, and always did what he wanted to, without fail.

It was a temperament Cecilus had inherited as well, but Rowan and Cecilus were different. It was the same thing he'd just said to Al about the boss, Schwartz.

It wasn't a matter of good or bad. It was a matter of how one lived and how one died.

"So, you damn helmet, where are you thinking of attacking from?"

"—. I want to avoid Area Three, where that sniper was earlier. So that leaves Four or Five on the north side... if the condition is even the slightest chance of it being thinner, then Four, the northeast."

"Ehh! But I want my revenge!"

"Don't spout damn nonsense when we've got no room to spare! Who d'you think you're dealing with—"

"Tch, fine, fine, I get it."

Yelled at by Groovy, Cecilus pouted and withdrew his opinion. At that reaction of Cecilus's, Groovy, who'd had his fist raised, widened his eyes in surprise.

When Cecilus asked "what is it?" at that, Groovy lowered his raised arm and said—

"You actually listen when you're told, huh. You were a bigger damn pain before you shrank than after."

"Ho ho, somehow that doesn't feel like a compliment?"

"Shut up! We're damn short on time. Let's get moving already."

Jerking his chin, Groovy turned his face toward the aforementioned Point Four. As Cecilus made to follow with a "yes, yes," Al, the one who'd proposed the plan, spoke up: "You sure?"

"I answered 'cause you asked, but you two... no, you probably know the capital better than I do."

"I ain't the type to nod along to some damn stupid proposal either. No complaints about your plan. ...And I don't wanna deal with that damn fool Balleroy myself."

"...Old comrades, huh."

"That's part of it, and also 'cause he's damn strong. If someone's gotta go up against him, it's Moguro. He won't lose, guaranteed."

Strong — that was how Groovy flatly declared the sniper's skill.

He was an opponent Cecilus himself had crossed paths with several times in this capital, yet had still never properly laid eyes on. No doubt he was strong. Perhaps nodding along earlier really had been a mistake after all. If he whined about it even now, would they agree to a change of plans? ...No, probably not.

"Let's get it done quick. Since keeping the hole open isn't the goal, there's no sense putting off time either."

"Watch it. This damn fool's planning to finish this fast and go hunt down Balleroy."

"...Once the main act's had his turn on stage, be my guest."

"Calling someone else the main act and setting me aside — what arrogance!"

As Al shook his head slowly and let that out as if giving up, Cecilus raised his voice, unable to contain his indignation.

The route to Point Four went so smoothly it was almost anticlimactic.

It wasn't that they saw no Undead searching for survivors along the way. But an opponent who blocked their path left only one option — elimination — while one who didn't was nothing more than a passing extra.

What actor would go out of his way to hand a spotlight moment to a bit player meant only to liven up the stage?

"Unfortunately, the audience's interest is finite. I won't say there's no such thing as a supporting actor whose fine performance lifts the whole stage's quality, but whether that's what's called for is another matter entirely... that said—"

Even as he savored the peaceable journey, Cecilus knit his brow.

Of course, he was all for cutting unnecessary trouble, and Cecilus didn't think a program that spent time and effort dispatching rank-and-file soldiers was desirable either.

Still, wasn't the way they looked right now more than a little unbecoming?

After all, Cecilus was riding piggyback on Al's back, while Groovy clung to his front — in other words, Cecilus and Groovy had Al sandwiched between them.

The reason for this state of affairs was that, without it, the two of them would have stuck out beyond the range of the cloak Al wore pulled over his head.

"Even so, this looks terrible! This isn't how I market myself at all, Groovy-san!"

"Shut it! Even if it hides our presence, it ain't erasing sound or smell! It'll be a damn pain if the bastards around us notice and surround us!"

"You're both too damn loud...! And quit squirming, I'll trip...!"

When Cecilus shook Al's shoulders in a fit of pure discontent, the two of them, wrapped together in the same cloak, scolded him for it.

This fur cloak Groovy had prepared was quite the remarkable thing. From what he'd heard, wearing it kept you from drawing attention from those around you — an excellent piece of gear. Rowan, Heinkel, and Groovy, the three of them, had apparently used this fur cloak to slip past the enemy's eyes and enter the capital.

In fact, even accounting for their choice of inconspicuous routes, the fur cloak's effect was surely a large part of why the three of them hadn't once been spotted by the Undead.

"With the difference in our builds, it's already this stifling just for us — I can't even imagine how you three managed it with my father and the others. Anyway, where did you buy such a bizarre cloak?"

"Didn't buy it. I made it. You're too damn thick to remember, but that 'Cursed Sword' of yours — I melted that down and forged it back into a katana myself."

"Ho ho, a 'Cursed Sword'! What a cool name! I'd love to see it, learn about it, lay eyes on it!"

"That's what I'm saying, it's your damn katana!"

Groovy pulled off the impressive feat of shouting in a quiet voice.

Setting the shrunk-or-not debate aside, listening to Groovy tell him things about himself he didn't know was quite interesting. Especially the talk about the sword — that was a favorite topic of his.

"Still, forging it back into a katana — how interesting. Are you a swordsmith, Groovy-san? Playing 'general' and smith both, you must be a busy man."

"Not a katana specialist exactly. I forge swords, weave cloth, tinker with magic tools too."

"Which means this strange cloak here is a kind of magic tool too, is it?"

"Nah, this one's just a Werewolf's hide."

At Groovy's answer, Cecilus made an impressed "hoh," while Al gave a startled shudder of the shoulders. Cecilus scrutinized the look of the fur cloak closely, and said—

"A Werewolf — how rare. I know they exist, but I've never had the chance to lay eyes on one."

"They're a damn hard bunch to even find in the first place. Like Earth Dragons, or the Uruha People of the Holy Kingdom, the whole race has a Divine Protection on it. Skin 'em alive and the hide keeps the protection lodged in it. Good use for those damn bastards who're so good at ambushing."

"The Divine Protection of the Concealing Cloud, huh..."

"Hah? Now that's a damn old-fashioned name to know. 'Divine Protection of Ambushing' is the more common one... though talk of damn Werewolves ain't exactly common to begin with."

Catching what Al had muttered under his breath, Groovy showed off that bit of knowledge.

In any case, Werewolves were despised in the Empire, a sentiment rooted in a very, very old tale. Though, since the tale was based on historical fact, it was deeply linked to reality as well.

Cecilus himself held no particular hostility toward Werewolves or Wolf Humans — but—

"Being saved by this very Werewolf hide, given how the Empire treats Werewolves — my, what an ironic story. Skin-deep irony, you might say."

"――――"

"Ehh? Ignored? Am I being ignored? I thought that was a fairly good line—"

Just as Cecilus thought Al and Groovy had both given a cold response to his touching on the matter of the fur cloak — he sensed a moment later that this wasn't it at all.

『――■■■■■■』

Not because the peanut gallery, as usual, had gotten rowdy.

It was because Al's whole body, pressed right up against Cecilus, had gone tense, and Cecilus felt the reason for that tension slip into him as well.

It was something that had, setting aside the shrunk-or-not debate, taken hold within Cecilus's slight chest.

— Gray thorns were coiling in a spiral around Cecilus's chest.

"By any chance, is this showing up for you two as well?"

"...Yeah. Oi, Groovy-san. Weren't we supposed to be safe from getting spotted with this cloak..."

"Damn it...! Some bastard's pulling a damn stupid stunt...!"

Al and Groovy both seemed to be in the same state as Cecilus, and Groovy in particular — the way he lost his composure, or rather the sheer scale of his anger — was considerable.

Was it because the effect of his prized fur cloak had been seen through? — No, that didn't add up.

If their opponent had pinpointed Cecilus and the others' location, they wouldn't leave the three of them, sandwiched together as they were, alone for long. Of course, there was also the possibility that the thorns were meant as a warning.

"Doesn't seem like it. What's the reasoning?"

"—It's an indiscriminate area attack. What damn fool is it!? Whoever's flinging around such an absurd bit of Curse Arts like this...!"

Groovy's throat shook with raw feeling as he cursed the sheer unreasonableness of the still-unseen master of the thorns.

In that instant, the thorns at all three of their chests began slowly writhing, sharp barbs slipping past their hands as the curse made to unleash its effect without reservation.

And then—

"—So the world really won't stand for me skulking about after all, is that it. Well then, a fresh hardship has arrived! Let's make a grand, showy leap right over it!"

That was the exhilarated cry the laughing anomaly let slip, an instant before searing pain came to grip his heart.
