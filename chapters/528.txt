Arc 7 Chapter 30: "Self-Proclaimed Hero Natsuki Subaru"

Mizelda's expression, as she declared she would step down as chief, was resolute.

The wild, striking beauty of her face, its strength of gaze undimmed, hadn't shifted a hair's breadth from the impression she'd made the very first time Subaru had laid eyes on her. Through and through, she was exactly what she'd always seemed: a strong woman.

True to that impression, Mizelda had lost her right leg, and decided to step down from the chief's seat.

"​—​—​—​—​"

It was the people around Mizelda whose faces were steeped in undisguised regret.
The People of Shudrak, who had watched their chief's strength up close for so long — their frustration and sense of loss weren't things that could be brushed away so easily.

Kuna's habitual bluntness had hardened into something even more expressionless, and even Holly, who never let anything disturb her easygoing pace, wore a downcast look. Utakata, eyes brimming with tears, bit her lip and hung her head, and the other Shudrak wore uniformly grim expressions.
But of them all, the one most visibly shaken was—

"S-sister, I can't. There's no way someone like me could be chief..."

"—Taritta."

"It's a role only you could fill! I don't have that kind of vessel in me...!"

Shaking her head fiercely, Taritta pleaded with desperate urgency.
The younger sister, personally named by Mizelda as the next chief — from the way she spoke and carried herself day to day, it was easy to imagine she revered her sister, held her in something close to worship.
Precisely because of that, Taritta couldn't come to terms with the fact that Mizelda had lost her leg, and was thrown into utter disarray. If anything, her distress seemed to outstrip even Mizelda's own, the very person concerned.

"...It's my own lack of ability."

To Taritta's anguished appeal, a weak, self-reproaching voice answered.
It came from the edge of the floor, crowded with the wounded — Rem, haggard, standing with the aid of a cane.

The only one present who could use healing magic, Rem had been treasured for it, running herself ragged back and forth to save as many of the injured as she could. Just how brutal that ordeal had been was plain from her blood-stained clothes, her disheveled hair, and the exhaustion written on her face.
Though, of course, she herself refused to admit it, meeting even Subaru's first words of concern with nothing but a flat "I'm fine."

Even so, no one had the right to blame Rem for any lack of ability.
Without her, there was no way they could have come through Arakiya's assault without a single death. Everyone there understood that.
And so—

"Rem, there is no need for you to trouble yourself. That I got off with only one leg, fighting an Imperial Sword, was good fortune. ...No — it was thanks to your help."

"Mizelda-san... but—"

"I am grateful to you. There is nothing more to say."

Thanked once more, and by Mizelda of all people, Rem had nothing left to say.
Beside the silent Rem, Rui, her long golden hair in disarray, pressed close. Rem laid a hand on the shoulder of Rui, who was quietly pinching the hem of her clothes, and lowered her eyes without a word.
The self-reproach and helplessness Rem was feeling — Subaru understood it painfully well. But before Subaru could say anything, the black-haired, handsome figure stepped forward first.

"Mizelda. You have no intention of reconsidering."

At Abel's question as he stepped forward, Mizelda, seated on a makeshift chair, nodded.
Touching a finger to the bandaged stump of her severed leg, she said,

"No, I've no intention of changing my mind. The covenant between the Shudrak and the Vollachian Emperor will be upheld. As for what comes after — ask not me, but Taritta... no, ask the chief."

"—I understand. Mizelda. Well done."

"Heh."

Her mind made up to retire, Abel, arms folded, acknowledged Mizelda's answer with understanding. At that, Mizelda let out a small breath and her stern expression eased.
A faint smile rising to her lips, she looked up at Abel and said,

"Since you're at it, why not smile properly. That's the duty of a handsome face."

"—Hmph."

"That'll do."

Even right after losing her leg, Mizelda was formidable beyond words.
Her bearing was such that even the arrogant, imperious Abel didn't reproach her insolence, and instead let himself smile.
It was proof, if any were needed, that the emperor stripped of his throne had shown respect to the woman warrior who lived in the forest.
And then—

"—Hear me, my kin!"

Her smile hardening back into resolve, Mizelda lifted her head and raised her voice.
At that fierce tone, the Shudrak all at once straightened, turning to listen.

"As I said before, I cannot fulfill the duties of chief! Therefore, I pass the role of chief to my sister, Taritta! All of you, follow Taritta!"

"—​—​—​—​"

"This is my last command as chief. —Gratitude to the oath of our forebears, and to the pride of our ancestral spirits."

"—Gratitude!"

At Mizelda's closing words, the Shudrak echoed the phrase back as one.
Subaru had no knowledge of their customs, the traditions built up over the years. But even as an outsider to all this, he could sense, on instinct, that this was a rite of succession. Short, unceremonious, a blend of the practical and the symbolic.

And so, here, the chieftainship of the People of Shudrak passed from Mizelda to Taritta.

"Sister..."

"Don't wear such a dark face, chief. Your doubt is our doubt. Your hesitation is our hesitation. Your death shall be our death."

"—​—​—​—​"

Mizelda stepped closer, encouraging the new chief, whose expression remained clouded.
It wasn't enough to ease Taritta's feelings all at once. But perhaps Taritta, too, understood that clinging to the past would change nothing now.
After a long silence, Taritta, still wordless, gave a hesitant nod.

"—​—​—​—​"

The complicated emotion that flickered across Mizelda's eyes at that sight was something the bowed-headed Taritta never saw. —And it was a sanctuary that no one but Mizelda herself had any right to speak of.

"...Honestly, that surprised me."

Once the changing of the chieftainship and the damage report had concluded, Subaru spoke up to Abel.
Called to a halt and handed an impression with no clear subject, Abel frowned, and with obvious displeasure demanded, "What did," pressing curtly for Subaru's meaning.

"There's much that needs thought. Don't add to my burdens, you as well."

"Don't have to be so sharp about it every time. ...It's just, it surprised me. That you accepted Mizelda-san's withdrawal from the front lines so readily."

"—​—​—​—​"

"I figured, losing one leg — so what. That you'd say something like, work until you die, for my sake."

Even thinking it an extreme opinion, Subaru conveyed his honest feelings to Abel.
This was, after all, the emperor who'd taken the People of Shudrak into his service to reclaim his throne, who'd even considered, in the worst case, poisoning the fortress city of Guaral.
Abel — no, Vincent Vollachia — surely wouldn't shy from that kind of attitude.

"Fool. What possible meaning would there be in forcing such a thing?"

But to Subaru, who'd braced himself for a tongue-lashing in speaking his mind, Abel's answer was composed.
Before a nonplussed Subaru, Abel gazed at the Shudrak conversing in the distance and said,

"To begin with, I ask nothing more of a subordinate than what they are capable of. To order one to do one's best, and then demand they exceed their own limits, would be nothing short of raving nonsense."

"—​—​—​—​"

"Work beyond what I've assessed only throws off my calculations. I demand of my subordinates neither more nor less. And Mizelda fulfilled her own domain of duty. That being so, all I owe her is reward."

Spur them on with words, coax more than their ability out of them with promised rewards, secure the next task with praise.
Subaru had simply assumed that was how those in power commanded their subordinates. So Abel's answer stood in direct opposition to what Subaru had pictured.
Not demanding of a subordinate more than their true ability.
That seemed, in a way, like an environment easy to work under — and yet, at the same time, a lonely one.

"Naturally, work unworthy of praise is answered with punishment. Reward the deserving, punish the guilty — you understand the meaning."

"...Does that mean you'd punish me?"

"If you were my subordinate, yes. But are you my subordinate?"

Met with a direct, unflinching gaze, Subaru's eyes went wide at Abel's words.
Of course, in his own heart, Subaru had never once considered himself to have become Abel's subordinate. In the discussion with Priscilla, Abel had treated Subaru as something like a strategist, but Subaru had no intention of accepting that title either.

"Not that I'm not drawn to the title of strategist, but being your subordinate — no thanks."

"Just so. You are not my subordinate. Therefore, you do not fall within the scope of reward and punishment."

"Thinking about it that way, what exactly are you to me..."

As things stood, Subaru had ended up forced into Abel's company, but originally there was no master-servant relationship between them, nor any bond with more warmth to it than that.
They'd simply become bound by fate through circumstance — a relationship where, once the trouble was resolved, their paths would part.
Not allies, not comrades, not brothers-in-arms, either. If anything had to be said, they were partners in cross-dressing.

"Someone once took it upon themselves to call me a friend, but I doubt you'd be so bold."

"Yeah. I'm shy around new people, so I don't make friends that easily."

Given the current situation, anyone calling him a friend here would have to be either an extraordinarily good person or a con artist. Flop just happened to be the former.
And then—

"I've more to discuss with Priscilla. See to your own domain."

"My own domain..."

"You need not be told, surely."

Cut by that narrow, sharp gaze, Subaru's own eyes slid off Abel, toward the corner of the room.
There, Rem sat slumped on the floor. He couldn't make out her downcast expression from this distance, but there was no mistaking that she couldn't simply be left alone.
Though it grated a little, being told as much by Abel first.

"If you and Priscilla start fighting and the battle starts back up, count me out. Watch your mouth."

"Most would advise that it's you, not I, who ought to mind such words."

Trading insult for insult, Subaru parted from Abel, who returned to the meeting hall.
With Mizelda's leg lost, the shape of the People of Shudrak would change, and that fact would have to be factored into the coming talks with Priscilla.
Still, there was little room for Subaru to step into a conversation between two people so far above him as Abel and Priscilla.
What mattered more was a conversation only Subaru could have.

"—Rem, got a minute?"

Taking a light breath to settle himself, Subaru made his way to Rem.
Sitting with her knees folded, back against the wall, Rem stirred slightly at his words, her hazy pale-blue eyes reflecting his approaching figure.

"...Oh, it's you. When exactly are you going to change your clothes?"

"Rem comes before changing. I'll change the second this conversation's over."

"I see. Then the conversation's over. Please go change."

"That's way too abrupt!"

Rebuffed with nowhere to plant his feet, Subaru raised his voice at Rem's curt treatment. At that, Rem's eyes sharpened and she said, "Please be quiet."
Then, with her chin, she gestured toward the girl leaning against her left shoulder.

"You'll wake Rui-chan, who's sleeping. Please be considerate."

"That's..."

"Or is even that much consideration something you don't want to give this child?"

"Don't put it like that. My bad."

Rui, breathing softly in her sleep, nestled against Rem.
Rui, whose white clothes were stained with blood in several places, had apparently been helping Rem as she made her rounds treating the wounded. From Rem's manner and what Subaru had heard from Utakata, clumsy as she was, Rui had properly done as told.
Naturally, Subaru's feelings on the matter were complicated.

"Seeing that severe look on a face like that throws me off."

"Ah, yeah, sorry. My makeup's a mess too, huh. Must look pretty ugly."

"It was already unsightly back when your makeup was properly done."

"Ohh, the pain..."

At Rem's characteristically biting remark, Subaru let his shoulders slump in defeat. Then he slowly lowered himself down on Rem's right side, opposite Rui, and sat beside her.
A brief protesting glance flew from Rem, but he deliberately ignored it.

"Rem, you did really well. Everyone's alive thanks to you."

"...I felt keenly how lacking I was. Honestly, I feel useless."

"Rem..."

In response to his words of thanks, Rem looked down at her own two hands with frustration. Gazing at her white fingers, she bit her lip weakly.

"You're not useless at all. Even with your memories still uncertain, you managed to properly use healing magic and saved so many people. And yet..."

"I understand it. That this magic, truly, shouldn't be like this."

"What do you mean, shouldn't be like this?"

"Healing magic. The magic I use now is intuitive — in other words, self-taught. Rui-chan helps support it, and somehow it takes shape, but..."

Rem's words trailed off there.
They trailed off because what came next was something that didn't need saying, and because she knew that speaking it would become a blade to wound herself, and that the pain, however slight, would also console her.

Weak words, spoken aloud, wound both oneself and others. In exchange, the weight pressing on one's chest lightens, if only a little. And Rem was averse to that lightening.
It was proof that she truly blamed her own lack of ability for failing to save someone.

"—​—​—​—​"

Subaru understood Rem's feelings there all too painfully, once again.
Regret over what more one might have done tormented oneself far worse than a wall one simply couldn't reach, no matter how one struggled. All the more so when it concerned someone else's future — someone who wasn't oneself.

—The matter of Mizelda's lost leg had left something heavy in the depths of Subaru's chest as well.

It wasn't a life. But the weight of losing a limb wasn't hard to imagine.
Most recently, there was Al, the one-armed man who'd lost his left arm. In the water gate city of Priestella, Ricardo had lost an arm in the midst of battle against "Gluttony."
It was a deeply shocking thing to happen, but it wasn't so distant a tragedy as to be unthinkable. If anything, for someone living a life of battle, it might be more accurate to call it a familiar one.
Even so—

"It's rough, huh."

"...Yes."

At Subaru's dry murmur, Rem beside him gave a small nod.
That unresisting, honest agreement was practically the first reaction of its kind from her — but unfortunately, Subaru wasn't in a state of mind to be glad of it. As Subaru sat there with a knot in his chest, Rem glanced over at him.

"The previous me... how would she have been, I wonder."

"...The Rem with her memories, you mean?"

"Yes. That me's healing magic, back then — Mizelda-san's leg..."

The words that should have followed — *could it have been saved* — hung unfinished, and Subaru shut his eyes at them.
It wasn't that he didn't understand Rem's feelings. It was simply that Subaru, an outsider to the matter, had no way to judge how much the presence or absence of her memories affected her healing magic.
Even if healing power could somehow be quantified, evaluating it would be beyond Subaru.
And yet—

"—​—​—​—​"

Rem stared at him, her pale-blue eyes holding an earnest, desperate feeling.
Whether the answer she sought was one Subaru could provide, he didn't know. What lay before Subaru was a choice between two answers only — that the Rem of before *could have* done it, or *couldn't have.*
Which of the two would be a salvation to Rem.

—No, rather, which answer would spare Rem from being hurt any further.

"...Even with your memories, I don't think it would have made a difference."

"—​—​—​—​"

"Healing magic isn't all-powerful either. Within that, Rem must have done her best."

After some seconds — though it felt far longer within Subaru's mind, in his agonizing — he gave that answer.
That even if Rem had had her memories, even if her healing magic had been in perfect condition, she still wouldn't have been able to leave Mizelda her leg.

It wasn't about what the facts actually were.
There was no end to playing the game of what-ifs. Certainly, Rem hadn't been able to save Mizelda's leg. But she had saved the lives of the many other wounded besides Mizelda.
That achievement deserved praise — there was no reason at all for her to blame herself.
If anything, if there was reason to place blame anywhere—

"I was the one lacking."

"Eh..."

"My thinking fell short. I should have weighed things more carefully, more seriously, in every way."

Hearing Subaru's answer spill out, Rem's round eyes went wide.
Before her, Subaru clenched his back teeth hard and covered his face with both hands.

If a lack of ability was to be cursed, that condemnation should rain down on Subaru above all.

"It's all my fault."

For all his high talk of a "Bloodless Conquest," reality had brought him a result far from that.
Arakiya's intrusion had produced a great many wounded, and then, in the wake of recovering her custody, the city's guards had suffered multiple deaths as well. No one from within their own ranks had been sacrificed, true — but looking at Mizelda's lost leg, how could he possibly still call it "bloodless"?

He'd failed. Failure piled on failure, chances to recover slipping past, failures accumulating.
What he'd sought was the best possible happy ending, and yet what lay before Subaru's eyes now was, at best, a middling happy ending — or perhaps something that should rather be called a middling bad ending.

Following Abel's words from earlier — reward the deserving, punish the guilty. He deserved punishment.
If Subaru had been Abel's subordinate, this wreckage was exactly the kind of thing that would get a man's head cut off without anyone batting an eye.

—In the worst case, even by Return by Death.

The thought crossed his mind, that far.
If it came to that, the most likely point he'd be dragged back to was the very middle of that bloody, brutal struggle against Todd in Guaral. —Just remembering it made every hair on his body stand on end with dread.
And yet, even if he went back to that moment of terror, if it meant he could pick up everything he'd let slip through his fingers—

"...Why?"

Suddenly, those words struck Subaru's eardrums as he sank into thought.
Lifting his head reflexively, he found himself caught head-on by Rem's staring eyes. Those eyes, which had been brimming with self-reproach only a moment before, were now, for some reason, fixed on Subaru with an even fiercer self-condemnation.

"Why does this become your fault?"

Subaru, shaken by Rem's gaze and frozen in place, found more words piled on top of the first.
Still with her eyes glistening, she reached out a hand, gesturing at the city hall turned into a scene of ruin.

"Mizelda-san's leg, Rui-chan and Utakata-chan's injuries, Medium-san and Flop-san's wounds — is all of that your fault too?"

"That's — yeah, it is. If I'd prepared more thoroughly, this wouldn't have happened."

"You devised a plan, and even though it looked reckless, you properly produced results with it. You pinned down both Imperial Swords and got everyone into the city without a single fight. Exactly as planned."

"But after that..."

"—What happened after that, no one could have—!"

At Subaru pressing his point, Rem's brows rose, her voice growing louder. Turning abruptly, the motion sent Rui, who'd been leaning against her, tumbling onto her lap.
Rui stirred faintly, gave a small groan, but didn't wake. Supporting Rui's shoulder, Rem, her breath slightly quickened, looked back at Subaru once more.

"No one could have foreseen what happened after. That half-naked woman appearing, going on that rampage — none of it could have been predicted. And yet—"

"—​—​—​—​"

"And yet, why do you bear the responsibility for all of it?"

Asked again, *why*, Subaru caught his breath.
If asked why, Subaru thought it was the responsibility of one who held power. Just as Rem lamented the insufficiency of her own healing magic, Subaru too sometimes regretted and lamented what his own Authority couldn't make up for. With Return by Death, the scope of that was far greater.

Because if Subaru exercised his power, the future could turn out better — or worse.
However—

"That's..."

—That was a truth he could never convey, even to Rem.

It wasn't limited to Rem.
The matter of the Authority Subaru held — this alone he couldn't tell to anyone, no matter how much he'd opened his heart to them. —No, rather, it was precisely to those he'd come to truly connect with that he couldn't speak of it.
A truth that, if spoken, might lead to that very person's death — how could he ever reveal such a thing?

Pain was frightening. The pain inflicted just by attempting to confess Return by Death was terrifying.
The agonizing pain of having one's heart gripped — no matter how many times anyone endured it, it was never something one grew used to.
But what was truly frightening wasn't the pain. It was loss.

Was there anything in this world more frightening than losing something?
Was it not precisely because it feared that, to the utmost extreme, that this Authority had been granted to Natsuki Subaru?

"...Why did you protect me?"

"Eh...?"

As if giving up on Subaru, who'd fallen silent with no answer to give, Rem continued.
For a moment, not understanding what she meant, Subaru blinked.

"When that half-naked woman attacked me. You knocked over the pillar, and even that didn't work... and when that woman came at me, you stood in front of me."

To save Rem from Arakiya's attack, Subaru had thrown himself in front of her without thinking.
Arms spread wide, desperate to keep any threat from reaching Rem. In that instant, even if it had cost him his life to Arakiya, Subaru wouldn't have minded.
He'd wanted Rem to outlive him, even by a single second.
That was—

"The outcome of proposing the Bloodless Conquest, protecting me back there, even Mizelda-san losing her leg — you're trying to shoulder every bit of it all by yourself..."

"—​—​—​—​"

"You're not a strong enough person to carry all of that. ...At first, I was wary of you, because of that awful stench."

There, Rem paused, and for a moment let her gaze fall to Rui on her lap.
Gently stroking that golden hair, Rem held her breath, then quietly turned her gaze back to Subaru.

"I have my own will, and so does Rui-chan, and Abel-san and Mizelda-san and the others, and Medium-san and Flop-san too — we're all people with our own wills. There's no need for you to work yourself up trying to protect us all."

"Ah..."

"Please don't try to carry it all through by yourself like that. The responsibility for what we do — there's no need for you to bear that."

Overwhelmed by the words piling down on him, Subaru's mouth opened and closed uselessly.
His brain refused to swiftly process what he was being told. All he knew was that some unidentifiable urgency was burning through his heart, warning him he mustn't hear any more than this.

Rem, pressing in on Subaru — who had cursed his own lack of ability — with an earnest, desperate feeling.
He mustn't let her say any more than this—

"You're not some special person—"

He'd known he mustn't let her say it.


"—Because you are not a hero."

Unsteadily, unsteadily, Subaru wandered through the city hall as if lost.
He had no destination in mind. In fact, he wasn't even sure when he'd started walking. He'd simply become aware he was walking, and even now his mind remained hazy, indistinct.

"—Ngh."

Suddenly, he collided head-on with something hard.
Looking up, it seemed he'd walked into a wall, having kept his eyes fixed only on his feet. Slamming his forehead against the featureless wall, Subaru pressed a hand to the throbbing spot and let out a breath.
Then, almost absently, he struck the same bruised forehead against that same hard wall a second time.

There was a hard impact, a dull sound, and Subaru felt a sharp ache run through his skull.
Rattling his brain like that from the outside, it felt as if something murky, something that had been seeping slowly through his whole body, was being driven back a little.

Following that same rhythm, Subaru struck his forehead against the wall again, and again, and again.
Strike, strike, strike—

"—Hey, hey, quit it, brother."

A hand caught his shoulder from behind just as he'd drawn it back for another blow, and a voice spoke.
Turning around, Subaru's gaze collided with a pair of eyes watching him through the visor of an iron helmet. Still gripping Subaru's shoulder, the man cracked the bones of his neck and said,

"I get wanting to die and all. But there's no end to it, no matter how many times you try that."
