Arc 7 Chapter 7: "It Was Tough Being a Man"

"Ow."

At the sharp pain that shot through his fingertip, Subaru couldn't help but frown.
Looking down, he saw a thin cut on the tip of his index finger, a bead of blood welling up from it. He'd been tidying the documents scattered across the desk, and apparently that was how he'd cut himself.

"Damn, I did it. Guess this is my baptism for underestimating this other world's papermaking technology..."

"Oh my, Subaru-kun, wha-a-at happened? Cut your finger on paper?"

"Yeah yeah, I know what you're gonna say—'weaker than pa-a-aper, how frail'—Roz-chi. I just messed up a little. ...Hey, can you patch this up real quick?"

Showing off his throbbing finger, Subaru asked Roswaal, the master of the room, just that.
Roswaal, who'd been at his desk doing paperwork in his own study, set down his quill and said, "Let's see, let's see," as he examined the cut on Subaru's finger.

"Oh my, that looks like it hu-u-urts. Still, a cut this small is nothing much. Lick it and it'll heal soon enough. Or would you like me to lick it for yo-o-ou?"

"No thanks, I'm good. If it's between you licking it and shoving it in Beako's mouth, I'll take the latter."

"Ha, now that's amu-u-using. ...Sounds like you and Beatrice are getting along well enough."

Releasing Subaru's finger, Roswaal narrowed his eyes with his elbows propped on the desk. Hearing his words, Subaru tilted his head. "You think?"
The relationship between Subaru and Beatrice was hard to put into words, complicated in its own way. Though saying it like that made it sound grander than it was—really, Beatrice just hadn't met him halfway much.

"She's fun to tease, so I keep needling her from my end, but I'm not too confident about how she actually feels about it."

"No no, no need to worry about tha-a-at. Beatrice is an honest girl, for all that. If she truly meant to reject you, she wouldn't even let you set foot in the Forbidden Archive."

"I see..."

"In fact, she won't let me in there at a-a-all, you know."

"...I see?"

Roswaal put a hand to his own chest as he made that boast, and Subaru gave a wry laugh.
Still, it seemed Roswaal, like Subaru, thought of Beatrice as a girl worth teasing. And his history with her was far longer than Subaru's, at that.

"So, what, did you misjudge the distance between you two, and it's been dragging on ever since? Shouldn't you apologize?"

"Well, you're not far off the mark. To apologize now... it's been just a little too lo-o-ong, for both me and Beatrice."

"Blaming the passage of time as an excuse not to apologize doesn't seem great either, though..."

"Hmm, that stings my ears all the more."

Loosening his lips into a small smile, Roswaal picked the quill back up. Taking it as a sign he was returning to work, Subaru shrugged and resumed tidying the bookshelves.
Fortunately, the bleeding from his cut finger seemed to have stopped.

"If that finger really hurts, have Rem or Beatrice heal it—or the Great Spirit, if not them."

"You're not gonna heal it for me, Roz-chi? Spartan much?"

"No no, that's not i-i-it. —The truth is, I can't use healing magic."

"Really? I figured you were some all-around mage who could do anything."

At Roswaal's unexpected confession, Subaru raised his eyebrows slightly.
Burning down a whole forest along with a pack of witchbeasts in raging flame, soaring into the sky like the wind—hearing he was a mage of that caliber, Subaru had simply assumed he'd mastered every basic form of magic.
But at Subaru's words, Roswaal shook his head.

"Sorry to disappoint, but magic depends heavily on one's inborn aptitude. It just so happens I was blessed with talent for many kinds of magic, but even so, that's not enough to master every form of magic that exists in this world."

"So there are others besides healing magic you can't use?"

"No, I can use pretty much everything except healing magic."

"Seriously, just the one gap!?"

Contrary to the weighty preamble, Roswaal's weakness turned out to be just the one.
Still, that single gap being the inability to use healing magic felt, surprisingly, like a rather hard hole to fill.

"I mean, whether or not you can heal your own wounds in the middle of a fight seems like a pretty big deal."

"Oh? So you're capable of thinking about things like that, Subaru-kun?"

"Well, I graduated elementary school past the phase of ignoring buffs and debuffs and just mashing the 'Fight' command to win through raw power. In power-system manga, I'm the type who wants the guy with no powers using wits to go toe-to-toe with the ability users."

"Sounds like you're saying something complicated, but I don't think it actually is."

It had only been a few weeks of acquaintance, but Roswaal seemed to have already seen through the truth behind Subaru's flippant remarks.
At that, Subaru scratched his head and stuck out his tongue, and Roswaal closed one eye, blinking the blue one at him, and said,

"Actually, you're quite right, Subaru-kun. Whether or not healing magic is available can drastically change a battle—a battlefield, in a sense. So it's a surprisingly precious talent."

"Really? But Puck, Beako, and Rem can all use it, right?"

"The first two are special ca-a-ases. As for Rem... it's partly her natural disposition, I'd say. I also put considerable effort into cultivating that talent of hers."

According to what Roswaal explained afterward, the usefulness of healing magic was regarded as especially important even among the various types of magic, to the point that a formal master-apprentice system existed at dedicated healing houses.
Rem could have studied there too, but since she'd been reluctant to leave Ram's side, Roswaal had taught her directly himself.

"So, if one becomes too thoroughly steeped in the existence of healing magic, there's a real fear of forgetting how to endure an injury when it actually happens, I supp-o-ose."

"Ah... you mean losing your tolerance for pain. Yeah, that could definitely be a thing."

Just as someone raised in a sterile room never builds resistance to outside viruses, if you always erased pain instantly with healing magic, it could turn fatal the moment you couldn't rely on it.
With that thought in mind, Subaru glanced at the cut on his finger and said,

"Got it. It's not a wound worth making a fuss over anyway—I'll bear through this pain as a lesson to myself."

"That's a bit much for just a little nick on your fingertip, don't you thi-i-ink? A wound that small won't make any difference whether it's healed or not—why not just have Rem fix it up?"

"A sweet trap luring men into laziness! ...No, no, this much is fine, fine. No problem."

Roswaal's remark had been made lightly, but it seemed to Subaru there was a point to it.
Subaru already knew from the two incidents at the royal capital and the mansion that this other-world life was no easy thing. There was no such thing as being too free of unbearable pain.
And yet—

"For when the unavoidable comes knocking, I'm gonna steel my resolve."

"It's just a cut fingertip, thou-u-ugh."

Roswaal gave a wry smile at Subaru, who declared this while clenching his raised index finger with feeling.
Though, in the end, all that spirited resolve of Subaru's went to waste—the moment he finished cleaning the study and rejoined Rem and the others, Rem noticed the cut and healed it on the spot.

"Ngyaaagh...!"

"Here, bite down on the splint or something. This'll hurt, this'll hurt."

So saying, Todd straightened out the fingers of Subaru's left hand, applying a sharp-smelling medicine to the three fingers that had turned reddish-purple. Then he swiftly set splints against the fingers, wound bandages around them to hold them in place, and finished the treatment.

"Last thing, this tonic. Should ease the pain a fair bit."

Todd handed a bottle of medicine to Subaru, who was dripping with cold sweat. The contents were a thick, viscous green liquid, but if it was medicine, there was nothing to be done about that.
Steeling himself, Subaru knocked it back in one go.

"Oorbleh! Gross! Th-this heavy liquid is clinging to my throat...!"

"It's famous for being hard to swallow, that one. But I guarantee the effect's top-notch. It's a precious item, highly valued even in the army. Speeds up wound healing."

Todd laughed, pinching the now-empty bottle between his fingers after Subaru had drained it.
Wiping his mouth, Subaru bowed his head at Todd's words. "Sorry about this."

"Giving something that valuable to some guy you don't even know like me."

"Don't sweat it. Truth is, leave it any longer and those fingers would've rotted clean off. Just think of it as me doing a favor for the guy I got that swordwolf dagger from."

At Todd's breezy, generous answer, Subaru's brows drooped and he bit his lip.
Todd, apparently a soldier of the Vollachian Empire, had convinced himself—thanks to the knife Subaru had received in the forest—that Subaru's true identity was someone of noble standing. Thanks to that, his treatment was remarkably good despite being a prisoner.
But precisely because of that, Subaru felt guilty for deceiving him.
On top of that, a certain question had been flitting through Subaru's mind.
It was this—

"Still, y'know, at a time like this, if there were healing magic, you could just zap the wound closed."

Checking the state of his left hand, Subaru murmured that as offhandedly as he could manage.
Todd, catching the remark, raised an eyebrow.

"Oh? Now that's a luxurious thing to say. Healing magic ain't something you come across just like that."

"...I figured, huh."

"Course. If you could heal wounds and sickness the same easy way you conjure fire or wind, that'd be handy as hell. That left hand of yours would've healed up in no time."

Todd shrugged, speaking with a subdued weight to his voice, and Subaru lowered his eyes.
And at finding his own suspicion confirmed, he felt anxiety and relief stirred up in him at once.

Before, back when Roswaal's schemes hadn't yet been exposed and Subaru still thought of him as just a good-humored, clownish noble, they'd talked about the rarity of healing magic.
Magic was governed by talent, and practitioners of healing magic were precious.

In addition to that information, the medical tent set up at the camp was lined with herbs and bottles of tonic—stocked not with magic, but with an array of purely medical tools.
Todd too, when offering to tend Subaru's wound, had relied on nothing magical at all, resorting instead to herbs and splints. So there was no mistaking it.

"Healing magic is rare here."

"At least, I've never once seen it myself. Word is the capital keeps whatever practitioners exist locked up tight. Either way, it's a world away from us common folk."

"..."

"If anything, I'm more surprised the words 'healing magic' came out of your mouth at all. From where I stand, it's not even an option worth considering."

Something unfamiliar couldn't even claim a foothold at the edge of one's thoughts to begin with.
That was how unfamiliar healing magic was in the Empire—at least, to Todd and people like him.
Having expected that kind of response, Subaru shook his head. "No, well—"

"As you can see, I'm actually a traveler. I've been all over the place, and I ran into someone who used healing magic along the way."

"Ah, traveling gear, huh. I did think you two had a strange getup on. The heat around here and your clothes never quite matched up."

So saying, Todd looked Subaru up and down. His outfit was still the sand-proofed gear meant for crossing the sand sea to reach the Pleiades Watchtower.
Contrary to the desert imagery, the Augria Sand Dunes hadn't actually been a hot place, but to withstand the sandy winds, nearly all his skin was covered. As a result, in Vollachia's humid, higher-temperature climate, it was an outfit he had to admit was rather out of season.

"So, you met some healer or other on your travels and got spoiled rotten by the convenience."

"That's a rude way to put it! Though—yeah, it is convenient."

In truth, Subaru had relied on healing magic several times—no, quite frequently.
Right from the very start, when he'd first been summoned to this world, surviving that first crisis would have been impossible without Beatrice's healing magic.
If Beatrice hadn't been there that time, Subaru would have had to live on to this day with his stomach split open, entrails spilling out.

"Tripping over my own guts—that's an experience I never want to have again."

"Healing magic, huh."

"...? Todd?"

As Subaru recalled his rather rare experience, Todd let out a quiet breath. When Subaru frowned at the shift in mood, Todd closed one eye and said, "Nah, it's just—"

"I'm just thinking I'm glad a cruel thing like healing magic isn't part of my everyday life."

"Cruel? ...Why? Isn't it the opposite, if anything?"

When Subaru showed he didn't follow Todd's view, Todd, still with one eye shut, went on, "Well, see—"

"A wound healing means not dying. It means you don't get pulled off the line just for being hurt. Your wound gets fixed, and you're sent right back to keep fighting, on and on. That's what a wound healing means."

"..."

"It scares the hell out of me. Whoever first came up with healing magic—just how much did they love fighting? Feels like I'm being shown that, plain as day."

Hearing Todd's quiet words, Subaru found he had nothing to say back.
He thought it a lopsided way of looking at things. In reality, healing magic's use wasn't confined to warfare alone. There was its role, too, as an extension of everyday life, saving people from accidents and illness.
And yet, seen from one angle, Todd's way of thinking was also true.
Treating someone wounded on a battlefield and driving them right back into the fight—as long as one couldn't say that side of it didn't exist, there was no denying the feeling behind his fear of it either.

"Sorry, sorry, got off track there. Didn't mean to make you scrunch up your face like that."

Seeing Subaru fall silent, Todd said that as if resetting the mood. Subaru, for his part, nodded back—"I'm fine"—at hearing a perspective he'd never considered himself.
Then, meaning to change the subject, Todd turned his attention toward outside the tent.

"But that being the case, the girl in the cage's your travel companion too, right? So how come she's snapping at you like that?"

"...An unfortunate accident, I guess. We used to be pretty in sync with each other, but now, for various reasons, it's a completely one-way street. I'd appreciate you not judging too fast."

"Fine by me, but you look like you're having a rough time of it... Well, that being so—"

Not prying further into the complicated business between Subaru and Rem, Todd touched his chin and looked away.
Where Todd's gaze landed was Subaru's right arm—the one Subaru had been deliberately ignoring. Clinging to that right arm of his, and had been for a while now—

"Aa?"

—was Rui, letting out a vacant sound with a face that showed no thought whatsoever.
Still clinging to Subaru's right hand, she was grinning for whatever reason pleased her, occasionally toying with his fingers or tangling them in her own hair, doing exactly as she pleased.

"She's definitely not old enough to be your kid, right? What's the deal between you two?"

"I've told you already, I don't know her. But she's sure as hell no good."

"Real salty way to talk about her. ...But the girl in the cage seemed to have her eye on this one, y'know?"

"That's the whole problem..."

Pointed out again by Todd, Subaru sighed at the tangled mess of his situation.
As things stood, Subaru's feelings for Rem were spinning their wheels entirely in vain. Rem's cold treatment of him, now that she'd lost her memories, was heavily influenced by the way he neglected Rui.
But even knowing that, Subaru couldn't bring himself to accept Rui.
Of course he couldn't. She was an Archbishop of Sin, one of the purely evil beings he could never coexist with.

"So how'd it end up like this? What are you scheming, what do you even want?"

"U~? Aa, aa~o~"

Even at Subaru's interrogation, Rui just laughed her vapid laugh, giving no answer back.
It was an infuriating attitude through and through. Of course, being treated to the same vicious behavior she'd shown in the Corridor of Memories would be a problem too—but at least then his heart wouldn't be led astray by being able to flatly call her an enemy.
Far more than the current state of things—being treated to behavior like some baby or toddler, with Subaru the only one who could even recognize the danger in it.

"Well, she's your travel companion. Wherever you're headed, you'd probably do well to patch things up between you two a bit more."

"...Which one of them is that advice for?"

"Advi—? Well, take it however you like, for whichever one."

Tilting his head at the unfamiliar word, Todd said that and got to his feet.
This was the medical tent, after all—lingering here for idle chat probably wasn't welcome. Subaru, Rui still stuck to his right hand, followed after Todd.

"Well then, now that the wound's patched up... straight to it, but mind doing some chores?"

"Sure. Yeah, beats feeling guilty about being left with nothing to do. Just tell me whatever needs doing. Any job besides eating shoes, bring it on."

"Guess Jamal really got under your skin... alright, alright, I won't have you doing anything like that. For now, let's see."

Looking thoughtful, what Todd glanced toward was a cluster of black tents.
Following his gaze to the tents, Subaru asked, "What's this?"

"Supplies for the camp. They've been gathered up because we need this and that, but nobody's much good at the fiddly business of sorting it all out. So, this is where you come in, seeing as you're good with organizing."

"...Did I ever say I was good at organizing?"

"Nope. But I figured it'd be nice if you were. And even if you're not, I figured you'd push through out of gratitude for the help you got."

"...You've got quite the personality, Todd."

Wearing a good-natured smile, Todd said something rather mean-spirited. Subaru's cheek twitched at his words as he cast a quick glance over the cluster of black tents.
At a glance there were around twenty tents, and if they were all packed full of supplies, sorted only haphazardly as Todd said, this would be heavy labor indeed.

"Doesn't look like this'll be done in a day or two..."

"Ah, no worries, long as it's done before the supply corps' dragon carriage heads out. Hahaha."

"Hahaha..."

In other words, that meant: move fast.
Given the injury to his left hand, the difficulty was somewhat elevated, but there was no help for it.

"This too is for today's pay, for getting Rem back to Emilia-tan's side..."

"U~!"

As Subaru clenched his fist, steeling himself to face the grueling labor, Rui let out a sound beside him. Still dangling from his right arm, she made Subaru scowl with something close to loathing.
An Archbishop of Sin with a face that understood nothing at all, piling burdens on both Subaru's body and mind. Her viciousness intact, she left a strong impression of having become a difficult foe simply harder to handle.

"Man, between this and Shaula, my rate of getting tangled up in things I never asked for is way too high..."

"Aa, u~"

Whether she understood or not, Subaru all but dragged the good-humored-looking Rui along as he made his way toward the black tents.
Unlike Shaula, whom he'd eventually come to understand, there wasn't even any communication to speak of with Rui to begin with. Mutual understanding, he thought, was simply impossible.

"...So you were tidying tents the whole time, until it got dark?"

Rem, sitting sideways on the floor, greeted Subaru with that as he returned from finishing his heavy labor.
Subaru's brows drooped at the sight of her, and at his reaction she narrowed her eyes and said,

"I did not greet you."

"Hey, don't go reading my mind. ...But they let you out of the cell, huh?"

"...At the very least, the people here didn't seem to bear me any hostility."

The awkward way she lowered her eyes was likely guilt over that clash by the riverside he'd heard about—the bad first encounter Rem had with that one-eyed man Jamal's squad.
As a rule, Rem tended to be curt toward anyone she hadn't opened her heart to.
That side of her had softened considerably once she'd warmed up to Subaru, but now, having lost her memories, that temperament had somewhat resurfaced. He figured she was reflecting on that.

"But being able to reflect on it is pretty admirable, Rem. Gold star."

"...From what standpoint are you even looking at me? Being praised by you doesn't make me happy in the slightest. To begin with—"

She said this sharply to the smiling Subaru, then lifted her gaze.
Drawn along, Subaru looked up too, but all that was there was the narrow, conical ceiling of the tent. Huh, he thought, tilting his head. Rem clicked her tongue, sounding displeased.

"Why are the two of us in the same tent? I don't want to sound demanding, but you could have shown a little more consideration..."

"No, if anything, I think this is a mountain of consideration. I told Todd we were traveling companions, and with that... ow-ow-ow-ow-ow!"

"How dare you do something so presumptuous!"

The moment he sat down beside her, Rem's hand seized him hard around the hip bone. As she twisted his lower spine and Subaru writhed in pain, Rem's eyes went sharp.
But a small shadow interrupted with an "Aa," stopping Rem's action.
It was—

"You again..."

"U~!"

Rui threw her whole body against Rem's hand gripping Subaru's hip, protesting bodily.
Rem, indulgent toward Rui for whatever reason, let out a resigned sigh at the act and, having no choice, called off her punishment of Subaru. Instead, she pulled Rui's body onto her own lap.
There, she rested Rui's head on her stiff, hard-to-move leg and gently stroked her.

"Tch."

"...Now why are you clicking your tongue? This child is so attached to you, and I don't understand how you can be so heartless about it."

Rem's reaction to Subaru's poor attitude was less than favorable.
As for Subaru, all he could do was watch Rui's every move closely, lying there borrowing Rem's lap, so that he'd be ready to protect Rem the instant Rui's true nature ever showed itself.

—On Todd's instructions, Subaru had set about organizing the black tents.
Just as he'd first expected, sorting out the contents of the tents was not something to be finished in a short amount of time. Part of that, of course, was because Subaru's left hand wasn't in perfect condition, but beyond that, the Empire's people had far less sense of organizational rules than he'd assumed, and then—

"This one here kept messing up my work at every turn. Just when I'd get something tidied, she'd knock it right back over and scatter it again. Thanks to her, I barely made any progress."

"She doesn't understand anything, so it can't be helped, can it?"

"You don't understand everything either, Rem, but you'd never do something like that. There, proof complete! QED!"

"There you go saying nonsensical things again!"

Knowing full well that the cause of Rem's cold treatment toward him lay with Rui, how could Subaru possibly help but be cold toward Rui in turn?
Even knowing this was an unhealthy spiral of coldness, he couldn't manage to get along with Rui even on the surface. —A visceral, physical revulsion wasn't something you could simply mask.

"..."

Carrying that helpless feeling, Subaru flopped straight down onto his back on the floor.
The tent held only the three of them—Subaru, Rem, and Rui—one Todd had allotted them for their stay, just a few days' worth.
While setting up camp, a comrade who'd gone into the forest hadn't come back, so with the tent's owner now gone, Todd had laughed and said they were welcome to use it freely.

"Yeah, not really something to laugh about, though."

Still, being given a spare tent to use was an enormous help.
Subaru aside, leaving Rem out in a camp full of men was deeply worrying. Todd had said he'd treat them as guests, but how far that attitude would actually spread was unclear.
What's more, Rem had already earned Jamal's displeasure.
Ideally, Subaru wanted to keep his eyes on her without letting up even while doing chores, wanted to stay by her side—but—

"Stop sulking already and eat."

"Huh?"

Subaru, still lying on his back, sunk in thought. What blocked his view was a skewer of grilled meat that Rem held out above his face.
He sat up on reflex, and found Rem with her face turned away, thrusting the skewer at him.

"What's this?"

"Apparently it's a meal. They were handing it out to everyone in camp, so I got some for us. ...I need to practice moving around, even if only little by little."

So saying, Rem rubbed her own leg with her free hand.
There was no telling when her leg, still in its impaired state, would return to normal, but surely Rem herself, the one actually living with it, felt more anxious about it than even Subaru did.
A camp surrounded by strangers, no memories, her own standing left uncertain and adrift. Even if she wanted to move to find something out, her leg simply wouldn't cooperate.

"...Would you take it already? My arm's getting tired."

"R-right, right, got it. Um, have you already eaten, Rem?"

"Huh? Why would I eat first when this child hasn't eaten yet? There's no way I could do something that selfish."

So saying, Rem drew over a bone dish that had been set aside at the edge of the tent, pulled off the cloth covering it, and brought a skewer to Rui's mouth.
Rui, spoiled by Rem, took full advantage of her kindness, nibbling at the meat in tiny bites, just like a chick pecking food from its mother bird.

"Fufu."

Rem watched Rui peck away at her meal with a fond, warm look.
Unable to tear his eyes from the sight, Subaru too nibbled away at the skewer he'd been handed. The meat, seemingly nothing more than skewered and grilled, gave no clue whatsoever as to what kind of meat it actually was.
At any rate, it was tough and bland, hard to call delicious.

"About on par with the food Emilia-tan or Beako make..."

"What are you muttering about... ah."

"...?"

As Subaru voiced his idle thoughts about the meal, Rem glanced at him and suddenly her eyes went wide. At her reaction, Subaru frowned, wondering what was wrong.
Her blue eyes were fixed on Subaru's face. Which meant, he figured, whatever had startled her had to do with his face.

"What's wrong? Don't tell me you're about to say something sad, like this is the first time you've properly looked at my face."

"That's not it, but... um, there are tears."

"Tears?"

"...Tears are running down your face. Haven't you noticed?"

At Rem's hesitant words, Subaru held his breath. Then, gingerly, he touched his own cheek, and was startled when his fingertip met a hot droplet.
It wasn't some out-of-the-blue lie from Rem—it was true.

"Huh, am I... crying?"

"Y-yes, you're crying. What's wrong? Is it your finger, the injury...?"

Wiping away the tears trickling gently down, Subaru grew confused by this swell of his own emotions. But the cause of the tears wasn't the pain of his broken fingers.
It was something else entirely—probably, simply, spending this quiet time with Rem like this.

"..."

Their situation was nowhere near settled.
Separated from Emilia and the others, with no way to get in touch, in a land where being found out could get him killed, stuck in a state of complete non-communication with a memoryless Rem. On top of it all, his traveling companion was an Archbishop of Sin, the very pinnacle of this world's evil, and leading the whole sorry group was Natsuki Subaru—ignorant, incompetent, powerless, and reckless in equal measure.
There wasn't a single reason to feel optimistic. Not a single one—and yet—

"...Talking with you like this, eating a meal with you. That's what made me happy."

"..."

"S-sorry, this must make zero sense to you. I'm saying something weird. I think it's totally fair if you find it creepy. ...But it's how I really feel."

Giving up on holding back the unstoppable tears, Subaru gazed at Rem while the tears streamed down in earnest and he sniffled back his running nose.

"I've wanted this for so long—to spend ordinary time like this with you again."

Setting the half-eaten skewer down on his lap, Subaru forced the words out.
The sound of him wiping his streaming tears on his sleeve and sniffling echoed through the quiet tent.
For a while, only that awkward sound filled the silence—but then—

"...I don't understand what you're saying."

Rem said this suddenly, in a voice like the faintest breath.
Wiping his tears, Subaru held his breath. And he told himself that Rem's hard, cold voice was only natural, biting down on his own shame.

There was no way anyone wouldn't find it unpleasant—a strange man right in front of you, one giving off a smell you couldn't warm to, breaking down in tears.
Once again, he'd damaged Rem's trust in him. And in a way beyond any recovery, at that.

"But I don't intend to laugh at you for crying. I do find it... unsettling, but not so far as to call it disgusting."

"...Eh?"

At the unexpected words, Subaru lifted his head, eyes going wide.
Across from him, Rem stroked Rui's head on her lap without ever looking his way, her lips trembling as though she were carefully choosing her words.

"...That's all. Hurry up and eat. I'm already worn out for today."

"...Ah."

Said quickly by Rem with her eyes shut, Subaru's reaction lagged for a moment, unsure what she meant. But once he realized she meant the skewer on his lap, he hurriedly bit back into it.

"R-right, yeah. Mm, good, real good. Salty and good."

"The saltiness is because of your tears, you know. ...I, meanwhile, can't taste my meal properly because of your body odor. It's unfair."

"That's... well, I'll think of ways to improve on that. Yes."

Rem's manner of speaking was cold and sharp.
And yet, she hadn't told him to leave, hadn't said she didn't want to eat with him. If so, there was nothing for it but for Subaru to come up with a different plan himself.
If that was what it took to protect this small stretch of peace he'd found, then so be it.

"...But if we're talking about what's unfair, I've got something to say myself."

"Something to say? What is it? If it's about your finger..."

"—It's her. The one lounging carefree on your lap right there."

Pointing sharply, Subaru curled his lip at Rui, who was thoroughly enjoying Rem's lap-pillow. Seeing that, Rem narrowed her eyes as if to say "here we go again."
But what Subaru wanted to say this time came from a different angle than his usual complaints.

"You keep going on about my body odor—yeah, I hate how that sounds, but about my smell, anyway—but she's got a similar smell too, doesn't she? Are you just gonna ignore that?"

The Witch's Lingering Scent, which grew stronger in intensity with every repetition of Return by Death.
But if this scent was tied to the Witch—connected to the Witch Factor—then naturally, the same foul odor ought to be drifting off Rui too, an Archbishop of Sin herself.
Thinking back on how furiously Rem reacted to anything to do with the Witch Cult, that seemed like a foregone conclusion—

"...? What are you talking about. Please don't lump this child in with you."

"...Huh?"

"I'm saying, this child doesn't have any smell like yours at all. Please don't start saying strange things just because you're grasping at straws."

But Rem's answer to what Subaru had said was entirely unexpected.
He couldn't help but stare intently back into Rem's eyes, but there was nothing strange in her expression. It didn't look like a lie, nor did it seem she was trying to deceive Subaru.
Which meant she truly wasn't sensing any Witch's Lingering Scent, any miasma, from Rui at all.

"No way—could she disguise the miasma somehow? No, but... to what end?"

From Subaru's experience so far, few people could actually sense the miasma known as the Witch's Lingering Scent. Setting aside Rem, who reacted most strongly of all, it was only an extremely limited handful—Beatrice, Ryuzu, and the like—who'd ever shown any reaction to it at all.
To begin with, it didn't seem plausible that a Witch Cultist would even conceive of hiding such a thing.
They were agents of grand treason, trampling this world underfoot as if it were their own. And yet—

"Are we done now? If you're finished eating, this child seems sleepy too, so I'd like to start getting ready for bed soon..."

"Ah, um... hey, was that thing just now really true?"

"You're repeating yourself."

Sharply, Rem shut Subaru's question right down.
But if anything, her attitude only seemed to prove all the more that what she was sensing wasn't a lie.

"...No miasma detectable from Rui?"

What exactly that meant, Subaru couldn't quite grasp.
But he did have this horrible sense that something deeply unsettling, something bad for him, was quietly underway.

"Sorry, but could you clear away the dishes? I need to get the bedding ready."

"Ah, right, got it. And, um, don't worry, I won't try anything."

"...Saying that only makes me more worried."

Told this again in that hard voice, Subaru shuffled out of the tent, dejected, to clean up the dish holding the finished skewers.
In the darkness of night, campfire lights could be seen scattered across the camp. No one had ordered Subaru and the others to it, but there were probably men keeping watch through the night.
He only knew it from manga and games, but preparing for war really was a serious undertaking.

"...I want to get away from here soon."

Todd was a good-natured guy, but even so, Subaru didn't think he could ever get used to the atmosphere of a battlefield.
He had to leave as soon as possible and find a way to rejoin Emilia and the others.
Resolving as much, Subaru tightened his grip on the dish—and noticed.

"...Huh, my finger doesn't hurt. No way, has the medicine already kicked in?"

Looking at the fingers of his left hand, which had just gripped the dish hard without thinking, Subaru was startled by how well the medicine had worked.
Some sense of wrongness still lingered, but the tingling warmth beginning to spread through it was solid proof that his left hand was starting to do its job properly again.

"He was going on about healing magic and all, but this medicine's more than up to the task itself..."

Recalling Todd's words, Subaru gave his left hand a light shake and started walking.
There was a lot to think about—Rem, Rui, himself.
A lot, but he'd work through it one thing at a time, improving bit by bit.
Just like the fingers of his left hand—one step at a time, moving things in a better direction.


△▼△▼△▼△


"..."

And so, as Subaru left the tent and walked slowly away, a single shadow watched him go.
The man with an eyepatch over one eye narrowed his remaining eye, clicking his tongue at the leisurely retreating back.
And then—

"Look at him, all cheerful. Pathetic."

So he muttered.
