—Kicking hard off the packed earth, he flung his body forward.

Sweat streaming down his forehead caught the wind and grazed the corner of his eye; he ignored it with a blink.
Feeling his lungs ache with each breath he sucked in, his body wrung itself tight around his solar plexus.

"—Gh—"

He gritted his teeth and drove the pain wholesale from his mind.
The only thing he pictured in his head was a single word: *GOAL*.

"——!"

From somewhere far off, he thought he heard someone's high voice.
It kept drawing closer, chasing after Subaru as he ran. Relying on that voice, hand over hand as if hauling himself forward, he pressed on, and on—

"——!"

Drawn on by the earnest voice, not caring that his vision was clouding white at the edges no matter how far he went, he ran.
And then, at last—

"Go-o-oal, I suppose!"

He crossed the clumsily drawn white line laid at his feet, and in that instant, heaven and earth flipped over.
About to plow headfirst into the low weeds, Subaru instinctively planted his hands and rolled forward. Killing the impact with a motion that had already become second nature, he added a couple more unnecessary rotations before sprawling flat on his back on the ground in a big "大" shape.

"Bwuh—! Aah! Rough! Painful! But it's over! I did it!"

Even as he gasped for breath, he kept forcing himself to shout.
The half-cursing outburst was to whip up his own flagging spirit. He couldn't let himself think of exhaustion as mere fatigue, or of effort as mere hardship.
If he didn't tell himself there was still a bit further to go, that this wasn't the end, his heart would stall out.
And every time he was tempted to draw his own arbitrary line of "done" and "satisfied," Subaru would put a hand to his chest and remember that night.

"Subaru, good work, I suppose."

A small shadow cut sideways into his upside-down field of view, looming over him as he lay there.
A girl with lovely features, her long cream-colored hair done up in lavish curls—Beatrice.

The hem of a dress ill-suited to a meadow swayed as Beatrice quietly held out a towel to him. He took it, threw it over his head, and scrubbed roughly at his face.

"Ahh, that helps. This subtly-cold consideration of yours is downright evil."

"That's Petra's doing—she chilled it before handing it to Betty. If you thank her later, that child will probably jump for joy, I suppose."

"Petra really is thoughtful, as always. But it's rare for you to come all the way out here, Bea. Something got you in a different mood than usual?"

He swung his arms and pushed himself upright, sliding on his backside to face Beatrice. Under Subaru's upward gaze, Beatrice put her hands on her hips and looked away.

"It's nothing, just a whim, I suppose."

"Oh-ho, is that so. A whim, huh."

"...There was also a part of me that wanted to see with my own eyes what Subaru's *really* like, when he's always putting on that air of trying so hard, I suppose."

Still turned pointedly away, Beatrice bluntly laid bare the true feelings she'd been hiding.
It struck him just how honest she'd become—proof of all the time he'd spent gently working her heart open.
Beatrice shot the grinning Subaru a look like she had more to say, but in the end left it at a small sigh and nothing more.

"Anyway, it can't be that you were just going to run around recklessly and call it done, I suppose. What are you planning to do after this?"

"Even just running around recklessly is plenty tough, you know, young lady. Not sure I can meet your expectations, but from here we're moving into the dream athletic zone."

"...Ah, I wondered what that was, I suppose. You mean the pile of logs you had Garfiel build in the forest. 'Athletic,' was it?"

"Athletic zone. You don't have to force yourself to remember it, you know? Just let it go in one ear and out the other."

"Betty wants to be able to understand every single word Subaru says, I suppose."

At the offhand delivery of what amounted to a killer line, Subaru's cheeks loosened even further into a grin. Beatrice, puzzled by his reaction, then seemed to catch the implication of her own words and her expression changed with a start. Her faintly reddening cheeks were lovely, like ripening fruit.

"Th-that's not what I meant just now... it just happened to come out sounding that way, I suppose."

"No no no, it's fine, no misunderstanding here. Relax, relax, I love you too."

"You understand absolutely nothing, you—!!"

Grinning foolishly, Subaru bounced to his feet and, looping an arm behind the knees of the scowling Beatrice beside him, lifted her up with ease.
Cradled in his arms, Beatrice wore a face of displeasure but didn't complain about being carried.

"Subaru, do you smell like sweat, I suppose?"

"Then breathe through your mouth. Or just go direct with a Mana drain."

"If it's fine to drink until you're dried up, Betty will do it, I suppose."

"Pretty sure you're the one who'd end up crying if you did that."

"W-who says Betty would cry! Don't be ridiculous, I suppose!"

Bickering the whole way, Subaru set off running again with Beatrice in his arms.
His breathing had already recovered while they'd been sparring. From the meadow of the running course to the forest of the next exercise ground, carrying a weight about Beatrice's size was just right for running.
After all, this girl, contrary to appearances, was extraordinarily light. Like a feather, almost.

So with the ease of someone who'd sprouted wings, Subaru ran as if flying, Beatrice in tow.

The new mansion that had replaced the one burned to the ground was, just as before, surrounded by greenery.
Thick undergrowth and deep, verdant hills. A cool wind blew through, and Subaru felt it catch his short bangs as he kicked off the dirt.

"Hup! Hop, and—!"

In the forest where green filled his entire field of view, Subaru sprinted along, lightly planting a hand on a fallen log lying across his path and clearing the obstacle with nothing more than a short hop and a shift of weight.
It was a technique called a "vault," a method of movement that showed its worth in terrain riddled with obstacles like this, or in places crowded with buildings, like city streets. Sports built around this kind of technical system were called parkour, and Subaru used to watch, dumbfounded, as players who practiced parkour performed their superhuman feats on TV.
He'd never once imagined that he himself would master a sliver of that jaw-dropping human skill.

"Yah, ha, hup!"

The climbing structure made of multiple stacked logs was the centerpiece of the athletic zone Subaru had asked Garfiel to build.
With a great tree as its central pillar and logs strung crisscross in every direction, it was practically a full-fledged jungle gym born of a rather avant-garde sensibility.

Even climbing it slowly would leave one bewildered about where to put hand and foot next, just from the look of it.
Yet Subaru leapt at it with the momentum of his sprint intact, and relying on the barest of handholds and footholds for his fingertips, hauled his body up in one motion, in a move like scaling a sheer vertical wall.
His speed reaching the top of the jungle gym was too fast for the eye to follow.
But the true terror of parkour technique, and the point of a proper jungle gym, didn't end there.

"Hup, hup, and hup."

Reaching the summit, he hopped nimbly across the narrow footholds and came to the edge, peering down below. Roughly six meters or so to the ground.
The weed- and moss-covered earth had, naturally, nothing laid out to cushion a fall. Even the dirt ground, which might have offered some softness, was now packed firm.
In other words, landing normally would guarantee injury—the kind of drop that made your legs go stiff with fear. And yet—

"—Yah!"

Without a moment's hesitation, Subaru leapt down toward that hard ground. The utterly unguarded jump might have looked, to anyone watching, like his usual recklessness finally reaching its logical extreme.
But Subaru, without flinching, extended both legs and touched down on the ground. And then—rather than being swallowed by the shock stabbing through his entire body and writhing in pain—

"——"

He bent his knees, dropped his hips to disperse the impact, and rolled forward to shed the rest of the damage. Rolling once, twice, three times across the ground, he rose to his feet on all fours without a scratch on him. All he did was brush himself off lightly, wiping the mud off his tracksuit.

This too was one of parkour's techniques—the "landing" and the "roll."
The landing technique was called landing, and the rotation that dispersed the impact was the roll; combined, they made a single technique.
For a fall from a certain height, dispersing the shock let you resume activity unharmed. A problem that would be nothing to other superhuman types was, for an utterly mediocre ordinary man like Subaru, literally a matter of life and death.
Just mastering this technique alone should have vastly widened the range of what he was capable of.

"So, yeah, that's about the level of results I've got so far. Reconsidered your opinion of me a little?"

Spreading both arms where he stood, Subaru looked over at Beatrice, who had been watching the whole sequence of moves just now.
Sitting obediently on a tree stump set up for spectators, Beatrice widened her already round eyes further.

"Honestly, Betty was a little surprised. Reconsidered you a bit, I suppose."

"Fell for me all over again?"

"Betty has no idea what Subaru's trying to get her to say lately, I suppose!"

"I just want to feel like you love me, that's all."

Which, honestly, Beatrice already made plenty clear through her attitude—not that it wasn't easy to read.
Still red-faced and indignant, Beatrice glared, and Subaru grinned back at her before glancing behind him.

As demonstrated by the parkour just now, part of the forest had been remodeled primarily into an athletic zone for Subaru's training.
Since it was on Roswaal's land, no one complained, but Subaru had more than half seriously considered recommending Garfiel go into the civil engineering business, given how effortlessly he'd cleared a stretch of forest and built all this. Just between them.

For all his rough edges, Garfiel was surprisingly deft-handed and meticulous, too. Young and wild, his talent would no doubt keep blossoming in all sorts of fields going forward.

"Anyway, I think that's enough for today."

"Here, catch, I suppose."

Catching the tossed towel, he wiped away his sweat just as he had in the meadow.
Then he crouched and stretched right there, loosening the muscles of his legs and hips. The importance of a supple body had been talked about even back in his old world, but ever since he'd started seriously working on reshaping his physique, he'd felt the benefit of it every single day.

Not quite enough to do the splits, but his joints had gotten considerably more flexible.
He hooked a foot on a nearby tree trunk and stretched hard. When he sat on the ground and spread his legs, Beatrice, without a word, moved around behind him and leaned her weight forward to push against his back.

"Stretching's done. Alright, let's head back to the mansion and laze around."

"Let's do that, I suppose."

No argument came back, where once there would have been one.
Fully accustomed to how Subaru handled things by now, Beatrice's reactions had become familiar these days.
He caught the hand reaching toward his, and hand in hand with Beatrice, headed for the edge of the forest.

"Bea, are you holding back on the Mana drain, by any chance? Feels like it's taking less out of me than usual."

"Even Betty has the sense to be considerate toward a tired partner, I suppose."

"Oh-ho, quite the change of tune in just a couple hours. But it's no good if you end up out of sorts because of it, so just do it like normal."

Smiling wryly at the uncharacteristically demure Beatrice, Subaru lifted their joined hands to show her. When Beatrice caught sight of it out of the corner of her eye and sighed, the familiar sensation came at once.
Inside Subaru's closed Gate, the vessel that was Beatrice cut directly in. It was a dedicated back door for drawing Mana out of Subaru's body without passing through the proper gate.
That back door, usable only by Beatrice, was now a literal lifeline for Subaru.

Through overuse, Subaru's Gate had completely lost its function.
But even with the Gate gone, that didn't mean the trickle of Mana welling up from the Od within Subaru simply vanished.
If anything, despite having lost its exit, the Mana just kept being produced one batch after another.
Left alone, the Mana with nowhere to go would run rampant inside Subaru's body and, eventually, burst like a frog someone had pumped full of air—or so Subaru understood it, anyway.

Whether it would actually explode was a separate question, but it was Beatrice who'd warned him of that danger. And so, to kill two birds with one stone—handling the matter of the Mana Beatrice needed to maintain their Contract—Subaru and Beatrice made a point of physically touching at least once a day like this.

Subaru, whose body kept accumulating trace amounts of Mana, and Beatrice, who needed Mana to function. Beyond their personalities, even down to their physical constitutions, the two of them were an exquisite match.
Although—

"If Bea's Mana drain could work on someone other than her contractor, you could've kept up that powerful loli form from before without any trouble."

"Don't say it so wistfully, I suppose. We already talked that over ages ago and Betty accepted it, I suppose. Besides, even if it's a trace amount, Betty is properly storing it up little by little, I suppose. Even if it's about a sparrow's tears' worth."

The trouble was that Beatrice's Mana Drain only targeted her contractor.
At the old Roswaal mansion, apparently, she'd siphoned Mana indiscriminately from anyone who came and went, but that had relied on a special method using the Forbidden Archive as an intermediary.

"'The Forbidden Archive served as a medium for Betty's Mana intake—there was a mechanism where the Archive itself collected Mana from anyone spending time in the mansion,'" was how Beatrice explained it.

Because of that, the scheme of drawing Mana from Garfiel, who probably had Mana to spare, or from Emilia, who'd struggled with handling her own enormous Mana right after leaving the Sanctuary, had come to nothing. It felt like some unseen someone telling him there was no such thing as a free lunch.

Even so, disappointed as he'd been at first, Subaru now thought things had turned out for the best.
Making contact with Beatrice on a regular basis felt like an important ritual, one that gave meaning to his current relationship with her, and being able to feel that connection had a good effect on him.
The partnership between Subaru the Spirit Arte user and Beatrice, his contracted Spirit, had a different flavor from that of an ordinary Spirit Arte user and their Spirit. Confirming their own particular way of doing things, in moments like this, was something Subaru and Beatrice needed.

"It's done, I suppose. That should be plenty satisfying for today, I suppose."

"Right, haah... piece of cake... haah, haah."

"Betty's decided not to say anything more when you're putting on a brave face like that, I suppose."

Their daily routine finished, Beatrice's eyes on Subaru were exceedingly, embarrassingly warm.
On the way back, once they cleared the forest path and their feet hit paved ground, that was the sign the mansion was near. The road resembled the one near Arlam Village, but the road leading away from the mansion on the other side connected to a nearby city called Costuul, so the sense of being in the middle of nowhere was considerably reduced here.

"But Betty preferred the quiet forest from before, if she has to choose, I suppose."

"I think a noisy town has its own good points and a quiet forest has its own good points. It's not really an either-or thing for me. Though the only big city I'd ever seen before was the royal capital, so Costuul is fresh, at least."

"Hmph, I see, I suppose. So Subaru and Betty just don't share the same tastes, I suppose."

Pouting, Beatrice made her displeasure with his answer plain.
Tugging her along by the hand, Subaru soothed her with a "there, there" and turned toward the road to the mansion. Just then—

"—Subaru-sama! Beatrice-chan!"

A high voice called both their names, and Subaru and Beatrice looked up at the same moment. Ahead of them, from beyond the road leading to the mansion, they saw a girl come running toward them.
A familiar voice, a familiar face—her expression brightening with joy at the sight of them was lovely, her shoulder-length brown hair streaked with red fluttering lightly in the wind.
Her big, round, cat-like eyes made the girl's already expressive face all the more captivating. The kind of endearing charm that made anyone who saw her movements smile without meaning to.

A delicate flower blooming untouched in an open field.
If one were to describe a girl named Petra Leyte, that expression would suit her best.

"I was just about to go get you two. Good thing we didn't miss each other."

Catching her breath, Petra put a hand to her chest once she'd run up to them and said as much.
In response, Subaru patted the head of Petra, whose height now reached up to about his own chest.

"What's got you in such a hurry? No need to rush, we're not gonna run off. Did the tart you were baking come out amazingly well or something?"

"If that's it, I can see why you'd rush to come get us, I suppose. Is it an important matter?"

"Oh, come on! It's not that! Don't you two go teasing me!"

Cheeks puffed out, Petra scolded Beatrice for her all-too-plausible nod of agreement, then reached to remove Subaru's hand from her head, only to stop halfway, having grabbed it with both hands instead.
Holding Subaru's hand in both of hers, Petra, cheeks still flushed from running, said,

"The tart can wait—this is something else. We have a guest at the mansion. And Emilia-sama said to go call for you two..."

"Wait, Petra. That's enough. Something about the way that's going is stirring up a bad feeling in me."

"Eh?"

At what Petra said, Subaru's wariness surged and he called a halt.
Petra looked startled at his words, but Beatrice wore a face that said *of course.*
And no wonder. Ever since they'd moved into this mansion, Beatrice, who'd been at Subaru's side the whole time, had witnessed the same things he had.
From the day they left the Sanctuary up until today, well—all sorts of things had happened, to say the least.

"A pretty large chunk of them started off exactly this way. Whether it's Petra coming to fetch us, or Frederica, or sometimes Otto or Garfiel—it's basically always trouble. Even I've learned by now."

"A sudden guest, and Subaru conveniently out and about... this is definitely a misfortune-comes-knocking pattern, I suppose."

"E-even Beatrice-chan is starting to talk like Subaru-sama...! Subaru-sama, don't go teaching Beatrice-chan weird things!"

"When it comes to raising Bea, it's the whole mansion's consensus policy to let her grow up free and easy. That aside, though, it's a guest, huh. Petra, my stomach hurts, Beatrice and I are gonna have to sit this one out."

"NO. YOU. DON'T! Emilia-sama will get mad! I don't have any reason to go against Emilia-sama. Come on, let's go, let's go!"

Subaru showed every sign of rejection, but Petra, burning with a sense of duty, wasn't about to go easy on him.
She used to cling to whatever Subaru said without question, but living here had turned Petra into someone thoroughly capable, and now she argued back at him head-on.

Pulling Subaru's right arm with both of hers, Petra put her full slight weight into it. Dragged along by her, Subaru looked over at Beatrice, whose hand he still held with his left.

"Bea."

"Off you go, I suppose."

"You're coming down with me too!"

"Gyaa, I suppose!"

The direction of his appeal for help flipped instantly into a direction of dragging someone down with him. Beatrice tried to yank her hand free on reflex, but Subaru held it fast with his left hand. And since his right hand was held fast by Petra, that left no escape for either of them.
With Subaru refusing to let the reluctant Beatrice go, and Petra refusing to let Subaru go, the three of them, in this baffling formation, headed for the mansion together.

"I get that you can't turn away someone who's already arrived at this point, but... if that's how it's gonna be, I really wish they'd give us a heads-up beforehand."

"You mean like an envoy to announce the envoy? At that point you wouldn't know how far in advance to send word, right? Even I get that much."

"I just think it'd be nice for everyone's peace of mind, and for keeping good relations going forward, if people made a bit more of an effort. By the way, do you know who's here today, Petra?"

Given they were guests at the mansion, whoever had received them would've been Petra, Frederica, or Ram—those three were the only candidates. Since Petra had come to fetch them, presumably one of the other two was handling the guest, but—

"Um, that's the thing, I'm not really sure."

"Not sure? You might not have recognized the family crest, but you saw the messenger, right? Even if you didn't, someone must've told you something when they said to go call us..."

"Everyone was in a huge rush, and I heard it's an important guest. But they didn't look like that at all, so..."

"'Didn't look like that'—judging people by appearance isn't a great idea. Doesn't matter how dangerously done-up someone's vertical curls are, sometimes you get a little girl with terrifying power. Even if she just looks like a loli in a fancy dress, her strength might be beyond human comprehension..."

"Quiet, I suppose!!"

Halted by Beatrice's interjection, Subaru clammed up.
And with Subaru falling silent, Petra kept her troubled expression and said,

"I don't judge people by their looks either, not after what happened before, I don't."

"Oh, good for you, Petra. Don't know what happened before, but that's an important thing to learn."

"The new handyman who came to the village, the one with the mean-looking eyes—I thought he'd be a weird guy, but... he wasn't like that at all."

"BOOMERANG!!"

Taking unexpected damage, Subaru tilted his head at Petra's words. Setting aside what her first impression of him had been, it was what came just before that that mattered.
Someone who, even for Petra—who'd made up her mind not to judge by appearance—still struck her as strange.

"So what kind of guy was it?"

"To put it simply... a kitty?"

"A kitty?"

At the word "cat," the first thing that came to Subaru's mind was a small cat-type Spirit with gray fur and a long swishing tail. It was someone toward whom Subaru held complicated feelings, and there was something he'd eventually have to say to that Spirit's face.

"'You must give me your daughter's hand, Miss,' or something like that, I'll have to say."

"Betty also thought of Nee-cha, but Petra should've met Nee-cha before, I suppose. So it can't be that. Petra, what kind of kitty was it, I suppose?"

"Beatrice-chan calls it 'kitty' too, that's cute."

"P-E-T-R-A!"

Beatrice bristled at Petra's teasing tone.
Petra offered a breezy "sorry, sorry," then tilted her gaze upward as if trying to recall,

"'Kitty' isn't quite right, I guess. I never really got a good look, but I think it was a Demi-Human, one of the Cat Humans. Whenever I hear 'Demi-Human,' I always end up picturing Garfiel-niisama, though."

"Garf's a half-blood, and there's nothing about him that reads as obviously Demi-Human at a glance. At most you'd notice his eyes look kind of dangerous if you look close."

There was one more thing, though—his overly sharp fangs, if you wanted to call that a difference too.
According to Garfiel, those fangs kept growing continuously, like a rodent's front teeth, and to keep them at a manageable length and sharpness, he apparently had to gnaw on hard things.
It wasn't uncommon to spot Garfiel gnawing on the mansion's handrails now and then, and getting an earful from Ram or Frederica for it whenever they caught him.

"So basically, we've got a proper Demi-Human showing up for once. If it's Cat Humans, that'd be Beastfolk, and I might actually know a few faces."

In the royal capital, of course, but also in Costuul, Subaru had plenty of chances to see Beastfolk-type Demi-Humans around.
In Roswaal's territory, known for his fondness for Demi-Humans, prejudice against them had been patiently worked loose over a long time. As a result, compared to other regions, it had become a more comfortable place for Demi-Humans to live—or so said the dandy rabbit-eared bar owner Subaru had gotten friendly with.
That said, Petra, who mainly worked at the mansion and, on her rare days off, went back to Arlam Village rather than Costuul, naturally wasn't very familiar with them.

"Really? Then will you show me around Costuul on your next day off?"

"Hm, sure. You'll probably get plenty of chances to stop by anyway, for shopping runs and stuff, so make as many friends and acquaintances as you can while you're at it."

At Subaru's easy agreement, Petra pumped her free fist in victory.
Beatrice let out a sigh, and Subaru, hands held on both sides, could only chuckle wryly.

"Aand, here we are, here we are. Good old home, and all that."

The gate came into view as they talked, and he lifted the joined hands of the two girls up. Ignoring the girls' protests as they were pulled up on their toes, he straightened his back and looked at the mansion.

The new mansion, built to replace the one that had burned down, kept the same general atmosphere as before—the same style of Western-style manor, following the original mansion's design.
The distance from the front gate to the entrance had widened, with a front garden of trimmed lawn flanking a gravel path. To the right as seen from the front gate was a fountain; to the left, a path curved around the side of the mansion, lined with stables for mooring Dragon Carriages and housing Earth Dragons.

Beyond the fountain lay a flower bed bursting with color, arranged so that the fountain, which sprayed at a fixed time each day, doubled as its watering system. Incidentally, at the edge of the flower bed was the kitchen garden Subaru and Petra tended together, where they modestly harvested seasonal vegetables now and then—which, in a good season, got a fairly warm reception.

Crossing the front garden and stepping onto the gravel, they were greeted by a large pair of double doors. The door knocker bore House Mathers' family crest, a motif of a bird resembling a hawk—and indeed, it all held together with just the sort of look that suited the Mathers main residence.

"There was an unfamiliar Dragon Carriage over by the stables. Is that the visitor's?"

"It was a Dragon Carriage, but the thing pulling it wasn't like Patrasche-chan. It wasn't an Earth Dragon—it was a great big doggy."

"A big dog was pulling it... wait, could that be—"

Recalling a creature he had both seen and heard of before, Subaru caught the thread that might lead him to the visitor's identity. But before his own thoughts could reach an answer, the answer came from the other party first, and faster.
Namely—

"Ohh! Onii-san, long time no see! Been doing good?!"

An unnecessarily boisterous, high-pitched voice burst in, and Subaru, having opened the door, jumped in surprise.
Beside him, Petra gave a wry smile, while Beatrice, for some reason, gripped Subaru's hand tighter. Ignoring the two girls' reactions, Subaru looked at the small figure bounding energetically toward him.

A small silhouette.
Shorter even than Petra, only slightly taller than Beatrice. In other words, a child's height—though for her kind, that might well be the limit of how tall she'd ever grow.
Her whole body was covered in short orange fur, with a pair of perky cat ears that were utterly adorable. Round, curious eyes and a mischievous mouth that spoke with boundless energy. Her long orange hair, braided in a way that suited a girl, and a body wrapped entirely in a white robe made for an altogether endearing sight.

Something like a kitten walking on two legs—a dream come true, for any cat lover.
A Cat Human—and, moreover, someone Subaru already knew.

"Mimi! Long time no see. Still as full of energy as ever, I see!"

"Yep! That's right! Mimi's suuuper energetic! Onii-san gets it! Also, Mimi's gotten taller than before and become a grown-up! Ta-daa!"

Hands on her hips, tail wagging, Mimi struck a proud, self-satisfied pose.
On the surface, she looked like nothing more than an energetic, cheeky young girl, but in truth, this girl was the vice-captain of the mercenary band Iron Fang, made up entirely of Beastfolk, and her combat prowess was considerable—an unexpected bundle of contradictions.
Subaru had once fought alongside her against the White Whale and, later, in the subjugation of Petelgeuse. Sharing a common trait of being friendly and familiar with just about anyone, Subaru and Mimi were arguably the two who'd gotten along most easily and naturally out of everyone involved in that whole string of incidents.

Incidentally, since Iron Fang stood in a position not unlike private soldiers for Anastasia Hoshin, Emilia's political rival, on paper alone, Subaru and Mimi were about as adversarial as could be.
But holding any hostility toward Mimi over that would be nothing short of tasteless.

"So that's how it is, huh. Long trip, good work making it out here. Oh, right, let me introduce you. This cute maid here is Petra. A future all-around super-maid working at our mansion. And this one, all bristling with wariness, is Beatrice."

"Ohh! Got it! The maid doing the maid thing is Petra, and Subaru's kid is this one! Got it! Mimi's got it!"

"Wh-what a completely nonsensical way to remember that, I suppose...!"

Beatrice looked visibly rattled, but stayed hidden behind Subaru, fidgeting. She seemed intimidated by Mimi closing the distance at full force, but Mimi, for her part, dove right at the shrinking Beatrice without a shred of hesitation.

"Whaaat? If you shrink up small like that, you'll never get big like Mimi! C'mon, come out, come out!"

"W-wai, s-stop it, I suppose! Betty doesn't care about being small, and besides, you're too small yourself to even talk, I suppose!"

"Heh-heh, that's the thing with amateurs, you know. Mimi's insides are big, so eventually the outside catches up and gets big too. Danchou said so."

"That makes no sense, I suppose!"

Pulled by the hand, dragged forward, Beatrice was completely at Mimi's mercy.
Beatrice looked to Subaru as if pleading for rescue, but Subaru, satisfied at the sight of shy Beatrice making a friend, merely watched over her with a fatherly expression.

"Hey, Subaru-sama. Beatrice-chan's giving you a really scary look right now, you know?"

"People grow by facing the things they're not good with. Bea's a little too much of a picky eater when it comes to new experiences, so I figure it's good to slowly build up her sense of adventure. Let's just quietly watch over her, shall we, dear mother."

"M-mother...? U-understood."

Petra fell silent, red-faced, and Subaru reflected that he'd picked the wrong words there. Still, walking it back would be awkward, so he just let the moment carry him along.
Then he turned his attention back to Mimi, who was spinning Beatrice's hand around and dancing with her.

"So, if you're here, does that mean the others are around too—the two little brothers, or Ricardo? Please tell me Julius didn't show up without an appointment, I really can't handle that."

Subaru had an extremely complicated relationship with Julius Juukulius, Anastasia's knight. Honestly, he didn't feel he could face the man and respond to him with a straight head.
He had some reluctance about dealing with Anastasia too, but compared to dealing with Julius, that was a somewhat lighter burden on his heart.
But Mimi shook her head at Subaru's worry with a "nuh-uh."

"Hetaro and Tivey and Danchou aren't here, and neither's Julius or Ojou! Just Mimi today! I came all by myself properly, ta-daa!"

"Well, good for you, good for you, but... so what'd you come here for?"

"Um, um, oh, right, I remember now!"

Tilting her head at the question, Mimi then hopped up right on top of Beatrice. Ignoring Beatrice, who scrambled to hold her up, Mimi grinned brightly and said,

"It's a party invitation! See, Ojou said, let's all get together and play! So I came to invite you! Super excited! So excited!"
