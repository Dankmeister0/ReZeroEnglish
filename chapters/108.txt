Arc 3 Chapter 31: "Negotiations, Business Talks, and a Severance"

Subaru's experience riding in a Dragon Carriage numbered four times — few enough to count on one hand.

First, two round trips between the royal capital and the manor. Then, one more time — though he'd had no consciousness of it — when he was carried into the manor after falling to Elsa's murderous blade on the day of his first summoning, bringing the total to three.

And the one remaining occasion —

"The cargo-hauling Dragon Carriage I used when I snuck into the castle, riding on Kadomon's scheme... that makes four."

He recalled the circumstances of his covert entry into the castle, crammed into a cargo bed reeking of fish. All his memories of the castle amounted to nothing but a collection of unpleasant recollections for Subaru, but it was precisely because of that experience that he'd been able to come up with this idea now.

Namely — if there was no shop that properly rented out Dragon Carriages,

"Then I just need to borrow one from someone whose whole business already assumes they'll be traveling by Dragon Carriage."

Even Kadomon, proprietor of a fruit shop that didn't exactly thrive in the royal capital, had handled a Dragon Carriage. Now that he thought back on it, he recalled Kadomon calling it a carriage shared among the shopkeepers of that whole row, but the odds weren't low that traveling merchants without a fixed shop pulled their own Dragon Carriages.

"Actually, I've passed by Dragon Carriages plenty of times on that street full of street vendors. In the light novels I used to read, merchants who hadn't set up shop yet were basically either working odd jobs or hauling a carriage around peddling."

The innkeeper, nodding in agreement, confirmed that this reasoning made sense.
According to him, it did seem there were a handful of people in this village who kept a Dragon Carriage for personal use.
Their existence just barely kept the guttering flame of Subaru's hope alive. But —

"Earth Dragons are indispensable to their livelihood, and for traveling merchants they're quite literally a lifeline. I imagine the negotiations will be quite difficult."

The innkeeper conveyed, delicately but clearly, that this was the kind of proposal likely to run into trouble.
Still, for Subaru, this was the only option available to him now. So he shook his head at the innkeeper's consideration and said,

"No, I'm doing it anyway. Just tell me which houses have a Dragon Carriage."

With that plea, he chose the path of negotiation.


※※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※


— But that negotiation, just as the innkeeper had feared, ran into considerable trouble.

First, the households that ran actual shops in the village and used Dragon Carriages purely for stocking goods or trading with other villages were, without exception, reluctant to have their carriages used for anything unscheduled.
To them, the Dragon Carriage was a tool of their trade, not something to be lent out casually to strangers. On top of that, with their daily business to run, not a single one of them was eccentric enough to volunteer for Subaru's journey.

And after being turned down by every single one of those shop-owning candidates, the traveling merchants who ran their trade with their own Dragon Carriages proved even harder to persuade.

As far as Subaru understood it, these people — whose selling point was their light-footedness, never staying in one place — seemed like the perfect targets for this kind of request. And yet—

"Mathers territory, huh? Sorry, but there's no way I'm heading there right now."

The man who spat this out and cut off negotiations with Subaru was tall and gaunt.
He had a covered cart and, like himself, two lean Earth Dragons in tow. He looked at Subaru — who was still fishing for words, trying to press on — with reptilian eyes, and said,

"And, I don't mean this as an insult, but I don't think it's just me turning you down. In my case it's purely about the cargo, though."

"Cargo?"

"My cargo's iron goods — stuff for weapons and armor. Word is the market price's shot way up in the royal capital lately. Tomorrow I'll be tearing off myself, gotta get in on that windfall."

Tapping his cart lightly, the man said this while gazing off toward the setting sun — presumably the direction of the royal capital — with a distant look in his eyes. Perhaps noticing Subaru fall silent, he scratched his head and added, "And another thing—"

"The reason Fleur's as reasonably prosperous as it is for a village this size is because it's a gathering point for folks like me who pass through on the way to and from the capital. So there's a fair number of people you could try pitching this to... but they've all turned you down already, haven't they?"

"...Yeah. You're the sixth one to say no."

Subaru reported the result with a sour look, gnashing his teeth at the frustration of it.
In truth, he had spotted quite a few people who met the basic conditions for negotiation. That, as the man had said, was precisely because this was a village along the road to the royal capital.
Taking it as a fortunate miscalculation, Subaru had eagerly brought the offer that the shop-owning merchants had refused to these traveling merchants instead, only to hear—

"It's the season when folks who'd gone off elsewhere come pouring back toward the capital, nonstop. Can't be helped. I mean, there's a whole royal selection ruckus going on. All anybody smells is money."

"So that's how it is..."

Listening to the man's explanation, Subaru once again had his own shallow thinking thrown back in his face.
When you got right down to why traveling merchants traveled at all, it was an obvious conclusion. They weren't just merchants who happened to move around. Their light-footedness was the vitality essential to pouncing on profitable opportunities, and the goods they hauled were worth the trouble of hauling only because of the profit waiting at the destination.
And right now, with the whole country boiling over something as big as the Royal Selection, there was no way any traveling merchant would let a business opportunity in the capital slip by.

His enthusiasm crumpled under the weight of his own naivety. Perhaps taking pity on Subaru, frustrated at watching what should have been a ray of hope recede further away, the man said "Well, now," thoughtfully touching his hawk-like nose.

"And on top of that, there's some shady rumors flying around Mathers territory right now. Even if the money to be made in the capital weren't a factor, hardly anyone'd want to go there."

"Shady rumors... don't tell me that's Royal Selection stuff too?"

"Probably just baseless gossip, but — a half-demon's a candidate, and someone's backing her, or something like that... Everything about the Royal Selection's still just rumors flying around half-cocked. You know anything about it?"

"...Nah, I don't really know either."

Subaru lied on the spot, deflecting the man's question. Perhaps not expecting much of an answer anyway, the man took it in with a "That so," not particularly disappointed.
Watching this out of the corner of his eye, Subaru felt more keenly than he'd expected just how thoroughly the circumstances surrounding Emilia had spiraled into a vicious cycle.

And just as he was starting to grow sick of the sheer solidity of the obstacles blocking both his own path and Emilia's, the man suddenly clapped his hands and said, "Oh, right—"

"Hey, cheer up. I just thought of someone who might actually go for your offer."

"—! Seriously!? I was just about ready to give up, staring off with dead eyes over here!"

"Not sure what you're talking about, but yeah, I'm serious. I'll go get him."

Saying so, the man clapped Subaru's shoulder casually and trotted off down the road. Subaru followed him toward a shop just barely visible in the distance, and looking up at its sign as the man ducked inside, he muttered,

"...Some kind of tavern-ish lettering, maybe?"

He wasn't entirely confident, but part of the characters carved there used the Ro-script he'd only just started learning, and he could barely make it out. What's more, a faint boozy air was leaking out from beyond the double doors — the kind that made him question the character of anyone holed up in there at this hour, with the sun still high.

Since the man had run in so eagerly, the person he wanted had to be inside.

"What do I do if he drags out some hardcore alcoholic... I wonder if it's even okay to drive a Dragon Carriage drunk in this world."

Were there things like jail time or license revocation for driving under the influence here? Then again, maybe the concept of a Dragon Carriage license didn't even exist.
If some scary, booze-soaked, self-employed type with a name ending in "the Ripper" showed up, Subaru quietly resolved to just scatter some gold coins and make a run for it.
And just as he steeled himself for that grim resolution, the door swung open in front of him.

"What, I told you to wait, and you came over here anyway?"

The gaunt man laughed, looking somewhat deflated as he said it. Still laughing, he reached his arm into the shop — apparently pulling someone out by their clothes.
Peering into the shop as if trying to get a look at whoever it was, Subaru said,

"It'd be super rude of me to make the guy come all the way out here when I'm the one asking the favor. ...So, that's him?"

"Oh, yeah, yeah. This is the guy. Come on, Otto, say hi."

Dragged out by his arm, roughly pulled forward by the man, the person who stepped in front of Subaru was a young man with gray hair.
He was slightly shorter than Subaru, slender-built. His age looked to be a bit above Subaru's own — somewhere around twenty, at a guess. If he straightened his posture and composed his expression, his fine, delicate features would probably pass for a handsome young man. But—

"I'm Natsuki Subaru. Sorry for dragging you out here against your will. Word is you might actually take me up on my request... gah! Booze! You reek of booze! Ugh, I'm getting drunk just from the smell!"

Subaru, trying to open negotiations amicably, was instantly knocked out by the stench wafting toward him. The thick reek of alcohol, strong enough to make his stomach lurch, was coming off the gloomy-faced young man in front of him.
Just as expected — no, even more drunk than expected, by the look of it. Sure enough, the young man staggered the mere few steps between them like he was navigating a tightrope, and somehow managing to plant himself in front of Subaru, said,

"I'm hooome, pleashed to be introdushed, I'm Otto... uh, if you'd — *urp* — pardon me. Urp. *hic*. Urp."

He retched three times in the span of a short greeting.
The young man who called himself Otto, his flushed, drink-sodden face utterly at odds with his fine features, looked back and forth between Subaru and the other man, and said,

"So, um, what was it again? A business deal? Was it a business deal? *urp* Me, doing business talk, *urp*, ahaha, *urp*. Isn't that jusht — hilarious, *urrrp*."

Otto finally crouched down, laughing for reasons Subaru couldn't fathom.
Feeling his hope completely collapse at the sight, Subaru turned a resentful gaze on the man who'd made the introduction. Sensing this, the man raised his hands in a fluster and said,

"Wait, wait, wait, I wasn't tricking you."

"If you're seriously introducing me to this guy, I've gotta question how your head works. This isn't even 'arrested for drunk driving' territory. In this state he'd get stopped by the cops just for walking."

He'd been introduced to a man who wasn't just tipsy but utterly plastered, dead drunk on a whole different level.
Perhaps deciding Subaru's suspicion was only natural, the glared-at man roughly shook the crouching Otto's shoulder.

"Otto! Oi, wake up, you idiot! You're the one who said to introduce you if there was a shot to turn things around! You gonna blow it drinking!?"

"A shot to turn things around—!?"

Something flickered, and the eyes that had until then looked utterly dead came alive. Otto shook off the hand gripping his shoulder and rose to his feet with a motion that made his previous drunken stupor look like a lie.

"My deepest apologies. My name is Otto Suwen. A humble merchant who makes his living peddling goods."

Otto struck a pose sharp enough to make a *click* sound.
Subaru had no words for the sheer transformation. Otto looked Subaru up and down thoroughly, breathed out a "Hmm," and said,

"I see, so your standing is at least somewhat assured. This could indeed make for a valuable customer. Thank you, Ketty-san."

"Yeah, yeah. So, looks like this'll work out, huh? Well then, I'll be taking my leave. If you see me around somewhere, kid, give me a discount. And Otto, pay me back what you owe, next time."

Once Otto had displayed a demeanor sufficient to clear away any doubt, the man made a show of sighing in relief, patting his chest, raised a hand toward them, and walked off.
Subaru called out his thanks after the man's retreating back, and once he'd seen him off, turned back to the young man. Facing him now, Otto — sizing Subaru up — showed not a trace of the drunken stupor from moments before.

"Now then, shall we begin our negotiations? — What is it you're looking for, customer?"

Clapping his hands and holding up a finger, Otto put on a full smile and made his pitch.


※※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※


"I don't mind taking the job, yes."

When Subaru finished explaining the circumstances briefly, glossing over quite a bit, that was the single line Otto offered in response.

Subaru, who'd been introduced by the merchant called Ketty and had expected, at best, that he might not be flatly refused, was in a sense stunned by this ready acceptance.
But the moment the meaning of the answer sank in, Subaru immediately seized both of Otto's hands and gripped them with all his strength.

"Th-thank you! Oh man, you'll really do it! You're a lifesaver! Seriously, a lifesaver!"

"Ow! Ow ow ow! Wai— that's strong! P-please wait! I'm glad you're pleased, but I do have conditions on my end!"

As his arms were flung up and down, Otto raised his voice and wrenched himself free. Opening and closing his now-empty hands, Subaru tilted his head. "Conditions?"

Otto shook his freed hands lightly and, frowning faintly, said "Yes—"

"Since this would mean using my Dragon Carriage — my tool of trade, or rather, my lifeline — I can't simply take this on. Not even at the price a proper rental service would charge. Especially with all the uncertainty surrounding Mathers territory right now."

"Of course, that's only natural. I can't quite say I'll pay whatever you ask, though."

Glancing down at the bag in his hand, Subaru quailed, unable to imagine what sum Otto might propose. At the very least, if the amount demanded exceeded what remained in Subaru's possession, that would be an instant dealbreaker. Even short of that, he couldn't casually agree to just any conditions Otto might attach.

Seeing Subaru's wariness plainly on display, Otto closed one eye and said,

"Let's see. In that case — how about all the money you have on you?"

He raised both hands and shrugged, delivering the line as if joking around.
This, presumably, was his opening move in negotiations — a bit of groundwork laid, so to speak. From his gleaming eyes, one could sense his intent to seize the pace from here and steer the rest of the conversation.

Mouth against mouth, tongue against tongue. A battle of negotiation, pitting rhetoric against rhetoric, was about to begin—

"That work? Got it. Here, I'll leave this bag with you. Can you head out right away?"

That was not, in fact, how it went.

At Subaru's readily proffering the bag in his hand, Otto, dumbfounded, found himself reflexively accepting it. Feeling its considerable weight in both arms, he swallowed hard and turned trembling eyes on Subaru.

"W-wait, that's not how this goes!? Normally, this is where we'd start haggling back and forth to bring our demands closer together!? You can't just hand it over like that—"

"It'd be a waste of time, and I'd lose the haggling anyway. So there's no point fighting a fight I'd only lose, and if what's in that bag covers it, that's exactly what I was hoping for."

At worst, he might have lost everything in that bag and still failed to accomplish his goal. Given that, spending everything he had to buy passage was a bargain.
At Subaru's decision, made on that reasoning, Otto looked exasperated and pressed a hand to his forehead.

"This is... could it be that I've been introduced to an extremely troublesome customer...?"

"Relax. Personally, I don't intend to cause anyone any trouble. In spirit, anyway."

That this statement carried not one shred of persuasive force was something even Subaru himself understood — to say nothing of Otto, whose acquaintance with him was far too brief to know any better.

"Understood. I set my terms for taking the job, and you accepted on the spot. I have my pride as a merchant too. However paltry the sum, I'll see it through properly and— urgh!? Wh-what is this fortune!? Handing over this much so easily, what are you even—*urp*"

Ignoring Otto, who retched as if suddenly nauseated upon checking the contents of the bag, Subaru clenched his fist before the opportunity he'd finally managed to seize.
Various obstacles stood in Subaru's way, but he could somehow overcome every one of them. For now, he still didn't know what stood in Emilia's way — but surely he'd understand once he stood at her side. And surely, he could do something about it.

"Wait for me. Soon... it'll be soon."

Clearly, the corner of Subaru's mouth twisted into a smile.
Whether it rose because his goal was within reach, or because he'd glimpsed a vision of the beloved girl — even he himself, unaware he was smiling at all, couldn't say.


※※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※


"Now then, let's confirm the terms of the contract. Destination: Mathers territory, the domain of Margrave Roswaal L. Mathers. Time: to be shortened as much as possible, with a condition of pushing through even at night... The reward's a reward, so I'll accept it, but these are some reckless terms, you know."

"Don't complain, you were the one whose eyes changed color at the sight of gold coins. I'm counting on you. My whole future's riding on this."

"I feel like my future's simultaneously riding on quite a lot of things too, you know."

As he spoke, at the direction of Otto's hands working the reins, the whinnying Earth Dragons kicked off the ground and surged forward.
The Dragon Carriage Otto owned had a large, covered cargo bed, and the Earth Dragons pulling it had correspondingly powerful, massive builds. Their appearance leaned heavily toward the heavyweight side, and Subaru had worried about their speed, but—

"That's exactly why they have such stamina. Even among long-distance breeds, this one's especially known for endurance. It won't wear out even running three days straight."

"If you run it for three whole days, I think the person riding along would wear out before the dragon does."

"I once drove one myself, about two years back, trying not to miss a business opportunity. A person can manage surprisingly well when they're desperate enough. I collapsed the moment the deal was done, though, and spent about a week hovering between life and death after that."

"Desperate enough, huh."

Glancing sideways at Otto's face, Subaru caught him noticing the look and turning eyes back that seemed to ask, "Something wrong?" Subaru waved a hand in response to the silent question and, looking down at the running Earth Dragons to change the subject, said,

"Anyway, so that means we don't need to swap these guys out along the way. So, we can just pass right through this Hanumas village?"

"That's right. As a relay point, Hanumas is better equipped than Fleur, but if you've got enough water and food loaded and your own Dragon Carriage, there's no value in stopping there. And since the request is for express speed, I'll have us pass straight through."

Despite this being, in terms of planning, a purely spur-of-the-moment venture, Otto's orderly explanation contained not the faintest hint of uncertainty.
To him, this was likely just one more stretch of road he'd traveled countless times before. He couldn't be much older than Subaru, yet there was a presence about him, ill-suited to his refined looks, that seemed almost seasoned.
And yet, despite being such a person, he'd granted Subaru's reckless request. Of course, the reward played its part, but Subaru found himself curious about something else.

"Hey, why'd you actually agree to my request? The fact that you were that drunk is kind of a mystery in itself."

"Y-you really do just come right out and ask the hard questions, Natsuki-san."

Otto's face twisted into an expression too bitter to be called a wry smile.
Subaru straightened up in front of this rare person who addressed him by surname since arriving in this world. Realizing, not for the first time in a while, just how low his own conversational tact was for blurting out such a blunt question, he said,

"Well, what's asked is asked, nothing to be done about it now. So you might as well just come clean."

"You put that so simply. ...Well, to match that, I'll put it simply too — I made a bit of a blunder due to some crossed information."

Sighing at Subaru's rather brazen attitude, Otto haltingly began explaining the cause of his disgraceful state at the tavern.
At the word "blunder," Subaru raised an eyebrow, and Otto turned his head toward the cargo bed loaded with goods.

"In that cargo bed back there, packed tight, is this trip's merchandise... what do you think it is?"

"...At a glance, looks like a bunch of jars lined up. Were you planning to peddle pottery or something?"

"Wrong, I'm afraid. The merchandise isn't the container — it's what's inside. Those jars are packed full of oil. Originally, I meant to carry it north to Gusteko, but..."

Shrugging, Otto laughed, mocking himself for having his expectations dashed.

"The Royal Selection, no doubt. Passage to Gusteko, up north, has been temporarily shut down. By the time I realized it was unsellable, I fought tooth and nail to get through anyway... but, well, that came to nothing, and I was turned back."

What was supposed to have converted into a fortune in the frigid cold of Gusteko had been rejected outright, leaving him with no choice but to somehow dispose of it within Lugunica itself. To make matters worse, what Otto had traded for in order to acquire the oil in the first place was a whole stock of iron goods that had, until recently, been fetching next to nothing — talk about adding insult to injury.
In the end, he'd missed the chance to sell iron goods at a killing, and lost the market to sell off the oil he'd gotten in exchange, leaving him at his wit's end and drowning his sorrows in drink.

"Even in the royal capital, this much oil isn't going to sell out easily... and if I try selling it dirt cheap, that's my ruin right there. Just as I was thinking my career as a merchant was over, more or less throwing my life away, along came your proposal, Natsuki-san."

"With that money, can you actually make up for the loss somehow?"

"Buying up all this oil at its proper market price would leave me with change to spare. This has, one way or another, saved my neck. Thank you, thank you so much."

At Otto bowing his head, practically venerating him, Subaru waved a hand and said, "Cut it out." He was just as grateful to Otto, if not more so — he was half-convinced his own gratitude outmatched Otto's.
For a while, the two of them carried on with a farce of "It's all thanks to you," "No, no, it's thanks to you," "I'm here because you were," "Yes, we were fated to meet like this" — deepening their mutual sympathy as the time passed.

Once that conversation reached a natural pause and silence suddenly fell between them, Subaru happened to look away from the road they were traveling and narrow his eyes at the plain stretching on and on, and muttered,

"Hey, Otto. Couldn't we just cut straight across this plain?"

Catching this mutter of Subaru's, Otto broke into laughter as though he'd heard the finest joke, slapping his knee. "Oh, come now—"

"That's no laughing matter, however you put it. When the 'Mist' settles over the plain, the White Whale appears there. It's one of the most notorious of the Witchbeasts... run into it, and you're dead."

"That dangerous, huh? Hasn't anyone tried to hunt it down?"

"As long as you avoid the 'Mist,' the damage stays minimal enough. There used to be subjugation expeditions organized, I hear, but the fact that the White Whale's still alive and well today tells you how those went."

In other words, the subjugation had failed, and the resulting losses had been severe enough to discourage any further expeditions since.
Listening to this, Subaru felt a complicated pang at the word "Witchbeast." To Subaru, a Witchbeast meant the pack of Juggernauts that had lived near Roswaal Manor.
As a result, all the nearby dens had been burned out, the creatures wiped out entirely — and Subaru had felt there was something in that ending deserving of some sympathy. Of course, having also nearly been killed by them himself, there was no denying they were creatures whose nature made coexistence all but impossible.

"White Whale... huh. Is it shaped like an actual white whale?"

"From what the handful of survivors who've seen it say, it's so enormous they still don't really know what they saw. Something about everyone around them getting crushed one after another while they threw away everything they had, desperate just to escape with their life."

"A terrifying tale," Otto said with a long sigh, seeming reluctant to discuss the White Whale any further.
For someone in his position, traveling from place to place as a merchant, a White Whale that could occupy a plain for days on end, forcing detours, was surely an object of loathsome hatred.

If it got hunted down, that'd be a relief, but he wanted nothing to do with it himself — such was the sentiment Subaru could read from his attitude, and Subaru let the matter drop there.
Either way, without Otto's cooperation, Subaru had no way of making it back to the territory. He couldn't afford to sour the mood over some strange topic and end up on bad terms with him.

Quietly, with eyes suddenly drained of emotion, Subaru watched him — but Otto, his attention on managing the Earth Dragons, never noticed.
Subaru himself, too, had no way of knowing what light was flickering in his own eyes just then.
Events crept forward slowly, but surely, showing signs of distortion as they went.


— And then, as a result of a forced march that pressed on without rest even through the night.

"There it is. Once we clear that hill, we'll be in Mathers territory."

Roughly half a day and three hours since they'd set out together.
It was early morning, when the sun that had set since their departure had risen again and lit the world bright, that Subaru and his companion arrived at their destination: Mathers territory.

"We kept going nonstop without a break... I might've actually passed Rem."

"Well now, catching up to someone who set out more than half a day ahead is asking a lot, even so. More importantly, where in Mathers territory are we headed—?"

"Ah, the manor of the lord, Margrave Roswaal. You know the place?"

"Roughly. Well, leave it to me."

Answering Subaru in a reassuring voice, Otto's hands moved briskly on the reins.
Unlike Subaru, who'd managed a few bouts of shallow sleep along the way, Otto — who'd had no chance to rest while driving — was practically running on a full night without sleep, yet his answer carried a strength that betrayed no fatigue.

"Hahahahaha! Onward! Onward we go! I can see a shining road stretching before me now!"

If anything, seeing him blurt out lines with his tension pushed clean over the edge like that made him look almost endearingly like someone riding pure all-nighter adrenaline, which was oddly charming in its own way.

In any case, setting his companion's state aside, Subaru thought of the manor drawing ever closer, and let himself picture, for the first time, the reunion scene he'd avoided imagining until now.
He doubted he'd be welcomed easily. Subaru, who was supposed to have been left behind in the royal capital, was coming back with his treatment only half finished. He had to brace himself even for being cursed at.
And still—

"I came back to do what I need to do. There's nothing to be ashamed of. Yeah, nothing at all."

As if justifying his own actions, or perhaps making excuses to someone who wasn't even here, Subaru murmured, over and over, the magic words that had kept propping up his resolve all this while.
Saying them let him temporarily drive off the anxiety swelling up enough to crush his chest. So Subaru kept repeating that phrase.

Neglecting several other words he should have remembered, Subaru kept propping up a self that seemed ready to crumble into pieces, using words like these.

That resolve of Subaru's, backed only by such borrowed confidence, was—

"Natsuki-san... would you forgive me if I stopped here?"

— cut off at the outset, when Otto, after driving for some time through a forested area as the manor drew near, suddenly brought the Dragon Carriage to a halt.


※※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※


"What's that supposed to mean? Don't tell me you're gonna say the contract's over now that we've entered Mathers territory. That's not quite fraud, but it's a fraud-adjacent move, isn't it?"

From the driver's seat of the now-stationary carriage, Subaru grabbed at Otto, who had suddenly refused to go any further. Seizing him by the collar and yanking him close, Subaru was about to hurl even fouler words at his face — but swallowed them at the sight of Otto's ashen complexion.

Released from his collar, Otto settled back into the driver's seat and silently straightened his rumpled clothes. Then he bowed deeply to Subaru.

"I'm terribly sorry. I meant to see you through to the very end. And yet, I find I no longer have the courage to go any further."

"What are you even talking about? Courage has nothing to do with it. We're almost at the manor. It's not like the road's bad. C'mon, Otto."

"Even if you ask, I can't do it. I won't take the whole reward either. I'll return half. So please, let me turn back here."

To Subaru, who was still trying to push the conversation along lightly, Otto pressed his hands to the driver's seat, bowed his head, and pleaded in earnest for forgiveness.
The sheer desperation of his posture, too pitiful for words, made Subaru catch his breath despite himself.

"What suddenly brought this on? What happened to make you—"

"The Earth Dragons... they're terrified. This has never happened before, not once! This is exactly why traveling merchants use dragons for transport. The Earth Dragons know — they know we mustn't go any closer...!"

His hand trembling faintly on the reins, his face pale, Otto looked down at the Earth Dragons. Sure enough, the dragons, feet planted on the ground, awaiting their master's command, kept up a quiet, ragged breathing. But they kept snorting toward the direction of travel — toward the destination — shifting restlessly as if warning of danger, urging heightened caution.

From the dragons' behavior, and from Otto's reaction of trusting them completely, Subaru understood.

Some threat beyond imagining lay waiting ahead.
And he couldn't drag Otto and the others into it with him. Subaru alone, facing it by himself — there was no other option left.

"Thanks for everything. Sorry for scaring you, Otto."

"Eh?"

Hearing a startled cry at his back, Subaru leapt from the driver's seat down to the ground as if flying. Landing right beside the Earth Dragons, he lightly slapped his numb legs and looked up at Otto.

"I'm going on foot from here to the manor. It's not that far. You've done more than enough just bringing me this far. Keep all the money."

"That's not— no, more importantly, Natsuki-san! You mustn't go! Let's turn back together. There's mist settling in right now!"

"You're saying the White Whale's coming out?"

"I mean it as an ill omen for traveling merchants! Mist settling over our destination is a matter of life and death for us... but never mind that, please reconsider—"

"Sorry, but—"

Watching Otto desperately calling out, worried for Subaru's safety, Subaru gave a wry smile. With that good a nature, wasn't he poorly suited to a trade built on mutual deception and scheming?
Wondering, in spite of himself, whether Otto was even cut out for this profession, Subaru raised a hand, turned his back on the carriage and its driver, and said,

"Same way you're weighing your life and your money on an important scale, I've got something just as important as my life on my own scale. And that important thing is up ahead."

"Natsuki-san!"

"I don't think it's wrong of you to turn back. If anything, turning back once you realize it's dangerous is the right call. Just knowing that ahead of time is more than enough for me."

Somewhere along this road, at the destination Subaru was making for, some danger fearsome enough to frighten even the dragons awaited.
He had to hurry. He had to rush there. Because that was surely where the answer he sought was waiting.

"— Natsuki-san!"

Leaving behind Otto, who called his name in worry for him right up to the end, Subaru kicked hard against the ground of the deep-green forest and ran.
He ran, and ran, and kept running, leaving behind — without any reservation — a person who had genuinely worried for him, and pressed on toward his destination.


He had no clear sense of the distance remaining to Roswaal's manor.
Still, the cluster of trees he recognized was, without a doubt, part of the mountains that surrounded the manor. The road clearly showed the marks of Dragon Carriages passing through, and following it would surely lead him to the manor without fail.
Before that, the nearest village — right by the manor, the village Subaru had visited so many times — should have been there to greet him, but he felt no need right now to turn his attention there.

In any case, he wanted to reach the manor as quickly as possible.
If he did, it felt like the unresolved feelings tormenting him now would find some kind of resolution. Whether it came in the form he wanted or not, he wanted, for now, some kind of change.

He thought of Emilia. He thought of Rem. He thought of Emilia. He thought of Ram. He thought of Emilia. He thought of Beatrice. He thought of Emilia. He thought of Roswaal. He thought of Emilia. He thought of Puck. He thought of Emilia. He thought of the villagers. He thought of Emilia. Emilia, Emilia, Emilia, Emilia, Emilia—.

Subaru kept up his headlong dash — too full of stray thoughts to call it truly single-minded.
What finally stopped Subaru's feet wasn't arriving at his destination.

What stopped his running feet was a sense of wrongness that suddenly crossed his mind.
Even to Subaru, who'd been so absorbed in running that he'd ignored any changes in his surroundings, the abnormality was unmistakably clear — namely,

"It's... too quiet, isn't it."

Aside from the sound of the wind passing by, every sound had vanished from the world entirely.
Looking around, all he saw was a stand of trees, nothing actively making noise. But Subaru, having spent nearly a month in this place, knew.

He knew the abnormality of this forest's overwhelming stillness — not even the cry of an insect to be heard.

— And then, it appeared, slipping through a gap in Subaru's awareness, all at once.

"Wh—ah!?"

Crying out in shock, Subaru instinctively stumbled backward.
His face drained of color, unable to accept the reality before him — there, standing quietly, was a person of unknown identity, entirely swathed in black.
But his astonishment didn't stop there.

"This guy... no, these guys...!"

Turning his head, forming words with trembling lips, Subaru's gaze was followed, one after another, by black shadows sprouting up like plants at every point along the forest path.
Their numbers exceeded ten in the blink of an eye, and Subaru found himself surrounded on all sides, on guard.
What was even more abnormal was that, despite their inexplicable appearance, the same endless silence as before continued unbroken.

Without so much as the faint sound of breathing, the black-clad figures observed Subaru in silence.
Sensing only, viscerally, that these were by no means friendly presences, Subaru could say nothing, and could only wait, frozen, for whatever came next.

How long did that standoff continue?
The tension had stretched so unbearably taut that Subaru felt the flow of time grow horribly ambiguous.
His heart should have been pounding painfully, yet the silence was so violently absolute that he couldn't even hear his own heartbeat — and then, that balance collapsed, every bit as abruptly as it had begun.

And that was—

"————"

All at once, the black-clad figures bowed their heads reverently toward Subaru.


— Subaru's mind gave up entirely on making sense of the sight.

People who had appeared for reasons beyond comprehension paid him a respect beyond comprehension, and then, leaving behind an utterly bewildered Subaru, vanished just as incomprehensibly as they'd come.

Unable to utter a single word, left with nothing to do but gape in stunned silence at the scene before him, Subaru watched as the black-clad figures raised their heads and began to move, gliding smoothly.
They passed by the frozen Subaru without so much as a glance, and vanished from the edge of his vision with the same soundless gait they'd appeared with.

They had probably appeared, too, slipping through some gap in his awareness in just that same way. Understanding that much was all Subaru could manage — everything else about them remained wholly incomprehensible.
And so, after standing frozen on the spot for several minutes, once Subaru realized he'd been left completely alone, he gave up trying to understand them any further and took off running again.

Their presence had scraped, most unpleasantly, at the anxiety already gnawing at Subaru.
As if to shake off the chill and unease crawling up his spine, Subaru raced back down the forest path again, single-mindedly, trying to forget everything.

Subaru gave up trying to understand the black-clad figures whose very existence defied comprehension.
And so, he failed to notice something.

The direction those figures had glided off toward, vanishing from sight, was the very direction where Otto — left behind — should have been.
And Subaru never once looked back to consider that fact.
