Arc 7 Chapter 44: "Shrinking Field of Vision"

—An incomprehensible situation trampled through Natsuki Subaru's shrunken brain.

It was a shock severe enough to justify putting it that way.
Without any awareness of 'Death,' Subaru had come back right in the middle of hammering out the rules with Olbart—whether he wanted to admit it or not, there was no other conclusion to draw.

He had lost his life, and in this very moment, 'Returned by Death.'

And what's more—

"...during a game of hide-and-seek where dying wasn't even supposed to happen."

Covering his mouth with a small palm, Subaru frantically tried to recall what had happened.
The game of 'Hide-and-Seek' with Olbart had begun, and once they'd overcome the old monster's sheer nastiness, they'd succeeded in finding the shinobi hidden in the first room—'Behind the Eyelid.'

Riding that momentum, Subaru and the others had stepped outside the inn to pin down the old monster's next hiding place. And there, the world had abruptly gone dark.

And by the time he noticed, he had returned to this exact place.

"—"

"—If we're to accept your proposal, we'll need to make the terms explicit."

"Hoh? And what might that mean, eh?"

"You said it yourself. If it benefits us more to win, then we shouldn't leave much room for desperate tricks. Let both our demands be made explicit."

"—. Kah-kah-kah-kah."

While Subaru sat there lost in thought, the negotiation with Olbart pressed on ahead.
The exchange between Abel and Olbart was advancing toward the selection of the method of contest that would form the basis of the rules. Namely—

"—Hide-and-seek."

Abel was just declaring it, flatly.


　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　△▼△▼△▼△


—Afterward, the terms of the 'Hide-and-Seek' exchanged with Olbart played out exactly as Subaru already knew they would. No new condition was added, no necessary content trimmed away. Naturally, it stayed the same. Even so—

"Just to confirm again—Olbart-san's only hiding, right? He's not gonna sneak in some kind of attack on us or anything like that...?"

"Oi oi, now that's an awful lot of worryin', boy. Let me tell ya—what I'm keen on testin' in you lot is how sharp your heads turn, how canny you are. If it were just about brawn, you'd have been done for already, back at the keep yesterday, wouldn't ya?"

"—"

Subaru couldn't take Olbart's reassurance at face value.
As a matter of fact, there was no mistaking that Subaru had lost his life and 'Returned by Death.' And as things stood, the threat closest to them was none other than the Olbart standing right in front of him.

Had he broken the rule against interfering and gone after them himself?

"Well, well, seems I've gone and earned myself quite the suspicion."

"You ain't exactly in a position to be trusted, old man. That'd be pretty rich comin' from you."

"Kah-kah-kah-kah! Can't argue with that. Eight years back, even the fella I got friendly with turned cold on me."

Sensing the strong wariness in Subaru's stony silence, Olbart shrugged. Then, at Al's follow-up jab, he grinned, baring his teeth, and said as much without a trace of shame.
Naturally, Al fell silent too, looking unsatisfied with Olbart's answer.
In any case—

"Olbart, I trust you understand without being told—your hiding place is meaningless if it's somewhere physically unreachable to us. Don't try any clever tricks."

"I know, I know. Fussy lot, ain't ya. Though I'd have done it anyway, even without you sayin' so."

Abel raised the same point Subaru had raised last time. Once Olbart agreed to it, the start of the contest was declared once more.
Namely, the presentation of the hint for the first hiding place.

"So, where do you plan to hide first, old man?"

"First, just a little warm-up... I reckon I'll hide near this inn, in 'Behind the Eyelid.'"

For a moment an unpleasant tension gripped Subaru, but it turned out to be needless worry.
He'd feared Olbart might name a different hiding place than before, but it didn't come to that. Afterward, as Olbart turned his back on them without a shred of concern, Taritta and Al had to swallow the urge to pounce and could only watch him go—that, too, followed the same course as before.
And so—

"What's wrong, bro? You're lookin' pretty pale."

It was Al who spoke up, while Subaru was in the middle of worrying over Taritta's profile—she'd lowered her bow at Abel's order. His words this time followed a slightly different course than before.
Originally, he should have been voicing complaints about going along with Olbart's contest, but this time he seemed concerned about Subaru's complexion—no, about how quiet Subaru had gone.

That was only natural. Subaru himself still hadn't shaken off his confusion and bewilderment.
Not from the sudden 'Return by Death,' nor from the mental shock that came with it.

"No, sorry. In the middle of talking, I just suddenly got hit with this wave of exhaustion... or something."

"Oi oi, pull it together, will ya? Unlike me and Medium-chan, whose abilities got halved from shrinkin', bro's strong point oughta still be intact even shrunk down, right?"

"My strong point...?"

"Of course! The amazing thing about Subaru-chin is how smart he is! Anchan's amazing too, but Subaru-chin's amazing as well, right? Even with itty-bitty boobs now, I'm totally fine!"

Following Al's encouragement, Medium chimed in cheerfully as well.
Still holding Rui, she patted her own now-flat chest, where the fullness had vanished. Subaru's eyes widened at the sight of the two of them, and then he let out a long breath.

They were right, the two of them, trying to cheer him up.
To begin with, whether shrunk or not, there were almost no situations where Subaru was physically useful. Which meant he was the one least affected by shrinking.
And that Subaru had no business dragging around his mental exhaustion forever.

"...So quit lookin' at me like that. If you want the oni mask to do its job properly, the 'perception obstruction' function alone should be enough."

"So some awareness of your surroundings has returned, has it. Still, I confess I'm surprised. I'd taken you for a fool oblivious to this mask's effect."

"...I wouldn't be surprised if you were just wearing that mask out of some weird personal habit, though."

Bolstered by Al and Medium's encouragement, Subaru lifted his head and shot right back at Abel.
Even Abel, who'd been looking down on him with the eyes one gives a useless burden, seemed to notice Subaru had recovered enough spirit to lift his head, and swallowed back any further jabs.
The mere fact that this counted as a concession said something about the deficiencies in Abel's people skills, but there was no room to discuss that at the moment.

"Anyway. He said it was just a warm-up first, but even just 'around the inn' leaves plenty of candidates. Bro, Abel-chan, you two got any kind of plan?"

Looking to move things along, Al turned his focus on Subaru and Abel.
As an answer, what Subaru could offer was Olbart's first hiding place—the fact that the old monster would return to this very room they were talking in right now.
So long as the hint Olbart left behind was the same, there was no need to worry about it changing.
The problem was what came after finding Olbart—when they stepped outside the inn.

"Not exactly a plan, but I can guess where 'Behind the Eyelid' is."

"Eh, seriously? That's amazing, bro."

"...But there's a good chance a problem separate from 'Behind the Eyelid' has come up."

He needed to share, with Abel and Al and the others who knew nothing of it, the threat of some kind that had struck Subaru—no, all of them.
That said, Subaru himself had far too little information to work with, so there wasn't much he could say for certain. If there was one thing he could venture—

"The promise you made with Olbart-san... the one about him not laying a hand on us—is there any chance he'd break it?"

"Ah? You mean, breakin' the whole premise from the start?"

"Yeah, I figure it might be possible..."

"—No. It isn't."

Going off the fact that they'd been attacked the instant they stepped out of the inn, Subaru tried to subtly steer his companions' attention toward Olbart. But Abel shot it down with unexpectedly firm denial.
Subaru's eyes widened at the response, and Abel, folding his thin arms, went on:

"Such an act would gain Olbart nothing. Hence, it is impossible."

"B-but, Olbart-san was even considering assassinating the Emperor! Even you never imagined that at all... so then!"

"Posthumous fame was a notion I lacked, that's all. But I can understand that such a thing exists—that there are those who covet it. However, that and this are separate matters."

Pierced by the sharp gaze behind the oni mask, Subaru felt something in his gut shrink back.
It wasn't as though Abel was giving off a sharper presence than usual—probably. And yet the sensation of being pinned down wouldn't fade, and it threw Subaru's breathing into disarray.
Leaving Subaru to suffer through that sensation, Abel went on, "Listen well."

"As you yourself have seen through, and as the masked fool objected, Olbart Dunkelkenn has no reason to harm us of his own will. If he's after the Emperor's head, then my information is precisely what he'd want so badly his hands would reach for it out of his own throat. There would be no premise left to speak of."

"Ah, I'm with Abel-chan on this one too, bro. That old man's been ordered by the Emperor not to lay a hand on us. If he had a mind to break that, inside or outside the inn wouldn't matter. Right?"

"Um, is... is that how it works...?"

"Bro?"

"Ah! No, yeah, right! Yeah, I think so too. That's true."

Subaru's understanding lagged a few seconds behind Abel and Al's back-to-back explanations.
But once laid out logically like that, it was something even Subaru could nod along to.

Abel and the others didn't know it, but Olbart had an actual track record of killing Subaru and the others.
But within Olbart's own logic, it had been a consistent act, and that logic itself was something Subaru could accept. In that situation, the only thing at fault had been the timing, nothing else.
Following that, this time—the sequence where they'd been killed the instant they left the inn—was indeed a strange development by Olbart's own rules.

As Al said, if he'd intended to kill them regardless, killing them before they left the inn would have drawn less attention. Perhaps he'd had some intention of killing them somewhere visible—but that made no sense either.
Perhaps he'd thought that killing them while they felt safe, believing they wouldn't be killed, would shock them more, or make them suffer more.

"If that were the case, he'd show his face properly, wouldn't he."

If Olbart, out of sheer nastiness, had been aiming to make their moods swing wildly, wouldn't he want to watch their disappointment and despair with his own eyes?
Without that, Subaru couldn't think of a reason for Olbart to kill them outside the inn.
In other words—

"What does that even mean...?"

"Hey, hey, Subaru-chin, what are you worried about? Worried about outside?"

"Huh?"

Impatient as he tried to sort through the situation, Subaru had fallen silent, and Medium peered into his face.
With her round blue eyes right in front of him, Subaru let out an involuntary "whoa" and reeled back. At his reaction, Medium went "wao" and reflexively grabbed his hand.

And then—

"There, there, Subaru-chin, calm down, calm down."

"—Ah."

With that, Medium pulled him in by the hand she'd grabbed and pressed Subaru's head to her chest.
As his back was slowly stroked, his forehead and cheek felt the beat of her heart. The rhythm of her pulse was steady and even, gently breaking apart Subaru's rigid, frozen mind.

"When my head gets all jumbled up too, I get Anchan to do this for me~. Anchan said someone did it for him a long time ago, too."

"...It feels... calming."

"Mm-hm, good! Okay, you can stay just like that, so tell me? What's Subaru-chin worried about outside?"

Medium's voice, falling from right above him, wasn't rushing Subaru for an answer.
As is, he wanted to keep leaning on Medium's warmth and generosity, unchanged even now that she'd grown younger, but Subaru also understood that time wouldn't allow it.
So as a compromise, still held with his head against her, he began working through his thoughts haltingly.

"It's just, um... I have a feeling... that it's dangerous outside the inn..."

"A dangerous situation outside?"

"Yeah, that's right. Someone's... targeting us, I think..."

Nodding along, Medium listened to Subaru's far-too-vague opinion. Honestly, even Subaru himself could tell his statement lacked both persuasiveness and grounds.
If he couldn't manage a better explanation than that, he'd never win over Abel or Al, Medium aside. For instance—

"Actually, if we step outside the inn, we'll die there—"

—In that instant, the world went still.

"—"

The clear, audible beat of Medium's pulse vanished into the far reaches of eternity, and her face, so close before, and her breathing, receded beyond reach.

Everything was far away.

Color was lost, sound was lost, the flow of time was lost, the freedom to so much as stir was lost.
He couldn't move. He didn't move. He wasn't allowed to move. He couldn't be moved.

And then, at the edge of Subaru's consciousness—where even his voice, his breath, even his eyes wouldn't obey him—something ghastly, terrifying, something dreadful, came slowly creeping closer.

Why did you touch the forbidden thing, a black shadow crept nearer, as if lamenting.
Why did you forget this, slender fingers the color of darkness slid smoothly into his chest.
Why do you try to repeat it, over and over, came the voice of the 'Witch,' painting over everything.

'—I love you.'

That single voice, heard after so very, very long, dragged Subaru down into hell.
His heart was seized, and a ferocious, searing pain tore his immobile body to shreds. Tormenting it. Trampling it. Violating it. —Branding into him: never forget again.

And then—

"—!"

"Subaru-chin?"

Suddenly, sound and color and the flow of time came rushing back, and he felt a furious rush of blood through his entire body.
But what his head, pressed against Medium's chest, picked up was not her soft heartbeat, but the resumption of Subaru's own bloodflow, and his own abnormal, racing pulse, jolted by fright.

So overwhelming was the 'Witch's' fury at his touching the forbidden that it stole his voice, ground his understanding to dust, and violated his very soul completely—and Subaru cursed himself.
Why had he done such a thing, walking headlong into that much pain, that much suffering, of his own accord.

He could never, ever confess 'Return by Death' to another.
He couldn't even let slip something that might lead someone to infer it.
If he tried to convey that intent to anyone, that dark-colored demonic hand—the 'Witch of Envy'—would overcome any hardship at all to reach Subaru's heart.
Let alone—

"That was... close..."

Murmuring that, Subaru confirmed the presence of Medium, who was holding him, and of Abel, Al, Taritta, and Rui around her, each in turn.
The hot wetness welling up at the corners of his eyes was relief that none of them had been harmed. —This confession of 'Return by Death' carried the risk Subaru feared most of all.

Namely, the possibility that the demonic hand would go after someone other than Subaru—and unlike its threats against Subaru himself, would not loosen its grip until it had snuffed out that person's life.

If that possibility never came to pass, and Subaru alone had suffered, that was the next-best outcome.
Of course, there was no denying the best outcome would have been for Subaru not to suffer at all, either.

"Medium-san... thank you. I'm fine now."

"Really? You look even more like you're in pain than before, though..."

"If it's just me hurting, that's the best-case scenario, given the situation."

Saying so, Subaru lifted his head and freed himself from Medium's embrace. Medium's face showed some reluctance, but against Subaru's firm refusal, she couldn't say anything more.
He was grateful she worried about him, but the situation wouldn't allow for it. And without exaggeration, it was a plain fact that Medium had helped him, in part.

"Sorry for putting it in such a weird, worrying way. But I really mean it when I say I feel like outside is dangerous. So..."

"Don't go outside, is that it? But then there's no contest with Olbart to speak of. Of course, the option of not accepting his contest in the first place does exist."

"—"

"In that case, negotiations with Yorna Mishigure would have no choice but to fall through as well. This journey, in the end, would amount to nothing more than a long trip out that left you, the fool, and Medium shrunken."

"Well, I guess there might be folks out there who'd spend a whole lifetime wishin' for a result like gettin' younger."

Against Abel's cold-blooded remark, Al offered a half-hearted quip to soften it. But Al's point had already been flatly denied by something Olbart had said.
Though the details were unclear, it seemed the 'Infantilization' that had struck Subaru and the others carried some kind of drawback. It probably meant this was different from simply growing younger.
In the end, this 'Infantilization' was, without question, a bomb of its own.

"Either way, we can't stay shrunk forever. Since we didn't go with surroundin' and beatin' down that old man, we've got no choice but to win at 'Hide-and-Seek.'"

"I know. I'm not giving up on the contest. It's just, outside is really dangerous. So..."

"So?"

"So, um, if we go outside, I want us to be careful. And even if I say something really weird, I want you to believe me."

"—"

Subaru made his appeal head-on, looking up at the other with upturned eyes.
At that blunt, straightforward statement, Subaru could tell Abel had narrowed his eyes behind the oni mask. Al, too, seemed subtly at a loss.
Certainly, it lacked persuasive power, and lacked any real attempt at trial-and-error persuasion, too.

"But—there is some truth in what Subaru says, too. We have fallen into the enemy's trap, and this place, it should be called enemy territory. So, raising our guard, it costs us nothing."

"That's right, that's right, I agree with Taritta-chan too! Let's be careful! If we get any smaller than this, I won't be able to hold Rui-chan back."

"Auu—"

But Taritta and Medium sided with Subaru's opinion just then. What Rui, who'd spoken up last, had meant by it was unclear, but it didn't seem to carry any hostility toward Subaru.
Taritta and the others weren't saying anything complicated by agreeing, either. They were simply raising their own awareness—don't let your guard down, since carelessness is the enemy.
Subaru couldn't think of any reason for Abel to balk at that, other than simply disliking Subaru.

"If... if you don't like me for it, then, um, I'll show you proof."

"Proof, you say?"

"Proof that my thinking shouldn't be ignored. About Olbart-san's location—I'll tell you where 'Behind the Eyelid,' where he's hiding, is."

"You think that counts as a bargaining chip? Either way, you have no choice but to reveal it. Concealing it would disadvantage you all the same."

"Ugh..."

But Abel was a tough opponent, countering Subaru's argument just like that. Subaru groaned, unable to find a comeback. Al cut in with a "now, now," saying:

"Abel-chan, bro's sayin' he'll hand over material to help you judge. Honestly, bro's claim came outta nowhere so sudden, question marks are still poppin' outta my head too. But bro comin' out with wild stuff ain't exactly somethin' new. Right?"

"—"

"If you won't listen to bro's opinion here, there's no point draggin' him along. And if you're gonna treat bro like he don't matter, I ain't gonna feel too good about it either."

"Hoh."

Shifting his position as if to shield Subaru behind him, Al's voice dropped low as he faced Abel.
It was only as low as a boy's voice could go, but even so, it was enough to change the air in the room. Abel's gaze, looking down at him, turned cold as well, and Subaru couldn't help but hold his breath.

He hadn't paid it much mind until now, but the relationship between Abel and Al was far thinner than Subaru had thought.
To begin with, Al had come along on this journey to the Magic City intending to accompany Subaru. And being, like Subaru, a person of the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica, he held no particular stake in Abel's restoration to the throne.
If anything, it was only that Al's mistress, Priscilla, wished for it.

So Al, personally, had no reason to back either Abel's goal or Abel himself.
And now, here, that had surfaced as a clear crack in their relationship.

Physically, Al had shrunk to around ten years old—but could Abel actually beat him like this? With Al's abilities in this state unknown, there was nothing to gain from letting this standoff drag on.
So—

"Stop fighting! Fine, I lose! I surrender!"

Circling around to stand even in front of Al, who'd been shielding him, Subaru made his appeal loudly.
Nothing good would come of allies quarreling here. Rather than let that unproductive standoff continue, it was far better for Subaru to play the villain.

Besides, either way, he'd managed to raise the alarm.
Taritta, Medium, and even Al should all have taken Subaru's plea to heart. When they left the inn, they'd surely be plenty careful.
The problem was if 'Death' came at them in a form that vigilance alone wouldn't be enough for.
In that case, there'd be nothing left but for Subaru to put his body on the line and do whatever he could.

"We can't be dawdling around! Everyone, let's get ready!"

"—. So then. Where do you think 'Behind the Eyelid' is?"

"That would be..."

Subaru insisted, above all, that they avoid wasting time here. At his words, Abel finally showed a positive response.
That such a small thing brought relief spoke volumes about the deficiencies in His Imperial Majesty's interpersonal skills.
In any case, Subaru decided to answer honestly.

Olbart's first hiding place—it was—


　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　△▼△▼△▼△


"Next time I'll pick a hidin' spot with a bit more bite to it!"

"Wait, old man! ...Damn it, he's already gone!"

Throwing the room's window wide open, Olbart leapt out of the inn.
Al scrambled after the nimble old monster, but by the time he reached the window, the seasoned shinobi had already vanished into the bustle of the city streets.
His speed at fleeing was second to none—there was nothing left to do but grudgingly call it splendid.

"Subaru, that man's next hiding place, it is...?"

"Mm-hm, mm-hm, does Subaru-chin know? The one you'd know?"

The course of events followed the same flow as before—Taritta and Medium both turned expectant gazes on Subaru, who had so easily seen through Olbart's first hiding place.
But unfortunately, the answer to the second hiding place—'The Abyss with a Good View'—was unknown to him.
Unable to give them a favorable answer, Medium and the others slumped their shoulders, but their rallying spirit followed the same course as before too.

"So, what's Abel-chan think? Bro here went and nailed that old man's location perfectly."

"Producing a result is worthy of recognition."

"...That's it?"

"As ever, there is no room to spare in time. We must still expose Olbart's location twice more. What else is there?"

"...Is that so."

Perhaps because of the earlier exchange, Al, who'd pushed back against Abel, slumped his shoulders at the answer.
Subaru appreciated Al's feelings, but Abel reacting that way was only to be expected. Last time, too, he had no memory of receiving any particular comment on this outcome from Abel.
Or rather, he couldn't picture Abel praising anyone—not just Subaru—straightforwardly at all.
This time, too, was just one more scene that lived up to that impression.

"'Good view'—might that not suggest a high place?"

"But 'abyss' means a hole, right? If it's a hole, wouldn't it be in the ground?"

"I think you're both on the right track. And to figure out the answer... Abel, didn't you want to go somewhere crowded?"

"—"

Taritta and Medium speculated about Olbart's next hiding place.
To push that discussion further, Subaru wanted to put into action the plan of Abel's that hadn't come to fruition last time. If he recalled right, they'd originally meant to head for a crowded tavern.
Following that same course, the moment they'd left the inn, a sudden 'Death' had struck Subaru and the others out on the street.
Which meant the critical moment in question was drawing closer by the second.

"Hey, Abel?"

"—. Your thinking isn't wrong. A place where many people come and go... one with many outsiders, ideally."

"A place where outsiders come and go, huh..."

"A place to drink... a tavern, was it."

The conversation moved along smoothly, and Abel gave a quiet nod at the word Subaru offered.
And so, the whole group set out to leave the inn and head for the tavern—

"We'll avoid the front and leave through the inn's back door."

That was what Abel, of all people, said.
At that seemingly abrupt about-face, Subaru let out a dumbstruck "ueh?" and Al and Taritta's eyes went wide too.

"Huh? Abel-chin, does that mean you believe what Subaru-chin said?"

"There's nothing better than caution. Having pinned down Olbart's location as well, there's room enough to consider it. That's all there is to it, is it not?"

"...You're seriously a nasty piece of work, you know that?"

Subaru said it like a grudge, but Abel only gave a "hmph" in response.
Still, setting aside his high-handed attitude, Abel's judgment was, from Subaru's perspective, a relief. He'd likewise been trying to figure out how to steer everyone to the back door anyway.

"Guess that means he does intend to properly acknowledge bro's achievements, huh."

"...Seems like it. No, I mean, he's kind of always been like that, but..."

For some reason, Subaru had found himself doubting even Abel's stated principle of 'reward and punishment.'
He'd been starting to feel like, once he understood that much, he'd generally have a handle on how to deal with Abel—or so he'd thought.
It was as if he were relearning, from scratch, how to deal with Abel all over again.

"Anyway, nailing that old man's location dead-on was somethin' else. How'd you pull that off? —Kinda looked like you'd read a strategy guide or somethin'."

"A strategy guide, huh. That's a damn nostalgic phrase. Sure, it'd be convenient if something like that existed, but it's nothing like that. That was just experience paying off."

"Experience? With hide-and-seek?"

"Something like that. Before... before?"

With the credit for finding Olbart being dug back up, Subaru's train of thought, mid-response, ground to a halt.
Part of how Subaru had been able to see through Olbart hiding in the first room was that such behavior was the standard move that type of person always pulled.
But there should have been someone who'd pulled that same standard move before Olbart.
Which meant Subaru should have remembered it clearly.

"Blanked on it? Forgettin' things is somethin' old folks do. And here you've gone and gotten younger, yet you're blankin' on stuff—that's a weird one, bro."

"Blanked on it..."

"Ain't it, like—if it's someone who did it, it'd be one of bro's own people. Not the silver-haired lady, so the loli girl you always got with you..."

"—Beatrice!"

"Whoa!"

Snapping his head up, Subaru cried out so sharply that Al's shoulders jolted.
But Subaru had no room to spare for Al's surprise. Naturally.

"You've gotta be kidding me..."

No matter how he thought about it, it was wrong.
Beatrice. Beatrice, it was. Subaru's partner, his beloved, beloved Great Spirit. The very first prank she'd pulled on Subaru had been the clue that led to finding Olbart.
If that counted as an achievement, it was an achievement Subaru and Beatrice had won together.
And yet, for that to have slipped his mind entirely—that couldn't be.
It was something that must never happen.

"—Subaru! Al! Come here, quickly!"

Taritta's sharp voice suddenly called out to summon Subaru, frozen in shock.
Looking up at her on reflex, he found Taritta, who'd gone around to the inn's back door, peering outside through the door she'd gently pushed open, her profile taut with sharp vigilance.
The cause of that vigilance was, in all likelihood, none other than the 'Death' that had struck Subaru and the others—


"—We are already surrounded. By close to a hundred opponents, I think."
