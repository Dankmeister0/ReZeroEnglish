Arc 8 Chapter 1: "Change in Victory Conditions"

—The battle between the Imperial Army and the rebel forces, which had begun with the imperial capital of Lupugana as its battlefield, plunged into its final phase, scattering seeds of chaos into every corner as it went.

That "Vincent Vollachia" had fallen before the throne deepest within the Crystal Palace to a bullet of light was one such thing. The dereliction of duty by the Nine Divine Generals tasked with guarding the walls that protected the capital, the sudden arrival at the front of the Empire's main fighting force that had been reported missing, the mysterious group from the west that tore an enormous hole in the battle line—these, too, were without doubt seeds of chaos that had been scattered.

But at present, the place where the seed that would sprout largest in this battle had taken root was—

"—Obey me! The situation has already moved beyond the bounds of a mere armed clash between the regular army and the rebel forces! With the intervention of a third-party faction, the conditions for victory have changed!"

Standing atop a mountain of rubble, on an impromptu stage, a man raised his voice.

His sharp black eyes held a heated but composed reason, dried blood clung to his black hair, and even amid the thundering roar of the battlefield, his voice carried well—a handsome, imposing figure of a man.

On a battlefield ruled by violence—no, in the Vollachia Empire, where violence itself ruled—what was it about this man's powerless-looking stance that could possibly draw anyone's attention?

However much one might wish to sneer at him, no one did. They could only turn their attention to him and listen.

The blood of the Empire's people flowing within them, and the very way of being the man himself had defined for himself, would not let them turn their ears away from the voice raised atop that misshapen pedestal.

—From atop the rubble, proclaiming his imperial reign: Vincent Vollachia himself.

"——"

The ones hearing Vincent's declaration up close, delivered in that ringing voice, were an illustrious gathering indeed. Lined up before him were Goz Ralfon and Olbart Dunkelkenn, members of the Nine Divine Generals; Berstetz Fondalfon, Chancellor of the Empire; and even Ubilk, the Stargazer permitted special access to the imperial palace by virtue of his unique post.

Considering which faction each of them had aligned with only moments before in the fighting, for these particular faces to come together at this distance, unguarded, could only be called a fatal risk.

And yet—

"Moguro Hagane is holding him in check, but the one facing off in the skies is none other than Balleroy Temeglyph, who should be dead. By some means, he has been revived, together with his beloved dragon."

"Regarding that matter, there is something I must report to Your Excellency. —In the audience chamber, I encountered Lady Lamia Godwin. She appeared to be the very woman herself, unchanged from her time."

"You! That report was mine to make! Your Excellency! Present there was not only Lady Lamia, but also other members of the imperial family who perished in the Imperial Selection Ceremony!"

It was Berstetz and Goz who responded thus to Vincent's words.

Goz, who despite going missing had continued to devote unwavering loyalty to Vincent, was one thing—but there was something galling about Berstetz's swift change of allegiance. Still, since Berstetz's concern had always fundamentally been the prosperity of the Vollachia Empire, now that the Empire's very survival was at stake, the old man's attitude could be called simply natural. Setting aside such petty grudges as trivial matters, the focus of Vincent's true concern narrowed down.

"Ubilk, you knew in advance of the coming of the 'Great Disaster.' Then—is the revival of Balleroy Temeglyph, of Lamia, of the other members of the imperial family, the 'Great Disaster' itself?"

"On that point, I could say both 'yes' and 'no,' Your Excellency. I was whispered to that the trigger for the 'Great Disaster' lay in the death of Vincent Vollachia, and so, to prevent the Empire's ruin, I schemed this way and that way..."

"But you do not know the details?"

At the question, Ubilk nodded, face grinning loosely, with a "Ye-e-es indeed." It was, in some sense, the answer he'd expected, but the certainty gained from it was a gain for Vincent nonetheless. It was already clear from the fact that Ubilk had saved Vincent as he fell—Ubilk and the Stargazers who followed him stood in opposition to the 'Great Disaster.'

"If that is so, then for the present your existence is beyond consideration. Simply conduct yourself so as not to die."

"Your Excellency! Please, leave this place to me and prioritize your own safety! I, Goz Ralfon, to wash away the disgrace of my absence, shall devote myself body and bone to—"

"—Silence."

"—!"

With a single glance at Goz, whose blood ran hot, Vincent crushed his momentum with one word. Goz, driven by loyalty to the Emperor and a desire to make up for having arrived too late at the critical moment, wanted a chance to redeem himself—but to begin with, his service was more than sufficient, having already gotten Vincent away from Chisha's treachery. Without Goz's hard fighting, Vincent would likely have been captured by Chisha, and this very situation within the capital might never have come to be. —Yes: the situation was being deliberately constructed.

"——"

Hand to his chin, Vincent sketched a map of the capital in his mind and pieced the situation together. The blood he had lost and his depleted stamina affected his powers of thought, but fortunately, the pain from the collarbone Chisha had struck in the throne room kept his consciousness sharp. His will, too, remained full. He did not for a moment think that even this pain was going according to Chisha's calculations, but—

"Um? Sorry to interrupt while everyone's having such a busy little chat, but I've got my own job of captivating the world, so mind if I take my leave?"

"Wha-!? First General Cecilus!? What are you saying!?"

"No, I mean, I do hear something about a 'first general' floating around every now and then, but since I'm not that interested it just doesn't stick in my head—I bet it's a case of mistaken identity, don't you think? Because if there's really some 'first general' out there being compared to me, well, that's pretty pathetic for him!"

Under these circumstances, it was not only inside Vincent's head that things were moving at a dizzying pace. Even now, within a range of a few dozen meters—within a single step, as far as Moguro's massive body was concerned—the battle between the 'Cloud Dragon' Mezoreia and Balleroy's combined force continued. The water of the reservoir, seen shattering in the distance, was already flowing out, and it was only a matter of time before its waves surged across the whole of the capital. On top of that, the one kicking up a fuss was the now-shrunken Cecilus.

Even with Goz, Berstetz, and Olbart all waiting on Vincent's decision, this one small bolt of lightning alone would not fit neatly into anyone's plans. Not only in appearance but, it seemed, in the very contents of his head, Cecilus had swung heavily toward childishness, and even facing Vincent's declaration, his eyes showed no sign of entrusting his heart wholesale to it.

"That speech just now was quite a splendid one! I'm sure it's also true that you really are the Vollachia Emperor, but unfortunately, on the one point of being an emperor, I don't bow to anyone else. If you want to change the road I'm walking, you'll have to lay out a proper flower-path retirement for me first!"

"—. As Natsuki Subaru once did, is that it?"

"—? Sorry, I've got no idea who that is. Though the name does feel kind of similar to someone I know—wait, didn't I have this exact conversation with the pretty lady earlier, too?"

Tilting his head, kicking his short little legs about, Cecilus answered. Just who this "pretty lady" weighing on his mind might be, Vincent could not tell—but the moment the child-formed Cecilus had turned up amid the fighting for the capital, Vincent was already certain. The unexpected group that had torn through the battlefield from the west was led by Natsuki Subaru, and it was surely he who had brought Cecilus along as well.

And then—

"It is not only in the present circumstance that you fail to follow my orders; that has never once been the case, from the start. Run about as you please."

"Oh, now that's a man who understands things. Though, mind you, I had no intention of obediently following whatever you said anyway, so no offense meant on my end."

"Cecilus."

Waving a hand at Vincent, who had decided to let him run loose, Cecilus's small frame made to leap away. Called by name to his retreating back, the little bolt of lightning turned around with a "Yes?" Into those eyes—unchanged in their sheer unmanageability since the day they'd first met—

"As per our former arrangement, I shall prepare your grand stage."

"—Ahaha."

Leaving behind one small burst of laughter at Vincent's brief declaration, Cecilus vanished. A speed of blinding wind and thunder that left not even an afterimage to grasp—and to think that was still incomplete told all one needed to know: the title of Vollachia's strongest would not be shaken from that man.

In any case—

"Your Excellency, was that wise? First General Cecilus is..."

"As with your own treatment, and Ubilk's, there is no time to be occupied with trivial matters. More importantly—"

Rather than the matter of the vanished Cecilus, dealing with the problem at hand took priority. Vincent faced Berstetz's narrowed eyes, and in a voice pitched to reach Goz and Olbart standing beyond him as well, declared it.

That was—

"—We abandon the capital. We withdraw, while preserving as much of our fighting strength as possible."

Through the imperial capital, swirling with confusion and chaos, a party rode astride a red Galewind Horse, cutting a path through.

At the head, naturally, was Natsuki Subaru; flanking him were the accompanying cavalryman Idra Missanga and the sole splash of red and powerhouse girl, Tanza—the breakthrough force of the Pleiades Battalion was alive and well.

"And what's more, right now we've got the two headliners, Beatrice and Rui, riding along, and we're just gaining momentum!"

In front, sandwiched against Idra's back holding the reins, Beatrice was cradled in Subaru's short arms, while on Subaru's back, Rui clung on ticklishly. Unintentionally, it had become a formation in which the adult Idra was chaperoning all four of the younger members—but—

"—Miinya!"

"Uaw!"

"Forgive my rudeness."

The violet-indigo arrow loosed by Beatrice with a raised hand, the omnidirectional attacks of Rui freely warping through space, and Tanza's forceful fist sweeping obstacles aside by brute strength—together they carved the path open. Vaulting over the shattered city wall, Subaru and the others plunged into the streets of the capital, aiming for the distant Crystal Palace—the absurdly glittering castle where the false Emperor sat enthroned.

The false emperor within that castle, the being masquerading as Vincent Vollachia. If they could bring that being down, then the great civil war of the Vollachia Empire, into which Subaru had been dragged whether he liked it or not, could be brought to an end.

Believing that, they had charged in prepared to raise a wild, reckless ruckus. But—

"Schwartz! No matter where we break through, these things keep popping up! What in the world are they!?"

Idra's voice, though not quite a scream, had a throat tightened with a kind of primal fear. Whether his agitation had communicated itself through the reins, the Galewind Horse's gait, too, had begun to show hesitation—but to tell it to bear down and forget about it would be a cruel thing to ask. After all—

"Seriously, what the hell is with these pale-faced creeps!"

Charging into the capital, Subaru had, of course, expected the Imperial Army's fierce resistance to intercept them. But in fact, the imperial soldiers had only greeted Subaru and the others at the very start. From then on, what stood in their way, again and again, were strange, pallid figures of an eerie sort.

Pale blue-white faces with golden eyes, their whole bodies covered in cracks like shattered glass, obstructing Subaru and the others with a cold, heatless indifference.

—No, it wasn't only Subaru's group that they stood in the way of.

"Sir Schwartz, those people—they are also attacking the Imperial soldiers."

Tanza reported this, even as she kicked away a cluster of the pale-faced things and slammed them against a wall. Just as she said, at the edge of Subaru's vision, these 'enemies' were not making for Subaru's group at all, but rather assaulting the imperial soldiers keeping watch over the city. Naturally, unlike the Pleiades Battalion, these 'enemies' showed no consideration whatsoever for Subaru's feelings—swinging their weapons, they moved to take the imperial soldiers' lives without mercy. And so—

"—Tanza! Please!"

"Understood."

Hearing Subaru's reckless request, Tanza kicked off the ground and accelerated. At a speed like a bouncing ball, she closed the distance to the enemy line—the group attacking the Imperial soldiers, intent on taking their lives—and with her thick-soled boots, swept their backs aside.

"—!"

A short groan rose, and the kicked-away 'enemies' slammed into the buildings to either side. Amid the destroyed buildings and the billowing smoke, the imperial soldiers who had been spared a moment's death stood beyond—Tanza gave them a single glance and told them, "Please go," sending them fleeing from the spot. Fortunately, the rescued soldiers did not target Tanza's back, choosing instead to withdraw from the scene.

"Glad that worked out... but these things—"

Thanks to Tanza's efforts, unwanted deaths had been avoided, but in their place, Subaru caught his breath at the grisly sight of the 'enemies,' struck by her single blow and smashed against the buildings. That the 'enemies' did not rise again owed partly to how powerful Tanza's kick had been—but more than that, it was because they were simply in no state to rise.

The bodies of the 'enemies' had shattered, scattered into pieces. And yet it wasn't a grotesque corpse that resulted—it broke the way a porcelain cup does when dropped on the floor. No blood flowed from it, either. As if there had never been any blood flowing inside that body at all.

"What kind of mechanism is this? Like a doll, or some kind of robot..."

"—Could it be... the 'Sacrament of the Immortal King'?"

"—! You have some idea what this is, Beatrice!?"

Beatrice, entrusting her body to Subaru's chest with complete and total trust—Subaru did not miss the words that spilled from those cherry-like, adorable lips. At Subaru's reaction, Beatrice gave a small nod of her chin, and continued,

"It's a magic that calls a soul back into a body it has once left through death... a kind of forbidden art, I suppose. Something Mother was originally researching—but it should have ended in failure."

"So a forbidden art for reviving the dead—that's like some necromancer-type magic. I've got a soft spot in my head for your mom given all the Fluffy business, but still, she was seriously up to no good, huh!"

"It's not like this is the first time Subaru's spoken ill of Mother. But if you keep saying it, Betty will get mad too, I suppose, so watch yourself."

Told this by a Beatrice with raised eyebrows, Subaru stuck out his tongue, closed one eye, and reflected on his ways. Still, Beatrice's cute opinion was one he could by no means simply let pass. If this necromancy, called the 'Sacrament of the Immortal King,' was what was producing those 'enemies'—

"You mean to say there's someone like a necromancer among the Empire's Nine Divine Generals or something?"

"...If so, then it's odd of Abel not to have mentioned any such thing beforehand, I suppose. And it would also mean whoever it is can't tell friend from foe, I suppose."

"That, I could believe of an empire whose sense of ethics is straight-up bugged out, but— wait, hold on! You know Abel!?"

"...I know him, I suppose."

An unexpected name spilling from Beatrice's mouth made Subaru's eyes go wide. And in response to Subaru's surprise, Beatrice confirmed it with a sour expression. The connection between Beatrice and Abel—that this wasn't simply a case of two different people sharing a name—came across clearly just from the look on her face.

Fundamentally, Abel got along poorly with just about everyone, so it was, naturally enough, no doubt a matter of him displaying that same poor chemistry with Beatrice as well. Just what kind of arrogant attitude Abel might have used to torment Beatrice, Subaru couldn't say—just imagining it was enough to turn his stomach—but—

"—Of course he is. He's here too, isn't he."

At this decisive moment, it was hard to imagine Abel not being somewhere in the midst of the fighting—but since Subaru had no clear idea of the rebel army's actual composition, hearing it confirmed outright stirred up a different sort of feeling. If Abel was here, then Subaru's own way of wreaking havoc would need a bit more finesse.

"So, does that mean it's better to leave zombie-countermeasures to him rather than me? Or should I go handle it together with the cute Beatrice, who happens to know all about zombies... ow ow ow ow! What!?"

"Hmph, I suppose. Subaru's face just annoyed me a little, I suppose."

"Why!? This look, right between charming and cheeky—that's just how I was born!"

"It annoyed me that you looked strangely relieved on hearing Abel's name, I suppose."

Pinched on the thigh by Beatrice, making an unreasonable accusation, Subaru twisted his mouth into a wry line. It was a little hard to say exactly how much, but it seemed like Beatrice's eyes toward Subaru had grown stricter in the short time they'd been apart.

Certainly, Subaru acknowledged that Abel was competent in ability—but he wasn't the sort to feel relief at hearing Abel's name amid this massive, chaotic war. However he thought about it, being reunited face-to-face with Beatrice was a far happier event than anything to do with Abel.

"You're number one to me, Beatrice, no misunderstanding that."

"Then you'll just have to keep proving that from here on, I suppose."

"Yeah, absolutely, no doubt about it!"

Responding thus to Beatrice's merciful verdict, Subaru clapped Idra's shoulder and had him bring the Galewind Horse to a halt for a moment. Around them, no sign of enemies or civilians—but he wanted to settle their next course of action first.

"Sir Schwartz, if you're quite finished with your little games, what will you do?"

"That's a bit thorny, huh! We weren't playing games or anything... but anyway, Beatrice! This zombie magic—does it stop if we take down the caster?"

Even as Tanza's expressionless face pierced his profile, Subaru sought Beatrice's detailed opinion. Asked this, Beatrice fiddled with one of her ringlet curls with a finger, and said,

"Magic that interferes with another's body falls mainly under Shadow magic or Light magic, I suppose. Regardless of which affinity, generally speaking, once the caster falls, the effect of that magic breaks off as well—that's the common rule, I suppose. So, I'd like to think this is the same, I suppose."

"That sounds like it's got some wishful thinking mixed in. You're not sure?"

"Even Betty is seeing an actual realization of the 'Sacrament of the Immortal King' for the first time, I suppose! It would be childish and irresponsible to make baseless assumptions from mere guesswork, I suppose!"

"Okay, okay, calm down! Then, do you have any idea about the range of effect, or where the magic's actually being cast from?"

"...Frustratingly, that would depend on the one wielding it, I suppose."

Perhaps thinking Tanza might needle her again, Beatrice answered this while glancing at her with a frustrated look. But Tanza did not blame Beatrice for admitting her own limits. That was simply how Tanza was—she did not put down even those she didn't get along with.

"Not really the time to be squabbling, and that info helps anyway. Thanks, Beatrice, too."

"That's not really an answer worth praising, I suppose."

Saying his thanks while stroking her head, Subaru made Beatrice puff out her cheeks. He felt the urge to poke that puffed-up cheek with a finger, but he also sensed they'd been placed in a rather difficult position.

Given the circumstances, this zombie assault was clearly an anomaly. From the fact that Imperial soldiers were being attacked, there was a possibility that the rebel army had a necromancer among its ranks, and that Abel—whose sense of ethics and warm humanity were as good as dead—had unleashed some tactic that treated people as less than people. But the manner of its deployment nagged at him somewhat, and the way the zombies kept appearing felt off.

These zombies clearly weren't being brought into the city from outside—there was an atmosphere of them having arisen within the city itself and now running amok. If this were truly Abel's scheme, it would seem far more advantageous to have unleashed a war of attrition from the very start. Perhaps some military tactic Subaru wasn't aware of was in play, but—

"If it turns out he's actually gone and started using zombies, I'm seriously going to have to rethink how I deal with him."

Besides, though it was admittedly little more than a hunch, it felt to Subaru that Abel, however much he might exploit "death" politically, wasn't the type to use "the dead" as a military asset. That Abel's view of life and death—hopelessly Vollachian to the core, however one sliced it—simply wouldn't allow it.

And so—

"This zombie-master's existence has to be an irregularity. If we go ahead and punch out the fake emperor in the castle and the war still doesn't end..."

"What will you do, Schwartz? We will follow your lead."

"Idra..."

Turning only his head toward Subaru, who had his hand to his chin, Idra spoke his trust aloud. This wasn't Idra tossing responsibility onto Subaru's shoulders—it was proof that, in a situation where he himself might well be marching into the jaws of death, he was giving his assent to risking his life alongside Subaru.

Of his own will, Subaru had claimed this position for himself. He had to fulfill that responsibility. And thanks to Idra and everyone in the Pleiades Battalion, the reach of the hand Subaru could extend was wider now than before.

"It's a bit short on a decisive answer, but for now, I guess we should just... ow ow ow ow! Ow ow!"

"Subaru!?" "Sir Schwartz!?"

Having nodded in acknowledgment of his responsibility, Subaru let out a yelp from a pain distinct from the earlier pinch on his thigh, and Beatrice and Tanza both widened their eyes at it. This time, the pain Subaru felt was from his hair being pulled. And the one who'd done it was neither Beatrice, nor Tanza, nor even Idra—

"What is it, Rui! Don't pull my hair! Taking good care of it from a young age is a defense strategy against suffering from thinning hair once you're older..."

"Uaw! U! Auu! Aaw!"

Rui, desperately trying to convey something to Subaru, who was covering his abused scalp. She struck Subaru's back, then pointed insistently toward the north side of the capital—the direction of the Crystal Palace, though slightly off from it—and kept appealing to him about it.

He understood she was saying "go there"—but,

"What's there?"

"Auu!"

At Subaru's question, Rui repeated herself, letting out a high-pitched cry once more. It might be strange to put it this way, but having managed to reunite with Subaru, she seemed to be in a fairly stable mental state. Though their relationship remained complicated, Subaru was, honestly, relieved she was safe. Given all that, if there was something Rui was appealing about with such desperation—

"—Beatrice, just tell me one thing. Is everyone able to stay together?"

"...Not everyone, I suppose."

To Subaru's quiet question, Beatrice's answer avoided a clear statement. But it was plain that she hadn't dodged it out of some coy withholding—it was to spare Subaru a shock. Understanding that much, Subaru also understood why she'd chosen to be vague about it.

And so—

"Tanza, Idra! And Beatrice, Rui too—change of plan!"

Summing up what needed doing, Subaru called out to the members gathered around the Galewind Horse. In all likelihood, the fighting centered on the capital wasn't heading toward calming down, but toward escalation. With the zombies and their master being an unknown quantity, they needed to prepare for that. Of course, he could line up plenty of that kind of reasonable-sounding logic—but—

"First, we're heading where Rui's pointing! Let me go make an important pickup there!"

"Rui's... then, is he over that way, I suppose?"

From Subaru's decision, even Beatrice grasped what lay in the direction Rui was indicating. Nodding at Beatrice's words, Subaru turned toward Tanza and Idra, who were watching him intently, and continued.

That was—

"—We're going to pick up Rem, and get as many people out of the capital as we possibly can! It's a race against time!"

And so, unbeknownst to them, it was a decision that—by an odd coincidence—matched exactly that of a certain man not present at this scene.
