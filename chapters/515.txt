Arc 7 Chapter 17: "Creeping Malice"

"――――"

Chewing over a fact he could hardly believe, Subaru felt cold sweat soak his entire body.
As far as Subaru knew, this was one of the most terrifying situations imaginable.

A scene he'd seen before, a conversation he'd heard before — this wasn't the realm of déjà vu.
It wasn't the sensation of something that felt like it had happened; it was something that had actually happened, happening again. And that could mean only one thing — he had *Returned by Death*.

"But, why...?"

There had been no warning sign at all.
If anything, it felt to Subaru as though the world had switched scenes the instant he blinked. It felt so utterly unreal that he almost suspected the Authority dwelling within him had malfunctioned — that somehow the "dying" half of Return by Death had dropped out, leaving only the "returning" to activate on its own.

"Am I an idiot. No — I am an idiot."

Even now, faced with this, Subaru reined in the part of himself that wanted to take an optimistic view of the situation.
Ridiculous as it sounded, he had to trust Return by Death. Not once, in all this time, had the power to turn back time lodged within him ever triggered on anything but "death."
If time had rewound, then Subaru had lost his life. That much was absolute.

"—Okay. I've swallowed that. Swallowed it, so what now."

Pressing his fist to his forehead, Subaru urged his badly shaken self to stay calm.
Having accepted that he'd Returned by Death, the next thing to focus on was the cause of death. He cast his mind back to just moments before — mere seconds ago — trying to recall what had happened.

"...Nothing's coming."

But no matter how hard he tried to recall the moment he must have died, nothing surfaced.
He groped through his memory for even the smallest foothold, and came up with nothing.

The tavern he'd been heading for, Flop, whom he'd been talking with, the bustle of the main street growing slightly distant, the faint smell peculiar to a shaded alley, a faint hard sound he'd caught — that was about all he could recover.
None of it connected to any reason his life might have been endangered.

"Damn it, why is it always like this for me...!"

If he'd kept himself sharply attuned to changes in his surroundings, as he always tried to, he wouldn't have been caught in such a disgraceful blind spot — Subaru cursed and berated his own carelessness.
But a part of his reason looked coldly upon just how unreasonable a demand that was. He wasn't a security camera. He couldn't stay on guard around himself twenty-four hours a day. Nor was he skilled enough to catch every trivial change in his surroundings and react instantly.

Natsuki Subaru was, unmistakably, an ordinary man.
That evaluation hadn't changed even now, after reliving his own "memories" and taking a fresh look at himself.

Which was precisely why he couldn't afford to stop thinking, and stop acting.

"—This situation is close to the first time Shaula killed me."

On the road to the Pleiades Watchtower, when Subaru and the others had challenged the "sand wind" of the Augria Sand Dunes, they'd been wiped out by a strike from Shaula, who had been defending the sand sea from the watchtower.
That first baptism by the sand sea had begun with Subaru being obliterated by a white flash of light he hadn't even had time to react to — the opening blow of a hellish battle.

Not knowing what happened, not knowing the cause of death, certain of nothing but the fact that he'd died — this was, precisely, a replay of that first death in the sand sea.
Except this time, unlike then, he was in the middle of a city.
The environment was fundamentally different from the sand sea, where he'd steeled himself with the awareness that death lay right beside him at every step.

"In this situation, what the hell could take my life...?"

When it came to a "death" with no warning, the strongest impression that came to mind was still Shaula's sniping.
So maybe this time too he'd been sniped — but Subaru had died in the middle of an alley. Not a particularly tall one, but a place hemmed in on both sides by buildings, with poor sightlines.
If someone were sniping, the line of fire would only run front to back — hardly terrain suited to a clean shot. Of course, given that he had no idea how far he could trust his own memory, it was possible the alley had actually been some sniper's dream, with a perfectly clear line of fire —

"—Master? What's this, you've been frowning even harder for a while now?"

"Ah..."

"Weren't you listening to my advice? Good fortune flees from a furrowed brow! And once it's fled, it's a devil of a job to chase down! I, for one, am mostly up on the ox-cart, you know!"

Flop pressed a hand to his own chest, gesturing grandly in front of Subaru.
Subaru could tell it was an exaggerated performance meant to cheer up his own scowling face, but he couldn't help tensing at the man's flamboyant gestures all the same.

If he was going to hunt for the cause of death, he'd have to hunt for the culprit at the same time.
In that case, if Subaru's death hadn't been a sniping, the prime suspect was Flop — the man who'd been acting alongside Subaru, closest to him at the time.
But at the moment Subaru had died, Flop had been within his field of view, and there hadn't been the slightest sign of him making a move to attack.
Of course, Subaru knew perfectly well that this world was full of people with the skill to take a life in a single stroke without their target even noticing. But Flop didn't seem like one of them.
For one thing—

"Does Flop-san have a reason to kill me?"

The most likely possibility was that he was playing the good Samaritan as a cover to rob Subaru. But there seemed little reason to go to the trouble of doing it in the middle of a city.
To begin with, he'd met the O'Connell siblings outside the gate, in the line.
If they'd wanted to get away with it unnoticed, they could've talked Subaru and the others into anything and lured them out before entering Guaral, where the guards were. Even if it had been about the money from cashing in the Witchbeast's Horn, in the end it was thanks to none other than Flop's own intercession that Subaru hadn't been lowballed by the buyer. — There was no reason to involve Subaru in the middle of it at all.
It would've been far simpler to trick them outside and take the horn, then cash it in inside the city themselves.

There was no rational explanation for the motive.
Plenty of people had tried to kill Subaru before, but every one of them had had some rational reason. Unless it was a Witchbeast that couldn't even speak, that should be an absolute rule.
So—

"Flop-san, why are you being so good to us?"

"Hm? Now that's a rather abrupt question."

"Ah, sorry for springing it on you. It's just — I got a little uneasy."

At Subaru's seemingly abrupt question, Flop raised his well-shaped eyebrows in surprise.
Feigning a wry smile at that reaction, Subaru waited with bated breath for Flop's answer. — There was no rational suspicion left. All that remained was whether he could believe.

Flop, who had gone out of his way to look after them so attentively, so kindly.
Subaru wanted the reassurance that he and his sister Medium harbored no ulterior motives, that it was safe to trust them.

"—Hmm."

Perhaps something of what was in Subaru's heart showed in his eyes, because Flop narrowed his fine-featured gaze.
When the baseless confidence and ease that colored his usual expression faded away, what remained was simply a handsome man with an intelligent bearing.
And then, shaking his head in front of the tensed-up Subaru, he said,

"It's not a complicated story. It's quite simple, really. What my sister and I are doing for you, your wife, and your little niece — what we're doing is..."

"What you're doing is?"

"—Revenge, of course!"

"...Eh?"

Flop spread both arms and declared it to Subaru in a ringing voice.
The gap between the boisterous cheer of his delivery and the ominousness of what he'd actually said was so great that, for a moment, Subaru couldn't process it. He simply stared, wide-eyed, dumbfounded.
Before the frozen Subaru, Flop brushed his bangs aside with a "Now then," and said,

"My sister and I once lived a life where it was genuinely touch-and-go whether we'd live or die, you know! Abandoned by our parents, we were raised in a facility that took in orphans... and that place was, shall we say, quite a terrible environment!"

"An orphanage..."

Vaguely, an image of something like an orphanage floated up in Subaru's mind.
Though it wasn't hard to imagine that the conditions and facilities of an orphanage in this world differed vastly from what he pictured of modern society.
A wretched environment here was likely a world beyond anything Subaru could imagine.
That's where he'd been raised, Flop revealed to Subaru, gesturing as he spoke,

"Every night, we huddled together with children in the same straits, resolving in our hearts to escape that situation. And in time, my sister and I got our chance, and we managed to run away from that place. The first night we ever spent without being beaten, I made myself a vow. — A vow of revenge."

"Revenge... against the people at the orphanage?"

"No, not that. — Against the world."

With the same face he'd worn when he declared "revenge" moments ago, Flop clenched his fist.
To the taken-aback Subaru, he leaned forward, his expression heating with fervor.

"My sister and I grew up beaten by the adults at that facility. But tell me — were the adults who beat me happy while they did it? No. They weren't. They too were unhappy. Unhappy adults, beating unhappy children. Is there anything more hopeless than that?"

"――――"

"Even the adults committing the violence weren't happy. So I became a merchant, and resolved to pull myself and my sister out of unhappiness. And I want as many other people as possible to escape unhappiness too, if I can help it. Just like the person who took us away, that night."

"That's your revenge on the world?"

"Exactly. My sister and I struggle on, taking our revenge against a world that forces unhappiness on people. Helping you and your wife — that's part of it too."

Having said his piece, Flop rubbed his nose, looking a little bashful.
At that gesture, and the heat behind Flop's words, Subaru found himself at a loss for words. As that heat slowly sank into his mind, his gut settled the matter at once.

"Thank you, Flop-san. I'm glad it was you and Medium-san we met in that line."

He'd wanted a guideline for whether he could trust the man, and he'd asked for one.
And the answer he'd been given exceeded what he'd asked for. So that settled it.

Subaru would not doubt Flop O'Connell's good intentions.
He resolved to believe wholeheartedly in that noble spirit of vengeance, the resolve to defy the world's injustice.
And then, next—

"Flop-san, the feng shui reading for this road isn't great right now. So could we use a different route?"

"A reading? What's a 'reading'? Is that perhaps why your brow's been so furrowed, and your eyes so sharp?"

"It's a sort of fortune-telling thing, but the eyes probably aren't related! Anyway, this road's no good. It's fine if it's a detour, please."

Leaning shamelessly on Flop's goodwill, Subaru pushed the conversation forward rather forcibly.
Having decided Flop wasn't the culprit, the next problem was avoiding the "death" bearing down on him. Whether it was sniping or something else, it was still an attack.
If he could avoid the conditions that triggered the attack, that should lead to avoiding "death."
For that, the first priority was not to retrace the same steps as before.

Change the route, shift the timing, preemptively avoid whatever harm would befall him.
If he could manage that, he should be able to dodge an unwanted "death."

"Not that I've ever wanted 'death,' in the first place... well, once or twice, maybe."

Even then, it had only ever been a last resort, forced by circumstances.
If he hadn't been driven into a corner where death was the only way to save someone, Subaru would never have chosen "death" even then. Argh, even Subaru felt like taking revenge on the world at this point.

"I don't know about this 'reading' of yours, but I like the seriousness on your face. It'll be a bit of a detour, but let's take a different route there."

"That'd be a huge help! Let's go by way of the main street, where there are more people around."

"Understood."

Fortunately, Flop didn't press Subaru on his unnatural request, and simply took him at his word.
Following Flop's guidance, they altered the route to the tavern from the original plan. They backtracked from the path they'd been about to take, choosing a route from the main street to the tavern that used as few alleys as possible.
In the end, if the line of fire had run in front of the tavern, Subaru might still be a target regardless. But if he knew in advance he'd be attacked in the alley in front of the tavern, there were plenty of countermeasures available.
Worst case, if the enemy saw Subaru on guard, they might even withdraw on their own—

"Well then, Master, this way—"

Passing by Subaru's side, Flop started to lead him down the different route.
That was the moment. — Flop glanced back at Subaru and, as if noticing something, his eyes went wide.

"—Uh?"

Subaru tried to check Flop's reaction, to ask what — but he never got the chance.
Because his mouth wasn't opened to form a question to Flop, but flung fully open to spew forth an overflow of blood.

"Ah, ka!?"

It happened in an instant.
He felt something grab the back of his head, wrenching his face up forcibly. And then something hot passed across his exposed throat, and blood burst forth.
Drowning in the flood of blood and pain, Subaru understood — his throat had been torn open.

"Gah...!"

Pressing both hands to the wound at his neck, Subaru searched for some way out through the agony and the blood loss.
The wound was deep and wide, an important blood vessel severed, blood gushing out. Take off his jacket and wrap it around his neck to stop the bleeding — no, first, he had to get away from this spot, away from the enemy behind him.
And besides, Flop was here. Sorry for doubting him. Even if he trusted him again afterward, he might not be forgiven — but Flop was here, right now.

"Eh, m..."

Rem was at the inn. Somehow, even bleeding from the throat, he had to get back to her.
Get back, take her away, it's dangerous, take her hand. Even if she resisted, pull her along. If only Rem could stay alive. She had to stay alive. For that, the blood at his neck, had to be stopped.

Blood, blood, ch-ch-ch-ch-ch-ch, blood, stop, stop it, stopstopstopstop——

"—u."

...op.

"—Master, that scowl won't do."

"――――"

"Good fortune doesn't visit those without smiles and composure. From here on, you're going to look for a traveling companion, aren't you? Then you'll need to seek out a good match."

Right in front of him, digging his own fingers into his brow, Flop earnestly held forth.
Watching that gesture, Subaru instinctively pressed both hands to his own throat. He felt no hot sensation, no heat of life draining away. No sensation of gushing blood with every heartbeat.
No despairing pulse of his own life flowing out, drawing nearer to "death."

"So, wipe that frown away and... what's wrong, Master? You've gone pale."

Seeing Subaru fall silent, Flop looked over, startled with concern.
His earnest gaze brought back to Subaru's mind the moment just before — the instant his throat had been slit.

Yes, his throat had been cut.
His throat had been cut, blood had burst forth, instinct had rung the alarm that he had to flee, and then even that alarm had gone silent, and he'd returned to this moment.
Which meant he'd died. He had Returned by Death again.
And this time, unlike the first instant death, it had been more explicit — malice given concrete form.

"...Bhah."

Touching his own throat, Subaru scrambled to remember the breathing he'd forgotten. Flop touched his shoulder as it heaved up and down, saying "Are you all right?" but Subaru had no room to reply.
Still, he couldn't stay rooted to this spot either.

"F-Flop-san, today, um, it's a bad day feng-shui-wise. Let's turn back for now...!"

"Feng-shui? But with that complexion, you'd best rest a moment..."

"No, resting won't help! It's an attack that won't settle unless Rem holds my hand!"

"I-Is that so... that sounds like quite the ordeal!"

Burning with anxiety and urgency, Subaru had blurted out something nonsensical, but the good-natured Flop seemed to weigh the mood over the content of the words.
He grasped the hand on his shoulder in return, nodding despite his surprise. Pulling Flop's hand along, Subaru hesitated over which way to go, forward or back.

Forward followed the original route; backward was the direction where his throat had just been slit.
Either way felt like being swallowed into a life-threatening dead end, so Subaru tried to head for the main street through some other alley, neither forward nor back.
He'd failed just now, but surely the enemy wouldn't try to kill Subaru — or anyone — where there were witnesses around. If he wanted to put the enemy on guard, heading for the main street was the priority.

"Master! Your hand is terribly cold! You'd best have your wife warm it up quick!"

"Yeah, I want to see Rem's face as soon as possible."

If they ended up going back without accomplishing their objective, so be it, even if he got scolded for it.
Anyway, right now, get to Rem—

"—No."

Was it really all right to go back? A question spiraled through Subaru's mind.
With the enemy's identity still unknown, he considered the danger of returning to Rem. He had no idea where the enemy had been targeting them from — if he went straight back to the inn now, wouldn't he be handing their base of operations right to the enemy?

"――――"

Cursing his own lack of foresight, Subaru bit his lip, and at the same moment his view opened up.
They'd cleared the alley, and Subaru and Flop emerged onto the city's main street. Left and right, the number of people coming and going was nothing compared to a great city, but it was far better than the alley with the hidden tavern.
Still, it took courage to plunge into a crowd. He had to decide their next course of action while avoiding that.

"Now that we're here on the street, the inn should be that way. Well then, shall we..."

"No, Flop-san! We can't go back to the inn. Meeting Rem is out of the question!"

"That's not what you were saying a moment ago!?"

His behavior had become that of someone utterly emotionally unstable, but the concern from before kept Subaru from letting himself return to Rem. And yet, dragging Flop around without giving him any information felt like a betrayal too.
But what could he say to convince him?
This wasn't the Emilia Camp, or Anastasia or Julius, companions of a long journey. He had no persuasive power or grounds that would make Flop take his opinion seriously.
Even relying on Flop's goodness for that much felt like asking too much.

"Damn it...!"

Subaru had far too few cards in hand to break through this situation.
Separated from Rem, someone precious to him, attacked in an unfamiliar city, with only the good-natured but combat-useless Flop at his side. Subaru himself had no strength he could bring to bear.
Without knowing who was targeting him, he didn't even know who to be on guard against.

"Could it be — the Hunter!?"

When he tried to define the enemy as an invisible assassin, the first possibility that surfaced in Subaru's mind was "the Hunter."
The dangerous figure who had killed Subaru once in the Buddheim Jungle, and had later targeted him again while he was with Rem. In the end, he hadn't encountered him since — but what if the man had been carefully stalking Subaru all this while?

In the Buddheim Jungle, at the Imperial camp, in the Shudrak settlement — someone had always been acting alongside Subaru. To avoid facing an opponent with combat strength, maybe the man had chosen to move now, while Subaru was with the powerless Flop.

"If so, that's an obsession like a bear whose prey's been stolen. Why is he coming after me this hard? What is it about me that..."

"Master? Are you all right? Whatever is troubling you so? If it's something I can help with, try telling me first. You look strange."

Scanning left and right along the street, his nerves frayed, Subaru heard Flop speak up.
Flop grasped both his shoulders head-on, and at that earnest gaze and the offer he wanted so badly to cling to, Subaru seriously considered whether he should simply gamble on the man's goodness.
If it was Flop, maybe — just maybe — he'd believe Subaru and lend his strength.

"Flop-san, I feel pathetic leaning on you like this, but — will you hear me out?"

"Ah, of course, naturally! Well now, even if it's something I can't help with, my sister might be able to. She and I make up for each other's weaknesses, you know."

"Actually, this might be more of a Medium-san matter, if anything..."

If the enemy was going to hurl merciless violence at them, maybe the only answer was to throw back stronger force. Medium's strength, clearly greater than Subaru's or Flop's.
With that in mind too, Subaru tried to explain the situation to Flop. First, keeping quiet about Return by Death, he'd say that someone was relentlessly targeting them.

"Actually, someone's been following us..."

"—What's that?"

"Eh?"

Steeling himself, Subaru began carefully explaining the situation to Flop, taking care not to touch on the penalty of Return by Death.
But the moment he began to speak, Flop's gaze suddenly turned off to the side. He knit his fine brows, narrowing his eyes toward the street.
Sensing that Flop had noticed something, Subaru belatedly noticed the same thing.

"—!"

In the distance, someone was screaming.
Not just one person — multiple people, screaming, shouting in fury, raising their voices. And the sound was gradually compounding with urgency, and something other than screams—

"Wha—!?"

Flop's throat caught as his eyes went wide, and Subaru turned to follow his gaze.
An instant later, seeing whatever it was Flop had seen, Subaru too was struck speechless.

—A massive wheel was bearing down straight at Subaru and Flop.

"Master, look ou—"

Before Flop could even finish saying "out," the impact swallowed Subaru's whole body with violent force. He was flung far through the air, bouncing and rolling again and again across the hard-packed earth.
The momentum didn't stop; he slammed into a wall. But it didn't end there. — In quick succession, another impact crashed into the falling Subaru, sending his body flying again.

"—Ah, guh."

Tumbling and rolling, sprawled on the ground, Subaru's vision drowned in black.
It wasn't that the sky had suddenly clouded over. Something else, some other reason, had crushed his sight. He didn't know the cause, and he understood that even finding out the truth would do him no good.
But if there was one thing he could say—

"I-I'm..."

Going to die, every cell in his body was screaming at his instincts.
Having experienced over thirty "deaths" by now, Natsuki Subaru had a vague sense of just how much damage his body could take before it lost his life.
This time, he had clearly, obviously exceeded the limit of what he could grit his teeth and bear.

"――――"

Throbbing, aching pain.
But it wasn't on the level of "this part of my body hurts." What hurt was his entire body. The existence called Subaru had become one with the phenomenon of "pain" itself.
It wasn't that some part hurt. Natsuki Subaru himself *was* "pain." And since he was "pain," of course it hurt. Everywhere, everywhere, it hurt. The pain wouldn't fade.

Far off, ringing in his head, a sound like tinnitus, or a train whistle.
Mixed in with it, he could hear the joys and sorrows of people flying about — no, there was no joy. Utter chaos and lamentation was more accurate. Utter chaos and lamentation — funny, what a phrase. What a set of characters to string together.
Contrary to the ringing of the sound, what was happening was too heavy, too hard.

"Ahi, ou, ah..."

He couldn't form words.
The mouth that had tried to speak was in tatters. His teeth were gone, air passing freely through his mouth. Somewhere had been torn open, air leaking out. There was blood, and sound, and "pain."
Something, something, something—

"Ai, ah."

Something had killed Natsuki Subaru again.

"—Master, that scowl won't do."

"――――"

"Good fortune doesn't visit those without smiles and composure. From here on, you're going to look for a traveling companion, aren't you? Then you'll need to seek out a good match."

Pressing his fingers to his own brow, Flop worked the skin and flesh, rubbing them loose.
Watching his performance play out, Subaru wrapped both arms around himself. And he found himself dwelling on the mouth that no longer leaked air, and the "pain" that had vanished without a trace.
—He'd died again.

"And this time, this time... that was, a Dragon Carriage, wasn't it..."

Ground down by the impact, sent flying, unable to move, the candle of his life left to thin and gutter out.
Dominated to the last by the "pain" screaming through his whole body, Subaru had been killed. His life had been lost.

"――――"

Trembling, his gripping hands shook, his knees beginning to buckle.
Even if his body didn't remember the disaster that had befallen it, his soul did. The impact and pain that had felt like his whole body being torn apart still gnawed at his soul, even now that it had faded away.

At last, Subaru could no longer hide the terror at the malice descending upon him.
The first instant death, the second throat-slitting, and now a third death, run down by a Dragon Carriage — none of these could possibly be coincidence. Unmistakably, this was intent to kill Natsuki Subaru.
And it had killed Subaru, mercilessly, each time. And the most terrifying thing of all was that, even after three Returns by Death, Subaru still had no information beyond "an enemy exists."
The enemy left Subaru, as he died, with no information whatsoever.

"So wipe that frown away and... what's wrong, Master. You've gone terribly pale."

Once again, Flop showed concern over the change in Subaru's complexion.
The chaos on the main street a moment ago must have been the reaction to the Dragon Carriage running wild. And in the instant of the collision, Flop had reached out to try to save Subaru.
He hadn't made it in time, and Subaru had taken an impact severe enough to kill him — but there was no way Flop, standing right beside him, had come through unscathed. — He too must have been caught up in that disaster.

"To begin with, up until now too..."

There was no guarantee the enemy would kill Subaru and be satisfied, withdrawing afterward.
Both the first and second times, Flop had witnessed the scene. If the enemy was a careful sort, silencing Flop, the witness, would be the natural course of action.
Which meant — up until now, hadn't Flop been killed too, alongside Subaru's own death, each time?
Flop might already have been dragged into the situation Subaru found himself in.
That wasn't something he could allow, Subaru thought, clenching his back teeth.

"—! Flop-san!"

"W-What is it!?"

Biting back his own cowardice between his clenched back teeth, Subaru faced forward with resolve.
And with that same resolve, he grabbed Flop's hand, startling him greatly. But Subaru had no room to spare for Flop's surprise.
Given everything so far, the countdown to death had already begun.

Going forward was hell, going back was hell, avoiding it was hell — a threefold torment.
Even so, he had to find a path to survival. — Not just for himself and Rem, but to return the good-natured older brother who'd helped them back to his sister safely.
So—

"—Flop-san, let's run!"

"That's sudden!? What's gotten into you!?"

"There's no 'what's gotten into me,' life is short! We can't afford to waste a single second!"

Stopping to persuade him patiently and thoroughly would be a bad move here.
Judging as much, Subaru pushed through with the same forceful logic as before, trying to break down Flop's resistance. As expected, swept up in the momentum, Flop nodded, saying, "Well, that's certainly true!"

"Life is short. To achieve my sister's and my goals, time is indeed precious, but—"

"Let's run! Straight to the tavern, now! Details can wait!"

"A-Alright! Alright, so don't pull me like that! You'll muss my bangs!"

Dragged forcibly by the arm, his feet tangling, Flop let out something like a shriek.
Listening to that near-scream of a voice, Subaru pressed forward along the road they'd taken before — rushing, just as originally planned, to the tavern to hire a bodyguard.
Except now, the goal wasn't to hire an escort for their journey.

To be saved from this hopeless, endless chain of "death," to obtain the help he desperately needed right now — that was why he ran.

"—You want to hire the best fighter here?"

The tavern keeper, finally reached, raised his eyebrows at Subaru, who was pressing him breathlessly.
The middle-aged proprietor, whose hair was starting to show white, looked Subaru up and down as he heaved for breath, eyeing him with something like suspicion.
Subaru himself was well aware that his appearance and age didn't exactly lend weight to his words. Especially having burst into the tavern and immediately made such a proposal.
It might even count as a kindness that he hadn't simply been dismissed as some kind of prank.

At the moment, Subaru and Flop were inside the tavern that had been their original destination all along.
Subaru had first been killed instantly, by some means, in the alley just before reaching the tavern — which had left him plenty uneasy. But perhaps because they'd hurried along the way, no malicious hand had appeared to stop Subaru and the others as they rushed, without pausing, into the tavern.
Still, it would be far too hasty to conclude they'd escaped the crisis just because of that.

"So I need to borrow the strength of your best fighter."

"...The best fighter around here — that'd be Rowan, I suppose."

Catching his breath, the proprietor shrugged at Subaru's repeated question. Then his gaze shifted to Flop beside Subaru, who was likewise still panting.

"Flop. Here you are, finally showing your face after so long, and it's to help someone out again, is it. Not that you ever make a copper off it, I'd wager."

"I'm only conducting the deals I want to conduct. My trading partners aren't limited to people, either. Sometimes I even propose a lopsided deal to the world itself."

"That's called taking a loss, you fool."

The two seemed to be old acquaintances, as the proprietor gave that exasperated assessment of Flop's conduct.
Subaru had heard directly from Flop his reasons for helping them, but it seemed his goodness was common knowledge to those who knew him.
Wiping a glass, the proprietor gave Subaru a sharp, sidelong look.

"He's exactly the fool you heard he is. But don't get too comfortable with that. This idiot and his sister are the type to help just about anybody, sure, but that means plenty of folks owe them, too."

"...I don't intend to be ungrateful either. Whatever debt I run up, I mean to pay back with interest."

"Hah, well, isn't that something to look forward to! The day you get spoiled rotten!"

Combing his mussed bangs back into place with a comb pulled from his coat, Flop gave that unguarded reply.
Saved by Flop's generosity and breadth of spirit, Subaru turned back into the tavern and asked, "Who's Rowan?"

"Ah, Rowan's the one passed out at the end there."

"Passed out at the end... that's not a phrase I especially wanted to hear."

Saying so, Subaru cautiously looked where the proprietor pointed. In the dim tavern, amid a group of men griping loudly over their drinks, he spotted a man slumped face-down at a table in the corner.
A man of about fifty, his long, disheveled hair carelessly tied back, a sword — of all things — worn at his hip.
On the table where he lay collapsed, several empty glasses stood lined up, evidence that he'd been drinking with quite some abandon even while it was still bright out.

"...That's the best fighter, is it?"

"His drinking habits are terrible, no question, but the man can fight. His drinking habits are terrible, though."

"Being told twice does nothing but pile up dread."

The tavern keeper had warned him twice, no less, about the man's terrible drinking habits.
He seemed likely to be quite the drunkard, but beggars couldn't be choosers. What Subaru needed right now wasn't good character or a strong liver — it was raw fighting strength.
The ability to save Subaru from the crisis bearing down on him, and nothing else.

"Hey, Rowan-san, could I get you to listen for a minute?"

"Hnh?"

Bringing Flop along, Subaru steeled himself and approached the table where Rowan lay collapsed. He shook the slumped man's shoulder and called out to him; Rowan raised his head, responding with a dim-witted grunt.
A flushed face, drowsy half-lidded eyes — features that should have been fairly presentable were thoroughly ruined by a nose gone entirely red.

"Whaddaya want, mister. What business does yer humble servant have with ye...?"

"You're wasted... anyway, listen. I want to hire you for a job... ugh, the smell of booze!"

"Oi, oi, that's rude, that is..."

Hauling himself up unsteadily, letting out a long breath, Rowan made Subaru turn his face away.
A tremendous reek of alcohol wafted over, distorting Subaru's vision as if it were an illusion. This was well beyond just drinking in the daytime. Even a heavy drinker had to have limits.

"Come to think of it, I've never had anyone close to me who drank this much... Roswaal and Otto barely drink more than a taste..."

Among the Emilia Camp, the ones with a drinking habit were Roswaal and Otto. Ram and Frederica drank a little, socially, and no one else touched alcohol at all.
Subaru himself was apparently of legal drinking age in this other world, but somehow he'd never gotten around to it, right up to today.

"So honestly, I don't have a great impression of drunks... hey, is this guy really the best fighter here!? This isn't some game to us."

"Funny, I'm not running this place as some game either. He's the one, no mistake."

With the proprietor's assurance renewed, Subaru turned back to Rowan with a bitter expression.
Rowan, still slouched sloppily in his chair, was tipping an empty glass upside down over his face, trying his best to catch the last dripping drops on his tongue.

"Tch, not enough booze. Oi, misters, buy old me a round, won'tcha?"

"Hm, you want a drink? Very well. Proprietor, a round for him—"

"Wait, wait, Flop-san! I know it's rich coming from someone who's been saved by your kindness, but could you put that goodwill back in your wallet for now!"

Silencing Flop, who'd been about to buy Rowan a drink at his mere request, Subaru moved to stand in front of the pouting man. Planting both hands firmly on the table, he said,

"Rowan-san, let's not beat around the bush. We're not buying you a drink. But we can pay you enough to buy your own. I want you to take a job."

"Ahn, a job, is it...?"

"That's right. I want you as a bodyguard. Starting right this instant."

Right in the man's alcohol-soaked breath, Subaru laid out those terms with all the composure he could muster.
Hearing this, Rowan, still drowsy-eyed, stared steadily back at Subaru.

"...Awful sharp edge to yer voice. Mister, don't tell me you're in a rather dicey spot?"

"Yeah, and it's no laughing matter. So, how about it? This is the max I can pay."

"Master, that's—"

Flop's eyes went wide as he tried to stop Subaru's action. But Subaru, instead of being stopped, pulled Flop back and, in a single motion, dropped a pouch onto the table with a thud — the whole sum of money they'd gotten from selling the Witchbeast's Horn.

Just to be safe, he hadn't split off a portion to give to Rem separately.
This was, quite literally, every last coin their party possessed. There was no larger amount he could offer than this.

"Oi, oi, this is..."

"That's my hand of cards, right there. So, will you take it?"

"――――"

Opening the pouch and glancing inside, Rowan fell silent. His face was as flushed as ever, but the drink-dulled look in his eyes had cleared, replaced by a serious, appraising gaze.
Of course, showing his entire fortune to Rowan — a man doing what amounted to mercenary work hired at a tavern — was quite the gamble.
Having crossed a national border, Subaru wasn't especially well-versed in the Empire's cost of living, but he didn't sense a huge gap in currency value compared to the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica.
With the amount Subaru had offered, a man could easily live comfortably, doing nothing, for a year.

"Yer humble servant's arm comes at a high price, I was about to say... but seeing this on the first offer..."

"Just so you know, I'm not open to negotiating a raise."

"I'm not doubting ye. And that talk of it not being a game — sounds like it's for real, too."

Rubbing his reddened nose with a finger, Rowan slowly rose from his seat. He picked up the pouch of gold coins Subaru had offered, tucking it carefully away inside his coat.

"Ask me to give it back, and I won't, y'know?"

"That'd be a shame, but I'm not going to ask for it back. Not if you do the job properly."

"Fuhah."

Laughing with alcohol-scented breath, Rowan accepted Subaru's job.
Hearing that answer, the tension that had gripped Subaru's shoulders the whole time finally eased. His nerves had stayed strung tight, anxiety endlessly dominating his chest, but—

"Master, was that really all right? That money..."

"It's fine. There's nothing more important than staying safe, right now, this instant. Sorry for startling you, Flop-san, and thank you for the introduction."

"—Well. If you say it's fine, then I say it's fine. Fortunately, the room's already paid for in advance!"

As for the room, it had already been paid for, covering not just Subaru and the others but Flop and Medium as well, who'd shared the room.
It wasn't the sort of unfair arrangement where Subaru hired Rowan on impulse and left Flop covering the lodging costs.

"Now then, my employer. I still haven't had the pleasure of your name."

"Ah, right. Sorry. I was in a hurry. My name's—"

"—Hold."

Asked for his name, Subaru scrambled, realizing he'd been unfair to Rowan yet again by not introducing himself. Just as he was about to give it properly, Rowan cut him off.
He held out a hand in front of Subaru's face, his sharp gaze fixed on something behind him — toward the tavern's window.

"Rowan-san?"

"...Odd feeling in the air. So, mister — you been followed, have ye?"

"—!"

At Rowan's low, hushed voice, Subaru caught his breath.
Beside him, Flop looked baffled, asking "What do you mean?" but only Subaru had any idea what presence Rowan had sensed.
It wasn't a matter of "could it be" — it was, unmistakably, exactly that. The strange presence Rowan had noticed, the thing that had been tracking Subaru, was the enemy that had attacked him in the alley.
The enemy who had killed Subaru three times over had not, even now, given up on him.

"Who exactly is after you?"

"...I'm not joking when I say I don't know who it is. But I figured, if we stayed outside, they'd make a move. That's why I hired you in such a hurry."

"I see, I see. Might've been worth asking for a bit more, at that."

Even if he'd turned the pouch upside down, there'd have been nothing more to shake out of it, but Rowan said it lightly all the same.
Still, though there was a faint tension and gravity in Rowan's tone, there was no anxiety in it. That, no doubt, was a sign of the confidence he had in his own skill.
Indeed, with the drunken haze fading from his eyes, Rowan's bearing carried the distinct air of a seasoned fighter.

"――――"

Rowan's weapon was the single sword worn at his left hip.
Even now, encountering a sword as an actual bladed weapon was a rarity in this other world. Amid a landscape dominated by Western-style swords, Rowan's blade was clearly out of place.
Looking closely, combined with the air of his tousled hair, Rowan's appearance had something vaguely reminiscent of a samurai about it. Though, lacking the sharpness of bearing, "ronin" might be more fitting.

"Rowan-san, the enemy is..."

"No need to get jittery. Whoever they are, if they stand before yer humble servant, they'll be cut clean in two. Got a bit of drink in me, and my blade's in fine form for it."

"Drunken sword instead of drunken fist? Never heard of that one."

Even so, it didn't sound like an empty boast, and Subaru trusted Rowan's confidence.
He circled around to the side opposite the window Rowan was glaring at, pulling on the sleeve of the still-baffled Flop.

"Flop-san, let's leave this to Rowan-san."

"Leave it to him or not, I don't even understand the situation in the first place! Master, and you, bodyguard-kun — just what exactly are we fighting?"

"I don't really—"

Before he could give an answer that would only stir up Flop's anxiety further with "I don't really know either," the situation moved faster than his words.
Though it wasn't a case of the enemy charging straight in, or Rowan leaping outside to meet them head-on.

"Bubeh..."

With that groan of pain spilling out, a large man came crashing through the tavern's entrance and collapsed onto the floor inside.
At the suddenness of it, Subaru literally jumped in surprise. But the rest of the tavern's patrons reacted with flat indifference, as though they were used to such sights.

The other drunks besides Rowan — likely doing similarly mercenary-adjacent work themselves — merely glanced at the fallen man with a minor reaction.
Only Flop and the tavern keeper rushed toward the collapsed man.
Though of the two, only Flop seemed genuinely worried about him — the keeper's face showed obvious distaste at the thought of the floor getting dirty.

"Hey there! Are you all right, hold on now!"

"Fu, fuga, fuga..."

"Fuga-fuga!? Is that your name!? Hold on there, Fuga-fuga-kun!"

"N-no, Flop-san, this person is..."

Flop tried to help the fallen man up, but the man's only response was a groan — hardly a proper introduction to a stranger they'd just met.
And besides, in a situation like this, a man crashing into a tavern like this—

"Fuga..."

"—! Flop-san! Get back!"

The man, lying face-down, grabbed the crouching Flop's arm as if trying to pull himself up.
At that instant, gripped by a bad feeling, Subaru yanked Flop backward, pulling him away from the man.

—And it was right after that.

"—!!"

Groaning "fuga, fuga," the man's body swelled, and in an instant blazed a brilliant, blinding red.
Then the swollen body burst, and a tremendous explosion tore through the interior of the tavern.

"Guah—!?"

Screams echoed through the tavern, the crimson light leaping from place to place. Almost at once it became clear that it was spreading flame, and that the blown-apart body of the man had been a human bomb.

"Flop-san!"

"Bu— I'm fine, Master! But this is..."

Buffeted together by the blast, Subaru and Flop stared in shock at the tavern, now a scene of utter chaos and lamentation.
The trick behind the man's body blowing apart had likely been a fire magic stone he'd been made to carry — judging by the way he'd been suffering, that fire stone might have been inside the man's own body.
It had exploded, and the fire had spread to everything nearby. But that wasn't all—

"Gehok! Wh-what is this!? My eyes, my eyes are—!"

Amid the surging flames, one of the drunks caught up in the chaos covered his face and screamed. Looking around, he wasn't the only one screaming — the men around him were the same.
Blown apart at the epicenter, now the center of the blaze, drifting smoke washed over the men, and they covered their eyes and noses, letting out shrieks of terrible agony.
And then—

"D-Don't stay inside! Everyone, get outside, quick!"

"Wai—"

A man streaming tears shouted, and the men with covered faces surged toward the entrance all at once. Seeing them shoving each other, Subaru instinctively tried to shout for them to stop.
The exploding man, and the painful smoke that followed — both seemed designed to sow chaos inside the tavern and drive everyone toward the exit.
But no matter how desperately Subaru shouted, he couldn't stop the feet of men driven onward by pain.
And as a result—

"—!?"

A second explosion went off, and the men who'd surged toward the entrance were blown apart together.
This second one was even bigger than the first, and the men caught in it, unable even to shield one another, were charred black, burned head-on by the blast fire.

The scorching heat and blast wave surged over him, and Subaru froze at the sight of the horrific scene.
One nightmarish sight after another, occurring one after another, too much to believe was real.

"This, can't..."

"Get a hold of yerself, employer! I can't have ye collapsing on me here!"

"—!"

Frozen, stunned, Subaru's collar was grabbed, forcibly hauling him up. The one doing it was Rowan, his face covered by his sleeve.
He gestured for them to keep low, so as not to breathe in the smoke that had tormented the men.

"Keeper! We're going out the back! The front's being watched!"

"A-Alright! This way...!"

At Rowan's instruction, the proprietor, blood streaming from a cut on his forehead, nodded obediently.
Moving behind the crawling proprietor, Rowan gestured with his hand and slapped Subaru's back, shouting "Go!"

"You too, employer, and you, mister! If you don't want to die, move now!"

"D-Dying is out of the question! My sister and I still have a road ahead of us...!"

"Damn it...!"

Spurred on by Rowan's shouted rebuke, Subaru and Flop followed after the proprietor leading the way. As he ran, Subaru's mind filled with confusion, and fury — at both the enemy and himself.
Of course he felt fury at the enemy, who had piled up this much atrocity. But he also felt fury at himself, for having so badly misjudged the danger even knowing an enemy existed.

To begin with, the enemy had made a Dragon Carriage run wild on the main street.
Even that must have caught a considerable number of people in its wake. He should have taken more seriously the fact that this was someone who cared nothing for the surrounding damage.
Because of that, the men who'd merely been in the tavern had been caught up in it too—

"—! The back door won't open!"

Ahead of Subaru, blaming himself, the proprietor, having reached the back door first, let out a cry.
He was struggling desperately to open it, but the door itself refused to answer his efforts. Whether the blast damage had warped the door, or—

"This is bad! The front entrance is caved in! At this rate—"

"Argh, outta the way! A door like this—"

In the tavern, red flames raging ever higher, black smoke on the verge of swallowing everything, Rowan burst forward, drowning out Flop's cry. He set his hand to the sword at his hip and drew it in a single sweeping flash toward the unyielding door—

"—Oh."

A streak of silver light ran through the air, cutting the sealed door down in a single stroke.
It was swordsmanship close to the iai techniques Subaru knew of. Though it was a stroke worth stopping to admire, Rowan showed no pride in it, simply kicking the remains of the door apart.

"Alright! It's open! Everyone, keep low!"

Throwing open the sealed back door, Rowan shouted back to Subaru and the others behind him.
The chaos spreading through the place was tremendous, but from this dead-certain doom of a burning tavern, it looked like they might finally escape.
Rowan's judgment and skill in the heat of battle had proven solid. Now all that remained was to get away from here immediately, and deal with this vicious enemy—

"――――"

And then, suddenly, Subaru held his breath.
Something had snagged in his mind. — He was trying to predict the thinking of this thorough, merciless enemy.

Turn a man into a human bomb and hurl him into the tavern, drive out the people inside with irritant smoke, then blast them all at once with a magic stone rigged at the entrance.
And then seal the entrance, and block the back door too by some means. They'd broken through that blockade, and now Subaru and the others were about to escape out the back—

"Rowan-sa—"

Something was off, Subaru tried to voice that faint sense of wrongness.
Wouldn't such a thorough enemy never let up their attack? To convey that unease, Subaru called out to Rowan. There was no answer.

"――――"

With a lurch, Rowan's body, leaning out through the back door, tilted backward and fell.
Subaru, Flop, and the proprietor stared in stunned silence at the fallen Rowan. His head, above the forehead, had been caved in, his eyeballs forced out and dangling.
There was no mistaking it, to anyone who saw — Rowan's skull had been crushed, and he was dead.

"Hi."

Seeing that gruesome death, the proprietor's eyes went wide, and he tried to scream.
But even that, he couldn't manage. A blow swung from beyond the flames plunged into the chest of the proprietor, who'd drawn a deep breath, and mercilessly tore his upper body apart. His chest was wrenched open, bone and organs scattered as he tumbled and rolled among the flames.
The smell of charring flesh rose almost immediately, but no scream came from the proprietor. He'd died instantly.

"――――"

Amid the roiling black smoke, something slowly emerged from beyond the flames.
Climbing over the door that had been cut down and kicked in, what appeared was the silhouette of a man, his face wrapped in a wet cloth.
The man gripped a long-handled axe in his hand — the very thing, Subaru understood, that had crushed Rowan's skull and killed the proprietor instantly. But that was as far as his understanding went.

"J-Just who are you. Why would you do such a thing."

Stunned, unable to move, beside Subaru, Flop, who had likewise witnessed the deaths of Rowan and the proprietor, spoke with a trembling voice. Swallowed by fear, he still glared with earnest eyes at the silhouette in the flames.
Hating this vicious man who had massacred the tavern's people without mercy, one after another.
But—

"How could such a thing be allowed—o"

"――――"

Resolute, as Flop tried to condemn that wickedness, the man's axe struck his head, direct.
No striking with the blunt side, no such meaningless gesture. Blade set true, the man struck down through Flop's forehead, beneath his long bangs, and spilled its contents outward.

Brain matter scattered, the red of blood mingling with the red of flame, and Flop died.
With a grating, wet sound, Flop's body crumpled to the floor. The blood, spreading gradually, soiled the seat of Subaru's pants where he'd slumped to the floor. — No, Subaru's pants were already soiled with something else entirely. Wet with terror.

Amid the flames, at the sight of the man's terrible figure, axe lowered and dripping blood, Subaru felt terror.
At his tenacity, his relentlessness, his sheer horror, Subaru's soul trembled.

"Wh, why..."

It was a hopelessly foolish question to ask.
Flop had asked something similar, and been killed without mercy for it. Naturally, there was no reason the man would show mercy to Subaru alone. Subaru would die too. Be killed.
He had no strength left to back away, no courage left to crawl. He couldn't take his eyes off the man now.

Even though it was terrifying to look at the man, it was just as terrifying to look away, and he couldn't manage that either.

"Why... why!"

Like a fool with only one thing to say, Subaru screamed it desperately, over and over.
Hearing that scream did nothing to make the man answer. Face still hidden, he slowly raised the axe, holding it aloft, straight above the head of the slumped Subaru.
And then—

"Why!"

"—I'm not telling you a damn thing, am I? Wouldn't want you slipping away on me again, now would I."

To Subaru's blood-spitting scream, the man finally answered, at the very last.
Hearing that tone, matter-of-fact, as though stating something perfectly obvious, Subaru caught his breath.
Trying to match that voice to something he'd heard, somewhere, before.

"Bu—"

Before he could, the axe swung down and split Subaru's skull open.
A small, hard sound.

Sounded.
