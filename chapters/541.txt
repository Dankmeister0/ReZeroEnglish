Arc 7 Chapter 43: "Behind the Eyelids"

——The proposal that hoarse voice spun out left Subaru and the others thoroughly bewildered.

"――――"

Olbart Dunkelkenn, the perpetrator behind the "Infantilization" that had struck Subaru and the others.
In order to get him to undo the "Infantilization," Subaru and company had sought to negotiate with him, and the condition Olbart presented was as bizarre and mind-muddling as befit the old man — no, the eerie old man — who had toyed with their bodies and hearts every step of the way so far. Because, of all things—

"Wait, a game of tag...?"

Subaru, who had been dreading what manner of impossible demand might be hurled at him, haltingly repeated back what he'd just heard.
At the sound of Subaru's voice, Olbart, hands now empty, arched a suspicious eyebrow.

"What's with that question mark. Don't tell me you don't even know what tag is?"

"Of course I know the word. It's just not the word I expected to hear in this situation."

However you dressed it up, "tag" was nothing but a children's game.
It seemed unlikely to carry some other meaning. Though, granted, if it was a game of tag played out in a Shinobi village, there was a decent chance it came with its own special rules.
For example—

"Something like, 'during tag, killing each other is fair game'?"

"Ka-ka-ka-ka! As if. If a scary game like that caught on, the Shinobi'd wipe themselves out in no time flat. And by my own hand as their chief, no less. Ain't that a bit much?"

Olbart denied Subaru's concern, but given his murderous conduct and everything he'd said and done up to now, Subaru had come to see him as a man who wouldn't hesitate to stain his hands with the blood of his own kin if he judged it necessary.
Honestly, it wouldn't even come as much of a surprise if the Shinobi Village had already been wiped out.

"—This game of tag. What sort of arrangement is it?"

It was Abel who pressed on, taking over for Subaru, whose body was being slowly scorched by mounting suspicion.
At Abel's prompting, Olbart twisted his cheek into a grin and said, "Oh," 

"Seems you're surprisingly keen on this, masked whelp."

"Fool. We're well past the stage of choosing whether to accept this wager or not. That threshold was crossed the moment we revealed that we hold the information you desire."

"Ka-ka-ka-ka! Well, that's about the size of it."

Olbart opened his mouth wide enough that his jaw looked ready to come unhinged, and laughed shamelessly.
In truth, Abel's point was correct, and Olbart's scheme was malicious. Regardless of who won or lost this game of tag, Olbart would get the information he wanted.
Whether it came through negotiation or came at the end of torture — letting them choose the method by which the information got wrung out of them was the whole purpose of this "tag."

"Now then, as for how tag works, ain't nothin' special about it. One side runs, one side catches 'em... ah, but it'd help me out more if I'm the one runnin'. See, chasin' down a whole crowd of you — that's a lot for an old geezer's stamina to handle."

"So if we catch the fleeing grandpa, we win? Easy to understand!"

"Easy to understand, yes, but..."

Medium reacted optimistically to Olbart's rule explanation, while Taritta reacted pessimistically.
Subaru's own opinion, if anything, leaned closer to Taritta's. The rules were indeed simple, with no room for uncertain factors to intervene — in other words, raw ability would decide it outright.
And in terms of raw ability, the combined strength of Subaru's group wouldn't come close to Olbart's.

"Well, might be rough goin' with nothin' but a buncha little runts. If that's the case, I could loosen the terms a bit, you know."

"The very man responsible for that, saying it without a shred of shame...!"

"Now don't go gettin' mad at me. If my finger slips off this bowstring, well, I'd have grounds to call it self-defense, and this'd all go a lot quicker."

"――――"

Olbart licked his gaze over the faces in the room and shrugged, and Taritta ground her back teeth.
Just as the eerie old man had pointed out, Taritta's bow remained trained on Olbart without wavering. And yet the man himself ignored it with a cool expression, going so far as to use it as a bargaining chip.
Subaru could well imagine the anguish that caused Taritta. But right now, the priority was—

"What do you mean, loosen the terms?"

"Talkin' about how the game of tag's played. Instead of havin' to catch me, how about a condition where findin' me counts as a win? Only, in that case, we make it best of three."

"Best of three..."

"I hide three times. You lot find me three times. Fail that, and you lose. In that case, guess it ain't so much 'tag' as 'find-me'... nah, that sounds kinda weird."

Olbart tilted his head, muttering that it didn't quite sit right.
Swallowing down the old man's proposal, a word rose up from the pit of Subaru's stomach—

"—Hide and seek?"

"Oh, that's a good name. Let's go with that."

Snapping his fingers, Olbart latched onto the ring of "hide and seek."
Then, holding up both hands, he raised one finger on each, displaying them for all to see, sweeping them left and right.

"With tag, all you gotta do is catch me once. With hide and seek, you gotta find me three times. — Which one d'you figure gives you better odds? Ain't like I gotta spell it out, right?"

Closing one eye, Olbart posed the question, and Subaru caught his breath.
Just as Olbart said, it wasn't even worth thinking over. Subaru, who already knew the scope of Olbart's transcendent abilities, had no reason whatsoever to choose tag.
And yet—

"That's awfully generous of you. Going out of your way to suggest the method that gives us the better odds — makes me start wondering what's behind it."

Al pressed the point, needling Olbart, who was trying to hand over the advantage of his own accord.
In place of the helmet he'd taken off, Al had a cloth wrapped around his face. His voice was somewhat muffled through the fabric, but since he'd shrunk younger, his voice pitch had risen, so it wasn't so much that it was hard to make out.
Olbart, too, caught Al's words without missing a beat, and shrugged.

"Oi oi, you thinkin' I'm some geezer who's gotta win no matter what, whelp? Way I see it, I'd be more'n happy if you lot won. This whole thing, I'm doin' it to find out if your story's worth listenin' to in the first place."

"――――"

"Personally, I'm real interested in what you lot have to say. But if I get fed some convenient lie and end up doubtin' my loyalty to His Excellency for nothin', that ain't good for my old-age savings, is it? So I don't let that happen — I use what little blood still flows to this old head o' mine and cook up somethin' like this."

Waving the raised fingers on both hands, Olbart answered Al breezily.
Even hearing that answer, it probably didn't wipe away Al's suspicion. But it was true the reasoning held together, and it seemed to give him pause about pressing further.

Subaru wasn't naive enough to believe what had just been said was Olbart's unvarnished true feelings.
But to lay bare the true intentions of this seasoned, eerie old Shinobi, they lacked both the time and the material.

Even as they stood here, Yorna's stated deadline crept closer, second by second.
Given the constrained choice Olbart had presented — a situation where neither option could be called ideal — the chance of coming up with some way to turn things around from here was next to nil.
More than that, one could even say that giving it their all was exactly what had led them to this very situation.

"—If we accept your proposal, we'll need to make the terms explicit."

"Ho, and what's that supposed to mean?"

"You said it yourself. If it benefits us more to win, then we shouldn't leave much room for underhanded maneuvering. — Let both sides' terms be made clear."

"—. Ka-ka-ka-ka."

Abel, who seemed to have reached the same conclusion first, pressed forward, fixing his gaze on Olbart.
The demand for explicit rules — what lay behind that stance was plain enough. It was proof that they had already decided to accept the wager, and had already decided on the terms of the wager they would accept.
Olbart, laughing low, met the gaze of those gleaming eyes, and Abel nodded.
And then—

"—Hide and seek it is."

—Afterward, the terms exchanged between the two parties came down to three main points.

The first was a mutual pledge to "do no harm to each other."
To begin with, Olbart could easily wipe them all out if he felt like it. The only things holding him back from such an atrocity were the fake emperor's orders and his wariness of Yorna.
But since he might well break that restraint depending on circumstances, this pledge was essential.

The second was that "the hiding spots be limited to within the city."
If one believed the stated premise of leaving them some chance of winning, limiting the area was also indispensable for maintaining fairness. Of course, even limited to within the city, the usable area was still quite vast.
Still, on this point, the condition Subaru had gotten them to swallow should do some work.

And the third was "making the victory conditions explicit."
They'd gone to the trouble of choosing "hide and seek" because it offered better odds. So no one could fault them for pursuing every chance at victory.
Hence—

"I get that our odds change between tag and hide and seek. But why three times? Why not just make it one round each, no need to be stingy?"

"Ka-ka-ka-ka! That's askin' for too much, young'un. Not to put too fine a point on it, but — one round, there's room for a fluke. Three rounds, though, that's skill."

"There's an argument that luck is part of skill too."

"Unfortunately, I don't believe in luck or fate. Heck, most folks in the Empire feel that way. You're the odd one out, sayin' somethin' like that."

It was a thoroughly inescapable way of thinking, befitting an Empire built on the principle that ability is everything.
Neither fortune nor misfortune existed — every outcome shown was, in this view, brought about by one's own ability alone. Such an attitude sounded suffocating for anyone who couldn't survive without some place to retreat to.
Someone like Subaru during his hikikomori days, for instance, would have had nowhere to belong in this Empire.

"Also, how about this — since there's three of you lot who got shrunk, three rounds. Sounds like a pretty solid excuse for somethin' I just thought up on the spot, don't it?"

"Then shall we undo the spell on one of us each time we find you?"

"Oh, tryin' to weasel in on that, are ya. Fine, scratch that excuse then."

Waving both hands vigorously, Olbart rejected Abel's confirmation.
In any case, once they'd hashed things out this far, there was nothing left that needed scrutinizing.
In other words—

"Well then, shall we get this show on the road?"

"Olbart, small thing, but let me confirm again — the hiding places don't include anywhere we physically can't reach. If you hide somewhere out of the way, there's nothing we can do about it."

"You really are a stickler for detail. — Well, I woulda done that even without you sayin' it."

"――――"

"Just so you know, I'm bein' straight when I say I ain't gotta win no matter what. But don't forget — this here's a test of your mettle."

Which was to say, they were being watched for tact, cunning, and quickness of mind as well.
For all his easygoing, unhurried air, Olbart was a man who cut things off with severe judgment. If he decided something wasn't worth seeing, he'd gouge out their vital point without mercy.
Just now too, if Subaru hadn't spoken up, he'd probably have gone through with it in earnest.
Though, of course—

"—Figured that masked whelp there wouldn't let it slide, on that front."

Jerking his chin toward Abel, Olbart showed a smile that made no attempt to hide his cunning.
Feeling the trouble that posed even as he did, Subaru exchanged glances with Abel and Al, and seeing the two of them give a small nod, resolved himself to take on the challenge.

"If we win, you're turning us back."

"If I win, you'll be waitin' ten years to get back to normal. Well, if His Excellency and that fox girl have a change of heart, I'll be gettin' your secrets my own way."

At the eerie old man's hint that this meant the interrogation techniques passed down in the Shinobi Village — methods bordering on torture — Subaru felt a chill well up within him, his black eyes reflecting Olbart.
And then, before the eerie old man headed off to his first hiding place, Subaru pressed him with a question.

"—So, what's the first hint?"

It was the kind of question that, if this were a genuine, serious contest, could be dismissed as ridiculous.
And yet Olbart didn't laugh it off. Because this was exactly the condition Subaru had gotten him to accept for victory in this "hide and seek" against Olbart.

Given that Subaru and the others had been "Infantilized," what Olbart wanted to see to evaluate them wasn't combat strength or physical ability, but rather — as mentioned before — quickness of mind and inventiveness.
Put simply, it came down to nothing more than the entirely natural sentiment: "I don't do business with fools."

"First off, a little warm-up... I'll hide near this inn, in 'behind the eyelid.'"

"—Behind the... eyelid."

"Now then, do your best, young'uns. Let an old geezer with not much time left savor at least this much of a good time."

Waving his hand idly, Olbart turned his back on Subaru and the others. — In that instant, Subaru felt the tension in the room draw taut against that unhurriedly retreating back.

"――――"

At this very moment, surely not one of them had failed to think that if only they could pounce on Olbart and settle everything at once — hide and seek, "Infantilization," every problem lumped together — that would be ideal.
But no one carried out that reckless act. And that was the right call.

"Ka-ka-ka-ka."

Just before the door shut, Olbart laughed — perhaps because he'd sensed the nature of Subaru and the others' hesitation.
Including that hoarse laughter, it was, to the very end, conduct fitting of the name "Wicked Old Man."
And so, once Olbart had left the room and only allies remained inside—

"Taritta, lower your bow. There's no one left to aim it at."

"...Yes."

In the room now emptied of the enemy, Abel had Taritta lower her drawn bow.
Taritta's face, as she followed the order, showed a shade of mortification. Understandably so — Olbart had kept his cool expression unbroken the whole time she'd had her bow trained on him.
As one of the Shudrak — no, as the one already entrusted with the role of the next chieftain — from Taritta's perspective, that eerie old man's attitude was tantamount to mocking the strength of the Shudrak.
She had let Olbart hold the "People of Shudrak" in contempt.
And that, because of her own lack of strength.

Originally, the reason Taritta had accompanied them to the Cursed Metropolis was to seek encouragement to push her resolve to succeed as chieftain.
—As things stood, far from gaining confidence, this could only have the opposite effect.

"You seriously plannin' on playing along with that old man's little game, brother?"

Al spoke up to Subaru, who couldn't find the words to say to Taritta.
Al, still not quite settled into his masked getup, had a tone tinged with irritation and dissatisfaction over the exchange with Olbart. Given the impression that Al usually took everything in stride and let things roll off him, such a reaction felt unexpected.
That said, having been thoroughly outmaneuvered by Olbart, Subaru understood the frustration.

"I'm not exactly thrilled about getting played like that either. But we managed to keep a shot at this, barely, right?"

"A shot at this, sure, but..."

"A shot at us getting back to normal, right, Subaru-chin?"

Medium, sitting cross-legged on the floor, was restraining a squirming Rui. With their size difference now barely noticeable, she was holding Rui down not through brute strength but through technique.
Likely a skill honed at the orphanage where she'd grown up, whatever that was.
Soothing Rui like that, she turned her blue eyes to Subaru, who nodded, "Yeah."

"If we'd soured his mood back there, our bodies would've stayed shrunk like this... and that's unacceptable, both for future plans and for the road ahead. Getting back to normal is a must."

"...Could've gone with surrounding the old man and beating him down instead, you know."

"Don't be stupid. The only reason we didn't die was because Olbart was planning to obey that emperor's orders. If he'd been serious, we wouldn't have stood a chance."

"—. You say that like you actually saw it happen."

Averting his eyes from Subaru, Al said it in a tone that seemed to say "no way."
Al's jab that Subaru talked "like he'd actually seen it happen" was, in fact, an apt one — since it amounted to cheating by peeking across worlds. In truth, Subaru had seen it with his own eyes.
Even the things Al considered impossible did happen, whether he wanted to admit it or not.
Still—

"――――"

Keeping his eyes averted, Al didn't argue further.
Al, too, had grasped a fragment of Olbart's true capability at the castle tower yesterday. He surely understood how slim their odds would be if that eerie old man — who likely hadn't even been serious back then — ever showed his true strength.
That was precisely why he'd made the call to watch Olbart's retreating back and let it go.

"Regardless, we're short on time. If we're going to meet Yorna, I'll need time to do my makeup too. Counting everything, I want thirty minutes, so... that leaves us a bit over two hours."

"So you really do have to cross-dress, huh..."

"The three from yesterday are requesting it too. And... "

As he spoke, Subaru turned his gaze toward the silent Abel.
Since he hadn't interjected, it seemed he was basically on the same page as Subaru. Surely he wasn't lost in reflection over having just crossed through a brush with death.
Or was it—

"Shocked that Olbart's got his eye on the emperor's life, or something?"

"Fool. I was aware he harbored ambitions beyond his station. Though, I hadn't imagined it would reach as far as the emperor's head. I assumed there was nothing to be gained by such an act, but to think what he seeks is a final sense of accomplishment and infamy after death..."

Abel shrugged his thin shoulders, saying he truly couldn't fathom it.
Abel — a man who, even driven from the throne and cornered into an utterly hopeless position, knew no such thing as giving up.
From the perspective of a man who considered it the best course to live, return to the throne, and reclaim the imperial seat, an attitude of throwing away one's own life in pursuit of posthumous fame was surely incomprehensible.
On that point, though it was complicated, Subaru shared the same view. He had no desire to be praised after death.

Subaru wanted the important people left behind, after his own death, to forget him.
Rather than have them grieve or suffer over it, he was the type who'd rather, the instant he died, become nothing more than a memory — happy to be forgotten.
Still, he had no intention of sharing that sentiment with Abel.

"...But it's kind of surprising Olbart's got a blind spot too. Having ambitions like that, and he doesn't notice the true identity of the guy standing right in front of him at all."

So, to cover himself, Subaru steered the topic elsewhere.
But the moment he heard that, Abel's eyes, even through the demon mask, clearly took on a look of bewilderment.

"—. What are you talking about?"

"Huh?"

"I am wearing this mask. Naturally, that accounts for Olbart's reaction."

Touching a finger to the cheek of the demon mask, Abel stated this flatly.
Subaru knitted his brows at the remark, his expression showing he didn't understand. At once, the bewilderment behind Abel's demon mask shifted into blatant disappointment.
With that same look still in his eyes, Abel let out a deep sigh.

"This mask has the effect of distorting others' perception. An effect that conceals the identity of its wearer."

"Wha—?! You mean... don't tell me it's 'Perception Obstruction'!? But wasn't that mask originally something that belonged to the Shudrak settlement...?"

"Y-yes, that's correct. Long ago, when the emperor and the Shudrak forged their bond of friendship, it's said the emperor used it to visit the forest without being recognized."

"It had that kind of history behind it...?"

With this astonishing fact revealed, Subaru's jaw hung open in shock. To Subaru, reeling from that blow, Abel, still sustaining his disappointment, continued—

"Did you seriously believe, all this time, that I was wearing this mask on a whim or for show?"

"――――"

"Your foolishness has truly reached new heights. To begin with, the act of concealing one's face is invariably accompanied by a corresponding reason. Surely you didn't think I came to this Cursed Metropolis on some sightseeing whim?"

"You didn't have to go that far — you could've just explained that from the start with one sentence...!"

Granted, Subaru himself was at fault for assuming it was just one of Abel's eccentricities and never asking. But surely Abel bore some blame too, for assuming it would be understood without any explanation.
Thanks to that, Subaru ended up suffering embarrassment he needn't have felt, and worry he needn't have carried.

In any case, it now made sense why Olbart hadn't noticed Abel's true identity.
Just hiding his face — with his haughty way of speaking and overbearing manner completely unconcealed — Abel had, without even trying, made all of Subaru's worrying over when his identity might be exposed a needless concern after all.

"Hey, hey, Al-chin, Al-chin. Abel-chin's saying all that, but is there some reason Al-chin's hiding his face too?"

"In my case it's a complex about a scar on my face... anyway, forget about me. Hide and seek's already started. We gotta get moving already."

Saying that, what Al pointed to with his hand was outside the window — the streets of the Cursed Metropolis.
Chaosflame, counted among the Empire's great cities, was probably a good five times larger or more than even the fortified city of Guaral.
Finding a hidden Shinobi chief somewhere within all that was no ordinary feat.
And that, with only two hours and a mere six people to do it, three times over.

"You said it was just a warm-up the first time, but even just 'around the inn' leaves plenty of candidates. You two got any kind of plan, brother, Abel-chan?"

"Wouldn't call it a plan exactly, but..."

At the very least, Subaru had something he wanted to try.
This warm-up round of "hide and seek" — unbeknownst to itself, shared the same aim as Olbart's own. An idea worth testing once, if only to gauge the thinking of their eerie old opponent.
As if in step with Subaru's own thinking, Abel crossed his arms and nodded, "Naturally."

"There are a few approaches. Though no doubt that clown over there thinks differently."

"Demoted from strategist to clown — you're really holding a grudge over that mask thing, huh. ...No, I mean, it's not like I'm attached to calling you strategist or anything."

Whether it was the fact that his masked disguise had been treated as mere eccentricity that truly rankled him, Abel's voice turned cold, and Subaru could only twist his lips and groan.
In any case, even as they discussed this here, time kept ticking away relentlessly.

"Better get moving before we forget what it felt like to have long arms and legs."

"Well, me and brother here never had long legs to begin with, so..."

"I'm talking relative to how things are now."

Brushing aside Al's jab about how they'd all been short-legged together, the group readied minimal equipment.
Al slung his dao, sheath and all, on his back; Medium fastened just one of her twin swords at the small of her back. Subaru, for his part, carried his whip along too, though honestly he had no confidence in wielding it. As for the rest — Abel and Taritta, both in full readiness, and in addition—

"Ah, uh!"

"Ooh, Rui-chan's full of energy! Alright, let's do our best and find that old grandpa!"

"Uh!"

At the voice of Rui, who couldn't very well be left behind, Medium raised a small fist.
Realistically, there was a high chance Rui would end up a liability. Since Olbart had been forbidden from changing hiding spots, if he sensed the noisy Rui approaching, he presumably wouldn't try to put distance between them — that, at least, was what they wanted to believe.

"Isn't there a chance he keeps shifting his hiding spot bit by bit and then insists he's been in the same place the whole time?"

"I corrected that point too, but Olbart's aim isn't his own victory. Going to such a roundabout length is to gauge our caliber through this game."

"Gauge our caliber, huh. That basically means..."

"Whether we have the ability to back up such big talk as aiming for the emperor's throne."

To Al, who doubted Olbart's true intentions, Abel stated this plainly.
Subaru, too, saw no lie in it. He felt some resistance to taking Olbart's own claims at face value, but given that his objective was the emperor's life, it suited Olbart's purposes as well for Subaru's group's aims and the information they held to be believable and genuine.
Olbart wanted to be convinced by having Subaru and the others demonstrate their ability.
To that end, as well—

"I didn't ask, but that talk about the flames of the 'Sunlight Sword'..."

"Unfortunately, there's no time to lecture at length on the history of the Vollachian emperors. Setting aside your shallow schemes, my own strategy isn't something that can be set in motion within a single room at an inn."

"Tch, fine, got it."

Faced with an evasion that made it clear he had no intention of revealing anything, Subaru clicked his tongue.
Still, if what Olbart had said was true, it seemed to suggest that Abel — the Vollachian Emperor — had some kind of protection, and that as long as it held, he couldn't be harmed.

"Now that I think about it..."

Up to now, Subaru had never once witnessed Abel actually lose his life with his own eyes.
Since coming to this Vollachian Empire, Subaru had been exposed to mortal danger at a considerably rapid pace, actually dying and undergoing "Return by Death" — yet there had never once been a case where he'd been present at Abel's death. — There had been situations where, given the circumstances, Abel was surely dead.
Exactly like the time Olbart had revealed his true nature before, or when Todd had burned down the Bardheim Forest along with the Shudrak settlement — times when, as a captive, Abel's life should have been forfeit.
Only, that was merely how the situation appeared; Subaru hadn't directly confirmed his death himself. — So there was one thing he wanted to make sure of.

"That flame from the 'Sunlight Sword' — is it still protecting you, even now?"

"You're being tiresome. Or is it that, unless I answer, you'll turn against me as well?"

"...There's no merit in doing that."

His cheek twisting at the mocking remark, Subaru gave up pressing the matter, this time for good.
And so, having finished readying to leave the inn, Subaru swept his gaze around at the faces of his companions.

"Didn't expect to end up having to play a game like this. But if the condition is just finding the guy, it should still be a fair contest even with half the members shrunk down. If anything, with everyone this close to being kids again in body and mind, now might actually be the more advantageous time for a game of hide and seek."

"Wh-what kind of logic is thaT...?"

"Ignore it. Not worth listening to — pointless nonsense."

In truth, it was nothing more than a quip, so the response was fair, but Subaru let a furrow of his brow register his displeasure anyway.
With that done, Subaru raised his voice louder than necessary and said, "Anyway!"

"Everyone, pay maximum attention to your surroundings. — Alright, let's go!"

"Yeah!"
"Auu!"

"...You're pretty fired up, brothers."

Subaru, dressed in whatever clothes were available, cut a rather ungainly figure as he psyched himself up. Medium and Rui, following suit, thrust their fists up, and Al let out a sigh at the sight.
Meanwhile, Taritta, now their greatest combat asset, had her eyes tinged with tension and unease, while Abel, his expression unreadable behind the demon mask, remained as arrogantly empty-handed as ever.

With that lineup, they all filed out of the inn room at once.
First, to pin down Olbart's first hiding spot for round one. Fired up with that resolve, Subaru shut the door with a loud bang and stepped forward vigorously—

"—And, back already."

"Huh?"

Kicking off the floor hard with the foot he'd just stomped down, Subaru's body spun halfway around. Seeing him turn to face the door he'd just come out of, Al let out a dumbfounded sound.
Paying that no mind, Subaru shoved the door open with force.
And then, pointing into the room they'd just departed from—

"—Olbart-san, found you."

"Ka-ka-ka-ka! Oi oi, figurin' that out right away — ain't that just mean-spirited? Somethin' like this, gettin' seen through in a flash, I oughta be blushin', that's downright embarrassin'!"

Olbart, who had slipped into the room just as Subaru and the others left, laughed heartily, thoroughly delighted at Subaru's first declaration as he pointed at him.


△▼△▼△▼△


"For real, brother...!"

Inside the room, Al gaped at the sight of Olbart, who'd been in the middle of re-brewing his now-cold tea.
Following his shock, Medium and Rui chimed in with "Ah, it's grandpa!" and "Aah!" while Taritta, peeking into the room as well, widened her eyes.
Trailing behind those reactions, Abel, the last to lay eyes on Olbart, narrowed his eyes.

"I see. 'Behind the eyelid,' hm."

He murmured, looking down at Subaru's young profile.

"Did you know it was here from the very start?"

"The phrase 'behind the eyelid,' plus the first candidate that came to mind around this inn — this was it. Guys who pull this kind of challenge always try this trick at least once."

As Subaru answered, what came back to his mind was a memory from long, long ago and dearly nostalgic — the memory of the first time he'd met Beatrice at the old Roswaal Manor.
Beatrice had used the power of magic to create a looping corridor, trying to make Subaru walk the same space over and over, endlessly. Unfortunately, Subaru had seen through her scheme at a single glance, promptly figuring out that the very first starting point was, in fact, the goal.

Thinking back on it now, he reflected with some regret that he should have played along with adorable Beatrice's little scheme. But his opponent here wasn't the mischievous Beatrice — he was a nasty, ill-natured old eerie man, so there was no need to hold back.
So, twisting his young face into a grand look of triumph, he gloated without mercy.

"Honestly, I saw right through it, 'Wicked Old Man.'"

"Oi oi, rubbin' it in — spare an old man that, would ya. This sorry state, ain't somethin' I could ever show the young'uns back at the village. Say, why don't you go by 'Wicked Young Man' instead?"

"I'll pass on that. Besides, I haven't really done anything worth calling wicked."

"Well now, ain't that modest of you. — Seein' as you went and made a big show of announcin' your departure loud enough for me to hear on purpose. Quite the belly act, that."

Raising one eyebrow, Olbart fixed Subaru with a steady stare, and Subaru scratched his cheek.
Well, if he pulled off something that theatrical, of course his intent would be read. Though, even if Olbart had seen through Subaru's true aim, there was no way for him to change his hiding spot.
Because the rules of this contest had him decide his hiding place first, then relay the hint.

"That's a pretty quick clear on the first round, but you'll still count it properly as one, right? Don't tell me you're gonna get sour just because I turned down succeeding to the title of 'Wicked.'"

Subaru couldn't afford to have Olbart excuse himself out of it just because the result had been anticlimactic.
At Subaru's confirming question, born of that concern, Olbart closed one eye and said, "Course."

"That it wrapped up quick was my own slip-up. Havin' you lot clean up after my mess, that wouldn't quite add up. As agreed, two more rounds to go."

"Ohh~! Way to go, Subaru-chin! First round done in like ten seconds!"

"Yeah, good news."

Alongside Medium's overjoyed reaction, Subaru let out a breath of relief, patting his chest.
For now, Olbart's first riddle — yes, this was, in a sense, a test wearing the name of "hide and seek." One could even say they were being made to solve a test question.
Whether they'd meet Olbart's standards or not — that was what was being tested.

"And that's all thanks to Bea-ko's..."

Meaning to say "thanks to her," Subaru tried to conjure the image of the lovely girl in his mind.
But that thought, for an instant, whited out and stopped.

"――――"

A faint sense of wrongness, a small snag, raising a rough patch on his heart.
Before he could chase down a clear answer for what it was—

"Well then, guess I oughta go make up for my honor, better hurry off to the second hidin' spot."

"Ah, ah, right. So, what's the next hint?"

"Hmm... the next hiding spot, let's see, something like—"

Clasping his hands behind his lower back, Olbart tilted his head and body in thought. But that deliberation didn't take long; the eerie old man twisted his cheek into a grin and said—

"—'A pit with a fine view,' I'll say."

"A fine viE..."
"A pit?"

Taritta and Al picked up the words in turn, repeating back the bizarre, mystifying hint for the next hiding place.
But Olbart had no intention of leaving any hint beyond that.

"First round exceeded expectations, but the real match starts here."

Saying that, the old man showed his white teeth in a grin, then leapt backward, light and spry. In one motion, Olbart threw open the room's window and casually put a foot up on the sill.
Then, before the wide-eyed gazes of Subaru and the others, he leapt outside.

"Next time, I'll hide somewhere with a bit more bite to it!"

"Wait, old man! ...Damn it, he's already gone!"

Rushing to the window in a panic, Al peered outside and clutched his masked head, crying out.
In truth, catching Olbart when he committed fully to fleeing was next to impossible. That overwhelming speed of escape only reinforced their certainty that switching the contest from tag to hide and seek had been the right call.
In any case—

"Subaru, that man's next hiding spot..."

"Yeah, yeah, can you figure it out, Subaru-chin? Got any ideas?"

"Uh... that's, um..."

Met with expectant looks from Taritta and Medium as they turned toward him, Subaru found himself at a loss for words.
The hint for the second hiding place, "a pit with a fine view" — honestly, Subaru couldn't find any foothold in his mind connecting to anything resembling an answer.

"Sorry. Nothing's coming to me right away. Not because I read his mind or anything — I just read how things would play out that first time."

"I see~, sorry! Can't just keep relying on you! Let's think about it together!"

"Yes, that's righT. I'm not exactly good at using my head eitheR, but I'll think on it."

To Subaru, who lowered his head feeling inadequate, Medium and Taritta answered like that.
The first hiding spot being the starting point had been, in a sense, a kind of expected trope playing out as expected. Even if the guess had been wrong, since it was a place that would've cost them almost no time loss, it had been something they could afford to try lightly. But from here on—

"Simply put, it's going to come down to a race against time."

"...So even you can't guess the next hiding spot, brother. Well, guess that makes sense."

"'That makes sense,' huh... maybe my hopes weren't high to begin with, but hearing it put so plainly still stings."

At the very outset of the full-fledged "hide and seek" match, Subaru got stabbed in the back by an ally right out of the gate.
Of course, Subaru accepted that the assessment was fair, but honestly, he'd have appreciated at least a little tact. Even among close friends, there's such a thing as a dress code.
But at Subaru's words, Al waved a hand, saying "Ah, that's not it,"

"It's no wonder brother wouldn't know. I mean, after all, I'm here with you."

"Huh? Not following at all. Is this some kind of 'having Al around lowers my IQ' thing? How does that even work?"

"It's not really that either, it's just hard to explain... right?"

"Don't ask me to agree when I don't even get it."

It was strange that even as he spoke, Al himself sounded unsure of his own words.
Setting aside Al, who was tilting his head in confusion, this topic didn't seem likely to head anywhere constructive from here. Unraveling the "pit with a fine view" that Olbart had left behind was the top priority.

"It was the first room for 'behind the eyelid.' I figure 'a pit with a fine view' is probably a similar kind of rephrasing, but..."

"If it has a fine view, wouldn't that suggest somewhere high up?"

"But isn't a pit supposed to be a hole? If it's a hole, wouldn't it be down in the ground?"

Or perhaps there was some spot in Chaosflame that was actually described that way.
Either way, it didn't seem like they'd reach an answer with the amount of information available in a room at the inn. It seemed they'd really need to head out into the city after all.

"...My head's just not working well. Come to think of it, what about your plan, Abel-chan?"

"I told you it wasn't something that could be set in motion here. It's best done somewhere with as many people as possible... especially somewhere outsiders come and go."

"You mean like an 'outsiders welcome' kind of spot? Where in this city would that even be..."

Al tilted his head over how Abel's plan would actually be carried out.
The true intent behind seeking out a place where outsiders came and went was unclear, but there was a high chance Abel wouldn't explain until it came time to actually execute it. It wasn't quite an act of aiding the enemy, but he certainly wasn't easy to work with.
Of course, they already knew he was a man who'd play his own cards without mercy if it meant winning.

"If it's a place with a lot of people coming and going, there's that... you know, that place where you drink."

"A tavern, you meaN?"

"Right, that. Let's aim for there, I think that'd be good."

Whether it was more beneficial to search for Olbart without any detours, or to help Abel's plan succeed — that was up for debate, but for now, prioritizing Abel seemed the wiser course.
With that thought, Subaru and the others renewed their resolve and headed outside the inn.

"A whole troop trailing along with children in tow — nothing draws the eye quite like it."

"You're one to talk, with four out of six of us being kids. Besides, if you're gonna bring up drawing attention, that demon mask alone makes it a lost cause. Or does the perception... um, does the mask's effect make it look like a different face or something?"

"It merely conceals one's identity; it does nothing to change how the mask itself appears."

"Then it's definitely gonna stand out..."

Letting Abel's misplaced complaint slide, Subaru dropped his small shoulders and sighed.
After greeting the inn staff at the entrance and asking for the tavern's location, they headed out onto the main road. From there, to fulfill Abel's aim, the whole group set off walking toward the establishment.
And then, partway there, all of a sudden—

"Might be better to split up roles instead of everyone going to the tavern together? One group to search for Olbart, one group to go with Abel to the tavern—"

He was just about to propose splitting up to act separately.

"—Ah?"

It was then that a red light suddenly flickered across the edge of his vision, and Subaru's eyes went blind.
And then—


△▼△▼△▼△


"Oh, that's a good name. Let's go with that."

"Wha?"

His eyes seared by the red light, Subaru blinked, and an abrupt voice struck his eardrums.
At the sound and its suddenness, a dumbfounded noise escaped him, and a stifled laugh rose up right in front of Subaru. It was a low, hoarse laugh, and at the sound of that voice, Subaru's eyes went wide.

"Ka-ka-ka-ka! What, makin' such a stupid noise. I was praisin' you for a good name."

"...Olbart... san?"

The eerie old man stood right in front of him, laughing with a rattle in his throat, his thin shoulders shaking.
It was all so sudden that Subaru blinked several times, unable to process it. Then he swallowed and voiced what he was supposed to declare.

"O-Olbart-san, found you."

"—? What's that about. Actin' like the game's already started."

"Huh...?"

At this unexpected second discovery, and in his eagerness not to let the chance slip by, Subaru caught the wrongness of it.
Olbart's puzzled attitude and words, and, above all, the greatest oddity — the scenery around him. He should have been out on the main road of the Cursed Metropolis, yet Subaru found himself inside some room.

—No. Not just "some" room.

"...No way."

Where Subaru had been was a room at the inn. — The very lodging room they had just left.
And if he was here, face to face with Olbart, talking to him, that meant—

"――――"

Under a rule that was supposed to guarantee he wouldn't die, he had no choice but to accept that he had undergone "Return by Death."
