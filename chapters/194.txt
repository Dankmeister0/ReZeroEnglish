Arc 4 Chapter 28: "Tea Talk"

Feeling the light of the morning glow burning against his eyelids, Subaru sat up amid the lingering dimness.
It seemed he'd dozed off while lost in thought. Still, since he'd been sunk deep in that sea of contemplation until well into the night, he'd probably only slept for two or three hours at most.

"Unthinkable back in my old world. Daylight hours used to be my prime sleeping time."

Muttering that, Subaru turned and swept his gaze over the cathedral's interior, where a scattered few were already awake. Noticing his glance, some of the Arlam villagers waved or gave him a small nod; he returned the gestures as he rose and made for the cathedral's entrance.
Stepping outside into the cool morning breeze, he found that several residents of the Sanctuary were already outside, working together with the refugees to prepare the morning meal.

"Oh my, awake already, Subaru-sama?"

"Yo, mornin'. Nice and crisp today, huh?"

"It is. The wind's a bit chilly... And it seems you slept quite soundly, Subaru-sama."

The familiar woman who'd greeted him touched a finger lightly to her own cheek, stifling a laugh. Following her lead, Subaru touched his own cheek and felt something dried there — a telltale streak of drool crusted across it.

"Oh no, how embarrassing."

Why was it that shorter naps tended to bring out this kind of sloppiness more than a full night's sleep? Was there some law of nature behind how the worst bedhead always struck during the briefest catnaps?

While Subaru mulled over such trivial nonsense, another woman — this one sporting short dog ears atop her head, distinct from the one who'd laughed — held out a damp cloth. He accepted it, offered a quick word of thanks, and wiped his mouth.
"Get it all?" he asked, checking with a wipe. Both women nodded in unison. Deciding he'd wash both the cloth and his face at the water station, Subaru waved to the pair and headed off.

Glancing back briefly as he left, he studied the two of them exchanging a few words — no trace of awkwardness or friction between them. They communicated across their different races with perfect ease.

These past several days — or a full week, counting the do-overs — even in an environment like this, no notable friction had arisen between the refugees and the Sanctuary's residents.

That was likely thanks to the refugees' high moral standards, plus — irritatingly enough — the sense of security that came from their lord Roswaal staying in the same place. In truth, on top of those factors, there was also the trust built up in Subaru himself, from sharing meals and lodging with everyone — though Subaru, chronically underestimating his own influence, hadn't given that possibility much thought.

The Sanctuary's residents, too, contrary to the shady air hanging over the place, included plenty of people easy enough to talk to — starting with the beast-eared woman just now. At least enough that Subaru had come to believe nothing separated humans from Half-Elves except the awareness in their own minds.

"And yet the Barrier draws a hard line between them regardless... No idea what whoever set it up was thinking, but it's a nasty piece of work."

If Garfiel's words were to be believed, this place was the "Witch of Greed's testing ground." Which meant the Barrier keeping the Half-Elves from escaping was probably that Witch's doing as well.

"Echidna, huh... More and more, she's a Witch whose goals I just can't figure out."

White hair, white skin, a monochrome girl wrapped head to toe in what looked like black mourning clothes.
A girl who'd died four hundred years ago, yet was in some sense still a ghost bound to this present world. She claimed not to interfere with reality, yet she'd been right there at the Trials, sticking her nose into every one of Subaru's choices.
And then there was the way she'd hidden the memories of her own involvement inside Subaru's head, only for her slipshod concealment to let him remember everything with almost no effort — everything she did was a mess, top to bottom.
He assumed there was some profound reason behind it all, but—

"If it turns out she's just doing all this for kicks, I genuinely don't know what to do. Actually, what am I even supposed to think about a woman who makes a total stranger drink her bodily fluids for no reason..."

The unpleasant memory of being made to drink "Donna Tea" resurfaced. That had presumably happened in some mental realm, so he wanted to believe he hadn't actually absorbed any Echidna-derived compound into his real body. Still, whatever her intentions, it didn't change the fact that the Sanctuary remained wrapped in its Barrier, its residents unable to leave.

"Which brings it back around to the biggest obstacle right now... Garfiel, huh."

Whether he negotiated directly with Echidna or challenged the Trials himself, the sticking point was dealing with Garfiel, whose hostility toward him had spiked sharply. If the shift in Garfiel's attitude was the side effect of the Witch's Miasma — the stench Subaru himself couldn't even perceive, a byproduct of Return by Death — then turning that impression around would be a herculean task.
During the Juggernaut incident and the fight against the White Whale, he'd actually used that same stench to break through the situation, but—

"I can make myself smell worse, sure, but I have no idea how to get rid of the stink once it's there... And it's not like some deodorant's gonna fix it. Actually, hold on, what am I even saying — smelly, not smelly, like I'm some kind of filth."

Still, he had deliberately intensified the stench before, by trying to tell someone about Return by Death. And judging by the pattern so far, the heightened stink didn't seem to linger on indefinitely at full strength either.
In that respect, it seemed to behave like an ordinary smell — fading over time. Which, conversely, meant that time was the only way to make it fade.

"Which means I can't expect Garfiel to soften up any time soon. And there's something I really don't want to think about... if I mess up again and end up dying and looping back—"

If he lost his life again and had to restart from inside the tomb, he'd be layering fresh Witch's Miasma on top of what already lingered now. Just imagining how Garfiel would react to that was terrifying enough on its own.

Facing the worst possible outcomes, dying, and starting over — that was how Subaru had changed results before.
His greed — to save everything, to let nothing slip away — hadn't been fully realized, not yet. There were still things he hadn't managed to reclaim.
And yet, even so, he believed that by starting over, he'd chosen futures better than the ones that would have unfolded without a redo. It was hard to feel grateful for Return by Death itself, but without that power, he could easily imagine any number of futures sliding straight toward the worst outcome imaginable.
And yet—

"I've made it through by repeating things over and over... but this is the first time a relationship's gotten worse every time I redo it. The difficulty going up with every retry — that's a first, even for me."

For now, Garfiel was still capable of a rational conversation, but if Subaru ever faced him again with the stench layered even thicker, there was no telling whether he'd even be given the chance to talk it out. At the very least, Rem, unable to trust a Subaru reeking of that stench, had once come at him with her iron ball to take his life. Remembering that, he suddenly felt a pang of loneliness in his left side.

Thinking of those he'd left behind at the manor — especially the sleeping Rem — his mind then snagged, static crackling through his thoughts, on the jet-black assassin. A pleasure-killer who'd reappeared once more, the wielder of a murderous blade who now held sole first place, proudly, in Subaru's personal "times killed in this world" tally.
Incidentally, the gray cat-shaped Spirit tied for first place, and the crowded field tied for second — a single kill apiece — was, embarrassingly, packed with more than a few familiar faces.

"Looking back at that kill count is depressing. Or, I guess in this case, 'times killed'? ...Anyway, dealing with Elsa's the priority. Though there's no way I'm winning a fistfight with her, so realistically it comes down to either Roswaal or Garfiel."

But even setting aside Roswaal's raw magical ability, his injuries were a factor. The best answer to this problem really was to win Garfiel over as an ally. And to have him fight Elsa when she struck the manor, the Barrier surrounding the Sanctuary would need to come down — which, in the end, meant the key issue was this:

"Clear the Trials before the manor gets attacked, free the Sanctuary, patch things up with Garfiel, get him to come along to the manor, drive off Elsa, and happy ending... right."

Saying it out loud himself, he frowned at the contradiction buried in the middle of it.
To reconcile with Garfiel, the Sanctuary needed to be freed.
To free the Sanctuary, he needed to get past Garfiel and take on the Trials.

The two couldn't coexist. It was impossible.
Maybe, if reconciliation through words alone were possible, it could still work — but recalling last night's conversation and every prior encounter with Garfiel, the odds of that panning out were low enough to make him want to clutch his head. For better or worse, Garfiel was blunt to the point of simplicity, and precisely because of that, it was next to impossible to make him change an answer he'd already settled on from the start.
Which meant, if Subaru had any chance at all, it was this:

"Watch for a chance to sneak into the tomb and contact Echidna, and see if there's some other opening to be had. Or sneak in during the hours the Trials are open, and just push through them."

Washing his face at the water station and having reached his conclusion, Subaru turned his feet away from the direction of the morning meal preparations. Wiping his face with the wrung-out cloth, he headed toward the outskirts of the Sanctuary, where fewer people were about. He crossed a low hill and followed a single road onward—

"...Guess I was hoping a little too hard for a convenient plot twist."

Right in the middle of the clear, straight road leading to the tomb, he spotted Garfiel — sitting in the same posture as the night before, evidently waiting for him.
— The plan had been to head to the tomb first thing in the morning and slip in while Garfiel's guard was down.

His morning scheme, one he'd figured would be a nice bonus if it worked, had — for now, at least — fallen through.

"Hard at work bright and early, huh?"

"You're one to talk, showing your face this early. What, you got some actual reason for going out of your way to piss me off, huh?"

Subaru raised a hand in casual greeting, and Garfiel, cracking one eye open, growled that out in a voice dripping with irritation. Right on cue as expected, Subaru let his raised hand droop and turned his gaze instead to the small figure standing beside the cross-legged Garfiel.

"I figured Garfiel would be here, but Ryuzu-san — this I didn't see coming. Morning."

"Mm, fine morning, isn't it. Out for a stroll too, Suu-bou?"

"Stroll, sure, though it's not that wholesome. Came hoping for a maybe, and also to needle Garfiel a bit."

"You little—"

A vein bulged on Garfiel's forehead as he glared down, pointing-and-confirming style. Watching that unfold, Subaru breezily ignored him and tilted his head at Ryuzu instead.

"'Too,' huh — so you're out for a walk as well, Ryuzu-san?"

"For me, the walk's more of an afterthought. Heard Gar-bou's been sitting here on the bare ground since last night, not even going home... so, well, I came to check on him."

As she spoke, Ryuzu twirled a lock of her long, wavy hair around a finger. In her other hand, the one not toying with that pale red hair, she held a small bundle — its size and shape suggesting some simple food wrapped inside. Probably breakfast for the stubbornly immovable Garfiel.
Subaru, touching his chin, glanced between the two of them.

"You and Garfiel go way back, huh?"

"At least since Gar-bou was small... though his height's still small now."

"Oi, hag. I passed your height ages ago."

"But not nearly as much as I'd hoped. Anyway, we've known each other since he was smaller than he is now. This kind of back-and-forth is old hat by now."

Garfiel snapped back with more insults, but Ryuzu deflected them all with the easy composure of long experience. A child's appearance housing an old woman's soul — a splendid, unabashed display of the "loli hag" archetype in full effect.
Still, something in what they'd said caught Subaru's attention, and he raised an eyebrow.

"Way you two talk... Garfiel wasn't born here in the Sanctuary?"

"...Don't go poking your nose where it don't belong. You want a taste of the Blackened Bortok Ambush Counter?"

"Yeah, that doesn't land, so it's not much of a deterrent. Anyway, Ryuzu-san, if you'd do the honors."

At Subaru completely disregarding the warning from the night before, Garfiel's teeth had begun to grind audibly with irritation, but Subaru pressed on regardless with his prying. Watching the two men sidelong, Ryuzu let out a weary sigh.

"Gar-bou came to the Sanctuary some ten-odd years back. He was still a toddling little baby then. Roz-bou brought him along, together with—"

"—Hag. Don't say another useless word."

Garfiel narrowed his eyes, growling low. Something about the way he said it seemed to drop the temperature a few degrees, and Subaru felt a flicker of alarm inside — had he pushed a bit too carelessly far? But—

"And just who do you think you're using that tone with, you fool."

"Ow, that hurts!"

Ryuzu closed the distance in a flash, her open palm cracking against Garfiel's spiky blond head with a satisfying smack. It was a child's arm strength — surely not much force behind it — yet Garfiel clutched his head and looked up at her with the expression of a man just struck by lightning.

"Wh-what the hell was that for, hag..."

"You're the one who needs to watch your mouth around me, practically the one who raised you. Honestly, it's so pathetic and embarrassing and sad it nearly brought me to tears. There, there, there—"

"Stop, ow, agh, people are — people are watching, damn it!"

Ryuzu windmilled her arms with the kind of motion that practically came with its own sound effects, while Garfiel, barely holding her back with his palms, wore the face of a man mortified at having his family's shame put on public display.
Subaru, fighting down the urge to burst out laughing at the whole spectacle, said,

"Well, that settled the length and depth of your relationship pretty objectively. ...Garfiel, are you seriously planning to just sit there the whole time?"

"I ain't movin' except to take a leak. Can't rule out some jackass taking advantage the second I look away."

Even amid all this deflating back-and-forth, Garfiel remained perfectly vigilant against anyone trying to sneak past. Subaru hadn't held out more than a maybe-chance anyway, so it wasn't much of a letdown — if anything, if Garfiel had gone back on his word after half a day, Subaru would've had to revise his opinion of him. In any case, moving the stubborn man remained just as difficult as ever.

"Still, the Trials only run at night, right? Sneaking in during the day doesn't do me any good. So you camping out here's kind of pointless too, isn't it?"

"Trying to talk me into leaving ain't gonna work. If you get into the tomb during the day and just wait for night, taking advantage of the fact I can't follow you in, the condition's met anyway, ain't it. I ain't the only one who can settle in for the long haul. Don't underestimate me, huh?"

"Tch, you saw through that, huh."

Subaru raised both hands and shrugged in a gesture of surrender. Garfiel snorted to express his feelings on the matter, then shot a sidelong glare up at Ryuzu.

"So like I said, I ain't movin' from here for a while. Hag. Food."

"What an attitude, after I went out of my way to bring it too. Shameful. Here."

Grumbling all the while, Ryuzu handed over the food anyway. Garfiel unwrapped the bundle and began putting away the dumpling-like food inside. With Ryuzu cooperating like this, the standoff looked set to drag on for the long haul.

"Hard to do anything about the situation as it stands... Fine, I'll come back later."

"I said you don't gotta bother comin' back. I ain't lettin' you through, I ain't lettin' you do a damn thing, and I ain't forgivin' you neither. You just shut up and make yourself small, that's all you gotta do."

Licking his fingers clean, Garfiel fired off that warning shot at Subaru's back as he turned to leave. Subaru waved a hand over his shoulder and took his leave; Ryuzu fell into step beside him.

"Feeding time for Gar-bou's done, so. I'd like to talk with you a bit too, Suu-bou."

"Funny, I've had a few things I wanted to ask you too, Ryuzu-san. Honestly I wanted to get a look at Emilia's face first, but..."

He glanced up at the sky, gauging the sun that had only just begun its climb. He recalled that on the morning after the first day's Trial, the exhausted Emilia hadn't woken until nearly midday. Giving in to the urge to go peek at her sleeping face had its own charm, but for now, he ought to push forward with a different thread of the story.

He cast a sidelong glance at Ryuzu walking beside him, studying this childlike old woman with her long, pale red hair swaying. Her face was drowsy and heavy-lidded, her small steps pattering along quickly to keep pace with him. Loli hag — even knowing exactly what she was, something about it still tickled at his heart.

"Want a piggyback ride?"

"...And here I was wondering why you suddenly got that gentle look in your eye looking at me. Don't tell me, Suu-bou, you've got a thing for young girls? That'd be even more hopeless than Roz-bou."

"Slapping a lolicon charge on me is a straight-up false accusation. When I'm buying a dating sim, whether it's got a senpai-type or an onee-san-type heroine is my main selection criteria. Even the girl I'm desperately trying to get to notice me right now is the onee-san type... and just recently it came out she's seriously way older than I thought, and that hasn't changed a thing. That's the kind of guy I am, all right?"

"I'd love to ask what kind of guy that even is, but never mind. No piggyback needed. If I don't walk properly, my legs and hips'll get weak."

"There you go again with a line that clashes hard with how you look!"

It was only her appearance that was childlike — inside, she was genuinely an old woman, and the remark carried real weight because of it. Maybe her skin was young, but her internal organs could be a wreck underneath. Being a loli hag, apparently, was harder than it looked.

"Hm, you've got the face of someone thinking up something stupid again."

"Wait, seriously? I was being real careful just now not to let it show, making my best poker face and everything."

"You had the exact same face Gar-bou makes when he sneaks a bite of sweets he'd hidden away. Children are children, whichever one you're looking at."

"Hey, hag, quit hammering home how much of a hag you are, one crack after another."

Setting aside Ryuzu's relentless age-flaunting for the moment, Subaru, walking alongside her, suddenly cocked his head. "Huh, wait."

"'Let's talk a bit'... sure, fine, but where are we even headed? Also, way too late to be asking this, but — you lent your place to Emilia-tan for sleeping, so where do you actually sleep, Ryuzu-san? Out in a field?"

"Lending out my house and immediately being treated as homeless... That's the kind of thing you'd only say if you'd forgotten that I happen to be the head of this place. I've got acquaintances who'll put me up for a few days, thank you."

"Well, yeah, fair. Only been here a few days, but the people here are surprisingly nice, all things considered."

Recalling the Sanctuary's residents he'd had a fair number of chances to interact with, not just during meal preparations, Subaru frowned slightly, wondering if the friction Garfiel had described had been overstated.
Watching Subaru fall into a brief silence out of the corner of her eye, Ryuzu gave a small "hm" and nodded.

"Something not sitting right with you?"

"No, I mean, this might sound off, but... I guess it's just different from what I imagined. Emilia, being a Half-Elf, got treated pretty rough at the royal capital, so I figured Half-Elves probably get that treatment just about everywhere. And if that's the case, I figured the Half-Elves might have some complicated feelings of their own toward pure-bloods in turn."

At least, for people trapped in a place branded a "testing ground," the residents here showed no outward sign of that kind of dark sentiment. Of course, some probably did feel resentment on the inside, but Subaru hadn't seen any of that negativity surface openly. Garfiel, who never minced words or feelings, did seem to voice such grievances — but even his anger read less as personal fury than as righteous indignation on behalf of others, not himself.
Given how poor their circumstances were, the emotional standards of the Sanctuary's people seemed almost too high. It wasn't just strange — it verged on inexplicable.

At Subaru voicing that puzzlement, Ryuzu blinked, her eyes widening slightly in surprise.

"Well now, you think about more than your looks let on, Suu-bou."

"'Than your looks let on' feels unnecessary, don't you think? I'm at least confident I look more like the intellectual type than Garfiel. Well, if I don't keep my head working I fall short in all kinds of ways, so."

"If you're aware of your own shortcomings, that's already commendable enough. There are some who know full well they're lacking and just brazen it out anyway. ...Now then, this way."

They came to a fork in the road, and with Subaru's feet hesitating over which way to go, Ryuzu took the lead. It was a direction different from both the cathedral and Roswaal's temporary residence — the outskirts of the village, roughly diagonal from the tomb. There, the lodging Ryuzu led him to stood off by itself, isolated.
Unlike the scattered clusters of houses he'd seen elsewhere, this single home, for some reason, made its base in a place set apart from everything else. A certain word naturally floated up in Subaru's mind.

"This is way too lonesome. Why are you sleeping way out here?"

"Can't be helped. Right now, this is the only building in the Sanctuary with nobody living in it. It's a bit far from the meeting hall, but it's spacious, and I make good use of it."

"What happened to the 'acquaintances who'll put me up' line? Why'd you pick a sleeping spot where you're all alone again? A loli hag dying alone — even a guy who's known plenty of loli hags in his time has never seen anything that sad."

"Make up your mind whether you're worried about me or mocking me. Here, get in. I'll at least brew you some tea. Though not nearly as well as Ram would."

"Any tea with leaves in it just tastes like leaves to me no matter what, so don't worry about it."

"You, on the other hand, ought to worry a bit more about the things you say."

Ushered in by a sighing Ryuzu, Subaru passed through the pushed-open door into the house. The building was about half the size of Roswaal's temporary residence — though that one had simply been too large for one person anyway, and this three-room house had plenty of space of its own.
Settling into the nearest chair and glancing around, he could see the small, cozy room's interior was simple, yet clearly well cared for. Ryuzu had said no one lived here, but—

"For a house nobody lives in, it's got the feel of somebody hanging around here pretty often. Even the fluffiness of the bed passes muster with me, a first-class sheet craftsman... Don't tell me, Ryuzu-san—"

"What, that face you're making — like you think I come here every chance I get when I want to be alone, just to zone out and let time pass until things blow over."

"Wow, that's a really specific and complicated face I must've been making!"

Maybe something had struck a nerve, because Ryuzu's rapid-fire defense was almost pitiable. The "dying alone" line earlier had been a joke, but the part about her being a lonely old woman with no family nearby didn't get denied with much conviction. Her back, as she silently set about brewing tea, looked lonesome, and Subaru let his gaze wander in search of some way to change the subject.

The room was tidily kept. A slightly clouded vanity mirror and a clothing chest. A flower vase with no flowers in it, and an iron shield mounted on the wall. —A shield?

"Why's there a shield here? Two of 'em, no less."

"Those belong to Gar-bou. That boy uses this place as a storage shed."

"So he hangs around here too, huh. I mean, sure, it does have a bit of a delinquent hangout feel to it... but don't tell me he's the one keeping every little corner this spotless."

*Way too out of character*, Subaru muttered under his breath, studying the shields said to belong to Garfiel. In manga and the like, noble manors often had crossed swords mounted decoratively on the wall — these shields hung in a similar fashion, tilted at matching angles. Though "decoration" seemed like the wrong word for something this worn; they bore scratches and dents that upkeep alone couldn't erase, so at the very least, they didn't look like antiques unfamiliar with real combat.

"Even so, what kind of real combat do you even do with just shields?"

"Back in the day, the two of them used to bash their shields together out in the grass outside this house all the time. Each holding one shield apiece, striking at each other, going round and round and round."

"That's way too dangerous to call horsing around. ...Mind if I ask who, exactly, was doing this with Garfiel?"

In all his time in the Sanctuary, Subaru had never once seen anyone interact with Garfiel in a way that looked genuinely close. Sure, as the local figurehead who showed up for the meal preparations, Garfiel clearly got along fine with the residents in general — but setting that aside, when it came to anyone actually close to him personally, no specific name came to mind. If pressed, Ryuzu herself might qualify, but the image of her squaring off against Garfiel, shield in hand, was fairly alarming to picture.

Faced with Subaru's question laden with such suspicion, Ryuzu stayed quiet for a moment. Then she came over carrying a tray with two teacups, held one out to Subaru, and settled herself onto the bed. He accepted it and brought the warm cup to his lips, wetting his throat.

"Yeah, still just tastes like leaves."

"Not much satisfaction in brewing for you, is there. Well, figured as much, so I used the cheap leaves anyway. ...Tea leaves are a fairly precious thing around here too, you know."

Such precious goods and small luxuries, apparently, were brought in about once a month thanks to Roswaal's arrangements. Subaru found himself grudgingly impressed at that little bit of accommodation, so very like that sly fox — and for a while, the two of them sipped their tea in silence. Then, once a stretch of peaceful quiet had passed—

"—Frederica."

At the name Subaru murmured quietly, Ryuzu's shoulders gave a small tremor. She lifted her gaze from her teacup to look at him. To that face, with its faint trace of unease, Subaru said again,

"The name of the person who used to bash shields with Garfiel — it's Frederica, isn't it?"

"...Did Gar-bou tell you?"

"Nah. Just pieced it together from a few scattered bits of conversation, figured it was probably that. Kind of already had a sense there was some complicated relationship between Garfiel and Frederica anyway."

At Roswaal Manor, Frederica had named Garfiel as someone to watch out for in the Sanctuary. And Garfiel's expression had changed the moment he'd heard Frederica's name. On top of that, he'd shown signs of quietly trying to learn how she was doing — it would've been unreasonable *not* to suspect some connection between the two. And the decisive piece of evidence—

"Their fangs are way too similar. Even Buddha wouldn't buy 'unrelated' after that."

"...Ah, indeed. On that point, I can't think of a single word to deny it."

At that clinching piece of evidence, Ryuzu let out a resigned breath, then burst into laughter. The single biggest resemblance between Garfiel and Frederica — those overly sharp canines and that overly ferocious grin — had been more than enough on its own. It also made clear that whatever the two of them were to each other, it wasn't anything romantic like lovers. Somehow, the relationship that seemed to fit them best was—

"Siblings... no, more like older sister and younger brother, I'd guess. If anything, Frederica seems like the big-sister type."

"Well, well... if you can see that much just from instinct alone, that's more than impressive enough."

At Subaru's guess, Ryuzu could only marvel. Then, nodding as if resigning herself to it, she set what remained of her tea back on the tray and straightened her posture.

"Just as you imagined, Suu-bou, the shields' owners are Frederica and Garfiel — brother and sister. Frederica Baumann, who's now left the Sanctuary, and Garfiel Tinzel share the same blood. They're family."

Confirming Subaru's guess, Ryuzu nonetheless let out a wistful sigh as she added—

"—Though these days, they've drifted apart from each other, and gone their separate ways."
