Arc 2 Chapter 38: "The Meaning of Courage"

Bursting out of the room, sprinting down the hallway, leaping down the stairs.
Skipping three steps at a time, he landed on the landing, spun his body around, and immediately leapt again, and the moment his feet hit the ground floor, he tilted his face upward—

"—Ram! Rem! I need to talk to you!"

Saying it loud enough for the whole mansion to hear, Subaru's feet carried him toward the entrance hall.
Subaru looked ready to burst straight out the door with the momentum of his run. What stopped him was Ram, already standing before the entrance.
Not that she'd gotten ahead of him—more likely she simply happened to be nearby already. Which was also to say she had more time on her hands than Rem, who was busy preparing food in the kitchen.

Whatever Ram saw in Subaru's blood-flushed expression, a faint trace of surprise crossed her otherwise composed face as she said,

"What's wrong, Barusu? In such a rush..."

"Sorry, but I'm heading to the village. Won't do any good to stop me, and I'm going even if you do stop me, but I figured leaving without saying anything would just cause more confusion."

"To the village...? To do what... no, before that—did you not hear Roswaal-sama's instructions? Tonight, Ram and the others have been entrusted with watching over the mansion. Do you not understand what that means?"

Ram's gaze sharpened as she pushed back against Subaru.
Above all, prioritizing Roswaal's wishes was her stance. Subaru's attitude, seeming to trample on their master's orders, must have struck a nerve.
But Subaru wasn't in a state of mind to back down either.

"I don't have time to waste arguing back and forth, so I'll get straight to it. There's an evil mage in that village. I figured out what it really is, so I have to go."

"...And you expect me to accept that on the strength of an excuse like a child's?"

"There's no other way to put it, so it can't be helped. If you want proof it's true, ask Bea-ko—she'll back me up. And besides..."

"Sister—"

Just as she was arguing back against Ram, whose suspicions only deepened, the great door behind them opened and Rem appeared. Seeing the two of them arguing at the entrance, she glided smoothly to stand beside her sister.

"Sister, what is this...?"

"He says he wants us to let him outside so he can go deal with an evil mage in the village. —Now what should we do about this, Rem?"

At her sister's terse summary of the situation, Rem blinked in confusion.
Then, apparently having fully digested the content, she turned a dubious gaze on Subaru.

"Sister, sister. Subaru-kun is telling quite a boring joke."
"Rem, Rem. Barusu really does have first-rate talent as a clown."
"Ram, Rem. I'm always fooling around, sure, but there are times I'm serious too."

At Subaru's line, cutting in right over their unison delivery, the two of them fell silent at the same moment.
Pressing the advantage against their reaction, he stepped forward, wetting his lips with his tongue, and made his proposal to the two who'd gone quiet, pointing a finger at them.

"I get that it's hard to believe, and I get that it's a lot to ask you to trust me suddenly springing into action like this. So I'm not saying you should just see me off with no conditions at all."

This was where things came to an important fork in the road, for Subaru too.

"I'm going to the village now. If you're suspicious, feel free to come along. Judge me for yourselves. But—I can't leave Emilia-tan alone. If someone's coming with me, it's only one of you."

"Deciding things on your own terms... to begin with, if we're to follow Roswaal-sama's orders, there's no reason for Rem or Sister to accompany Subaru-kun..."

"Yeah, none at all—if we're just talking about following Roz-cchi's orders from this evening. But is that the only order Roz-cchi gave concerning me?"

"————"

As if struck in a sore spot, Rem lost her words.
Which meant that what had just been a bluff on Subaru's part had hit the mark.
That the two of them had been ordered by Roswaal to keep watch on Subaru's standing—that much he'd already guessed from fragmentary information across his past loops.

Rem's eyes still wandered as if searching for a rebuttal, but before she could find one, it was Ram who broke the flow of the conversation with a sigh.
She raised a hand slightly.

"Very well, Barusu. Ram will permit your unilateral decision."

"Sister—!?"

Rem was stunned at her sister's readiness to raise the white flag. But turning her raised palm toward her flustered younger sister as well, Ram continued, "However—"

"As Barusu well knows, we can't let you go alone. If Ram allows Barusu to act independently here, it would mean defying Roswaal-sama's orders."

"Right. So, what's the compromise?"

"—Galling as it is, there's no choice but to accept Barusu's offer from before. Ram stays at the mansion. Rem goes with Barusu. Should you do anything suspicious...—that's the condition."

"Couldn't ask for better."

Subaru thrust a fist into the air, signaling his agreement to the terms Ram had set.
Ram let out a small sigh and turned to her sister, who seemed somewhat left behind by the conversation.

"Rem, that's how it is, so please. Ram will confirm things with Beatrice-sama and watch over Emilia-sama. —And Ram will be keeping a proper eye on your side of things too."

"Sister, please don't use that eye so much..."

"This isn't the time to say such things. If it's necessary, I'll use it. Rem, you should too."

At her sister's flat, matter-of-fact tone, Rem could find no further words to interject.
After an exchange that only the two of them could fully understand, Rem swallowed down several things she might have said, and turned a gaze at Subaru that was anything but friendly.

"Subaru-kun, Rem would like to hear the details."

"On the way there. This might turn into a seriously bad situation."

If Subaru's worst-case imagination proved correct, the damage that could result wasn't something he could laugh off as an exaggeration. And that damage wouldn't be limited to Subaru personally—it would be damage on a far larger scale.

He gave Ram a light pat on the shoulder to show his gratitude for letting him pass.
Then, bringing along Rem, who still didn't seem entirely convinced, Subaru's feet carried him toward the entrance.
And then—

"—Subaru, are you going somewhere?"

From above the great staircase in the entrance hall, a sound like a silver bell drifted down.
He couldn't help turning to look up, and there stood Emilia, her silver hair swaying. It seemed Subaru's earlier shout had reached her too.
Slightly out of breath, she looked at the three of them frozen at the entrance and said,

"I heard a loud voice, so I came down to see... did something happen?"

"Might be something, is what it is, Emilia-tan. Well, no need to worry. Ah, but if you're a little worried about me, that'd make me happy."

To spare Emilia unnecessary anxiety, Subaru deliberately put on his usual act.
His unchanged, glib demeanor—but whatever Emilia saw in it,

"You've got that face on again, the one you make when you're about to do something dangerous."

She saw through him instantly, and made a face like she had more to say. Subaru scratched his head at her sharp insight, tilting his head as he wondered what to do.

"That whole back-and-forth just now finally wrapped up..."

"There's no point stopping you, is there?"

"Well, yeah, pretty much. If anything, being stopped would cause problems in all sorts of ways..."

"Yes, yes, I understand. I won't stop you."

Emilia came down the stairs, all the way to stand before Subaru and the others, hands on her hips.
Met straight on by her violet-indigo eyes, he found himself unable to look away, as if drawn into their glow.
She reached out toward the still-frozen Subaru, lightly touching his chest.

"Even if I told you not to push yourself, not to do anything reckless, that'd be pointless too, wouldn't it?"

"Depends on the situation... no, honestly, I don't want to do any of that stuff either."

Effort, hard work, desperation—words like those, he didn't want anything to do with any of them.
And yet, when he was the only one who could change the situation, Subaru had no choice but to move. Why had he ended up with such a troublesome nature—probably largely the influence of the girl in front of him, he thought, and a wry smile escaped him.

"—May the Spirits bless you."

"Come again?"

At the murmur, her hand still resting on his chest, Subaru tilted his head. Emilia gave him a knowing little smile.

"It's a send-off blessing. It means something like, come back safe."

"Ah, like seeing someone off with a flint spark. Guess I've gotta answer that then. Emilia-tan, glory and victory to you—"

"Yes, yes."

Brushing off Subaru's mock salute, Emilia turned her gaze to Rem as well. Rem, who had been silently watching the exchange between the two, straightened her back under that gaze.

"Rem, take care too. And keep an eye on Subaru so he doesn't do anything reckless."

"Yes, Emilia-sama. Understood."

"No trust in me at all, huh."

"What are you talking about. It's because I trust Subaru's character that things are turning out this way, isn't it?"

"What kind of trust is that. Could be taken as a good thing or a bad thing—that's downright uncanny."

Hands on her hips, Emilia was in full lecture mode.
There were probably several more things she wanted to say, but she pulled the conversation back before going further. Reading Subaru's urgency, she gave his back a push.

"Off you go."

"Yep, heading out!"

Pushed from behind, he responded with a pose of raised fists, then left Emilia and Ram behind and burst out of the mansion. Rem, bowing before leaving the mansion behind, followed after him, and he sensed her come up to run leisurely alongside him.

"Now then, Rem would like to hear the details..."

"There's a Curse Arts User in the village. Beatrice lifted the curse for me, but I'd been cursed too. —Worst case, the whole village could be wiped out."

"—Are you serious?"

Rem gasped as she ran, her eyes going wide as she asked. Subaru answered with a silent nod and kept pushing straight for the village.

If the Curse Arts User were a rational human being, there'd be no need to imagine such a method at all. But if Subaru's suspicion proved correct, the worst-case scenario could genuinely happen.
So Subaru kept running. Rem, running alongside him, had likewise grasped the gravity of the matter and ran on in silence.

Aiming for the distant village beyond the trees, the two figures tore through the night's darkness.

"—Subaru-kun, look at that."

Subaru heard Rem cry out, a note of tension rising in her voice.
Rem's figure, calling out, was well ahead of Subaru's line of sight. In the direction she pointed lay the village, its outline now clearly visible, and what her expression showed was—

"The village is... awfully bright."

Struck by the same thought, Subaru too sensed something was wrong.
His pace had grown heavy, too slow to call a run, too fast to call a walk—his sprint had degraded to something in between.
Rem, unable to suppress her impatience, looked at Subaru with frustration, but even being pressed like that, his side ached too much to even raise his voice.

"Go on ahead, ahead—!"

He gestured for her to go on without him, but Rem shook her head.
To begin with, the reason for her accompanying Subaru was the reason it was. It was also meant to serve as surveillance of him—splitting up here would defeat the whole purpose.
Perhaps growing impatient with Subaru's less-than-dignified state,

"Honestly, there's no helping it. Please don't bite your tongue."

"Eh, wait, Rem-rin, what are you—!?"

Rem dashed back to Subaru's side, reached out an arm, grabbed him by the scruff of the neck as he wobbled about, and lifted him effortlessly.
The same overwhelming difference in physical strength she'd shown with the sake barrel.

Carried over Rem's shoulder just like that, Subaru's body rode the wind upside down.
Feeling firsthand the speed of Rem's true sprint, Subaru was left speechless at her physical prowess. At the same time, he became all the more convinced that his decision to bring her along had been the right one.

If a powerless, useless Subaru had leapt alone into the thick of things, just how much could he possibly have accomplished? Including the bluffing exchange at the entrance—securing Rem as a fighting asset had definitely been the right call.

"We've arrived. Subaru-kun, are you all right?"

"That was fast, as expected... now, the state of the village... ohhh, urgh..."

Bracing a hand against a fence, Subaru was assailed by nausea from the jostling and exhaustion combined.
Ignoring Subaru's pathetic state, Rem swept her gaze around. Their conspicuous appearance caught the eye of a villager passing right by.

"Aren't you two from the mansion? At this hour..."

"Perfect timing. Did something happen?"

Rem caught the young man as he approached and questioned him from above. He blinked, seemingly a little startled by her forceful tone, and answered with a flustered "Eh, y-yes,"

"Actually, several of the village's children can't be found. We know they were playing before it got dark, but after that..."

At the young man's unclear answer, Rem opened her mouth to press further with questions.
But it was Subaru who moved first. Still breathing raggedly, he grabbed the young man's shoulder and spun him around.

"The ones missing—Lucas, Petra, Mild?"

"Th-that's right... do you have some idea where they went?"

Receiving an answer that confirmed his fears, Subaru clicked his tongue on the spot and kicked at the ground with a "Damn it!" Then he fixed his gaze on the edge of the village—toward the fence leading into the forest.

"Who's out looking for the kids? You and...?"

"The young men's group, all of them, and the village chief."

"The kids are in the forest. Won't find them searching around the village."

At Subaru's declaration, the young man's expression changed. He still looked like he had more he wanted to ask Subaru, but Subaru gave his shoulder a pat and took off running.

"I'm going into the forest first. You go tell everyone. The kids are in the forest!"

Behind him, the young man raised a voice of confusion, but Subaru paid it no mind and took off running again.
Flustered, Rem followed. Unable to make sense of Subaru's certainty, she asked,

"How could you possibly know that..."

"I just know. I heard it, twice, about something crying all alone deep in the forest—"

Surrounding the village was a tall wooden fence. Vaulting over the section built adjacent to the forest, the two landed on the earth and their bodies plunged into the woods.
Weaving between the trees, mindful of branches and muddy ground underfoot, they pressed deeper.

"Even saying 'in the forest,' the area's too wide. Subaru-kun, do you have some idea..."

"If what the kids said is right, it should be this way..."

Relying on memory, he pressed forward, but his steps, tracing only what he'd overheard secondhand, were heavy going. On top of that, visibility was pitch black, with no way to confirm they were even heading the right way.
But then—

"—The barrier's been broken."

At Rem's startled voice, Subaru clenched his fist, certain his judgment had been correct.
Before them, what Rem had pointed out was a crystal embedded in a great tree. Long since drained of light, it was a mysterious object he'd had occasion to see a few times in the forest.
Talk of a barrier concerning the forest had stuck in Subaru's memory a few times. When had it been that Ram had directly told him not to go deep into the mountains?

"What happens if the barrier's broken?"

"'Witchbeasts' will cross the boundary line. That's exactly why maintaining the barrier is a duty for the villagers, and yet...!"

"No use dwelling on it, what's in front of us is fact. More importantly, what's a Witchbeast?"

Still shaken, Rem answered Subaru's question.

"Witchbeasts are... natural enemies of humanity possessing magical power. Said to have been created by the Witch to bring ruin upon humankind."

"The Witch again, here too..."

Subaru frowned at the recurrence of a word he couldn't afford to let slide, but the first half of what Rem had just said gave him certainty—of the true identity of the Curse Arts User, and of the calamity's omen still bearing down on the village.

"—! Subaru-kun, what are you—!?"

Startled, Rem raised a voice to stop him.
Before her, Subaru was crossing the tree called the barrier, moving to head deeper in. Turning to face her surprise, he held out his left hand.

"The kids are further in. I've got to go help them."

"Do you have proof? To cross the barrier requires Roswaal-sama's permission—"

"The scar on the back of my hand is proof."

The back of his outstretched left hand—there lay a wound he'd taken earlier that day, during his visit to the village.
The mark of canine teeth, left when the children had surrounded him and given him a taste of their own welcome as well.

—Pointing to this wound, Beatrice had said it.
That whatever had made this scar was the very culprit that had cast the curse on Subaru.

The first time, the second time, and now this time.
The one presence connected to every single case in which a curse had befallen Subaru. And the same thing that, in the fourth world, might also threaten Rem's life.
The only thing that could possibly encompass all of those possibilities was that presence, and nothing else.

"It's the dog the kids doted on. What if that dog-looking thing wasn't a dog at all, and it's been cursing everyone it bit, one after another?"

Not something man-made, but something more like a natural disaster.
Just as there had once been plagues spread through rats as carriers, it was easy enough to imagine what could happen to a village this size if such a thing spread explosively through an animal.

And the safety of the children who had chased after that dog into the forest was, by the same token, very much in question.

"The longer this takes, the worse it gets. No way to know if the kids are cursed or not, but either way we need to get all of them back to the mansion for the curse to be lifted."

"Wait, please. Making that kind of judgment on your own—to begin with, this whole situation is far too suspicious."

"Huh?"

As Subaru, driven by impatience, tried to push into the forest, Rem still held her ground. She pointed toward the village, and by extension toward the mansion.

"With Roswaal-sama absent tonight of all nights, does a problem like this happen to arise as if targeting that very fact? What if this isn't aimed at the children at all, but at the mansion..."

"Then what, huh? Abandon the kids, who are in danger right now as we speak, and go back to the mansion to shore up the defenses? If you're fine with every villager turning up dead by tomorrow, sure, that's one option."

Subaru knew even as he said it that it was an unfairly harsh thing to say.
Rem was only trying to avoid unnecessary risk. That was a perfectly natural feeling, and she had absolutely no reason to be blamed for it.

But whether one stayed silent or curled up and hid, a moment demanding a decision would always come.
No matter how limited the options, one had to choose from whichever cards were laid out in front of them. Of course, one could also choose not to choose. But the fact that doing so had led to the deepest regret was something still carved, deep and heavy, into the bottom of Subaru's heart even now.

"Rem, let's go. We're the only ones who can do something about this."

"Why do you care so much... what connection does Subaru-kun even have to this village..."

Perhaps because she was still torn over the decision, Subaru heard Rem murmur something so uncharacteristically weak for the first time. Rem, who never let her composed bearing slip, no matter when, no matter what.
But when forced to make a decision in a place without her trusted master or sister, she took back on the face of a fragile girl her own age.
At that human weakness of hers, Subaru felt an almost painful sympathy. But neither of them had the luxury of licking each other's wounds right now.

Not in terms of time, and not in terms of emotional composure either.
Even as he urged Rem toward resolve, Subaru couldn't hide that his own legs trembled for reasons beyond exhaustion. Of course they did. He knew full well that the presence responsible for taking his life twice, and Rem's once, was likely waiting somewhere ahead.
And worse, his greatest fighting asset and companion wasn't eager to head deeper in together with him. Considering the possibility that, worst case, he might have to press on alone—how could he possibly suppress the cowardice that had been forcibly held down, now peeking its face out?

But Subaru slapped his own cheek to make himself forget that weakness.
At the dry, sharp sound, Rem looked up. Meeting her gaze, Subaru took a deep breath and spoke in a low voice.

"—Petra wants to make clothes in the capital when she grows up."

"...Eh?"

"Lucas says he's gonna take over from his old man, the best woodcutter in the village, and Mild wants to make a flower crown from the flowers she picks in the field and give it to her mom."

"............"

Counting off on his fingers, Subaru continued, calling to mind one face after another behind his eyelids.

"Meina was so happy about her little brother or sister being born soon, and Dain and Cain, those two brothers, are competing over which one gets to marry Petra."

A small laugh escaped him despite himself.
Then, shaking his head at Rem, who had fallen silent,

"How could it not matter to me? I know their faces, I know their names, I know what they want to do tomorrow."

Subaru didn't like kids.
They were loud, overly familiar, talked to him like they were his equals, and above all, had no manners.
Rude, impudent, reckless, and inconsiderate—it was almost like looking at himself in a mirror.

Subaru was far from a fundamentally good person.
Even watching news reports of gruesome incidents on TV, no matter how big the numbers looked on paper, if it wasn't close to home, he was callous enough to forget it after one night's sleep.

So he didn't like it, and children who weren't close to him—whatever happened to them, it wasn't his concern.
Exactly as Rem had said—prioritizing Emilia and the others and holing up in the mansion would, he himself thought, be far more like him.
But even so—

"I promised them we'd do radio calisthenics again tomorrow."

Even back on the loop of the first day after his summoning, Subaru had thought the very same thing, surely.
Abandoning them would be the easy way out. But because he couldn't do that, he ran.

He looked at Rem.
She was still torn over the decision. Still hesitant.
A fragile figure. A figure lamenting her own lack of strength. It was Subaru's own reflection.

Seeing her, weaker than himself right now, made him sick with himself for steeling his resolve because of it.
Even though he was scared out of his mind and shaking with fear, in the end he was still using someone else to prop himself up—that pettiness of his was thoroughly detestable.
But if even that cowardice of his could be put to use, then he'd use it too.

"I keep my promises. And I make people keep theirs to me too. —I'm doing radio calisthenics with those kids again, no matter what. So, I'm heading in."

—He hadn't known courage could be something this frightening.
Desperately trying to steady his trembling hands, Subaru declared it, not even noticing his own voice was shaking too.

Watching Subaru's back as he spoke, Rem quietly closed her eyes.
If she chose not to follow, that too would be something that couldn't be helped, she thought. Going back now to call the villagers would waste far too much time. If nothing else, she wanted to at least leave Subaru with that much before he went—

"Well then, I suppose there's no helping it."

"Rem?"

Her lips curling into a small smile, Rem looked up at Subaru.
Toward the startled Subaru, she wore an expression that betrayed a clear emotion, perhaps for the first time.

"The task Rem has been ordered to do is watching over Subaru-kun. If Rem lets Subaru-kun go on alone here, wouldn't that mean failing that duty?"

At her teasingly phrased words, Subaru stood stunned for a moment, then shook his head.

"Yeah, that's right. Make sure I don't try anything shady, keep a close eye on me."

"Yes, I will. —So then, shall we go?"

Rem glided up to walk beside him, and Subaru straightened his back, feeling for the first time that he'd truly come to stand alongside her, in the fullest sense.
Feeling a ticklish sort of warmth, he was about to thank her when he suddenly noticed something.

—At some point, an iron ball had appeared, gripped in the hand of Rem walking beside him. Connected to an iron handle by a chain, it swung in her hand, producing a light metallic clink utterly at odds with its evident weight.

"Um, Rem-san, is that..."

"For self-defense."

"No, but that's..."

"For self-defense."

Exchanging words like that with Rem, the two of them pressed on into the unexplored forest.
All the while, Subaru fought with desperate resolve to keep the courage he'd only just barely managed to scrape together from wilting away again.
