Arc 7 Chapter 14: "An Unbridgeable Gap in Values"

"—————"

—The Sacred Vollachia Empire's seventy-seventh emperor.

Hearing the title Abel had claimed for himself, Subaru's thoughts went pure white.
It wasn't incomprehension born of failing to grasp the meaning of the words, nor was it born of any awe at coming face to face with royalty.

Granted, other world or not, this was the first time Subaru had ever met royalty directly.

By the time Subaru was summoned to this other world, every member of the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica's royal family had already fallen ill. Felt, suspected of being a surviving royal, didn't count—partly because Felt herself didn't acknowledge it, but mostly because she had not a shred of royal bearing about her.

On that count, the most eminent people Subaru had met in this world so far were probably the assembled dignitaries of the Council of Wise Men he'd glimpsed at the Royal Selection, or else Crusch, a duchess in her own right.
Roswaal, as a margrave, held a fairly high standing too. —And come to think of it, what standing did Emilia, a candidate vying in the Royal Selection for the next throne, even correspond to?

"...No, this isn't the time to be running from reality."

He reined in his thoughts just before they scattered completely.
In any case, what mattered was that Abel had declared himself "Emperor." From the moment they'd met on that plain in the jungle, Subaru had sensed the man was no ordinary person.
But someone in a position like emperor was completely outside anything he'd imagined.
Except—

"That is, if it's actually true."

"You doubt my word?"

"Of course I doubt it. Why the hell would I run into the top dog of an entire country in the middle of a forest? I'll believe your shamelessness is emperor-class, but..."

Subaru cut himself off there, glaring straight at Abel behind his menacing mask.
A confrontation where all Subaru could sense from Abel's side was the sharpness of his gaze—that wasn't fair. It was true that Abel's voice carried a strength of will only a singular kind of person could possess.
Subaru's instincts, honed by encounters with all manner of high-standing people in this other world through some twist of fate, could vouch for that. —But that was a separate matter from this one.

"I think I already told you this a little while ago. What exactly am I supposed to trust about some guy who won't even show his face?"

Across the bonfire, amid the flickering flames, Abel took in Subaru's words.
Dredging up the exchange they'd had while looking hazily down on the burning camp, Subaru glared hard at the masked face before him.
Back then it hadn't been a mask but a filthy rag hiding Abel's face, and Subaru had said the same thing to him then, too.
And upon hearing it, Abel had removed the cloth and shown his face.

"A tiresomely long-winded man."

He'd said, setting the removed mask aside—just as he did now, in this very moment.

"—————"

"What is that impertinent look for. There is nothing about me especially different from you."

"...The parts, sure. But I'd say some god had it in for whoever laid them out."

Abel's sarcasm cut into Subaru as he stared intently at the man's face.
As he'd said, Subaru and Abel could both be called human, sure enough. But that was only true at the level of category—broken down further, they surely belonged to wildly different classes.
Glossy black hair, and sharp, striking eyes with a fine, commanding cut. An intimidating impression, but the demonic face held an odd, magnetic charm that made it hard to look away from—

So this was the true face of the emperor of the Sacred Vollachia Empire.
And yet, Subaru had absolutely no recollection of that face. Not even Subaru, who as Emilia's knight in the Royal Selection was nominally involved in this neighboring country's national affairs.

"Is that just how it is? You're the top of the whole country, and your face..."

"There is no reason you would know it. To begin with, my face is not one seen outside the imperial capital. There are far too many in this country who would have my head."

"So it's for self-defense? You've earned that much resentment from people?"

"No. It is because strength is the empire's creed. The weak shall die, the frail shall die, the feeble shall die... and so, the strong have the right to claim everything."

"—————"

"The imperial throne is no exception. That is why the empire stands unrivaled in strength among nations."

Resting his cheek on his fist against his knee, Abel expounded on Vollachia's extreme philosophy, and Subaru fell silent.
That his words weren't lies, Subaru knew well enough from the days he'd spent in the imperial soldiers' camp. Todd and the other soldiers would surely agree wholeheartedly with what Abel had just said.
Including them, the people of the empire seemed to genuinely believe that victory settled every account.

It was, in essence, an ethos of *long live the empire.*
For that cause, they wouldn't hesitate to burn down a jungle to wipe out a tribe.
For that cause, they wouldn't hesitate to storm a fellow imperial camp and slaughter every soldier in it—

"—Wait, that's not right."

"What isn't?"

"Say... say you really are the emperor. Then why did you attack the imperial army's camp? You could've just walked out and talked to whoever was in charge there..."

"Fool. Do you imagine I could simply stroll out unguarded? I have no death wish. Do not lump me together with you."

"I don't have a death wish either. But wait, death...?"

He didn't understand why an emperor making contact with his own country's soldiers would be called suicidal.
To Abel, every one of those imperial soldiers ought to be his subordinates. It made no sense for contact with them to be an act of suicide. —No, wait, there was a way it would make sense.
It was—

"If I didn't mishear you before... you said you'd been driven from the throne, didn't you?"

"I did not misspeak, then. But I also told you I would not repeat myself."

"Don't dodge it! That's the important part. Being driven from the throne means..."

Subaru began to doubt whether the utterly unchanging expression on Abel's face belonged to a fellow human being.
Because if Abel's situation was what Subaru suspected, it had to be a hopeless one.
That thought made Subaru hesitate to finish the sentence.

"—Your thinking is correct."

But Abel stepped right over Subaru's hesitation and nodded.
Before Subaru, holding his breath, Abel's gaze dropped slightly to the burning fire. A log popped in the flames, the faint sound like the death cry of a piece of wood.
Listening to that death cry, Abel quietly closed one eye.

"The imperial force deployed outside Buddheim was sent to finish me off by my political enemies. You, correctly, got caught in the crossfire."

"But... but the people at the camp never said anything like that. Those people said their goal was to negotiate with the forest's 'People of Shudrak.'"

Subaru had no special ability to see through other people's lies.
Even so, he couldn't believe it realistic that dozens of men crowded into one place would all keep their story straight solely to deceive outsiders like him and Rem.
So Todd and the rest of the imperial soldiers must genuinely have believed their objective was the "People of Shudrak."

"And yet the real target was to catch you...?"

"Do not dress it up. It was you yourself who said they were plotting an attack. And you, to protect yourself and the woman, weighed Shudrak against the imperial army on a scale."

"That's not—!"

"It is not wrong. What happened cannot be changed. Nor will the dead return to life. The dead say nothing, and can affect nothing among the living."

"—————"

—The dead do not return to life.
Struck by Abel's biting words, Subaru shut his eyes tight.
Someone who knew nothing, nothing at all, saying whatever he pleased.

—There was a way to bring back the dead.
The one and only Authority Subaru alone possessed was the means.

"—————"

By using *Return by Death*, Subaru might be able to go back to before the camp's annihilation.
If he did, he might be able to warn the imperial soldiers and save them from that fate of death. But if he did that, this time the "People of Shudrak" would be the ones in danger.
Raise one side up and the other falls; saving both opposing parties was difficult.
More than anything—

"I don't have..."

He didn't have the resolve to use *Return by Death* for their sake.
Even if he redid it all over again, how much chance was there it would go better? This time, the fact that Rem and Subaru had both come through unharmed was, if not the best outcome, at least the second-best.
To hope for more than that was tantamount to taking up an endless, hopeless challenge.
And how far was he supposed to grind down his own life for that—

"You are a foolish man, burdened by an anguish of unknown origin."

Abel's words suddenly struck Subaru's eardrums as he questioned himself thus.
For a moment he had no idea what had been said to him, and opened his eyes in stunned silence. Across from him, beyond the flickering flame, Abel was gazing at Subaru.
That gaze felt, somehow, almost like pity.

"Why do you wish, above all else, to curry favor with others?"

"Curry favor... me?"

"You look only at others. You have deliberately fashioned yourself into someone who does this. As a warrior hones his own skill, you have concealed your own heart beneath the deception of self-sacrifice."

"—! You have no right to say that to me!"

At Abel speaking as if he understood, Subaru felt an unbearable surge of anger.
Someone who knew nothing—not about *Return by Death*, not about Subaru himself—had no way of understanding even one ten-thousandth of what Subaru carried.
He had no reason to be lectured, and no reason to be pitied.

"Besides, you still haven't answered my question! The imperial soldiers' target was the 'People of Shudrak.' Not one word about you..."

"It is not a tale that would reach even the rank and file. Likely not even the commanders knew. That the emperor had gone to ground is not a story that should leak beyond the capital's walls. Those who drove me out have no wish to see the empire shaken either."

"—————"

"Furthermore, it was inevitable that they would target Shudrak. The only ones who might take the hand of an emperor driven from the throne and fled from the capital are the Shudrak. —Hence, killing Shudrak is much the same as cutting off the limbs of a man barely keeping his head above water, and sinking him to the bottom."

The flat explanation sank into his brain, and Subaru's doubts melted away.
Why Todd and the imperial soldiers had surrounded the jungle, why they had aimed to neutralize or annihilate the "People of Shudrak" through negotiation—all of it.

"...Why are the Shudrak the only ones you can rely on?"

"Once, an emperor of Vollachia saved the Shudrak from dire straits. The Shudrak do not forget a debt. There is also the 'Lifeblood Ritual.' That is my chance of success."

An ancient debt of gratitude toward the emperor spanning tribes, and the Shudrak way of holding rituals sacred.
Abel, driven from the capital, had staked everything on that and attempted contact with the "People of Shudrak." And to prevent it, Abel's political enemies had sent the imperial army into the jungle to bury the emperor.

It had been a gamble with poor odds, but Abel could be said to have won it.
He had brilliantly outmaneuvered his opponent's scheme and repelled the first wave of the enemy closing in on him. But even Subaru understood—there was no way the enemy would back off after this.

"Your fight with your enemy isn't over."

"Of course not. —If I die, that is the end of it. But I live. And so, to reclaim what is mine, I will use every ounce of strength I possess to take it back."

That was the choice of Abel as Emperor of Vollachia—Vincent Abelux.
The word "mine" that he'd used sounded to Subaru as if it carried a silent gloss reading "the nation." That was a way of viewing things on an entirely different scale.
Overwhelmed by its sheer enormity, Subaru belatedly realized something.

"So then, are you saying you're about to start a war... with the Shudrak people!?"

"I am. I have secured their promise of cooperation. The result of the 'Lifeblood Ritual,' and the covenant with a former emperor. Those who sing the praises of pride are easy to make use of. They will fight alongside me."

"After everything you've already done... is that still not enough!?"

Reclaiming the imperial throne would mean any number of unavoidable battles yet to come.
That meant war—the ferocious opening act of humans fighting humans, taking each other's lives.

"—————"

At the burned-out imperial camp, as far as Subaru knew, there had been over a hundred imperial soldiers.
If the camp Abel and the Shudrak had attacked wasn't the only one, then even accounting for survivors, the casualties would number over a hundred. Not quite a thousand, probably, but somewhere in that range.

—In the span of a few hours while Subaru was unconscious, over a hundred people had lost their lives.

"That alone is..."

Of all the days Subaru had lived through in this other world, this was the greatest loss of life by far.
Even the fight against the White Whale, even the battle with Petelgeuse, had claimed victims. The battle in Priestella had left many he couldn't save, and many he still, ongoing, hadn't been able to save.

But the number of lives lost here exceeded even that unavoidable toll of victims.

And this time, the opponents they'd fought weren't Witchbeasts or some malevolent existence—they were fellow human beings.
Not opponents incapable of understanding words, but ones who could have been reasoned with, if only someone had tried.

"Why did it have to be killing...?"

"There was no other way. That is all."

"...Was there really no other way? Did you seriously search for another means? Before killing them and taking away every possibility, right up to the very last moment?"

"—————"

At Subaru's faint, mosquito-thin protest, Abel narrowed his eyes.
It didn't look like he was mulling over Subaru's opinion so much as asking, at some fundamental level, why he should even have to do such a thing.

It wasn't that words failed to reach him, nor that he had no will to engage in dialogue.
And yet they couldn't communicate, for the terrifying, unbridgeable gulf between their values.

Until now, Natsuki Subaru had been fortunate enough never to find himself having to build a relationship with someone whose values differed so drastically from his own.
Most of the people he'd encountered in this other world had been rational, willing to listen to his words, sincere in dealing with him even as they conveyed their own opinions. There had been times his own foolishness ruined things, failing to notice their consideration because he couldn't understand it—but even so, he'd managed, somehow.

The only people whose differing values had made communication truly impossible were, if anything, the Witch and the Sin Archbishops.
But by defining them and her as clearly "different" existences, Subaru had, in the end, managed to avoid ever having to clash values with them directly.

But Abel was different. So were the "People of Shudrak." So were the imperial soldiers.

There was no malice in them, no toying with human life and death, no wielding of overwhelming power for its own selfish sake.
Setting aside the very root of their way of thinking, they were the same human beings as Subaru.
And yet—

"...I just want to take Rem home. That's all."

From here, the battle for Abel to reclaim the imperial throne would begin.
If this were someone else's affair—or some past event handed down through a biography or the like—Subaru's heart might have burned, his hand eager to turn the next page.

But this was reality.
In another country with no one to rely on, a ferocious internal conflict was on the verge of shaking that nation to its foundations. Subaru had absolutely no intention of intervening in that situation, of trying to change history.

His goal was to bring Rem back to the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica.
To reunite with Emilia, Beatrice, and the rest of his companions at Roswaal Manor as soon as possible, to share the joy of Rem waking up, and to discuss what came next.

He couldn't afford to get tangled up in anything beyond that.

"—Tell me the nearest town or village. I'll look for a way home from there."

Slapping his own cheeks with both hands, Subaru declared his goal, narrowed down to just the one thing.
At that, Abel let out a small "Hoh" of a breath, and said,

"A reasonable choice. But it will not be an easy road, either."

"Easy or hard, if it's necessary I'll walk the road. Preferably a paved one, if it's all the same."

"Hmph."

Standing up before the snorting Abel, Subaru bit down hard on the inside of his cheek.
Stimulating his brain with pain, he drove away the various doubts welling up, and faced forward. Then he looked toward Abel, who hadn't changed his posture at all.

The solitary emperor, one eye closed, meeting Subaru's gaze.

"I haven't thanked you yet. ...Whatever the means, thank you for getting Rem out safely. I'm grateful for that."

"Not only that girl—I retrieved the other one as well."

"That one was extra. ...Thanks to that, my problems are on hold for a while longer."

Of course, even if Rui had been lost at the imperial camp, that wouldn't have neatly resolved the problem weighing on Subaru. If anything, as far as his relationship with Rem went, it likely would have made things more serious.
He had no idea which would have been better. And because he didn't know—

"I'll choose whichever one I can live with. ...It's complicated, but do your best on your end too. But the Shudrak people..."

"Do not involve them, you mean to say? Either way, had you and I not intervened, the forest and all in it would have burned—that was to be their fate regardless. This is already their fight as well."

"—————"

There was no room to deny that.
That they now stood in a position where they had to fight to protect themselves.
And yet—

"I could never do that. ...I don't think I could ever become like you."

Shaking his head, Subaru murmured this while looking at Abel.
Abel, one eye closed—the eye shut now was the opposite of before. More than that, Abel's way of blinking was distinctive. One eye at a time, never both eyes closed together.

Subaru understood that this was so that he would never, even for an instant, close both eyes at once.
And he felt fear, and awe, toward this emperor of Vollachia who had survived in a world where such a habit became necessary—the man at the pinnacle of the Empire of the Sword Wolf.
Hearing Subaru's murmur, Abel, still with one eye closed, said,

"Naturally. Neither you nor anyone else could ever take my place."

That was what he said.


△▼△▼△▼△


Parting ways with the "People of Shudrak," they would head for the town nearest Buddheim Jungle.
When Subaru announced that decision, the reaction around him was, surprisingly, rather matter-of-fact.

"I zee, a pity, but it can't be helped. If that iz our comrade's decision."

That was Mizelda's response upon hearing Subaru out.
True to her word, Mizelda, her fine features tinged with a touch of loneliness, respected Subaru's choice just like that.
Honestly, Subaru had confessed his decision braced for her to berate him—that anyone who wouldn't fight wasn't a warrior—so her consideration instead only made him feel worse.

Once he'd secured the chieftain's permission, there was nothing left to stand in the way of Subaru's decision.
He felt a pang of reluctance over Utakata, whose sorrow ran even deeper than Mizelda's, but even that couldn't become a reason for Subaru to stay.

"Su, going somewhere. U sad..."

"Yeah, sorry about that. ...Hey, does Utakata even understand what's about to happen?"

Petting the head of the girl tugging at his hem, Subaru asked Utakata that question.
He had no doubts about the "People of Shudrak's" values or sense of solidarity, and no doubt that Mizelda's decision was the tribe's decision as a whole—but Utakata was still just a child.
If she understood nothing, and was simply swept up in the fervor around her—

"—? From now, fighting starts. U fight too, with Mi and Ta and everyone."

"...I see."

Sighing at Utakata, who showed him the bow equipped on her back as she spoke, Subaru let out a breath.
He wished young children could remain ignorant of such things. But even that hope, it seemed, was too naive a notion to hold in this jungle.

To begin with, Utakata already had one accomplishment to her name: shooting Subaru dead with a poisoned arrow.
However she might appear, Utakata was undeniably one of the "People of Shudrak." She was equipped with the resolve to fight, and the resolve to kill an opponent.
And still—

"Don't die, Utakata."

"U won't die! Su, don't die too, do your best."

Meaning it as encouragement, Subaru instead received an even bigger dose of encouragement in return, and found himself at a loss for words, managing only a weak smile in reply.
Honestly, he had no way to imagine how good the odds were for Abel and the Shudrak in the coming fight.

Targeting Abel, driven from the throne, the other side had dispatched a subjugation force into Buddheim Jungle.
Which meant the enemy was in a position to freely move imperial military strength at their own discretion. Abel's forces, meanwhile, consisted only of the Shudrak—a small band of fewer than a hundred.
Of course, there was the possibility that, using the same kind of brilliant, devious strategy that had let him unilaterally attack the imperial camp and repel the subjugation force, Abel might rack up victory after victory—

"—The preparations are complete."

"—!"

Just then, as his mind was gripped by thoughts he'd meant to set aside, someone spoke to him from the side.
Subaru's shoulders jerked, and he spun around with such force that the other person's eyes went wide in turn.

"—? What's wrong, to startle you so much?"

The one looking at Subaru's tensed face as she said this was Rem.
A wooden staff in one hand, a small bundle of luggage on her back, Rem appeared to have finished preparing to leave the forest, just as Subaru had.

—Yes, when it came to relaying Subaru's choice to leave this forest, the most unexpected reaction of all had come from none other than Rem herself.

Honestly, Subaru had expected Rem to stubbornly resist, and had thought he'd need to persuade her.
In the worst case, he'd even been prepared to drag the sleeping Rem out by force and slip away from the Shudrak settlement in the dead of night, like some kind of moonlight flit.
But when he'd braced himself for refusal and explained the situation to her straight on, Rem's response had been unexpectedly simple.

*"—. Understood. I'll finish preparing by tomorrow,"* she'd said.

So even now, looking at Rem with her traveling gear all in order, Subaru still couldn't quite accept it as real.

"...Um?"

"Ah! No, sorry, it's fine. Yeah, your travel gear's all set. So cute, so cute."

"What?"

"I mean, so reliable! You're a big help. Having someone reliable like you around makes me a lucky guy."

His responses came out so clumsy that Rem gave him a rather dubious look.
It wasn't that he was trying to curry favor with her, particularly. Of course, having Rem not resist him was a huge relief, so ideally he'd like to preserve the current state of their relationship. Ideally, he'd like them to grow even closer.

"—————"

That was something to try again once his own mental state had settled down more.
Right now, his real priority was to put as much distance as possible, as quickly as possible, between himself and the looming smell of war.

"So, are your own preparations finished? It looked like you had time to say your farewells..."

"Ah, yeah, I'm fine on that front. I didn't have much luggage to begin with, and Rem's carrying most of it anyway."

"...But you're the one who has to carry me, you know."

Rem, her tone dropping, glanced briefly toward a handmade wooden frame set in the plaza.
Built from thick branches lashed together with vines, it was what people would call a pack frame—something Subaru had put together to carry Rem on his back as they traveled.

With her staff, Rem could walk, however haltingly, but the journey from here to the nearest town would take several days.
If he had to match Rem's pace the whole way, there was no telling how long it would take. So Subaru had built this, with the Shudrak's help.

"It's got a pretty handmade feel to it, but the sturdiness should be fine. I already tested it out with Taritta-san, who weighs more than you do."

"I don't have much attachment to weight one way or the other, but I do think that's rude to Taritta-san."

Earning Rem's displeasure yet again, Subaru gave a wry laugh and scratched his cheek.
Then he shifted his gaze past Rem—to Rui, their traveling companion. Naturally, if Subaru was bringing Rem along, Rui would be coming too.

"No matter how you look at it, I can't just dump a live bomb on the Shudrak and leave..."

Not to mention, letting a Sin Archbishop out of his sight was simply out of the question.
There had already been plenty of chances so far where he'd taken his eyes off her, and each time, Rui could have shown her true colors—he didn't want to give her any more such chances going forward.
Even as he found himself starting to believe that Rui's behavior wasn't an act, but genuine.

"Ah, uu."

As for Rui, her long hair now tied up at the back of her head, her white outfit mended, she looked quite different from before.
It seemed she'd been doted on in the Shudrak settlement, and the mended clothes appeared to be a gift from its people.

"Sorry for relying on you all for everything."

"Do not fret over it, Subaru. You overcame the 'Lifeblood Ritual' and showed the shine of your own Soul. That we lend our strength to you is a rightful honor toward a comrade."

"Comrade..."

Arms crossed, Mizelda had come to see them off, and at her words Subaru looked down.
He couldn't bring himself to face Mizelda properly as she called him "comrade" with such warmth. Because Subaru knew a brutal fight lay ahead for them, and he was aware he himself was one of the very people who had dragged them into that fight—and yet he was trying to slip away from this place regardless.
Was that really something a comrade of the pride and honor she spoke of would do?

"Do not trouble yourself over it, Subaru."

But Mizelda spoke as though she'd read exactly what was in Subaru's heart.

"Mizelda-san..."

"We fight, and prove our own worth. But bY protecting what matters most, we alzo build a future. It iz a necezzary way of thinking, for protecting one'z tribe."

"—————"

"Protect Rem and Rui. That iz the honor I expect of a comrade."

At Mizelda's straightforward words, the corners of Subaru's eyes grew hot.
He didn't feel like correcting Mizelda's misunderstanding about Rui. At the very least, by fulfilling that role, he could answer the trust Mizelda and the others placed in him.
Even if he and they would probably never meet again after this—

"We're ready over here-oh. About time to head out-oh."

Right as Mizelda gave him that send-off, a woman waving broadly—Holly, the tips of her hair dyed yellow—called out to Subaru with a beaming smile.
Beside Holly stood the slender, green-haired Kuna. In contrast to the cheerful Holly, Kuna gave off a quiet, taciturn impression; the two of them were to be their escorts.

On the road to the nearest town—Guaral—Holly and Kuna would protect Subaru and Rem from anything that might attack them.

"I thought having an escort was almost too much to ask..."

This was a foreign land, and in the span of just a few days Subaru had already died several times over.
No denying the circumstances had been unusual, but there was no such thing as being too careful. Subaru himself had no combat ability worth relying on, and expecting anything of the sort from Rem and Rui was asking too much.
He wasn't sure about Kuna, but he'd seen with his own eyes Holly lift a boulder like it was nothing. As an escort for the road, she was more than trustworthy enough.
And then—

"—Rem, does anything hurt anywhere?"

Hefting the pack frame firmly onto his back, Subaru asked over his shoulder.
Since the pack frame put them back to back, Subaru had no way of seeing Rem's face from where he stood. He'd stuffed in as much soft leaves and cloth as he could to secure her body, but over a long journey there would surely be plenty of inconveniences.

"I'm fine. ...More importantly, are you going to be all right?"

"I've been training reasonably hard, so yeah. My stamina's not fully back yet, but I want to avoid a situation where Holly and the others have their hands full when it matters."

To begin with, having them provide an escort at all was already a huge help—there was no way he could shamelessly ask them to carry Rem on top of that.

With Subaru showing off that stubborn streak and Rem on his back, Rui standing neatly at their side, and Holly and Kuna, traveling light but with their own preparations complete, standing ready, the People of Shudrak began gathering at the entrance of the settlement one after another.

"Then, to the peace of our comrade Natsuki Subaru, and to the fulfillment of hiz purpoze."

"—To itz fulfillment!"

At Mizelda's call as she stood at the front, the other Shudrak raised their voices together.
Feeling as though the wind itself crackled with the force of it, Subaru answered their spirit the only way he could—with a smile and a shout of "Yeah!"

"Thank you, everyone. Please stay safe!"

The moment he said it, he thought to himself how hollow a greeting that was.
Given the turbulent road that lay ahead of them, "stay safe" felt like an empty, meaningless thing to say.
But there was nothing more he could say. Nothing more he could hope for.

Stay safe, and please, all of you good-hearted women, keep on living.

"—————"

Wishing that, Subaru searched the line of well-wishers for that menacing masked figure.
But, unsurprising as it was, that figure was nowhere to be found.

He wasn't the sort to come see them off, nor was theirs that kind of relationship.
Even if Subaru had seen him there, he'd have had nothing to say to him.
So instead—

"—We're off!"
"Uu!"

Rui's voice rang out high and clear, joining Subaru's half-desperate call.
And so Subaru and the others left the "People of Shudrak" behind, taking their first step toward Guaral, the nearest town, to flee the flames of war that were surely about to erupt.
