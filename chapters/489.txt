Arc 6 Chapter 82: "A Battle Bound by Shackles"

—Slowly, stage by stage, she unbinds her shackles, and the heat within her own body begins to rise.

There is a sensation of blood coming to a boil, and the muscles of her slender frame all draw taut.

A girl's body—soft to the touch, seemingly fleeting and fragile—yet the blood flowing within it, the flesh and bone that make it up, belong to the finest race this world has ever known.

And, among them, the return of the one called the "Oni God"—the greatest masterpiece of them all—

"That's Sister! Ahh, how wonderful! How radiant she is! No matter how far Rem reaches, Rem could never catch up, never reach her, never match her—that's Sister for you!"

"Don't spray spit everywhere. It's unpleasant. And stop comparing Ram to things."

"Heh, and why's that?"

"Isn't it obvious? Ram has no need for any title greater than 'Rem's sister.'"

Saying so in an exasperated tone, Ram spun her body, and her elbow strike pierced the wind.

A graze would split skin, a solid blow would shatter bone, even piercing through to the organs within—such a fearsome martial art, unleashed without reservation, a relentless barrage of strikes launched with hands and feet alike.

It was not a technique that relied on pure physical ability or skill alone.

If this had been pure technique, Batenkaitos—who could reproduce any technique from any era or place, relying on the "memories" of those he'd devoured—would surely have been able to counter it.

But Ram's combat art was a cut above anything so simple.

"—"

As Ram closed in, the elbow and knee strikes she unleashed would freely accelerate or decelerate mid-motion. That was because she wrapped herself in the wind magic she excelled at, weaving feints and truths together in the midst of battle.

With wind she accelerated her speed, or slowed it, freely warping the perception of Batenkaitos as he tried to counter. On top of that, Ram used the wind to scatter her presence in every direction, slipping into her enemy's blind spots with swift movement while driving in fatal blows again and again.

"Haah! I can't get enough of this!"

Dodging Ram's attacks by the barest margin, Batenkaitos cheered on, delighted.

Blood flowing from his torn right cheek, his long, ragged dark-brown hair whipping wildly, "Gluttony" was enjoying the comparison of their abilities.

"A spice with a kick I just can't resist! A trick with a bite, oh so slick! Bursting with delight, purely dynamic! Sister, you're a feast made just for our taste!"

Leaping about the spiral staircase, swapping positions again and again, Batenkaitos laughed.

He clashed the daggers in both hands against Ram's staff, and whenever distance opened between them, drool dripped from his open mouth.

"Nice, nice, how nice, ain't it nice, sure is nice, isn't it nice, it's gonna be nice, it's surely gonna be nice, precisely because it's so nice—! Gluttonous drink! Gluttonous feast!"

"—"

"Best of all, I love the smell of that anger. An enemy who hates us—hates us personally—this much... when I get to savor that on my tongue, what kind of exquisite feast will I get to enjoy, I wonder!?"

Clapping his hands together, Batenkaitos trembled with anticipation of some pleasure beyond comprehension.

Watching this before her, Ram let out a long breath and checked the condition of her own body.

"Far from being in top form, but..."

Though it didn't show on her face, Ram was, in all sincerity, astonished.

"No one knows themselves better than I do" is a stock phrase of a certain kind of person who doesn't actually understand themselves at all—but unlike such fools, Ram truly, completely grasped herself.

Having lost her Oni horn and become "Hornless," Ram's body was, day after day, on the verge of being crushed beneath an immense burden. That was why Ram had placed shackles upon her own consciousness.

Normally, while working as a maid, she kept every shackle fastened.

At times, an emergency arose where she had to use magic to break through the situation. In such cases, she'd remove a single shackle, permitting herself the bare minimum use of magic.

And if even that wasn't enough to settle matters, she'd remove a second shackle—viable only for a short, decisive battle, but enough to bring out roughly twenty percent of Ram's true performance.

About a year and a half ago, when she'd crossed blades with Garfiel in the "Sanctuary," that was right around that level.

That, she'd been certain, was the full extent of what "Hornless" Ram could bring to bear—beyond that, her body would fail to endure the strain and become useless.

And now, Ram was about to remove that double-edged sword of a shackle one stage further.

"—"

For an instant, a rare hesitation twisted through Ram's chest.

As stated before, Ram had a perfect grasp of herself.

And for someone like her, this state of barely feeling any backlash at all from a fight like this—that was something that truly hadn't happened since she was a child not yet ten years old.

Normally, if the backlash from a fight like this struck back at her, Ram's head would split with pain, blood would pour endlessly from her nose and even her eyes, and she'd have to resign herself to being unable to move for a while.

None of that was happening. —Because it was flowing instead into Subaru, bound to her by shared fate.

"...If I take too long, Barusu is going to die."

If things were going as discussed, Subaru would by now be running himself ragged dealing with the rampaging Shaula and the surging horde of witchbeasts.

With him would be Beatrice, and probably Meili. Emilia, whose memory throbbed at the back of her mind, must have headed for the tower's upper floors and be fighting alone there.

Strangely, she found herself concerned about the doings of that ever-forward-looking silver-haired girl. Before her "name" had been stolen, she must have been quite a handful indeed.

Every last one of them was simply too unreliable without Ram around.

And so—

"Endure it like your life depends on it, Barusu. —Rem will cry if you don't."

Casting those unheard words out, Ram removed her third shackle for the first time in ten years.

—The next instant, the backlash of "Cor Leonis" bared its fangs at Natsuki Subaru.

"—Guh."

It was a strain that felt like coughing up blood—no, one that actually made him cough up blood. For an instant he felt one of the faint lights he could sense within the tower swell in size, and the very next moment it was upon him.

"Subaru!"

"—!"

Coughing blood-tinged spittle, Subaru began to collapse, and Beatrice, held close against him, caught his body.

Unfortunately, what carried Subaru and Beatrice now was not the well-ridden, familiar Patrasche, but a witchbeast never meant to carry anyone—a Famished Horse King.

With little experience riding without the Divine Protection of Wind Evasion, and no saddle to sit in or stirrups to brace against, one moment's carelessness could send him flying off in an instant.

That it hadn't come to that yet was only because Beatrice, using the Guiltywhip wrapped around the witchbeast's upper body as makeshift reins, was meticulously controlling their weight with magic.

Subaru couldn't afford to ruin her ceaseless effort.

"Is that Ram's backlash hitting you, I wonder?"

"Just as you'd guess... sorry, I'll hold on somehow...!"

Gripping the whip tight once more, Subaru answered the perceptive Beatrice.

The nausea welling up in him tasted and smelled of rusted iron, and he had no idea at all where in his body the strain had struck.

Some organ screaming in protest, something inside him torn open—damage of that sort, perhaps.

Either way, this was proof that Ram was getting serious.

"If Ram can just take care of one half of 'Gluttony'..."

He wouldn't say that would make everything easy all at once.

For one thing, he had no confidence that defeating "Gluttony" would even mean the return of the stolen "names" and "memories." All that was certain was that it would lighten Subaru's own burden.

—If that happened, Subaru could concentrate all the burden he was absorbing onto Meili alone.

"Haah, haah...!"

Clinging to the back of her own Famished Horse King, Meili was desperately shouting instructions to the witchbeasts.

To hold off the giant scorpion fiercely swinging pincers and tail stinger at Subaru, Meili's desperate fighting was indispensable.

Making up for their lack of fighting strength with sheer numbers, Subaru and the others were forcibly manufacturing a grace period that shouldn't have existed.

That was why, when Subaru had shouted that it was vital to dote on Meili like a delicate flower, it hadn't been playing around or a joke—it had been dead serious.

At the Pleiades Watchtower, now beset by five different threats, the strength of people like Emilia, Ram, and Julius was indispensable to breaking through each one—but the linchpin holding all those battlefields together was none other than Meili Portroute.

That was why Subaru had already, for some time now, been taking upon himself the feedback from the Divine Protection falling on Meili.

"Winged Earth-Dragon-chan! Oiran Bear-chan! Together, now!"

In a high voice, surveying the battlefield from above, Meili drove the witchbeasts into the jaws of death.

Clapping her hands, snapping her fingers, or calling out orders aloud—the method varied by witchbeast, but commanding any of them demanded the full power of her Divine Protection.

That it brought considerable strain was something Subaru keenly felt himself, from the throbbing, swollen sensation in his own brain.

"—"

The benefits granted by Otto's Divine Protection of the Soul of Language, or Meili's Divine Protection of Demon Manipulation, carried a strong sense of amplifying an ability humans already possessed, rather than something purely magical.

If anything, it seemed less like magic and more like a psychic sort of power.

Given that, it made sense the feedback would be greater than with other Divine Protections.

In the sense that the Protection's target wasn't the surroundings or other people, but ultimately oneself.

"Divine Protections, Authorities—they're all just parts of some incomprehensible system..."

The Authorities that the Sin Archbishops and the "Witches" wielded—which, by whatever twist of fate, Subaru had ended up using too.

Or the Divine Protections, bestowed by chance upon those born into this world.

That power is only as good as its use—Subaru acknowledged that as a kind of truth—but even so—

"It's because things like that exist..."

...that tragedy is born—he couldn't stop himself from thinking it.

Behind the fear the Sin Archbishops and "Witches" inspired, behind their ability to wield such unchecked, outrageous power, the might of Authorities that kept everyone else at bay was, without doubt, deeply involved.

Divine Protections too—there were plenty of people who wouldn't have had their fates twisted if such things didn't exist.

Meili herself, for instance, was a victim of a Divine Protection's darker side.

Because she'd been born with the power to commune with witchbeasts, she'd been abandoned by her own birth parents. —Whether that was true, he didn't know. But it was a convincing theory. A self-evident truth.

At the very least, within Meili, it was the truth.

Which was exactly why—

"Man, I really am a walking double standard, aren't I..."

He didn't want to rely on Authorities or Divine Protections at all, and yet, if it was necessary, he couldn't hesitate to wring every last drop from them. It was true a weak person's options were limited, but he didn't want his heart to grow petty along with it.

He didn't want to become someone who could forget he was forcing another down a path she shouldn't have to walk, and just laugh about it.

"Onii-san! Are you focusing!? If you and Beatrice-chan get taken out, that's the end of everything, you know!?"

"I know. —Hey, Meili. Once this is all over, how about becoming part of my family?"

"—! You're saying that right after telling me to focus!!"

Enduring the heaviness in his skull, Subaru had asked that, and Meili shouted back at him.

Neither of them had any room to spare. If their focus broke, they could be finished off in an instant.

But even so—

"After going through this much suffering, it just doesn't add up unless I believe a good tomorrow's waiting for us."

So thought Natsuki Subaru, even as he coughed up blood.

Continuing the battle atop the sand, Meili muttered under her breath, "This is insane."

Of course, there were plenty of candidates for "insane" here.

Shaula, transformed into a giant scorpion right before her eyes, certainly qualified. So did that lively, energetic silver-haired half-elf. So did Ram and Julius and the rest, fighting all manner of enemies inside the watchtower. And Subaru and Beatrice, fighting beside her now, most of all.

But surely, the most insane one of all was—

"Me, I suppose."

How had it come to this? Even Meili herself no longer understood herself.

To begin with, she thought her out-of-character behavior had already started the moment she'd tagged along on Subaru and the others' journey across the Augria Sand Dunes.

Going with the flow, as she was asked, without caring whether it was right or wrong, she'd obediently carried out whatever she was ordered to do.

That had been Meili's way of getting by, so she'd intended to do the same here too.

And yet—

"—It's because a thing like the 'Book of the Dead' exists at all."

Hearing that she could relive the feelings of the dead, Meili had lost to the curiosity within her.

She'd had no choice but to unseal the feelings for Elsa Granhiert that had been smoldering inside her all along, and as a result, ended up piling up the most un-Meili-like acts of foolishness of her entire life.

On top of that, she'd been caught in the act, and Subaru had even lectured her all high-and-mighty.

And yet, strangely, she hadn't been able to just laugh off his words.

Somehow, she'd ended up talked into promising her continued cooperation after that, one thing leading to another without her ever really deciding it.

Subaru had even told her he'd keep showing her his back forever, but she figured he was the type to say something rash and regret it later, with no real idea how much responsibility he was prepared to take.

In the sense of being easily swept along, Meili had to admit she recognized something of herself in that.

The difference between Meili and Subaru, though, was that with him it wasn't just talk.

If Subaru said he'd do something, he'd try to do it, and the one who'd been present when that promise was made—Beatrice, was it—she'd surely make sure he kept it too.

Given that, one-sidedly taking advantage of it weighed on her conscience, and she'd even felt like helping out somehow—but she hadn't signed up to go this far.

"—"

Even now, a flock of Winged Earth-Dragons, leaping at a gesture from Meili, were swept aside by the giant scorpion's tail stinger and scattered in an instant.

Normally, the timid Winged Earth-Dragons would never attack an enemy stronger than themselves, but even though Meili could issue orders that overrode their instincts, that didn't mean she could make the witchbeasts any stronger.

"Aahn! They got wiped out!"

Swinging its massive pincers, the rampaging giant scorpion's violence killed witchbeast after witchbeast.

Torn open, crushed, punctured—the blood spilling from the witchbeasts soaked into the parched sand, and the sea of sand that had looked yellow was gradually turning a dark reddish-black.

"—"

Gritting her teeth at that, Meili quickly cast her eyes about, searching for the presence of witchbeasts in the sky, on the sand's surface, even down beneath it, taming whatever she could as she found it.

She couldn't afford to be picky about quality. Right now, sheer numbers—an inexhaustible supply of pieces—were what she needed.

"Ugh, ugh, ugh, ugh! I'm definitely making Onii-san grant me a selfish wish for this later!"

With no room even to wipe the sweat beading on her forehead, Meili grumbled spitefully at Subaru, keeping the giant scorpion in check from a careful, hovering distance.

Riding the back of a Famished Horse King was no comfortable experience for Meili either, but unlike Subaru, she'd driven a knife through the cloak wrapped around her body and into the witchbeast's back, forcibly anchoring herself in place.

It was a witchbeast under her control. It accepted an outrage like that without complaint.

The problem wasn't the witchbeast so much as it was Meili herself, placed in this situation.

To begin with, a last-ditch battle like this wasn't Meili's real way of doing things at all.

Meili's way of fighting lived and died on preparation beforehand—laying the groundwork to bring surrounding witchbeasts under her control, positioning them across the battlefield, then watching their combined assault unfold from a safe vantage point.

That was the true forte of the "Witchbeast Tamer," Meili Portroute.

Of course, commanding directly on the battlefield sharpened the witchbeasts' precision in attack and defense, but it wasn't something worth doing at the cost of endangering Meili herself. It was because she'd misjudged that trade-off that she'd lost her fight against Subaru and the others and ended up a captive.

"Elsa..."

Part of the reason she'd died was the accumulation of blunders just like that.

Of course, Elsa surely wouldn't want Meili blaming herself over it at all, and there was even a chance she didn't care that she'd died in the first place. That was exactly why it gnawed at her.

Just as she couldn't understand Elsa's feelings, she couldn't really understand her own either.

For one thing, here she was staging a delaying action against the giant scorpion, devoting herself entirely to buying time in exchange for witchbeasts' lives—but Meili had never fought this way before.

Meili Portroute, young as she was, had been an assassin.

On someone's orders, someone's instructions, someone's request, taking lives had been her trade.

So this was a first for her.

—Having to fight in order to save someone's life. To protect it.

"This isn't how I do things at all!"

That earlier remark of Subaru's, delirious as it sounded, was maddeningly infuriating.

How dare he say something like "become part of my family" to her, of all people.

That attitude of his, as if he had no idea at all why he'd be hated—it was truly aggravating.

Even if her direct enemy had been Garfiel, it was Subaru who'd engineered that whole situation. And here at the Pleiades Watchtower too, it seemed like he was, once again, arranging the situation entirely to his own liking.

"—"

And yet, even as she thought so critically of Subaru's words and actions, Meili realized that she herself was following his instructions, being moved like a piece on the board just like everyone else.

If this situation had been shaped by some deep, far-reaching scheme of Subaru's, that would still be easier to accept.

But by any measure, Subaru didn't have that kind of capacity. All Subaru had was desperation, presumptuous expectations, and an almost insane trust that staked even his own life on it—

"Somehow, I feel like I'm turning into an idiot too."

As she said this, Meili drew up a witchbeast she'd captured from underground and set it against the giant scorpion.

"—"

Bursting up through the sand sea now stained red, what shot out from underground was a Sand Earthworm, its enormous body trembling. Writhing its bulk of over ten meters, it came crashing down onto the giant scorpion with tremendous force.

If it could crush and bury the scorpion in sand, that would be a major upset—but things wouldn't go that smoothly.

White light burst forth, and the thick body of the Sand Earthworm was blown away in an instant.

As the Sand Earthworm, cleaved clean in two, came falling while spewing a bizarre shriek and bodily fluids, a barrage of tail-stinger strikes shattered it further into smaller pieces.

But that was a smokescreen of flesh and blood, bought with the Sand Earthworm's life.

—The real attack was the three Famished Horse Kings emerging from the very hole the Sand Earthworm had burst out of.

"—!!"

Letting out a roar like a wailing infant, the burning half-man, half-horse creatures charged the giant scorpion in a suicide rush.

Various witchbeasts had been confirmed across the Augria Sand Dunes, but when it came to raw firepower on an individual basis, none surpassed the Famished Horse King.

Using the Winged Earth-Dragons—numerous but never decisive—the stamina-lacking Oiran Bears, and the Sand Earthworm trying to crush everything under its bulk as cover, Meili had been holding her real trump card in reserve.

Drawn in by the presence of this battle, three Famished Horse Kings had gathered.

In raw combat power they fell short of the giant scorpion, but this was the witchbeast their opponent was most wary of. That, in turn, was proof it felt threatened by this witchbeast's firepower.

"I really am in good form today, aren't I."

One that Meili herself rode, one carrying Subaru and Beatrice, and the three Famished Horse Kings prepared for this attack—moving all of these at once should have carried a considerable burden.

Strangely, Meili couldn't feel any of that backlash right now.

It was possible the exhilaration of battle was responsible for that, but if so, she was happy to lean on it.

She had no intention of trying to finish it off.

But if she could inflict even a little damage, it would make Subaru's goal that much easier to accomplish.

"—"

—In a sense, this might have been the third time that Meili, a girl who had lived her life simply going with the flow, had wished to do something of her own will.

The first time was when she'd walked the watchtower at night, seeking salvation in the "Book of the Dead."

The second time was when, driven into a corner, she'd closed in on the back of Subaru as he stood on the spiral staircase.

And the third time was now, seeking to achieve results beyond what was asked of her, in order to fulfill the goal of buying time.

"—Meili!!"

The instant the three Famished Horse Kings closed in on the giant scorpion, a blood-spitting scream called out her name.

It was, quite literally, the shout of Subaru, blood trickling from the corner of his mouth.

Meili found it strange that it wasn't a cheer, or delight, or amazement.

She was doing this so properly—what was there for him to be so angry about?

But—

"—Eh?"

The three Famished Horse Kings leveled their spears of flame and hurled themselves fiercely at the giant scorpion.

No matter how skillfully it wielded pincers and tail stinger, there was no way it could fend this off entirely—that calculation crumbled the instant she saw the change come over the giant scorpion's body.

It was as if it had been scorched by flame, or had drunk in blood just like the sand around it.

The outer shell, which had gleamed dully like black steel, changed color as if suddenly waking up. In a single beat, the next instant, the jet-black carapace had transformed into a blood-like red.

—Certain witchbeasts undergo a change known as an "aggression coloring."

Their behavior clearly shifts from what came before, becoming more ferocious and aggressive.

As an easily recognizable sign that it's occurred, their appearance often changes as well. The Famished Horse King's flames swelling larger, or countless eyes appearing across the White Whale's entire body, are both examples of this.

And that applied to the giant scorpion as well—no, to the "Crimson Scorpion."

More aggressive, more destructive, more annihilating than ever—

"—Ah."

White light burst out in every direction, and the three Famished Horse Kings that had leapt in were blown away.

At the same time, the aftershock of the scattering white light raged across the sea of sand as if sweeping it clean.

And then—

"—"

Swallowed by the bursting torrent of light, Meili's small body was sent flying through the air amid a spray of blood.

—The sensation of blood boiling, and the overflowing exhilaration that came with it—she'd hated both since she was a child.

An omnipotent feeling, as though she could rule over everything in this world.

Had she gone on drinking in that illusion indefinitely, then no matter how firm and unshakable a spirit she'd been blessed with, time would surely have led her astray eventually.

She was aware that she was superior. But she placed no blind faith in it.

She made mistakes too. All she had was the will to try not to, and the resolve to correct them when she did.

That she'd been able to hold onto that resolve was thanks to never getting drunk on that sense of omnipotence.

Thanks to never mistaking herself for something wonderful just because those around her sang her praises, and never having to bow to the whims of those who worshipped stale old customs and outdated mysticism.

And what was it, exactly, that had let her avoid being crushed by such outside forces?

She knew, surely, that it was thanks to some missing piece she couldn't remember.

Because—

"—Ram isn't just cute, she's clever too."

Leaving that bit of self-praise behind her, Ram's advancing step shattered the steps of the spiral staircase.

In that instant, the peach-haired Oni wrapped in wind swung her arm, smashing head-on through the arm her opponent raised to block. Wrist, elbow, and straight up to the shoulder—she wrenched and snapped it all in an instant.

"—Gah!"

She didn't even give him time for a groan of pain.

She drove her fist into his face just as he began to scream, and Ram's white fingers became like a spray of buckshot.

Struck by countless blows all over his body, Batenkaitos vomited blood and went flying. Chasing after him, Ram rode the wind rising beneath her feet and leaped high into the air.

"Hihi!"

As if to intercept Ram, Batenkaitos, launched into the air, swung both his legs.

Immediately after, a distortion in space touched Ram's shoulder, shallowly slicing through her clothes and skin. —An invisible blade left behind in midair, an irritatingly clever trap conjured by wind magic.

"This much—"

"Blow it away with wind? No, no, that won't work, it's useless! That's because it's fixed right into space itself! Even Sister can't scatter that away, too bad!"

Anticipating her thinking before she could act on it, Batenkaitos, wearing a blood-stained grin, kicked off against the air.

He wasn't actually kicking off the air—it was a trick-laden form of walking on air, using the blade that had cut Ram's shoulder as a foothold. Using footholds whose position only he could know, he leapt freely about the spiral staircase, making full use of the tower's scale.

But even so—

"—'Clairvoyance.'"

Even if she couldn't perceive it directly, so long as she could overlap her sight with that of the person who'd set it, a slight shift of awareness would reveal exactly where it was.

As "Gluttony" tried to flee upward and upward, foothold after trap-laden foothold, Ram overlapped his vision to see through each trap, and used those very same footholds to chase him down.

"Hah! Ahahahahaha! Sister, are you serious about that? For real!?"

"That's a very Barusu-like way of talking. —It's a relief; it makes my hesitation to slap you disappear."

Leaping into the air faster than the astonished Batenkaitos could rise, Ram looked down at his unlikable face below her.

And then, without mercy, she drove the sole of her shoe into that face, its mouth open and lined with sharp fangs.

"Bu—"

"Now then, I wonder how many times you'll get to taste the sole of Ram's shoe before you hit the ground."

His nose crushed flat, Batenkaitos's stunted body flipped over in midair.

With his upward momentum killed, "Gluttony," propelled by the force of Ram's kick, was now sent plummeting straight down instead.

Chasing after him, Ram raised both hands to the sky, generated wind from her palms, and dove down steeply, driving a second kick—no, a third, a fourth—into the falling Batenkaitos's face, again and again.

Mercilessly, with every strike, Ram's heel shattered his nose, his front teeth, his jaw, his forehead.

That smirking face, and the mouth that kept calling her "Sister" over and over, were both unpleasant to her.

She ruined them both, and still the fall continued.

Incidentally—

"If you happen to hit your own trap, that's not Ram's fault. That's just you reaping what you sowed."

"Gih! Gyah! Higiiiii!"

On his way down from the kick, the invisible blades set in midair sliced through Batenkaitos's body.

Blood sprayed, and among the wounds were deep gouges carved into flesh, but Ram paid it no mind whatsoever.

Ram had no particular taste for deliberately tormenting and hurting an opponent.

But that only applied when there was something about the opponent worth sympathizing with. If the opponent was a hated enemy, she had no hesitation about making them squeal like a pig.

"So? Feeling regret yet?"

Skillfully braking her fall in midair, Ram asked this while keeping her foot planted on his face and chest.

His face a bloody, mangled mess, Batenkaitos didn't answer the question, and instead tried to raise the daggers in both hands.

In that instant, both of Ram's hand-blades struck his shoulders in a flash, forcibly wrenching both joints from their sockets.

"—Gwah."

"So? Feeling regret yet?"

No matter how superior a technique one has, an arm can't swing from a dislocated shoulder.

Looking down at his wide-eyed, astonished face, Ram repeated the same question once more.

"—"

She searched for fear taking root in the eyes of Batenkaitos as he looked at her.

Carving it in was the goal. Fear, pain, the crushing sense that defiance was forbidden. It wasn't for the sake of revenge—no trivial reason like that.

"To bring Rem—"

back.

That was why. To bring back not just Rem, but the great many other victims "Gluttony" had preyed on until now—those who'd had their "names" and "memories" stolen, whose histories of connection with others had been usurped.

If simply killing him would do, Ram could accomplish that even now, in the midst of this.

With the same effort it took to kick his face to pieces, she could wrap her sole in a wind blade and take his head off. Even a Witch Cultist notorious for their disgraceful refusal to die would surely die once head and body were separated—that had already been proven.

In the burning Oni village, in the process of exacting retribution on those who'd destroyed it.

Witch Cultists, after all, were nothing but a mob of weak, foolish beings with rotten natures—she knew that much.

That was why, before taking his life, she asked her question in order to kill his spirit first.

"So? Feeling regret yet?"

"—! 'Solar Eclipse'!"

Hearing the question for the third time, a faint trace of fear mixed into the color of his blood.

But right before Ram's eyes, it vanished, quite literally, in the blink of an eye.

This wasn't a disappearance on the level of simply moving at high speed.

The feel of his face and body pressed beneath her shoe simply vanished. But she immediately had an idea where Batenkaitos had gone.

"—'Clairvoyance.'"

As long as she held onto his line of sight, no one could escape Ram.

"—"

Tracing the visible sight, she pinpointed his location on the spiral staircase. Wrapping herself in wind and catching up to that spot, she found a bald old man clad in something resembling priest's robes.

Anyone other than Ram might have been thrown off by such a change in appearance.

But through the sharing of sight, Ram was certain that whatever form he took, this was Batenkaitos.

"I'd heard shapeshifting was 'Lust's' trick. What other little tricks do you have up your sleeve?"

"Ah, aaahh, damn it! And here I hadn't meant to use it... no, I shouldn't have needed to...!"

"—? What are you talking about?"

Catching up to him on the spiral staircase, she found the old man kneeling there—Batenkaitos, groaning in pain.

Frowning at an attitude that didn't look like acting, Ram nonetheless immediately tossed out the question weighing on her.

What mattered was that he still had breath left in him, and that his spirit hadn't yet been broken.

And besides—

"—I can't take much longer."

It was a mutter only in her own mouth, but it had already been over a minute since Ram had removed her greatest shackle—there was a faint burden on Ram herself, but most of it, right now, was flowing toward Subaru.

She'd told Subaru to fine-tune it, but just as she'd expected, he'd gone and taken on the entirety of Ram's burden all on his own. He just had to keep playing the hero, no matter what.

He ought to save that sort of thing only for the woman he loved, and for Rem.

"—. I don't know your situation, but have you decided to accept my demand? Return everything you've devoured as 'Gluttony' up until now. If you do that—"

"...If we do that, what? You'll let us go, is that it?"

"No. If you do that, I'll kill you quickly. Isn't that a good deal? I'll forgive, in a single stroke, a crime that deserves ten thousand deaths."

"Fuhah."

Batenkaitos, even his first-person pronoun grown elderly, let out a laugh just as aged.

Observing his behavior, Ram guessed that the transformation in his form was likely a necessary process for fully drawing out that person's ability.

What had made him vanish completely from before her earlier was a technique that could be called a short-range spatial leap.

The one capable of using that was surely this bald old man Batenkaitos had transformed into.

"Still, it's strange. If you had a trump card like that, it wouldn't be odd to have played it much earlier. And yet, why keep it hidden?"

"—"

"...Is there some reason you don't want to use it? Something about being dragged down by the vessel, perhaps?"

"Well now... truly, I've never in my life come across such a frightening woman. ...Never have. Really, Sister, you're terrifying."

At her guess, drawn from the scattered possibilities, the old man's form slowly shifted back into the stunted body.

It was confirmation of Ram's guess, and at the same time, proof that the transformation didn't erase the wounds it had sustained. Batenkaitos was drenched in blood, his face a horrific sight.

"Your shoulders?"

"I popped them back in by slamming them into a wall. Took more than one try, though... ow, ahh, that hurts."

As if testing the feel of it, Batenkaitos swung both freshly reset arms in a big circle. Watching this, Ram reflected that she should have severed the arms at the shoulder rather than merely dislocating them.

Or perhaps, crushing the tips of his limbs would have crushed his ridiculous little tricks along with them.

"This, that, everything, absolutely everything we try to do, you're already one step ahead... amazing, Sister. Could it be that you're actually the one using an Authority, just like Onii-san?"

"How insulting. This is nothing but Ram's own superior insight. Don't lump it together with Barusu's nonsensical intuition. It's unpleasant. Die."

"Ahahahaha, so harsh. But, well, I see, I see, that makes sense."

Letting his split tongue hang loose from his fang-broken mouth, Batenkaitos wore a blood-stained grin. Widening her eyes at that unsettling gesture, Ram's shoulders tensed.

If he made any strange move—no,

"Take this before you can do anything."

Before any strange move could come, Ram chose to blow away his limbs with a blade of wind.

There were ways to stop bleeding. There was no way to stop pain, but as long as he didn't die, the interrogation could continue. Judging as much, she drove in an unrestrained wind blade.

But—

"—The bet's ours, we win!!"

Taking the wind blade full-on, a plump, bearded man went flying backward. However thick his skin was, the wind blade—one that should have severed not just limbs but even a thick torso—was deflected off the fat man's skin, leaving nothing but a reddish mark.

"Sister, aren't you making a bit of a mistake here. —Just so you know, you're not the only one observing your opponent, y'know?"

That voice called out to Ram just as she was about to move forward, determined not to let the huge man escape.

Immediately after, Ram's neck was shallowly cut by a blade left behind as a parting gift, and her advance was delayed by half a step. Into that half-step gap, Batenkaitos wedged himself.

"From our approach, straight into our big hit!!"

At the speed of a blink, the old man's form vanished, and the oppressive presence that appeared behind her grew stronger still. Without even a moment to turn around, a ferocious fist slammed into Ram's side, sending her slender frame flying.

Coughing out a "gah" as she was sent flying, she saw that the one who'd punched her was a burly, roughly built man with a wild air about him—switching between three distinct forms in an instant, a combination attack fully exploiting each one's traits—

"Even so, don't think a technique you've already shown me once will work again—"

"I don't. I don't think so. I don't think that. Of course I don't. Because I don't. Precisely because I don't. Because I can say I don't!"

The howling form of Batenkaitos shifted back into the old man once more, and with his own hand, he covered one of his eyes.

Feeling a chill of dread at that motion, Ram stretched out her long legs, kicking off the wall on reflex, trying to position herself to spring at him quickly.

But she wasn't in time.

"We know. —Rem's sister cannot keep moving like that without some little trick of her own."

"—"

It wasn't Ram's movements, but Rem's knowledge that had drawn out that answer.

Seeing Ram move beyond the limits of a "Hornless" Oni, Batenkaitos had confirmed his guess, and by the time Ram returned to the staircase steps, he'd already completed his act of malice.

Namely—

"—Sister, it's not like we ever wanted a contest of strength with you in the first place, you know."

So saying, that crafty, aged smile melted away, dissolving into the space around it.

Instantaneous movement through short-range spatial distortion—chain enough of those together, and escaping the battlefield was easy. She hadn't honestly expected him to keep fighting her fair and square until he died, anyway.

"That was a quick decision to run. No, more than that—"

If he knew about Ram's "Clairvoyance," he shouldn't think he could just get away.

That he'd put distance between them despite knowing that meant he'd realized the time limit placed on Ram. And, frustratingly, Ram had indeed had no small amount of time stolen from her.

The burden on Subaru grew with every passing second.

As quickly as possible, she needed to find where Batenkaitos, having fled back into the tower, had gone.

Just as she thought that, she suddenly realized something.

That Sin Archbishop, a lump of pure malice, wouldn't have retreated into the tower simply to run away.

He knew exactly what method would torment Ram, his opponent, the most.

And that was—

"—Rem."

At last, the wicked one's line of sight, caught once more, overlapped with Ram's own vision.

Reflected there was the tail of a black Earth Dragon, running while carrying the "Sleeping Beauty."
