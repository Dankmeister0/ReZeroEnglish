Arc 10 Chapter 13: "Cannot Be Stained with Blood"

—In the noble quarter of the royal capital of Lugunica, the mansion belonging to Miklotov McMahon, a member of the Council of Wise Men, wore a subdued façade that reflected the modest, upright character for which its master was known.

Most nobles favored balls and banquets, hosting such affairs again and again to flaunt their power, but Miklotov had never chosen to behave as a noble was expected to. Having distinguished himself as a civil official of the kingdom from his youth, the wise elder—prized for his ability—had never taken a wife, as if to demonstrate his resolve to devote his entire life to the kingdom, working himself to the bone in its service.

For that reason, the McMahon estate in the noble quarter had never once been ruled by eye-catching splendor. —That is, until this very day.

A scene of carnage in which countless lives had been cruelly cut short, scattered corpses trailing the scent of blood and death—it was this that had brought Miklotov McMahon's own life to its end as well.

Aghast, faced with a sight he could not believe, Natsuki Subaru found himself unable to move.

"——"

He had been invited here, yet in the McMahon estate he had not encountered a single living soul thus far. Thirteen dead—likely the mansion's guards and servants—each cut down and killed with a single stroke; having borne witness to them all, he had finally arrived at this innermost room.

Judging by the furnished desk and bookshelves, this was likely Miklotov's study.

A storied place where the wise elder must have spent his days fretting over the kingdom's future and racking his brain over one problem after another—against the far wall of that room, the very Miklotov in question now lay slumped.

With his legs splayed out and his back against the wall, he looked for all the world like a child who had worn himself out playing and dropped off to sleep—and that description was not far from the truth. —The wise elder had fallen asleep.

Covered by the flag of Lugunica, cut down from its pole, in a sleep from which he would never wake again.

And beside the elder who had breathed his last—

"Crusch... san?"

Miklotov had collapsed, the lids of his deeply wrinkled face closed. With the body still at her feet, the figure that turned to look back at him over one shoulder made Subaru's voice tremble pathetically.

Standing there was a beautiful woman clad in a deep navy garment reminiscent of a military uniform, with long green hair and amber eyes—her left eye covered by a large eyepatch. Crusch Karsten. Standing with unhurried composure, she was someone Subaru knew well.

"——"

At Subaru's halting call, Crusch narrowed her single amber eye. She adjusted her grip on the slender blade in her hand and, her gaze still sharp, parted her thin lips.

"A wind of fear is blowing, Natsuki Subaru. —What is it you fear?"

"Fe-fear...? Me, afraid? That's..."

"To begin with, what did you come here to do? Do you mean to say you and Miklotov McMahon shared some personal connection?"

"That's... he called for me himself. There was something I wanted to ask him too..."

"On this very day? I find that hard to credit. What is it you wished to ask him? Something concerning the Royal Selection, or some other secret dealing? It's not that I've struck upon the truth of it, yet your agitation only builds by the moment. Let me ask you once more, then. —Are you the genuine article I know?"

"—!"

Overwhelmed by Crusch's interrogation, which came crashing down on him like a raging tide, Subaru couldn't find the words to answer properly.

With the scene before him still more than he could process, Crusch's sharp-tongued thrusts bore down on him like an actual blade.

Still merciless, Crusch's gaze seemed poised to cut Subaru's chest wide open—but—

"—That's quite enough, I suppose."

"Yeah, this isn't much fun to watch."

Unable to bear watching the exchange between Subaru and Crusch any longer, two people cut in from the side: Beatrice, who stood here holding Subaru's hand, and Tiga, who had come along with them.

Beatrice did it with the feel of her small hand; Tiga did it by stepping diagonally forward to block Crusch's line of sight—both, in their own way, snapped Subaru out of the daze that had been swallowing him whole.

Thanks to them he remembered how to breathe, and the cold sweat that broke out belatedly cooled the heat in him.

In the meantime, Tiga, a crescent-bladed curved sword in hand, squared off against Crusch. He tugged down the brim of his hat, hiding his eyes, and called out, "Hey."

"You'd do well to realize this is the kind of situation that's easy to misread in all sorts of ways, Duchess."

"Misread?"

"That's right, misread. After all, this is a scene where this many people lost their lives, where one of the kingdom's pillars has died. And there you are, standing with a sword in your hand. Anyone would think it. —That it was Duchess Crusch Karsten who killed everyone in this mansion."

Even without abandoning his characteristic phrasing, it was a question asked in dead earnest. And it was the very fact that Subaru, too, wanted more than anything else to confirm.

"——"

Tiga was right.

In this massacre that had unfolded at the McMahon estate, the prime suspect for slaughtering everyone, master Miklotov included, was unmistakably Crusch.

But Subaru, who knew Crusch's character, wanted to deny that suspicion outright.

Crusch was a woman of noble spirit, a person of remarkable caliber capable of acting on correct judgment.

Even after losing her memories, when she'd had to rebuild her dignified, noble bearing from scratch, the goodness left at her core and the decency of her as a person were things even Gluttony had never managed to devour completely.

For a woman like that to have stained her hands with an atrocity like this—it was impossible. It couldn't be.

That was why Subaru wanted to hear a clear denial from her own mouth.

He wanted her to say that she, too, had sensed something wrong at the McMahon estate just as they had, had rushed here, and had simply arrived too late. That, carrying regret and fury in her heart, she would help track down whoever had killed Miklotov and the others, and together make them pay for their crime.

The words of denial, for that very purpose, from her lips—

"It seems you're a man given to the most bald-faced pretense."

Heedless of Subaru's prayer, Crusch, her expression unchanging, fixed her gaze on Tiga and declared it flatly. In response, Tiga gave a light shrug.

"A harsh assessment, but I'll accept it without complaint. So then, how about it?"

"——"

"Was it you who killed Lord Miklotov McMahon? —Duchess Crusch Karsten."

"—Yes."

To Tiga's question, this time asked plainly once more, there came a short reply.

It confirmed the suspicion and betrayed Subaru's prayer-like hopes—the worst possible answer. Unbidden, a rasping sound escaped Subaru's throat, and that short reply echoed again and again inside his skull.

As if to push Subaru's violent shock further still, Crusch drew her chin in slightly.

"Everyone connected with this house, and Miklotov McMahon himself—I cut them down with my own hand. These people were traitors, enemies of the kingdom."

"—That's you, Duchess, not them!"

"Wa—!"

In that instant, Tiga roared and closed the distance in a single bound, swinging his curved sword—a shamshir—in a graceful strike at Crusch.

It was an unhesitating opening blow that gave no room even to say "Wait," but Crusch swept it aside with an upward arc of the blade in her hand, and the crisp ring of steel against steel rang through the study.

That single note became the trigger, and between the two of them, the shriek of steel and bursts of sparks began to erupt.

"——"

A spark-scattering sword fight had begun, and the two swordsmen showed off their respective skills against the backdrop of the spacious study.

What Crusch wielded was swordsmanship as mastered by one who had earnestly devoted herself to the blade—whether in her fight against the White Whale before losing her memories, or in the battle at Priestella afterward, the straightness of the sword-lines she drew never changed. Sharp flashes of her blade swept freely through the space in every direction, ricocheting back and forth.

Tiga's swordsmanship, by contrast, was something altogether unique—body low, spinning like a top.

Starting with Crusch, whom he now faced, the swordsmen Subaru knew who excelled with a blade—Julius, Wilhelm, Cecilus, and the like—setting aside Reinhard, whose hand-strikes and kicks left a stronger impression than his swordwork, were all swordsmen who had honed what could be called an orthodox art.

If their swordsmanship could be called the righteous path, then Tiga's belonged to what would be branded the deviant path—rather than cutting an opponent down head-on, it targeted fingertips and toes without mercy, mixed feints with real attacks, and prized catching the enemy off guard. It was the art of one who craved victory, and craved it greedily—

"Sei—yah!"

The strike unleashed, gripping the shamshir in a reverse hold, traced an unusual arc as its flashing edge closed on his opponent. When Crusch arched her body back to dodge it, what came next was Tiga's long leg, whipping like a lash.

Kicks woven in between sword strikes functioned as a single unbroken sequence of movement, almost like a dance, generating an unceasing wind of blades that assailed the Crusch standing opposite him.

Against that top-spinning, high-speed barrage, Crusch, her expression never changing, kept her distance with quick, precise footwork, matching it stroke for stroke as she aimed to seize control of the dizzying exchange.

Perhaps there was a steel plate fitted into his boots, for even against Crusch's blade meeting his kicks, his foot was never taken off—and against Crusch, who embodied the very essence of the orthodox school, the deviant swordsman showed a style that refused to yield so much as a step.

"You're skilled. Does the Divine Dragon Church teach swordwork of that sort?"

"Sadly, it's self-taught. My hands and feet have had bad habits since way back!"

"A gallant wind. That much isn't a lie."

Dodging Tiga's kick by a hair's breadth, Crusch drove in a vertical downward cut. Tiga caught it on his reverse-gripped curved sword, and as the two locked blades in a hilt-to-hilt struggle, they traded those words.

It was a give-and-take contest; both being fighters of exceptional skill, the advantage tilted to neither side—but—

"—Subaru! Pull yourself together, I suppose!"

"—Ah."

"It's only natural you're in shock, I suppose. But you can't just stand there dumbstruck, I suppose!"

So saying, Beatrice hopped up and slapped his cheek, and Subaru's eyes went wide.

At the snapping impact, Subaru realized once again that his thoughts had stopped, that he'd been staring, transfixed, at the fight between Tiga and Crusch. —He owed Beatrice a deep debt of thanks.

He was in shock, certainly. If he looked back on the situation calmly, there was no way he could possibly keep his composure. So right now, before turning to face things calmly, there was something he needed to do.

"My friend."

Looking over, Subaru could tell that Tiga—catching the exchange between him and Beatrice at the edge of his vision even as he locked blades against Crusch—had murmured those quiet words.

The call had no object attached to it, but Subaru grasped Tiga's intent all the same. What he was asking for wasn't help or backup in the fight—

"—Go outside."

They had to get word of this situation out and put a stop to Crusch's outrage.

Fortunately, the royal capital right now had plenty of forces Subaru could rely on. Garfiel and Wilhelm were here, and so was Reinhard.

This was the noble quarter, after all—surely knights and guards were posted at mansions other than this one. They could call on such people and subdue Crusch.

And then—

"——"

—And then, would they punish Crusch? As a traitor to the nation, for killing Miklotov?

"You'll stay right where you are. I haven't finished appraising you yet."

The instant an inescapable hesitation flickered through a corner of his mind, a gust of blade-wind tore past just above Subaru's head.

"Whoa!?" Subaru ducked his head on reflex. The blade-wind, grazing the tips of his standing-on-end hair before passing on behind him, struck the upper part of the room directly behind him—shattering the ceiling and walls around the entrance with its cutting force, and the resulting collapse buried the room's entrance entirely.

"The way out...!"

The instant he understood it had been sealed off, Subaru was forced, whether he liked it or not, to recognize that his options had been whittled down, leaving him in a state with far too few cards left to play.

"—!"

With the escape route blocked, the option of leaving this place was simply gone.

In the middle of this firestorm, there was no time to fumble around clearing rubble to secure a way back. Minya, his Shadow magic, was poor in raw physical firepower and couldn't be counted on to blast the entrance open all at once.

In other words, the only choices left to Subaru and the others were to watch from the sidelines or to intervene—and standing by and watching was, in this situation, an option that was absolutely out of the question.

But to intervene meant—

"—Fighting? Crusch-san?"

He couldn't make sense of it.
He couldn't grasp the reality of it.
He couldn't accept what his own eyes had seen.

Well, of course he couldn't. Standing there was Crusch Karsten. Subaru respected her. Even as opposing camps in the Royal Selection, her virtue was something that deserved to be acknowledged as a matter of course. Crusch was noble and upright, a person of outstanding character who never once slackened in her effort and self-cultivation as a ruler—and, in the sense of what it meant to have the bearing of royalty, she was surely the first person who had ever captivated Subaru with it. When they defeated the White Whale, and his achievement was acknowledged from her own mouth, no one could deny—and he wouldn't let anyone deny—how much that had meant in the life of Natsuki Subaru.

He'd chosen to prioritize his love for Emilia. But even so, at that time, Subaru had genuinely thought it: that Crusch Karsten was, without a doubt, one of the people worthy of becoming ruler of the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica.

"—Please, stop this, Crusch-san!"

And so, what Subaru found himself able to choose there could only be called the most spineless card in his hand.

"——"

Crusch, still exchanging fierce blows, did not respond to Subaru's cracked plea. Her gaze remained fixed straight ahead, on Tiga, who stood opposing her.

But believing she couldn't ignore him entirely, Subaru raised his voice.

"Put your sword down! Please! I don't know what I'm supposed to do! I don't know, but I'll talk too! I'll try to talk this through so it works out somehow!"

Without realizing it, the hand not holding Beatrice's had been clenched tight, tight around the black orb hanging at his neck. —With Al, so many of the words that had needed to be exchanged had gone unexchanged; there had been nothing to do but forcibly seal away his own will instead.

Priscilla's death, the very thing that had driven Al into that atrocity, had carved into Subaru the absolute, irrecoverable weight of a loss that could never be undone.

To lose someone he knew without even being able to talk to them first—for Natsuki Subaru, right now, that was a thing far more terrifying than his own death.

"I don't want it to end like this! I don't want to... to be torn apart from the people I care about without understanding anything, not like this!"

"Subaru..."

"Please! I'm begging you! Please, I'm asking you... please... just stop."

Subaru couldn't see just how anguished his own face looked as he wrung the words from his throat, but he could see Beatrice, her eyes wavering as she stared at his profile, bite down on her lip.

Pathetic. Shameful. To be the partner of the Great Spirit Beatrice and yet be unable to put her wise, formidable power to any use at all—to be capable of nothing more than screaming like this.

"Please..."

Even knowing his own powerlessness full well, Subaru couldn't choose otherwise. If he couldn't flee, then fight Crusch—he couldn't bring himself to run to a conclusion that clear-cut.

There had been times before when his relationship with someone he'd once met changed drastically afterward.

But most of the time, that meant a negative impression turning into a positive one.

Cases running the opposite direction existed for no one but Todd and Al. —To add Crusch to that list was simply, utterly unbearable.

"——"

Subaru's clumsy, halting plea was thin enough that it might well be drowned out by the intensifying clash of blades—thin enough, even, that it seemed as if it might be dismissed as lightly as Subaru's own presence in this fight, and never reach Crusch's ears at all.

And yet—

"—There seems to be no lie in your words."

"—!"

Hearing that murmur, spoken as if in understanding, Subaru's eyes flew open.

With Tiga positioned squarely between them, Subaru's gaze crossed with Crusch's, looking his way past the deviant swordsman.

Facing that amber eye, Subaru felt a flicker of hope that his words had reached her—

"Very well then, I'll set the matter of you aside for the moment."

—In that instant, with the words still on her lips, a violent wind swept through the room, centered on Crusch.

"Wh—"

The instant his whole body felt the pressure, his feet lifted off the ground, and Subaru grasped instinctively that it was the wind Crusch had stirred up—the result of her signature technique, One Slash, a Hundred Men, unleashed in every direction at once without any set aim.

The formidable wind-blades that should ordinarily have been loosed together in a single direction—unbundled instead and spread out on all sides—generated a blast of wind pressure that shook the already-battered study without mercy, sending thick books, bundled documents, and even the corpse of the dead elder himself flying into the air.

But that was as far as Subaru's grasp of the situation went.

"Guh—!"

His body, lifted defenseless off the ground, slammed into the ceiling with furious force, and the breath was knocked out of him. He'd yanked his arms in on reflex and pulled Beatrice into his chest to protect her, but that was all he managed.

Still holding tight to Beatrice as she screamed, "Subaru!", Subaru's body rode the wind, was slammed into the wall, and—unable even to break his fall—was thrown down onto the floor in an unsightly heap.

"—Ngh."

Pressed down by an aching pain that spared not one inch of him, as the last breath spilled out from his lungs, he felt the back of his skull go cold in a rushing sensation, and instinct screamed that this was bad.

It wasn't Death. But his consciousness was on the verge of going out. It was the price for prioritizing Beatrice's safety so much that he'd neglected his own. As if a hole had opened in the head he'd struck, his consciousness was draining out through it. —Bad, bad, this is bad.

"No... good..."

If he lost consciousness now, who would there be to throw words at Crusch?

Tiga didn't know. Didn't know Crusch's character, didn't know she truly possessed a good and kind heart. He was misunderstanding her. He had to be stopped. Crusch was a good person. Talk to her, and she'd understand.

Talk to her, and she would understand—that was all there was to it.

"...She'll... understand..."

So please, please don't shut your ears to my words. —Praying that, right up to the very last moment of his fading consciousness, Subaru's mind sank with a soft splash into the dark.

Sinking—

"—Yes."

"——"

The instant consciousness abruptly returned, Subaru nearly lost his sense of balance.

His brain couldn't process the gap between who he'd been a moment before and who he now instantaneously was, and that confusion acted on his limbs, throwing him into panic.

His feet were on the ground. Obvious enough—he was standing. Standing. Of course he was. Was that really so, though? Had standing been his default state? It hadn't, which was exactly why he was, right now, experiencing the sensation of heaven and earth flipping upside down—

"No."

What Subaru needed to focus on right now wasn't the world turned upside down. It was the cause—why it had felt that way in the first place.

And that cause needed no explaining. —He had traveled back through time.

"——"

His knees threatening to give out, Subaru's field of view held the McMahon estate's study before it had been violently ravaged, and Miklotov's corpse cradled in the diagonally cleft flag. Before that corpse stood a green-haired woman turning back over one shoulder, and the back of a young man standing half a step ahead of Subaru.

It was the confrontation between Crusch and Tiga. And this was the exchange that had taken place—

"Everyone connected with this house, and Miklotov McMahon himself—I cut them down with my own hand. These people were traitors, enemies of the kingdom."

It was the exact moment of that irreversible, fatal confirmation of the question.

"—That's you, Duchess, not them!"

Before Subaru, his blood frozen with shock, Tiga let out a piercing battle cry and dove in.

Tiga's sword-dance began, his shamshir held in a reverse grip, and Crusch met it head-on—a clash of blades between two swordsmen, one walking the deviant path, the other the orthodox.

Witnessing, once again, the fight raging through the study amid chains of ringing steel and scattering sparks, Subaru nearly grew dizzy for a reason quite separate from the loss of balance he'd endured a moment before.

"No, that can't be..."

He clapped a hand to his mouth, and a stifled voice leaked out. Like blood seeping unstoppably from a wound, shock pounded again and again against the inside of Subaru's skull.

—Return by Death. He'd Returned by Death.

Just before his consciousness had cut out, it was the wind Crusch had raised that had blown Subaru away, slamming him into wall and ceiling, then down onto the floor, and knocking him out. —The fact that Subaru had now returned to this very moment, without ever waking up, meant he had Returned by Death without regaining consciousness first.

If Subaru, unconscious in that situation, had had his life taken from him, then the one whose hand had done it was—

"—Subaru! Pull yourself together, I suppose!"

"—!"

"It's only natural you're in shock, I suppose. But you can't just stand there dumbstruck, I suppose!"

Beatrice pulled Subaru back to his senses with the same rallying cry as before, though he'd frozen up this time for a different reason. Struck on the cheek by her small palm, the pain and heat of it brought him back to himself.

"Sorry, Beatrice."

Rubbing the back of his hand over his struck cheek, Subaru clenched his teeth hard enough to grind.

Even with a death sandwiched in between, Subaru's confusion and turmoil over this whole affair hadn't faded one bit. And yet, screaming that what had happened was a lie, refusing to accept it, would change nothing about the outcome.

Without ever mustering the courage to intervene in the fight between Crusch and Tiga, screaming unsightly, pathetic complaints the whole while, Subaru would die. —His life would end up plucked away by Crusch's own hand.

"No good."

That could not be allowed to happen.

Crusch Karsten must not be allowed to take Natsuki Subaru's life. He couldn't let his own weakness be the thing that dragged down her nobility.

Crusch's hands must never be stained with Subaru's blood. That could not be allowed to happen.

"Beako, lend me a hand."

"—! You needn't even ask, I suppose!"

Between the shock and the panic, the puzzle pieces of his reason remained scattered in pieces.

But from among that scattered puzzle, he seized on only the pieces whose shapes he could still make out clearly, and Subaru squeezed back hard on the hand of the partner in whom he felt nothing but reassurance.

The exchange still going on before his eyes, the fight between Crusch and Tiga, was a matched contest of skill, presenting exactly the sort of give-and-take that deserved the name.

Knowing Crusch's true strength, Subaru wanted to marvel at Tiga's own skill in matching her blade for blade—but as things stood, Crusch was the one who held the decisive finishing blow, One Slash, a Hundred Men, and that was a clear disadvantage.

Whether or not Tiga knew it, he'd chosen tactics that closed the distance, and thanks to that he was giving Crusch no time to gather her wind. But even amid the clash of blades, Subaru knew firsthand that Crusch was still building up the wind that would overturn the whole balance of the fight.

And so—

"Don't rely on magic, my friend!"

"I couldn't use it in the first place anyway, my friend!"

Whether that answering call was reassuring or the opposite was hard to say, but Subaru pulled his arm up and drew Beatrice into his chest. And so the two of them, close enough that their cheeks nearly touched, fixed their eyes firmly on the same battle—

"—E.M.T.!!"

Their incantations overlapped, and at that same moment, what spread through the study was an invisible field centered on the two of them—E.M.T., an original technique that could be called anti-magic magic: it unraveled constructed mana formulas, neutralizing them and stripping them of their power.

Within this field, casting magic was out of the question—even mana-fueled Meteors and Magicreation Tools would malfunction and turn to mush. Or so it seemed, though according to Beatrice, if someone managed to adapt to that mushy state and weave a formula anyway, things would get more complicated.

"It's plenty enough to make an opening to slap Roswaal upside the face when he's startled, I suppose!"

"Not to mention, seeing it for the first time... you'd have no idea what's even going on, right!"

The effect of the deployed field engulfed not only Subaru and Beatrice but the two locked in combat as well, dragging them under its influence.

True to his own claim, E.M.T. had no effect on Tiga's tactics, since he didn't use magic. But for Crusch, who had been weaving wind for her trump card, it was a different story.

Crusch, who seemed to have braced herself at Subaru and Beatrice's incantation, wondering what was coming, flicked her single eye toward them at the effect the E.M.T. field's influence had on her. —Even before he'd Returned by Death, Subaru had crossed gazes with Crusch just like this.

That time, his hope had been betrayed, and Subaru had lost his life without ceremony—but—

"Surrender, Crusch-san! I've cut off your path to victory! You can't use One Slash, a Hundred Men anymore! I won't let you! This is as far as it goes!"

Painting over a voice on the verge of trembling with pure bluff, Subaru put on a display of unbending, iron will.

He'd learned by now that no reality could be changed by a weak-kneed, near-tearful voice, by anguished pleading. What was needed here, now, was a fierce word-spirit strong enough to convey his will to his opponent.

A word-spirit had to be filled with soul. —Deceive your opponent, deceive yourself, from the very bottom of your heart.

Make her believe that fighting Natsuki Subaru simply wasn't worth the cost.

"——"

Subaru's black eyes and Crusch's single amber one measured each other's light head-on. In the meantime, blade struck blade once, hard, and the sword fight between Crusch and Tiga reached a pause.

Tiga, curved sword still in hand, standing at an angle, kept his position shielding Subaru and Beatrice from behind.

"That's the warning my friend's giving you. And if I might add one thing more—I'd guess that friend was a friend of yours too, once. ...Why not lend him an ear?"

"Tiga..."

"To put it plainly, I don't have the strength to strip your sword away and put you down single-handedly. Trying to restrain you would take a willingness to let my own flesh get cut, and at worst, I wouldn't come out of it alive. That's not something I want, and it's not something these two behind me want either."

Even as he said this, Tiga's eyes never let up their watch, tracking Crusch's every move without a shred of carelessness.

Perhaps, from his perspective, he'd gone along with Subaru mostly on the chance that even a single crack in Crusch's resolve would be worth the gamble. Even so, what Tiga had conveyed had unmistakably chewed Subaru's own intent into something easier to swallow, and let it sink in.

There was no way Tiga could know what had passed between Subaru and Crusch.

If anything, if he knew anything about the Royal Selection, he ought to have assumed on principle that they belonged to rival camps. But Tiga had inferred, from Subaru's brief words and actions here, that his relationship with Crusch had never been a simple matter of rivalry.

Subaru rode on Tiga's quick understanding. —He would see Crusch restrained.

Restrain her, and then think from there. Everything, from that point on, he'd figure out how to make work.

"I—"

"—Minya."

With Subaru in a state of near-prayer, right as Crusch began to open her mouth, Beatrice, still held in Subaru's arms, raised a hand, and a violet-indigo radiance bored into the ground at Crusch's feet.

A single violet arrow drove into the floor near her toes, crystallizing the study's floor and cracking it apart.

"You'd best refrain from any careless moves, I suppose. Betty and Subaru are the sole exception who can still use magic within E.M.T., I suppose. Can you really keep up your act against that flippant man there while handling us on top of it, I wonder?"

"That "flippant man" remark stung a little, but so you two can still use your own magic. That's cheating, isn't it."

"That's the whole concept behind it, yeah. But that's exactly how it is."

Beatrice's warning shot, driving home their advantage, seized the initiative, and Subaru could see Crusch's expression stiffen ever so slightly. It was the reaction of a woman who, up until now, hadn't counted them among the threats present, but who had now clearly and firmly added them to her list of things to watch.

There was a pain in being regarded that way. But if they'd made themselves seem a genuine threat, that was a stroke of good fortune. If the odds turned against her, surely she too would be capable of the right judgment—

"I see."

So saying, Crusch swung her sword in a seemingly casual motion, tracing an arc through empty air where no one stood.

It was an unexpected move, but it was likely meant to confirm the effect of E.M.T. The blade-wind that should have arisen didn't, and she nodded to herself at the failure of One Slash, a Hundred Men to form.

And then—

"—Natsuki Subaru, what is it, exactly, that you fear so much?"

Once again, a question came flying from Crusch.

But this time was different from the first. Without letting himself be swallowed up by confusion, he clenched his teeth and endured it.

"The outcome of this fight? Your own safety, or that of those beside you? The kingdom's future? Or perhaps"

"——"

"—Or perhaps, my sanity?"

It was a question spoken slowly, deliberately, as if chewing over each word.

Even against that question, Subaru used every muscle in his face to hold his expression steady. He built, with everything he had, a face that let none of his own faintheartedness or hesitation show through to her.

What did Crusch see in that face of Subaru's, made with his whole soul poured into it?

Only briefly did Crusch part her thin lips,

"I see."

was all she murmured.

"Duchess, we've made our demand clear. How will you answer it?"

As if deliberately ignoring the wordless storm of emotion that had risen for an instant between Subaru and Crusch, Tiga asked her again, his voice hard.

The tension packed into his voice made it plain that this was an ultimatum. As the killing intent in the blade rose again, pressing in wave after wave, Crusch, exposed to it, looked at Tiga.

"I understand your demand. Under these circumstances, with my wind sealed off, I am the one at a disadvantage. And so I should surrender—that is your claim, is it not."

"—. That's right. From here on, Beako and I will join the fight too. Your odds of winning are..."

"—To declare that there are none would be premature, Natsuki Subaru."

Speaking with cool composure, as if crushing Subaru's bluff underfoot, Crusch declared this and slowly raised her left hand, the one not holding her sword. —Those slender fingers, wrapped in a white glove, came to rest on the large eyepatch covering her left eye.

The eyepatch that concealed so much of the left side of her face was, by her own account, the mark of an effect of the Dragon's Blood, one being healed by the power of the sacred rite Filore had applied, yet still not fully cured. Watching those slender fingers hook into it and strip it away, almost tearing it off, Subaru's eyes went wide.

Because—

"It isn't a sight I care to show for very long. —I am a woman too, after all."

Having said that, Crusch's left eye, exposed now with the patch removed, unlike her natural amber, gleamed a shimmering gold. A pupil narrow as a reptile's, and beneath it, lines of light running like capillaries—seeing them, a single word shot through Subaru's mind like a bolt of lightning.

"—Ah."

Whether it was correct, he had no way of knowing.

He simply thought it, looking at Crusch's inhuman, golden eye.

"——"

—That Crusch Karsten's left eye had become the Eye of the Dragon.
