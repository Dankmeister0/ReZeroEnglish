Arc 8 Chapter 46: "The Taste of Wind"

—Reflected in the blue-gleaming orb was a landscape of blazing flame that looked as though it could be the end of the world.

Before the eyes of Priscilla, chained and suspended in the underground cell, Sphinx—her skin the pallid blue-white of the dead—was putting on display the ferocious battle that had begun in one corner of the imperial capital.
On one side, someone who had taken the Empire's very core into herself; on the other, a furious clash against an overwhelming presence in which not a single step was yielded. Combine these two unthinkable circumstances, and the resulting reality was nothing short of a passage torn from myth.
The kind of scene that made one think this was exactly how the songs and tales handed down since ancient times had first been born.

"—But that's only if there's someone left to pass down what they've seen and heard."

"Do not intrude upon another's sentiments—least of all mine. When tactlessness reaches its extreme, even the effort of anger becomes tiresome."

Watching the same scene in the orb, Priscilla answered the one who had spoken in a voice thin with heat.
Thin. Yes, a voice thin with heat. Not lacking heat—thin. Sensing this, Priscilla, her finely shaped brows knitting, looked at her counterpart through the orb.

The Undead across from her—Sphinx, who had named herself the mastermind of the "Great Disaster"—had gone out of her way to come before Priscilla, to display for her the tragic present state of Arakiya, a woman deeply connected to Priscilla.
What Sphinx had whispered into Arakiya's ear during the time the captive Priscilla had been sealed away, there was no way to know. But the result of it was projected in the orb, and its purpose lay right before her eyes.

Arakiya, having taken into herself something far too vast, was on the very verge of bursting, and Sphinx was watching Priscilla as she witnessed Arakiya's suffering.
In those eyes of black sunk into gold there was a curiosity that could not be concealed, and a color of expectation.

Sphinx was hoping—hoping that Arakiya's tragedy would shake Priscilla's heart.
And that was, without question—

"—an obsession with me."

Meeting the eyes of Priscilla, who had spoken those words, Sphinx let her lips curve into a smile without saying a word.
It was an affirmation, an expression of delight. Including the very trigger that had brought about the "Great Disaster" in the first place, there was no doubting Sphinx's obsession with Priscilla.
Its origin remained unknown, but every act driven by that obsession was one worthy only of contempt.

"To have something given to you taken away—I have learned from direct experience that nothing frays the emotions quite so much as that. What about you? Confirmation required."

"How pleased you are, now that things have gone according to your wishes."

"Yes. Confirmed. Just as you say, I feel a sense of elation. I had considered it only natural for things to proceed according to a plan laid in advance...but perhaps my past self should have known what it was to feel accomplishment. Had she, the outcome of the 'Demi-Human War' might have been different."

"—"

"—But then, I would be able neither to be given anything, nor to gain anything."

Sphinx, who had been peering at Priscilla and speaking with such vigor, placed a hand over her own chest and bowed her head as though savoring something bitter.
In that, Priscilla perceived an emotion turned toward herself, one that was neither delight nor anger, and she understood.
That posture, one that spoke of sorrow—that was what had driven Sphinx to the "Great Disaster."

"—"

Setting aside what lay within Sphinx, Priscilla turned her attention back to the sight in the orb.
There, unchanged, on a battlefield where a fight that seemed as though it might alter the very shape of the world was still being waged, what clashed was a mass of power that could be called the Empire itself, and a bolt of lightning that refused to be overwhelmed.
But within it, Priscilla did not overlook something so faint it seemed on the verge of vanishing altogether.

"Weak, fragile, faint of heart—born innocent and possessing nothing, struggling against the notion that it could never become anything—could such a thing leave its footprint upon a passage of myth?"

"What are you—"

Sphinx's attention turned to what Priscilla, having looked away, might have seen in the orb. But she never came to confirm the true nature of what had caught Priscilla's notice.
Before she could, a tremor that reached even the underground cell erupted throughout the imperial capital.

"I'd guess that would be my brother."

"Emperor Vincent Vollachia?"

Sphinx, puzzling over what had happened, knitted her thin brows at Priscilla's murmur.
It was likely, in her estimation, not a particularly high possibility. Priscilla, in the underground cell, had no way of knowing, but just how great was the total fighting force Sphinx commanded?
That the Emperor, who had once abandoned the imperial capital, should find a way to break through that horde of Undead—

"The swordsman who ended my life is, at this very moment, locked in single combat with General Arakiya herself...Even if the Emperor of Vollachia is a man of wisdom, a checkmated board cannot be overturned. —No, wait."

Running a finger along her lips, Sphinx tried to dismiss the possibility that troubled her. But partway through her deliberation, she narrowed her black eyes.
And then—

"—A foreign element mixed in, one that thwarted Valga's scheme?"

"Oh? So something did come to mind."

Sphinx had blurted it out as though suddenly struck by the thought.
Having put the thought that had risen in her mind onto her tongue, she began to shake her head, ready to deny it at once. Priscilla, however, deliberately called a halt.
To Sphinx, who turned to look at her on reflex, Priscilla smiled voluptuously, and said,

"You're in a hurry to dismiss it as unreasonable, are you not? Then here is some 'advice' from me. —Call that which you have felt intuition."

"Intuition..."

"Think of it as your heart catching the taste of the wind. Ironic, I imagine, for one who is already dead."

With that scoff, Sphinx fell silent, weighing Priscilla's words.
That she neither laughed it off nor dismissed it out of hand was simply her nature by birth—though calling it "by birth" was itself ironic—and Sphinx continued to think, setting aside all notions of life and death.
At last, she slowly raised her eyes upward—

"I will admit it. There exists a foreign element capable of throwing my plans into disarray. —Revision required."

With Sphinx before her, having acknowledged this shift within herself, Priscilla likewise turned her gaze upward.
There was only the dark, grimy ceiling of the underground cell, faintly lit by the orb's glow, unable to conceal the weight of the history it bore.
But that merely proved the narrowness of the one looking at it that way.

Priscilla did not look to the heavens to confirm the state of the filthy ceiling.
It was so that her heart would not miss the moment it caught the taste of the wind.

—A battle that had begun abruptly, been settled in matter-of-fact fashion, and then resumed in blasphemous defiance.

Against the attacks of countless swordsmen, all bearing the same face, assailing her from every direction, Iris—after a moment's surprise—smashed them all head-on.

"How uncanny. And yet it be nothing more than a bluff."

The Dress-clad arm of Iris swept out, and the surging blue-haired swordsmen—the mass of Rowan Segmunt—had their heads, their torsos, their waists, their legs cut down, sent flying like scattered leaves. But a portion of the Rowans slipped past that opening blow and closed to a distance where their blades could reach Iris. The full-force strikes they hurled at her, Iris stopped two of them without concern using the fingers of one hand, tilted her body to dodge another, and scattered the rest with the shock of bringing down a raised leg.

Literally a single touch of the sleeve was enough to end them—no matter how many they gathered, they were no match for her.
That was the gap in power between Iris and the Rowans who had become Undead.
Only—

"Next!" "Next!" "Next!" "Next!" "Next!" "Next!" "Next!" "Next!" "Next!" "Next!" "Next!" "Next!" "Next!" "Next!" "Next!" "Next!" "Next!" "Next!" "Next!" "Next!" "Next!" "Next!" "Next!" "Next!" "Next!" "Next!" "Next!" "Next!" "—if you please."

"—"

At the sheer absurdity of foes she had scattered springing up one after another without end, her thoughts froze.
Men baring triumphant smiles, expressions that seemed to declare they had finally laid hands on their heart's desire, closed in—no, it was a single mass of identical Rowans, every one of them turning their blades on Iris.
Reflexively meeting their raging swordstrokes, deflecting every one of them—what crossed Iris's mind was self-reproach, the realization that she herself had erred.

She had meant to force them down by strength, to drive them off without taking their lives, to reduce the number of the dead by even a little.
That arrogant notion of hers had blinded her to the fact that it would shatter the hearts of those steeped in the Empire's ways into pieces, and make them suffer for it.
The result of that, the victim of it, was the state of the Rowan before her now.

"I..."

Dodging the blades swung at her, she struck down the countless faces closing in with her kiseru pipe. When her long, slender leg kicked one up from between his thighs, she seized the leg of that floating body and slammed it boldly into the Rowans around her, scattering the nearly ten of them once more.
But whether struck down or worn away, the momentum of the resurrected Rowans knew no end.

"I...!"

Struck by a single blow from Iris, Rowan's Undead body, unable to bear it, shattered.
Rowan, breaking apart into pieces like a porcelain vessel—yet the next Rowan trampled the remains underfoot in his sandals, unconcerned, and leapt in only to be shattered in turn.
To have this repeated again and again was no different from being placed in the position of an executioner without end.

By nature, there are those who feel nothing at all about destroying something shaped like a human being.
But in most cases, the act of destroying something in human form, a vessel of life, is one made possible only by great resolve or determination, or else by habituation or resignation.
Iris, if anything, could be said to stand at the very extreme of the latter.

Even now, she could not kill an enemy, however hostile, without her heart feeling the sting of it.
Precisely because of that, the power called the "Soul Marriage Technique"—one that could not be wielded without compassion for others—had manifested in Iris.
That it had pushed Iris to the summit of strength known as the Nine Divine Generals was, admittedly, due to the malicious miracle that bound her soul to the soil of the Empire.

Regardless, whether facing something as far beyond the ordinary as Rowan Segmunt, or a being that kept appearing one after another no matter how many times it was shattered, Iris's heart creaked with every one she broke.
Tormented by unbearable pain, her heart eaten away at more and more, Iris was laid bare.
Every time she plucked away a single life, every time she destroyed something with form, every time she caused the world's present shape, its truest state, to be lost—Iris, whose soul cracked a little more each time.

"I—!"

And so, where a cracked soul comes to rest at last.
It was a plane of existence unknowable—whether to Iris, who had lived on unwillingly through an endless span of time, or to Sandra Benedict, who had borne Prisca and lost her life to misfortune, or to Yorna Mishigure, who trembled at a beloved reunion centuries in the making—

"—Ah."

A hoarse breath spilled from red lips, and in the next instant, blood sprayed.
The spatter struck the cheek of the blade's wielder, and an outstretched tongue licked the blood away.
A smile spilled out—a wicked, swordsman's smile.

"—Of the Heavenly Sword—

Slipping past the outstretched hand, the bared white blade grazed the lady's shoulder.
A beat later, the spattering blood vividly stained the gray street, and Rowan renewed the finest swordstroke of his lifetime—no, since he was already Undead, not of his life, but of his existence.

"Still more, still more, still more to come, if you please."

Yet his insatiable hunger was sent coursing through his entire body from a heart that no longer beat, in place of circulating blood, evolving the being that was Rowan at an ever-accelerating pace.

Directly ahead, the beautiful fox-human he faced was frighteningly strong—an opponent difficult almost beyond belief. A gap in power so overwhelming it could not be resisted had easily shattered everything Rowan had built up.
By rights, Rowan's challenge should have ended the moment he was defeated like that. But this abnormal state of affairs, threatening the capital, the Empire, the world itself, did not let Rowan end.

He set a blade to his own throat, giving up on a life spent in idleness and worthlessness.
Believing that there was a realm reachable only by casting away his own body, and drawing the blade with the thought that if he could not reach it he would simply rot and die—it was then that Rowan's vision truly opened before him.

"Ah, ah, ahh! How ugly, how misshapen the world I'd been looking at until now, if you please!"

With his vision opened, Rowan effortlessly strode past the realm his living self had bloodshot-eyed struggled to reach.
Life, after all, was no good. Life was not a good thing. Precisely because it was the very first thing given to him at birth, even a recluse like Rowan had harbored an attachment to it.
Only by casting all of it aside entirely did Rowan gain the lightness needed to aim for the Heavenly Sword.
This, this very lack of attachment, was the path to the Heavenly Sword—

"Well now, watch closely, you fool of a son. Even you couldn't manage this, could you."

This was a plane one could finally reach only after losing one's life.
In other words, a sight that even Cecilus Segmunt, unable to overcome death, could never see.
That one could not reach it without dying—what a spiteful place the Sword God had set the Heavenly Sword.

The bet had been won. —And so, he would take his swollen winnings.

"Ha ha ha ha ha, ha ha ha ha ha ha ha!"

Rowan's sword, laughing uproariously, began to reach the heights that had once been so far away.
However much the closing Rowans swung their blades, Iris opposing them cut them down one by one from the outset. They couldn't keep up with her as she kicked off the ground, and a single blow from the woman's slender arm was enough to leave them unable to stand.
Yet that overwhelming gap had begun to close at an incredible speed.

—Let us here, once more, speak of the misfortune of the man called Rowan Segmunt.

Rowan had a cherished wish. There was something he had gone on seeking. There was a prayer he had gone on thirsting for.
But he never once encountered the opportunity, the worthy rival, that could make it come true.
That was the tragedy of Rowan Segmunt, a man who remained unfortunate until the very end of his life.

—Let us here speak of one miracle belonging to the man called Rowan Segmunt.

Rowan had a cherished wish. There was something he had gone on seeking. There was a prayer he had gone on thirsting for.
And at last, he encountered the opportunity, the worthy rival, needed to make it come true.
The obsession with the "Heavenly Sword," unlost even in death, revived Rowan Segmunt as an Undead. Shattered again and again or not, he went on producing, one after another in endless succession, identical existences of his own soul, transforming the man into a monster beyond any comparison to what he had been in life.

There are those who, facing a mighty opponent and witnessing skill far beyond their own, make a life-or-death battle their nourishment and achieve a leap of extraordinary growth.
The very same thing was about to happen to Rowan, as a kind of miracle.

An environment in which he faced a mighty opponent named Iris, witnessed skill far above his own again and again, and made nourishment of a battle in which his very life was, quite literally, shattered over and over.
Had Natsuki Subaru been present, he might have called this business of learning through death something like "death-taught lessons."
That Undead's abnormal obsession joined with an eagerness to learn, and Rowan's sword was honed ever sharper.

That, precisely—a swordsmanship of the Undead, made purely for killing, leaving the swordsmanship of his living self behind—was the technique of Rowan Segmunt, the "Undead Swordmaster," now clinging in pursuit of Iris's life.

"Ngh, ah...!"

Surrounded from front, back, left, and right—four directions at once—Iris, unable to fully deal with the swordstrokes raining down, had her arm and side torn open by a blade, and let out a small groan as blood spilled from her.
Hearing that feeble breath, Rowan shook his head from side to side, as though refusing to accept it.

He didn't want to hear it. He didn't want to hear it. He did not want to hear the weakness of the strong.
Rowan was grateful to Iris. Thanks to her, he had grown stronger. Dying was nothing more than the trigger for it. It had been a necessity for a man destined to one day reach the "Heavenly Sword." There wasn't the slightest need for Iris to trouble herself over it.
So he didn't want to hear it, because it grated on his ears unbearably, because as the inferior overtook the superior, what he wanted was not a lament but applause.
So—

"Don't you go crying now, miss. That pretty—"

The swordstroke brought down from overhead was caught by the kiseru pipe Iris raised on reflex, while her other hand punched clean through the very center of Rowan's torso.
That Rowan was finished. But no matter—another Rowan sprang out next, closing in on Iris, whose arm was now pinned, until the pierced Rowan finally shattered apart.
A kicking leg shattered that Rowan. With that, one of her arms and one of her legs were now pinned. Then a Rowan thrust in from behind; her agile tail struck it away, but blood sprayed from the base of the tail—the strike had landed. Iris's expression twisted in pain. Not good. Truly not good.

"Why don't you die and come back too, and keep me company forever, eh?"

It would be a truly delightful future—but probably one that could never come to pass.

"—"

As her eyes met the closing Rowan's, Iris was looking not at the life or death before her, but somewhere else entirely.
It was not the victory or defeat, the life or death, right in front of her—it was something altogether different, eyes that looked, one might say, upon the past.
Glory never visits one who looks not to the future but to the past, and regrets that past.
That much, at least, Rowan regretted from the bottom of his empty, hollowed-out heart—

"—Your life, if you please."

A silver light raced forward, a swordstroke renewing the finest of his existence, and it fell upon the woman's slender neck.
It should have resulted, just like that, in a decapitation vivid enough to be called art.

"Wha—"

Blocked by a sensation hard, firm, unyielding, difficult beyond measure, the sword technique meant to peck away a life came to a halt.
The blow of gratitude meant for the woman who was his formidable foe, his greatest rival, and in some sense even a kind of teacher, had been stopped, and Rowan's gold eyes, stained black, went wide in astonishment.

That the blow had been stopped was mortifying enough, but the one who had stopped it was the real problem.
It was not Iris, whom he had acknowledged as a mighty foe, a formidable enemy, a worthy rival. It was not some hidden reserve of strength she had shown while standing at death's edge—it was, precisely and simply, an interruption from the side.

"—Yorna-sama."

So a young voice called out a name belonging to no one present, cutting off Rowan's swordstroke.
The forceful evasion, a mighty blade thrown bodily into the gap between them, was still, by Rowan's estimation, something that should never have been possible.
Even if she had thrown the whole of her slender body into the gap, his strike should have carried enough force to cleave straight through her along with it. And yet—

"You've all got the same face, but everyone, out of the way!"

Next, a voice like the ringing of a bell rang out valiantly, and the sound of the air drawing taut pierced through the world.
In an instant, flowers of ice blooming across the ground swallowed the surrounding scene all at once, turning the Rowan and Rowan, and every other Rowan mixed in among them, into nourishment for those flowers.
Glancing sidelong at himself, frozen solid and unable to move, the Rowan who had leapt back stared hard, sword still raised, trying to make out what had happened.
And there—

"—At last. At last, I have been granted the chance to see you again."

In a quiet voice, yet one deep with feeling, the deer-horned girl who murmured this wrapped Iris in an embrace.
Bleeding, down on her knees, Iris was held against the small girl's slight chest, her blue eyes rounding in surprise as she received the embrace.
And then the girl who had embraced Iris turned her black eyes toward Rowan, and—

"Any villain who turns a blade upon Yorna-sama, unworthy though I am, shall have me for an opponent."

So the deer-horned girl declared, clear and resolute.
