Arc 7 Chapter 80: "Iris and the King of Thorns"

—The discussion in the great hall, chaotic as it was, finally began approaching its conclusion, at least for the time being.

"To put it plainly, the situation has diverged greatly from what we initially anticipated. That said, avoiding the fall of the fortified city and securing the cooperation of General Yorna can certainly be called solid achievements."

It was Zikr who briskly picked up the threads of the scattered discussion and set about drawing it to a close. With his fluffy mop of hair, he swept his round eyes across everyone's faces in the hall. At his words, Emilia nodded. "Yes, that's right."

"There were some sad, disheartening moments, but the fact that everyone's gathered here now is the result of all our hard work. Let's rely on that, and this time, let's make everyone's goals come true."

"Splendid — thank you for such a beautiful answer, Lady Emilie. As you say, let us aim for the fulfillment of all our goals. To that end, however, there is one more matter I should report."

"One more matter, at the very end? ...If it's a continuation of what you were saying earlier, I don't get a very good feeling about it."

Zikr dipped his chin at Emilia's words, and at the sight of the single finger he raised, Otto muttered with a twitching cheek.

As if to reinforce Otto's bad feeling, Zikr continued, "Indeed,"

"This is a continuation of what I mentioned before. It concerns Lady Natsumi as well — the one being treated as the black-haired crown prince."

"Natsumi...? Huh? But isn't it Subaru who's being treated as the emperor's child...?"

"Emilie, do we not already know what name Subaru-sama has been going by in the Empire?"

"Oh, right. That's true. Sorry, I got all tangled up in my head."

Realizing she'd interrupted the flow of the conversation, Emilia put a hand to her mouth.

The false name Subaru had been using in the Empire, "Natsumi Schwarz," was a device of his — a way to hide his true identity within the Empire while still leaving a trail for Emilia and the others to find him by, should they come looking.

To that end, Subaru had deliberately used the name of someone Emilia and the others already knew — the name of Emilia's friend, "Natsumi Schwarz."

It was while Subaru was using that false name that Zikr had first met him.

That was surely why Zikr referred to Subaru as "Natsumi." It was that which had gotten tangled up in Emilia's head.

"Wait, hold on. But then why is he calling her Lady Natsumi...?"

"Speak, frizzy-head... I suppose. And Betty supposes it's about time for another nap, too."

"Oh, right. That's true. Sorry, I need to be thinking about Beatrice right now."

The question that had begun to surface in Emilia's mind was pushed back down by the voice of Beatrice in her arms. If it concerned Subaru, then of course Beatrice would want to hear it even more than Emilia did.

For the sake of the girl who couldn't stay awake much longer, that question would have to wait.

Prompted by the gazes of Beatrice and Emilia, Zikr began, "Actually..."

"Strictly speaking, it's not about Lady Natsumi herself. Her safety is a concern, since her whereabouts remain unknown, but as Abel-dono surmised, I believe that wherever she's found, she'll no doubt be given the utmost protection. However—"

"Is there something that makes Subaru's safety uncertain?"

"As I prefaced, it isn't about Lady Natsumi herself, but a separate matter that ripples out from it... With the existence of His Imperial Majesty's child now known among the common people, there has been movement within the Empire's internal affairs. In other words—"

"—Flames have risen in other lands as well, is that it?"

It was Abel who cut in on Zikr's words, speaking in a quiet voice.

At that, Zikr bowed his head deferentially with a "Yes," while Abel, not even looking his way, ran a finger along the cheek of his demon mask, concealing his own expression.

But just before that hand finished hiding it, Emilia's eyes had certainly caught it.

—That Abel's lips had, ever so slightly, curved into a spiteful smile.

"I don't think this sounds like a fun story at all..."

"Just as the half-devil says, it's an unpleasant tale. That the fence-sitters would so conveniently jump on the bandwagon."

Priscilla nodded at Emilia, who had murmured that while looking at Abel with his hidden expression.

She shrugged the bare white shoulders she'd once declared would never be covered by so much as a coat, no matter the cold, and said,

"Given a righteous cause, and the opportunity to satisfy one's own greed, all that's left is choosing the right 'timing' to move. Act too soon, and you're forced to bleed for it yourself; act too late, and you'll show up only to go home empty-handed. Call it a season for taking one's measure."

"Rebellions springing up all over... I had heard that Emperor Vincent Vollachia's reign was stable, and that the people of the Empire enjoyed peace."

"In the end, peace is nothing more than a fleeting, bubble-like dream."

At the words of Priscilla and Otto, Abel lowered the hand he'd pressed to his mask. From lips where the last trace of the smile Emilia had glimpsed had already vanished, Abel went on in a flat tone.

"The nature of the Empire's people — no, of humanity itself — lies in strife. The flame of a fighting spirit does not go out so long as there is life. Even if one clamps a lid upon it, the heat continues to smolder within."

"And eventually, with nowhere left to escape, it explodes?"

"What terrible timing..."

At Abel's manner of speaking, Otto and Petra each voiced their own reaction.

Emilia thought she understood the gist of what Abel's convoluted phrasing was getting at. And looking at the results that had come of it, she couldn't deny his reasoning either.

Even so, she didn't like the idea that fighting was simply a given for humans.

"Somehow, that sounds really, really lonely."

"Lonely? Why would you think that, half-devil?"

"Because the Empire's emperor must have worked so hard, all this time. For it all to just be destroyed like that — it's lonely, isn't it, when your hard work goes unrewarded? Win or lose, I think it must hurt."

Emilia felt terribly sorry for the emperor of Vollachia, a man she had never even met.

There was the fact that Abel and Zikr and the others were raising a rebellion against him like this, and there was the fact that the people of the Empire were riding along on it, beginning to raise their own voices too.

Emilia had learned that the absence of any major war in the Vollachia Empire these past several years was the achievement of Emperor Vincent Vollachia. Whatever Vincent himself might think of it, at the very least, he had gone on building a world without war.

And now that was being torn down. If he lost, his country would be taken from him; and even if he won, he would have fought after all.

"It's like the moment someone gets angry, it's already decided that things are going to turn painful."

That was why Emilia couldn't help but feel so sorry for Vincent.

If it were possible, she wished they could all sit down and talk properly about just what he was thinking, and how they might reach a conclusion that was good for everyone.

"...I see. Whichever came first, it hardly matters — it makes that one's disposition fall into place, at least."

"Huh?"

"Beyond doubt, you lot are birds of a feather with Natsuki Subaru."

At that remark from Abel, arms folded, Emilia quietly held her breath.

Facing the light of the black eyes visible behind Abel's demon mask, Emilia realized — the thing she'd been about to say earlier, the impression she'd felt, hadn't been wrong after all.

Subaru and Abel were supposed to be comrades, allies who had gone on cooperating with one another right up until Emilia and the others had come charging into the Vollachia Empire like this — and yet—

"—Do you really, really dislike Subaru?"

"——"

"I'm sorry if I'm wrong. But I don't think I am."

Abel's black eyes narrowed as he took in Emilia's question, and he fell silent. Witnessing that reaction firsthand, Emilia didn't take back what she'd said.

Abel disliked Subaru. That was how it felt to Emilia.

It wasn't simply because Abel was quiet, or because he spoke with a haughtiness somewhat like Priscilla's. There was a clear difference from the way he spoke with Otto or Zikr and the others.

Emilia sensed that difference as revulsion — the revulsion Abel harbored toward Subaru.

"—Zikr. Coordinate with those who have begun to raise the voice of rebellion. Stir up a stronger wind, and stoke the flames higher. Until black smoke reaches the capital."

"Are you certain? Our original objective was the 'Nine Divine Generals'..."

"That was, in the end, only a secondary objective. What matters is establishing the shape of the battlefield. The positions of Groovy and Moguro are cause for concern, but the situation has changed."

Abel abruptly turned his gaze away from Emilia and began issuing instructions to Zikr beside him.

Emilia thought she was being ignored, but it was really Abel's way of signaling that he had no intention of continuing this particular conversation any further.

Whether his silence on the matter meant she'd been off the mark, or for some other reason, she couldn't say.

Either way—

"—The preparations to move against the capital may come together sooner than planned. Do not, under any circumstances, let your guard down."

—For the sake of reuniting with Subaru and Rem as well, parting ways with Abel and the others was simply not an option Emilia and her companions could take.

"Emilia, do be careful... I suppose. No matter how far we go, in this country, Betty supposes it's only us who can truly worry about Subaru and Subaru alone."

That was what Beatrice left her with, just before drifting off to sleep again after the discussion in the great hall.

The talk had scattered off in all directions, but narrowing the focus down to just Subaru, it was hard to call the situation the best one for him — even if the danger to his life had been reduced, if only a little.

"And Subaru's not the kind who could stay calm while everyone around him was fighting."

"Just so... I suppose."

Otto and Abel had guessed that no matter which side in the Empire caught him, his life would be guaranteed. Since it was clever people doing the thinking, that guess was probably correct.

But had Subaru ever once moved exactly the way Emilia and the others expected him to?

If the people around him were in danger, Subaru was the type to throw his own safety to the wind and act at once.

She couldn't imagine him ever accepting a situation where only he himself was safe.

And so—

"—Subaru and Rem matter the most to us, after all, so I want you to remember that we mean to make that our top priority in everything we do."

"And why go out of your way to declare that to me?"

"Well, if we just went and did whatever we wanted without telling anyone, everyone would be in trouble, wouldn't they?"

It was important to properly communicate their own goals and policy.

If she didn't say whether she'd be back in time for dinner, she might end up wasting the meal Frederica and Petra had made.

"Subaru always used to say that Ho-Ren-So was important, too. I never quite understood what horenso actually was, though..."

"And on the basis of such a hazy understanding, you dare bring such a proposal to me. That nerve of yours — is it something your persecution as a half-devil cultivated in you?"

"No, I don't think so. Back when everyone in my home forest hated me, I never once felt like I was getting used to it, or growing any stronger."

Back when she'd eked out a quiet existence in the Elior Forest, whatever anyone said to her had hurt. She would hope over the smallest things, be betrayed, and learn nothing at all no matter how many times it repeated.

So if the Emilia of today looked even a little bit stronger now—

"—it's thanks to Subaru and everyone. ...I left the forest, took part in the Royal Selection, went through so many hard things, and became who I am now. Isn't it the same for you, Priscilla?"

"Do not lump yourself together with me. I was perfect from the moment I was born."

"Is that so... Well, I won't lose to you either, then."

"Whether you can live up to such an overreaching tongue is for you to prove yourself. Whether you're even worth a glance from the corner of my eye depends on it."

Priscilla shrugged in answer to Emilia's declaration, made with fists clenched tight.

After the discussion in the great hall had settled down and Beatrice, now asleep, had been brought back to the inn, Emilia had gone looking for Priscilla to pass along the policy she'd just decided on.

She'd thought Priscilla might be busy, but fortunately, she was willing to listen.

"Now that I think about it, you always do listen to what I have to say, Priscilla."

"Oh? It seems some manner of tale unpleasant to my ears is about to begin."

"Should I stop?"

"No, I permit it. Go on."

Emilia nodded at Priscilla, who closed one eye and urged her on.

Having left the city hall, she walked alongside Priscilla down the street. With neither Schult nor Al, her attendants, anywhere in sight, Emilia glanced this way and that, searching for the two of them, as she said,

"It's been like that since the Royal Selection began. You say unpleasant things, Priscilla, and you cut people off mid-sentence, but you always do listen properly. You lend an ear, too."

"Even I cannot fathom everything in this world. How could I not take some interest in what nonsense might spill from a mouth other than my own?"

"Is that so? But I still remember Anastasia telling me, more or less, 'I have no intention of talking with you.'"

"That vixen, was it."

"Yes. Oh, but it's not that I'm holding a grudge over it, okay?"

Glancing sidelong at Priscilla as she strode along at her leisure, Emilia thought back on her exchange with Anastasia.

At first, in the setting of the Royal Selection, Anastasia had tried to shut Emilia out of the discussion entirely. That kind of treatment wasn't unusual in itself, so Emilia didn't hold it against her.

But what she really meant to say was—

"—I became friends with Anastasia."

"——"

"To be precise, we promised to become friends. We said once the Royal Selection is over, we would... No matter how things start out, I realized something like that is possible. So—"

"Don't tell me — you mean to forge a friendship with me as well?"

"Yes. Is that no good?"

With her success with Anastasia behind her, Emilia had mustered up her courage to try.

Most people, comparing the two of them, would surely find Priscilla the harder one to deal with. But Emilia was different.

For Emilia, whose experience with human relationships was so limited, her assessment of Anastasia and Priscilla came out about the same.

Which was, one could say, what had led to this proposal.

And in response to Emilia's proposal, Priscilla drew her fan from between her breasts and said,

"There, you see. Just as I said — nonsense that even I could never have imagined has come spilling out."

She snapped her fan open with a crack, cutting Emilia's words down without ceremony.

Emilia's eyes went wide at Priscilla's answer, and she gave a small, wry smile. Seeing that smile, Priscilla narrowed her eyes. "What now?"

"After nonsense comes outright mockery? Either way, you'll earn my displeasure."

"Oh, no, no, sorry. It's just, I thought you might turn me down, Priscilla, and, well, there it was after all. I'll ask you again tomorrow, okay?"

"My answer will not change, come tomorrow."

"But we don't know about the day after tomorrow."

For now, at least, Emilia felt that boldly trying wasn't the wrong thing to do.

Of course, there was the worry that it might be taken as harassment and make Priscilla dislike her even more.

But if Emilia's read on her was right, then Priscilla was—

"Do as you please."

"All right, I will."

Even while dismissing it flatly as pointless, she never tried to force it down by main strength.

Emilia did feel a bit like she was taking advantage of the fact that Priscilla was in a position where that was hard to do.

In any case,

"So? Is that the whole of the business you lay in wait to spring on me?"

"No, that was only the first thing... actually, wait, since there was the Subaru business too, that makes it the second. There's still more I want to talk about, though."

"To begin with, why bring even the first topic to me at all? Whatever policy you lot have decided on has nothing to do with me, so long as it doesn't get in the way of my own road."

"But you get along well with Abel and Zikr, don't you, Priscilla? It looks like you all talk things over a lot, so I thought maybe you could pass it along to everyone."

"I retract my earlier words. Your nonsense dances about outside even my imagining. It is a rare thing indeed for me to go back on what I've said."

"—? Is that a good thing? Or a bad thing?"

Given only a sidelong glance from those crimson eyes, Emilia twisted her lips at not getting an answer.

Probably, it hadn't been very welcome, but without knowing exactly what had been wrong, there was no way to fix it even if she was expected to.

Her guess that Priscilla got along with Abel and Zikr must have been right, so perhaps it was the other part — asking her to pass their policy along to everyone — that had been the mistake.

But Emilia still thought it was far better than going to speak to them directly herself.

After all—

"I'm not really sure I can get along well with Abel..."

"Hgh."

"Priscilla?"

Priscilla pressed her open fan to her mouth and gave a small sound in her throat. Peering at her profile, Emilia saw her eyes crease ever so slightly at the corners as she said,

"To be disliked by one who would even try to talk to me — that one's clumsiness has truly reached its limit."

"Mm, it's not that I dislike him. I just thought I might not be very good with him, that's all."

"The accumulation of such feelings of ill-ease and dislike is precisely what one calls liking or hating. But it's only natural that you lot would judge Abel so. —Your read on him is likely correct, after all."

"...Do you think so too, Priscilla?"

Priscilla gave no answer at all to Emilia's question, remaining silent.

But to Emilia, that silence felt as though it were saying it agreed with her own conclusion — that Abel disliked Subaru.

"Do you know the reason why, Priscilla? You were talking with Abel and the others just now, weren't you?"

"Unfortunately, what I was held back for had nothing to do with your dullard. Not a single fragment of the talk touched upon him... though, no — having heard the tale of the Great Disaster that destroyed the Magic City, I suppose it isn't entirely unrelated to him either."

"The Magic City...? Oh, then were you talking with Yorna? Your mother, Priscilla?"

The parent-child relationship between Priscilla and Yorna had come up partway through the discussion in the great hall. In the end, the conversation had drifted off into the matter of what to do about Rui afterward, but the people actually involved surely wouldn't let it go so easily.

Naturally, the reunited mother and daughter must have talked it over between themselves.

"But I was surprised too. That you're half demi-human blood as well, Priscilla..."

"Fool. Do not lump me together with you. Don't tell me — did you come fawning up to me because you felt some kinship over that?"

"Well, it's not that there was nothing to it, but is that not it? Then is Yorna the one who raised you, Priscilla? If so, that's surprising too. Because I was also..."

"Do not make me call you a fool over and over, fool. That, too, is you getting ahead of yourself."

"Eh? Then what is it?"

If she and Yorna were truly mother and daughter by blood, then the blood of the fox-human should run in Priscilla's veins too — but that had already been denied.

And when Emilia thought perhaps it meant Yorna had raised her instead, that too was flatly denied.

Emilia, who was a half-elf raised by Fortuna, a mother who was in blood relation actually her aunt, had thought she and Priscilla were somehow alike in that regard.

"Not blood-related, and not the one who raised you either... then how does she come to be your mother? What am I supposed to make of that?"

"Is there some reason I must explain everything to you?"

"No, I don't think there is. But if you don't tell me, I'll be really, really curious."

Knowing full well it was selfish of her, Emilia tried asking Priscilla for the truth of it. Of course, just as her offer of friendship had been turned down, the odds were high that this answer would be refused too.

But Priscilla, after only the briefest silence, said,

"—'Iris and the King of Thorns.'"

"Huh?"

"It's an old tale. Do you know it?"

"Um, I do. Petra likes that sort of story, being the little miss that she is."

At the words that had suddenly slipped from Priscilla's lips, Emilia's eyes went wide as she nodded.

"Iris and the King of Thorns" was one of the old tales passed down since ancient times, and she had been told it was a piece of historical fact originating in the Vollachia Empire.

Emilia had only heard a brief outline of it herself, but according to what Petra had told her, it was apparently a love story between a bygone Vollachian emperor known as the "King of Thorns" and a girl named "Iris."

Unfortunately, she'd been told that she still didn't quite understand what made it interesting—

"What about that story? Is it one you like, Priscilla?"

"A half-devil who cannot read context, aren't you. Naturally, it's connected to the flow of the conversation thus far — that much should be obvious."

"Thus far... You mean the old story is connected to your relationship with Yorna, as mother and daughter?"

"—Iris and the King of Thorns joined hands and led an internal war of the Empire, one that had swept up many races, to its end. But the two of them, their feelings for each other understood, met with betrayal just before they could be united, and there the tale draws its curtain."

"...What a sad story."

Emilia murmured that, the corners of her brows drooping, at the outline of the tale Priscilla had told.

It was terribly lonely when someone who'd worked so hard went unrewarded. Just as she'd mourned the situation the current Vollachian emperor now found himself in, she felt the same way about the end that had come to this man and woman, already reduced to a mere legend.

At that reaction from Emilia, Priscilla let out a breath. "Hmph,"

"However, legend and historical fact differ. In truth, the King of Thorns, having lost Iris to betrayal, fell into madness, and wiped out the wolf-humans and mole-humans who had turned their blades against him, root and branch. Even now, including the handful who escaped with their lives, the Empire burns any of their kind at the stake the moment they're found."

"Well, it's no wonder he was angry, but... still, the people living now—"

"Have nothing to do with it, is that what you'd say? For one who has herself been persecuted as a half-devil, this can hardly be someone else's problem."

"——"

Priscilla's words cut sharp, but Emilia's skin had grown as hard as iron against that kind of barb. Still, even if no blood was drawn, being struck hurt all the same.

She'd make sure to properly complain about that later, but for now, Emilia found herself far more curious about what came next in Priscilla's story.

Having lost Iris and taken his revenge on the traitors, what had become of the King of Thorns after that?

"So is that the end of the King of Thorns's story?"

"...Simply giving oneself over to rage could hardly be called falling into madness. And so, after that, the King of Thorns's madness turned toward the Iris he had lost."

"Toward Iris?"

Emilia raised her brows at the unexpected turn the story had taken, repeating back the word that had caught her ear. Priscilla nodded at that and turned her crimson eyes up toward the sky.

Impatient, Emilia trembled her lips, seeking what came next in Priscilla's words.

"What did the King of Thorns do to Iris?"

"He cast a curse."

"A curse? That's..."

Was that something you cast on someone precious to you — all the more so on someone whose loss had caused you such pain?

Setting aside Emilia's doubts, Priscilla went on to describe the details of the curse.

It was—

"—A secret art that withholds a dead soul from being delivered to Od Lagna, and instead drags it back down to the earth once more."

"The soul... of someone who's died?"

"I've heard there also exists a secret art called the 'Sacrament of the Immortal King,' said to revive even the dead, though its very existence is regarded with suspicion. But this 'Thorned Commandment,' one could say, is a curse born of an unquenchable, thirsting love."

The idea of the dead being brought back to life was startling in itself, but what mattered here wasn't that — it was the other thing, the one called a "curse."

The treatment of the souls of those who had died had come up before, too, at the Pleiades Watchtower, in connection with the Book of the Dead, in which memories from before death were burned in.

The very existence of that Book of the Dead was what confirmed, for certain, that a soul existed within every person.

"But what does it mean, to drag a soul back down? Does that mean coming back to life?"

"Things don't go so conveniently. Iris's body, once dead, had been torn apart — even if her soul returned, there was no bringing her life back with it. To begin with, the 'Thorned Commandment' holds no such power."

"Then what happens? If there's no body—"

If there was nowhere for the returned soul to enter, then where was the soul supposed to go?

At that question from Emilia, Priscilla quietly lowered her gaze. Rather than looking up at the sky, her crimson eyes fixed on Emilia's violet ones, and then—

"It's obvious enough, is it not. If a soul does not return to Od Lagna, then that soul, never having received its proper disposition, returns to the earth and enters the next vessel. —One might call it rebirth. Or reincarnation."

"——"

"So long as the curse remains unbroken, so long as the clinging Thorned Commandment goes unloosed, the return of that dead soul will repeat, over and over, again and again, piling life upon death without end. —And it so happens that once, in one of those lives — the one immediately preceding Yorna Mishigure's — that soul was my mother."

"—'Sandra Benedict.' That caught me quite off guard."

"And yet it's you, milord, who hid Prisca away — now that's far too wicked of you, isn't that so."

Hard, cold voices clashed against one another, a quiet heat crossing back and forth within a stone-walled chamber.

The two of them faced each other, choosing their words as if probing at what lay beneath the other's surface — Abel, who had removed the demon mask covering his face and shown his bare features, and Yorna, letting purple smoke curl up from her pipe.

Until just a short while ago, a third person, Priscilla, had also been present.

But once she judged that the necessary talk was finished, she had taken her leave of the place at once. Abel accepted her attitude without complaint, but Yorna's own agitation had not yet faded.

It was only natural. Even the mistress of the Magic City could hardly take in so easily just how much her own child had changed.

"Why not simply admit, the both of us, that our secretiveness came back to bite us — isn't that so?"

"There was reason to keep it secret. You, too, can no doubt imagine why."

"...That much is true, isn't that so."

Yorna lowered her eyes at Abel's answer, her long lashes trembling as she, too, fell to thinking.

The unexpected encounter with Priscilla had sent enormous ripples through Yorna's heart. It was because this was a reality she had never dared to hope for — not even a fragment of hope, let alone expectation, that it could ever come true.

That impossibility had been overturned, and standing there before a Priscilla so plainly alive had been, for Yorna, a joy beyond all expectation — and, at the same time, must surely have been a fearsome gamble on Abel's part.

That Priscilla — that Prisca — should be alive at all was something that ought never to have been.

For there was no doubt that behind that impossible event lay Abel's own involvement.

"Milord, just what were you trying to—"

"—What I promised you was a means of breaking that Thorned Commandment. If it's the answer to your question you desire, then I have no reason to grant you any other reward."

"——"

Head-on, Abel rejected Yorna's question.

He refused to speak that answer aloud, setting it instead upon the scale against Yorna's greatest wish.

For so very long now, through death and life repeated over and over, this soul had gone on gazing out at the world.

Even now, when she could scarcely recall that person's face anymore, even so—

"Yorna Mishigure — no. Iris, chosen by the eye of the King of Thorns."

So he called her — by the name that her beloved, so long ago, had called her by so many times — this man who stood at the far end of generations upon generations of that beloved person's blood.

"Rouse yourself further still. For the sake of your own long-cherished wish, and above all — for the sake of letting Prisca Benedict, the daughter you should have lost, live on into the future."

In a voice colder than that beloved person's could ever have been, by any comparison, he pressed her to it.
