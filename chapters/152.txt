Arc 3 Chapter 75: "Reunion x Postponement x The Winning Hand"

—The loss of sensation in his limbs, and violent pain like his entire body burning to ash. Consciousness blacking out, falling headlong and leaving behind all sound and everything else.

For Subaru, that was close to what "death" felt like.
Having died so many times, in so many different ways, it was precisely because of that experience that he understood the commonalities that arrived whenever he'd tasted "death."

And "Return by Death" was something close to all those lost sensations being shaken awake at once.
Into a place where the lost sensations, the sensory organs that felt those sensations, and the brain that organized that information had all ceased functioning, an enormous flood of information came rushing in all at once.

It was only natural that he couldn't process it all, that his eyes would spin, that he'd be slow to grasp the situation.
In other words—

"Nom."

"Wuhyaaaah!?"

Feeling teeth nip playfully at his earlobe, Subaru's spine went ramrod straight and he went tumbling backward as if he'd been rolled over. His heart pounded at this shockingly sweet first-time sensation, and when he frantically looked around—

"I figured you were spacing out so I decided to mess with you, and what an adorable reaction, nya... Feli-chan might get addicted to this."

Saying so, Ferris smiled mischievously, put a finger to his lips, and struck a coquettish pose.
At the same moment, Subaru realized the feeling of teeth on his earlobe had been his doing, and he collapsed in a heap.

"M-my first nibble on the ear... taken by a boy in girl's clothing...!"

"No need t' be so down about it. More importantly, let's get back to the discussion. Your eyes suddenly went off somewhere, so we're still mid-sentence, yeah?"

Ricardo said this, scratching one of his large ears, to Subaru who was slumped dejectedly on all fours. The people around him nodded in agreement, clearly hoping for a swift recovery.
In front of them, Subaru continued to physically display the shock of what he'd just experienced, while inwardly struggling to come to terms with the impact of having tasted "death."

—He'd died. Died again.

He'd failed. He hadn't thought things through enough. Even after telling himself, again and again, and everyone around him, not to let his guard down, not to ever stop being vigilant—it still hadn't been enough.
Just before his consciousness faded, before his life was returned to the heavens, he recalled Emilia's tearful voice.

His chest tightened at the memory.
He'd fought on with the single thought that he absolutely could not make her cry, could not make her sad—that was all he'd wanted.

And yet through his own death, belonging to no one else, Subaru had given her a grief that would never heal.

"—Let's get organized."

As Subaru sat frowning in silence, weighed down by regret, Ferris crept closer again as if to spur him on. But he was interrupted midway by Julius raising a hand.
Julius waved a finger at the pouting Ferris, then turned his handsome face toward Subaru.

"From here, we head for the Mathers domain, where the Witch Cult awaits. Our objective is the annihilation of the Witch Cult—and essential to that is the capture of the Archbishop of Sin leading them. And in order to keep Margrave Roswaal's household staff and the surrounding villagers out of the fighting, we'll have them evacuate. As for transportation for the evacuation..."

"I already asked Anastasia-san and Russell-san to secure some merchants for that. ...Sorry. I'm calm now, so it's fine."

Wiping his mouth where he'd been breathing hard, Subaru returned to his spot in the circle and offered Julius a brief apology. In response, Julius raised his eyebrows slightly in surprise.

"There's no need to apologize. If anything, I'm glad I could confirm I understood correctly. It doesn't seem explaining things is your strong suit."

"Like I said before, I'm not used to standing in the middle and talking with this many people surrounding me. Remember that a shut-in's social skills decrease in proportion to the number of people involved."

Julius's sarcasm—in this case, it was probably meant largely to distract the tense Subaru. Sensing that consideration left Subaru with both irritation and, at the same time, an indescribable itch of embarrassment, both of which he deliberately ignored.
Then he cast his gaze around once more, taking in the roughly fifty people watching him.

This would be the third time he'd explained the situation in front of them.
Even a shut-in with little life experience could get used to something after retracing the same scene three times. The weight of having other people's lives entrusted to him still bore down heavily on his shoulders, but—

"The Witch Cult's Archbishops of Sin are formidable enemies. The Witch Cult's people won't go down easy, either, and this fight is sure to claim casualties on our side too. First, I need everyone to know that."

"—————"

Everyone answered Subaru's opening words with silence. But it wasn't a silence of denial. It was because every single one of them was focusing their full attention on him, determined not to miss a single word.
That, in other words, was a form of trust, and—

"We beat the White Whale, and honestly, I think everyone'd rather head straight back to the royal capital in triumph. Everyone's exhausted, there's not a single person here without injuries. And in a situation like that, to all of you lending your strength to protect my... my liege—I don't know what to say except... thank you. That's all I've got."

He turned his head, scanning the crowd.
Subaru etched into his heart the faces of the many eyes fixed on him, determined to remember each and every one of them. Among the faces being etched into his memory, he recognized several that, in the previous loop, had been torn apart by the Unseen Hand.

He would not forget those people's faces.
People who, through Subaru's death, through Return by Death, had had the lives they'd lost restored to them.
And yet, through the battle ahead, Subaru would once again cause someone's death by his own choices. Whether enemy or ally, a bloodless victory was not something he could achieve.

If he himself were more capable, perhaps things wouldn't have to be this way.
If Subaru were strong enough that no one could touch him, wise enough to find a way out of any situation, noble enough in spirit to never break under any adversity, an idealist who could never forgive even a shred of weakness in himself—then maybe he could avoid any sacrifices at all.

But that was impossible. Subaru was weak, not very bright, and even his heart was fragile.
He couldn't keep standing without someone's hand to lean on, and he didn't trust himself enough to get by without clinging to someone.
So—

"Let's take down the Archbishop of Sin, this time for good. To do that, I intend to give everything I have, hold nothing back. So please, lend me your strength."

"—————"

"I need all of you. —Please, help me protect the people who matter to me."

Placing his hands on the ground, Subaru bowed his head.
It looked uncool, cheap, and his head wasn't worth anything—but he lowered it firmly, pressing it down as if grinding it into the earth. It was the first time he had ever bowed his head this sincerely, this seriously.

"Subaru, raise your head."

"—————"

At Julius's voice, Subaru slowly lifted his face.
What spread out before him was a dazzling riot of light in the dawn-lit world, bright enough to sear his eyes.

—None other than the sight of knights kneeling, swords drawn and raised high.

This was the second time he'd had his heart supported by the gleam of swords like this.
Subaru was the only one who could truly appreciate it—the only one who had actually experienced "Return by Death." Through repeated, unwanted returns, there was something Subaru had come to understand.

A world that showed different developments, over and over, each time in a different shape.
And yet even in such a world, the things that happened unchanged were the work of "a strong, strong human will unswayed even by the world's fluctuations."
Which meant that the feelings of these people, who kept helping Subaru in this same form—

"No matter how many times I repeat this, no matter how stupid I am for coming back... you'll still lend me your strength, won't you. You'll keep picking me back up, no matter how many times."

Just as Rem had.
Just as Rem had continued to support him no matter how many times things repeated, their presence too gave powerful support to Natsuki Subaru's wavering resolve.
That was, to Subaru, more than anything else—more than any power granted, any wisdom bestowed—a "salvation" surpassing any hope.

"Sorry for saying something that brings down the mood right before the decisive battle. Forget it... or you don't have to, but keep it between us, okay?"

"Let's consider it a fine story to bring back before we meet Lady Emilia. Now then, would you tell us about the Witch Cult?"

"I'm not about to bring some drifter mercenary in front of noble Emilia-tan. Once the job's done, take your pocket change and go home. —Alright, here goes."

Trading the now-customary banter with Julius, Subaru slapped his own cheek to hide his embarrassment. Then he let out a breath and raised his face.

"First, about the Archbishop of Sin leading the Witch Cult—he's in charge of 'Sloth,' and—"

There was no time to hang his head in regret over having been killed, over having failed.
He would turn the cause of his death, the reason for his failure, into the possibility of struggling forward, and fight it out to the end.
That was, after all, why he had come back here again.

Reorganizing in his head the facts newly discovered in the previous loop and the conditions that had to be overcome, Subaru conveyed every scrap of information to the subjugation force without holding anything back.

The "Unseen Hand," "possession"—hearing such information caused no small amount of unease to spread through the subjugation force. Neither technique resembled the magic they knew even in feel, so it was no simple matter to ask them to accept it easily.
With only one known form of magic in this world, even imagining a heresy beyond it wasn't easy.

If anything, Subaru—who'd been exposed to nothing but fiction—found it easier to accept these things flexibly. A worldview where superpowers, magic, and curses all coexisted was something the blood of the Japanese, homeland of subculture, had apparently inherited the comprehension to swallow without much fuss.

"Well, setting aside that dumb conclusion, let's talk about Pete-kou..."

As for dealing with the "Unseen Hand," it remained the case that only Subaru could fight it off. As a special condition, Wilhelm's skill might allow him to handle it the way he had two loops ago, scattering sand—but in terms of certainty, that was difficult. Subaru facing it directly was still the best option.

Regarding "possession," something even more troublesome had come to light. Petelgeuse—or rather, in this case, the body being possessed—had a self-destruct function prepared for it.
Given that he switched possession targets by dying, Subaru should have anticipated that he'd always have some means of suicide ready. That was the biggest factor behind the previous failure.
Since the self-destruction had occurred while the host was unconscious, it meant the self-destruct could be triggered by a will separate from the possessed target's own. In other words, it was highly likely that every Witch Cultist had a self-destruct mechanism implanted in them.
And even if they wiped out every single one of those possession targets—

"In the end, he'll just come and take me over..."

That was the biggest snag.
Two loops ago, he'd had complete control wrested from him and been driven to suicide; last time, he'd managed to hold out through considerable resistance, but the result was the situation he now found himself in—which spoke for itself.
It was a rough estimate, but once it came down to a struggle for control, it seemed extremely difficult to push back Petelgeuse's mind.
That was precisely why, last time too, he'd concluded that the answer was to avoid being possessed in the first place, but—

"Puck's useless, huh. If the ice-block-him strategy is out, we're basically boxed in on every side."

The despair of when Emilia and Puck had appeared on the battlefield was still hard to forget, even now.
Just recalling it made a chill run down Subaru's spine that wouldn't let go, and his breath quickened. But even now, he still didn't understand why such a strong sense of refusal welled up in him. What he did know clearly was one fact: he never wanted to see that situation repeated again.

"I can't let Emilia-tan and Petelgeuse meet. ...I don't know why, but I've got a bad feeling about it. No, wait, it's not like I don't get it at all."

Bringing a creep like that face to face with a sweet girl—there was no way to spin that as anything good for the girl, no matter how charitably you looked at it. Of course he'd feel a sense of rejection about it.
Setting aside that unanswerable worry with appropriate speed, Subaru turned to searching for a plan.

"With Puck out of commission, and Emilia-tan unable to be brought face-to-face with Pete-kou, that rules them out as options. ...Julius, can't you use your Spiritual Arts to freeze a single person in ice or something?"

Hand to his forehead, mind racing, Subaru called out to Julius, who was riding alongside him.
They'd already moved on from the war council held in a circle, and Subaru and the others were now racing full speed down the road to the Mathers domain.
Of course, the one carrying Subaru, kicking powerfully off the ground beneath them, was his dependable partner—the one whose existence had made Subaru deeply grateful for the chance to redo this situation through "Return by Death": Patrasche.

Trusting his body to the reins beneath him, Subaru lightly stroked the back of the running Patrasche. He could tell the running Patrasche glanced back at him with a look that seemed to say, "Don't touch me so casually."

"I don't mean to be rude, but haven't you improved remarkably fast at riding? Just a little while ago, I recall you relying heavily on the sensation of simply being carried by that Earth Dragon."

"It's about trusting her and letting the wind take you, right? Having overcome a crisis together, this girl's trust gauge for me maxed right out. Oh no, I've been captured...!"

Twisting his body, Subaru threw his whole body into hugging Patrasche's back. In response, Patrasche shook her upper body as if annoyed, and Subaru, up top, was flung about violently.

"Whoa, whoa, whoa!? H-hey, don't be shy! Acting all tsundere like that isn't gonna fool anyone—we both know you're crazy about me!"

"I can't shake the feeling there's some fundamental disconnect in your perception somewhere... but it wouldn't be elegant of me to comment on how you and your Earth Dragon conduct your relationship, so I'll refrain. —Now then."

To Subaru, who patted her back lightly as if to calm her, Julius let out a breath as if resetting the topic. Then he closed one eye at Subaru in a wink.

"As for the answer to your earlier question... I can only say it would be difficult. It's true that through my contract with the six spirits I can also handle the fire attribute, but I can't really call myself either a pure Spiritual Arts User or a spirit. If you're expecting that precise a use of the art, I can't confidently say yes."

"Surprisingly weak-willed for you. Won't that make the name 'Sword Saint' cry?"

"I have no particular attachment to that title. Far greater a sin, to me, is failing to admit what I cannot do and betraying someone's trust for the sake of vanity. Is that not the same for you?"

"...You're really an unpleasant guy, you know that?"

Sighing, Subaru looked away from Julius, acutely aware of his own smallness.
At present, Subaru had just had it revealed to him that Julius was a Spiritual Arts User, and it was nearly time to move into the full council for continuing their travel via Necto, the combined yin-yang magic.
Still, no plan had come together that he could confidently call a hundred percent success rate, but—

"With the pieces we managed to tighten up as tight as they'll go, we just gotta run with it and make it work."

Relying on the handful of new pieces of information they'd gained, and weighing the words the madman had spoken, Subaru worked the plan over in his mind. —It was also a decision that brought him considerable heartache, and—

"Let me confirm, Subaru. Are you truly certain you want to go through with this, exactly as planned?"

"You're persistent. I'm the one who came up with it—there's no way I can turn around now and say 'never mind, forget it.' ...Though Emilia-tan's probably gonna be mad."

After using Necto to convey the outline of the plan to everyone, Julius asked Subaru to confirm his resolve.
Subaru answered with his expression wiped blank, picturing the beloved girl behind his eyes.

The smile he'd only just received, the warmth of her hand when they'd made up, the tearful voice on the verge of being lost—all of it blurred, little by little, behind his eyelids, and drifted further away.

Biting his lip, he chastised himself for nearly being swept up in maudlin sentimentality.
Thoroughly disgusted with his own nature—the part of him that, under the pretext of "isn't there some other way," was about to use his own weakness as an excuse—Subaru accepted the painful choice before him.

"Don't make that face. It'll dull my resolve—not like you."

"But this leaves you far too unrewarded for it. If we shouldered more of the burden, perhaps we could reconsider the conditions..."

"Don't give me a chance to whine 'I don't wanna!' like that. Even this courage of mine that looks like I'm squeezing it out—every bit of it's borrowed anyway."

To Julius, who kept checking his resolve again and again, Subaru answered with a wry smile.
Being worried over like this, no matter who was doing it, was something he was grateful for. Still, Subaru had already accepted the burden it placed on the others as necessary, and chosen this path in full knowledge of that. And so—

"It's not like I'm throwing away the future where Emilia-tan and I end up laughing together in the end. It's just that the road to get there looks a bit rough right now. And it's the kind of rough that nobody but us will even notice, so long as we pull it off right. Piece of cake, right?"

"...I envy your thick skin, being able to call it 'a piece of cake' like that. I, for one, cannot help but feel unsettled by something that will not be properly recognized for what it is. I am still immature, it seems."

"Come on, give me a break, Mr. Sword Saint. The reason I can act all optimistic like this, the reason I can even imagine passionately embracing Emilia-tan at the end of all this—your strength is essential to that, you know? ...Hey! Don't make me say something like that! That's not what I really meant! Don't get the wrong idea!"

"You're a busy man. —But I understand what you mean."

Julius couldn't help laughing at Subaru, who flustered and changed expressions rapid-fire.
Snorting at the handsome man's laughter, Subaru turned his face away and gazed off toward the horizon.

In the distance, the end of the grasslands and the edge of a forest were just barely visible. Once they reached it, they'd pass through several forest paths and enter the Mathers domain.
His heart uneasy, feeling something like a grinding ache, Subaru nonetheless kept his eyes fixed ahead.

"Hm? What, worried about me? You're a cute one."

As if sensing Subaru fall silent, the running Patrasche gave a faint sideways shake of her back. Subaru answered that consideration with a pat of his palm, then rummaged through the pack tied to her back.
Inside was the linchpin of this plan, and the moment his searching fingers found its shape, relief and a faint pain settled into his chest at the same time.

But he would hesitate no longer.
This was decided. And he'd asked himself, again and again, whether this was truly the best way, and each time answered yes. That he still couldn't just flatly accept it was his own weakness. It was a problem Subaru alone had to resolve.

"Alright, this time for sure, I'm gonna take the good part for myself—"

"Of course, allow me. Don't worry, we've planned this out this thoroughly. There's no need to even consider failure. Preparations are complete. Once this is over, I'd like to share a drink with you."

"Don't go raising a death flag right when it counts—!!"

Subaru shouted in outrage at Julius's remark, made by someone who surely had no idea what the concept of a death flag even was.
Far, far into the distance, his voice echoed, seeming as though it might reach all the way to the skies over the Mathers domain.

—The morning sun pierced sharply into her sleep-deprived eyes, and as if roused by the faint ache, Emilia raised herself up from the bed.

The silver hair that had fallen across her forehead streamed down with the motion of her sitting up, catching the light like a moonlit waterfall's glimmer.
She blinked a few times, and for a while remained half-risen, drifting in that space between sleep and waking. Lately her sleep had been shallow, day after day, and she was aware that the fatigue never fully left her. Still, this was a burden she'd known would be necessary, a problem she'd chosen to shoulder herself. She couldn't make excuses.

"Morning already, huh..."

She'd probably fallen asleep several hours after the date changed—which meant, in practical terms, she'd gotten something like two hours of sleep.
Her head felt heavy, her thoughts sluggish, as if mired in mud.

She'd never been especially good at waking up to begin with.
It took her time to shake off drowsiness, and time again before she could get moving afterward. And given the difficult problems that had been troubling her these past several days, it was only natural that the fatigue that wouldn't lift and the worry that wouldn't end were wearing away at her spirit.

—It had been a week since those involved in the Royal Selection had been gathered and summoned to the royal capital. Four days since she'd returned to the mansion from there and been formally granted authority as its mistress. In just those few days, the pressure that had poured down upon her had been more than enough to crush a girl like Emilia.

"I thought I understood... but really, I only thought I understood, didn't I."

Thinking of her own inadequacy, Emilia gripped the sheets and bit her lip.
What crossed her mind was this past week, which had raced by in a blur. Being summoned to the royal capital, meeting once again with those involved in the Royal Selection—the other royal candidates—giving her statement of intent with unfamiliar diplomatic maneuvering, and then—

"...Subaru."

Speaking the name of the boy she'd left behind, Emilia closed her eyes as if enduring pain. That cheerful, easily-hurt boy, who for some reason was always desperately doing things for others, and who could be just a bit too convinced of his own assumptions—what was he doing right now, she wondered.

The argument with him at their last parting, that expression like an abandoned child—it was burned into her eyelids, searing her conscience again and again.

Because it was all her fault—that she'd made him wear that expression, that she'd made him say those words, that she'd made him hear those words he hadn't wanted to hear.

And yet, even now, Emilia still believed that had been the right thing to do.
She didn't think she should have avoided that moment, when she and Subaru had laid their feelings bare to each other and it had, in the end, led to their parting—that emotional explosion.
If anything, venting their mutual grievances there and going their separate ways had been the right call. The correct choice. She and Subaru shouldn't be together.

Because she was a half-elf.

A being considered evil simply for existing.
A being whose very presence was denied.
A being resented, excluded, looked down upon, rejected, made the target of negative feelings.

Being together with her could not possibly be good for anyone.
If anything, that gentle-hearted boy would surely be made to suffer unpleasant things simply for being close to her. In fact, Subaru had already been badly hurt, in body and spirit, by dueling Julius—precisely "because he'd been by Emilia's side."

She never wanted him to go through that again, didn't want to be the cause of it again.

By the end of their argument, she was sure Subaru had given up on her.
She'd thrown her selfish complaints at him, rejecting Subaru even as he tried so single-mindedly to offer her nothing but unconditional kindness. To Emilia, as he raised his voice, practically shouting at her, he'd looked like nothing so much as a crying child.

The one true feeling that had slipped out at the very last moment—that alone remained Emilia's true regret.

—The faint, fleeting hope that Subaru might, perhaps, treat her apart from what she was as a half-elf, without regard to that at all.

It had been a faint, fleeting, futile hope.
She, selfish enough to push him away and hurt him while still craving his comfort. Subaru had flatly rejected that weak, unbecoming plea of hers. Rejected it cleanly, cut it off entirely.

—That he could not treat Emilia as anything other than special.

With those words as the last, she had closed the door, and put physical distance between them as well. And feeling a clear sense of disappointment spread through her chest, she'd been disappointed in herself.
Disappointed at feeling uncomfortable with kindness simply given, at using various excuses to hurt and push away a gentle-hearted boy—and then, once she'd actually put that distance between them, at how shameless she was to feel it so keenly as loneliness.

Shaking off the parting with Subaru, she'd thrown herself desperately into things since returning to the mansion from the royal capital.

There was so much Emilia needed to know, so much she needed to learn. She had spent her life deep in a forest with no connection to worldly affairs, avoiding contact with people.
She hadn't even known what a "king" was, until she left the forest.

And now that very girl was aiming for a throne that would place in her hands the lives of the people living across a vast territory.
Emilia was well aware that this was an outrageously reckless, appallingly ignorant decision. But it had to be done.
—Because she had reasons that made it something she had to do.

"Lia, you've got a wrinkle between your brows. Not cute, you should stop that."

Just then, as she leaned forward from the bed, that unhurried voice interrupted her thoughts. When she looked up, reflected in her violet eyes was a small spirit wrapped in gray fur.
To her, he was a presence that had been by her side for as long as she could remember, as good as family.

"Morning, Puck. You're up early today."

"Morning, Lia. I woke up a bit early... well, I'd like to say that, but honestly it's because I was worried about you. Yesterday must've been quite a shock."

Drifting lazily, tail swaying, Puck's words made Emilia lower her eyes.
What came to mind was yesterday—the event that was, quite directly, the cause of the heaviness weighing on her head and heart. The moment when a nearby villager had refused even to let her speak to them.

"I knew... it would happen."

"Even if you know you're going to trip, when you actually fall, the wound still bleeds and it still hurts. I think understanding something and actually bearing that pain are two different things."

At Emilia's childlike tone, Puck mercilessly cut off her escape route. But it wasn't that he was being unkind—Emilia knew he was simply, in his own way, laying out fact and honest feeling side by side, which left her with no rebuttal to offer.

"Puck..."

"Hmm?"

"What do you think I should do? To... no, not just me—to make things right, properly, for everyone too?"

"I think you should do whatever you want, Lia. I'm on your side no matter what you do, and I'm only the enemy of whoever gets in your way."

It was the reassurance of the most stalwart ally anyone could ask for, but it wasn't the answer she'd been seeking.
And Emilia had known full well that such would be his answer. Puck was on Emilia's side, but he wasn't going to agonize alongside her over the problems she carried.
He was her unconditional affirmer, and nothing more, nothing less.

"Whatever you decide to do, you're not going to abandon the village, are you? Ram headed back to the village again this morning, so shouldn't we wait for her report?"

"...Right, Ram. Even though she must not have rested at all either, by now."

"You just haven't noticed, Lia, but that girl cuts corners on her work in plenty of situations, so I think she's got more slack than you'd guess."

Puck's grumbling voice didn't reach her; Emilia, deep in thought, pressed a finger to her lips.
What rose in her mind were the several strange occurrences that had taken place yesterday.

—Ever since returning from the royal capital, with Roswaal away from the mansion on the pretext of negotiating with influential figures, Emilia had focused on absorbing knowledge from the academic and specialized texts she'd been given to study.
Begrudging even the time to sleep, throwing herself into her studies as if to escape her guilt—it was to that Emilia that Ram had reported, two nights ago, "There is a strange presence in the forest."

Even using Ram's Clairvoyance—her ability to synchronize with another's field of vision and switch between targets to expand her range of sight—she hadn't been able to identify the true nature of that "presence."
Still, they couldn't simply choose to do nothing about its existence, and so, purely as a matter of tightening their defenses, Emilia had gone down to the nearby village and called on the villagers to evacuate to the mansion—only to be refused.

Emilia had been surprised to find herself shocked by that.
She'd thought she was thoroughly used to being disliked, avoided, and shunned because of her identity, and yet she was surprised to find her heart still capable of being hurt by it.
And then, with a start, she realized why.

She had been hoping.
That by throwing herself into the great flow of events, by taking a step to change her own position, the reactions of those around her would naturally change from what they'd been before.

There was no way something so convenient could happen.
Having had some degree of contact with the village's people before was likely part of what added to that misplaced hope. Though, of course, back then she had hidden her true identity—she could hardly claim to have interacted with them so familiarly even under those conditions.

"In the end, what am I even doing?"

Hurt by rejection, and yet, having pleaded earnestly and still not been heard, she had no choice but to retreat sheepishly back to the mansion.
Just as she was agonizing over whether all she could do was stand by helplessly, the next thing that came in was a report that a large group of merchants had suddenly descended on the village all at once.

While she was still blinking in confusion at that baffling report, next came someone claiming to be the merchants' representative, bearing a formal letter. The seal, of all things, bore the mark of none other than Duke Crusch Karsten, and when she broke the seal on a letter whose contents she couldn't begin to guess—

"It just had a single blank sheet of paper inside."

As Emilia's eyes fell on the paper spread out on the desk, Puck, as if reading her thoughts, spoke up first with the outcome.
Indeed, the letter's contents had been blank.

When she'd asked the representative what it meant, he'd shaken his head, saying he hadn't been told the contents. Ram, who'd been present, had looked deeply displeased and driven them out of the mansion. She was presumably still down at the village now, engaged in something like negotiations over eviction with the merchant party.

—A sense of duty told her she perhaps shouldn't leave it all to Ram.

But an equally strong feeling made her afraid that her own involvement might make matters worse. Even the merchant who'd delivered the letter had, in his glances toward Emilia, betrayed a mix of fear and disgust.

The fact of the Royal Selection had already been made public, and the names and identities of the candidates were now known throughout the kingdom.
There was hardly anyone left who didn't know Emilia was a half-elf. From now on, that look, that attitude, that rejection would become commonplace everywhere she went.

She should have been used to it by now—to freezing her heart.
Used to ignoring the sting of any fresh wound, forcibly wiping away the blood, and continuing to walk without hanging her head. So why, now, of all times, did she find herself dwelling on it—

"Lia, someone's come back to the mansion."

"...Ram, maybe? I wonder if things got settled at the village."

Interrupted by Puck's call, Emilia broke off her train of thought, finished changing, and stood up.
There'd been no particular request from Puck about her hairstyle today, so she simply ran a comb through it and let it fall down her back. Her silver hair swaying, Emilia let out a small breath and left the room.
She headed straight for the entrance hall to greet Ram, who should have returned by now. Whatever had come of the village situation, there was much to discuss, including what would follow.

"Ram, I'm sorry for leaving everything to you. From here on—"

"Lady Emilia."

As Emilia opened her mouth to call out, seeing Ram enter through the front doors of the mansion, Ram looked up in response to her voice, and stepping aside from the doorway, said:

"—Lady Emilia, please forgive this sudden visit."

Passing through the door Ram had opened, an elderly figure stepped into the mansion.
Recognizing the man, Emilia raised her eyebrows slightly in surprise.

"Um, you were... with Lady Crusch, weren't you?"

"Indeed. I have been granted a place among the lowest ranks of the Karsten Duke's retainers. My name is Wilhelm Trias. I have come today as my lord's proxy."

The elderly gentleman knelt as he introduced himself, taking the most respectful posture possible, and Emilia felt rather abashed.
And once the initial impression of his razor-sharp presence faded somewhat, she noticed that the old gentleman's appearance bore visible traces of injury in numerous places.

"Why are you... like that?"

"My apologies for such an unsightly appearance. On the way here to the Mathers domain, we had the misfortune of encountering a Witchbeast. I was obliged to swing my sword somewhat."

"You're not... hurt, are you?"

"Nothing worth troubling yourself over. More importantly, I would like to convey my lord's wishes."

Wilhelm urged her to get to the matter at hand rather than worry over his condition, and Emilia nodded, and then, at the mention of Crusch's name, recalled the letter.
"A letter arrived from Lady Crusch," Emilia said by way of preface,

"but the contents were blank... we've been at a loss as to how to respond."

"Blank, you say. —I see. So it is as I had been told."

Hearing Emilia's words, Wilhelm narrowed his eyes gently. That gesture alone carried an inexplicable intensity, and Emilia found herself catching her breath. Noticing her reaction, Wilhelm shook his head and said, "No, forgive me—"

"Regarding the blank letter, it was not our intention, but... I am aware of its contents. Please, do not concern yourself."

"The contents... I see, so it was some kind of mistake. That's a relief, that it wasn't... some kind of harassment or anything."

She added that last small worry, one she needn't have voiced, and Wilhelm gave a small, "Hardly," loosening his lips slightly before his expression turned serious again.

"I would ask that Lady Emilia and Lady Ram board the merchants' Dragon Carriages currently stationed in the village. And that you evacuate from this area for the time being—that is my lord's wish."

—To summarize Wilhelm's explanation, it amounted to this.

A criminal group, notorious even by the kingdom's standards, had infiltrated the Mathers domain. There was a strong likelihood they were lying low in the nearby forest, and a formed subjugation force was to move against them.
On top of that, Crusch Karsten had been made the one responsible for the subjugation force; under her name, the force had been organized and dispatched to the Mathers domain with a certain degree of authority granted.
And in order to minimize harm to the surrounding population as the subjugation force smoked out the group in question, an evacuation of the residents had been recommended—the merchants had been hired to serve as transportation for that evacuation, with the plan being to load the villagers and Emilia's party aboard and evacuate them.

"Why would Lady Crusch be involved in maintaining order in Roswaal's... the Mathers domain?"

"This stays between us, but Margrave Roswaal has proposed an alliance resolution. The content concerns the parceling out of mining rights to the mana ore in the Elior Forest. Do you understand?"

"—Yes, I see how it is."

At Wilhelm's discreet answer, Emilia nodded, hiding a measure of indignation.
Even while Emilia had been agonizing alone over what she should do, it seemed Roswaal had been working behind the scenes, pushing matters forward. It was something she'd already more or less known, but it stung all the same—that even as he made use of Emilia's existence, he still had no real expectations of her.

"But even if you say evacuate..."

"If at all possible, we would like Lady Emilia to head to the royal capital and hold a meeting with the head of the house, Lady Crusch. However, it will be difficult to bring all the residents to the royal capital, so..."

"Half will be guided by Ram to the Sanctuary. It should be safe there, and there should be enough capacity to take them in."

The worries and concerns Emilia had been about to voice had apparently already been considered. With Wilhelm and Ram answering her questions so efficiently, there was hardly room for Emilia to raise any objection.
Still, she found it hard to accept the sense that events were moving too quickly, that she was simply being swept along, and—

"Hey, I still can't help but wonder... is this really—"

"—Excuse me!"

Just as she was about to press further with her doubts, the sound of the door being flung open violently cut the conversation short. Looking over, she saw a young man come tumbling in as if he'd kicked the door open.
The young man, clad in a white robe, stared fixedly at Emilia for a moment, then slowly turned to face Wilhelm.

"We've spotted strange movement from the group hiding in the forest. They may be about to move. There's not a moment to lose..."

"Sooner than we expected. ...I suppose it was only a matter of time before this many people entering the village drew attention."

"What are your orders, Wilhelm-sama?"

"—Lady Emilia."

At the young man's question, Wilhelm turned his gaze, sharpened, up toward Emilia.
Pierced by that blade-like sharp look, Emilia caught her breath softly, and understood there was no time left to waste.

She did still have some lingering questions.
But as a matter of fact, there was indeed some strange presence in the forest that Ram had sensed, and Wilhelm's identity was vouched for under Crusch's name. And more than anything, with Roswaal currently absent, it was Emilia who held the authority to decide matters for this mansion and its domain.
No matter how much the situation made her head spin, the decision had to be hers to make.
And so—

"—Understood. I'll gratefully accept your kindness. As for persuading the villagers..."

"That has already been handled without a hitch, Lady Emilia."

Ram declared, without hesitation, that the greatest outstanding concern had already been resolved. Emilia then turned back toward the mansion, furrowing her brow over what to do about the last remaining resident. But even that was answered almost immediately, as a small kitten came drifting down from the upper floor like a leaf caught on the wind.

"Betty says she's not coming. She's closing the door-crossing, so after that, do whatever you like. And it'd be best if the subjugation force people don't do anything to the mansion, okay?"

"Understood, Great Spirit-sama."

Before Emilia could even finish deciding, Puck returned, having gone to confirm Beatrice's wishes. He promptly burrowed into Emilia's silver hair, secured his usual spot, and let out a yawn.
Then, close to her ear, so only she could hear,

"Do what you want to do. I'm on your side."

"—Let's move. I don't want to put the villagers in danger."

Emilia swept her arm and gave the order.
Ram indicated her compliance with a curtsy, pinching the edge of her skirt, and Wilhelm nodded magnanimously. And only the young man who had brought the report, quietly gripping the hem of his robe, murmured—

"That's more like it."

—but Emilia did not hear it.

—The two from the mansion made their way down to join the village, and the work of loading everyone onto the Dragon Carriages began.

The villagers seemed to be following the subjugation force's members with remarkable obedience, showing little grumbling and not much visible anxiety—if anything, many of them seemed almost excited at the sight of such a rarely-seen mass of Dragon Carriages.

Emilia was quite struck by how smoothly things were going, but what surprised her most of all was when she heard how she herself had been assigned among the carriages.

"—Thank you for having us, big sister."

"Um...."

A cheerful girl with blonde hair bowed her head politely. Someone Emilia had seen many times in the village, one of the children who'd been close with Subaru—her name, if Emilia recalled correctly, was Petra.
She looked to be about eleven or twelve, one of the older children in the village. And she, along with several other children, had apparently been assigned to the very same Dragon Carriage Emilia was to board.

"Isn't this some kind of mistake? Because..."

"It's not a mistake. Lady Emilia, we'd like you to ride in this carriage, together with these children. It's simply how the carriage assignments worked out—there's no helping it."

Driven by unease, Emilia caught hold of the young man who'd been busily running around nearby. But his answer was entirely different from what she'd expected, and only served to stoke her anxiety further.

—Traveling in the enclosed space of a Dragon Carriage, together with children.

To Emilia, this seemed less a concern about herself than about how deeply uncomfortable it would surely make the other party feel, and—

"Isn't there another carriage? These children, too..."

"You mean, they'd surely hate riding with you?"

"—————"

"Did you actually ask them that? Or are you just assuming they'd hate it, that they dislike you?"

The young man pressed on, driving his point home relentlessly at Emilia's silence.
Thinking he was being remarkably forward, she realized he was the very same young man who'd come dashing into the mansion earlier. Tilting her head slightly at the odd feeling this gave her, Emilia stammered, "That's not..."

"I don't need to ask, I already know. So, for these children's sake too, a different carriage..."

"Using kids as an excuse to run from your own weakness isn't a good look, y'know."

This time, Emilia's breath caught at just how rude that remark was. And in front of her, he knelt down, bringing himself eye-level with the girl who'd been watching this exchange with growing unease.

"So, Petra. Do you not want to ride in the same carriage as that big sister there?"

"—————"

Please don't ask her that, Emilia thought, a pain gripping her chest.
It was a question with an obvious answer, and one whose answer she already knew would hurt. Knowing something would hurt didn't make the hurt any easier to bear. Just as Puck had said—no matter how many wounds you'd already taken, a new wound still brought new pain. And yet, why would he—

"That's not true at all. I'm fine riding with big sister."

"...Eh?"

Emilia let out a small, stunned sound. Petra walked up to her and gently took her hand. From the fingers held in hers, Emilia felt the warm heat particular to a child's body, and blinked.
To Emilia, who couldn't hide her surprise, Petra gave an endearing, shy smile.

"Big sister... you're the potato-stamp big sister, right? You always used to come with Subaru to watch the morning Radio Taiso."

"............"

"I couldn't see your face, but I could still tell big sister always looked happy. I saw how happy Subaru looked talking with you. Subaru really, really... so I'm not scared of big sister either, you know?"

"...Ah."

Something welled up inside her, and Emilia let out a small, involuntary sound.
The back of her eyes burned, her throat closed up so no voice would come, and a sharp ache shot through the back of her nose.

"Big sister, let's ride together, okay? Someone told me everyone was trying to make big sister be all alone. But I'll hold your hand the whole time."

"—Yeah. Yeah."

"You don't have to be lonely anymore, okay?"

"————!"

Innocent, guileless eyes, holding within them nothing of this world's unfairness or malice.
The sensation that had always been a given for Emilia, the persecution that had always been a given, the discrimination that should have been a given—she simply couldn't feel any of it there. That was all it took.

"Me too!" "I wanna ride with big sister too!" "Let's go already!"

The children broke out into a clamor, and a boy about the same age as Petra—Mild—began scolding them and herding them toward the carriage.
Watching the rowdy children, Petra gave a small laugh, as if she couldn't help it, and said,

"Shall we go, big sister? It might be a little noisy."

"...No, it's fine. I've gotten used to being around noisy people this past month, right next to me."

Shaking her head, Emilia felt a smile rise naturally to her lips.
She began walking. Still led by the hand by Petra. Still feeling the warmth of another person's hand against her fingers. Still feeling, without quite realizing it, the blessing of no longer having to know the pain she'd been putting on a brave face and calling "something I'm used to."

"Oh, right... I should thank you too."

Stopping and turning back, Emilia searched for the young man from before.
Meaning to offer just a word of thanks to the one who'd guided her to this small piece of happiness, she looked—

"...Huh? Where did he go?"

But wherever she turned to look, the young man in question was nowhere to be found.

Pushing aside branches, treading over grass, darting through a game trail like something leaping.
Slipping between trees, breaking through the underbrush into deeper shadow, he came upon an open space thickly enclosed on all sides by green.

He stopped and waited a moment, and three more shadows came dashing into the same spot in the same way. Now four in total, they nodded to one another, and one crouched down in the center while the remaining three kept watch on their surroundings.
The figure crouched in the center rummaged through his coat and produced something like a hand mirror. Opened, it was unmistakably a mirror, but in the depths of the forest, where the available light was reduced to almost nothing, even confirming one's own reflection was no easy task.

But for the mirror's owner, that was of no concern whatsoever.
His purpose was never to see his own face to begin with. The purpose was, through the mirror, to "connect with another location."

Channeling magical power into the hand gripping the mirror, he poured mana into the engraving carved along its rim. Once the initial activation was triggered, the mirror itself would absorb trace amounts of mana from its surroundings to fulfill its function thereafter—a rare Magic Vessel: a Dialogue Mirror.
Most such devices were fixed installations, far too large in scale to carry around; one small enough to be portable, like this, was exceedingly rare.
Being entrusted with it, given this role, was a mark of how highly his position was regarded—and the man felt his sense of mission swell into elation at the thought.

And just as he opened his mouth to speak through the mirror to the other side—the very instant before he could—

"I see, so that's how you've been passing information along. Handy things, these Dialogue Mirrors. ...But maybe before burning with a sense of mission, you oughta learn your comrades' faces, huh?"

Right beside the crouching man, someone else, likewise crouched, spoke up.
Startled, the man looked up, only to be struck with an intense sense of wrongness at the figure standing before him. As if, even standing right there in plain view, his awareness refused to register it.

"If you can't tell my face, guess to you lot I'm just some fellow cultist who happens to reek of the same stink as the rest of you. —Give me a break, you small fry."

As he spoke, the figure in the white robe stood, spitting the words out with contempt.
Then, before the shock-frozen men—the Witch Cultists—he shook his head and threw back the hood he'd had pulled fully over his own head.

Revealed was hair of black—a color possessed by only a very limited few in this world.
His three-whites eyes, plainly showing his displeasure, and a bold, provoking smirk.

Only then did the Witch Cultists finally, clearly, understand who this was.

"Interrupting my touching reunion with Emilia-tan is a heavy crime, you lot."

Pointing a finger to the sky, the hem of his white robe—woven with a "recognition inhibition" formula—swaying, Natsuki Subaru delivered his declaration to the men,

"Now then, why don't you spill everything you know!"

—And, to show them the winning hand of a reversal, he snapped his fingers high into the air.
