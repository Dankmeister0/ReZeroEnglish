Arc 7 Chapter 77: "The Whims of Fate"

"—That concludes the damage report from the Flying Dragon attack."

So saying, Otto tapped the documents in his hands, and Emilia's brows drooped painfully at his explanation.
The 'Flying Dragon Cataclysm' that had struck the walled city of Guaral—the days it had taken, pursued by repairs to the city and care for the wounded, until things could finally be called settled, and now the results were being reported.

Even to Emilia's eyes, the state of the city was terrible.
The splendid ramparts that had once encircled the city were destroyed, and here and there throughout the town, great boulders dropped from high above still lay where they'd fallen. The damage to the buildings was severe too, and less than half remained unscathed. Not a few people had died, and even the two-story private house, now crushed, where Emilia and the others were lodging had been one whose residents would never return.

"If only a little more..."

If only they had rushed there sooner.
Such regret pricked sharply at Emilia's chest like a needle, but—

"'If we'd been a little faster' is a meaningless hypothesis, so let's not go there."

Reading Emilia's heart, Otto nonetheless cut her off sharply. Emilia flinched a little at the bluntness, but Otto merely jutted his chin toward the window,

"The only reason we were even allowed to go on ahead back then was because the flock of Flying Dragons overtook us. Without that, there wouldn't have been any option to hurry in the first place. In other words..."

"In other words?"

"There was no way to have prevented it beforehand. It's a matter of causality. You can't have your cake and eat it too. Fretting over that would be irrational."

Shaking his head slowly as he said this, Otto drew a wry smile from Emilia.
An opinion that seemed to read straight into Emilia's heart—that itself wasn't surprising. Otto was very good at observing people, so that much was ordinary for him.
On top of that, Emilia thought the words Otto offered her were his own way of consoling her.
He was telling her that agonizing over hypotheticals wouldn't amount to any realistic reflection.
So Emilia had convinced herself—but—

"Still, there's a way of saying things. I don't like the way you just said that."

Petra, turning her face away with a pointed huff, looked thoroughly cross.
Seated in a chair in front of Emilia, Petra was having her bright brown hair combed through, polishing up her already considerable cuteness even further. It was a duty that had lately been entrusted to Emilia, and she was continually impressed by Petra's dedication to never neglecting her grooming, even on the road.
Even yesterday, after running herself ragged all over the city, exhausted—she still properly remade herself anew for the new day.

That too was a testament to the position and sense of responsibility Petra was conscious of.
After all, she was—

"—When my mistress, Lady Petra, says it like that, it does weigh on my conscience."

As Otto let slip, Petra had been entrusted with the role of the "employer's young lady" overseeing Emilia's group.

—Emilia's party, having entered the Vollachia Empire in search of the missing Subaru and Rem.

They had reached the empire after many twists and hardships, but just as they'd known from the start, crossing the border through proper means was difficult, so they'd ended up sneaking across illegally.
At that time, since it would cause enormous trouble if Emilia or Roswaal's true identities were discovered, each had adopted a false name, and they'd fabricated a false purpose for their journey as well.
As a result, Emilia had been assigned the role of bodyguard under the name "Emilie," and the people that Emilie was supposed to be protecting were—

"Lady Petra and Lady Beatrice, the two of them."

"Our stated purpose for coming to the empire is, to the last, Lady Beatrice's recovery... and in truth, that goal would be fulfilled if we recovered our real objective, so it isn't entirely a lie."

"I get it, but somehow... ah, never mind."

"Being cut off partway makes me curious, so I'd rather you just said it."

"Really? Otto-san, are you starting to catch some of the master's bad habits?"

"I really should not have asked...!"

Otto clutched his head, and Emilia hastily swallowed back the words she'd been about to say.
Otto looked thoroughly displeased, but Emilia found Roswaal's cleverness very reassuring. So the thought that two of the camp's particularly sharp minds were thinking along the same lines was quite reassuring to her.
Though saying so would probably only cause Otto more suffering.
In any case—

"The injured in the city have been cut down a lot too, thanks to Garfiel."

"That's certainly true. I think Lady Petra and Frederica-san, and of course Emilie's help, all contributed greatly as well."

At Otto's nodding reply, Emilia was finally able to loosen the tension in her cheeks.
Garfiel's hard work, and Frederica's and Petra's help—all of it made Emilia proud. It was the striving of her beloved, treasured companions.

"Otto-kun, you worked reeeally hard negotiating with Zikr-san and everyone too."

"Fortunately, Zikr-san was a reasonable man to talk to, so the trouble was kept to a minimum. I'm grateful to Mizelda-san, who happened to run into us and helped smooth things over."

"Mizelda-san likes your face, doesn't she, Otto-san?"

"I've never had it put so bluntly before, so I was quite thrown off."

Raising a small fist, Petra smiled as she said this, and Otto gave a wry laugh.
Mizelda, whom Emilia had also spoken with, was a very strong and approachable woman. She had been kind from the very start of their acquaintance, and had shown consideration in various ways.
Though it was a bit curious why she treated Otto and Garfiel so differently.

"I understand liking Otto-kun's face, but Garfiel is cute too..."

"Maybe it's because Garf-san often has creases between his brows and around his nose. Also, he's irritable from being apart from Ram-neesama."

"...I think I'll refrain from commenting."

Petra tapped a finger lightly against her own well-shaped nose as she gave that assessment of Garfiel.
Emilia thought Garfiel's ever-changing expressions were charming too, so she wished Mizelda would look at that side of Garfiel properly as well.
Setting such digressions aside—

"But now that the cleanup of the city has settled down..."

"Yes."

Otto nodded at Emilia's words, then looked out the window once more. In the direction of his gaze stood the face of the city, weathered but still standing despite the damage—the city hall.
Gathered there were the influential figures of this walled city of Guaral.
In other words—

"We should finally be able to begin discussions about moving forward."

Just as Otto said, the talks were about to begin.

"Hey, I ain't heard no thanks yet, old man."

Climbing up onto the half-ruined city walls, Garfiel called out to the back of the man who'd arrived there first.
Honestly, he wasn't someone Garfiel wanted to talk to, but turning around the moment he saw that back felt like losing, so his stubbornness won out over his distaste.
In the first place, why should he be the one to retreat?
If anyone should flee unable to bear the awkwardness, it ought to be the other guy, not him.
With that thought, he'd opened with a line straight out of some punk's playbook—

"—Tch."

What came back was a resounding click of the tongue, and Garfiel regretted his choice almost immediately.
The result of his petty stubbornness was only a worse taste in his mouth. He could practically hear Otto in his head sighing, "Well, of course."

Even setting that aside, this was a man he disliked.
Garfiel hadn't personally been present when this man before him had behaved rudely toward one of his own people, but even the secondhand account was more than enough to make him hate the guy.
That was Garfiel's unshakable impression of Heinkel Astrea.

"——"

Heinkel, a fine sword at his hip and dressed lightly in leather armor, stood on watch.
But Garfiel judged that this wasn't out of any noble desire to protect the city's peace—it was just posturing to cover up having nothing to do and nowhere to belong.
The reek of alcohol drifting off his back, and the empty liquor bottles scattered around him, were proof enough of that.

During the Flying Dragon Cataclysm, Heinkel had apparently fought like a demon against the swarming Flying Dragons, but he'd suffered a bitter defeat against the 'Nine Divine Generals.'
That little runt Schult had gone to great lengths defending Heinkel with all sorts of words, but Utakata had put it bluntly: "Couldn't do a thing," which was probably closer to the truth.
As proof, among the wounded Garfiel had gone around healing with magic, Heinkel had been one of the more troublesome serious cases. He'd apparently taken quite a beating—without Garfiel's healing magic, he'd likely still be bedridden.

"That tongue click just now supposed to be your thanks? That's a hell of a lot different from the common sense I know, y'know."

Even while taking the man's circumstances into consideration, Garfiel spat that out anyway.
Garfiel too had experience with losing. He thought he understood that frustration, so he didn't particularly want to needle the fact of the man's defeat out of spite.
But if the other party wasn't going to show proper gratitude, he felt like saying at least one snide thing. Especially since Garfiel had absolutely no good impression of Heinkel to begin with.

"This here's the empire. Might be common sense here I don't know... but you ain't from here, are you? You're from the kingdom. And what's more, word is you're that 'Sword Saint's' old man."

"——"

"I talked to him too—that guy's somethin' else, I tell ya. Strong as hell like that, figured his parent must be somethin' too, but with manners like this—"

"—However great the parent, it doesn't follow the child will be."

Garfiel, who'd kept up the barrage of snide remarks at the unmoving back, blinked in surprise.
He'd expected to be ignored outright, but instead came an unexpected rebuttal. Eyes widening, Garfiel watched as Heinkel, still without turning around, continued,

"Same goes the other way. No reason a monster's parent has to be a monster too."

"Wha—"

"You're terrible at talking trash, you know that? If you want to provoke someone, take a page from Lady Priscilla. At this rate you'd fail the class."

"Nugah?!"

Having tried to gain the upper hand through insults only to be seen through and neatly turned back on himself, Garfiel found himself at a loss for words.
Answered back as though shown what true provocation actually looked like, the conversation ended there. But if he turned his back now, it really would be a total defeat.
In the end, Garfiel stubbornly held his ground and sat down a short distance from Heinkel, looking out over the southern horizon where the wall no longer stood, becoming himself a defense for the unprotected city.

Assaulted by countless Flying Dragons, the walls of Guaral, famed as a walled city, had taken a massive blow.
The damage from stones dropped from high above had been severe, and although repair work was proceeding urgently on the crumbled, broken walls, the city's defensive capacity had dropped remarkably.
Above all, it was this southern wall that had suffered the worst damage in the entire city.
The western wall, which had taken the first attack, was in fairly wretched shape too, but it was nothing compared to this. Here, not just the wall but an entire district had been leveled flat.

"Heard Lady Emilia... no, Emilie and that Priscilla princess went toe to toe with one of the 'Nine Divine Generals.'"

The ferocity of that battle was written vividly in the devastation below.
Most of the buildings had lost their original shape entirely, a tremendous shockwave having swept everything away wholesale.
The battle against the Dragonkin who was one of the 'Nine Divine Generals,' and the white light that had staged its conclusion—Garfiel too had been caught up in that shockwave somewhere in the city, and Emilia had described its true nature thus:

"—A Dragon's roar."

Even among dragon-kind like Earth Dragons, Water Dragons, and Flying Dragons, there existed beings one rank above them all: 'Dragons.'
A Dragon, wielding power said to lie entirely outside the realm of reason, had upended everything with a single breath.
If Emilia and Priscilla, both of them, hadn't fought back, the damage might well have spread further still.

"...No matter where I go, my timing's rotten."

Even while praising Emilia's—and incidentally Priscilla's—achievements, a bitter feeling sat in his chest.
Unable to accompany them to the Pleiades Watchtower, Garfiel had missed the chance to meet the 'Divine Dragon' Volcanica, whom Emilia had come face to face with. And not only that—now, even while in the very same city, he'd once again failed to make contact with another 'Dragon.'

To put it in Subaru's terms, he had no choice but to call his luck rotten.

"——"

It wasn't that he was sulking simply because he hadn't gotten to meet a 'Dragon,' a being spoken of since ancient times.
It was because fighting powerful enemies and defeating them was Garfiel's role. Striking down those who would thwart the camp's aims, fulfilling his duty as its military officer—that was supposed to be his role.
Certainly not going around treating the wounds of those who'd been hurt.

"And on top of that, I ain't done a damn thing useful gettin' into the empire either."

In sneaking into the Vollachia Empire, Garfiel had been of no use whatsoever.
For making contact with the people who guided them across the border, they'd relied on Otto's family, the Suwen Company, and when there'd been trouble with them along the way, it was Petra who'd handled the negotiations with poise. After that, when a problem arose right after entering the empire, it was Frederica who used her own blood to break the deadlock, and all Garfiel had done, in the end, was punch out someone he didn't like to shut him up.
At the very least, he'd hoped that with the rumor going around the walled city about a black-haired battle maiden named 'Natsumi Schwartz,' he might find hope for a reunion with Subaru—

"Captain went and left right before we got here... it's like every damn bit of this rotten timing is somehow all my fault..."

Of course, he knew full well that was just overthinking things, a kind of persecution complex.
It was just that, about as much as Garfiel and the others had had their own grand adventure making their way into the empire, Subaru had likewise had his own grand adventure in the empire he'd been flung into.
Even flung off to some faraway place, still restlessly getting swept up in huge events—that was exactly the kind of situation Garfiel would expect from the Subaru he knew.

"—Fightin' over the top spot in the whole damn empire, that's way too big a story, Captain."

The walled city—no, a sign of a great conflict that would engulf the entire Vollachia Empire.
What sort of chain of causes could lead someone into the very center of such a thing? But Garfiel thought: surely, it was simply the result of Subaru being himself.
Desperately struggling to make his way back to Emilia and the others in the kingdom, forging bonds along the way with the people he'd met, unable to abandon them, running and running still.

Right now, he was apparently far away, in a great city to the southeast, negotiating with some influential figure there.
Regardless of how it turned out, Garfiel just wished he'd come back safe—

"—Oi, brat."

"Ah?"

"You've been clacking your teeth this whole time, it's annoying. Can't you keep your mouth shut for once?"

Heinkel suddenly spat that at Garfiel, who'd been lost in thought.
The red-haired man glaring resentfully over at him hooked a finger in his own mouth and bared his canines. It was Garfiel's habit of gnashing his fangs together that had apparently gotten on his nerves.

"Hah, why the hell should I listen to what you say? If it bugs you so much, then you're the one who oughta get lost."

"A mouthy brat like you, is that the best insult you've got? I was here first. You're the one who should know your place."

"—Tch, don't go actin' all high and mighty when you're the one who got scared shitless facin' one of the 'Nine Divine Generals'!"

Not wanting to be put in his place the same way as before, Garfiel snapped back forcefully.
The moment the words left his mouth, Garfiel realized he'd said something pretty harsh. But it was this guy he was talking to. Saying that much back shouldn't require—

"Yeah, that's right. —I was scared shitless. And look at me now."

"Old man...?"

"Pissed off Lady Priscilla and now I'm drinking myself stupid. Ah, hopeless."

But the words that came back from Heinkel were weak, and Garfiel found himself greatly thrown off.
Bringing the liquor bottle in his hand to his lips, tipping back its contents, Heinkel muttered in a low voice, as if cursing this world—no, cursing himself.
As though abandoned by the world itself, in a self-destructive kind of way.

"...You were up against the 'Nine Divine Generals.' The strongest fighters in the whole empire—ain't it obvious you couldn't win, old man?"

Unable to bear watching that pitiful state, such words of comfort slipped out.
Even as he said it, Garfiel was disgusted with himself, wondering what face he had to say such a thing. Just a moment ago he'd been cursing the man for losing to the 'Nine Divine Generals,' and now he was comforting him by saying of course he'd lose.
It made no sense, and probably wasn't comforting at all. If Garfiel had been in Heinkel's position and someone said the same thing to him, he might have flown into a rage.
And yet, Heinkel laughed self-deprecatingly at Garfiel's attempt at comfort,

"Win or not, I had a role to fulfill. All the more so, given my position, scraping by on the coattails of Lady Priscilla's journey. ...Heh heh, what a joke."

"A joke, huh?"

"Something I've been chasing for over a decade slips right through my fingers. No matter how far I go, seems the nature of a coward who wets himself just can't be changed."

At Heinkel's unstoppable self-mockery, the way Garfiel looked at him began to change.
At first, the impression Garfiel had held of him was one of disgust and hostility over the outrage Heinkel had apparently committed at the Water Gate City. Just as he thought that had been confirmed in the short time since, watching the man curse himself made his impression shift once more.

What sat before him now was a weak, worn-down, pitiable man.
The words that spilled from his mouth were blades turned both on others and on himself, and their sharpness was painful to witness. If they'd been directed only at others, Garfiel could have simply felt contempt and left it at that.
But knowing they were turned on himself too, Garfiel felt a sickness rise in his chest.

"Why the hell are you so rotten like that? You're still alive, ain't ya. So then..."

"—. Why am I even still alive, I wonder."

"—Tch, you got some nerve sayin' that in front of the guy who just saved you."

The self-destructive words continuing to spill out stirred fury in Garfiel.
At Garfiel's reaction, Heinkel glanced over at him, and only then, as if just now realizing something, murmured "Ah."

"Come to think of it, you're the one who patched me up. Next time, be more careful."

"Ahh?!"

"Don't miss the chance to abandon someone you can't stand, without any regrets."

Laughing bitterly, Heinkel said this.
The instant he heard it, Garfiel felt his vision flood red, and before he knew it he'd grabbed the man by the collar, glaring at his liquor-stinking face from point-blank range.

A light clatter of breaking glass sounded from underfoot.
Seized by the collar and forcibly hauled up by Garfiel, the still-partly-full liquor bottle had slipped from Heinkel's hand and fallen off the wall below.
Following the fallen bottle's trajectory with his eyes, Heinkel sighed.

"What a waste."

"That all you got to say, huh?!"

Offering no resistance, Heinkel turned listless eyes on Garfiel.
Those blue eyes devoid of any vigor shared their origin with those of Reinhard, whom Garfiel had also spoken with, and Wilhelm, whom he'd fought alongside.
And yet, Garfiel could not for the life of him think of them as the same thing.

How could a human's eyes become clouded to this degree?
Just how many years had it taken for the emotions swirling in the depths of those eyes to be ground down like this?

"What the hell's gotta happen for someone to end up with eyes like that?"

Heinkel had said his defeat against the 'Nine Divine Generals' had soured Priscilla's mood.
The truth of it was unclear, but Garfiel thought that being blamed for lack of ability just because he hadn't put up a good fight against the 'Nine Divine Generals' was a heartless opinion, to say the least.
At least, coming from Garfiel, who hadn't even managed to reach the point of fighting at all.

At that look and those words from Garfiel, Heinkel's eyes wavered ever so slightly.
His eyes, which hadn't even been focused on Garfiel standing right in front of him, finally seemed to resolve into a clear image, properly reflecting him at last.
And then—

"—My wife—"

Despite having been drinking, Heinkel's lips, dry and cracked, let slip a hoarse voice.
But Garfiel, gritting his back teeth, thinking that finally something meaningful was about to follow, waited to hear the rest from the wavering man.
However, the words Garfiel had been hoping for once again went unheard.

"—Ah."

"Oi?"

Heinkel, still held by the collar, had his eyes lose focus on Garfiel again. His gaze leapt past Garfiel, toward something behind him.
That struck Garfiel as frustratingly maddening, but—

"A carriage."

At those words that followed, Garfiel had no choice but to turn and look.

"——"

Toward the southern edge of the city, where Garfiel had turned his back in order to grab Heinkel—far off on the horizon beyond it, he could indeed make out a small shape approaching.
Even straining his eyes, Garfiel could barely tell whether it was a Dragon Carriage or an ordinary one, but if he believed Heinkel's word that it was a carriage, then it was—

"—So, the folks who went out are comin' back, huh."

Heinkel affirmed the thought catching in Garfiel's throat with different words of his own.
Still no bigger than the tip of a fingernail, the shape whose outline was only just beginning to become visible—might, to Garfiel, be the carriage carrying the person he'd been longing to find.

"Oi, let go, brat."

Garfiel's eyes shone with anticipation and excitement, and into that came a breath reeking of alcohol. Scowling and looking straight ahead, he was met with Heinkel's sour, displeased expression.
Gone was the man who, just a moment before, had seemed on the verge of baring his own heart—all that remained was the detestable face of someone who scattered insults and irritation all around him.

The moment he noticed that, Heinkel twisted his neck and slipped free of Garfiel's grip.
Garfiel felt that he'd been let slip both physically and, somehow, spiritually too. As though something he'd thought he'd hooked between his fingers had heartlessly slid free.

"The one you've been waiting for has come, hasn't he? Get going and welcome him already."

"...You—"

"I'll fulfill my duty as lookout. If I see something, reporting it is the job. Even a drunk can manage that much. ...Though it probably won't earn me any points."

Waving a hand dismissively, Heinkel turned his unsteady, weaving back on Garfiel.
For a moment, Garfiel hesitated over whether to call out to that retreating back, but in the end no words came. Just as Heinkel had implied, Garfiel's heart had already begun racing ahead.
Wanting nothing more than to run right now toward that shape approaching from beyond the horizon.

"Miss your chance and it's a disaster, you know? You end up chasing someone's last words forever."

Leaving just those words behind, Heinkel jumped down from the wall ahead of Garfiel. Landing surprisingly steadily within the city, he headed off on foot toward the city center.
That final line, the desperate feeling packed into it, stuck in Garfiel like a thorn, asserting its presence.
But even so—

"Right now—"

Shelving the thorn's presence for later, Garfiel jumped down off the wall on the opposite side from Heinkel.
Landing outside the city, Garfiel took off running with the force of straightening his bent knees. Exploding off his kicking feet, faster than the wind, he ran in a straight line toward the approaching shape.

"Captain...!"

Through the gap in his sharp fangs, an unbearable feeling leaked out of Garfiel.
Thinking of the feelings of Emilia, Beatrice, Petra, and the others made him feel a bit sheepish, but Garfiel too was one of those who had been longing for a reunion with Subaru.
So, converting his impatient heart into leg strength, he charged toward the carriage with tremendous force.

Just a little more, just a few dozen more seconds of running, and his reunion with Subaru would come true—

"—Ah?"

That overflowing anticipation suddenly turned to confusion, catching him off guard.
His speed, once leaving the wind behind, began to slacken, and soon the wind overtook him. Worse than that, his speed dropped to where everything besides the wind was leaving him behind, until at last he came to a stop.
In Garfiel's wavering emerald eyes was reflected, coming from beyond the horizon—not a single carriage, but a great many carriages and ox-carts, pressing forward in a mass.

"Th-this is..."

Having expected, without a doubt, to come face to face with Subaru, Garfiel blinked.
That sight was, considering the purpose for which Subaru and the others had left the walled city, one entirely possible outcome—but Garfiel, in that moment, couldn't think that far.
In any case, before the overwhelmed and halted Garfiel, at the head of a group making its way along the road toward the city, a splendid Galewind Horse-drawn carriage came proudly forward.
And then—

"—You there, what are you gawking at? If you're an envoy of the city, surely you have things you ought to be doing."

So spoke a demon-masked man who poked his face out of the carriage, and Garfiel found himself catching his breath.

"Frederica-jou, you've worked hard."

At the fragrance of black tea quietly offered to her just as she was feeling fatigue and thirst, Frederica looked up at the man's face with faint surprise.
The one who'd offered Frederica this consideration, his mild features carrying a gentle smile, was a man of lower stature than her own line of sight.
Originally, being tall, Frederica often exceeded men in height, but the man before her was short in stature even setting that aside—and yet, in contrast to his height, Frederica had come to know over these past few days that he possessed a large-heartedness and was someone easy to feel goodwill toward.

"—Zikr-sama, I gratefully accept your consideration."

On the top floor of the city hall, amid the bustle of people moving busily about, Frederica gratefully received the tea the man—Zikr Osman—had brewed for her with his own hands.
A fragrance carrying soft warmth slipped into her nostrils, and Frederica smiled without meaning to.

Originally, such a role should have been Frederica's own, as a maid. But Frederica's assigned role in the empire made her hesitate to engage in the work she'd been steeped in for so many years.
Nominally, Frederica's position in the empire was that of a bodyguard for a person of importance—and thinking on it now, since Otto and Garfiel's positions hadn't changed either, there was probably no need for Frederica alone to have set aside her role as a maid, but she'd realized this rather belatedly.
So, obediently, she allowed herself this once to indulge in the kindness of the heavily-responsible commander brewing tea himself, and quietly focused on wetting her tongue and throat.
Indeed, true to having taken the initiative himself, Zikr's skill in brewing tea was impressive.

"I suppose those who become 'Generals' of the empire are skilled at such things too."

"Ha ha, I'm honored by the compliment. Though I can't vouch for the taste of the other 'Generals'' tea. In my case, I was trained by a great many older and younger sisters back home."

"My, my. Then I suppose I must thank your older and younger sisters, Zikr-sama. Even in such a hectic situation, I get to enjoy the taste of good tea."

"Indeed, one never knows what will prove useful in life... though of course, I put more effort into it when preparing it for a beautiful lady like Frederica-jou than I ever did for my family."

There was no ulterior motive in Zikr's casual way of speaking, and Frederica found herself naturally accepting the compliment as well.
Perhaps it was the influence of having spent his life among so many sisters—his attitude of praising women, without a trace of falsehood in his words, carried a deep sense of respect that was almost ticklish to be on the receiving end of.
At first, when she'd been told that the city's representative was a man known for being a 'womanizer,' she'd had no choice but to approach him with considerable tension and wariness.

"It would be a problem if anything happened to Emilie, or Lady Petra, or Lady Beatrice, wouldn't it."

Still, that had been a concern dispelled the moment she'd actually met Zikr.
No doubt the fact that he and the others had thrown themselves into rescue efforts amid the confusion of the city struck by the Flying Dragon Cataclysm had played a part, but Zikr and his people had welcomed Frederica's group warmly.
She was grateful, too, for Otto's judgment in negotiating with the city's representatives right away upon their arrival.

"I hear he won over Mizelda-san the moment they met—Otto-sama really is dependable at times like this, isn't he."

Frederica nodded quietly, harboring a thought that, had Otto himself heard it, would probably have made his voice crack as he protested, "That's, um, a bit of a mischaracterization, I think!"
Even thinking this way, Frederica had been considerably helped by Otto's presence.
After all, in Emilia's current party, the responsibilities entrusted to Frederica and Otto, the elders of the group, were quite heavy—since Roswaal and Ram were off acting separately.

"——"

In searching the empire for the whereabouts of Subaru and Rem, the party that had successfully smuggled itself across the border had split into two groups to act.
That division consisted of Roswaal and Ram on one side, and the other six of them, including Frederica, on the other.

Separating from the Emilia group, who were searching for Subaru and the others through painstaking legwork and inquiries, Roswaal and Ram had headed for the residence of an imperial noble said to have connections with Roswaal.
Frederica had never quite been able to grasp who Roswaal was on close terms with to begin with, but she hadn't even imagined that his reach extended as far as the empire.
In any case, since Roswaal had the option of relying on an acquaintance, he'd gone off to negotiate in that direction, leaving Frederica's group to search for the two missing people from a different angle.
As a result, the responsibilities placed on Frederica and Otto had naturally grown heavier.

"Lady Emilia was resolved that she herself had to do her best."

Recalling Emilia's enthusiasm, brimming with motivation and a sense of responsibility, insisting she was the eldest and so had to push herself, Frederica's lips curved into a smile.
If seniority by age were to be strictly applied to the Emilia Camp, the eldest would be Puck and Beatrice, followed next by Emilia.
Placed in a situation separated from Subaru, and with Mana replenishment difficult, Beatrice remained in a state of 'energy conservation' to curb her consumption, spending most of each day asleep. Because of that, Emilia, next in line by age, had been the one to rally herself to do her best.

Of course, while the final decision-making authority rested with Emilia, the discussions leading up to that point had been carefully weighed mainly among Frederica, Otto, and Petra.
The reason they'd made their way to the walled city in the first place was because of a rumor that had reached their ears, one almost too incredible to believe.

"A black-haired maiden supporting the ringleader of the rebel army that raised its banner against the empire..."

When she'd heard that this was the name of a woman called 'Natsumi Schwartz,' Frederica's mind had gone completely blank, unable to make sense of it.
Could it be that someone existed in the empire bearing the very same name as someone Frederica herself recognized?

"If Otto-sama hadn't realized the true meaning, I wonder if I would ever have thought of it."

In the end, it was Otto who had unraveled the truth—that the name of the person spreading through the rumors was a message from Subaru.

"That guy's got this notion that cross-dressing is some kind of universal solution... and honestly, it's scary that it seems to actually work often enough to reinforce that belief."

Such had been Otto's remark, his expression somewhere between exasperation and grudging acceptance.
Presumably, since Subaru was something of a known figure even in the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica, this had been a measure to avoid his name coming up in the Vollachia Empire—that was the conclusion the group had reached.
Emilia alone, until the very end, had kept tilting her head, wondering "Why did they use Natsumi's name...?" over the fact that the name of a woman she knew had been used.
In any case—

"It shouldn't be much longer before Natsumi-jou returns as well. At long last, Emilie-jou and Petra-jou's wishes will be granted."

"—Yes, truly."

Enjoying the fragrance of the tea, Zikr nodded toward Frederica as she sank into thought.
Just as he said, the two people they were searching for—at the very least, a reunion with Subaru—was expected to come true before long.

Frederica and the others had correctly interpreted the name Natsumi Schwartz as a message, and indeed, Subaru's presence had been confirmed in this walled city.
The problem was that he'd already left the city to resolve some other matter, and the situation that had befallen Rem, the other person they were searching for—

"...If only we had been able to protect Rem-jou."

"In a situation like that, there's no reason for Zikr-sama or anyone else to be blamed. Though of course, it's regrettable."

At Zikr's downcast words, Frederica shook her head slowly.
When Frederica's group had reached the city, it wasn't only Subaru who had gone missing—Rem had too. And what's more, Rem's disappearance had occurred in the very midst of the Flying Dragon Cataclysm.
In order to drive back the 'Nine Divine General' who had attacked the city, she had apparently offered herself up as a hostage.

"...To think, right upon waking, she'd already convince us so thoroughly that she's Ram's sister."

Worrying over Rem's safety, such was the impression that slipped from Frederica's lips.
Frederica had no memory of Rem, whose 'name' and 'memories' had been stolen by the Authority of the Sin Archbishop of 'Gluttony.' At the very least, she had no memories shared with the Rem who had awoken.
What she knew was the character Subaru had described, and the fact that her appearance was the very image of her twin sister Ram. But it seemed her boldness, too, resembled Ram's.
Otherwise, she surely could never have carried out negotiations that refused to yield an inch against a 'Nine Divine General,' saving a city whose very survival had been placed at risk.

"——"

Having accepted those negotiating terms, the other side had reason to keep Rem alive.
Believing that, logically, was the one small comfort available. Without it, Frederica and the others would surely have gone chasing after the vanished Rem's trail immediately.
Of course, if she were honest, even now she was desperate to rush out after her.

"If Ram were here, she'd have done exactly that... no, that girl is clever, so perhaps she wouldn't have."

It could go either way—that was Frederica's assessment, knowing Ram well.
Ram was wise. But she was also deeply affectionate. Frederica knew that she had visited her sister's room, night after night, as much as or even more than Subaru, who visited Rem each evening.
She had wanted them to be reunited. —However impudent she could be, Ram was still a beloved little sister to her.

"Frederica-jou, please don't torment yourself too much. The city's situation has settled down for the time being. At long last, we can discuss what comes next."

Gently, mindful of Frederica placing a hand over her own chest, Zikr spoke up to her like that.
Just as he said, the scars the Flying Dragons had left on the walled city had finally begun to heal, and the situation had come together enough to discuss what lay ahead. Of course, what came next would depend on the direction the discussions took—but—

"—Second General Zikr!"

Just as Frederica was about to answer Zikr's words.
A voice, sharp but taut with a feeling distinct from mere urgency, rang out through the city hall. It was one of Zikr's subordinates, dashing up the stairs and hurrying toward them.
As Zikr turned and asked, "What is it," the man, pressing his hand and fist together in front of his chest, said:

"Reporting. Numerous carriages have arrived at the southern side of the city, all of them from the Magic City."

"—! From the Magic City. Then!"

At the report to Zikr, Frederica reacted unconsciously, leaning forward. Ordinarily, embarrassment would have won out, but this time she couldn't bring herself to care.
At Frederica's eagerness, Zikr nodded deeply as well,

"So he has returned. As expected of a man who never misses his timing. Summon Lady Priscilla and Lady Petra and the others at once."

"Yes, sir!"

At the happy news that the one they'd been awaiting had returned, Frederica's heart quickened as well.
The spirits that had begun to sink just moments before, worrying over Rem's safety, lifted no small amount. At least, for now, half of their purpose would be fulfilled.

"Wonderful news, Frederica-jou."

At the sight of Frederica, relief washing over her, Zikr smiled gently as well.
To his words, Frederica was about to return "Yes" along with words of thanks.
But—

"—Second General, actually, about that—"

Cutting off Frederica's words, the imperial soldier spoke first. And what's more, it was said with an opening that carried a note of unease, and Frederica couldn't help but catch her breath.
Zikr too seemed to share the same concern, and turning toward his subordinate who had spoken in a low voice, he asked, his tone dropping slightly, "What is it."
In response, the subordinate hesitated for a brief moment before—

"Regarding the carriages that returned from the Magic City—Lady Natsumi Schwartz's figure was not among them... it seems that in Chaosflame, her whereabouts became unknown."

So it was, that the whims of fate cruelly declared themselves to continue still.
