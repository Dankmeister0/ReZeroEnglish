Arc 7 Chapter 27: "Those Who Have, and Those Who Have Not"

"If you're alive, you get a chance to wipe out the shame later. But if you're dead, that's the end of it. So that's why I'm leaving. I'm not getting on board with a fight I can't win."

There was no lie in those words.
Throwing yourself into a battle with slim odds of victory was the kind of folly only an unbelievable fool would commit.
He didn't think himself clever in the slightest, but precisely because he wasn't clever, he had to judge things carefully. A conclusion a sage might reach in an instant, a fool had to arrive at the long way around, in his own foolish fashion.

That, Todd knew, was how the have-nots fought.

You couldn't get on board with a fight you couldn't win.
But, put the other way around—

"—Which means, if it's a fight I *can* win, I get on board. That's the story, and there's no mistake about it."

He brought his clenched fist up in front of his right eye, peering through the narrow gap he'd left, looking far into the distance beyond.
A primitive method — narrowing your field of view to see farther. Todd's eyes could see quite far when he squinted, but even so, burning banners aside, he couldn't make out what exactly was happening inside the chaos of the city hall.
Even given how much more "ventilated" the situation had become.

"Oi, the roof of the city hall is gone! What the hell's going on!?"

Right beside him, his ferocious companion — restless with pent-up eagerness — was making a racket.
It was noisy and broke his concentration, but he waved a hand to signal *shut up*. What was happening was something he himself was seriously trying to work out.

"The 'Nine Divine Generals' sending someone in — that much has to be certain."

Otherwise, there'd have been no point in staying put in a fortress city that had already fallen into enemy hands.
As mentioned before, the moment the city hall fell to the enemy's scheme, Todd had intended to abandon the city and flee. He had no intention of obediently disarming and surrendering either.
To begin with, so long as the enemy's mastermind was that self-proclaimed child of war — the one calling himself Natsumi Schwartz — dangerous elements like Todd and Jamal were bound to be eliminated first.

Extrajudicial killing of surrendered prisoners was frowned upon even in Vollachia, but that man would surely trump up some excuse and have them executed anyway.
At the very least, that's what Todd himself would do. It was only reasonable caution, if you wanted to stay alive.

So he'd chosen flight first and foremost, resolving to abandon the fortress city without hesitation. And yet, he'd reversed that choice and stayed — because he'd confirmed the presence of "reinforcements" heading toward the city.

"No mistake about it. Two years ago, when I served in the campaign to subjugate the savages, I saw that woman. — She was the 'Ni,' General Arakiya."

Watching the lightly dressed woman vault effortlessly over the closed main gate and stroll into the city as though she owned it, Jamal snorted hard and declared this with certainty.
He was a man who might as well have been wild instinct wearing clothes. Whether in terms of raw strength as a living creature, or a male's instinct for sizing up a female, he wasn't the type to forget someone who'd once struck a chord with him.
In other words, one of the empire's strongest — one of the "Nine Divine Generals" — had appeared to put down the rebellion. This, without question, was the "chance of victory" Todd had judged had vanished.
And what's more—

"Whether to get on board or refuse — that's more than worth agonizing over."

Natsumi's schemes and tricks were dazzling, but at the very least, the man himself, whom Todd had crossed paths with three times now, had no direct combat ability. Even the "People of Shudrak" who followed him, known for their toughness, were only tough by the standards of ordinary people.
True powerhouses, the "Nine Divine Generals" chief among them, existed outside that kind of measuring stick.

Even Jamal, whose only real virtue was his terrifying swordsmanship and combat strength, was worth maybe ten regular Imperial soldiers. And that, too, was a yardstick meant for measuring ordinary people.
The ones who could toy with Jamal without effort were the extraordinary ones, and the "Nine Divine Generals" were the very embodiment of that. — The fallen city hall would be retaken.

"Then it's wise to get on the winning horse."

There was no need to actually coordinate with the "Nine Divine Generals."
It would be enough to be more useful to the general than the city hall's soldiers, who'd been captured and rendered useless. Restrain the enemy, interrogate them, and if command authority slipped their way by default, the general's opinion of them would improve too.
Do that, and he might get promoted, and the road back to the imperial capital might open up sooner than expected.

"Right, we're turning back, Jamal. We're going to help retake the city hall."

"Oh? Oh, is that right! Kuhaha, that works for me. Running away never suited me anyway, itched all over the whole time. Like hell I'm letting the general take all the glory!"

"Don't be stupid. We're the ones mooching off the general's leftovers."

Jamal clenched his fists at the change of plan, delighted at the prospect of returning to a death zone. Todd sighed at the sight but went back to the city hall with him anyway — not charging directly into the building, but observing the situation from a position where he could check on the state of things inside.
Sticking to his own convictions, being careful upon careful upon careful, and then—

"—Kill him, Arakiya."

The top floor of the city hall, blown away as if by a storm wind, and dimly visible within, the general's rampage.
The silver-haired, dark-skinned "Ni," visible from a distance, swung what looked like nothing more than a branch she'd picked up off the ground, and ran wild as though she ruled the very laws of the world.
Shudrak and Imperial soldiers alike, with no distinction, lay collapsed throughout the devastated hall.
And in the midst of that, standing between Arakiya and a blue-haired girl at her back, blocking the way, was none other than Natsumi Schwartz.

"——"

The moment he saw that, the thought that rose in Todd was nothing so slack as *"what a stupid thing to do, standing in front of a general"* or *"impressive that he's still alive after all this."*
Rather, tightening his guard to its utmost, Todd cried out silently within his own heart.

—Kill him here, without fail, Arakiya.

At worst, his own achievements didn't matter anymore. It was fine even if staying put, if all the time he'd spent deliberating over reversing his decision, turned out meaningless.
So long as, without fail, the breath of life was snuffed out of Natsumi Schwartz, the child of war.
That was—

"...Oi oi, you've got to be kidding me."

At the very instant Arakiya was about to kill Natsumi, in the upper part of his field of vision — fixed with total concentration so as not to miss that decisive split second — something flashed, and an instant later, Todd's hopes were shattered.

A flash of red brilliance cut in between Natsumi and Arakiya.
It faced Arakiya head-on, boldly, sheltering Natsumi behind it as an absolute powerhouse standing guard. The moment he saw that, the scales inside Todd tipped violently.

"What the hell just fell out of the sky!? A Flying Dragon!? Whose Flying Dragon!?"

"——"

"Are you listening, Todd! What do we do! Shouldn't we be backing up General Arakiya! Oi! Are you listen—"

"—Shut up, Jamal."

Jamal, raising his voice at the sudden change in the situation, choked on his words at that one line from Todd.
Without turning his eyes toward Jamal, Todd kept his gaze fixed on the scene at the city hall. The woman who had appeared, clad in a crimson dress, stood face to face with Arakiya.
He understood at a glance. — That woman, too, was one of the "haves," a being utterly removed from ordinary people.

And Natsumi, who had seemingly retrieved his own life in what should have been a certain-death situation, was also, Todd now keenly grasped, a "have" — in a form apart from combat power or physical ability.

"——"

Before the red woman had cut in, Arakiya alone would have been more than enough, with plenty to spare.
But now the situation had changed, and the outcome was no longer clear. Would Todd and Jamal, as counterweights alone, be enough to tip the tilted scale back this way?

"Todd...!"

"Don't move, Jamal. — Moving won't change anything now."

The chance of victory that had existed was gone, and the situation only worsened as Todd watched.
He understood Jamal's mounting fury, but charging out now would just be a meaningless death.
Because, after all—

"Just now, General Arakiya went down."

Taking the red woman's blade across her back, Arakiya collapsed helplessly, unable to do a thing.
The tilted scale broke, and it would never again tip back the other way.

"——"

With Arakiya fallen, the affair at the city hall came to an end.
This time, it could truly be said, completely and utterly, that the fortress city of Guaral had fallen into enemy hands.
There was no way to turn things around from here. Surely, even with the "Nine Divine Generals"' "Ni" having been dispatched, there was no chance other reinforcements would come rushing in one after another.

The "Nine Divine Generals" were each worth a thousand soldiers, the very pinnacle of martial might in the Vollachian Empire.

For one of the "Nine Divine Generals" to move was equivalent to moving a great army, and no commander would deploy two or three great armies against a single incident. This was the end of the line.
The scale had tipped all the way over. All that remained now was how to handle the aftermath.

"So, what to do, hm."

In the shadow of a building, watching how things had unfolded at the city hall, Todd quietly pondered.
Honestly, he was seething inside with a mixture of anger and frustration, but venting it would solve nothing.
With the reason for coming back gone, the wise choice would be to make a quick, quiet retreat.
And yet—

"Oi, Todd... You're not seriously thinking of slinking off with your tail between your legs, are you?"

Saying that, Jamal had a vein bulging on his forehead, and Todd couldn't find the words to talk him down.

At first, right after the fall, when the city hall's banner had been burned, persuading Jamal had already been a chore.
Even then, though, it had still been a situation where Jamal's emotions could be steered relatively easily. He'd sheathed his half-drawn sword and, however reluctantly, gone along with it.
But now, having drawn that sheathed blade again and come charging back — this time it wouldn't go so smoothly. Telling him to sheathe his sword once more and quietly flee — he wouldn't listen this time.

In fact, depending on what Todd said next, there was a real chance the drawn blade's point might turn on him instead.
If that happened, there'd be no reason left not to kill Jamal, no matter how you looked at it. — He was a man who was going to become his brother-in-law someday. If at all possible, Todd didn't want to kill him.
Besides, he wouldn't be able to keep the promise he'd made, to bring him home safe and sound.

"Let's try that angle, then."

"Ahh?"

"...I get how you feel. But calm down. With General Arakiya taken down, there's no chance of victory even if we charge in. You want to die for nothing and leave your little sister grieving?"

"——"

He tried invoking family love as a shield to persuade him.
The instant he looked at the other man, hoping this might work, an arm shot out quietly and grabbed him by the collar. Then, thrusting his face in close, one-eyed Jamal bared his fangs.

"You bastard, if you think I'll back down every damn time you bring up Katya, you've got another thing coming."

"I see. ...That's a shame."

Shaking his head slowly, Todd voiced his disappointment, and it was fairly genuine.
If letting the man land two or three punches would settle things, Todd would have let him — but Jamal, clicking his tongue and shoving Todd away, didn't seem inclined to bother.

There didn't seem to be a need to lay hands on him directly, but there was also no way to stop him.
As things stood, Todd considered changing direction along with Jamal and scaling the fortress city's wall instead.

Fortunately, he already had the city's geography mapped out from when they'd been sealing up the escape tunnels.
And Arakiya, though she hadn't achieved her purpose, had at least stirred up chaos to work with. If anything, escaping would be easier now than before Arakiya had ever shown up.
It seemed Jamal, for his part, intended to kick up one more storm and draw attention their way.

"Jamal, sorry, but I'm out. Not that it'll do any good telling you, but you're going to die for nothing. Charging in there, killing every last one of them isn't..."

"You idiot! I'm not doing something I can't pull off! I'm getting General Arakiya out of there."

"...What?"

The sentiment he'd known to be pointless nevertheless dragged an unexpected line out of him.
Stopping his feet, which had been about to carry him away, Todd stared hard at Jamal's face. Jamal, in turn, made an irritated sound in his throat and grunted, "What?"

"Don't tell me you thought I was gonna go in there planning to die gloriously."

"Yeah, I did. I figured you were dead set on dying for nothing."

"The hell're you on about?! I've been going along with your clever little schemes, but I've got a brain of my own, you know! I know the difference between what can be done and what can't."

The remark, wholly unexpected, genuinely surprised Todd.
He'd thought Jamal had his moments worth noting on the battlefield, but otherwise, between his impulsiveness and terrible conduct, Todd had wondered what his head was even good for.

"If you're some spineless coward, fine, whatever. Katya just had no eye for men. I'll drag the general out myself, if I have to. She had a damn fine ass on her, that one."

"—Wait. I'm coming too."

"Ahh!? You can't get satisfaction from Katya's ass, is that it, you—"

"I wasn't going to keep you company on a suicide run, but if that's not what this is, that's a different story."

Ignoring the dishonorable insinuation, Todd clamped a hand over Jamal's mouth. Forcibly silencing him, Todd began building out an action plan in his head, in line with his newly shifted policy.
Normally, working things out on the fly wasn't Todd's style at all. But, sadly enough, through repeated bouts of acting alongside Jamal, his experience with improvisation had grown.

If Jamal wanted to charge in alone and die gloriously trying to retake the city hall, Todd would have left him to it. But if the objective was recovering Arakiya, that was another matter entirely.

"——"

The city hall was gradually moving into the cleanup phase.
The ones to watch out for were Natsumi and the red woman; acting anywhere near them would be suicide. That said, they hadn't come through the affair unscathed either.
If their guard, kept taut this whole time, ever slackened, there'd be an opening to exploit there.

"We charge in and cause a scene. If we use that opening to rescue the general..."

"So much for being impressed with you a minute ago. ...There are two people we have to watch out for. We absolutely cannot make our move anywhere those two are. No, don't worry."

Chiding an impatient Jamal, Todd, peering through the gap between his fingers, let his lips relax. Behind them, he prodded a white canine tooth with the tip of his tongue and smirked to himself.

At the far end of his gaze was Arakiya's form, near death but being carried out.
As long as she wasn't a corpse yet, there was some means or another to get her out of there.

—Sneaking into the city hall was easy.

It had, after all, been a base of operations for them, even if only briefly.
Todd had a habit, ordinarily, of memorizing the layout and geography of any place he'd ever passed through even once. He wasn't the type to feel at ease without knowing his escape routes and hiding spots ahead of time.
So after the encampment in the Buddheim Jungle had been burned down, once he'd entered Guaral, he'd made a point of visiting every corner of the city, city hall included, and hammering the map into his head.

He knew intimately where he could kill someone without being noticed, and where he could hide a body so it wouldn't be found.
You should always have your methods of killing and fleeing ready, so that whatever threat you might run into, wherever, you'd be prepared.

"Guh."

Slipping into the interior of the city hall, he took out the scattered watchmen one by one.
Standing sentry weren't Imperial soldiers or the "People of Shudrak," but the city's own self-defense corps — the autonomous organization that had kept order in the city. Summoned as guards, they were weathervanes now being used by the destabilizing element that had brought down the city — no longer fit to be called that; a proper "rebel army" now.

"I ain't showing you bastards any mercy."

Grabbing a sentry's neck with both hands, Jamal snapped it upside down, spitting the words out irritably.
More than an Imperial noble, Jamal took pride in being an Imperial soldier. His fury toward the sentries who'd sided with a rebel army raising its bow against that same empire was immeasurable.
Given that, up until the city's fall, these were men who'd been working in cooperation with the Imperial soldiers, it wasn't unreasonable that their swift change of allegiance provoked his anger.

"Well, that's not a mindset I share."

He killed only because it was necessary, and if there was no need to kill, he wouldn't. If their too-quick change of coat had just been a matter of siding with the stronger party in order to survive, there was no cause to blame them for it. Of course, they'd still have to pay for a mistaken judgment with their lives.

So, clearing out the troublesome sentries as they went, the two of them advanced toward their target.
In the basement of the city hall was a jail, customarily used to hold criminals awaiting the city lord's judgment. Arakiya, taken prisoner, was very likely to have been placed in that same jail.
Of course, against a "Nine Divine General," an iron cell would be little more than spun sugar — but that didn't mean it was in human nature to instead entertain her as a guest in some parlor room.
Todd himself might even have considered that possibility—

"—Found her. It's the general."

In the underground space they descended into, multiple cells were arranged on either side of the hall. The nearer ones held criminals with lighter sentences; the sturdiest cell was at the very back.
And of course, the innermost cell holding Arakiya was heavily guarded.
Standing watch, unlike before, was not a sentry but a Shudrak woman. A big-bodied woman with yellow-dyed hair; it was clear at a glance she was skilled.
On top of that, given the layout, there was no way to reach Arakiya's cell without being spotted by the woman.
In other words—

"So much for sneaking in bit by bit. This is as far as that gets us."

"...Why the hell do you look so happy about that?"

Faced with an unavoidable fight right in front of him, Jamal's cheerful expression was hard to fathom.
Todd didn't want to kill anyone if he could help it, and didn't want to fight if he could help it either. If both were unavoidable, he'd choose whichever was easier and less risky.

And yet Jamal looked as though he were positively enjoying leaping into danger of his own accord.
Probably he was thinking that the more dangerous the path, the more blood spilled, the more it demonstrated loyalty to the empire, or some such thing.

"Obviously. Fight like an Imperial soldier and seize the fruits of battle! That's how I get to hold my head up and call myself an Imperial soldier."

"——"

"Hah, what're you looking so surprised about."

"No, it's just that it went almost exactly the way I figured."

It was rare to find a person whose words and deeds matched up so consistently.
Jamal looked displeased at Todd's answer but quickly shook his head. Then, tracing his stubble with a finger, he grunted "ahh," and said,

"Speaking of which, you sure did a good job reconsidering, didn't you."

"I'm not keeping you company on a suicide run. If that's not what it is, I'll think it over. I didn't reconsider anything."

"You were about to run, and you didn't."

"I decided to bring back a souvenir while I was at it. My policy hasn't changed."

"You and your damn word games..."

Eyes bloodshot with anger, Jamal ground his teeth.
Paying that no further mind, Todd cut his attention away from him and focused on the target ahead.

The woman standing guard had a thick build, her limbs protected by proportionate muscle. Given Shudrak physical ability, even Todd's hatchet might not take down her limbs in a single blow.
That being the case, the target was inevitably narrowed to the neck and above.
Striking the head, severing the neck, or splitting the skull were all options, but—

"—This is exactly the moment for me to step up, isn't it."

Saying that, Jamal stepped forward slowly, walking straight out into the open with foolish honesty.
Todd hesitated a moment over whether to call him back, but said nothing. In fact, shoving Jamal out to draw attention while moving in during the opening it created was the best move.
If it saved him the trouble and the man himself was eager to do it, there was no need to dampen his enthusiasm.

"Mumu, who're you, Nau!?"

"You think I owe you an answer? You people have sullied the Sword Wolves of the Vollachian Empire. Don't go thinking you've won just because I wasn't on that battlefield!"

"A strange one's shown up, Nau!"

Watching Jamal advance into the hall, the Shudrak woman leveled the great spear at her side. Jamal, facing her, drew his twin swords and danced forward with a maniacal grin.
For all his many behavioral problems, his actual skill was beyond question. At the very least, against someone who was merely a Shudrak, he wasn't going to fall behind.

"There there there there there there there there there!"

"—Ha! A strong one, Nau!"

Bellowing with a raucous voice, his raging twin swords rained down on the Shudrak woman countless times. The woman deftly parried them with her spearwork, but was purely on the defensive.
She'd likely been placed there as a fighter of some competence, to guard Arakiya. But it seemed no one had imagined that someone would show up to try a rescue this soon after locking her up.
Combined with the shortage of trustworthy fighting strength, it was a fatal hole in the rebel army.

"Though that's not something we can put to use right this moment."

Wetting his lips with his tongue, Todd sprang out a beat behind Jamal, slipping past the two of them as they exchanged fierce, sparking blows, and dove straight for the cell.

"Ah! Our comrade's...kyau!"

"You got time to look away? Ahh!?"

Aided by Jamal, unusually quick-witted for once, Todd swung his hatchet down on the cell's lock with all his strength.
There was no time to look for a key. He couldn't break down the cell itself, but a lock should be breakable.

There was a dull sound and a hard jolt of resistance, and the hatchet's edge was badly bent out of shape. But in exchange, the cell's lock broke apart spectacularly, and with a creak it swung open; Todd dove inside.

"General Arakiya!"

Inside the cell, laid on a simple cot, was a single girl lying face down.
Even serving in the same Imperial army, an ordinary foot soldier had few chances to interact with a "General." Even a Sanshō was the commander of an entire army. A Nishō, let alone an Ichishō, was a being above the clouds.
So this was the first time Todd had ever seen a General — a member of the "Nine Divine Generals" — up close.

"——"

Arakiya, unconscious, had been laid face down because of the wound cut across her back. Painful gash marks were carved into that back, and on top of that, cauterized shut by fire.
The wound had apparently been sliced and cauterized at the same time, resulting in a gruesome scar. — Wounds like this wouldn't happen unless inflicted with a heated blade.

"That red woman... what did she..."

She'd been no ordinary woman, that much was clear, and the sacred sword gripped in her hand had possessed extraordinary power to match.
There was no further detailed information to be had, and Arakiya gave no response to being called. Left with no choice, Todd scooped her body up and dashed straight back out of the cell.

"Recovered! Jamal, let's go!"

"Like hell I'll let you... agh!?"

"So! Quit! Looking! Away! From! Meeeee—!!"

With Arakiya taken, the Shudrak woman's attention slipped for a single instant.
In that instant, Jamal drove a slash from his twin swords into the gap in her awareness; the woman hastily brought her great spear up vertically to block it. The impact wrenched the spear from her grip, and with her torso wide open, Jamal's leaping back-spin kick drove home.

Letting out a small cry, the woman's body was sent flying, slamming hard into the underground jail's wall. Striking her head hard, she collapsed limply and stopped moving.
Seeing this, Todd was about to order the finishing blow, but—

"—Tch, it's gotten noisy up above. They must've found the sentries' bodies."

"Damn it, no time to dawdle. The general?"

"Unconscious, but not dead. That'll do."

Answering Jamal's question briefly, Todd ran out of the underground jail. Overtaking him effortlessly, Jamal took the lead, taking on the role of clearing the way ahead.

"Just who the hell...guoh!?"

"Move, move, you half-witted dolts!"

A sentry peering down into the basement took a slash and was sent flying; with the city hall's alarm rising, Todd raced along, chasing after Jamal's back.
Sorry as he was about it, there was no room to spare consideration for the body cradled in his arms. If she was one of the "Nine Divine Generals," her body was probably built sturdy enough. All he could do was trust in her endurance and run.

"They're out! Where are they headed!"

"The main gate'll be sealed off. — Follow me."

Weaving through the city's uproarious night, Todd led Jamal into the back alleys. Using narrow paths and side streets, he threw off the eyes of the enemies giving chase.
In a situation right after a battle had ended, on a battlefield where the confusion hadn't yet settled, and with Imperial soldiers in identical uniforms numbering some three hundred still in the city by sheer count alone — there was no telling them apart.
All that remained was—

"—Tch!"

The instant he heard the whistle of wind cutting through the air, a blade swung right behind Todd.
Turning, he saw a single thick, large arrow buried in the ground at his feet. Severed and knocked down, it had been aimed at Todd with tremendous force, and it was Jamal who'd instantly dealt with it.

Within the city, a shot precisely aimed at Todd and the others as they fled and hid.
Without a doubt, it had to be the same archer who had shot clean through Todd's torso a few days ago.

—They were being watched.

If that was the case, they couldn't afford to move carelessly.
Step out of the alley and they'd be made a target; carrying Arakiya, Todd couldn't move nimbly either. Trying to kill the archer was out too — given the angle, the enemy's position was back at the city hall — going back there a third time wasn't an option.
Then, abandon Arakiya and flee? That would offer the best odds of saving their own lives, but if he did that, it would leave him wondering just what he'd risked all this danger for in the first place.
Weighing the current situation, searching for available options, the one with the greatest yield was—

"—Jamal, you can tell we're being targeted, right?"

"Yeah, troublesome bunch. Too far to go kill 'em. At this rate, we'll just be picked off one-sided. What do we do?"

"...There's only one option."

Jamal's single eye narrowed sharply at Todd's words.
Facing Jamal's gaze, seeking a plan, Todd let out a deep breath and closed one eye.

"The moment we break out into the open, the enemy's going to target us for the kill. I need you to take point and cut down the arrows again, same as before. Not just one shot — two, three in a row. I'll run at full speed so as not to drop the general."

"Hah, not like you. That's your plan? Sounds like a desperate gamble."

"Ultimately, when you run out of cards to play, that's the only option left. But I'm still lucky, aren't I?"

"Ahn? Where's the luck in that."

"You've still got a pretty strong card left in your hand, that's where."

The plan he'd laid out was, more or less, wholly entrusted to Jamal's swordsmanship.
If Jamal couldn't cut down the incoming arrows, they'd both die on the spot. Betting their lives on such recklessness went against every one of Todd's principles — insanity, by his own standards.
And yet, he proposed it anyway. With Jamal's swordsmanship, the possibility wasn't zero.

"...Katya really did have no eye for men, after all. I figured you for someone smarter than this."

"Don't badmouth my fiancée, big brother."

Scratching his head, Jamal got that answer from Todd, whose cheek twisted into a wry expression. Hearing it, Jamal let out a short "hah" of breath, then re-gripped the hilts of his twin swords.
Then, turning that sturdy back toward Todd, he said,

"Fine, I'm in. Every once in a while, a stupid gamble like this isn't so bad."

"If it weren't for me, you'd probably be making gambles like this all the time."

"Shut it. — You just keep quiet and stick to my back."

Trading barbs, Jamal quietly raised his fighting spirit.
Watching that back, Todd readjusted his hold on Arakiya's body, and,

"Jamal, once we're out of the alley, run straight ahead. When you hit the dead end, take the road on the right. There, we should get a moment to catch our breath."

Hearing those instructions, Jamal turned them over in his head, closed his eyes slowly, then opened them once more and stepped forward.

"—Ha!"

The instant they burst out of the alley, an arrow wrapped in fierce wind plunged straight at Jamal.
He responded with astonishing reflexes, bringing his twin swords together to cut it down. The impact shot back through his wrists; grinding his back teeth, he laughed like a mad dog.

Blood burning, heart leaping, life boiling over — the sensation ruled Jamal completely.

Extreme concentration slowed the world down; he felt he could sense every drop of sweat trailing down his skin, every grain of dancing sand, even the very shape of the air itself, which should have been invisible.

"—Hahaa!"

One after another, a storm of arrows poured down like rain.
Planting his feet on the ground, moving as if dancing, he swung his swords, slicing arrows apart, batting them down. What unfolded was a sword dance — the sword dance of Jamal Aurélie.

By rights, he'd been supposed to enjoy a dancing girl's performance at the city hall the following day.
That plan had fallen through, and instead here he was, dancing himself — Jamal laughed at the thought. But it was a dance, a performance, a display of swordsmanship given with everything he had.

Even while ferociously fending off the assault, Jamal marveled at Todd's desperate effort as well.
Amid a storm of lethal arrows raining down like a tempest, Todd followed along without so much as a sound. Proof that he understood — if Jamal's attention wavered even slightly, it would lead directly to death.
So Jamal drove Todd from his mind entirely, pouring every ounce of himself into evading the approaching "death."

Straight ahead, dodge, deflect, close the distance, leap, sweep aside, cut through.

"The dead end—!"

Miraculously surviving that straight run through death, he reached the dead end of the road as instructed.
The passage of time had grown hazy; what must have been mere seconds had felt like hours of sword-dancing. But this was only the first checkpoint cleared. Refusing to let his guard down, Jamal turned right at the dead end as told, and there—

—Beyond the turn, a group of Shudrak stood blocking the way, spears leveled at them.

"—Damn it."

Targeted by the archer, dealing with that many Shudrak at once would be difficult. Call it impossible.
Of course, he was resolved to fight to the last of his strength regardless — but exhausting himself would leave nothing behind to show for it. To be outflanked this perfectly — it seemed his luck had abandoned him completely.

"Play it clever all you like, and in the end you get cut off by luck, huh. ...Ha, what a hollow thing. But it wasn't bad."

Confirming the feel of the twin swords in his blood-slicked hands, Jamal muttered that to Todd behind him.
"Wasn't bad" wasn't sour grapes — it was genuine.

In all sorts of ways, Todd's thinking and actions had jerked him around, irritated him plenty of times.
But in the end, he too had chosen to wring out every last drop of his own strength, like a proper Imperial soldier.

"Sorry to do this to Katya, but it can't be helped. She's an Imperial noble too, even if a minor one. She probably braced herself for the chance that you and I would end up like this."

Thinking of the little sister he'd left behind in the imperial capital, Jamal felt a faint pang in his chest. But it was immediately swallowed up by his fighting spirit against the enemy before him, the smell of blood painting over everything.
And feeling that happen, he felt relief. — He was, to the marrow of his bones, a Sword Wolf of the Vollachian Empire.

"Let's do this, Todd. At the very least, let's give them something to remember us by, right to the end."

Leaning forward into a crouch, Jamal licked the blood trailing from beneath the eyepatch over his right eye.
And then, ferociously, to demonstrate an Imperial soldier's dignity to the very last, he charged headlong into the enemy.

A storm of fatal blows rained down, but he no longer felt any regret at all.
That he'd been able to remain himself right up to the very last moment — that, to Jamal, was reward enough, above all else.


△▼△▼△▼△


"...Right to the very last, you really were an idiot, weren't you."

Listening to Jamal's fierce roar in the distance, Todd murmured this as he slipped through a wall.
The hole they'd passed through was sealed immediately, and he took care to erase every trace so they couldn't be followed. Jamal would likely keep the city's attention tied up for a good while, which meant Todd had time to escape.

Not like him, Jamal had said, and he was right.
Staking your life on a desperate, all-or-nothing plan — that was something Todd would absolutely never do, not even if it meant dying. No — precisely in order not to die, that was exactly the kind of thing he'd never do.

"Get them chasing after you the moment you burst out of the alley, and that pulls their attention away from me. Well, sorry to Katya about this, but..."

The promise to bring her brother back would go unfulfilled, and his fiancée would surely be devastated.
To comfort her, if nothing else, he wanted to get back to the imperial capital as soon as possible. Fortunately, in exchange for losing Jamal, he'd gained another means of getting back there.

And what's more, a trump card that seemed likely to lead to a far, far bigger foothold than even Jamal — whose promotion to Sanshō had been uncertain at best.

"...Hime... sama."

In Todd's arms, tears spilled from Arakiya's closed eyes. Watching those tears trail down her cheek, Todd thought absently that, come to think of it, his companion had an eyepatch on too.
And then, after thinking that, he found himself, quite suddenly, tilting his head.

And then—


Which side had Jamal's eyepatch been on again...?
