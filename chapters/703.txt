Arc 9 Chapter 8: "How to Regret"

"I heaard. That blue-haired big sister's woken up, hasn't she? Big brother, you were worried the whooole time, so I bet you're relieved, huh?"

"Yeah, I can still remember the fresh emotion of the moment Rem opened her eyes. Even getting my finger broken right after — it's a fond memory now."

"……Hey, Petra-chan. Big brother's gotten weirder since he flew off somewhere, hasn't he?"

"Um, a lot of things happened. It's a long story, but there was cross-dressing, and there was a time he got as small as Beatrice-chan…"

"Huh?"

Meili, propping her cheek on her hand at the counter, having just rejoined them, made a dubious face.
Subaru wanted to protest being treated like a weirdo, but hearing even just the surface of what had happened to him in the Empire, Meili's reaction was only natural. The connection between the situation he'd been placed in and how it all unfolded felt pretty thin even to Subaru himself, enough to make him want to tilt his own head in confusion.

"Well, if I explained it from the start, it'd all make perfect sense."

"……Would it, though? Betty heard the whole thing from the start too, and thought it turned into something hard to follow right around when Subaru cross-dressed again."

"Why's that the part that trips you up, that's the most logically consistent part of the whole thing…"

The operation to take the walled city, which for some reason nobody understood — the results proved that having Subaru, Abel, and Flop all cross-dress was the best strategy.
But that earnest appeal of Subaru's didn't seem to land at all with Beatrice, Petra, or Meili, who was now accepting the cold milk she'd ordered.
Anyway—

"Still, thoughIi, going right back to that tower again as soon as you got back… won't big brother get sent flying somewhere again and make Petra-chan lonely?"

"That scary discussion's already been had, plenty of times… well, not plenty, but at least once. At least, the cause of the glitch that sent me flying — I don't think that'll happen again."

"S-somehow that makes me even more uneasy…"

Under Meili and Petra's worried gazes, Subaru resettled Beatrice in his lap — since Beatrice's original seat had been taken over by Meili, Subaru now sat with Petra and Meili on either side and Beatrice on his knee — and gave it some thought.

—To begin with, the incident that caused Subaru to be flung to the Vollachian Empire was triggered by a badly irregular event that occurred at the Pleiades Watchtower.

At that time, right before things happened, an irregularity occurred in the tower's green room. Spica — at that point believed to be Rui — appeared, and in the midst of the flustered response to that, the situation took a sudden turn.
That was—

"—A mistaken application of the penalty for speaking the words 'Return by Death.'"

That's what Subaru guessed.
Once before, at the Sanctuary, he'd attended Echidna's tea party and confessed the truth of "Return by Death" to her. The result had been disastrous, but the disaster wasn't just Echidna's rotten treatment of him — it was also the real-world time that had passed while he was at the tea party.
His confession of "Return by Death" to Echidna had triggered a penalty, and the entire Sanctuary had been engulfed by the threat of black shadows, driven to the brink of ruin. — Subaru guessed this was the same thing.

In short, Rui Arneb, who'd encountered Subaru in the Corridor of Memories and learned of "Return by Death," had appeared in the green room for some reason.
And the black shadow — the Witch of Envy — had mistakenly moved to eliminate her.
And then, as a result of the Witch of Envy's rampage colliding with the power of the Divine Dragon Volcanica, who bore the duty of guarding the tower, Subaru had been blown away by the resulting force field—

"—that's how I figure it, but past that point it's all just guesswork."

Ultimately, why Subaru had been sent flying to the forest of the People of Shudrak in the Empire remained unknown.
Whether it had been accidental, or whether the will of the Witch of Envy or the Divine Dragon had been at work, wasn't clear. But given what happened afterward, he didn't think it was the Witch of Envy.
The Witch of Envy had lost track of Subaru until he'd called out to her himself, on the Sword-Slave Island.

—There, for the first time, Subaru had called out to the Witch of Envy — to Satella — of his own will.

Since then, the nature of "Return by Death" hadn't changed at all, but within Subaru, his feelings toward the Witch of Envy — the one who'd presumably brought this on him — had shifted, just slightly.
That shift was—

"—Subaru? Is something wrong with your eyes?"

Turning around in the arms holding her, in the midst of Subaru's silent brooding, Beatrice pressed a finger between his eyebrows. At the sensation, and her words, Subaru gave a wry smile.

"Bit late to bring up my eyes being bad. Normally people'd just say my face is scary."

"Once you get used to it, Subaru's face is full of charm, I suppose. That impression of bad eyes is nonsense from people who don't know Subaru, I suppose."

"Yeah, yeah, Beatrice-chan, well said. Right, Meili-chan?"

"Weeell. There's a part of him that's about as cute as the witchbeasts, isn't there?"

"Bea-ko and Petra are soft on me, so Meili's opinion's probably the normal take. I've lived with these eyes for eighteen years. I'm not expecting any weird surprises…!"

Even the standard other-world trope of a "beauty-ugliness reversal" development — one year-plus of experience had already proven that wasn't something he could hope for either.
Just as Subaru's own aesthetic sense told him: the beautiful people were beautiful, and Subaru's eyes were the most terrible of all.

"Never mind my eyes. Anyway, looks like there's no worry about getting sent flying, so no need to worry about me going to the tower and causing trouble for everyone — at least for now."

"Riight. Well, it's fine by me either way. While big brother was gone, I was working haaard, you know."

"Working…? Wait, wait! Meili-chan!"

Somewhat proudly, Meili made a sly grin, and Petra's eyes lit up.
Subaru too had heard what Meili had been doing, having stayed in the Kingdom while the others made their way back from the Empire.
She'd been selling the Kingdom's higher-ups on her own ability to safely guide people across the Augria Sand Dunes on the way to the Pleiades Watchtower — that was supposed to be what she was negotiating.
In exchange for a pardon for the crimes she'd committed in the Kingdom up to now, and a guarantee for her future livelihood.

"So, judging by that tone…"

"Thaat's right, I went and did it. The country's big shots… the Council of Wise Men, was it? I made those old men promise me my freedom."

"Yay! You did it, Meili-chan!"

"Eeek!"

Meili made a textbook smug face, and at her report Subaru's eyes went wide, while Petra, delighted even more than Subaru, reached out past him and took Meili's hand.
As a result, the two girls ended up holding hands right over Beatrice's stomach, and—

"I'll ride the wave and quietly add my hand too."

"It tickles, but Betty will too, I suppose. Well done, I suppose."

Subaru and Beatrice joined in as well, and the four of them ended up with their hands stacked together in a friendly pile.
The shopkeeper across the counter was looking at the sight with a face like he was seeing something strange, but Subaru consciously ignored him — worrying too much about the eyes around you at a moment of happy news wasn't good.
With their hands stacked like that, Meili said in a small voice, "T-thank yoou…" and then—

"S-so, anyway, my power to control my witchbeasts is something the Kingdom values, you knoow. I'm doing them a favor by using it, so you'd better be grateful."

"Whoa, big attitude. But that's fine, I don't hate a big mouth. And of course I'm plenty grateful, always have been, still am. Really, you've reflected well, you have."

"What are you grateful for and where!? You want to get pushed off again!?"

Tit for tat — Subaru knew Meili's comeback was just her hiding her embarrassment, but the memory of that harrowing tumble down the stairs came rushing back, and every bone in his body creaked with the old memory.
But more than that, the one who caught Meili's slip of the tongue just now was—

"Meili-chan? What do you mean, pushed off? What did you do?"

"Ah……"

"Hey, come to think of it, I never really heard properly what happened at the tower. What happened? You apologized for not being able to stop Subaru from getting sent flying, but is there something else you need to apologize for?"

"A-um, Petra-chan, your eyes are a little scary right now…"

"Meili-chan."

"Eep!"

Petra's hand, still resting on top of Beatrice's stomach, tightened its grip on Meili's, refusing to let her escape.
Caught like that, Meili turned pleading eyes toward Subaru for help—

"B-big brother… you too, say something…"

"Sorry, Meili. Looks like the Council of Wise Men's esteemed elders are willing to overlook things for your rare power, but your friend here's going to give you a proper scolding. A friend like that is precious, you know."

"Regret doesn't come before the fact, I suppose. Might as well take the lesson while you can, I suppose."

"Hey now!"

Saying that, Subaru pulled his chair back with Beatrice in his arms and retreated from between Petra and Meili. Right after, Petra closed the gap between the chairs, and Meili had no way to escape.
Watching the two of them, Subaru and Beatrice sipped their milk together—

"Betty's glad Meili could be saved that time, I suppose."

"……Thanks."

Beatrice, voicing Subaru's own feelings in that small voice, had her head stroked by Subaru with affection as he watched the endearing exchange between the girls.

—On top of the teary-eyed Meili's head, a small scorpion clicked its pincers, snip-snip.

—The duty entrusted to Garfiel Tinzel was a heavy one.

Garfiel understood, painfully, the trust his comrades had placed on his shoulders.
He was trusted. Trusted that with Garfiel around, Subaru, Beatrice, Petra, and the rest of the party heading to the Pleiades Watchtower wouldn't come to harm.
And Garfiel, too, was resolved to answer that trust — to prove himself worthy of it.

"Yo, you'd best drink some water. The sand wind's dryin' out the air somethin' fierce. No tellin' when your throat's gonna go bone dry."

Saying that, Garfiel called out to Al, seated at the back of the dragon carriage.
Inside the single carriage pulled by Patrasche, Al sat on the seat with one knee drawn up — the man who was, in a sense, the reason or the cause of this whole journey to the Pleiades Watchtower.

A Royal Selection candidate's knight, once in service to Priscilla Barielle. He'd lost his liege right before his eyes, and now sought the tower's Book of the Dead to fill the emptiness that loss had left.
Whether that was a good thing or a bad thing, Garfiel didn't know.

"――――"

At first, when he'd heard about the Pleiades Watchtower's Book of the Dead, honestly, Garfiel had wanted very much to lay eyes on those books himself.
Given the circumstances — the rush to go rescue Subaru and the others who'd been flung to the Empire — there'd been no chance to seriously discuss it, but he really had thought that.
Garfiel had always liked reading about great figures from history. He remembered reading The Legend of Reid Astrea — the chronicle of the life of Reid Astrea, whom Subaru and the others had actually met in person — until the pages wore thin, back at the Sanctuary.
Not just Reid; hearing that the memories of countless historical greats, in life, were lined up row upon row in the archive housing the Books of the Dead — that alone was enough to make his heart race with anticipation.

So what principle was it, exactly, that made Garfiel feel a faint resistance to Al wanting to read Priscilla's Book of the Dead?
Was it fine because it was someone he knew? Was it bad because it was someone he knew? — Neither Garfiel, nor it seemed even Subaru, had the right answer to that.
So—

"Can't have you droppin' before you get your business done at the tower. Here."

Clicking his canines together, Garfiel tossed his water flask to Al. It spun through the air, drawing a slow arc toward him—

"—Oh, thanks, kid. My throat was just about bone dry."

Catching the flask with a light smack, Al deftly used just his right hand to lift the visor of his helmet and slip the mouthpiece gently between his lips.
He drank with considerable force, then let out an appreciative breath. "Ahh."

"Whew, revived, revived. Really, I'm nothin' but grateful for how much you all take care of me. Especially when I'm the one who went and split up the Brothers' team, that oughta be frowned on."

"Cut it out, you even realize that?"

"Well, it's not like I'm oblivious. Better than some old man who up and disappeared, anyway — calm, calm."

"――――"

Shaking the nearly-emptied flask as he answered, Al drew a scowl from Garfiel, wrinkling his nose like he'd bitten into something bitter.
The person Al had brought up, the one who'd disappeared, was Heinkel.
A member of the Priscilla Camp, he'd been in quite a state of shock after her death, and a few days before Garfiel and the others left the Empire, he'd vanished without a trace.
Garfiel had joined in the search, but in a city with thousands of people coming and going every day, tracking a scent had proven near impossible, and in the end they hadn't been able to find him.

"Emilia-sama, too, was awfully down about it…"

Emilia had felt a heavy sense of responsibility over Heinkel's disappearance.
Apparently, without the others knowing, she'd made contact with Heinkel, and he'd clung to her with quite intense desperation. It was said the situation had been resolved with a third party stepping in, but afterward, without ever getting a proper chance to talk again, Heinkel had disappeared.
Garfiel figured he was about as down about it as Emilia was — he'd had surprisingly many points of contact with Heinkel, having stood his ground alongside him in the fight against the Great Disaster.

"Ain't you worried about the old man too?"

"Hm? Ah, worried, sure, I'm worried, but me and the old man are both gettin' on in years. Two old guys worryin' over each other, huh?"

"The hell's age got to do with it! 'Course you worry about a comrade! You start countin' that way, Beatrice, Emilia-sama, even Granny — they're all over a hundred!"

"True enough, the Brothers' bunch is exhibit A for how seniority don't make a lick of sense with you all. Ahh, I figure everything you just said is a hundred percent right. Just…"

"Just what."

"My bad. Truth is, I got my own hands pretty damn full right now too."

Saying that, Al lifted the hand holding the flask and bowed his head, and Garfiel found himself at a loss for words.
Having gotten that way, he felt embarrassed at having gotten emotional and pressed Al so hard. What he'd just done was pure venting, born of his own helplessness.
And on top of that—

"—Somehow ya seem different from the boss, huh."

Narrowing his green eyes, tongue running over the sharpness of his canines, Garfiel studied Al's demeanor solemnly and recalled what Otto had told him.

—Having witnessed Priscilla's death, both Subaru and Al bore deep wounds to their hearts.
Each of them had been struck by the shock of Priscilla's death, but of everyone, these two had taken the deepest wounds, and how each faced that wound set them apart from the rest.
Calling it settling accounts, a "Sparka," Subaru had let the many comrades he'd met in the Empire take turns striking him.
That painful, reckless attempt had been called off through the support and intervention of those around him, but Al needed something — the same, or perhaps even more of it.

Talking with him like this, there was nothing wrong with his responses — but at first, when Subaru and the others hadn't offered to come along, Al had tried to force his way to the tower alone.
That was, in all likelihood, tantamount to suicide, and Garfiel — having heard from the others how brutal the Augria Sand Dunes were — was certain of it. It was even possible Al had grown so self-destructive that he wouldn't have minded dying halfway to the tower.

"I want to respect Al's feelings — his wanting to read the Book of the Dead. I'm gonna get him to the Pleiades Watchtower. …That's what I can do for Al, and for Priscilla."

When they'd learned of the dangerous signs in Al, no one had been able to stop Subaru from saying that.
Not accompanying Al was as good as looking the other way while he died in the sand sea. — There'd been enough death already; that had been the unanimous feeling of the camp.

"Still, y'know. The Brothers went off shoppin', right? Shoulda had the strongest of the bunch tag along with 'em. The carriage'd be fine with just me watchin' it."

"—Leave someone from another camp behind by himself? Not happenin'. I'm your watchdog."

"Callin' me an outsider, that's cold. We saved the Empire together, didn't we?"

And that, too, was part of the reason Al had lost everything precious to him.
How one grieves, how one despairs, how one regrets — it's different for everyone. Garfiel too had experience of being cornered into a dead end, unable to move, in despair.
To pull himself out of it, Garfiel had borrowed the strength of Subaru, Ram, Otto, and the others.
This time, he wanted to be able to be that presence for someone else.

"…I felt the same way about the old man, y'know."

Heinkel, who'd disappeared — he must have had his own despair too. Garfiel could somehow tell it had cast a long shadow over his life.
Precisely because he could tell, he pitied Heinkel, who'd had no choice but to carry it all alone.

"—It would seem the air in this carriage has grown rather stagnant. Gloomy."

Just as Garfiel was thinking that.
The carriage's entrance opened, and the person who showed their face there tossed out those words.
Startled, Garfiel turned around, and let out a breath at finding a familiar face there,

"Don't go scarin' me outta nowhere like that. Thought it was the 'Sound of Uzuru's Wind' or somethin'."

"My apologies. Though, given your duty, wouldn't it be a problem if you couldn't handle something sudden? A point for consideration."

"Ugh, w-well, that's…"

"My apologies again. That was me picking at a weak point. An apology."

Saying that, pressing a finger to his lips with a wink, was a slender-faced man with neatly trimmed navy-blue hair and a monocle over one eye — Clind.
The butler in service to Roswaal's relative Annerose Miload, a man who carried an unfathomable air about him, and who didn't get along with his older sister Frederica.
And the one they'd arranged to meet at Mirula, the nearest town to this sand sea.

"Damn, perfect timin', ain't it. — What about Meili?"

"I have brought her, of course. As for the timing, it hardly warrants praise. As a butler, I merely accomplished my expected duty."

"Flyin' a whole damn carriage clear across the country like that, from one end to the other — ain't no other butler gonna pull that off so easy."

Hand to his chest, thoroughly modest, Clind left Garfiel a little exasperated.
In bringing Subaru and Rem back from the Empire, the role Clind had played was substantial — to the point that he might well have been the camp's real MVP.
Clind apparently could use magic similar to the "Door Crossing" that let one traverse space, and he'd used it to put in an all-around effort preparing everything needed for the trip to the Empire.
And the reason he'd joined them here now was to bring Meili, an indispensable asset for crossing the Augria Sand Dunes.

"As for Meili-sama, she has gone straight to Subaru-sama and the others. I came to the carriage to report our arrival. Dutiful."

"Thanks. By the way, couldn't you bring Emilia-sama or Otto along too?"

"My apologies. The compensation my master paid for this intervention was strictly limited to bringing Subaru-sama and the others back from the Empire. Bringing Meili along like this was already a courtesy on my part, at best. Sincerity."

"…Got it. If there's a reason, I ain't gonna push it."

Clind bowed deeply, and though his flat tone never changed, there was a consistent note of apology in his manner, suggesting there was a reason even beyond his own control.
Garfiel himself had once had a bad habit of losing his reason when he underwent Beastification, and he'd only been able to overcome it thanks to a fortunate turn of events. He didn't know if Clind's constraints were similar, but having gone through something like it himself, Garfiel didn't press the matter unreasonably.

"Anyway, can't leave it at just us. Al, lemme introduce ya. This is one of our own, buddy-buddy with that Roswaal bastard — Clind."

"Thank you for that introduction, which manages to be both subtly chilling and hard to deny. Pleased to be introduced. I am called Clind. A pleasure to make your acquaintance. A bow."

Realizing he'd gotten caught up in talk and left Al out of it, Garfiel introduced Clind. Clind gave a faint wry smile at that, then turned properly to Al and greeted him.
Garfiel privately thought it was odd for Clind, who'd apparently been palling around with Roswaal for ages, to deny the "buddy-buddy" label at this late stage — but—

"—Al-sama? Curious."

Regardless of Garfiel's thoughts, Clind's brow furrowed in suspicion.
The reason was Al's reaction — he hadn't responded to Clind's greeting at all.

"――――"

Silent, Al just stared fixedly at Clind's face.
Of course, with his helmet on, his expression couldn't be seen, but even so, the air he gave off — something close to surprise or confusion — could be felt.
Still, the meaning behind that reaction was lost on Garfiel.

"What's up? Clind, you know Al?"

"――. No, I'm afraid I have no recollection of him. Al-sama, have we met somewhere before? A confirmation."

Shaking his head, Clind denied Garfiel's question.
If Clind had no memory of it, that was likely the truth of the matter.
As mentioned, Clind could use special magic, but beyond that, he was a man of exceptional skill as a butler. He was the one who'd educated Frederica and Ram as servants, and the one who'd taught Subaru how to use a whip. He probably wasn't stronger than Garfiel, but even from his bearing alone, one could tell he was no ordinary man.
If there was nothing in his memory, then at the very least, it was a fact that Clind, for his part, had no recollection of him.
And as for Al—

"—Nah, nothin'. No memory of it. No doubt about it, this is our first meetin'."

"…Then what the hell was that meaningful silence just now?"

"My bad, kid. Just felt a little tired of learnin' new things, that's all. — Clind, was it? I'm Al. The Brothers and your people have been takin' care of me."

"So I have heard. Please, I look forward to working with you going forward. Permanence."

"Permanence's a bit much, but sure, nice to meetcha too."

Waving a hand lightly, Al responded easily to Clind's banter.
Watching the exchange between the two of them, Garfiel felt something faintly unnatural in Al's manner, but he missed his chance to bring it up.

"Anyway, whatever."

Now that Clind had brought Meili, the true crossing of the Augria Sand Dunes would begin in earnest.
Meili's power could drive off the witchbeasts swarming the sand sea, but there were plenty of things to prepare for — like the whirling sand-wind "Sand Hour" that had to be crossed no matter what to reach the tower.
He'd heard that last time, it was a scene where Emilia, and Julius who'd come along, had shown their strength.

"Gonna do it! Boss and the others — I'll protect 'em no matter what it takes!"

Fists pressed together in front of his chest, Garfiel roared.
The sense of mission welling up inside him was proof of the resolve bursting forth from the unshakable self-awareness now spurring Garfiel on.


△▼△▼△▼△


—Or so Garfiel had declared, snorting with fired-up resolve.


"Aaall right, safely arrived. Nothing happened this time, so I'm kinda relieved."

Saying that, Meili hopped down from the driver's seat onto the sand, spreading her arms wide with her back to the great, great door that marked the tower's entrance.
The eastern edge of the Augria Sand Dunes — the path to the Pleiades Watchtower, which even the Sword Saint had given up trying to cross and never reached — had been overcome without a hitch.

"Gao……"

Garfiel, who had sworn firmly to protect his comrades from any and every obstacle, ought to have been glad at this anticlimactic outcome, but instead all he could manage was to mutter with a face of deep, unconvinced dissatisfaction.
