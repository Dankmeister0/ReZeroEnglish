Arc 7 Chapter 12: "The Vollachia Empire"

—There was a sensation of something, something horrible, coiling in a whirlpool.

A whirlpool—yes, a whirlpool.
Round and round, spinning with tremendous force, a whirlpool that swallowed everything whole, whirling and whirling.
Somewhere—no, at the very center of himself—round and round, a whirlpool was whirling.

A vicious black vortex that devoured everything, fierce as a storm, vivid as lightning, blazing as magma—that kind of malevolent black whirlpool, whirling.

Perhaps it was the horrible binding that had slept forever in the depths of this body.
A curse of "death," entwined and knotted and joined together endlessly, never to be undone.
A greedy seal declaring that this life was already spoken for, and would never be sold off to anyone else.

The curses that should have eaten away at this life instead interfered with one another, hating each other, each refusing to hand its prey to the other, resisting, fighting over it—and, as a result, arriving at contradictory conclusions.

The curses were refusing to let this vessel die.

Round and round, spinning with tremendous force, a whirlpool that swallowed everything whole, whirling and whirling.
Around the beast, around the dragon, around the cursed vessel at its center, round and round, whirling—.

Tents with red flags were for medical treatment. There were five in total at the camp.
Tents with black flags were for supplies. There were twenty-five in total at the camp.
Tents with white flags were for officers. There were three in total at the camp.

The tent with the golden flag was for the commander. There was only one at the camp.

Since he'd been given the freedom to run all over the place under the pretext of being on menial duty, he'd managed to get a surprisingly good look around, even in the short time he'd had.
This was the second time Subaru had ever seen a real military encampment—the Empire made it the second.

The first had been around the time of the battle against the White Whale, in the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica.
When they'd set up positions around Flugel's Tree to prepare for the White Whale's appearance, they'd built something resembling an encampment, though nothing this elaborate.
After that, he'd had a few chances to camp out on a smaller scale. Even so, those had all been makeshift affairs—nothing as thoroughgoing as what the Empire had put together.

So, helped along by his own curiosity, he'd made a point of observing everything closely.
Of course, aside from one person, he hadn't exactly made a good impression on the imperial soldiers, so if he'd tried to poke around anywhere that actually mattered, his head and his body would surely have parted ways. So it had only ever been the surface of things.
And yet, even knowledge that shallow—


"—It's plenty useful. Having information versus not having it makes all the difference in the world… and more than anything, knowing the layout of their positions gives us a rough read on enemy strength. After that—"

"Is showing our courage and strength, Ya. We know this well, kinsman."

"Yes. Show them, then. The pride and might of the People of Shudrak—brave warriors who once rode stirrup to stirrup with the emperor they called the Warrior Emperor, cutting down every foe before them."


He drifted in and out, feeling a lethargy like floating in lukewarm water.
Amid it all, what reached his ears were the voices of a man and a woman, both brimming with fighting spirit.

"——"

Besides the voices of that man and that woman, he could hear many other breaths.
He sensed the presence of a great many people, a crowd of them.
He sensed something like the hot, burning fervor of a great many people.

And he felt all of it, strangely, building up around him, at his very center—

"—Now then, let us begin, Shudrak! From here, we raise the smoke signal of our counterattack!!"

"O-Ohhhhhhh—!!"

—A tremendous roar rang out, as though it would chew up the whole world.



"—Ouagh!?"

Something cold and wet slapped down over his face, and Subaru's whole body jolted in surprise.
Not knowing what had happened, his consciousness was hauled up rapidly, and the blinking, fluttering field of his vision was covered in white. —No, wait, that was the answer to the wet sensation.
What had been draped over Subaru's face was a strip of cloth, soaked and barely wrung out.

He'd once read in some book that there was a torture method where you covered someone's face with a towel and dripped water onto it. All you needed was a towel and water, and it let you make someone feel like they were drowning with hardly any effort—a hellish waterboarding technique.

"I-I don't have anything to tell you...!"

"Ohh, Su, woke up. Feeling better, Uu relieved too."

"A-Ah...?"

The voice that reached him was too young for an interrogator, and Subaru shook his head in surprise. The cloth draped over him slipped, and his vision cleared normally.
It seemed it hadn't been torture after all; in his now-open field of vision, he could see a sky faintly blocked by the leaves of great trees. And peeking out to hide that sky was—

"You're... "

"Utakata! Uu, Su's guard! Nurse! Babysitter! Glad you woke up!"

"...I'm not really following, but okay."

Laughing merrily, insisting in that high-pitched voice, was the girl with the tips of her black hair dyed pink—yes, Utakata.
One of the People of Shudrak, a girl he'd crossed paths with back at the settlement too. —Also someone who had, once, killed him with a poisoned arrow.
Though judging by that smile, they seemed to be on friendly terms now, so there probably wasn't much risk of having his breath stopped by a poisoned arrow at present.

"And no sign of waterboarding either, I take it... hm?"

"Su, 'Lifeblood Ritual,' finished! Erugina, won! Uu and Mī and Hō, everyone everyone surprised!"

"...It's slowly coming back to me. Right, we were made to go through the 'Lifeblood Ritual.'"

To make the People of Shudrak acknowledge them, he'd taken on the "Lifeblood Ritual."
It was a coming-of-age rite passed down among the Shudrak, meant to prove one was a full-fledged adult. Subaru and Abel, who'd been captured alongside him, had faced it together, the two of them as one.
The enemy of that ritual had been Erugina, a giant serpentine Witchbeast that lived in the Buddheim Jungle.
Subaru and Abel had risked their lives trying to somehow snap off that great serpent's horn—

"...No good. Probably because I was out of my mind with panic, but I can't remember the second half at all. If I'm alive, does that mean Abel pulled something off...?"

"—? Su, don't remember? Mī, laughed so hard."

"Laughed so hard—at how pathetic I looked? Give me a break, seriously... wait."

Scowling at the tilted head of Utakata, Subaru tried to sit up. Partway through, as he planted his right hand on the floor, he felt something strange.
It wasn't the sort of wrongness you'd get from the floor being wet, or something hard being placed there. The cause felt like it lay somewhere more fundamental.
Put plainly, it felt like the cause lay not in the floor, but in Subaru's own arm.

"—Um. Utakata-san? Is there something going on with my right hand?"

"Su's right hand? Ah, it was amazing! All squelchy, then—whoosh!"

"All squelchy, then whoosh!?"

Faced with a string of sound effects that promised nothing good, Subaru's eyes went wide.
He took a few deep breaths, first getting his mental preparations in order, then steeled himself. First he turned his head toward his left hand. Three fingers were broken. It hurt, but he was relieved.
Then, slowly, he turned his gaze toward his right hand—

"...What in the world is this?"

For a moment, it seemed so bizarre a sight that he thought his eyes must be deceiving him.
Originally, Subaru's right arm had been marked with a horrifying pattern of black blotches—the lingering aftereffect of his clash with the Sin Archbishop of Lust at the water gate city of Priestella.
Capella of Lust had claimed her own blood was Dragon's Blood, and doused both Subaru and Crusch with it. As a result, Crusch had suffered a wound that would never heal, and Subaru, as though shouldering the horrifying blood running through her body, had had black markings carved into his right arm.

Still, since it hadn't caused any ill effects beyond its appearance, Subaru had normally hidden it under long sleeves, and hadn't let it drag him down in any particular way.
But now, that black pattern—

"——"

It wasn't a matter of a pattern anymore—it was solid black.
Subaru's right arm, from the fingertips all the way up through the wrist and roughly halfway up the forearm to the elbow, had turned pitch black, as though he'd pulled on a black glove.

Gulping, Subaru cautiously reached out and touched that black right hand with his left.
His left hand felt a squishy, rubbery give, while conversely, the sensation coming back from the right hand was faint. The right hand felt like it was wearing a rubber glove, and its movements were sluggish—

"...No, wait, could this be..."

To give shape to the wrongness he felt, Subaru pressed the nail of his left hand hard into the black right hand. He dug his finger in, dragging it like a scratch.
Then, the black part of his right hand crumbled away in a chunk, like peeling plaster off a mud wall.
"Whoa!?" Subaru yelped in surprise, but he twisted his finger into the peeled spot and, as though possessed, kept peeling, until finally the black pattern from fingertips to forearm had all come away, and beneath it emerged a clean, brand-new right hand—Natsuki Subaru's own.

"W-what in the woooorld is thiiiiis!?"

"Eek!?"

Witnessing the shocking event that had befallen his own body, Subaru let out a scream. Startled by his voice, Utakata fell on her rear.
But Subaru had no room to spare, and couldn't even lend a hand to the girl who'd tumbled over.

"Wha—what, what... what's going on with my hand! This is my hand... right?"

Dumbfounded, Subaru opened and closed the fist of that clean right hand, then cautiously touched his own face with it, and pressed it to the floor.
The feel of it, the way it moved, everything about it was fine—perfectly ordinary.
The black pattern that had originally remained on that right hand was gone too; Subaru's right hand was clean now, exactly the right arm of the "Lolicon"* that had been worked over for a whole year of life in another world.

"—wait, who's a 'lolicon'!"

"—I thought I heard a voice, and this is what you're shouting about?"

Checking that his right hand was in one piece, Subaru, still in a daze, had someone approaching him. —No, "someone" wasn't quite right. There were only two people who spoke with this kind of arrogant, high-handed manner, and since one was male and one female, telling them apart was easy.
This was the man's voice, meaning—

"Abel. You're alive after all."

"Naturally. Far more composed than you, at any rate."

Snorting as he said it was Abel, whose masked appearance Subaru had, unchanging as it was, grown used to.
He'd taken on the "Lifeblood Ritual" together with Subaru, but it seemed he'd survived his battle with Erugina without dying. If anything, it was thanks to him that Subaru had survived at all.
Probably it was Abel who'd defeated Erugina, too.

"—Hm. What happened to that right arm of yours. Have you done away with that horrid appearance?"

"Would you stop talking like I changed the color of my own right hand on purpose? This isn't some Western RPG character creation screen, I don't have that kind of freedom. ...The black part just peeled off clean when I scratched at it. Right, Utakata?"

"Right righT. Su's right hand, peeled off in chunks! Gross!"

"I get it, but still!"

Wincing at Utakata's blunt assessment, Subaru thrust his right hand out at Abel. Abel turned the unharmed hand this way and that, examining it, and murmured, "I see."

"In any case, it makes no difference to me if it's back to normal. I hardly expected you to punch with the ring of mana-sealing stone still on your hand. I thought, with the hand gone past the wrist, you wouldn't survive."

"Wait, wait, wait, is this a scary story? Whose hand went missing past the wrist?"

"Yours."
"Su!"

Abel, arms crossed, and Utakata, thrusting her right hand up energetically.
Chilled by their answers, Subaru looked at his own clean right hand. Far from being blown off, it was in a state as good as new.

"C-come on, come on, quit joking around. If it was gone, then what's this right hand?"

"That is precisely the horrifying, peculiar phenomenon. Your arm vanished, and forced words out of you as you lay dying. I believed you would die after that... but then, black sludge overflowed from your hand."

"S-sludge...?"

"It took the shape of an arm in the blink of an eye, and became a black arm. If you're asking what happened, then I ought to ask you in turn what exactly you thought you were doing."

Pierced by that sharp gaze, Subaru's breath caught.
He turned his eyes back to his right hand, but no matter what Abel said, Subaru himself had no idea what had caused it. Still, it was surely no coincidence that the black pattern that had always marked his right hand—now vanished—was involved.
Perhaps it really had been the Dragon's Blood at work after all—

"Even so, then the question becomes why it never activated until now..."

Not to put too fine a point on it, but he'd been doused in Dragon's Blood nearly three months ago.
Since then, on the journey across the Augria Sand Dunes, and during the desperate battle at the Pleiades Watchtower, Subaru had slipped through more brushes with death than ever before, and on top of it all, had ended up entering the Empire.
In that time, he'd literally encountered situations that ought to have killed him, again and again—yet none of those times had brought about anything so convenient as a healing wound.
So why now, here—

"...There's no point thinking about things I can't figure out. Besides, none of my wounds besides the right hand have healed at all."

As for Subaru's body, as he muttered this: the fingers of his left hand were broken, as he'd confirmed; the wound on his back near the shoulder blade, the unfamiliar burn near his neck, and the many other bruises besides—none of it had healed.
Seriously, only the wound on his right hand had been healed, which raised the unpleasant possibility that it had only healed because that was where the black pattern had been.

"And now that whatever it was on my right hand is gone, it probably won't happen again... not that I could try it by having someone blow off my right hand a second time."

"So in the end, it's a matter you can't explain. You seem to have a great many secrets."

"I don't need to hear that from a guy hiding his face..."

Answering with a sour look, Subaru let out an "Ah" of breath.
He'd been leisurely turning his thoughts to the strangeness of his right hand, and confirming that Abel was safe, but he remembered there was something he should have been prioritizing over all that.

If he'd taken part in the "Lifeblood Ritual," and finished it without dying, that meant time had continued to pass even while Subaru was unconscious.
Which meant several more hours had gone by since then—

"—Rem. That's right, Rem! I can't just sit here, I have to go get Rem—"

His original goal, bringing back the Rem he'd left behind.
That was the very reason he'd taken on the "Lifeblood Ritual" in the first place—if the passage of time meant he could no longer save her, then the meaning of that life-or-death battle would be lost.

"Ah! Su, don't push yourself, no good! You'll die!"

"Don't be ridiculous! Even if I don't die, if Rem dies it means nothing—guh"

Pushed by his own urgency, Subaru shifted his posture to climb down from the bed.
Only now did he realize that he'd apparently been laid to rest in a strange bed—a box like a palanquin made of lashed-together logs—and carried outside the building.
The moment he dropped down from it with force, Subaru groaned at the pain that pierced through his entire body.

"Guh—ah..."

"Fool. Did you think that just because your right arm grew back, your half-dead body had recovered? I told you already, didn't I. That I judged you to be dying. Do you think my judgment is mistaken?"

"That's..."

Looking down at Subaru, who had crumpled from the pain, Abel's cold voice rained down.
As though to confirm Abel's words, Subaru noticed a sensation of something slowly seeping out from deep within his own body.
Having known so many fatal pains, Subaru could tell this was a danger signal.
Like a balloon or a bucket punctured in a place it shouldn't be, the water or air inside—whatever it was that kept it inflated—spilling out, that kind of sensation—

"But, Rem's..."

"—. Even in this state, you concern yourself not with your own self, but with a woman. Well, fine. I already knew as much. You wanted this even without a right hand."

"...Huh?"

"This way."

Concerned more with the safety of a Rem who wasn't even present than with his own life—
Letting out an exasperated breath at Subaru's words, Abel jerked his chin. Then, without so much as a glance back at Subaru, he began walking. As if to say, follow me.

"Su, okay? Lend a shoulder?"

"No, I'm fine... borrowing your shoulder would be extra rough with the height difference."

Wry-smiling at Utakata, who peered into his face with concern.
Then Subaru took a deep breath and somehow managed to stand. Dragging his feet, he caught up to Abel, walking ahead.

"——"

Abel had gone a little ways ahead and was waiting for Subaru to catch up.
Standing on a rock covered in green grass as a foothold, he was gazing out from a cliff's edge with a wide view over the other side. Huffing and puffing, Subaru climbed the great boulder as well, and came to stand at his side.
And then—

"—Look."

Following the small jerk of his chin once more, Subaru lifted his face.
Raising his gaze like that, Subaru laid eyes on the panorama spread out before them from the high ground, and his mouth fell open.
Slack-jawed, as though stunned. Because there—

"—Ah?"

Black smoke rose, and the camp—the imperial soldiers' encampment—wrapped in flame, was being consumed by fire.

—What reached his ears was a battle cry, a triumphant song shaking the very air.

"—!!"

Raising war cries, or singing, at the top of their lungs, songs he'd never heard before, were the dark-skinned women warriors, bows slung across their backs, tearing freely across the battlefield in every direction.
By the surprise attack of the People of Shudrak, the imperial soldiers' encampment had been reduced to ruin; the imperial soldiers, with no means left to resist, could only flee in a panic, cut down one after another.

"This is..."

"Take the offensive, seize their weapons, burn their medicines, pierce their commanders. Lose your hands and your head, and all that's left is to turn tail and run, however unseemly it looks. —Sad state, for those who call themselves sword-wolves."

"——"

Below, he could see the imperial soldiers, driven on by black smoke and powerful bows, fleeing however they could, crawling on hands and knees.
But there was no escape from the People of Shudrak, whose livelihood was hunting beasts in the forest. Their arrows, which could find their mark from impossibly far away, struck true into the hearts of the soldiers fleeing with their backs turned.

How many had managed to escape? How many had managed to survive?
Just how many, in the end, had died?

"This can't..."

"Why do you stand there dumbstruck, Natsuki Subaru. This is the fruit of battle your kinsmen achieved, with the information you wanted, that you brought them. If you won't laugh at this, then what will you laugh at?"

Looking down at the sight of the encampment, now truly transformed into a battlefield, Subaru felt his consciousness grow distant. On top of that, Abel, forcing reality upon him all the more, declared that this was what Subaru had wanted.
Unable to bear it, Subaru shot to his feet and grabbed Abel by the collar.
With the right arm that had only just healed, he seized Abel and glared into his eyes at close range.

"I wanted this? A sight like this!? Don't talk nonsense—"

"—Then did you think your wish could be granted without bloodshed?"

"—!"

But to Subaru, who tried to bite back in his anger, Abel bit back harder still. Cut to pieces by the sharpness of his tongue, Subaru had nothing to say in return.

"——"

Asked if he'd thought his wish could be granted without bloodshed, he had nothing to say.
He had thought it could be granted without bloodshed. He'd believed it was possible.
Because—

"Let me put it another way, Natsuki Subaru. —Did you think your wish could be granted without any blood being shed except your own?"

"—Ah"

"An absurd notion. A foolish, hopeless delusion. Did you truly believe that if you yourself bled, you could stop third parties from fighting each other? That is a hero's delusion, and worse in nature than the worthless hero-worship you profess."

"——"

"You are a human being, Natsuki Subaru. Neither hero nor sage. And so, whether you exist or not, people will bleed, will lose their lives, will take and be taken from, again and again."

Still gripping his collar, Abel battered the strength draining from Subaru with words, blow after blow.
Struck by those words, Subaru's teeth chattered, and he shook his head, no, no.

That was—that much was true.
It was a fact he couldn't deny. He understood that. But Subaru couldn't swallow it.
He hadn't lived in a world where he could take that as a matter of course, swallow it that easily.

Even here, in another world, Natsuki Subaru's moral compass remained that of a Japanese high schooler.
He couldn't accept the customs and common sense of the battlefield as simply the way things were.

"I do not wish to be a hero. I will not cling to them, rely on them, entrust anything to them. I will shoulder everything myself, and push toward the richer path. —A hero cannot do that."

"Wha—what are you even... what is it you're trying to do..."

His strength drained away, and Subaru sank to his knees again on the spot, unable to understand Abel.
This was someone he'd taken on the "Lifeblood Ritual" with, someone who had, together with him, wrung out a victory. Surprisingly, their conversational rhythm matched well; he didn't think their compatibility was bad. And yet he had no idea what the man was thinking.
Of course not. —How could he ever understand someone who wouldn't even show his face?

"Someone who won't even show his face, what would he..."

"My face. —Very well, I shall show it to you."

He wasn't even given time to raise a questioning "Huh?"
Hearing Subaru's mosquito-thin protest, wrung out in his desperation, Abel put a hand to his own face. Untying the knot of the tattered cloth wrapped around his face with his fingers, a wind blew.
Caught by the strong gust, the mask came off with force and flew away.
Flying, flying, it soared over the encampment turned battlefield, riding the wind toward some far distance, on and on, farther and farther it flew—

"Perhaps it will make it all the way to the capital. —To the city where the throne I ought to sit upon awaits."

"——"

Watching the tattered cloth carried off by the wind, Abel had made his grandiose declaration.
Seeing the man's now-bared face, Subaru held his breath quietly, unable to look away.

It was the face of a beautiful young man, black-haired, with striking, narrow eyes.
He looked to be several years older than Subaru—somewhere in his early to mid-twenties. His features were arresting enough to steal one's gaze; though his hair was disheveled and his cheeks smudged from time spent in the forest and the settlement, even that only served to set off his innate handsomeness all the more.
With his long, slender limbs and slim build supporting that face, he could be called the very picture of a fully realized, handsome man.
And yet, undoubtedly the most distinctive thing about him was those black eyes.

A gaze carrying tremendous force of will and pressure, the kind that could bring anyone who saw it to their knees.
Faced with it head-on, Subaru—already on his knees—felt himself unable to move for a reason quite apart from his wounds and exhaustion.
He understood it. His soul was submitting to the person before him.
The reason for that overwhelming presence was—

"—Vincent Abelux."

"...Huh?"

"That is my name. At least, it is the name I will bear until I sit upon the throne once more. Though I do think it wisest for you to keep calling me Abel going forward."

Saying so, Abel twisted the corner of his mouth at the stunned Subaru.
Only belatedly did Subaru realize that it was a terribly wicked smile, one that carried even a hint of something feral.
Without understanding what that name meant—

"—Abel! Subaru!"

"—!"

What broke Subaru's paralysis was a sharp voice calling out.
Turning his face that way on reflex, he saw a figure waving and approaching them—the red-haired warrior-woman, Mizelda, chief of the Shudrak.
Mizelda's combative expression relaxed into something friendly and familiar.

"Camp's taken. Our losses, minimal—oh? Abel, first time seeing your face, quite the handsome man..."

"Mizelda-san..."

"Ahem... Subaru, glad you woke up too. If you'd died like that, it wouldn't have sat right with us as kin."

For a moment, Mizelda, having gotten a little lost in Abel's bare face, cleared her throat and turned a gentle gaze on Subaru.
It was the kind of gentle smile you offer a living person on the verge of death, and it made Subaru's heart and body shrink. Like Abel, she too had judged that Subaru wouldn't last much longer.
And yet, that she could still act so brightly toward him could only be put down to a difference in how they viewed life and death.
Still, it soon became clear that this difference in outlook wasn't the only reason for Mizelda's smile.

"Holly, bring her here."

"Yes yes, got it, Nō~!"

Mizelda turned and called out to someone, and got back a cheerful, easygoing reply.
Trudging over toward them was the woman with hair dyed yellow, who'd carried even a boulder with ease—Holly, that was her name.
And cradled in the smiling, beaming Holly's arms was—

"No thrashing now, Nō~. Kuna got knocked flying and still hasn't woken up, so poor thing, Nō~."

"Stop this, this instant...! Let go of me! What do you think you're doing!"

"Ohh, such a girl who won't listen to a word I say, so troubling, Nō~."

Holly, wearing a troubled expression, held in her arms a girl squirming and struggling to break free.
It was a girl with blue hair, her lovely features flushed with anger—the girl Subaru wanted to see, wanted to hear the voice of, wanted to meet, more than anything else in the world right now—

"—Rem!"

At that moment, Subaru forgot his own poor physical condition, the awe he'd felt toward Abel, the revulsion at the battlefield wrapped in flame below—forgot everything, and ran.
His speed was slow. He couldn't risk jumping down from the boulder, since it might well kill him, so dragging his feet, he ran at a pace slower than a child's jog.
And then Subaru made his way to Rem, held in Holly's arms—

"You are..."

"Rem! Thank god, you're safe..."

"You're the one who had this done, aren't you! This is the worst!"

The instant Subaru reached out his arms toward her, Rem's swung hand struck Subaru across the cheek.
A loud crack rang out, startling even Holly, Mizelda, and Utakata into wide eyes. It was quite a powerful blow—enough that Subaru nearly went flying himself.
But he didn't go flying. And he didn't complain, didn't protest, "why'd you hit me."

There was no complaint anywhere in him.
Because Rem was alive like this, talking to him—that alone was enough.

"Rem..."

"—! You, of all people, are..."

Struck hard across the cheek, Subaru nonetheless pulled Rem's body into his arms, regardless. Snatched away from Holly's grip into Subaru's chest, Rem, after a moment of surprise, flushed red with anger.
Clenching her fist to deliver a fierce blow—

"...You're..."

Before delivering the finishing blow to Subaru's body, she seemed to notice just how battered he was.

"——"

Relief drained the strength from him, and Subaru sank to the ground on the spot. Still held in her arms, Rem lost her words at the sight of the injuries covering his body—his shoulder, torso, feet, and his broken left hand, and various other wounds.

"...well, the left hand, Rem's the one who broke it, remember?"

"I know that! But other than that, wounds like this... this is going to kill you! We need to treat this right away..."

"Pointless."

Seeing Subaru's weak smile, Rem pleaded desperately. But that plea was struck down by Mizelda's short, clear answer.
At the sharpness of that word, Rem too looked up with an "Eh?"
To that gaze of Rem's, Mizelda slowly shook her head side to side.

"Subaru's wounds run deep, treatment won't heal them. Right now, willpower's what's holding him together, Ga, but that too will run out soon."

"Run out—what, why suddenly...!"

"—? Because he's gotten his woman back, obviously."

Tilting her head, Mizelda said it as though it were only natural.
Hearing that, Rem let out an "Ah" and held her breath, while Subaru, unable to keep his head up, gave a wry laugh.

"Mizelda-san, way to put it..."

"Did I say something wrong, Ka? As the final wish of a kinsman, we gave it everything we had. He's a man worth that much, this one."

"Haha, much obliged..."

Mizelda's straightforward words of trust were both a joy and a torment.
Because Subaru himself knew that their judgment and their words weren't wrong. So it meant burdening Rem, here and now, with something extra, weighing down her feet.
Making feet that were already hard enough to move, heavier still—

"Why, why is it..."

To Subaru, who no longer had the strength even to hold up his own head, Rem's voice reached him like that.
Her voice was trembling, and he noticed that somewhere along the way, Subaru—who should have been the one embracing her—was instead being supported by Rem's arms.
Rem's pale blue eyes wavered, looking at Subaru with suspicion, distrust, and sorrow.

"Why do you go this far? For me, why...?"

"——"

"Why is it?"

He was being asked. Why do you do such a thing.
He remembered being asked something like this once before, too.
By someone precious to him, asked the same question all over again—what had he answered, then?

His memory grew hazy, his consciousness threatening to slip away, and he couldn't remember.
So, to the girl before him, on the verge of tears, he let his heart answer as it wished.

"Why—"

Since that was what he'd been asked.

"—I want you to be happy."

"——"

"I want you to smile. ...That's all I need."

Surrounded by so much love, in the same place as the people she loved most, smiling.
Smiling like a flower blooming, like a sky without a single cloud, like the stars shining brilliantly far, far away in the reaches of the heavens.

I just want you to smile. You.



"——"

"—Eh? Wait, wait, please wait...!"

Limply, slowly, the strength drained from Subaru's body.
His head hung, his neck could no longer support it, and his upper body began to sag and topple. Reflexively pulling him in close, Rem called out to Subaru, whose head now lay right beside her own.
There was no answer.

"—To our kinsman, peace for a warrior's spirit."

Straightening her back, Mizelda's lips wove together a song of respect, and words to carry it forward.
Following Mizelda's song, Holly and Utakata, the rest of the People of Shudrak, all those who had been singing songs of triumph on the battlefield, began to sing along.

It was a requiem, sent to a warrior who had fought until the end, who had fulfilled his own pride to the very last—

"Wait, please. That's, because, this person is—!"

Sent off by the requiem, Subaru was slowly, gently letting go of his life. Watching his peaceful face, Rem shook her head, no, no.

"——"

She didn't know why. Didn't understand the meaning of it.
The words he'd spoken, in the end, hadn't even answered the question Rem wanted answered.
And yet, she understood that if this went on, it would be lost forever—

"Please, don't die, not here, don't die, don't die, don't die..."

A man who, wrapped in a smell she found unbearable, horrible, one her instincts wanted to reject—had looked at her, all the same, with eyes that cherished Rem to no end. Her soul cried out that she must not lose him here.
Letting herself be carried along by that, Rem bit her lip, yearning for salvation—

"Aa—uu?"

Suddenly, along with a child's cooing voice, she felt a hand land on her shoulder.

"—Ah"

Turning around with tear-filled eyes, she saw it was a golden-haired girl who had placed her hand on Rem's shoulder. Her face was blank and dazed, gazing at the unconscious Subaru.
Then, the girl let out an "Uaa-uu," and—

"...This is..."

Slowly, a warm sensation flowed into Rem from the shoulder the girl was touching.
It was a soft feeling, one that made the depths of her chest tingle ticklishly, and Rem found her breathing grow tight, unable, before long, to hold back the tears streaming down her cheeks.

Whatever flowed from the girl's palm was stirring up a strange sensation inside Rem.
She couldn't keep holding that swelling thing inside her any longer. As if her instincts commanded her to release it somewhere, Rem let it pour out of her whole body.
And it traveled along the girl's palm still touching hers, overflowed from Rem, and then, just as it was—

"——"

—flowed into the body of Subaru, held in her embrace, on the very verge of releasing his life.

"—I see. Healing magic, then. Even I hadn't anticipated this."

"Eh...?"

Rem, unable to understand even herself what was happening, had her ears struck by the man's voice. Looking up, she saw the black-haired man, arms crossed, narrowing his eyes as he watched her.
She parted her lips to question him—

"Keep your mouth shut, woman. That thing you're doing unconsciously is a kind of miracle, activated because the conditions happened to align. If you let your focus slip, it will cut off and you'll lose the effect."

"——"

"Save your questions and your anger for after you've dealt with what's in front of you. Don't waste the chance."

There was a weight to the man's words that was hard to deny, and Rem shut her mouth, holding back her reply.
Then, as the man said, she focused on sending warmth into Subaru's body, held in her arms.
What kind of effect this warmth had, Rem herself had no idea. But the breath that should only have been fading away in her arms had regained just a bit of its strength.
That alone was enough, for Rem, right now.

"...Right now, I still don't know what you are. But..."

But, she said, breaking off her words, hesitating over what came next, and closed her eyes.
Because the words he'd said—that he wanted her to be happy—hadn't sounded like a lie.

"You have to be alive—or you won't get to see me smile."

That was what she whispered, calling out to him.


　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　△▼△▼△▼△


—The light of healing activated, and Natsuki Subaru's fatal wounds began to close.

"——"

Arms crossed, looking down at it all, the man who called himself Vincent Abelux let out a sigh at Subaru, who had connected a life that had nearly slipped away.
What an unbelievably lucky man, he thought. Half-dead, he'd won the hearts of the People of Shudrak, then gone and gotten back the thing he wanted to get back, and on top of it all, had even reclaimed his own life.

Could it be that he'd calculated, cunningly, that if he got that blue-haired girl—Rem—back, his own life would be saved? A tempting theory, perhaps.

"If he were that clever, he'd have healed the broken fingers of his left hand first."

Even the fingers Rem had broken had been left as they were—a man soaked in blood and mud, wishing only for her recovery.
If such calculation had truly been possible for him, it would be nothing short of a masterstroke of cunning—but there wasn't a trace of the gravitas or bearing of a schemer about him whatsoever.
Precisely so: a man without the faintest hint of the "hero" spoken of in legend.

"I meant it as a farewell offering to a man about to die... but if he lives, well, that's fine too."

Touching his bare face, exposed to the wind for the first time in a while, having lost the sensation of it being covered, the man narrowed his eyes.
Natsuki Subaru had reclaimed his life, and a blood pact with the People of Shudrak had been forged. It seemed the imperial soldiers at the camp had been told nothing—but that too fell within the range of what he'd predicted.

Still, most people in this land—no, in this Empire—had yet to notice.

The unprecedented political upheaval that had befallen the mighty Sacred Vollachia Empire.
But—

"Chancellor Berstetz, the turncoat among the Nine Divine Generals, and you foolish imperial soldiers who know nothing of the summit."

Atop the hill where the heated wind blew, the man—Abel—turned to face far to the west, toward the capital.
The heart of the Sacred Vollachia Empire, the imperial capital of Lupugana, the land where the throne to be reclaimed awaited—

"—Tremble, and await my return."

And then—


"You've gone through the trouble of surviving. So you'll be keeping me company, Natsuki Subaru. —Until Vollachia is mine to reclaim."
