Arc 4 Addendum: "Advent"




—Through a dim, cold space, the soft slap of bare feet rang out at even intervals.

Not a sliver of light pierced that space; the path being traced was no different from advancing through darkness, or through shadow itself.
And yet the footsteps moving through it showed no sign of hesitation, treading through the foul-smelling place with a stride as unconcerned as if it were only natural — with all the ease of passing through a room one knew by heart.

「――――」

The sound of dripping water. The skittering tread of crawling insects.
The feel of gravel and mud against bare soles. In a place that summoned nothing but discomfort, the shadow let slip not a single word of complaint.

As if to avoid the encroaching shadow, the swarms of insects whose nest was being trespassed upon parted to clear a path. Water trickled steadily along its feet, and it advanced as though the gentle downward slope were guiding the way.
Before long the shadow came to a halt, sweeping its long hair back as it looked up.

Still, there was no light in the world.
But in those eyes there was a light of another kind — one that could only be called certainty.

Softly, a faint glow scattered into motes, and wind began to coil about the shadow's feet.
Long, pale-red hair and the trailing hem of a robe draped over its shoulders were tossed by the wind. Then, at the point beneath its feet where the wind surged upward, a round magic circle abruptly traced itself into being.

"So the activation formula still worked, did it."

Murmuring the words, the shadow — a girl's body — rose up above the glowing magic circle.
Her body climbed toward the ceiling as though an invisible floor were rising beneath her, and just as she was on the verge of colliding with the dark stone overhead, in that instant her body was pushed outward, passing straight through the bedrock as though it weren't even there.

Seared by sunlight, the girl shut her eyes.
It took exactly ten seconds before she opened them again. Sunlight seeped through her eyelids and stung her eyes. Driven by resistance to it, and by a measure of impatience, she forced them open wide.

—Before her, she saw the sun, only just beginning to rise.

"...Huh. Guess it wasn't as moving as I'd expected."

Faced with the sunlight before her very eyes, the girl tilted her head.
Just as she'd murmured, her unmoved eyes held nothing more than a ripple's worth of feeling. Having only ever laid eyes on imitation suns, she'd half hoped that seeing the genuine article again would stir something in her — but the result fell short of that hope.

"Still, it seems I've made it safely outside the barrier. Annoyingly enough, that must mean *that one* got through the Trials of the Tomb without a hitch. I suppose I'll give credit where it's due, on that point alone."

Otherwise, getting herself outside the Sanctuary would have been extraordinarily difficult. It was, quite literally, a case of drowning in her own scheme. Thinking of the girl who'd cleaned up after her, she felt something like discontent sprout in her chest — a rare thing for her.

"Well, I'll let it pass. This body can't take much strain as it is, so for a while I think I'll just wander about, treating it as time to fill in the blanks."

Opening and closing both hands, the girl checked how her body was holding up.
A replica built atop the original vessel, into which a soul of matching quality had been folded. The soul had been settled to fit it, but it was only natural that growing accustomed to it would take time.

"As for a name — well, he went to the trouble of giving me one. I suppose I'll go by Omega."

Lips curling into a smile, she trod through the grass and slipped between the trees, out of the forest.
It was a path that left something to be desired for a girl's legs, but no matter. Fatigue, pain — both were only possible because flesh and soul were joined as one. This fullness of being alive again, after so long — she'd have to make sure to savor it.

"Beatrice has left the Forbidden Archive, and Roswaal has lost his Gospel. Still, between the young man who picked up what didn't burn, and Garfiel, who can't let go of his anger, there's plenty still smoldering. I suppose I'll watch — in shadow and in sunlight both — to see how he chooses to face it all."

Deliberately leaving unspoken the name of the girl who set her nerves on edge, she started walking.
Ahead of her lay the world. To this girl, it was a mountain of treasure that never faded no matter how much time passed — rich, endlessly capable of feeding an infinite curiosity, a hoard fit for one possessed by a hunger for knowledge.

"If I go on like this, I wonder if a day will come when I finally understand."

Along the way she spotted a single flower, and smiled faintly.
Plucking one petal between her fingers, she breathed in its scent, then gently popped it into her mouth.

Even a beautiful flower withers, someday. Why is it that flowers wither?
Do the beautiful memories shared between people, too, fade away someday?


"—Ah. Why is it that love diminishes?"


Murmuring this, the girl, her long pale-red hair swaying, stepped forward.


The 'Witch' was, once more, unleashed upon the world.
