Arc 3 Chapter 39: "Miracle"

—Kill, kill, kill, kill, kill, kill, kill.

Only hatred, boiled down to its essence, only murderous intent, swelled ever larger in the murky darkness.

—Kill, kill, kill, kill, kill, kill, kill.

No matter how many times he muttered it, spat it out, wrung it dry, the spiral of hatred never ran out.

He had never once hated another person, a fellow human being, this much before.

Since coming to this world, he'd hated the formless thing called fate more times than he could count—a merciless world that dashed Subaru to the bottom again and again, that thrust unforgiving reality at him every time he tried to climb back up, that made him pay with his life whenever he chose wrong. That, he'd hated too many times to count.

But he had never, in his entire life until now, hated an individual this much.

"Petelgeuse... Romanée-Conti... nngh!"

Every time he spoke that name, every time he recalled that figure behind his eyelids, every time his eardrums replayed that shrieking voice, every time his brain became conscious of that existence, a raging fury inside his body erupted into flame.

That man, that lunatic—what in the world had he even been?

Subaru knew nothing of his background. All Subaru knew was the fact: that the man was a maniac who'd departed entirely from sanity, a human being with some fatal flaw that made communication with him utterly impossible, a contemptible wretch who thought nothing of tormenting others according to his own twisted values, and a fiend who had mercilessly wounded Rem—who had thrown herself away trying to save Subaru—and violated her life, her Soul, to the very end. All of it screamed that letting that man go on living could never be forgiven.

So he would kill him. He had to kill him. With his own hands, entrusted to no one else. Petelgeuse had to die by Subaru's own hand.

Otherwise, how could he ever answer for Rem's death?

"I'll kill him. Kill him. I swear... I'll... kill him...!"

Voicing his murderous intent aloud, affirming it, Subaru writhed and set his shackles rattling.

He flailed his arms, his legs, with all his strength, trying again and again to break free of the restraints. They had been fastened tight enough to bite into flesh from the start. Under the repeated violent strain, Subaru's body gave out before the shackles did—the skin of his arms tore, the flesh beneath gouged out and bleeding.

A searing pain shot up from the wound, setting his brain to boil. But every time that pain assaulted his nerves, he gritted his teeth, thinking of the suffering Rem had endured.

If his hand were to be torn off, so be it. As long as he could escape the shackles, even if all that could still move was one finger, one tooth, he would use it to choke the life out of Petelgeuse.

Writhing desperately, pain and blood dribbling from the corner of his mouth each time, Subaru went on raging, searching for somewhere to unleash his hatred, searching for a target for his murderous intent.

—More than four hours had already passed since Petelgeuse left the cave, abandoning Subaru behind.

The light sources set at various points along the cave walls—Lagmite Ore—had lost their glow, now emitting only a faint light, just enough to make out where they were. Losing their function of glowing when struck with an impact, they left nothing but a pale blue half-darkness stretching on and on through the cave.

Even Subaru himself, desperately struggling to free his hands and feet from their shackles, couldn't clearly make out his own hands in that darkness. In a sense, it was precisely because he couldn't clearly see the state of the damage to his own arms in the darkness that he was able to go on committing this self-harm without hesitation.

"Petel... giusssss...!"

Spitting out the hated man's name through gritted teeth, Subaru kept his resolve high.

In darkness where nothing could be seen, a world without even the faintest trace of any presence besides his own. Ragged breath, his heartbeat, the clank of chains against shackles, the sound of dripping blood—in a place devoid of any change from the outside world, being alone quickly wears down the human heart.

For a moment, he imagined what it would be like to strip away every last sound, all at once.

"—Aaah! Kill him, kill him, I'll kill him!"

But he couldn't bear that imagining for even a second, and Subaru screamed out a howl of vengeful rage, foam gathering at the corners of his mouth.

In a world cut off from everything outside it, being alone in that world easily destroys the human spirit, rots it, ends it. In a world where nothing would begin unless he himself moved, Subaru screamed—as if to shake off the terror of being left behind, as if to look away from that fact.

As long as he screamed his hatred, he could stay sane.

By clinging to his murderous intent like a madman, he could keep from actually going mad.

To keep from going mad, Subaru needed hatred.

—How many more hours passed after that, Subaru had no way of knowing.


"Hyuu, hyuu... k-kill, hyuu... 'im."

Consciousness drifted, hollow, in the gap between waking and unconsciousness.

Exhaustion, weakness, attrition—slowly, steadily, they wrung out Subaru's mind and body.

His throat, worn raw long ago from ceaselessly voicing every ounce of hatred he had, could no longer form the shape of human words even as the vengeful grudge inside him refused to run dry; all that came out now were rasping breaths.

His body, too, which had kept fighting the shackles, had been pushed past its limit and no longer answered the brain's commands. His wrists were torn open, flesh split down to bone. His ankles had bared raw, blackish-red flesh up past the anklebone, and every time they scraped the ground, a spasm of pain lanced through him.

—Kill him, kill him, kill him, kill him, kill him.

Even so, even now, only murderous intent kept welling up from some wellspring deep in his heart.

Now that his body and his head no longer obeyed him, only his heart still supported the Subaru of this moment. Roughly a full day had passed since he'd been left behind, cast off into a world of solitude. His body and spirit had reached their limits, but Subaru had not let his consciousness close.

If this awareness were to snap off now, he would never wake again. And if he were to forget this hatred, he would no longer be able to keep perceiving reality.

Only hatred, only murderous intent, proved Subaru's sanity.

If he forgot it, Subaru would once again shut himself up inside his own broken world, degenerating into a sloth who could do nothing but laugh and weep like a madman.

Sloth, Sloth, Sloth, Sloth, Sloth, Sloth, Sloth, Sloth, Sloth.

Those three syllables echoed inside his head, again and again, again and again.

Petelgeuse's grating laughter, his mad gestures, his every act—the very embodiment of a lunatic—all of it was bound to those three syllables.

If he gave in to weakness and sank into Sloth, he would be exposing exactly the disgraceful spectacle that man had spoken of. That alone was something he could absolutely not accept.

Petelgeuse the "Sloth." The Witch Cult. Archbishop of Sin. Finger. Right hand. Left hand. The Unseen Hand. Index finger. Ring finger. Little finger. Diligence. Sloth. Sloth. Sloth.

What he recited, one after another, were the distinctive words from among the ravings Petelgeuse had shouted at the top of his lungs. Subaru's dying mind turned over what meaning these fragments could possibly hold, digging up memory after memory tied to Petelgeuse just to keep even the smallest, faintest thread of his fading consciousness connected.

More vividly, more clearly, more distinctly than before, he had to bring that man's face into focus. His voice, his form, his shadow, the way he walked, the way he spoke, the way he thought, the way he touched—Subaru had to dig it all up from memory as concretely as if recalling a beloved, beloved person, sear it into his Soul together with the hatred, and keep it burning as fuel for waking.

His stamina to break the shackles was spent, his throat torn past calling for help, and all his remaining will was poured into nothing more than delaying, for whatever it was worth, the loss of consciousness.

To anyone watching from outside, Subaru's mind had already reached the dimension of madness. He alone could not see that there was no meaning in holding onto consciousness, in keeping his hatred blazing. And in a world where he alone existed, a question he alone could not recognize was, in effect, a question that would go forever unanswered.

Would his mind wear away and his heart vanish first?

Or would his body fail to keep pace with his consciousness's wakefulness, his flesh giving out first?

All that remained was to choose between two fixed and predetermined endpoints. Holding onto consciousness should, by rights, have had no meaning beyond that.

What lent meaning to that futile, useless struggle was not Subaru's stubborn refusal to give up—

"—?"

Suddenly, in the pitch darkness, Subaru caught his breath, sensing a presence that was not his own.

Lifting his head—even that slight motion was a burden—Subaru turned toward where he sensed the presence. But the depth of darkness filling the cave was not something to be resolved merely by straining his eyes. There was no Lagmite Ore within reach, and with no means of dispelling the dark, all Subaru could do was hold his breath and make himself small before this presence that had suddenly arisen.

He couldn't tell whether the presence that had appeared was friendly or hostile. If some well-meaning third party had appeared to try to help Subaru, they should have called out to him—and the fact that no one had, that whoever it was had appeared without a word, made it all the more suspicious.

Holding his breath, deliberately stilling even his own heartbeat, Subaru killed his own presence.

And yet the presence that had arisen kept creeping toward him—slowly, truly slowly, little by little, at a crawling pace, but unmistakably drawing nearer to Subaru.

In the complete darkness, toward Subaru who should have been concealing his own presence, it approached as though it knew exactly where he was.

He felt a sense of danger toward that presence, an urgency, a shiver of dread. But almost immediately, a different sensation—the exact opposite of those feelings—flickered across his mind.

—Where, to begin with, had this presence even come from?

Subaru had never received any kind of training in sensing another's presence. Which meant that the reason he was now, like this, sensing someone's presence in the darkness was some biological sound made by an existence other than himself, one that hadn't been there before.

And those sounds were coming from fairly close by, at the very least not reaching him from some distance, like near the entrance. More than that, it hadn't started from that direction at all.

This sound had suddenly appeared, somewhere near the center of the cave.

Thinking that far, Subaru's lips trembled with disbelief, and—

"R... Rem...?"

He called out the name of the girl who seemed, of all possibilities for this sound, this presence, the most likely.

Reason told Subaru that couldn't be. The last time Subaru had laid eyes on Rem, back when there was still some light source left in the cave, her state had been too gruesome to look at directly.

Her limbs torn off, her ears cut away, her whole body pierced again and again by blades. Left in that state without any treatment, and moreover left for dozens of hours on cold ground that mercilessly stole away body heat.

There was no way she could still be alive. Of course she was dead. But if that were so, then what other possibility could there be? What was the true identity of this presence, creeping toward Subaru at that crawling pace from the very spot where Rem had fallen?

"Rem, Rem...?"

"————"

To his desperate, clinging call, only silence answered in return.

And yet the presence—perhaps having gained some confidence in its purpose from Subaru's voice—seemed to change its crawling speed, just the slightest bit. Even that was the faintest of changes.

Slowly, slowly, came the sound of something dragging itself across the cold ground.

There was no way it could be standing, walking. If it really was her, she would have to be moving, with all her might, whichever two of her remaining limbs still worked, crawling toward him like a caterpillar.

Raising himself up, pulling at his hand- and footcuffs, rattling his chains, Subaru tried to move as well. But the restraints on the wall were sturdy—having withstood over a dozen hours of struggle already, they weren't about to suddenly loosen now. Frustration, wretchedness, filling the pit of his chest with something hot, Subaru waited, on the verge of tears, for the crawling presence to arrive.

And then—

"R..."

At last, the presence that had been crawling toward him reached Subaru's body.

The throat that had been about to catch it, to cry out in joy, froze.

Because the touch of it was far too light, far too cold, to belong to anyone living.

"R-Rem...?"

At the feet of Subaru, sitting up on his knees, Rem's body lay collapsed face-down. Her body, trembling in small tremors, had already lost the warmth of blood, chilled through like ice.

Dragging that body—which by all rights should have already ended—Rem still clung to Subaru's body. There was no sensation from her left hand. Only her right hand climbed up Subaru's leg, his waist, his chest, coming to cover him from the front as though embracing him.

Silently receiving that embrace from the dead, Subaru had no idea what was happening. Before his eyes, he believed without a doubt that it was Rem holding him. And yet the body he touched, held like this, was clearly dead—there was only an unreal quality to it, as though it moved on nothing but the last smoldering ember of a life nearly extinguished.

The embrace continued for a while like that—how long, he couldn't say.

Then, suddenly, that ice-cold reunion was ended, from Rem's side.

The strength drained from her body, which had been holding Subaru with just the one arm, and she began to slump, collapsing toward his lap. Subaru hastily reached out to catch her, but his hand never got to support Rem's body. Because—

"—Uu!?"

The hand he'd held out was seized by Rem's body and pressed down against the ground.

Pulled forward and thrown down, Subaru was stunned by the suddenness of Rem's movement and by a strength beyond anything he could have imagined—and then even more astonished by what she did next.

A great quantity of liquid poured down from directly above onto Subaru's arms, pinned to the ground. Viscous, smelling of rusted iron, cold water—it was only after some of it splashed into his mouth and he tasted it that Subaru realized its true nature: it was blood, coughed up by Rem.

The revulsion of being drenched in another person's blood sent a shiver down Subaru's spine, but the change that followed, coming in rapid succession, wiped that negative emotion away in an instant.

"—Ma."

The whisper trembled the air by the barest fraction, interfered with Mana, and took effect.

"—Gyaah!"

Pain—an agony as though his wrists were being gouged by a sharp blade—assailed Subaru. Pain sharp enough to wrench his head back began at his wrists and spread its dominion up through his upper arms, to his shoulders.

He had no idea what was happening. Sprayed with blood, pain suddenly lancing through him, a chill of dread that at this rate both his arms would become useless—and then, right after that dread struck him—

—with a sound, the shackles, unable to withstand the pressure from within, cracked and shattered outward.

Metal breaking, a light sound of fragments falling, echoed through the cave floor. Subaru let out a ragged breath as the pain rapidly eased, and became aware of the sense of release spreading through his whole arms, along with a burn-like pain covering his skin. And then, he understood.

"Rem, you..."

She had frozen the blood she'd coughed up using magic, and used the resulting pressure to shatter his hand-cuffs. Naturally, Subaru's own arms, directly exposed to the effect of the magic, were not unscathed. But even so, his wrists could turn, his fingers moved at Subaru's will. Setting aside the pain, it seemed he could move them more or less normally through sheer force of will.

Which meant Rem's gambit had succeeded.

Subaru moved to praise her for it, and instead caught the light body that came falling against his rising form. Light—far too light. The blood had drained from her body, and even now, the very last of her consciousness was about to fade away.

"Rem... wait, Rem. Wait... don't—"

Had he meant to say, don't leave me behind? Or had he wanted to ask, don't you hate me?

Whichever it truly was, Subaru despaired at his own self-centeredness. At the shamefulness of still, even now, trying to protect his own weak self.

When Rem had, quite literally, overturned death itself to save him. When, after this, the flame of her life was about to go out—this time, truly and for good.

"—N."

"Rem?"

Rem's lips, a dead woman's cold lips, tried for the first time to weave together some words. Sparing even a single spoken word, crawling across the ground with a body that would not move, gathering magic with a consciousness gone hazy—the girl who had accomplished her purpose was trying to leave something behind, at the very end. And it was—

"L-live..."

"————!"

"I... love... you..."


She died.


Just now, Rem died.

In Subaru's arms, the light body grew heavy. Even having grown heavy, it was still a light body—a body from which the Soul had entirely departed—and its weight bore down on the whole of Subaru's being.

At the very end, in broken, halting words, she had told Subaru to live.

—A wail of grief trailed on and echoed through the dark cave.

It was several more hours after that before Subaru managed to free himself from the bonds of his footcuffs.

With his hands free, Subaru reached out within the range his footcuffs allowed and managed to grab hold of one of the crosses that had fallen from the hand of the black-robed man, dead from his fight with Rem. Its blade had snapped off, useless as a weapon—but Subaru thought that, if anything, it was for the best. Had that blade still been intact, he might well have committed the outrage of cutting off his own ankle.

"...Light, huh."

He rotated his ankle, most of the flesh scraped away, the bone visible, confirming its function through the pain. Ignoring the searing agony that whited out his mind every time he pressed his weight onto the ground, it seemed there would be no real obstacle to walking.

Standing, Subaru held Rem's remains cradled in both arms. Within his now-free arms, her body—missing two of her four limbs—was pitifully small and light. Held against his chest, small enough to be entirely enveloped and hidden, that smallness—the fact that his life had been saved by such a small figure—filled him with both deep shame and deep gratitude at once.

The cross, its blade in an imperfect state. He carefully, so carefully, kept striking its broken blade-edge against the joint of the footcuff's metal fittings. A contest of endurance—the cross's collapse against the footcuff's destruction, which would give out first—and by the barest margin, the cross won out.

He hurled the freed footcuff at the wall with all his strength, and the Lagmite Ore, struck by the impact, lit the cave with a faint blue glow.

The cold light brought the dim cave into view. Even so weak a light, after not seeing any for dozens of hours, felt dazzling, stinging at Subaru's eyelids.

He felt tears well up from behind his eyes at the pain, and Subaru felt an odd sense of admiration that he still had any tears left in his body. He'd thought he'd wailed and wept until his throat was raw and his tears entirely spent.

"Let's go, Rem."

While the shocked Lagmite Ore still glowed, Subaru set out walking, aiming for outside. Step by step, pain climbed up from his feet with every stride. The pain that climbed up transmitted to his shoulders, and enormous damage was communicated as well to the arms holding Rem.

There was nowhere on his body that didn't hurt. Nowhere that wasn't bleeding. But the part of him that should have felt the most pain, already, felt nothing at all.

Slowly, at a turtle's pace, Subaru and Rem made their way across the cold cave floor. Having lost his shoes, the cold of the bare ground came directly through the soles of his feet, but the body of Rem in his arms was far, far colder still. That cold was nothing, compared to hers.

Passing through the cave's long, narrow passage, he came to the entrance, blocked by rock. Reaching that point, Subaru suddenly found himself at a loss. Before his eyes, the rock blocking the hole was, in height, about equal to Subaru, and in width, about three times his size. Comparing weight, even ten Subarus together surely wouldn't come close.

There was no way he could move it. He wondered, to begin with, how he'd even gotten in here—but his memories from when he'd shut himself away from reality were hazy and blurred, and no manual for how to shift the rock could be found anywhere in his brain.

"It was... a single path, wasn't it..."

He considered that there might be some other exit, but he had no memory of anything resembling one along the way here. Or perhaps, like this rock, there was some cleverly disguised hidden passage—but if there were, how much time, how much luck, would it take to find it?

Flashes of insight, good fortune—neither had ever had much to do with Subaru.

"Don't tell me, after coming all this way... please, don't tell me it's a lie..."

Drained of strength, Subaru voiced his protest at the mercilessness before him in a feeble voice. As if to lean against it, he reached out and touched the rock ahead of him—and at that instant—

The rock, which should have possessed a mass sufficient to support Subaru's weight, suddenly lost its presence altogether.

Subaru, who should have fallen forward against the rock, stumbled, caught his footing before he could fall, barely bracing himself forward—and the world was set ablaze in the vermilion of the setting sun pouring down on him.

"—Guh!"

The orange sunlight struck Subaru with a searing brilliance sharp enough to wring an involuntary cry of pain from him.

Beyond the forest, sinking beyond the hills, the sunset filled the horizon, dyeing the world the color of flame in its final farewell to the day's duty.

Having been in darkness so long, Subaru crouched for a while, waiting for his eyes to accept the light. After some time, standing again, blinking several times, and confirming the light no longer overwhelmed him, he looked around.

As far as he could see, nothing but a spread of trees—naturally, none of it looked familiar. Scanning far in every direction, there wasn't even a hint of any trace of human activity, no forest path, no road. Considering the nature of the people who had been holed up in that cave, it made sense—it must have been a place of confinement completely cut off from any human settlement.

"But... I'll walk."

He had no idea where his destination was, no way to determine where to head. And yet, as though pushed forward by the light weight in his arms, Subaru began walking through the forest. Treading on leaves, stepping over roots, crossing patches of earth, Subaru pressed on into a night that grew steadily darker.

He was certainly somewhere near Mathers territory—near Roswaal's manor. He dug back through his memory: back when his consciousness had hovered on the edge of awareness, Rem had been taking Subaru toward the manor. Rocked in the Dragon Carriage, finding peace in her lap—that was what had been happening, he recalled.

There had been an accident, the carriage overturning—the work of those so-called "Witch Cultists." By their hands, Subaru had been taken captive, and Rem had burned her life away to save him, and now here Subaru was.

What had begun as an attempt to organize the situation instead gathered up, from the fragments of memory recalled, even his hatred along with it, and Subaru's insides were scorched by a bottomless flame.

Thinking of Rem, his heart ached as though squeezed, with gratitude and remorse both. Remembering Petelgeuse, his body creaked as if about to burst with hatred and vengeful rage.

Anger, grief, hatred, affection—all of it supported Subaru, drove him forward.

The road ahead was undetermined, and there was nothing to guide him either. And yet Subaru's feet did not stop, his consciousness did not fade, and he kept struggling on.

—And perhaps that was the miracle that befell him.

With no signpost anywhere, nothing to rely on, having refused only to stop walking, at the end of that road lay what he had sought, the place he had wanted to reach.

A miracle refers to some mysterious phenomenon that surpasses the power of man. Then surely, what visited Subaru—arriving at his destination by the shortest possible distance, without borrowing anyone's hand—was unmistakably a miracle.

For the first time since he'd come to this world, the world had at last granted Subaru a miracle. If there was a god who governed fate, it had, for the first time, smiled upon him.

And that smile, that miracle—

"—Ha."

—came pouring down only that one single time.

The exact same hell he had once seen before was, once again, trampling that village underfoot.

Houses burned to the ground, villagers stained with blood. Corpses whose lives had been taken despite their futile resistance had been carelessly gathered at the center of the village, piled into a mountain of the dead.

Slashed open by blades, run through, burned by magic, crushed—there was no hope of survivors here.

If anything was different from before, it was that the degree of mutilation on the villagers' corpses had grown even worse, and—

"Petra. Mild. Lucas. Meina. Cain, Dain..."

—that the mangled bodies of the children, too, had now been incorporated into that vile display.

Still holding Rem, the strength drained from Subaru's knees.

He collapsed on the spot, clutching the cold body in his arms tight, tighter still, and let out a sob.

What had he even been doing?

Why had he known this would happen, and let it slip past him unattended?

Until he'd come out of the forest path and spotted black smoke rising from the direction of the village, Subaru had completely erased this scene, this hellscape that had shattered his heart before, from his mind.

No—he had looked away from it. Pretending to be consumed with grief over Rem's death, using his unending hatred for Petelgeuse as an excuse.

Trying to run from what had happened, Subaru had once again chosen his own precious self.

The result was the scene before him now.

The children were dead here because Rem—who, last time, would have protected them—had never made it to the village. In exchange for Subaru surviving, the children had been brought, at the end of their suffering, to the taking of their lives.

The despicable reality ate away at Subaru's heart.

Now he understood. He understood everything.

It was Petelgeuse.

He had killed the villagers, killed the children, killed Rem.

Not once, but twice now—he had done something unforgivable.

His course was set. He understood now what had to be done.

Petelgeuse had to die. He had to kill him, kill him, kill him utterly, burn away even the smallest fragment of his cells, erase his very existence.

If he didn't, there was no way to answer for their deaths.

His thoughts dyed themselves entirely in the color of hatred.

His vision went red, and he could feel most of what little blood he had left rushing to his head, overflowing from his nose and running down his face. Roughly wiping away the blood dripping from his nostrils, he readjusted his hold on Rem so as not to defile her, and stood up. His knees trembled, his ankles shook violently—it was a wonder he could stand at all, let alone walk.

"Kill him, kill him, kill him, kill him, I'll kill him..."

But if he could walk, if he could go on, he would tear that man's throat out with his teeth.

Dragging a consciousness cemented over with murderous intent, Subaru headed toward the manor. He had borne witness to the village's hell. Next was the manor. What had been waiting for him at the manor, again? Right before he died, right before he was sent back to try again—what had happened there? His memory was frayed, indistinct, unclear.

He recalled reaching the manor, seeing something decisive, something that had shattered his heart to pieces. Straining his brain, desperately trying to recall what that had been.

He had found Rem, dead.

And that experience, this time, he had already lived through. Rem had died, and the shock that her death and devotion had dealt Subaru was immeasurable.

"Ku ha."

A laugh spilled out of him, unbidden.

Truly, truly—had nothing changed at all?

Only the order was scrambled. Nothing that had happened had actually changed. Had there ever before been a time when he'd spent his do-over so utterly, pointlessly?

No matter what the course of events, Subaru should have gained something by passing through death.

But now, his spirit shattered, unable to save a single thing, having crossed paths with hell once again—what value did he have?

His murderous intent, before long, began to lose track of who exactly it was directed at.

Petelgeuse—that name alone had been what supported Subaru. That should be enough. The one he wanted to kill should be him. Kill him, and he would kill, he would kill him.

After killing him—wouldn't it be nice if he, too, just died.

Noise began to mix into his thoughts, and Subaru's consciousness flickered on and off, again and again.

Standing once more at the boundary between sanity and madness, Subaru looked ahead with bloodshot eyes.

Whatever happened, getting to the manor was the priority now—choosing, as always, to put off whatever was in front of him.

And then—


"——————————!!"


The moment he crested the hill, Subaru watched Roswaal Manor collapse.

The roof caved in, the terrace crumbled to pieces.

The windowpanes shattered all at once, scattering glittering shards, and the cracked white walls were torn apart as though ripped to shreds.

Having reached the very gate, Subaru stared up, stunned, at that overwhelming destruction.

Like a demolition job carried out with explosives, the manor lost its outline in an instant. The familiar building lost its shape entirely, scattering its wreckage across the carefully tended garden, wiping away every trace that it had ever been a manor at all.

He searched his memory, hunting for any experience of encountering this sight before. But no matching memory came. Perhaps the shock had been too great, and he simply didn't remember the moment of his own death.

"Wh-what's..."

Bewilderment on his lips, what rose in his mind was the mocking sneer of that gaunt man. If the massacre in the village had been that man's doing, then surely he had turned the point of his atrocities toward the manor as well.

Perhaps that method was what had led to this—the manor's collapse. Even so—

"What could he possibly have done to cause this..."

Clutching his head at a sight beyond comprehension, Subaru breathed white breath, his eyes wandering in confusion. Seeking, in his loneliness, an even firmer hold on the sensation in his arms, he only found greater cold traveling through his palm, breathing sorrow into his chest.

And then, belatedly, Subaru realized—

—that his own ragged breath was visibly clouding white.

Once he noticed it, a pain like something piercing his skin was wrapping around his entire body. His exhaled breath was white, and the instant it scattered into the air it transformed into droplets of ice. And the oxygen he breathed in was ice-cold, freezing the lungs that took it in, tormenting Subaru from within.

He had no idea what was happening.

The warmth drained from his entire body, and standing itself became difficult; he collapsed. Crouching on the spot, he fell forward, toppling to the ground, still holding Rem's body, lying on his side, and could no longer move.

This was no longer on the scale of lack of blood, or exhausted stamina.

His will failed to reach his fingertips; his consciousness began to detach from his flesh—a sensation Subaru knew. He had experienced it many times already, a desolation and helplessness he could never grow used to.

Trying, even a little, to resist the approaching end, Subaru, inside a body that would not move, sent commands from his brain through his nerves to his whole body, searching for anywhere that would still obey his will. But the only thing that answered was the barest movement of the eyes within their sockets, and all it could do was gaze up at the collapsed manor.

"—Ah."

And then, within that now-truly-unmoving, fixed field of vision, Subaru saw it.


—It was a beast, standing atop the ruins of what had once been the manor.


A beast draped in gray fur over its entire body, with eyes that gleamed gold.

Standing on all four limbs, its long, long tail swaying, its bearing was serene, all too mystical.

And above all, that beast possessed a body so overwhelmingly massive it could almost be mistaken for the manor itself.

Watching that figure from a distance, Subaru understood the cause of the manor's collapse. That beast had suddenly emerged from within the manor. When a body of that size erupted from the inside, the manor, naturally, could never have withstood the pressure.

Shaking its body, surveying its surroundings with a commanding gaze, was a colossal beast, feline in appearance, clad in gray fur. From its mouth, it bared sharp fangs and exhaled breath like a white blizzard, repainting the world into a pure-white hell that froze everything living.

*What is that*, he thought, and then his eyes finally dimmed to white, and Subaru realized, without quite noticing when, that his throat had closed.

The cold he'd felt so intensely was gone now, and in its place he even felt a kind of warmth. A temptation flickered at the edge of his mind, to entrust everything to that warmth—and Subaru forgot it all: the hatred hot enough to scorch his own body, the grief sharp enough to tear his Soul apart, everything.

Forgetting, forgetting, his consciousness sank toward the far shore of oblivion, toward the far shore of a freezing warmth.


《Sleep—together with my daughter》


Right before he slipped into sleep, he thought he heard someone's voice.

Everything, everything, becoming pleasantly meaningless—Natsuki Subaru melted, sinking down into it.

Melting, melting, melting, —and vanished.
