Arc 8 Chapter 25: "The Wish of the Dying"

—His Imperial Majesty Vincent Vollachia.

Addressed that way by Flop, who regarded him with an earnest expression, Vincent closed one eye.
Flop and Medium, the two golden-haired siblings who had suddenly confined Vincent in a single compartment of the Coupled Dragon Carriages, plainly did not understand the gravity of what they had done.
It was an outrage that could earn them the death penalty if anyone found out, and the reason they'd committed it was, of all things, to deliver someone else's message. It was madness.
And on top of that—

"Before I tell you the message, would you mind if I told a little story from the past first?"

Flop said, still wearing that same earnest expression.

"—————"

Unable to read Flop's intentions, Vincent stayed silent, one eye still shut. Perhaps taking that silence as a tailwind in his favor, Flop went on with an "and also—"

"I know it's shameless of me... but once you've heard the message, I'd love it if you'd grant me one favor."

"Oi."

"My, my, what a scary face, Your Imperial Majesty! But don't forget—I'm the only one who knows the message you need to hear, so you can't very well silence me carelessly."

"There is also the option of ordering Olbart Dunkelkenn to have Shinobi torture the truth out of your mouth."

"Wouldn't it be better if we talked this out peacefully, Your Imperial Majesty!"

The moment Vincent's voice carried a hint of threat, Flop immediately threw up both hands.
Proposing to negotiate with an emperor was not something that could be dismissed as mere audacity. Still, whatever this preamble of an old story was, and whatever favor he meant to piggyback on it, if he was bothering to bring them up at all, they must matter to him.
That said—

"But bro, we don't have much time, right? If this keeps up, Gozchin and the others are gonna start freaking out that Abelchin hasn't come back~. Never mind the favor, but what's this old story thing?"

Medium tilted her head, apparently not having been briefed by her brother at all.

"You, for your part, should not go along with your brother's outrages without even asking what they're about."

"Eh~? But it's something bro's doing, right? Then whatever it is, I'll just help out~. That's my family's vow. Abelchin's the same, right?"

"Fool. Do you not know how a Vollachian emperor comes to be?"

Medium blinked at the answer, saying such nonsense with a straight face. At her utterly earnest reaction, Vincent shot Flop a look of reproach—there were limits even to a lack of common sense.
It was hardly a pleasant thing to say, but it was common knowledge that the Vollachian imperial family settled the succession through siblings killing one another. That Medium didn't even know that made Vincent suspect Flop had been rather selective in what he'd taught her.
But at that look from Vincent, Flop smiled, for some reason, and said,

"I understand what that look means, Your Imperial Majesty. You're surprised my sister has no common sense, aren't you?"

"What else could it mean. And stop calling me 'Your Imperial Majesty' at every turn. If there is no one else it could apply to, 'Your Majesty' alone will suffice."

"I see, I see. Understood, Majesty-kun."

"—————"

Nodding with a smile and continuing on like that, Flop made Vincent narrow his eyes.
As he'd sensed earlier from that earnest expression, today Flop was not merely the good-natured merchant he presented himself as—he seemed intent on showing the face behind the title.
Precisely the face of a man who had some matter of his own concerning Vincent.

"Sister, you asked me, didn't you? Why I'm telling an old story. Well, it's because there's a connection between our past and Majesty-kun here."

"Eh!? Really!? Between us and Abelchin!? What, what!?"

"Ha ha, now, now, you're being far too forgetful, sister. Majesty-kun is the Empire's Emperor. Which means he's connected to the 'Nine Divine Generals' too—"

"—You mean Bal-nii?"

Medium, who had widened her eyes in surprise, suddenly lost her vivacity from her expression.
The name she'd just spoken, combined with the words "Nine Divine Generals" just before it—when the two connected, a single name naturally rose in Vincent's mind as well.

"Are you two connected to Balleroy Temeglyph?"

"Ah, that's right, Majesty-kun. Sworn brothers, so to speak, you might call it. Precious, precious, beloved family, with whom we spent our most formative years!"

Clenching his fist tight, Flop answered in a raised voice, and Vincent let out a breath.
Medium's brows drooped as she looked back and forth between her brother and Vincent. Her expression was plainly clouded with confusion, and something like sorrow besides.

"As for this dragon carriage—I hear that Senior Count Serena Dracroy is riding along. Actually, my sister and I were once in the Count's care ourselves."

"Seri-nee..."

"Incidentally, that's the name Countess Dracroy forced on Medium so she wouldn't be treated formally, or—heaven forbid—as a woman older than her actual age. Amusing, isn't it?"

"Get to the point."

Dragging out a pointless topic to sap the other party's concentration was a peddler's trick.
The connection between these siblings and Serena could already be guessed from the moment they'd said they were tied to Balleroy. Balleroy Temeglyph had originally been Serena Dracroy's subordinate, elevated to the Nine Divine Generals on the strength of his proven ability—a "General" of promising future.

But if one knew how Balleroy's story had ended, the eyes of most soldiers and officers turned to disappointment.
Rebellion against the Emperor, failure, and a miserable death as the result—that was the end of Balleroy known to the world, the ending that would remain in the history books for the man these two called family.

"—No."

Or perhaps, having been revived as one of the Undead, what would remain of Balleroy Temeglyph in the history books would not be as a traitor, but as one of the beings who brought the Empire to ruin.
Vincent did not speak that thought aloud to Flop and the others.
Bringing up the matter of Balleroy's revival as an Undead here and now, he judged, would only push the topic he wanted further out of reach.
In any case—

"Right, let's get to the point. The old story isn't so long, either. The gist of it, my sister already let slip without meaning to."

Faintly drooping his brows, Flop slowly shook his head.
Medium's face showed she had no idea what he meant by that, but Vincent said nothing of it. He decided the siblings' endlessly meandering conversation only needed correcting when it actually strayed off course.

"Medium and I were orphans, raised in a wretched institution. We were rescued from there and taken to Countess Dracroy's domain... and it was there we met Balleroy and became sworn brothers. Following so far?"

"—Continue."

"They were busy, hectic days in all sorts of ways. Both Medium and I were so ignorant of the outside world that everything we saw was fresh and new. Countess Dracroy is a woman of very broad vision, so she had us, who had nowhere else to go, receive an education."

"Judging by the results, that education did not take with your sister."

"I liked movin' my body together with Bal-nii and the others better..."

Medium pouted, having recovered a bit of her usual spirit.
The way Flop described the state of the Dracroy domain had reached Vincent's ears before, and, incidentally, he considered such domain management commendable.
Called the "Scorching Duchess" and often praised more for the fierceness of her temperament, Serena Dracroy's true strength lay in a flexibility of thought unbound by convention.

In most cases, the Vollachian Empire had no perspective for taking the long view.
Most children were expected to serve neither as labor nor as fighting strength, so many were born, and among them, those blessed with innate ability and favorable circumstances survived, with only a very small number growing to become men and women of strength.
Unless they were of one's own household, the notion of educating children from a young age and cultivating talent broadly was, in this country, dismissed almost entirely as armchair theorizing.

Flop and Medium were siblings blessed with that rare opportunity. —No, Balleroy too, whose talent had likewise been discovered, was surely one such case.

"Of course, it wasn't all fun and games, and Countess Dracroy wasn't only kind to us either. Back then, the Countess herself had only just usurped the domain from her own father, so she was quite busy."

"We got into some real dangerous scrapes too, huh."

"That was when we were attacked by assassins after the Countess's life. That was quite the ordeal."

As the two reminisced fondly, Vincent probed for the thrust of the story.
Though, in truth, he'd already found his bearing early on. To put it plainly, there were words one grew used to hearing, looks one grew used to receiving.
This pair's, too, was likely something along those same lines.
Namely—

"Having weathered such days together, a strong bond formed between my sister and me and Balleroy. Precisely because of that bond, I want to ask you, Majesty-kun."

"—————"

"Our sworn brother, Balleroy Temeglyph—did he really lose his life, as is widely known to everyone, as a foolish, shortsighted traitor, chasing a desire beyond his station? ...Is that so?"

Having posed the question, Flop's expression had regained its earlier stillness.
Flop was asking Vincent for the truth behind the death of Balleroy Temeglyph—their sworn brother to them, a traitor to the Empire.

It was, after all, a familiar line, a familiar look.

To die on the battlefield is a warrior's honor; to remain unbowed even run through by the sword is the way of the Sword Wolf.
Even in the Vollachian Empire, where such things are exalted, not everyone can celebrate the death of someone close to them. Seeking meaning, seeking a reason, in a death is only natural to the human heart.
So being asked this by Flop and the others here was, too, a familiar lament.

"Why do you doubt it? Do you suspect that the rumors that spread even among the common folk have been contaminated with impurities, unworthy of trust?"

"No, that's not it. It's just that, since we happen to have this chance to speak with the party directly involved, wouldn't it be strange if we didn't want to hear something far more credible than mere rumor?"

"The chance to converse was one you two forced into being, however."

Vincent added that, to the somewhat convenient way Flop had twisted the conversation.
That said, he pondered for a while—unusual for him.

The death of Balleroy Temeglyph as told among the common folk.
His death, which would surely remain recorded in the history books, was recorded as a miserable end resulting from a failed rebellion he had plotted.
What Flop and Medium had heard should be no more and no less than that. And Vincent, too, had no consideration of restoring Balleroy's honor after his death.
A story no more and no less than that—that was the version that ought to spread through the world.

"I want you to answer, Majesty-kun. If I know that, then I too will tell you the message—"

"—. I know not what you two want, but..."

Vincent was about to answer that there was no mistake, that it matched the rumor the two had heard.
But then—

"—Abelchin."

A weak, brief call interrupted Vincent's words.

"—————"

Standing before the compartment door, blocking the way out—Medium. No, she probably no longer meant to block it at all.
Standing there, she'd drawn her shoulders in small, tall as she was for a woman, hugging her own arms tightly as she looked at Vincent. Tears welling in those blue eyes.

Tearful eyes were nothing new to him—he'd seen them in pleas for life, in supplications, more times than he could count.
So it wasn't as though this shook Vincent's heart in the slightest. Only, though his heart did not waver, his composure was granted a moment for thought.

—He did not know what Flop and Medium wanted.

That was what Vincent had been about to answer.
But was that really true? He surely knew what the two of them wanted. What they wanted was plain, and Vincent possessed it.

Possessing it, and still not speaking—that was Vincent's way.
Only, that way carried the crown of "as it had always been."

"—————"

The way "as it had always been" was one where Vincent decided everything entirely by his own judgment.
But by that same "as it had always been" way, Vincent had been swept off his feet by a loyal retainer. Struck down, his confident deductions knocked wide of the mark, by a man with a blessedly simple head.

The way "as it had always been" had its limits.
What was demanded of Vincent now had to be a way "beyond what it had always been."
And in order to obtain that way—

"—The death of Balleroy Temeglyph holds a truth different from the rumor spread among the common folk."

So Vincent chose an answer different from the way "as it had always been."

"—————"

The instant they heard Vincent's answer, a change came over the expressions of Flop and Medium.
Flop's eyes widened slightly; Medium blinked, stunned. Both looked like surprise, but the quality of that surprise differed between them.
From the difference in their two reactions, Vincent understood.

"Flop O'Connell, you knew that the truth did not match the rumor."

"Eh... bro?"

"'Knew' isn't quite the right word. The story being rumored was simply too unlike Balleroy, and even Medium thought as much, I'd wager."

"Y-yeah..."

Put on the spot, Medium nodded, confusion still plain on her face.
But Flop, having made his sister nod along, had not entirely denied Vincent's question.
At Vincent's gaze, Flop tilted his head slightly.

"Surely, Majesty-kun, you don't think that because Balleroy and I were close, I heard the story directly from Balleroy himself beforehand, and that this was all a bluff on your part?"

"—. It's possible, but next to impossible. The man I knew would not have breathed a word to anyone, right up to the moment of execution, so as not to raise even slightly the chance of failure."

"...Right. I think you're exactly right, Majesty-kun. Good. It seems I really can discuss the Balleroy I knew well, with you of all people. After all—"

"—————"

"Ever since that incident, no matter where in the Empire I heard Balleroy's name spoken, it only ever sounded like the story of someone Medium and I didn't know."

Furrowing his handsome brows, Flop's blue eyes wavered with desolation.
It was surely the truth. Vincent felt no surprise at it.
It was Chisha who had arranged the rumors to be that way, Berstetz who had given the order for it to be so, and Vincent who had commanded that it be done.
All of it, to conceal the true reason Balleroy Temeglyph had become a traitor.
That was—

"—That rebellion was not Balleroy's true intention."

"Bro!?"

At the words Flop had spun out, Medium cried out as if in a scream.
Her round eyes went wide, and she shook her head hard, side to side,

"That's weird, that doesn't make sense. Bal-nii, I mean... Abelchin too! Say something! Tell bro that's wrong..."

"I correct what needs correcting. What does not, I leave be. That is all."

"There's no reason to say anything at all..."

Stunned, at a loss for words, Medium looked as if she couldn't grasp the situation.
Flop, meanwhile, let out a long, deep breath, taking in the fact that Vincent had not denied the suspicion he himself had voiced—the truth behind Balleroy's death.

By the common rumor, Balleroy Temeglyph, dissatisfied with his rank among the Nine Divine Generals, had aimed higher, risen in rebellion, and died in defeat—a foolish loser.
That had served to prove the solidity of Vincent Vollachia's throne, and had done the work of dampening the reckless fires of ambition in many an aspiring schemer.

"Ironically, by my own scheming I fanned this recent civil war, and reignited the flames I had once weakened."

"—. Yes, the peace was short-lived. But that was probably just a result being put to use afterward—it can't have been the original purpose from the start."

"—————"

"Balleroy's purpose lay elsewhere. Majesty-kun merely made use of the outcome; the premise must have been something else entirely. Do you know that purpose as well, Majesty-kun?"

He'd had plenty of time to think it through. That was likely why Flop's account flowed without hesitation.
Balleroy had died, a rumor differing from the truth had spread, and Flop had gone on wondering all this time where the truth actually lay. Hence he had not let slip this once-in-a-lifetime chance.

The "Magic Bullet Marksman," Balleroy Temeglyph.
The purpose for which he had joined that rebellion, that was—

"—Revenge for Corporal Miles, who died on a special mission to the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica."

"—!"

"What is there to be surprised at, Flop O'Connell. This too must be a fact you already understood. You keep on testing your Emperor. That is disrespectful."

At Flop's reaction—having caught his breath—Vincent snorted and said as much.
Just as with the earlier question of whether the rumor about Balleroy was true, this too was likely a trap Flop had set for Vincent.
To confirm whether this conversation was worth continuing.

But at that single word from Vincent, Flop shook his head, saying "No."
He wore an expression of surprise—one mixed, in fact, of two kinds of surprise—as he said,

"I don't mean to feign innocence to weasel out of it. It's true, I was trying to test you. But—"

"What is it?"

"...I was startled by your answer, and by the fact that Miles-nii's name came up at all."

"—————"

At Flop, whose voice too carried surprise, Vincent narrowed his eyes.
Setting aside the former answer, which had been tested for, the latter deserved no surprise at all. He knew of Corporal Miles, deeply connected as he was to Balleroy. —No, not Miles alone.

"It is only natural to keep track of the names, faces, and standing of those who serve me. What do you imagine soldiers and officers receive orders from, and pour their all into, if not that?"

Even setting that aside, the position of Emperor was, at all times, tantamount to having a blade perpetually aimed at one's back.
Since he commanded them to stake their lives, he made a point of knowing those he commanded so. Since they might well come to take his life, he made sure never to be caught unaware of who they were.
And the names of those who, obeying his commands, lost their lives—those, he must never forget.

"I am not like those of the Kingdom. I do not grieve over the death of every single soldier and officer, one by one. I merely remember. Hence, both Corporal Miles and Balleroy Temeglyph—"

"Abelchin doesn't forget...?"

"—That is all. It is no comfort, even."

Vincent answered so to Medium's weak, halting words.
Having said that much, Vincent added, to Flop and Medium,

"If, by chance, you two wish for the restoration of that fool Balleroy's honor, it cannot be granted."

"—! W-why not?"

"Should it become known that the truth differs from the rumor that has spread, there will be those who cast suspicion on every other rumor as well. Their rise would put needless cracks in the Empire's foundation. Such a thing..."

"Balleroy wouldn't want that either. Right?"

Flop confirmed with Vincent just how much he already understood.
Vincent lowered his chin—concealment now would be meaningless. On Medium's face, unease spread all the stronger.
To his sister, Flop spoke, kind as an elder brother should be, yet with a firmness laced in as well,

"Sister, you said it yourself, didn't you? That whatever I do, you'll help with it, no matter what. That's our family's vow. I can say the same, with my head held high."

"T-that's right. But so what? What does that have to do with—"

"If Balleroy had truly, seriously intended to kill Majesty-kun and carry out the rebellion, he would have come to us and asked for our help. Even if it were only to raise the odds by the barest sliver, if he'd meant it in earnest, that's what he would have done."

"—Ah."

At Flop's words, Medium's eyes went wide.
The "family's vow" she herself had spoken of earlier—Vincent did not know just how binding a force it carried, but if it held enough weight that she would not refuse even an invitation to rebel against the Emperor, then Flop's theory held together.

"Balleroy never truly meant for the rebellion to succeed. And yet, he did have the purpose of avenging Miles-nii. Given that those two things contradict each other, it means Balleroy never considered Majesty-kun to be Miles-nii's killer."

"—————"

"Lastly, might I ask just one more thing?"

Surely he had already spent more than enough time being shameless, and yet Vincent found himself at a loss for words at the sheer nerve of Flop, still raising one more finger.
As Vincent remained silent, Flop apparently took it as a tailwind,

"Was Balleroy's cherished wish—his revenge for Miles-nii—was it fulfilled?"

"—. No."

"I see... is that so."

Briefly, Vincent gave no further information beyond that.
Even had Flop sought it, Vincent had no intention of handing it over. Even if Flop resolved to carry out the revenge for the two brothers, Balleroy and Miles, it was impossible for him to accomplish it.
More than anything, that was—

"Because Balleroy Temeglyph would not wish it."

"Sorry for going on so long, Majesty-kun."

Having finished the exchange with Vincent over Balleroy Temeglyph, Flop bowed his head.
A bow given somewhat belatedly, at that. In truth, it had indeed been a long, drawn-out exchange.

"In this moment of crisis, can you not imagine how precious my time is?"

"I have confidence in my eye for appraising things, if I do say so myself. Which is exactly why I judged that the message entrusted to me would fetch its highest price right now, and used it to obtain the answer my sister and I wanted."

"That is beyond mere disrespect. And besides, do not tell me falsehoods."

"Hm?"

"Not the answer you and Medium wanted—the answer you wanted. This was merely me indulging that wish of yours. Under the name of this 'family's vow' of yours."

Jutting his chin, Vincent corrected the error in Flop's words. At that, Medium's eyes widened, and Flop too, after a moment of surprise, nodded and said, "You're right."

"It was I who wanted to know the answer. If heads are to roll, I'd ask that it be mine alone."

"Fool. As if I could indulge any more of your idle talk. Medium, do not look at me with such eyes either."

Saying he could not spare even a moment more for such banter, Vincent snorted at Medium's gaze, which seemed to be pleading for her brother's life, before continuing,

"Now then, are you satisfied?"

"Ah, yes, quite. It came through clearly that Majesty-kun, precious as your time was, responded in earnest—without falling for my repeated bluffs, at that."

Vincent fell silent at Flop, who so boldly admitted to having tested his Emperor.
Had Vincent's answer not matched what he'd hoped for, what would Flop have done?
Sensing the unspoken question in that silence, Flop laughed.

"In that case, I suppose I would have taken this message not to you, Majesty-kun, but to milord instead, had the Empire saved and had him become Emperor."

"You..."

"Ah, well, good thing it didn't come to that! ...You turned out to be one of the makers of the world I ought to take revenge upon, and yet, not so, after all."

"A world you ought to take revenge upon?"

At Flop's words, mixed with a sigh of relief, Vincent's brow moved.
Then, alongside the conversation between Emperor and elder brother, Medium, sniffling with a runny nose, answered with an "Um, well—"

"Bro always says it. That when something bad happens, it's not that there's one bad person somewhere—it's the world that turns that person bad that's really scary~."

"Quite roughly put, but that's the gist of it! Well, since I dislike a world where such unreasonable, unpleasant things happen, doing my best to improve things bit by bit, even in small ways, is my own particular brand of revenge against the world."

"—. How trivial."

A world to take revenge upon, and the makers of that world.
Thinking it sounded just like something Cecilus Segmunt might say, Vincent murmured as much. Catching the murmur, Flop gave a wry smile, while Medium, her eyes red, protested with a "Wha~t's that supposed to mean!"

At the siblings' reaction, Vincent thought, all the more inwardly, how trivial it all was.
Without question, Vincent was one of those who had shaped the world in which Flop felt such injustice. Whatever reasons he had for contemplating reform, so long as he had not achieved it, he remained no less a target of that revenge.
Why, then, had they excluded Vincent from that reckoning? He could not understand it.

Surely it could not be that abandoning the way "as it had always been" was the cause—and thinking of whose ideas he'd drawn upon in aiming for a way "beyond what it had always been," he did not even want to consider it.
More than that—

"You said something. That should I fail to meet your wishes, you would convey the contents of the message to someone else, and by that means remove the Empire's peril."

"Hm? Ah, yes, that's right. I meant to leave it to milord."

"It matters not to whom it would be entrusted. Rather, if it can be said that entrusting it to that man could remove the 'Great Disaster,' then it must be a truly weighty move indeed."

It was a hand that Chisha—who had deceived Vincent and schemed this and that—must have left behind.
What sort of hand might it be.

"Speak it word for word, without omission. Consider that a single dropped clause could change the Empire's future."

"That was always my intent, but my, that's frightening to hear! I was entrusted with three messages in all... ahem, ahem. Cheer me on so I can pull this off well, sister."

"Yeah! Leave it to me, bro! You can do it~!"

At Vincent's threat, as Flop steeled himself, Medium raised her voice in encouragement.
Before this farce, Vincent said nothing, and Flop cleared his throat once more,

"First, one. —As for Cecilus Segmunt, that one had his skills traced and copied by Olbart Dunkelkenn, was shrunk down, and thrown into the Sword-Slave Isles. Likely he'll claw his own way out under his own power, but should he be delayed, you're to know he can be found there."

"—————"

"Oh, when he spoke in front of me, he looked the spitting image of you, Majesty-kun, you know. His manner of speaking and tone were the same too. But this—this was exactly how he put it."

At the sight of Vincent narrowing his dark eyes, Flop hastily added that.
But there was no need for such explanation. In fact, it was an undeniable fact that, ever since driving Vincent from the throne, Chisha had gone about disguised as the Emperor.
Being the cautious man he was, Chisha would surely never have reverted his appearance even once, and no doubt kept even his manner of speech mimicking Vincent right up to his final moment.

When he'd witnessed the apparently-shrunken Cecilus in the capital, Vincent had already more or less guessed it was Chisha's doing. That the purpose was to remove Cecilus, an obstacle to driving Vincent from the throne, was likewise obvious.

"That one cannot be talked out of it, being Chisha. Had Cecilus remained in the capital as he normally was, no matter what preparations were made, it would not have been possible to unseat me from the throne."

Put plainly: he had simply been in the way of the plan, so he'd been shrunk and cast out. Given the opponent was Chisha, it made sense that even Cecilus might let his guard down enough to be shrunk.

"No objection. Continue."

"Then, two. —The 'Sanctum' has been left within the Fortress City. Whether it is present or not will reveal the nature of the 'Great Disaster' that would lead the Empire to ruin."

"—————"

"Honestly, this one seems quite important, but I don't understand the details. It wasn't really an atmosphere where I could ask for clarification..."

Flop spoke without confidence, then caught his breath.
The reason lay in the change that had come over Vincent's expression upon hearing this message.

Gritting his teeth hard, Vincent pressed a hand to his mouth and fell silent. His two eyes did not close at once—only the right eye, he shut tight, hard.

The reason he'd headed for the Fortress City of Garkla was that he'd thought Chisha would surely have left some countermeasure there. —But he was wrong.
What Chisha Gold had left in the Fortress City was not a countermeasure. It was the "answer."

"We must enter the Fortress City at the earliest possible moment."

"Wait, wait, Abelchin! There's supposed to be three messages, right? There's still one more!"

Medium stopped Vincent, who—begrudging even a moment's delay—had started toward the door. At her words, Vincent halted his steps and let out a deep breath.
The first was Cecilus, the second, the "answer"—then what had been left for the third?

"Let me hear it. If there is yet some card left concealed—"

He would use that too, to protect the Vollachia Empire from the "Great Disaster."
Meeting the dark eyes of Vincent, who spoke so, Flop cleared his throat once more, meaning to convey the entrusted message just as it was—

"—Your Majesty."

"—————"

"My assistance in removing your wheels ends here, I'm afraid."


　—.
　——.
　————————.


"—What an utter fool."

Saying only that, Vincent turned toward the compartment door.
To Vincent's retreating back, Flop's own voice—no longer imitating anyone—called out, "Wait, would you!"

"Even if you rush out of the room in a hurry, won't your speed in reaching Garkla stay the same! Unless Majesty-kun can outrun an Earth Dragon, that is!"

"I must hasten to the Fortress City. If need be, I shall have a Dragon Ship, or anything else, sent flying."

"The sky isn't necessarily safe either! There might be dead Flying Dragons about! And besides, there's still one story left... Medium!"

"Y-yeah! Got it, bro!"

His heart straining to reach the Fortress City.
Flop, pointing out the dangers of a Dragon Ship from a perfectly reasonable angle, gave the order to Medium, and once again had his sister block Vincent's path.

"Whether it's hastening the dragon carriage or sharing information with Berstetz and the others, there is nothing to be gained by delay. If you obstruct me, I will have you executed."

"Bro, Abelchin's eyes are serious!?"

"But when it comes to seriously being alive, we're not exactly ones to lose either! Majesty-kun! I said it at the very start, didn't I? That once you'd heard the message, I wanted you to grant me a favor."

"—————"

Still burning with impatience, Vincent turned to face Flop, who stood before and behind him together with Medium, blocking his way.
Indeed, it was Flop who had shamelessly laid down the two conditions—an old story, and a favor.
Vincent had no memory of agreeing to it, but neither had he flatly refused, in order to draw out the message.

"Say it, then."

He had already told them that restoring Balleroy's honor was impossible.
Beyond that, Vincent could not, for the moment, think of anything Flop and Medium might wish for that would be impossible to grant.
If it proved too outlandish a request to accept, there would be nothing to be done about it, but—

"Majesty-kun, once this crisis has passed, you'll return to the Vollachian throne and work to restore the Empire to how it was... no, to make it even better, won't you?"

"I have my reservations about your phrasing, but broadly speaking, yes."

Vincent himself had certain plans of his own in mind, in part, though now that Chisha's scheming had swept his feet out from under him, he felt the need to reconsider whether those plans still held.
For that reason, Vincent found no room to deny it, and nodded.
At that answer, Flop nodded and smiled, as if to say this was just what he'd hoped to hear,

"Eh?"

"Eh?"

To begin with, the reason Vincent had gone without taking a wife until now had been to eliminate needless entanglements that might interfere with the aforementioned plan.
If that plan was to collapse, there was no longer such great need to cling to it.
As for the "Imperial Selection Ceremony" too, he had felt the need for reform.
Hence, Vincent, too, as Vollachian Emperor, needed only to face the duties he was obliged to fulfill.

At Vincent's answer, Flop let out a breath of relief.
And then—

"Eh?"

—only Medium, left behind by the conversation all on her own, kept tilting her head, again and again.
