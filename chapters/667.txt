Arc 8 Chapter 50: "Eugard Vollachia"

—Eugard Vollachia was a king forced into solitude.

Before he was even old enough to understand the world, he was struck with the "Curse of Thorns," and his body was left in a state that not even family or servants could approach — forcing him to grow up in a severely skewed environment.
Because of his congenital analgesia, Eugard — who was himself the source of the "Curse of Thorns" — never had to taste its suffering firsthand. But from the pained expressions and screams of others, he understood that his very existence was a source of affliction and agony to those around him, and so he accepted a life of isolation.

As for the "Imperial Selection Ceremony," his resolve to fight it through to the end was shaky from the start.
By any reasonable measure, he judged that for someone afflicted with the "Curse of Thorns" to ascend to the throne would bring far too many disadvantages to the governance of the Vollachia Empire.
Even supposing he could become Emperor, an Emperor who could never once appear in person to receive officials holding key posts in the nation, or to negotiate with dignitaries of other countries — such a thing, he thought, ought not to be.

Therefore, from the moment he concluded that he should not win the "Imperial Selection Ceremony," Eugard decided that his remaining lifespan would last only until the age at which the ceremony began.
Until that age, Eugard devoted himself to accumulating as much knowledge as he could, striving to fulfill his duty as a member of the imperial family — that is, to improve and secure the lives of the empire's people, entrusted to his care.

Eaten away by the "Curse of Thorns," forced into solitude — that was Eugard.
And yet, even placed in such circumstances, Eugard never thought himself unfortunate. As for being cursed, so long as there was a rational answer to the question of "why," he bore no grudge against the person who had done it.
There were those born blind, and those born with limbs that would not move freely. He thought of his own inability to stand beside others as merely another variation of the same thing.

And in a world where those who were blind or crippled never ceased to lose their lives because of it, he considered himself fortunate to have survived thanks to his position as a member of the imperial family.
Thanks to his birth into the imperial line, Eugard had been able to live on without ever knowing hunger, even without family or those close to him at his side. If so, then he would fulfill his duty as a member of that family.
It would be a disservice to his mother and the rest of his family, but he could not seize the throne. — If that was so, then at the very least, he would use the not-so-many years remaining to him for the sake of the world that had enfolded him.

—And so, meeting her was the greatest mistake of Eugard's life.

Not because it made him crave the throne he had already given up. Not because it made him cast aside his duty as a member of the imperial family.
He had simply come to wish to live. —To wish to live with Iris.

The "Curse of Thorns" was cast upon Eugard Vollachia, and its effect existed to force solitude upon him.
To achieve that purpose, what condition would bring out its greatest effect?
The opposite of solitude could also be called love. In other words, the "Curse of Thorns" is a curse that drives away that which one loves.
Which is to say—

"The thorns on my chest disappearing, and nothing else's disappearing — that's the answer, yeah."

Biting lightly at the mouthpiece of their pipe, Halibel murmured, stroking their own chest.
Between the Wolf Human and the Mole Human who betrayed the "Thorned Emperor" — and who, in the fairy tale still told to this day, are remembered as traitors — the thorns had vanished from the chest of the former, Halibel, because Eugard had recognized them as a Wolf Human, and they had thereby fallen outside the conditions of the "Curse of Thorns."
The "Curse of Thorns" does not activate on anything but that which is loved.

"Having such an enormous capacity for love — wonder who that was bad news for."

Mixing a note of something like frustration into the purple smoke, Halibel drew hard on the pipe clenched between their teeth.
The special tobacco packed into the bowl burned away in a single draw, filling Halibel's abnormally large lungs with smoke. In the next instant, biting down and flicking the pipe up over their head, Halibel — clad in a black kimono — tilted their upper body, and the long black hair trailing behind them was sliced through as the "Wicked Sword" streaked diagonally past.

Behind them, the capital's skyline tilted at an angle under the aftershock of the strike, and as Halibel's tall frame stepped forward, three more identical tall frames appeared alongside it at once.
Every one of those tall figures was Halibel — doppelgangers identical down to the last detail.
However—

"That trick — I already saw it a moment ago."

The "Sunlight Sword" raised overhead in his right hand came crashing down, and flame engulfed the path ahead of the four Halibels.
A curtain of flame, its heat so intense it looked almost white, was drawn across the street, forcing a choice upon the Halibels: leap over the fire, or go around it. And yet Halibel chose neither of those two options, but a third.

"This one's a bit different from my last trick."

Two of the four Halibels surged ahead, thrusting their palms toward the rising wall of flame.
Halibel's palm strikes, launched with a stride hard enough to shatter the cobblestones, were battering rams that could blow apart a lesser castle gate in a single blow. Two of them, halted a hair's breadth short of the flames at once, generated a gale that tore the fire apart.

At that blow, the brow of Eugard's Noh-mask-like face twitched faintly — but it was still too soon to be surprised.
Because the two who had lagged behind now overtook the leading pair and slammed their own battering-ram blows squarely into Eugard's torso.

"—!"

Swallowing a groan at the back of his throat, Eugard was sent flying straight backward by the impact. But the wound was shallow — he had caught Halibel's palms with the flat of the "Sunlight Sword" at the last instant, and had even leapt backward himself.
Even so, he hadn't fully killed the force of Halibel's attack — but what astonishing skill as a warrior, for a man who held the position of Emperor.
It made a mockery of Halibel's own position, someone who did nothing but Shinobi work and had never once been an Emperor.
But it couldn't be helped.

Given Eugard's condition, he couldn't rely on subordinates to protect him. This Emperor had no choice but to defend himself, by himself.

"I'm not gonna go easy on you."

Setting aside his circumstances, Halibel didn't loosen the pursuit of the sent-flying Eugard.
Dragging his toes against the ground to kill his backward momentum, Eugard turned toward Halibel's voice, which sounded right beside him, and twisted his body to drive home a slash.
But the Halibel there took the diagonal slash and instantly scattered into beast fur, vanishing. Another Halibel kicked upward from directly below the wide-eyed Eugard.

"Mrgh—!"

Kicked up into the air, Eugard had Halibels leap at him from all four directions. Front, back, left, right — each Wolf Human raised a knife-hand strike in mirror-image motion. But Eugard didn't simply take the hit; he shattered them using a function of the "Sunlight Sword."
Namely, he threw off his own trajectory with an explosion of air caused by ultra-rapid heating.

Spinning from the explosion caused by the "Sunlight Sword" heating to a blazing red, Eugard's slash traced a circle through the air and cut all four Halibels in two.
All four Halibels instantly burst into flame — and every one of them turned to beast fur.
In their place, a battle-axe-like elbow strike came crashing down from directly overhead, striking Eugard's head. The Emperor spun vertically and plummeted into the streets of the capital, carving out a circular crater with a thunderous boom.
Landing beside the crater, Halibel casually reached out a hand and caught the pipe — the one they'd tossed from their mouth just before impact — as it fell right into their grasp.
And then—

"Normally you'd be dead after that, but it's not gonna go that easy, is it?"

"—No, it isn't."

Amid the swirling dust, an unruffled reply answered Halibel's voice as they looked down into the center of the crater.
Doing all that and having it amount to no real damage was fairly rough, but since it was as expected, the blow to Halibel's heart was kept to a minimum. From this exchange, Halibel revised their assessment of the "Sunlight Sword" and "Wicked Sword." They had thought the "Wicked Sword" the more dangerous of the two, but the "Sunlight Sword" was more than formidable enough as well.
And more than that—

"So you're switching between doppelgangers with substance and hollow ones."

Righting himself within the crater, Eugard analyzed the exchange just past in those terms.
Without confirming or denying the observation, Halibel licked at the blood dripping from the cut that had opened on their left cheek, and let silence be the answer.

"A technique of this caliber must have demanded training fit to make one cough blood to master. It merits praise."

"Well, thanks for that."

It was the barest minimum of a reply, meant to give away no unnecessary information — but Eugard's observation had hit the mark.
Halibel's "doppelgangers" came in two varieties: hollow decoys made purely of beast fur, and genuine doppelgangers possessing substance in no way inferior to Halibel's own body.
Combining the two to confound an opponent formed the backbone of Halibel's fighting style, and even the decoy doppelgangers carried enough power to completely shut down an ordinary opponent.

The problem was that Eugard was no ordinary opponent, and moreover was someone against whom the Curse Arts tactics that were Halibel's specialty simply would not work.
Techniques meant to kill couldn't be expected to work on someone already dead.
Much less when Eugard's very Soul already bore a curse as unbreakably strong as any could be.
And yet—

"If I don't finish what I said I'd do, Ana's gonna scold me for it."

Twirling the pipe in hand, Halibel packed the next portion of tobacco into the bowl and lit it.
As before, drawing the smoke into their lungs in a single breath tricked the brain into an illusion of strength surging through all four limbs. And with that, Halibel moved to unleash the next attack on Eugard at the center of the crater—

"This won't do. You are too strong. I have no choice but to grow serious myself."

In that instant, Eugard had already leapt out of the crater and raised the "Sunlight Sword" overhead, right before them.
As the "Sunlight Sword" came slashing down, Halibel cut in on reflex — not catching the blade, but seizing Eugard's right arm that gripped it, blocking the attack that way.
An instant later, the shockwave released tore past behind Halibel, shredding and incinerating the capital's buildings, reducing them to scattered ash.

At this point, Eugard held only the single "Sunlight Sword." The "Wicked Sword," which he had held in his left hand until then, was left behind, stuck upright in the crater.
That wasn't a decision to discard a powerful weapon.

"Rather than two blades I am unaccustomed to, I will face you with the one blade I know well."

"Man, you really are an unpleasant one, Your Imperial Majesty."

Consistently choosing the move an opponent would hate most was, properly speaking, a Shinobi's trick.
Facing Eugard, who executed that trick through sheer insight and decisiveness, Halibel — while still pinning down his arm — sent doppelgangers of their own charging in from three directions.
A palm strike, a kick, and on top of that one who flung gouged-up chunks of earth like shot — all of these Eugard, even with his arm seized, crushed through sheer overwhelming swordsmanship.
He vanished the "Sunlight Sword" held in his right hand in the blink of an eye, made it reappear in his left, and with it burned away the three Halibels closing in on him.

Two of them were decoy doppelgangers made of beast fur, but the one throwing debris had substance.
Feeling the acrid smell of their own beast fur burning away in a great swath, Halibel drove a knife-hand strike home, grazing and gouging Eugard's cheek as a handful of seconds at the highest level began.

—Here lies one irony, the sort that might be called a trick of fate.

The "Curse of Thorns" forced solitude upon Eugard, but the curse itself could bring no pain at all to Eugard, who suffered from congenital analgesia. And yet, even though Eugard himself was untouched by pain, it remained a fact that his body was continually eaten away and affected by the curse.
In fact, even as a child, Eugard — though he felt no pain — had at times felt a suffocating pressure from the curse and found his actions restricted by it. But such effects were inconvenient for a Eugard who had already fixed the span of his remaining life and decided what he would accomplish within it.

Just how much his body responded to that state of mind is uncertain.
But at some point, Eugard's body came to function flawlessly toward achieving his own goals, entirely unshaken by anything the "Curse of Thorns" inflicted upon it.
In other words, Eugard's body grew to be able to respond to the mortal danger it was constantly exposed to. —Such was the ironic circumstance behind the birth of the strongest Emperor in the Empire's history.

Eugard's body, optimized for the sole purpose of simply surviving, served as the ideal foundation for cultivating his own almost mechanical diligence.
Unable to keep guards due to his circumstances, Eugard trained himself to protect his own body, and with both the talent and the physique to make use of that talent, he continued growing stronger with nothing to measure himself against.

It's a meaningless hypothetical, given that the "Curse of Thorns" was what made Eugard strong, but his skill and swordsmanship were such that, even without the "Curse of Thorns," he could have wiped out the "Nine Divine Generals" of that era.
Of course, in actuality, Eugard's battles consisted mostly of beheading opponents already suffering under the "Curse of Thorns," and it was much the same even in his fights against the "Nine Divine Generals."

To Eugard, battle was not a contest of strength, but the task of execution.

"I grant you my praise."

It isn't widely known, but among all the generations of Vollachia Emperors, Eugard Vollachia spoke this phrase more often than any other.
Precisely because he could not stand on the same ground as others, precisely because he knew he had been given a position apart from other men, Eugard never withheld praise from others.

Which made this an ironic encounter.
The fact that the "Thorned Emperor," who praised others more than any figure in the Empire's history, and the Wolf Human whose very way of being is called the "Extoller" in the present day, should clash against one another here, across the very divide between life and death.

But there was yet another irony to this encounter, another trick of fate.

As mentioned before, to Eugard, battle was always one-sided.
The "Curse of Thorns" ate away at its target, and Eugard would face an enemy who could be called neither whole nor at their best, winning only by taking their head.
That very concept of "battle," as it existed for Eugard Vollachia, was about to change.
Through this collision with the Wolf Human before him now, the "Extoller" Halibel, untouched by any curse.


"Hah—"

Breath escaped through his slightly parted lips, and Eugard's single flashing strike dyed the world red.
A red sword-flash cut diagonally through the sky, but the tall Wolf Human's form was nowhere along the path of that strike. The opponent had stopped throwing out doppelgangers at random and switched to thoroughgoing evasive movement.
It wasn't to buy time — it was to size up Eugard's swordsmanship.

"A sound judgment."

Honest admiration, praise for a decision he believed correct, filled Eugard's heart.
This revived body, for all the poor sheen of its skin, moved well, and Eugard even harbored the illusion that he could go on moving without limit.
Of course, in reality that wasn't so. This body was no stronger than it had been in life, and he shouldn't ask more of it than the repair of a broken body could bear.

Even an Undead's body still felt pain all the same.
That was why Eugard alone stood as guardian of this place. Anyone else, Undead or not, would be tormented by its shackles the moment they entered the curse's range.
Even if they were Undead, Eugard had no intention of needlessly tormenting his own countrymen.
More than anything—

"I do not wish for interference in my battle with you."

A battle — yes, this was a battle.
Trading techniques with Halibel, reshaping the very form of the capital as they fought, Eugard poured every ounce of strength into the arm holding the "Sunlight Sword," losing himself in this rendezvous with an enemy he could not finish off.

Feeling exhilaration in swinging a sword at another person was, whether counting from his birth or from his death, the first such experience of his existence.

The first time Eugard ever took a life was when an assassin broke into the mansion where he lived apart from his family, and that assassin, clutching his chest and writhing in agony in the garden, begged to be killed.
Ever since he first took a life at the age of seven, drawing a sword had, for Eugard, been synonymous with execution.

And now, what was this?
Wielding his trained swordsmanship to its fullest, and still chasing after a life that eluded him — what a sweet, what a precious sensation that was.

"—"

The arm he thrust out in a stabbing strike was caught between the opponent's knife-hand and knee, sandwiched from above and below, and shattered at the elbow. Vanishing the "Sunlight Sword," knocked from his grip by the impact, in midair, he switched it to his still-good right hand for a horizontal slash.
His opponent dodged this with a motion like sinking into the ground, and in slipping past, brushed their fingertips against his left hip, gouging out a palm-sized chunk of his waist.
Whirling around, he tried to land the "Sunlight Sword" on the retreating back, but it was blocked by a doppelganger's arm reaching in from another direction, and a kick released at the same moment collided at torso height between them, sending him flying furiously. Sent flying. Flying.

"Half a step."

Next time, he would try stepping in half a step deeper.
It was swordsmanship Eugard couldn't have managed just ten seconds ago, yet he was certain he could manage it next time. Techniques he had never performed in life or in death, and yet countless variant forms of them occurred to him.
It was a growth, greedy and almost hopelessly so, for an Undead.

—Because the "Curse of Thorns" had forced solitude upon him and turned battle into execution, Eugard Vollachia had missed his chance to raise his own strength as a warrior.
His sword existed for self-defense; given the singular position he was placed in, he trained it only as much as necessary, and asked no more of it than that.
And now that swordsmanship of Eugard's was being rapidly honed, gaining enormous experience all at once.

Eugard had no idea that the being he faced was the strongest in the Kararagi City-States — that is, one of the five strongest fighters in the present world.
But that ignorant confrontation, and the vast combat experience it let him absorb, was growing Eugard — Undead though he was — into something more ravenously formidable than he had ever been in life.

"I praise you… no, I thank you."

And so what spilled from Eugard's mouth was not praise, but gratitude.
This detestable line of Wolf Humans he faced had brought him a feeling that the living Eugard had never known. So long as it was not an unpleasant one, whatever had been offered to him deserved a fitting evaluation.
For Eugard, it was heartfelt.

"No need to thank me for that. I'm gonna win anyway."

Halibel's mouth, still running as ever, was disrespectful — but even that disrespect felt pleasant.
Thinking back on it, perhaps because no one could defy the "Curse of Thorns," Eugard had precious little experience of being talked back to. Setting aside those who had chosen betrayal, the only one who had ever spoken her mind to Eugard was Iris, more or less—

"—My star."

As Eugard murmured that, before his eyes the Wolf Human's figure blurred as though wrapped in haze.
Combining a peculiar gait with unbelievable speed of movement, it created an optical illusion of a different sort than the doppelgangers. At the sensation of part of his attention being drawn off by the afterimage, Eugard could only marvel at such versatility.
But there was no need to be deceived by mere sleight of hand. Presences bore down from overhead and both sides, harbingers of a fatal blow surging in with ferocity, and Eugard turned to meet them without flinching.

And then he stepped half a pace deeper — backward.

"Scattering presences about so obviously. —Then the one with no presence at all must be the real threat."

"Gh."

The angle was too shallow for a full swing, but even so, the pommel of the "Sunlight Sword" buried itself in Halibel's side. In the instant he felt the sensation of crushing bone, Eugard instantly heated the blade of the "Sunlight Sword" — the resulting explosion drove the pommel even deeper, twisting and crushing everything within.

"GRAAAAHHH—!!"

With an impact hard enough to break his own wrist, Eugard sent Halibel flying.
He noticed, unthinking, that he had roared as he sent his opponent flying, and Eugard felt a quiet astonishment at it. In his field of view, the flung Halibel collided with the castle wall, sending violent cracks racing through the stone, the tall Wolf Human's legs splayed out, head hanging low.

It had been everything he had. A tremendous blow, the single most refined strike of his existence, counting both life and death, had been unleashed.
And that sensation kept being renewed, one after another, in the course of this battle with Halibel. Half a step — he had stepped in deeper. Next time he might step in another half step deeper still.
Or perhaps, further still, further beyond, there spread a landscape Eugard had never seen.
Perhaps he could seize it, if it was with a Halibel rising back to their feet. So get up, get up, come back and stand — Eugard recognized that part of himself thinking so, from the depths of his heart—

"—I must go to my star. This is as far as you and I go."

That exhilaration, that sense of reaching new heights as a warrior, he crushed beneath his love for Iris.

"—"

Let it be admitted: the battle with Halibel had exhilarated him.
In every sense, it was a thrill he had never tasted in life or in death. Along the path he had given up climbing for himself, he had now glimpsed just what sort of view had waited there.
He had no idea whether he would ever again have an experience so meaningful.
But even so — whatever might come—

"Not even a single blink compares to keeping my star within these eyes."

That was Eugard's unwavering set of values, in life just as much as in death.
No unknown thrill, no exhilaration, could ever reach a Eugard who had known Iris.
He had wished for the throne even knowing he ought to let it go, ought to give it up, because he knew that without doing so he couldn't keep even one more second of time at Iris's side.
Having gained the throne in exchange for that selfishness, he went on fulfilling his duty as Emperor, even though the years after losing her stretched on far longer than the time he had spent with Iris.

Eugard Vollachia was, during his reign, the Emperor who spent the least time of any in the capital's Crystal Palace.
That life as Emperor, Eugard — having lost Iris — spent almost entirely alone. Even the matter of producing an heir he settled with the barest minimum of contact, devoting his life to the Empire.
Every part of his life besides that, he spent entirely for Iris's sake.
And so—

"—I shall strike you down, black Wolf Human."

Deeming any more time unnecessary, Eugard took up the "Sunlight Sword" and turned his steps toward the castle wall.
He meant to burn away the fallen Halibel with a single flash — and could not. An instant before, Eugard's right arm, poised to swing the "Sunlight Sword," burst apart at the shoulder.

"What?"

At the impact — struck through without any sensation of being pierced — Eugard raised an eyebrow. But the true height of his astonishment lay beyond that. —The Undead arm did not begin to regenerate.
Fragments of his right arm, shattered like porcelain, scattered about, and the "Sunlight Sword" plunged into the ground.
And then—

"—Finally, found the death point."

Saying so in a low voice, like letting out a breath, Halibel rose from the castle wall. Rising while leaning their back against the cracked stone, the Wolf Human set the pipe between their lips once more.
Slowly, they dropped tobacco into the bowl, snapped their fingers to light it, and drew the smoke into their lungs. Watching that gesture, Eugard laid a hand against the unhealing wound of his right arm.

The arm was gone from the right shoulder down, with not even a sign that regeneration would begin.
It was plainly clear. Eugard's right arm had died again, ahead of the rest of his revived body.
The one who had accomplished that was the Halibel before him, apparently through what they'd called the "death point."

"There is still so much overflowing that I do not know."

"Yeah, guess so. Be great if I could teach it to you — except I was about to get wiped out before I got the chance."

Chuckling low in their throat, blowing smoke out through their nose, Halibel nodded.
He had been made to marvel at this Wolf Human's versatility time and again, but this was the finest example yet. That they should even possess a technique to kill an Undead — the depth of mastery in a skill was a fearsome thing.

"And so, just as with my arm — can you kill my very life the same way?"

"That's what I was watching for, after all. Bit of a tall order, but... yeah, I figure I can manage it."

"A bold claim, and disrespectful. But it pleases me, so I shall forgive it."

Drawing the "Sunlight Sword" from the ground with his remaining left hand, he leveled it at Halibel. He couldn't read the expression on Halibel's face as they rose unhurriedly, but their words and bearing were bold and fearless — no bluff.
His opponent, too, was now fully prepared to slay Eugard. Once again, some ill-behaved Insect he'd only just discovered living within his own chest stirred to life, seething — but he crushed it underfoot at once.

Having come to know a desire he had never known before, he could say this much with certainty.
No brilliance in this world could fill Eugard more than Iris did.
And so—

"—The sixty-first Emperor, Eugard Vollachia."

"—The 'Extoller,' Halibel."

They declared their names simultaneously, and in the next instant, both Eugard's and Halibel's forms vanished, and time itself seemed to disappear.

"—"

Behind the two as they launched themselves, the ground they had kicked off from exploded, spewing dust all at once. Using that explosion as propulsion, the several dozen meters between Eugard and Halibel vanished.
At a speed so extreme it created the illusion that the world itself had shrunk, Eugard's blade cut through the air first. The red slash, launched diagonally, dyed everything along its line of fire crimson, and an instant later released heat, generating a scorching blaze fierce enough to melt even stone into liquid.

But Halibel dodged that slash with only a slight tilt of the body, keeping the damage to a slight reddening scorch on the flesh of the right shoulder and back, and pressed forward still further.
Just like that, Halibel's leaping front kick crashed into the arm-short Eugard, who caught it with a raised knee, and a shockwave burst between the two of them.
The swelling shockwave blew away flame and rubble, and at a distance close enough to feel each other's breath, they exchanged attacks with ferocious speed. Knife-hand against crimson sacred blade, kick against elbow, body-check against grappling technique crossed one after another, and in the space of a blink's worth of exchanges, pieces of Eugard's body were chipped away one after the next.

But his opponent bore wounds all the same.
Strangely enough, even setting the arm aside, the degree of attrition on both sides was close.
And so, for the sake of victory, Eugard stepped in half a pace further, in spirit.

"—'Sunlight Sword,' Vollachia."

In the midst of their clash, the sacred sword he had called by name vanished from within Eugard's hand.
It was the supreme sacred sword, one that could be sheathed into an empty scabbard and drawn freely again at will. Releasing it, he caught Halibel's knee with his now-empty left hand, and his gaze crossed with his opponent's narrow, thread-like eyes.
Ironically, the eyes of both Wolf Human and Undead were the same gold. Whatever they saw in each other's eyes, Halibel clenched their jaw with enough force to nearly bite through the pipe's mouthpiece, and arched their body sharply backward.
Over their head, the "Sunlight Sword," leaping out from the empty scabbard, grazed past by a hair's breadth.

The "Sunlight Sword," once sheathed into the empty scabbard, would likewise appear again from that same empty scabbard when drawn. Making use of that mechanism of sheathing and drawing, it had been Eugard's certain-kill technique — used for the first time, and beautifully dodged.
But Halibel, thrown off balance, planted a hand behind them in that arched posture, and rode that momentum into a furious backward roll, evading the next slash. Evading. Evading.
Freed by that momentum, Eugard took a single beat to build up for his next strike — and noticed.

"—"

Ahead of the rolling Halibel lay the crater in the ground where Eugard had been struck down earlier.
And into that crater, a small shadow came leaping in from outside the battlefield — Groovy. Groovy Gumlet, who was supposed to have withdrawn from the front line, came diving in, coughing up blood.
Heading straight for the "Wicked Sword" planted in the crater, reaching out a hand toward it—

"A fine bit of coordination. —However."

He wouldn't go so far as to call the sudden intruder cowardly.
He had known of Groovy's presence from the start. If Groovy came to Halibel's aid at the last moment, that was only natural between allies — if anything, it made perfect sense.
But he wouldn't allow it. Eugard would win, stepping past even that.

"—I will not let you take the 'Wicked Sword.'"

Raising the "Sunlight Sword" overhead, Eugard hurled it at Groovy as he leapt into the crater.
In that instant, the fire-spewing sacred sword's speed accelerated further still, becoming a single streak of red light that shot past Halibel's side and drove straight toward Groovy.

Even released from his grip, being able to call it back to hand instantly was the "Sunlight Sword's" strength.
Once Groovy was skewered on the sacred blade, he would call the "Sunlight Sword" back and challenge Halibel again. If anything, at this very moment, being without the "Sunlight Sword" made his body lighter for chasing Halibel down — a happy miscalculation.
Of course, he also accounted for the possibility that Halibel, aiming for exactly that, might counterattack, and braced himself, and then—

"—!"

Straight and true, the thrown "Sunlight Sword" skewered Groovy's small frame.
What the crimson sacred sword pierced, even as Groovy twisted his body, was his right side. His body driven deep by the blade, Groovy's eyes bulged at the impact, spilling clots of blood, about to let loose a scream—

"—Fell for it, you bastard."

He didn't.
On the contrary, Groovy smiled through blood-smeared lips, and with the hand he'd been reaching out, pointed at Eugard. Disrespectful — but an astonishment greater still occurred within Eugard's field of vision.

The "Wicked Sword," just barely out of reach of Groovy's hand, flew as though springing from the crater to escape his grasp — even though it had never been touched.

"This damn sword hates my guts…"

A logic beyond understanding, but such bizarre properties were only to be expected of Enchanted Swords and sacred blades.
Flung out like that, freed from the crater, the "Wicked Sword" spun through the air before being caught by a hand covered in black beast fur, its eerily gleaming blade wavering.

—Halibel, now holding the "Wicked Sword," took a stance before Eugard.

"—"

In that instant's exchange, Eugard instantly called the "Sunlight Sword" back, and Groovy's midsection, now that the sacred blade had been withdrawn, began bleeding profusely.
But Eugard had, indeed, retrieved his weapon, and swung it high against Halibel, who now bore the "Wicked Sword."
And then, the crimson flash brought down from a high overhead stance was unleashed as the finest blow of Eugard's existence, surpassing his swordsmanship in both life and death alike—

"Man, really, well done, well done. —If it wasn't me, whoever faced that would've been wiped out."

"What an arrogant thing to say. But in light of your exceptional skill, I shall forgive it."

At Halibel's words, so close now, Eugard drew in his chin without changing expression.
Struck by the "Wicked Sword's" rising slash, which surpassed even Eugard Vollachia's finest swordstroke, Eugard's body was cleaved diagonally in two.

Around the left breast of the Halibel who had accomplished that, the "Curse of Thorns" now coiled.
That this was Eugard Vollachia's heartfelt praise for the "Extoller" Halibel was something plain to both of them, who had crossed blades together, without need for further words.
