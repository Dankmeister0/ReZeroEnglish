Arc 7 Chapter 60: "Sowing Seeds of Turmoil"

"...And if she offers up the city, and it isn't enough?"

"Yorna-sama will not consent to relinquishing this city."

"You believe she can be persuaded?"

"If she cannot be, everything turns to dust. If it must be done, it will be done. That is the sort of man he is, and the sort of bearing he has maintained without fail."

"—Stop, I beg you!!"

Tanza ran on, leaving behind her Yorna's voice, hand outstretched, crying out in anguish.
Leaning forward, kicking lightly off the ground, Tanza's right eye burned red, and her small body brimmed with a strength that would put an armored imperial soldier to shame.
She was not especially gifted in combat, nor particularly skilled with magic or technique.
And yet, no one could stop her now.

For she knew, with certainty, that she was loved by Yorna more than anyone else.

She had stood at Yorna's side, supported her, been cherished by her, spoken to, helped her, thought of her, prayed for her.
To love Yorna, and to be loved by Yorna — that was the quality needed to receive the full benefit of the "Soul Marriage Technique." But there had been no calculation in Tanza's love for Yorna.

Someone wise might say that love asks for nothing in return.
But Tanza thought otherwise — that the very feeling of loving someone was itself the reward.

The heartbeat that quickened, thinking of that person — that was the return "love" gave.
And so Tanza had to answer, with the whole of her small body, for the return she had received.
That was—

"—the reason I was given this life."

The first time she had ever laid eyes on Yorna, Tanza, chased from elsewhere, had been too young. She had hidden behind her gentle older sister's back, able only to peer up at Yorna's commanding presence. There had only been the promise — that for the first time in her life, she could pass her days without being threatened by anyone. Believing in that promise, she had given herself over to thoughts of the days ahead with her sister.

The second time she had come before Yorna, her gentle sister had been stolen from her by loathsome things, and in her desperation Tanza had snapped at Yorna — yet Yorna had not reproached her for it.
Far from it — she had listened earnestly to Tanza's grievance, and had even lent a hand in exacting retribution on the things that had taken her sister. — Even at the cost of being branded a traitor for it.

How could she not love her?
The one who had poured out such love, who had cherished her, before Tanza had ever asked.
The one who had wept at her sister's death, who had regretted the promise she could not keep, who had apologized to Tanza for it.

—How could she not love her?

"――――"

Before her, she could see the hideously writhing black stain gathering beyond the rising smoke.
Struck by the very city Yorna loved, and still not shattered — a tenacious, obstinate will. Anyone else would want to look away from that dark, grim thing, and yet Tanza did not flinch.

If fear was the reason to flinch, then fear was not what ruled Tanza's heart.
And so Tanza leapt forward off the rubble, charging headlong at the wavering "Great Disaster."
To look back would be to leave regret. She knew it would become a curse.
But even so—

"Tanza—!"

She heard her beloved's voice call her name, and Tanza's eyes turned back.
She saw Yorna, fallen to the ground, hand outstretched. An arrow had pitifully pierced through her leg, and far off in the distance, Tanza understood, someone stood with a bow drawn, the one who had loosed it.

Had it not been for that arrow, Yorna would have run — toward the "Great Disaster," toward Tanza.
Without it, she could never have gone through with this resolve, Tanza thought, and she was grateful to the one who had loosed that arrow.
And then—

"—Yorna-sama."

Her lips moved.
Whether the words beyond them ever became sound, whether they ever reached Yorna, she did not know.
Only, as she had prayed every night before sleep, every morning on waking, in every idle moment — she prayed now, in this instant too.

—Please, let the days of the one she loved be ever peaceful and well.

The light blew everything away, searing every eye white.
When the light cleared, and seared eyes gradually regained their sight, the people beheld the magic city's end.

"――――"

The epicenter had caved in as if scooped out in a perfect circle, and where the Crimson Lazuli Castle had once stood at the city's heart, a vast pit now gaped open.
The enormous hole, as if dug out by a giant's arm, was proof — a scar left behind — of the terrifying existence that had, quite literally, swallowed the whole of the magic city.
And the calamity that had been fought with what could only be called total war had, in an instant, vanished without a trace.

That this was the fruit of the trump card wielded by Yorna Mishigure, mistress of the magic city — and that the final push had come from a young girl who had staked her life on it — was known to everyone who had taken part in the battle.
Which was to say—

"—We were saved by that girl. I am ashamed of my own inadequacy."

Peering down into the depths of the great pit, thorns still extended from him, the man — Kafma Irulux — murmured this.
It was, no doubt, an honest confession, spoken from the heart. As befit a warrior of some renown, this earnest young man, devoted to the empire's philosophy, reproached himself for having his own lack of strength made up for by another.
All the more so, given that accomplishing it had cost the life of a young girl.

"By rights, I am your enemy. Now that matters have settled, I believe this truce should end here, and that fact should be faced..."

"...So, does that mean you'll fight us?"

"—. I think not."

Turning back from the great pit, Kafma shook his head at the question.
Facing him was Medium, a sabre far too large for her small frame slung across her back. The question from this filthy, mud-caked girl was, even amid such devastation, dazzling.
That dazzling quality, Kafma thought, was exactly what was needed to settle matters from here on.

After all, the magic city of Chaosflame lay in ruins, not a single sound building to be found. From here, the survivors' struggle to live on would begin.
Of course, dealing with those refugees, and with Yorna Mishigure who would no doubt lead them, was also a role Kafma, as an imperial "General," ought to fulfill.

"This time, I accompanied this venture as the escort of a most esteemed personage. My first priority must be to rejoin that person. All else, for the time being, is a trifling matter."

"A trifling matter? Trifling — you mean, like an afterthought? That's an awful thing to say!"

"N-no, that isn't what I meant..."

Accused of insensitivity, Kafma flinched slightly under Medium's fierce indignation. But then, to Kafma's own relief, a weary voice threw him a lifeline, calling out "Medium, kiddo."
Looking over, he saw it belonged to a one-armed figure slowly approaching them—

"What he means is that's his own way of showing consideration. There's something more important goin' on, so he's not gonna tangle with us. And that settles things here for now — that's the gist of it, right?"

"...I can say nothing more myself. How you choose to judge it, I leave to you."

"Yeah, yeah. There's one of these guys everywhere you go — the roundabout type, like this fella here."

Shrugging with an exasperated air, the one-armed man — Al — came to stand beside Medium. Looking up at him, Medium's eyes went round with an "Ah."
Then, pointing at her own face, she said,

"Alchin, did you find your helmet okay?"

"Yeah, managed it somehow. It was lying around near where the inn used to be — small mercy in all this mess. Well, I figured that once it was found, it wouldn't be broken anyway."

"—? You mean it's sturdy?"

"Yep, sturdy's the word. Nothing in this world can break it."

As he spoke, Al tapped a finger against the jet-black helmet on his head. Medium nodded along with an "Oh, wow~," but Kafma sent a sharp, steady gaze toward Al.
Noticing the look, Al tilted his head. "Somethin' wrong?"

"You're of the opinion that you won't tangle with us? Naturally, neither will I — or rather, we're all too spent for that to even be a question."

"That direction you gave during the fight just now was masterful. How did you know how the 'Great Disaster' would move?"

"Trade secret. If I don't tell you, are you callin' off the truce?"

"—. No, I do not retract what I said."

Shaking his head, Kafma answered Al's response with earnest solemnity. Then he brushed the tattered remains of his cloak with his hand and turned his back on Al and Medium.
His feet carried him, just as he'd said, toward the esteemed personage he served.

"Circumstances made us cooperate this time. But we remain enemies... Unless matters change, we shall meet again on a battlefield. When that time comes, I shall not hold back."

"You don't have to tell us..."

"Same goes for us! ...But thanks for helping."

"As a 'General' of the empire, I did only what was natural."

With those words left behind, Kafma spread the translucent wings on his sturdy back and, with a great beating of wings, shot up and flew away in an instant.
Dust swirled up in the gust he left behind, and Medium and Al watched his retreating form until it vanished. Once Kafma was gone from sight,

"...Alchin, good work. Really, thank you. You saved me."

"That goes both ways. If it weren't for how hard Medium kiddo fought, that big thing's attacks would've been way more one-sided. And then there'd have been a lot more dead."

"Dead..."

Hearing Al's answer, Medium quietly lowered her eyes.
Watching Medium's pained, downcast face, Al fiddled with the seam of his helmet with a finger, and said,

"I know it probably doesn't make sense, and it probably won't be much comfort, but... if that kid Tanza hadn't done what she did, we'd all be dead. Every last one of us. For sure."

"Maybe... maybe there was more we could've done."

"No. There was no other way. — We tried pretty much everything, and none of it worked."

Al's downhearted words made no real sense to Medium. But to say they were no comfort at all would be a lie.
She understood that Al had said something, trying to comfort her, thinking of her. And because of that, it really had comforted her, in its own way.

"Thanks, Alchin."

"...You're welcome."

Al's follow-up words, once again, made no sense to Medium at all.

"So, you've more or less settled on what must be done, Yorna Mishigure."

"...So it's you, is it, master."

A mountain of rubble, the wreckage of buildings, and the unfathomable pit gouged out by the aftershocks of that fierce battle. On a rise from which one could survey the whole scene — the total war waged by the magic city of Chaosflame to destroy the "Great Disaster," its scars still starkly visible — that man appeared before where Yorna stood.
His face hidden behind an oni mask, his voice betraying no emotion whatsoever, Abel came, asking after what lay in her heart.

Slowly, picking his way through the rubble, Abel came, gazing upon the same scene as Yorna, and yet, with not a trace of emotion mixed into his voice,

"What I have to offer is as I laid out in my letter. Let me hear your answer."

"Is that the sort of thing one says, looking out on a sight like this?"

"Would comfort shelter you? Would sympathy give you somewhere to stand? Both you and I were born to it, and chose to do what must be done. That one second of ours is not equal in weight to some other person's second."

"――――"

She did not want comfort or sympathy. This man was one who had chosen to be that sort of person.
And since Yorna understood that too, she swallowed down her urge to push back against his manner. Above all, opposing him here over something as insubstantial as sentiment would gain her nothing.

"I've already lost too much, today."

"The city, its people, and the time for reprieve besides — no difference there, I should think."

"Tanza."

"――――"

"At the last, to save us — the Beloved Child who threw away her own life... that child's name was Tanza."

So saying, Yorna drew the obi clasp from her kimono sash and showed it to Abel.
Eyeing the smoothly polished thing sidelong, the silent Abel's gaze asked its meaning. To that wordless question, Yorna's eyes softened faintly at the corners.

"It's a clasp carved from that child's sister's own horn. When we laid her sister to rest, Tanza made it for me... And not only this."

"――――"

"This hair ornament, this kanzashi too, each and every one a tribute from my Beloved Children. Driven from their homes, owning nothing, those children — marks of their wish to repay me, carved from themselves."

Brightly colored ornaments, hairpins and kanzashi alike, all of them tributes from the residents of the magic city.
Some had scraped away their own scales, some had gathered feathers, some had polished down their horns or fangs, and offered them up to Yorna — giving shape, in this way, to who they were and to their gratitude.
To Yorna, these were worth more than any precious jewel or treasure, and having received such gifts, she had always felt she must answer their love in kind.
That was—

"I cannot break my promise to them, not one more time."

"—. Before long, Vincent Vollachia and his party, who departed from here, will begin to move. Given the circumstances, it's unlikely they'll act on this spot, but it's only a matter of time. There is no time to spare."

"And where do you intend for my children to go?"

"For now, the fortress city must serve as a base. Along the way, we will seize the other towns and bring them under our banner. With you and the magic city's people, it can be done."

With the magic city's function lost, a place was needed to take in the residents who now had nowhere to go.
Abel's proposal was high-handed, an unreasonable thing that forced hardship on others. But Yorna, too, had her priorities, weighed equally. Saving those she loved — that was hers.
And then, there was—

"Is it truly your intention to make good on the wish in your letter — the one you'd grant me?"

"I do not repeat myself twice. But think it over carefully."

"Think it over..."

"Whether you will prioritize your long-held wish, or this 'love' of yours."

Abel's words, emotionless as ever, carried something like a warning, but whether it sprang from goodwill or ill will toward her, Yorna could not tell.
Only, struck to the heart by his remark, Yorna drew her pipe from her sash, forcibly straightened its bent tip with her fingers, lit it, and let a trail of purple smoke drift into the air.
Below, moving among the ruined city, her Beloved Children were gathering up the remnants of the lives they had supported, and preparing for what came next.

Whether their tomorrows could be given shelter, whether a light could be lit for them further ahead — that was up to Yorna now.
To side with Abel, to drag Vincent Vollachia — no, the impostor claiming that name — down from the throne, and see the promise in the letter fulfilled.
The content of that promise to be fulfilled was—

"—Is there a parent who dies before their child, Tanza?"

So, into the wind, the purple smoke scattered and vanished — as fleeting as the choice of the Beloved Child who had determined her own fate.

Watching the rising trail of purple smoke from a distance, she went to greet the oni-masked man walking beneath it.
Receiving the greeting, Abel gave a small snort at the sight of Taritta standing there, unmoving.

"If you're worried about the leg you shot through, spare yourself the trouble. The wound has already begun to heal. By tomorrow it will be as it was."

"...That'S good to hear. Though that wasn't what concerned me."

"Oh?"

Just as Abel said, there was nothing amiss in the way Yorna stood on the rise. Her figure from behind, kimono and hair in disarray, showed her exhaustion, but her right leg, pierced by the arrow, seemed to want for nothing. That in itself might be a benefit of whatever secret art she commanded. Still, more than the wound itself, what weighed on her was the outcome that inflicting it had brought about.
Naturally, Taritta was keenly aware of that weight as well.

"――――"

In that final moment, entrusted with the choice, Taritta had staked her own decision on that arrow.
Had she followed the destiny left to her by Mariuli as a "Stargazer," Taritta should have loosed that arrow into Abel's heart. What manner of causality would follow, whether it would calm the "Great Disaster," she could not have imagined — but it was, without question, among the strongest possibilities within the choices available to her.
And yet, that would have meant obeying the very stars that had turned her beloved Mariuli into something unrecognizable — truly, a decision that would have cut her to the bone.

—In that last, final instant, what had decided Taritta's choice came down to just that one point.

Taritta hated the stars that had changed Mariuli. And so, she had not obeyed the stars.
For Taritta, who found comfort in being commanded, in being shown a path, in following someone's lead, destiny might well have suited her better than anything else.
But Taritta and the stars had met badly.
Had the stars spoken to Taritta from the very start, instead of to Mariuli, Taritta might have lived for the stars' sake. But that was not how it had gone.
And so—

"—In that moment, I pushed at the back of a Deer Human girl running without hesitation."

As a result, the girl had somehow given rise to a light that destroyed the "Great Disaster" from within.
In exchange, the girl's life had been scattered, and no small amount of damage had blasted away the land where the magic city once stood. But could it not be said that she had protected what she wished to protect?

"And yet your color is poor, for all that."

"...Was I righT? Not following destiny — not shooting yoU."

"I, who was not shot, will not say that I ought to have been shot. Nor is the rightness or wrongness of your decision something I am in a position to speak on. It is a hackneyed phrase, but the rightness of one's own choice can only be proven by one's own actions thereafter."

"...Those don'T sound like your own words."

"They aren't. 'Iris and the King of Thorns'... a quotation from an old classic."

Speaking of something Taritta did not understand, Abel shook his head and said she need not understand it.
Then he looked Taritta up and down, and said,

"Some doubt lingers still, but you've shaken off a fair measure of it. What will you do, from here on?"

"I don'T know for certain. But I wisH to return to that city, to speak witH my sister and my kin. And also witH my soul-sister's daughter, who is no longer with uS."

"A soul-sister, and a destiny entrusted to you. ...It gives a coherent shape to your claim that you yourself are not the 'Stargazer.' All the more loathsome a lot, these things."

"Loathsome..."

"Not you. I have, on the whole, understood your intentions now."

Responding to Taritta's murmur with an air of distaste, Abel turned his head about.
Across the ruined streets, crowds of people were moving in, gathering up household goods spared from the destruction, and whatever materials might serve some purpose, beginning the work of survival.

A resilient people, Taritta thought, genuinely moved by how the city carried itself.
Recalling how the magic city had come to be, she supposed its people were, of course, well accustomed to persecution, oppression, and loss. Even accounting for that, still.
However—

"Will you draw theM into your fight?"

"I will."

At the short, decisive answer, Taritta could not help but wince slightly.
There was no hesitation in it; Abel watched the people striving desperately amid the devastated land, and, resolving to remain consistent with the original purpose that had brought him to Chaosflame, clicked his heel against the ground.
There, into that moment—

"—Even if that's how Abel-chan sees it, will everyone else just quietly go along? Even that fox-eared big sister — feeling-wise, it can't be easy for her to side with us, can it?"

Voicing that reasonable point, Al rejoined Abel and Taritta.
He had been out of sight for a while, but seeing him walk over wearily, his familiar helmet back on his head, it seemed he had returned to normal — aside from his size, that is.
At Al's side was Medium, her round blue eyes fixed on Abel.

"I feel the same as Alchin. I'd be happy if Yorna-chan became our ally, but after everything that happened to Tanza-chan and the city..."

"You believe Yorna Mishigure won't fall in line with us, given that we brought along the very being at the center of that calamity? That's a thought steeped too deep in sentiment. She has already made up her mind."

"Really? You sure Abelchin didn't just say something awful again?"

"Regardless of the degree of cruelty involved, I say what is necessary. My intent is plain enough."

At Abel's failure to deny the doubt raised, Medium puffed out her cheeks.
Indeed, as far as Taritta's ears had caught, the exchange between Abel and Yorna had not been emotional, but it could hardly be called gentle, measured, or considerate either.
And yet, the reason Abel doubted Yorna's resolve so little was—

"You've got something ready to hold over Yorna kiddo's weak spot, huh... In the end, unless she walks the same road as us, she can't protect the town's people who've got nowhere else to go, is that it?"

"That's...! You said something awful again, Abelchin!"

"Is there another option? Otherwise, all that's left is clinging to pointless pride and dying by the roadside."

"Even if there's no other road, there's other ways to say it! Why don't you get that!"

At Medium's raised voice, pressing in on him, Abel turned a cold gaze on her from behind his oni mask.
For Taritta's part, what concerned her was whether Abel would make some merciless judgment against Medium's protest, but Taritta's own feelings, too, leaned toward Medium's side.
And yet, so long as she abided by the covenant forged between past emperors and the "People of Shudrak," Taritta had no option to abandon Abel, to sever ties with him.
Sympathy of feeling meant nothing at all on an actual battlefield.

"Medium kiddo's anger is justified. Still, personally, I don't dislike Abel-chan's way of thinking — use whatever's useful. If Yorna kiddo's siding with us out of practical interest, I've got no objection."

"I've got objections galore! I don't like you either, Alchin!"

"Hurts my heart to be disliked by Medium kiddo, sure, it does. And besides..."

Cutting himself off there, Al held up a hand to restrain both Abel and the glaring Medium, letting his gaze wander toward the great pit that had opened at the center of the ruins.
Following that motion, Taritta and the others' attention was drawn there as well, and—

"Let's talk about our own family. — About our brother."

"――――"

At the topic Al raised, the dry air seemed to tighten faintly with tension.
It was a subject everyone knew had to be addressed, and at the same time, one that left them all uncertain how to even begin speaking of it.
For, after all—

"I wonder where Subaru-chin went..."

Medium's gloomy murmur spoke to the small-scale damage the "Great Disaster" had wrought.
Compared to the fact that an entire great city the empire took pride in had been wiped out whole, and that the lives of two emperors had been imperiled, it might be called far too minor a loss to dwell on.
And yet, for the party that had come to the magic city, it was damage that could not be overlooked either.

"Yorna-chan said that shadow came bursting right out of Subaru-chin."

"Old man Olbart said the same. Said his own right hand was gone and he was still grinning away like nothing happened. Coming from someone with as much one-arm experience as me, that's hard to believe."

"Was iT the old man's attitude that's hard to believe? Or his account?"

"Hate to say it, but in this case, it's the attitude. What he said, that's probably the truth."

Clicking his tongue, Al voiced that he trusted Olbart's testimony.
Taritta had no good impression of the strange old man either, but it was also true he had no reason to lie. Since his account matched Yorna's, it was likely true.

Suddenly, what crossed Taritta's mind was Mariuli's dying testament — "the traveler with black hair and black eyes."
When she'd first seen Subaru in the forest and set her sights on his life, she'd had no doubts. Afterward, once she learned of Abel's presence, held captive in the settlement, and his true identity, she had come to believe, without ever doubting again, that she had misjudged her target —

"UnlesS."

Unless the one destined to bear the "Great Disaster," as foreseen by Mariuli, the "Stargazer," had been not Abel, but Subaru.
That would explain why the "Great Disaster" had come bursting forth centered on Subaru, wouldn't it?
That suspicion would not stop tormenting Taritta's heart.

"There is one matter we must settle a shared view on."

Ignoring Taritta's inner turmoil, Abel raised a finger at the center of the group.
Drawing everyone's attention, Abel looked over Medium, Al, and Taritta each in turn.

"None of you speak as though you doubt that... Natsuki Subaru survived. Do you genuinely believe he lived through that catastrophe?"

"—! Of course he did! There's no way Subaru-chin could just die..."

"Do not tell me you cannot bear to consider it. Whether or not you can accept it, things happen as they must. The living and dying of others follows that same course."

"Abelchin, you..."

Abel's flat, matter-of-fact way of speaking clashed head-on with Medium's emotional insistence.
For Taritta's part, her sympathies leaned toward Medium here too. But she could not honestly say she held out hope that Subaru, who had been at the very center of that calamity, had survived.

Perhaps it was because hunting, and dealing with life and death among living creatures, had been a part of her daily existence.
The Shudrak were brave warriors, but even ordinary hunts were a matter of life and death. Sometimes a beast, fighting for its life, struck back savagely, and comrades had lost their lives to it.
People died. Easily. It made no difference whether the person mattered to you or not.

"I'm sorrY, but SubarU..."

"Brother's alive."

"—Alchin!"

Shaking her head, Taritta had been about to offer words of mourning. But her words were cut off by Al's declaration, spoken with utter conviction, and hearing it, Medium's face lit up at once.
Naturally, Abel turned what looked like a displeased gaze toward Al.

"Clown, what makes you so certain he's survived?"

"Simple as can be — 'cause that's just the kind of guy Natsuki Subaru is. More than that, though—"

"More than that?"

"The world hasn't ended. That's my basis."

The logic Al laid out as his "basis" made no sense to Taritta. It seemed to be the same for Medium, who tilted her head, equally baffled.
Abel, too, dismissed it with a single "Don't be absurd," and continued,

"I have no time to spare for your nonsense. If you wish to play the fool, do it in front of Priscilla."

"I'd love nothing more, but the princess ain't here, so no can do. And speaking of no can do, there's something I want to ask Abel-chan too, while we're at it."

"What."

"What do you actually think? That our brother's dead?"

Touching a finger to the chin of his helmet, Al pressed Abel for his view. Even so, Abel's opinion seemed already settled — it was Al, after all, who had opened this whole exchange by asking, in that tone, whether believing Subaru had survived was even sane.
Naturally, Abel would say that Subaru, who had been within the "Great Disaster," could not have survived—

"—Given that it was not the 'Great Disaster' itself, a role must remain for it to fulfill. If it retains the ability for that purpose as well, then to conclude it has died would be premature."

"Eh..."
"Abelchin!?"

But what Abel actually said was the exact opposite of what Taritta had expected.
Taritta was struck speechless by the answer, and even Medium, who should have received the answer she wanted, blinked in surprise.
Abel, however, did not respond to their stares at all, and simply turned and began walking slowly away from the spot.

Exchanging glances, Taritta and Medium followed after him. Al, too, tilting his head, fell in behind the three walking ahead.

"Abelchin! What do you mean, explain it already!"

"Explain what."

"Everything! Because up until a second ago you were talking like Subaru-chin was already dead!"

"I merely said that if you wish to claim he has survived, you should provide grounds other than sentiment. I believe there is reason for him to have survived. Therefore, I conclude that he has. That is all."

"~~!"

At Abel's answer, devoid of any gentleness, Medium's face reddened with frustration. Not that it had any effect whatsoever on Abel, who did not even glance back.
And so, leading the three of them, Abel's feet finally came to a stop after walking some distance. There lay the ruins of the magic city, right before the gaping pit torn open by the vanished "Great Disaster."
There—

"Uwau..."

Crouched at the edge of the pit, head hanging low, was a small girl — Rui.
Her white clothes utterly soiled with mud, Rui was weakly clawing at the dirt with her bare hands. Her hands were filthy with soil, but also noticeably reddened with blood from her cracked, broken nails.

"Rui-chan...!"

In a rush, Medium ran to Rui's side and embraced the girl from behind. Even held in Medium's arms, Rui did not stop her hands.
Clawing at the earth, pushing aside rubble, the girl kept searching for something. — No, not "something."

"She's searching for SubarU, isn'T she."

"For better or worse, yeah... Tch. Don't like it one bit."

Watching little Rui's back, Taritta and Al each let their own thoughts escape in a sigh.
Without Taritta having noticed, Al's manner toward Rui had grown harshly prickly. And yet, it had been Al who protected Rui from the final blast that had blown the "Great Disaster" away, so the exact nature of the relationship between them was something Taritta couldn't quite read.

Prodding at it now probably wouldn't make anyone happy. Sensing that much, Taritta refrained from asking anything further into it.

"Enough of this now. Digging through the dirt, looking behind rubble — you will not find what you seek that way."

"U... a-u!"

Held in Medium's arms, Rui had Abel standing behind her. Turning to face the gaze that bore down on her from behind the oni mask, Rui's expression twisted into something between fury and grief.
It seemed to accuse Abel, and at the same time to plead with him not to stop her.
Whether it was the former, which he would not concern himself with, or the latter, to which he would turn a deaf ear, Abel jerked his chin from beside Rui toward the great pit—

"Tracking it down will be no small labor. At the very least, it is not something that will turn up simply from your clawing at the dirt alone. — To begin with, you cannot even know where it was flung to."

"Au! A-u! Uau-a!"

"Searching the same way over and over, like some fool who knows only one trick, will get you nowhere. Know your limits."

"U-! Uu-!"

At Abel's cold, unfeeling words, Rui's face flushed red, and she opened her mouth in fierce protest.
From the force and vehemence of it, it was clear she had no intention of ever giving up her search for Subaru — that she was determined to find him, no matter what.
And then—

"Abelchin, is there a way to find Subaru-chin?"

"U, u?"

"The way you just said that sounded like you meant Rui-chan alone won't find him. Does that mean you've thought of a better way?"

Holding the surging Rui close, Medium turned her gaze on Abel's eyes with the question. At Medium's words, Rui's fervor settled somewhat, and Abel closed one eye.

"Slow to grasp things, yet sharp in instinct, are you. You're the very image of that brother of yours."

"Of course, we're siblings! More importantly, answer me properly! Is there a way to find Subaru-chin or not? Is there? Is there!?"

Her expectations swelling, Medium hurled the same question again. Sighing at Medium's eager, leaning-forward manner, Abel paused a beat before continuing,

"It is not so mild a thing as to be called a 'means of searching.' My original aim, after all, was not to locate that vanished one's whereabouts, but to secure grounds for taking action against the imperial capital."

"Explain it so I can actually understand!"

"...If the scheme runs smoothly, it may grant what you and this girl wish for as well. After all, the whole of the empire will be searching for his whereabouts."

"The whole empirE, searching for SubarU... is that iT?"

Picking out the part of Abel's words that concerned her, Taritta furrowed her brow.
Like Medium, Taritta was not especially quick on the uptake either. The only difference was that Medium could loudly declare what she didn't understand, while Taritta kept it to herself.
Regardless, Abel's explanation, meant to be clear, still left Taritta short of understanding, for lack of the background knowledge it presupposed.
Only—

"—Uau, auau?"

The intent hidden within those words reached, correctly, the one person it needed to reach.
Rui, who had been on the verge of thrashing, went slack in Medium's arms, and, staring fixedly at Abel, threw out a question that did not take the shape of words.
Receiving it, Abel, though he surely could not know its precise meaning, nodded and said, "Of course."

"So, what do we do? What do we need to do to carry out this scheme to find our brother?"

"It is not so difficult a matter. We need only spread word."

"――――"

Responding to Al's question, Abel — anticipating, it seemed, that Medium would press him further — sighed and continued.
It was—

"—That the bastard offspring of Vincent Vollachia, an ill-omened child with black hair and black eyes, seeks his father the King's position. A recreation, precisely, of 'Magrizza's Guillotine.'"


　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　△▼△▼△▼△


—That same day, that same hour, in a certain place.

"—"

Soaked through, forcing his drenched body onward, he barely managed to drag himself toward ground where his fingers could finally catch hold. Once he let go of this sensation, there would be no coming back.
It was, quite literally, a death-defying struggle — no, a life-defying one.

Pitch-black water, an inescapable expanse of it, had stripped away his body heat and strength at a terrifying rate, and he had tasted the sensation of dying more times than he could count. He could no longer even tell whether that had been dream or reality.
Again and again, he thought he'd felt the bite of some great fish's shadow. His breath had given out, his lungs had filled with bitter water, and he thought he had drowned, tasting blood as he went. He thought, too, that he might have lost consciousness from the loss of heat and strength, and that his life had guttered out like falling asleep, more than once.

Repeating this, over and over, twisting through it, and finally, at last—

"Ah, bhaa...!"

Spitting up the water he'd swallowed, he forced his body up onto the bank. It was agony, it was suffocating, it was heavy. If only he could use both hands, it would be so much easier — he thought that, from the bottom of his heart. But he couldn't.
In his left arm, opposite the right hand gripping the bank, he held something he could never, ever let go of.

Before he understood what it was, he'd failed to hold on a few times. He'd let go of it.
But once he understood what it was, there was no way he could ever let go of it. And so, failing again and again, and still never giving up—

"――――"

Before himself, he pushed what he'd held in his left arm up onto the bank first.
Small in size, but heavy all the same. That he wasn't in peak condition, and that neither of them had been dressed for swimming, had been their bad luck.
Especially her — she'd been in a kimono, which held far more fabric than ordinary clothes. And more fabric meant, of course, that much more water soaked up.

He hoped he'd be forgiven for stripping off part of the kimono partway through, to lighten the load.
Also, when he'd pulled her close, he'd been sharply gouged along the ribs and the side of his neck. That, too, was the fault of the fine deer antlers growing from her head. — He hoped that pain could count as them being even.
And then—

"U, gu...!"

Having pushed the girl up onto the bank, all that remained was to summon the last of his strength and haul himself up as well.
But it seemed the thread of tension had snapped the moment he'd gotten her onto the bank — the last reserve of strength he meant to summon was nowhere to be found, and his hands only scraped uselessly at the dry earth.

His head swam, his ears rang without end, warning him that this would not do.
It was the sign that consciousness was about to slip away. And to lose consciousness here would mean "death."
If the thread of tension snapped, if the will to see this through no matter what was severed, he would find himself caught once more in that endless repetition of dream and reality just to draw this same stroke of luck again.

He hated that thought more than anything, and the harder, more desperately he tried to pull his awareness back together—

His consciousness whitened, and at last, the hand gripping the bank slipped—

"—Whoa there, careful now."

In that instant, someone caught the hand that had slipped from the bank, on the verge of sinking back into the water.
Slender fingers gripped his wrist tight, and his half-sunk body was pulled back toward the shore. His face rose up out of the water, and, breath ragged, at the edge of fading consciousness, he tried to look at the one who'd caught him.
Just who had grabbed his arm — but—

"Ah, let's not, shall we? You're right on the verge of losing consciousness, aren't you? That would be a rather anticlimactic way to end this. Might as well make a more dramatic start of it, while we're at it."

Of all things, the hand not gripping his own reached out and covered the eyes he'd tried to turn toward his rescuer with its palm. All he saw was the wrinkled lines of that stranger's palm.
An unfamiliar hand, with an unusually long lifeline.

That was the last thing he saw before his consciousness drifted away—

"But my, my, to think you swam the whole way! And how fortunate a coincidence that I happened to be out for a stroll here, carried along by the wind! No, no, no, no — how splendid!"

Even as his fading awareness slipped away, only that cheerful voice clung on to the very last.
That voice, like thunder delighting in its own peal.

"—Don't you feel, somehow, the beginnings of some grand tale about to unfold?"

To that question with no answer to give, his consciousness — the consciousness of Natsuki Subaru — could give no reply, and faded out.
