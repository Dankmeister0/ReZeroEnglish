Arc 4 Interlude: "Tea Party"

"There's a way of thinking called 'parallel worlds' — the idea that, apart from the world you're living in right now, there exist other worlds that have traced similar but different paths."

The voice had imposed upon itself a tone stripped, as much as possible, of inflection.
Accompanying its lecturing manner of speech was a small, brisk rhythm — fingertips tapping the tabletop at steady intervals.

"That sounds, *whew*, like an awfully, *haah*, complicated thing to say."

"It's not such a difficult idea. Parallel worlds, in particular, can be thought of as arising in inexhaustible numbers from even a single differing choice. Take, for instance, a fork in the road on your way home. A fork where either path leads home all the same — the you who went right, and the you who went left — that possibility alone could already be called a parallel world, on an extremely small scale."

"What's that supposed to mean? If you start saying things like that, there'd be no end to counting how many worlds there are. That's such a stupid way of thinking."

To the exhausted-sounding voice's remark, the headstrong voice replied in a rash, unthinking rush.
The lecturing voice gave a wry laugh, and raised a finger toward the hasty respondent.

"It's not something to scoff at. True, my last example may have had too small a margin of difference to get the point across... but the same holds true for far larger scenes as well."

"Larger, like... what, for example?"

"For example, let's see. — What if, on the Boroid Plains, you had been able to abandon that isolated elven suicide squad? How about that."

"— — — —"

"...Hm. If my prediction holds, I expected you to fly into a rage right about now."

"The reason I'm not angry is simple. Even if that scene repeated dozens, hundreds, thousands of times, I would always charge in and punch my way through. — There's no way a parallel world like the one you're talking about could ever exist!"

Declaring this with force, the owner of the voice flung a raised leg up and slammed it onto the table. At the headstrong voice's literal pose of leaning back with an air of bravado, the one who had been lecturing gave a small, knowing chuckle. Seeing this, the owner of the headstrong voice arched her shapely brows sharply in displeasure.

"What's so funny!"

"No, it's a very manly pose, but your underwear is showing, Minerva."

"Ah, wai—! What, you idiot! Ugh, I can't believe this! Idiot! Idiottt! Idi-ot! You total idiot! You're so stupid! Idiot! Seriously, idiot! Idiot, and, um, idiot!"

Hurling curses that laid bare the poverty of her vocabulary, the golden-haired girl — Minerva, the Witch of Wrath — lowered her leg with tears in her eyes, slipping a hand between the folds of her short skirt as she pressed her legs shut.
Eyes upturned and burning with fury, she glared at the white-haired witch seated across from her. But—

"*Haah*. Whether the argument itself was right or wrong aside, *whew*, when it comes to the underwear, *haah*, that's just indecent Minerva blowing herself up, *whew*. Taking it out on someone else is unbecoming, *haah*."

"Indecent — you're the last person who gets to say that, Sekhmet. You, a hardcore one-outfit wonder... just how long has it been since you changed that robe?"

Minerva turned a sharp glare to the side — toward Sekhmet, the Witch of Sloth, who had thrown her whole body across the table, her face buried in the sea of her long, reddish-purple hair.
From within that sea of hair, Sekhmet turned her neck, peering at Minerva through a gap in the strands.

"It's just something you pull over your head, *whew*, the easiest thing to wear, *haah*. I have Typhon wipe my body down, *whew*, so it's not like I'm actually dirty, *haah*."

"You go around criticizing other people's appearance and then you're like that yourself...! Ugh, ugh, ugh — what is wrong with you! Is it my fault? Is this on me? Do you want me to purify you with my fists!?"

To Minerva raising an indignant fist, Sekhmet did not respond, simply turning her face away with a smooth motion.
A vein bulged on Minerva's forehead at this display of having lost all will to converse, but the witch — thoroughly used to Minerva's outrage — had abandoned even the will to engage.
In place of the sloth witch who had spent her energy, it was the witch who'd first been conversing with Minerva — Echidna, the Witch of Greed — who clapped her hands.

"It's not that I don't understand your anger, and I do find it rather pleasant, myself. But right now, I'd like to get back to what we were discussing."

"Hmph. You're the one who provoked me, using parallel worlds or whatever as an excuse, Echidna. I'm angry, you know. I'm indignant. I'm furious."

"Yes, yes, I understand. Anyway, back to the matter of parallel worlds. If my earlier example doesn't hold... then, let's see. What do you think would have happened if Flugel had failed to forge his covenant with Volcanica?"

Echidna asked this of Minerva, a finger to her lips, smiling mischievously. Taking this in, Minerva caught her breath and narrowed her azure eyes.

"If there'd been no covenant between Volcanica and Flugel, then Reid alone wouldn't have been enough to stop that child... so the world would've been swallowed up."

"Swallowed up, and then what, I wonder. Would the world go on turning with only the Witch of Envy left in it? Or perhaps a world where that happened exists somewhere too, as a parallel world. If so, wouldn't that be rather fascinating?"

"Echidna, you really do get a nasty look in your eye whenever you talk about that child. — I'm not all that angry at her. That particular wrath, I won't share with you."

"That's one answer too, I suppose. — Your wrath is a pleasant thing. That's precisely why, among the witches, you were the one most worthy of being loved."

Echidna spoke in the past tense; before her, Minerva gave a small snort. Then, crossing her arms, she arched her back so as to thrust out her ample chest.

"Being loved isn't something I want. What I want is to stamp out, with these fists, every voice that cries in suffering, grief, and pain, so that conflict vanishes from this world entirely. Nothing else is needed on the road I run. My anger, my wrath, the healing of these fists — that's all there is to me."

Minerva declared this way of life to be without a single cloud of doubt.
A conviction utterly free of hesitation, reluctance, wavering, or worry — anything that might make her feet falter.
Truly "Wrath" — an inexhaustible anger toward the world formed, built, and sustained the very foundation of her being.
But—

"Well, sayyy what you like, but the cute thing about Nerneru is how she can't help grinning when someone praises her."

So spoke a distinctively lazy voice that drew out the ends of its words, cutting into the conversation.
It came from across from Sekhmet — that is, from Minerva's left.

"Nerneru's level of not-being-honest is basically witch-tier, isn't it. Daphne likes that about Nerneru so much she could just eat her up."

"Be quiet, Daphne. You were sound asleep up until this very moment — why are you suddenly awake!"

"Daphne's been awake ever since Nerneru got all loud and started showing off her underwear. Wearing a skirt so short it flashes if you move a little dramatically, and then underneath, such adorable underwear — honestly, Nerneru."

"Y-your own is worse! You're younger than me and you pick way too risqué stuff! What even is that, that's not underwear, that's just string! Idiot! You're such an idiot! Idiot! Honestly, you're hopeless, idiot! Idiot! Idiot, idiot!"

Face flushed bright red, eyes brimming with tears welling up from the surge of her emotions, Minerva shrieked. Daphne, the Witch of Gluttony, let it all wash over her cheerfully.
Bound head to toe in a straitjacket, both eyes covered by a blindfold crossing diagonally over her face, her small body housed within a black coffin — a bizarre sight that had, as if perfectly natural, taken its place among those seated around the table, making the tea party a thoroughly surreal scene when viewed from a distance.

Having exhausted her repertoire of insults (though it amounted to little more than repeating "idiot") on the unresponsive Daphne, Minerva sank back into her seat, covering her face with both hands as she collapsed face-down onto the table.

"What, what, what. It's like I'm, like I'm the bad one here. It's not like I do it to be praised, but of course I'm happy when I am! What's wrong with feeling glad I did it when someone says thank you? Is it my fault? Is this on me? I heal everyone, so I want to be healed too..."

"That you can't even manage to fully give up on yourself there — I think that's one of your finer points. — Now then."

Setting aside Minerva, who had sunk into a sea of self-questioning and withdrawn from the conversation, Echidna turned her gaze to Daphne, who had cut in.
Though Daphne's eyes were sealed shut and she shouldn't have been able to sense Echidna's gaze, she gave a small, endearing sniff of her nose.

"Donadona, what's with staring at Daphne like that? Unlike Nerneru or Metomet, Daphne can't keep patiently going along with a conversation, you know. In fact... *huff, huff*... Daphne's just about out of calories already."

"I thought I'd learned quite thoroughly, back when I lived, that nothing is quite so foolish as expecting cooperativeness from witches... but when things fail to progress even this badly, I find myself almost proud of you all."

As she spoke, Echidna raised her right hand and snapped her fingers.
At once, a steaming cup and a plate of cookies appeared before Daphne. Her sealed eyes went wide, and at the sudden appearance of food, Daphne came alive with excitement.

"Of course, I don't intend to make you wait, so first, go ahead and eat—"

"Gafu-gafu. Fumo-fumo. Mokyu-mokyu."

"I suppose I needn't have said anything. Though if possible, I would appreciate it if you kept your table manners."

Before Echidna, who shrugged, was the sight of Daphne, upper body plunged fully into the table, eating and drinking with her entire body. — Daphne's meals were, quite literally, taken with her "whole body."
Though she hummed chewing sounds with her mouth, in truth it wasn't through her mouth that the tea and sweets were being taken into her — it was directly through whatever skin made contact. The tea and confections provided, cups and all, were absorbed straight into Daphne's body, instantly becoming fuel for her "Gluttony."

"Ahh, delicious. So sweeeet. ...Oh, and, sorryyy. I got a little carried away and ended up nibbling on the table too."

"I won't go so far as to say I don't mind... but the moment I invited you, I was half-resigned to this happening anyway. I don't expect anything more than restraint from you."

"Would Donadona order a bird not to fly, or a fish not to swim?"

At Daphne's roundabout display of flat refusal, Echidna let out a breath. Daphne shook her whole body, having finished off the tea and sweets, and continued, "So then—"

"Now that I've got food in my stomach, I'll go along with Donadona's earlier talk. — It was something about parallel worlds or whatever, wasn't it?"

"That's right. Daphne, what do you think about that sort of thing?"

"Daphne doesn't think anything of it, really? Thinking about how, if I'd done this or that, things might've turned out like this or that — that kind of thinking doesn't fill your stomach any. Ahh, but if you think of it as branching depending on whether you ate meat or fish for dinner, maybe it's not such a silly idea after all."

"In Daphne's case, there's nothing lacking in her level of comprehension... it's simply that it doesn't hold enough interest to be worth discussing. This too was something I already knew well enough."

Among the witches, Daphne was, personality-wise, on the mild and approachable side.
The problem was simply that her very existence was, in itself, an outright harm to other living things, and that — quite apart from her mild temperament — a ferocious nature made her hopelessly unsuited to coexisting with "others."

"In the end, *haah*, thinking things over about parallel worlds, *whew*, it's probably just a case of, *haah*, thinking for the sake of thinking being pointless, *whew*."

So spoke the sloth witch, breaking into a conversation that showed no sign of developing, all while still sprawled full-length across the chipped table. Still wrapped in her own long hair, she addressed both Echidna, who turned her gaze toward her, and Daphne, who turned her sense of smell that way:

"Suppose there's such a thought, and a world branched off from it, *haah*. You can imagine it, but you can never actually know it or experience it, *whew*. Then its existence is just an untouchable bubble called possibility, *haah*. The moment you touch it, it'll pop and vanish, I'd think, *whew*."

"Indeed, from a realistic standpoint, that would be the case. One can be conscious of a parallel world's existence, but never observe it. 'Parallel' is well put, indeed. Two lines that never intersect — that, I'd say, is precisely what a parallel world, standing alongside as another world, amounts to."

"— But the Second 'Trial' isn't like that, is it."

To Echidna, who was summarizing Sekhmet's words, Minerva cut in on that final concluding part with a barbed tone. Her lovely face flushed crimson with anger, she went on:

"If Echidna's going out of her way to bring this up, it's got to be something nasty, right? That's it, isn't it? I hit the mark, didn't I? You think I've poked at a sore spot, don't you? If you don't want people probing your motives, then don't go around hiding sore spots in the first place!"

"It's a bit much to have you outraged when I haven't even said anything yet... well, I certainly can't deny it, that's true. After all, the Second 'Trial' is indeed structured that way."

Before Minerva, who dented the table with a punch of her fist, Echidna raised a hand lightly, and a black-bound book appeared within it.
It was the forbidden tome that only Echidna possessed, in which every scrap of knowledge concerning this world's "past," "future," and "present" was recorded — "The Memory of the World."
As the very embodiment of thirst for knowledge, Echidna stood in a position where, should she wish it, she could learn any and all information, knowledge, and history in this world. Though, owing to a matter of her own temperament, she herself apparently harbored something close to distaste for wielding that forbidden tome's power.

"The Second 'Trial' reads the mind of the challenger, seeking out a branching point along the path they've walked — or rather, a spot one might call a 'regret' — and has the Memory of the World reproduce the 'impossible present' that would have resulted from a different choice. By its very nature, it's somewhat easier to clear than the First 'Trial,' which confronts one with a symbol of past wrongs, or the Third 'Trial,' which awaits beyond it."

"What do you mean, easier to clear?"

"In essence, it's a matter of coming to terms with it, just as Daphne did. As Sekhmet said too, a parallel world is, in the end, ultimately an untouchable line, separate and apart. However much regret you carry, however much lingering attachment remains, it's a line that can never be reached."

"And your 'Trial' is what brings you right up to the very edge of that unreachable line!"

To Minerva, who raised the corners of her eyes irritably, Echidna gave a small shrug.
Smoothing back her own white hair, Echidna spoke as if to soothe the fired-up Minerva:

"For an ordinary person, clearing the Second 'Trial' is fairly easy. Unlike the First 'Trial,' which requires overcoming a past that has genuinely already passed, the Second 'Trial' only involves touching upon a 'possibility that might have been.' Whether you approach a parallel world with denial or with affirmation is entirely up to you... it's enough, in the end, if you can affirm the present, the true world as it now stands."

"The true... world..."

"So we come back to it being a matter of coming to terms with it. A simple resignation that even Sekhmet, or Daphne, or you yourself could manage. — Once you can do that, the 'Trial' can be overcome."

At Echidna's reasoning, Minerva drew in her chin, though reluctantly.
Indeed, going by Echidna's manner of speaking alone, the substance of the "Trial" didn't seem all that severe.
For the witches gathered here — or even, hypothetically, for anyone not a witch, so long as they possessed a firm sense of self — clearing the "Trial" would surely be easy.

"But then, if that's so, why did Subarun have such a hard time with it, I wonder. Subarun didn't look to me like a kid without a sense of self."

"— His case, is it."

Daphne, recalling Subaru from memory, worked her mouth in an oddly mumbling way for some reason. Ignoring her gesture and considering only her words, Echidna closed her eyes.

"The Second 'Trial' is the observation of a parallel world. In a sense, it's an act of being shown what lies beyond a past regret. And as I said before, it can easily be met with either affirmation or denial. — Because, as a matter of fact, one can simply resolve to accept that they never actually lived through that outcome."

However, Echidna went on.

"In his case alone, that doesn't apply. Even for me, it was unexpected that the Second 'Trial' would cut this deep into him. — Truly, unexpected."

"Sniff, sniff... Donadona, you smell like you're smiling happily."

"You're happy because it was unexpected, aren't you. You nasty, perverted... there's just no helping you!"

"Birds of a feather, they say. As my friends, I doubt you two are exempt from that either."

Daphne laughed, and Minerva bristled visibly with anger. From the silent Sekhmet's direction, the faint sound of sleeping breath had begun to drift; watching each witch's reaction, Echidna rocked the back of her chair. Then—

"Dona~, Typhon's hungry too~"

A girl came trotting up from the meadow, all but leaping onto the hilltop table as she called out to Echidna.
Short green hair and tan skin, a smile with dazzling white teeth — Typhon, the Witch of Pride.
Echidna smiled at the girl who, rather than join the overly complicated conversation, had spent her time out in the field.

"Sorry for boring you. As for tea, Typhon... shall I make it sweet? You can eat the tea cakes normally, right?"

"Anything's fine. Ran around and used up all my energy, so gonna drink, eat, and rest."

Full of energy, Typhon pulled out the empty seat beside Sekhmet and sat down. Then, playing with one hand in Sekhmet's long hair, she ate the tea and sweets Echidna had conjured with a snap of her fingers, smearing her face and the table as she went.
If one didn't know Typhon's true nature, it would have made for quite a heartwarming sight.

"Weren't you worn out too, looking after Typhon?"

"T-that's not... true... at all? T-Typhon-chan is a good girl, and, her power, doesn't... yeah, doesn't reach me, so it's fine, right? I-I'm fine. I'm... totally fine."

Beside Echidna, who lifted her gaze, the person who had arrived at the tea party after Typhon answered haltingly, a faint, fragile smile rising to her lips.
She was a girl of startling fragility, her pale pink hair grown down to her waist. Her features were nothing especially distinctive, yet somehow one's eyes were naturally drawn to her.
Above all, her small-animal-like bearing and expressions left an impression of tugging painfully at the strings of one's heart.

"Do sit, Carmilla. — There's a particular reason I went to the trouble of calling you here."

"S-something... are we... starting? W-will it be... scary?"

"It won't be scary, and it won't hurt. — I just need your help moving a piece on the board."

Taking the offered seat beside her, Carmilla — the Witch of Lust — looked timidly at Echidna. Echidna smiled at her and spread both hands lightly.

"— I'd like you to try saving a lost lamb, with your love."

So Echidna said to the trembling witch, extending her outstretched hands toward her—
