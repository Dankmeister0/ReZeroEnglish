Arc 9 Chapter 57: "The Last Service"

---

— The reason Ferris cooperated with the "Aldebusters" was, of course, none other than for the sake of his liege, Crusch Karsten.

Originally, Ferris had come to the royal capital together with Wilhelm to negotiate directly with the royal castle, seeking to join the survey team headed for the now-open path to the Pleiades Watchtower.
It was in the midst of that when Al's group struck the royal capital — the Sin Archbishop of Gluttony was broken out of prison, and a fierce battle between the Sword Demon and the Divine Dragon unfolded.

"—There's something I'd like you to hear, and something I'd like your cooperation on."

The one who had laid out the situation to Ferris and the others and requested their cooperation was Otto, the Emilia Camp's chamberlain. Hearing from him about Al — about Aldebaran's atrocities, and about Reinhard's involvement in the fighting — Ferris, unlike Wilhelm, who leaned eagerly forward, wasn't all that inclined toward wholehearted cooperation.
Of course, as a member of the Royal Guard in peacetime, he felt anger toward those who'd disturb the kingdom's peace. But what could someone do, when they lacked raw combat strength, and even the one gift they were supposed to have — their healing arts — couldn't be put to use saving the master who mattered most?

In fact, when Wilhelm was subsequently defeated by the Divine Dragon in the very next battle and left grievously wounded, unconscious, that feeling only grew larger in Ferris.
But then something happened that wouldn't let even Ferris keep hanging his head.

"It's been a while, Felix-sama."

Facing the maid who bowed and dropped a curtsy — Rem — the memories of conversing with her, of fighting alongside her, rose up inside Ferris, and he learned that the Authority of Gluttony could in fact be stripped away.
Which was to say, she was nothing less than living proof that one of the two Curses Crusch bore — Memory and Dragon's Blood — could be undone.

"...Well then, guess there's nothing for it but to do it, nya."

There was also the fact that Wilhelm — who had stayed by suffering Crusch's side and kept encouraging a dejected Ferris who felt he could do nothing — had been struck down.
Following the request, lending strength to the Aldebusters, stopping Al's group who seemed ready to make an enemy of the entire world to accomplish whatever it was they wanted — for that, Ferris decided to do anything necessary.

Although—

"—Never would I have imagined I'd end up playing the trump card."

Battered by tremendous plumes of ash, violent winds, and pouring raindrops, Ferris — covering his mouth with a hand against the dust — reflected on the role, of all things, that had been thrust upon him.
Using Petra's power called Compression — which could move a target in an instant, or wrap up a conversation in a flash — they had repeatedly pulled off the outrageous trick of transporting the wounded to Ferris, healing them, and sending them right back to the battlefield. Through that trick, again and again, the Aldebusters had sunk their teeth into a formidable foe.
That job alone had already been quite the responsibility, but being entrusted with the decisive blow against an Al possessing such absurd power — that, he had never imagined.

— Casting a healing spell on Al that would remain in constant effect. That was the role entrusted to Ferris as the trump card against Al, a task that allowed for no failure.

When it came down to it, even now Ferris's understanding hadn't fully caught up.
Putting together what Petra and Old Man Rom had said, he understood it as something like this: whenever Al judged himself to have suffered a "fatal wound," time would rewind to before that wound was taken. By abusing that, Al had kept weathering every crisis, and had even driven off Reinhard.
Honestly, it made him want to tilt his head and ask what everyone was so seriously discussing. If that were really true, it would be far too invincible. Nobody could ever win, could they?
And all the more so, given that the way to defeat such an invincible power was supposedly Ferris's own healing magic.

It felt like far too much of a gamble.
It might turn out to be nothing but the folly of fully healing an enemy everyone had worked so hard to corner.
Even so — if, by some chance, Ferris could become the deciding factor in this battle—

"Do not regret it, Ferris. Your heart is a noble thing. Take pride in your gift. ...It is the gentlest power in this world."

"—Your Highness."

There had once been someone who had praised Ferris's power like that, who had been proud of it like that.
Then and now, Ferris's power still fell short of the title of Blue. But even so, he wanted to believe there had been meaning in never thinking of it as a curse, in continuing to hone it all this time.
For that reason—

"—Roy!! If you're out there, come and get me! Eat me... eat my 'name'!!"

From somewhere beyond the blinding dust, mixed in with the roaring wind and the earth's screaming, a voice could be heard.
Ferris's healing magic did indeed seem to be working as the trump card. The proof was Al's violently shaken state, and this desperate cry.
But the enemy hadn't given up yet. — Ferris, too, drew up his thin knees and thought.

*What more can I do, for someone precious to me?*

"Rigel. —Natsuki Rigel!!"

Raising his voice into the thick haze of dust hanging in the air, Al strained his eyes desperately.
The falling rain, the fierce wind, the rumbling of the earth were all so loud that nothing around him was distinct. Unable to rely on sight or hearing, he still raised his voice as though willing his own throat to tear.
The detestable healing power that had been cast on him instantly mended his throat, raw from screaming until it drew blood. Even as he benefited from that ironic blessing, he kept shouting at the top of his lungs.

"Roy! Ro-oy!! Find me! It's Rigel! Natsuki! Rigel!!"

Each time he spoke it, he felt a pain like his soul being scraped by an invisible file, yet Al shouted that name over and over — shouted it, shouted it, kept shouting it.
Natsuki Rigel. — Al's true name, from before he'd taken up the title of the Chaser Star.

"—!"

He felt no reality in it at all. Only an irritation clung stubbornly to the corner of his mind.
To Al, the name "Al," called out so many times by the Witch, sat far more familiar in his ears than the name given to him by parents who had never once called him by it directly. Even that name had turned into a wound, from the moment the Witch had become the Witch of Greed.
By now, he didn't even know who he had "originally" been. — But self-recognition didn't matter. What mattered was how Od Lagna had recorded this pitiful life.
So—

"—I am Natsuki Rigel."

Al acknowledged as himself a name that held nothing but bitterness to speak aloud.
For no matter how much he refused it, resented it, or hated it, one couldn't choose one's own life, or one's own parents.

"—————"

Still shouting on and on, Al searched desperately through the ash cloud for Roy.
Tossed about by the destructive gale from before, everyone on the battlefield had been blown away without exception. Al himself had been sent tumbling a good distance, and surely the Witch of Melancholy, Ferris, and Roy were no exceptions either.
As things stood, there shouldn't have been anyone else on the scene still able to move. But this was an opponent who had held Ferris, their trump card, in reserve until the very last moment. It wouldn't be strange if they still had another card up their sleeve. — Now that Al's Territory had been sealed, that could well be the deciding factor.

"Damn it...!"

He'd thought that at the very least he might push Territory onto Ferris alone, but no good. The sense of freeing the bug he'd almost gotten a grip on had slipped further away again after being repelled by the Witch of Melancholy. Even setting that aside, the targeting was too heavily randomized. If it landed on the Witch of Melancholy again, it would be unbearable to watch. What's more, Ferris's healing magic — the spell continuously restoring Al — was only sustained by drawing the mana it required from Al himself.
And right now, because of his connection to "Aldebaran," that supply was effectively inexhaustible.

"If I can't rely on Territory...!"

Then he might as well burn through the mana binding him and blast the obstacles away with a great spell. That idea surfaced, and Al sketched out several incantations in his mind.
All of them were things he'd learned from the Witch, knowledge that had been shelved because he'd never been able to afford the resources needed to cast them — but now, connected to "Aldebaran," they were within reach.
Just as he had with Reinhard, he'd make full use of them for a breakthrough without Territory—

"Ugh...!"

—The instant he thought that, a surge of nausea rose up and stopped his mouth.

Struck by a violent dizziness and ringing in his ears, Al let out an "Ah?" and nearly dropped to his knees. Thinking he might have been attacked by someone, he threw a hurried glance around, but there was no one. — And then he realized.
The thing that had just made his vision swim was nothing other than an extraordinary pressure.

The pressure of a choice he could not afford to get wrong was crushing Al's very heart and nerve in its grip.

"...You're kidding, right."

Looking down at his own trembling right hand, Al fell speechless.
In this crucible, on the very brink of everything he'd done coming to nothing, with his body in top condition thanks to that infuriating healing magic, in a situation demanding the fastest and best judgment possible — to be so spooked by that, his head refusing to work properly, was a bad joke.
But reality wasn't a joke of any kind. However nightmarish it felt, it was fact.

— Because Al had never, not once, made a decisive choice without his Authority.

Before this battle even began — in the Vollachian Empire, in the Royal Selection, on the Sword Slave Isle, and in the days with the Witch before all of that — Al's choices had always been the optimal answer, guarded by his Authority.
No matter what mistake he made, he could redo it, pick a different option, and erase the failure. Living by stacking up that pattern again and again — that was Al's way of being. And now, it didn't work.

"—————"

He had no idea what scale of wind, blown up to clear the ash cloud, would avoid killing anyone. The heat needed to evaporate the rainfall, the shock needed to cancel the rumbling earth, even whether stepping into this ash cloud with his right foot or his left would yield the best answer — all of it was an unknown.

What could not be verified was terrifying.
Walking a path with no guaranteed right answer was the same as groping forward through darkness.
Al could not advance down such a path, one with no certainty of reward. — Because an Al who couldn't make use of his Authority was nothing but a hopeless puppet.

That puppet had been unable to save either of the two Witches, or the woman he loved.

"—!"

Once he started thinking, only bad thoughts crossed his mind.
Was the plan to have Roy eat his own "name" really the best move? Wasn't there some other hand he could play instead? Might it be better to use the curse mark as a shield, let Roy run wild, and strike at the opening that created? What about finding some groundbreaking way to die that slipped through the gaps of complete healing? Or would it be better to flee, lost in the dust cloud? Et cetera, et cetera — only his emotions grew more frantic—

"—See there? Again—"

"—————"

That frantic, impatient emotion stopped short, as though doused in cold water.
Hesitation and fear — the negative emotions that had all come gushing out of Al the moment his Authority was sealed — and as though dragged out along with them, the greatest regret he had sunk to the depths of his heart began to burn.
Contrary to that extreme heat — the kind that would burn on contact, that scorched the skin just from being near it — Al felt a sensation of every last drop of his blood turning to ice, and he let out a breath.

"Once again, this—"

"...Stop it."

"I—"

"STOOOP IIIT—!!"

Al screamed, on the verge of being forced face to face with the thing he'd only managed not to look at directly by covering his ears, averting his eyes, and forcibly stuffing it down into the depths of his heart.
He rejected the voice he could hear, rejected the smile he could see, rejected the heat pressing in on him, and bared his teeth. — In that instant, forcing his eyes open, a faint afterimage beyond the ash cloud came into view.

For a heartbeat, the figure was lost in the dust, but there was no mistaking it. It was Roy. It was Gluttony.
From this welling regret — no, from the worst situation now surrounding Al, the one and only ray of light that could pull him out of it — that was what he saw. In that instant, he broke into a run.

"—Roy! I'm right here! Eat me! Eat my 'name'!!"

The hesitation wavering in the foot he put forward, the cowardice searching for a way to die, the weakness trying to flee into some other plan, the sloth doubting his own choice — all of it was shattered by the shock of kicking off the ground in that instant.
In this moment, by throwing himself single-mindedly into seizing the choice that had come fluttering down before him, Al chose to flee from the most terrifying thing of all.

— Until now, Al had used the power of his Authority to keep averting his eyes from the one and only chance to truly "choose." And so, he hadn't known.

That when "choosing" any one thing, a choice made while driven purely by the desire to escape the moment could never open up a positive fate for the thing chosen.
That was—

"—Al-sama."

—Proven by the act of the one who caught Al's body head-on as he tried to escape by leaping toward the silhouette he'd seen beyond the ash cloud: Yae Tenzen, who was supposed to be his ally.

— The moment Yae lost her battle against Rem, her "next" battle had already begun.

Blown away, tumbling down the slope of an iceberg formed atop a frozen great river, she abandoned every method of stemming her bleeding that she could otherwise have used, and threw herself entirely into playing the part of a defeated woman on the brink of death.
The purpose behind that was—

"—I don't intend to take your life. That person would be saddened by it."

When the oni girl who said that cast her healing magic, Yae had won her first gamble.
Honestly, given how merciless Rem had been mid-battle, Yae thought, what nerve to claim she had no intention of taking a life — but since her life really had been spared, she couldn't very well complain. — More than anything, if she so much as voiced a complaint, the chance she'd worked so hard for would go up in smoke.

Unfortunately, there was, of course, no possible path left for Yae to turn the tables on Rem from here.
So long as she hadn't figured out the trick by which Rem had nullified her flashing strike in their final exchange, even a successful surprise attack might not be enough to finish Rem off. That too would be nothing but a waste of a once-in-a-lifetime chance.

So Yae's aim lay elsewhere. — In one final act of service, poured out with what little she had left, with her entire being.

Even Rem, wary of a counterattack, had kept the healing magic to the bare minimum — just enough to close the bleeding wound and halt the countdown to death. On top of that, the unresisting Yae's arms and legs were bound in chains, a thoroughness meant to deny her any role beyond that of hostage or captive. — And yet, even so, one had to say her understanding of a Shinobi fell short.
So long as it wasn't instant death, completing the job even with one's head flying off — that was the way of the Shinobi.
She could dislocate every joint in her body to slip free of the chains, and burn her tanden to disregard even a mortal wound.

By doing so, Yae thought, even she — who had failed to fulfill a single one of the roles entrusted to her — surely had something she could still do for Al, at the very last.
And then—

"—————"

— The once-in-a-lifetime chance Yae had been aiming for came around far sooner than she'd imagined.

Amid a tremendous shock that violently swallowed the Agzadd Canyon and the surrounding land, and while the aftershocks of the resulting cataclysm bore down as gales, floods, and earthquakes, Yae moved.
Slipping away from Rem, who had tried to shield her from the impact, Yae broke away from the canyon and reached the top of the cliff — and there, she was made to keenly feel the inadequacy of Al's accomplices, herself included.

With sand and dust filling her vision as if blanketing the entire length of the vast canyon, Al hadn't even reached the Grand Mogolade Geyser — far from it, he'd been pinned down by the enemy right at the point where he'd climbed up.
That was a hardship that needn't have happened, if Yae, if "Aldebaran," if Roy Alphard, if Heinkel, if the accomplices Al had gathered had only fulfilled their roles.
Precisely because of that—

"—————"

In a space where sight was disrupted by the dust, sound by the rumbling earth, and smell by the swirling wind, Yae pieced together the scant information her five senses could gather and unerringly picked out the person she needed to find in it.
Likely no one could make fuller use of this situation than a Shinobi could. Feeling grateful for the first time that she had ever been abducted to the Shinobi Village, Yae raced briskly to his side.
This time, no matter what, in order to be of use to him—

"—Roy! I'm right here! Eat me! Eat my 'name'!!"

— She had thought, she must be of use to Al. And yet.

"—Al-sama."

She caught, head-on, the body of the man who had burst forth through the hanging dust. Feeling against her own skin the heat of the body she now held, its weight, the pulse of life within it, Yae was utterly bewildered by her own act.

"—Ya... e?"

Forcibly stopped and pitched forward, Al peered at Yae's profile right beside him and murmured that, in a tone as though he'd seen something unbelievable.
In that trembling voice was astonishment and confusion — but the same was true for Yae. — No, considering the resolve she had held just moments before, one could say Yae was even more confused than Al.

"I..."

She understood, more or less, the situation Al was in.
Cornered by some means or another, he was trying to find a way out through this chaotic situation. What he needed to use for that was Gluttony, and rushing to the Sin Archbishop's side within the dust cloud, so that Al could accomplish his purpose — that was the best, the last, service Yae could render.

Knowing all that, Yae had blocked Al's path. She had thrown her whole body into it, and stopped him cold.
Why? She questioned her own astonishment. — Yae found Al terrifying, and that was precisely why she wanted him to die, and why she had been trying to let him accomplish his purpose, so as to bring about his death. But an Al cornered like this, now — perhaps Yae could kill him with her own two hands. Was that it? Was that why she'd stopped him — to make him pay the price for the terror she'd been made to feel?

"I..."

"—It's love."

At the question rising in her, the image of Rem declaring that answer without hesitation flitted through her mind, and Yae held her breath.
No, absolutely not that. They were both maids trying to let their masters accomplish a purpose, yes, but the underlying motive was completely different between Yae and Rem. It was no use as a reference.

Still, in recalling the oni who had pushed through on a single conviction, for the sake of a feeling so enormous it was almost absurd, Yae suddenly noticed a certain feeling within herself.

"I—"

Yae had wanted Al to die. Because the monster called Al terrified her, unbearably so.
But—

"—It seems I can't bear the thought of forgetting you, Al-sama."

—That she did not, in fact, wish to lose him for all eternity.

"—————"

At Yae, who had caught him and then spoken her reason, Al's mind went blank.
He didn't understand. He didn't understand. There was too much he didn't understand. — Al's Territory had broken Yae's mind, and she was supposed to have become an absolute collaborator on the condition of his death.
That selfish trust he'd placed in Yae had been betrayed, at this most critical of moments.

Had she been lying in wait for the very best moment to betray him?
He didn't understand. He didn't understand. All he knew was that, having stopped in his tracks, Al was caught — by the voice he most wanted to flee from, the one he'd covered his ears against, averted his eyes from, forced down into the depths of his heart.

"—Behold, once again, the victory is mine."

"...Priscilla."

In a moment when he should have been striving to grasp even the slightest possibility before him, what slipped from Al's mouth was neither Gluttony, nor the woman who had held him back, nor even his own name.
He existed now with the ash of regret piled up from every scene where he should have acted, should have accomplished something, and had failed to — and yet, once again, Al longed for a mountain of ash.

"What..."

—was ever a victory, Priscilla.
You died. You died, didn't you. To die means to lose. To die means your wish goes unfulfilled. The only ones in this world for whom dying doesn't mean the end are himself and Natsuki Subaru. So you were not one of them.
So then, just what could possibly have been a victory? You were the woman least suited in all this world to sore-loser talk — and yet, at the very last, did you spit out sour grapes after all?

Why, in the end, did you smile, Priscilla?

"The stars—"

"—Huh?"

"The stars were at fault, Al-sama...!"

As Al gave his mind over to a self-directed question with no answer, Yae looked up at him from within his arms.
Al held his breath at her voice, with no room left in it, and at the content of her words. Al himself had said many times that the stars were to blame, and Yae had heard him say it.
So was this some kind of retaliation? Having stopped Al in his tracks, was she now using it against him as spite— No, Yae's crimson eyes appealed that it was not that at all.

A sincere light shone in those eyes, the same light a certain Witch had once carried, and it said—
—that it was not Al's fault.

"—! There's no way—"

—that could be true, a sudden burst of fury welled up to say.
Everything about the situation up to now, everything that had happened, all the credit and blame for how things had turned out — it all lay with Al, it lay with Natsuki Subaru. There was no way it wasn't their fault.
That—

"—Al!!"

A voice like a silver bell, one not even the sand and dust could muffle, sliced clean through that instantaneous boiling-over in a single moment.

Springing up and hurrying to leap up out of the canyon, Emilia was astonished by the sight that greeted her.

"I thought that was a reeeally huge shake..."

Dust filled her vision so thickly it seemed to bury it, and the roar of the wind wouldn't stop howling. On top of that, the shaking of the ground, far from settling, kept growing worse — while she'd been unconscious, everything had turned into a huge mess.
But she wouldn't be discouraged. She wouldn't mope. Emilia had no time to spare for feeling down.

"I need to hurry and get to Al..."

The reason her companions had entrusted her with the role of stopping Al was because everyone believed she and Rem were best suited for it. And the reason Rem had sent her off, chasing after Al as he flew away, was because Rem had placed her hopes in Emilia.
Carrying all of that on her shoulders, she had steeled herself to help Subaru and Beatrice, so there was no way she could let Al get away—

"—There he is! Al and... eh!? That girl!?"

Charging fervently into the dust cloud, Emilia found Al and, beyond the swirling grit, Yae — locked in an embrace with him — and was startled all over again.
Rem was supposed to have been the one holding Yae back. If Yae was here, that meant she was worried about Rem — Ferris had cast a powerful healing spell on her, calling it a good-luck charm, but even so, Rem's safety weighed on her mind.

"I need to—"

—catch up and find out, right now — it was the instant she kicked off the ground hard with that thought. — In Emilia's field of view, beyond Al and Yae locked in their embrace, a great change came, shoving the dust cloud aside.
It was the true form of the tremor beneath her feet that had never once settled, the true source of the earth's endless screaming. Surging forward while violently shaking the ground was an absolutely tremendous flash flood.

Witnessing it about to mercilessly sweep away the battlefield, everything in its path, Emilia raised her speed off the ground without thinking,

"—Al!!"

and at her call, he looked up with a start; their eyes met, hers meeting his behind the helm, and Emilia leapt clean over the heads of the two embracing figures to face the flash flood surging straight at her head-on.
And then—

"—Icicle Line!"


△▼△▼△▼△


"—————"

The air let out a shrill scream as an unbelievable scale of freezing was wrought upon the floodwaters.
What had come surging in was a flood of water on a level near mythic, as though some great lake had been upended like a bucket. And the beautiful girl with silver hair streaming behind her froze the incoming flood solid, one stretch after another, building it into a glacier.

"Yaaaaaah—!!"

But it was a losing game of cat-and-mouse between ice and flash flood.
The cold Emilia produced would freeze part of the flood, but the next wave would come rolling over that frozen section, and Emilia would freeze that too. Repeating this again and again produced an umbrella of ice from the freezing, with a great glacier flowing on endlessly, spreading atop it.

"Uyaaaaah—!!"

Starting from Emilia, the source of the cold, the umbrella of ice expanded in a circle and, following the flow of the flood, grew larger and larger, arching over Al's head before eventually passing beyond.
The larger it grew, the heavier it became; the umbrella cracked, and it was only a matter of time before that immense weight came crashing down—

"—————"

For an instant, Emilia glanced this way, and her eyes met Al's.
Whatever it was that thought stirred in her, Emilia's lips pressed tightly together—

"Uryaaaaah—!!"

—and the ice umbrella, which had been creaking and cracking, was reinforced, and the construction of the great glacier, which had seemed on the verge of collapse, continued still. Icicles rose one after another from the ground, in an inventive display of support, propping up the umbrella just as it threatened to fall — but Al's attention was fixed elsewhere.

Emilia called herself the Witch of Ice, and in fact displayed power worthy of that title. But if the reason Emilia was, at this very moment, unleashing power beyond her limits, lay in that glance from before—
If, just as she had shielded Al from the boulder trap set in the canyon, Emilia was now, too, exerting her power in order to protect him—

Thinking that, he realized it now, of all times. — Emilia had been properly looking at Al all along, and had been trying to talk with him, too.

"I—"

A hoarse voice leaked from his throat, and no more would follow.
Only, it felt as though there had been some great, great, irretrievably great misalignment. — A misalignment that, even before his Authority had been sealed, could no longer be redone, no longer be taken back.

"—Behold, once again, the victory is mine."

What had ever been a victory? In the end, that remained unknown.
But Priscilla, saying that, laughing in triumph — you, truly—

"—That's far enough."

—In that instant, an unreasoning hammer that swept everything aside pierced the battlefield as light.
