Arc 9 Chapter 13: "The Most Beloved and the Most Unloved"

—Two hundred eighty-nine.

In the stoppage time after clearing the main event, the ambush from the hidden reinforcement showed less mercy than expected.

While Garfiel had gone off to protect the girls, I have no idea how the hidden reinforcement who hadn't been with them had managed to dodge that great flood. But having pulled it off on her own, the ambusher broke my neck over and over as I came down the stairs, worn out to the bone.

No questions. No hesitation. Truly, her actions—single-mindedly devoted to eliminating an obstacle—were the work of someone who understood she was the last deterrent left standing in the tower.

"You're something else, kid."

To build this much skill at such a young age—just imagining how much blood-soaked training she must have piled up filled him with respect.

He'd heard she was a twin. He wondered if her sister was just as strong. —No need to ask now; he'd probably get the chance to find out firsthand soon enough.

After all, from here on out, Aldebaran was about to make an enemy of the entire world.

"—!"

A faint gasp, and the ambusher's slender hand failed to break Aldebaran's neck.

An instant before the girl's hand—having crept up from behind—could connect, Aldebaran had wrapped his neck in a cast of stone, dodging the blow meant to snap his cervical vertebrae.

But she wasn't soft enough to ease off after that. The instant she saw the surprise attack fail, she slid immediately to Aldebaran's left—into the blind spot where he had no arm—and launched a knife-hand strike at his side.

And despite how cute her little hand looked, it was a terrifying blow wrapped in the 'Flow Method,' meant to punch through his flank, slip between his ribs, and tear straight into his heart.

A killing blow that made near-instant death almost unavoidable. But "almost instant death" is just that—*almost*. Not instant.

Even if brief, unbearable pain was still coming. So—

"Ngh—gah!"

Aldebaran extended his earthen prosthetic arm into the spot the girl was sliding toward, grabbed her twin-tied hair, and forced her against the wall by sheer strength. Then, right there, the clay arm changed into a great strip of mud, pinning the girl to the wall like she'd been crucified.

With both her hands and feet bound and only her head left free, the ambusher—Flam—and Aldebaran glared at each other.

"I'm weaker than you, kid. I'm short an arm. My helmet gives me lousy visibility. I'm worn ragged from back-to-back fights. I've got no skill and no talent. —So I win."

"Al-s—"

"Do me a favor and stay quiet."

He snapped the fingers of his right hand in front of Flam's face, sealing her mouth with a stone gag. It was the kind of thing she could've easily pulled off if her limbs were free—but with those limbs bound, she was helpless.

And so, at last, he could say he'd cleared the bonus stage.

"—"

A long, deep sigh escaped him.

There wasn't a trace of relief in it—if anything, it was a gloomy thing, worlds away from the satisfaction of having accomplished something.

As a baseline matter, if you compared this to climbing a mountain, this was still the first station—not even the most die-hard positive thinker would be so ridiculous as to celebrate wildly at the first station.

Especially when this particular challenge wasn't something he could be proud of in front of anyone.

"Man, you look at me like that and I can't even offer up one excuse, ladies."

So he said, and in the turning Aldebaran's field of view, two girls stood side by side in the fourth-floor corridor, quietly fixing their eyes on him.

—one with cold hostility in her narrow eyes, the other with a desperate, earnest color in her round ones.

"This girl's angry, so would you mind not coming any closer, hmm?"

Turning her half-facing body fully around, the braided girl—Meili—kept Aldebaran, now facing them, at bay. Perched atop her head, tail raised with her blue hair as a foothold, was the comic-relief Witchbeast that had shown its face so many times both on the journey and in the tower.

That tail, its tip glowing faintly, was also, surprisingly, no small threat as a weapon—assuming, of course, one was careless enough to take a hit to a vital spot.

"Well, fine, I get not getting any closer. I never meant to hurt you girls in the first place."

"Comin' allll the way down here to search us out so boldly? That's just too funny."

"You're right that I came to find you. It's the reason I came that's wrong."

Faced with Meili's utterly unyielding attitude, Aldebaran fiddled with the seam of his helmet as he chose his words. And then, beside that exchange, the girl who'd been standing silently murmured "Reason..." under her breath, and—

"Then what reason *did* you come down here for, Al-san?"

—Aldebaran fell silent before Petra, who had asked the question firmly, without looking away.

"—"

He understood well enough why Meili, a former assassin used to bloodshed, had nerves of steel. But that Petra—originally just a village girl throwing herself into maid training—could be this tough too, he had to take his hat off to her.

Aldebaran had timed his move for the moment everyone's guard would be at its lowest, setting things in motion without letting anyone catch on beforehand. Which meant Petra and the others should have had no idea what was going on at all.

Inside that small head of hers, every question imaginable, along with the anxiety and confusion that must have made her want to burst into tears and scream, had to be swirling. —And yet she didn't let a hint of it show.

"I got what I came for. I've got no more business in this tower. So, consider this my goodbye."

"Even if you say goodbye to me..."

"Garfiel and Ezzo are asleep at the top of the tower. Get them treated quick and they should live."

"—ah."

For an instant, Petra stiffened at Aldebaran's self-serving declaration and moved to brace for battle. Beside her, Meili too began to widen her stance, ready to match Petra's resolve—but the girls' fighting spirit was thrown into disarray by Aldebaran's words pointing up the tower, to the first floor.

And unfortunately, whether his opponents were girls or not, Aldebaran had no room left for mercy.

"So, what'll it be? Gonna stand here and fight me like heroes? Push hard enough and you might even beat me... but lose, and those two up top go down with you. Total wipeout."

"You... coward...!"

"I know it. And believe me, I hate myself for it, deep down."

Cursed at by a sharp-eyed Petra, Aldebaran reproached himself for having become a rotten adult.

But reality, it seemed, let the opinion of that nasty, cowardly adult carry the day. —Because Petra, clever and brave as she was, could do the math on the risk of what would happen if she lost.

She couldn't risk getting Garfiel and the others killed by throwing herself into a fight with such thin odds of victory.

"And what did you do with the mister and Beatrice-chan, hmm?"

"The two of them are napping together. They're so lovey-dovey close, splitting them up would be too cruel—my heart couldn't take it."

He answered that to Meili, who'd asked after Subaru and the others' safety in Petra's place.

Unfortunately, he had no intention of giving the two of them any more information than that.

Either way, once they got Garfiel and the others rescued, they'd hear the same story from their own mouths. He didn't want to carelessly provoke Petra and the others, who had made such a rational call, needlessly.

Even unfolding his domain, even with the strain shouldered elsewhere, being tired was being tired regardless.

"You told me not to get closer, but the exit's over that way. Let me through."

He said this with his hand raised, and slowly advanced toward the girls. To show he meant no hostility, he kept the raised hand up, and open, without lowering it.

"—"

As Aldebaran drew near, Petra and Meili silently opened a path for him. As he passed between the girls spread to either side, the only sound to echo hollowly through the quiet corridor was the click of the little crimson scorpion's pincers.

That crimson scorpion's threatening click seemed, precisely, like an expression of their hearts' refusal to accept this—

"I really meant to help you properly this whole three days, Al-san."

"—"

"I hate you. I really, really hate you."

The girl's tear-choked voice, wrung out from somewhere deep—of all the wounds Aldebaran had taken in this Pleiades Watchtower, that one was, without question, the deepest.

When Aldebaran touched down at the tower's entrance, a battle just as heart-fraying as the one against Petra and the others on the fourth floor was unfolding there too.

Taking part in that battle was a jet-black Earth Dragon named Patrasche, who had helped bring the party—Aldebaran included—all the way to this tower.

And facing Patrasche head-on was—

"'Yo, me. You get everything you left undone taken care of?'"

So spoke the massive creature, in a heavy, stern voice that carried not one shred of dignity in its tone.

At the tower's entrance, having leisurely pushed open the great main gate with a single hand, was a blue-scaled 'Dragon' glaring at the waiting Patrasche—Volcanica, now going by 'Aldebaran.'

"—!!"

Facing off against that 'Aldebaran,' Patrasche whinnied without giving an inch.

Honestly, every last woman among Subaru's companions was, mentally speaking, something else. —No, come to think of it, pretty much every woman Aldebaran had ever met had been a piece of work too.

He could think of maybe one weak woman, if that, in his whole life.

Anyway—

"I'm the Dragon here, so you're the one who should quit picking on the weak. You already look bad enough without adding 'bully' on top of it."

"'Don't go calling me bad. If I'm bad, then you're plenty crazy yourself. Normally a guy thinks something up and doesn't go through with it. —Hijacking the 'Divine Dragon,' of all things.'"

Speaking in an unfamiliar voice with an unfamiliar look, 'Aldebaran' said this while flexing his wings. At those words, Aldebaran shrugged.

Even called bad and crazy by himself, it was a desperate scheme he'd pulled off simply because he could.

In the first place, the 'Aldebaran' saying all this was Aldebaran too, so put in the same spot, he'd surely have done the exact same thing—which made getting nagged about it more than a little unreasonable.

"'Sorry about this, little Earth Dragon. But not moving was the right call. If you'd gone down, nobody could've carried your family out of this sea of sand.'"

"—"

"'What?'"

"Nah, just thinking, that's exactly like me. The way you threaten people is identical."

Looking down at Patrasche, 'Aldebaran's' logic for avoiding a fight had been exactly the same as the way Aldebaran had threatened Petra and the others—which left him with the most complicated feeling imaginable.

Anyway—

"How's the flying?"

"'Should be fine... I think. Let's just say I'll ease into it as we go.'"

"That's a way to keep the worry coming..."

At 'Aldebaran's' none-too-reassuring answer, Aldebaran couldn't quite hide a subtle unease, but he used the tail as a foothold and leapt onto its back anyway. Then, clinging to the many jutting ridges of the dragon's scales, he called out, "Go for it."

"'—Lift off!'"

The next instant, with that shout, wings beat and 'Aldebaran' rose into the sky.

Scattering sand in the roaring wind it kicked up, the 'Dragon' climbed in altitude all at once, closing in on the towering upper reaches of the Pleiades Watchtower before pulling away from it entirely.

As the tower fell further and further behind, Aldebaran looked back at the ones he'd left there—

"'Don't tell me you're having regrets.'"

"I did. Four hundred years' worth of it, in fact."

Mocked by a version of himself who knew every detail of the circumstances, Aldebaran clicked his tongue.

What 'Aldebaran' meant was the plain fact that there was no room left to be looking backward. And that was correct. As he'd once likened it to a mountain, this was still only the first station.

From here on, aiming for the starlight beyond countless leagues that he'd resolved to reach no matter what, Aldebaran couldn't afford to misplay even a single move.

"'Well, don't go putting too much on your own shoulders. I'm here from here on out too. That oughta make it at least a little easier, right?'"

Slicing steadily through the night sky over the sand sea with its wings, 'Aldebaran' said this easily.

Even though the same "memories" had been installed from the 'Book of the Dead,' was that optimism simply a difference in whatever Vessel they'd each landed in? Still, being overly negative wasn't good either.

In truth, he'd gained backup this powerful—more than he could ever have hoped for.

All that was left was—

"'—That's as far as you go.'"


　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　△▼△▼△▼△


—It was the instant he tried to set his next course of action that he was run through with light.

"—"

Violently, they crashed into the cold night's sea of sand, and the 'Dragon' slowly raised its body. Aldebaran, who'd been wrapped in that 'Dragon's' wing, shook his head and dropped onto the sand as well.

And then, into the separate fields of view of the one man and the one dragon—both Aldebaran—it approached, unhurried.

"—"

Red hair like burning flame, blue eyes that held a clear sky captured within them, clad in spotless white that mirrored his conviction and the knight's path he walked without deviation—a being chosen by the world itself—

"Flam, whom you left behind in the tower, is a bearer of the Divine Protection of Telepathy. She and her sister Grassis can exchange words once a day, no matter the distance or the time."

It was an explanation for how he'd managed to arrive on the scene—a genuinely conscientious response, considerate of the confusion his opponent, thrown into such an unreasonable situation, must be feeling.

"...Thanks for the explanation. You used to getting complaints or something?"

"I wanted to clear up the question on my end first. —There's a great deal I want to ask you."

"—"

"I recommend you surrender. I have no wish to cut you down."

So he said, touching the Dragon Sword at his waist, and the young man—Reinhard van Astrea—declared it outright, standing before Aldebaran and 'Aldebaran' both.

"—"

That commanding, dignified bearing, that valiant stance, a presence still dazzling even set against the night-shrouded world—toward him, who had become a hero because he was always meant to, Aldebaran, as the bearer of a decayed hero's fantasy, had a word or two to say.

The man had gone ahead and said an awful lot, all as he pleased—but—

"Don't drag this out, hero. I'm winning this no matter what. —The stars just weren't on your side."

Beneath a sky full of stars looking down on them, in the great sea of sand, Aldebaran squared off against Reinhard.

—It was a conflict, never to be reconciled, between that which the world loved, and that which it did not.
