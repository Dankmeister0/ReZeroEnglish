Arc 4 Chapter 40: "Collaborator"

The encounter was so sudden, so abrupt, so out of nowhere that a dozen different words flashed through his mind before he could speak—

"Impressive you can look at this eye and call it 'fine'!"

Subaru pointed at his own right eye — the crushed organ — and lobbed that jab, sky-high, at Ram, who had said as much with perfect composure.
Right now, the area around Subaru's right eye was wrapped in a strip of cloth torn from his sleeve, fashioned into a makeshift eyepatch, which lent him a look that thoroughly tickled the chuunibyou part of his soul. If this had been mere fashion — a one-eyed dragon-slaying pose — it would've made for a good laugh later. But for Ram, who surely knew the full extent of Subaru's ruin, that shouldn't have been possible at all.

Taking in Subaru's jab, Ram in front of him tilted her head slightly, and with the motion, her pink hair swayed; she smoothed it down gently with one hand.

"Sorry, but I don't look at you closely enough most days to notice the difference."

"Thanks for that shockingly candid remark, but did you know? Humanoid creatures generally come equipped with two eyes, two ears, and two nostrils."

"So, are you saying the current Barusu isn't a humanoid creature, but some inexplicable life form instead?"

"That's backwards logic!?"

Trading their usual banter in response to Ram's curt reply, Subaru swept his surroundings with his one working eye. He scanned for anyone lying in wait besides Ram, then searched his field of view for an escape route. He needed to buy time and pick his footing before making a move.

"Otto, on three we scatter and run. You're on loud-decoy duty, drawing the pursuers off. I'm on silent-escape duty. Objections?"

"Tons, but before that — why are you radiating this much wariness, Natsuki-san?"

"Idiot, we've been made. Look at Ram's eyes. She means to kill the both of us. No question. I see that look on her all the time back at the mansion — trust me."

"What am I supposed to trust from someone who apparently gets looked at like they're about to be murdered on a daily basis!?"

Subaru tried to whisper his escape plan into shape, but Otto's grasp of the situation was weak enough that the message wasn't landing. For a moment Subaru considered abandoning him and bolting alone, but recalling their earlier bout of friendship made him hesitate.

"Damn it, first you talk me out of my handcuffs, and now you're shackling me with the fetters of friendship instead. What exactly do you want from me...!"

"You're the one who needs to spell out what you want more clearly, Natsuki-san, or this conversation's never going to go anywhere! Also, I have a strong feeling some kind of misunderstanding has taken root here, and I'd like to do something about that too, if I may!"

"Could you two save the comedy routine for later and let me get to the point? Actually — I'm getting to it regardless. I don't have time to indulge this, and I won't. Wasted time is wasted life."

Stepping past the two men busy shoving blame onto each other, Ram cut straight to the matter at hand. That said, Subaru's remarks hadn't been pure joking, either. If anything, stretching the conversation out with quips while he watched for a chance to flee had been dead serious. After all —

"Honestly, I figured you'd see my face and go straight for the kill, no questions asked."

"If this meeting had happened right after you assaulted Roswaal-sama, that might well have been the case. Some time has passed since then, and the heat of my anger has cooled a little. ...Be grateful to Garf."

"Even if it's thanks to Garfiel that I'm not getting my neck wrung right here, that doesn't exactly change my opinion of the person responsible for this face of mine."

Without letting his guard down, Subaru scratched the back of his neck with a finger as he answered Ram. Watching that gesture, Ram — for all her unhurried gait — showed no sign of hostility.
At the very least, going by her word, she didn't seem to intend to strike without warning. But if that was true, the next thing nagging at Subaru was—

"According to Otto, we're supposed to meet up with someone here..."

"Yes, I know."

"Natsuki-san. I know it's hard to believe, but what's in front of you is fact."

His roundabout probing was answered with a nod, and Subaru glanced at Otto, only to get the same confirmation from him. Arms crossed, lips twisted, Subaru let out a low "Hmmm," and said,

"If I just accept what I'm seeing, then you showing up here means you're Otto's collaborator... which means, you're helping smuggle the people of Arlam Village out of the Sanctuary?"

"That's correct. Garf and Ryuzu-sama are both right in the thick of Emilia-sama's Trials right now. All the key figures are gathered at the tomb, so now is the only chance."

Stressing the lack of time, Ram tried to hurry things along, but Subaru's unease refused to fade. He thrust out a palm to stop her, and she silently glared back at him. Bracing himself against the sharpness of that gaze, Subaru prefaced his next words with "Let me hear it," and pressed on.

"Why would you help smuggle the villagers out? Even giving you the benefit of the doubt and assuming it's pure goodwill, dragging me into it makes no sense. Or rather — are you really the type to let me off without making me pay for something, just because your anger's cooled a bit? The questions keep piling up."

"You certainly do pile on the questions. A man of too many words isn't popular, you know, Barusu."

"Coming from someone who's got a soft spot for a man chattier than me, that's not very convincing. ...Answer me, Ram. As things stand, I can't imagine you working alongside me without some grudge left. And yet if you're standing here regardless, that means—"

Subaru caught his breath, dreading the words he himself was about to speak.
If things were exactly as he imagined, then what he was about to do here would be—

He clenched his eyes shut and gritted his teeth to push the weakness away.
Don't be afraid. Don't flinch. Don't show weakness. Steel your heart, and never waver.
—For now, all he had to do was become a single, unshaken piece of steel, untouched by emotion.

"Roswaal's orders — that's the explanation that makes the most sense. Why he'd help me out at all after what I did to him, that violence... you probably know more about that than I do."

"............"

Ram fell silent at Subaru's guess.
What surfaced in Subaru's mind, though, was Roswaal holding the Gospel — and if that book of prophecy had truly been in his hands all along, then Ram, his devoted confidante sworn to absolute loyalty, surely couldn't be ignorant of it.
Of course, she surely didn't know the details. Because if she did know the contents of the Gospel, its specifics, that would mean Ram had accepted a future in which she let Rem die.

"————"

But that was an impossible hypothesis. Subaru knew it was nothing more than that. When Rem was lost, in a situation unlike the strange present one where Ram has forgotten her sister's very existence, Subaru knew exactly how the demon elder sister would react to losing her sibling.
Precisely because of that, he believed she could never have known of a prophecy that might lead to Rem being left to die.

And yet, on the other hand — then how deep did her involvement in Roswaal's scheme run? Roswaal, who still hadn't shown the full shape of his plot. His confidante — just how far did she—

"Answer me, Ram. Why are you helping us? If this is all going exactly to that Roswaal bastard's script, then sorry, but from here on it's just me and Otto. We don't need you."

"Wait, Natsuki-san—!?"

"Quiet, Otto. I don't have time for the fine print, and telling you would put your safety at risk, so I won't. But I am furious over what happened — thoroughly, completely furious. At least furious enough that I can't face anything with the name Roswaal attached to it and stay calm!"

Touching his throbbing right eye over the patch, Subaru barked at Otto, who'd tried to cut in, and stamped the ground.
The sensation of lunging at Roswaal, of throttling that thin neck, still lingered in his palm. The feeling of harboring murderous intent toward another person, of acting on it, of the sensation left behind.
It carried a nauseatingly visceral, raw reality, and now that he'd returned to a calmer state of mind, recalling it brought on nausea, headaches, and ringing ears — a nightmare of a memory.
He never, ever wanted to repeat that scene again. But even so—

"I think what I did was wrong. Whether I regret it is a separate question. He wounded a part of me I can't forgive. So he got hurt for it."

"...By that logic, I don't think you'd have any grounds to object if Ram sought retribution against Barusu, would you?"

"That's exactly why the second I saw you I resolved to turn tail and run, isn't it? Otto here just botched it because he's a slowpoke."

"I'm being needlessly slandered here, but if I actually went all out and turned tail, Natsuki-san, you wouldn't even catch a glimpse of my rear before I vanished."

"Enough with the 'rear' this and 'rear' that. Can you only get laughs with dirty jokes, you word-poor man?"

"I don't understand it, but I feel like I've just been horribly slandered! Horribly!"

Ram cut off Subaru and Otto's routine banter mid-swing with a cough. Then she narrowed her eyes at Subaru again, and receiving that gaze, Ram let out a breath and said,

"...Set your mind at ease. At the very least, Roswaal-sama's will has no part in this action."

"No part... Roswaal doesn't? No, wait, that means..."

At Ram's denial of Roswaal's involvement, Subaru couldn't hide his surprise.
That meant she was helping with this evacuation of her own accord, unilaterally. And that wasn't the only issue at stake.

"If Roswaal has nothing to do with this, then... does that mean it's not written in the Gospel either? Wait, hold on. If we're going down that road, then how far can the Gospel's contents even be trusted, on what level?"

When Roswaal had produced the Gospel during their conversation, Subaru had flown into a rage so instantly that he hadn't been able to press further into the details of the book.
But if the so-called "complete version" of the Gospel truly was an all-seeing prophetic tome depicting the entirety of the future in the truest sense—

"How precise, how enormous would the text written in there have to be...?"

If it were to record everything in the entire world, every possible event that could occur, that volume of information obviously couldn't fit within a single book. The human brain, human comprehension, was far too small a vessel to perceive every single thing that happens in this world.
So Subaru had judged that the information recorded in the Gospel was a curated selection of future events — and moreover, curated within the bounds of what its holder could comprehend.

"The incomplete version of the Gospel that Pete-boy had — apparently its contents kept getting added on, over and over. Though I never actually saw it grow, so I can't say for sure."

That fanatic's Gospel had apparently been a book whose latter half was blank, with the blank pages filling in bit by bit each time a new prophecy was added.
At the very least, there was no mistaking that the page count differed between when Subaru had first gotten his hands on it and when Petelgeuse was driven off. After that, he'd meant to investigate further with Roswaal present, since he couldn't read the characters and the eeriness of the thing outweighed his curiosity — but under the current circumstances, that plan seemed impossible to carry out.
So, even trying to reason further, the sample size was far too small to be anything but idle speculation. Still, the fact remained that Subaru held genuine doubts about just how trustworthy the precision of that so-called "complete version" really was.

Hearing this, Ram raised a hand to her lips, taking on a thoughtful pose.

"...Ram doesn't have the authority to speak on that. Even though my being here now isn't by Roswaal-sama's will, that my heart is devoted to him hasn't changed."

"You're so head-over-heels for him it's almost a meal in itself, damn it."

"However..."

Subaru was about to curse and lament that his path to more information had closed. But Ram slipped in a word before he could. She looked at Subaru, who lifted his head in surprise, and said in a low voice,

"It's certain that this situation isn't in the record. And it's precisely because it isn't in the record that Ram is able to be here like this."

"...I have no idea what that means. In the end, what are you going to do? Are you going to help us? And if you are going to help, is that your own will?"

"I'll help with the evacuation. And that is Ram's own will. As for Roswaal-sama — now that things have come to this, I doubt he'll try to stop it any longer."

"Now that things have come to this...?"

It was a phrase that snagged more than a little, but Ram showed no sign of responding to Subaru's questioning tone. In other words, the answer lay somewhere beyond what she was permitted to speak.
In the end, he hadn't been able to press into the accuracy of the Gospel's record and its actual wording. All he'd gleaned was a suspicion — that even that so-called complete version of the Gospel might not, in fact, see through the entirety of the future.

"Well, if that's all I can get, it might at least give me a bit of an edge next time I press Roswaal for answers."

Setting his lingering doubts aside for the moment, Subaru forced himself to construct words that would let this matter settle here. And he didn't even stop to consider what weight his own remark just now might carry.

"Whether I trust Ram down to the bottom of my heart is something I'll leave open for debate, and set aside for later — for now, I want to confirm the logistics. In the end, where do things stand?"

"Since the day after Barusu went and did something stupid, we've set today as the deadline and been making preparations to evacuate. The villagers refused to move until they could confirm Barusu was safe, so we were forced to waste time searching for a Barusu whose life-or-death status was even in question."

"Sorry for not being clearly dead or alive, or rather — you idiot. Still, from the day after I went on my rampage, huh..."

He felt a faint snag around that timing too, but before he could pin down in words what exactly bothered him, Ram and Otto together gave Subaru a rough rundown of the escape plan. To put it simply, in short—

"The Moderate Faction is opening an escape route through Ram, so we make a full-speed getaway by Dragon Carriage under cover of night. Otto leads the way, and my role is to be the unanimously-agreed-upon mascot so the villagers can flee the Sanctuary without any lingering worries — is that it?"

"I'm not sure what this 'mascot' business even means, but the villagers won't evacuate until they can confirm Natsuki-san is safe. Well, to put it bluntly, I suppose it means Ram-san and I couldn't earn enough trust to serve as evacuation guides."

"So the credit I earned during the Witchbeast incident finally comes in handy. You never know where or how it'll pay off in life — worth going through those life-or-death moments after all."

"As far as I know, you've already gone through plenty of chaotic life-or-death moments with the White Whale and the Witch Cult, Natsuki-san. Are you constantly slipping through near-misses like that?"

Otto muttered, sounding half-exasperated at Subaru's remark. Subaru shook his head repeatedly at that, saying "No, no, no,"

"I just happen to run into a lot of malice from the world that I've got no memory of provoking. For instance, I've also crossed blades — sort of — with an assassin lady who loves nothing more than slicing people open and throwing a giblet party with their guts."

"Sometimes people brag about their painful stories, but coming from someone who's clearly been through that many painful, terrifying situations, it kind of makes the earlier heroics of the other guy sound laughable by comparison."

Subaru gave a thumbs-up, and Otto returned it with a weary, limp smile of his own.
Then, seeing that the conversation had reached a natural pause, Subaru said,

"All right then, shall we go through with it? How much time do you figure we have to spare?"

"If things go as usual, it'll take roughly two hours before Emilia-sama gives up and comes out of the tomb. Since she's only just entered the Trials, let's estimate on the shorter side and call it an hour and a half. Even spending thirty minutes before departure, that still leaves us an hour to spare."

"An hour, huh. That should be enough to at least get past the Sanctuary's barrier."

Once they cleared that barrier, at least no interference from the Sanctuary's side would come. They should also be able to avoid the reach of the Radical Faction, who wanted neither the Sanctuary's liberation nor any improved relations with the villagers. Still, there was one issue regarding the barrier that couldn't be overlooked.
Subaru lifted his head, resting a hand on his chin, and called out to the pink-haired girl, "Ram —"

"There's one problem with the barrier. Even once we're past it, and even if this so-called Radical Faction stops chasing us..."

"Garf, you mean."

"...You already know?"

"He's Frederica's brother, and my acquaintance with him runs far longer than yours. As unwilling as I am to admit it, he's an old friend of mine going back seven years, since Ram was taken in under Roswaal-sama."

A fragment of the information Subaru had gathered back at the mansion last time was casually disclosed just like that.
Even as he felt a bit deflated by that, Subaru felt some inward relief that they'd arrived at the same understanding. Keeping his composure toward the unruffled Ram, he continued with "What do we do,"

"If Garfiel finds out I escaped from where I was being held, he'll definitely come after us. He already suspected my behavior was off, and now there's an escape on top of it. I doubt he'll be in the mood to talk about holding back this time. Whether he crosses the barrier or not comes down entirely to his whim."

"And what grounds do you have for saying Garf will come after us? At the very least, he should respect Ryuzu-sama's will, which places him, in terms of standing, within the Moderate Faction. ...Though since he went and confined Barusu, he's technically unaffiliated with either side at the moment, given how things played out."

"His reason for targeting me... that's—"

The reason Garfiel harbored something close to hatred toward Subaru.
Without a doubt, it was the lingering Witch's Scent given off by Subaru's body.
Catching that scent, which grew stronger with each Return by Death, Garfiel had built up an unspeakable wariness and hostility toward Subaru. And that hostility baring its fangs, translating into action, was the result seen this time. Even so, the fact that he'd treated Subaru's near-fatal wounds and kept him alive hinted at a side of Garfiel that, while impulsive, was still rational.

Once he'd assembled that reasoning in his head, Subaru hesitated over whether to voice it.
It was a bit late to bring up now, but it had been Rem who first pointed out that the Witch's Scent lingered on Subaru. And from fragments of what she'd said, he'd more or less sensed that Rem harbored a deep hatred toward the Witch Cult, and that Ram was connected to it somehow.
In other words, the matter of the Witch Cult wasn't unrelated to Ram either. Just hearing the words "Witch Cult" was enough to make Rem lose her composure. What did this fact mean, for Ram?

"—Barusu?"

"Oh, ah."

"What's wrong, going quiet all of a sudden? Your face already looks vacant enough as it is without you needing to pile on stupidity and incompetence on top — there'd be nothing left worth looking at, in the true sense."

"No mercy, no restraint whatsoever — it's almost refreshing, you. Anyway, as for why Garfiel's targeting me... well, it's because I laid violent hands on Roswaal."

Averting his gaze, Subaru avoided bringing up the matter of the Witch's Scent with Ram. What he said instead was a recollection of the scene where he'd — rather than "gone at it" with Roswaal — one-sidedly attacked him.

"If something happens to Roswaal, it'll come back around and put a burden on the Sanctuary. I doubt he'd want to help someone who nearly caused that kind of disaster escape."

"...Frankly, that's an excuse of the most boring caliber, but I'll allow it. Since Ram is keeping her mouth shut about what she can't say, it would be unfair to forcibly drag out of Barusu what he insolently chooses to believe instead."

"Do you have to needle me in little jabs every single time we talk? Hey, Otto."

"That, Natsuki-san, coming to me for agreement — I find that pretty hard to accept."

Otto shot Subaru a resentful look in response to being asked for agreement. Subaru shrugged at that incomprehensible attitude. And behind the backdrop of the two of them going at it, Ram looked up at the sky, narrowing her eyes slightly in the moonlight, and murmured, in a voice almost too faint to hear,

"—A pale moon, a maddening moon. That night, too, was a moon-lit night like this."

—Once things were set in motion, everything afterward proceeded swiftly.

To begin with, the evacuation plan had been progressing even without Subaru; he'd only been needed as the trigger to kick off the final stage of the plan.
Preparation of each Dragon Carriage, the villagers boarding promptly, the assignment of who rode where, coordination among the drivers — all of it had been arranged so smoothly that what Subaru had expected to take thirty minutes to depart was finished in fifteen, and he couldn't help but let out a sigh of admiration.

"Actually, even fifteen minutes could've been shaved down further. Maybe five..."

Given how switched-on the villagers were, they probably could have cut that time down even further. And the one who'd thrown a wrench into that — of all people — had been Subaru himself.
Reuniting with the villagers of Arlam Village, Subaru had tried to jump right into evacuation preparations, but he'd carried himself with such swagger that his missing right eye was instantly discovered by everyone.
He'd managed to calm the villagers, who were on the verge of raising their voices to demand retribution for whoever had taken Subaru's eye, by lying that the eye wasn't actually destroyed but merely under treatment — and only by getting them to accept that had this evacuation formation finally come together.

"Being fussed over like that wasn't the worst feeling, honestly."

That so many people had been shocked at Subaru being wounded — even having witnessed it firsthand, he still could hardly believe it.
He'd spent so long assuming that a person like Natsuki Subaru was isolated, self-contained, a trifling presence to everyone and anyone, someone who couldn't sway anyone's joy, anger, sorrow, or happiness. And yet when had he come to have people who'd get this angry on his behalf?

He'd boasted earlier about the credit he'd earned during the Witchbeast incident, but what Subaru himself had actually accomplished wasn't that significant an event.
It had been Rem who'd saved the children who wandered into the forest, and it had been Rem, afterward, who'd hunted down most of the Ur Gharuwolves in the Witchbeast forest. The one who'd wiped them out entirely had been Roswaal. There shouldn't have been a single moment where Subaru's contribution had stood out.

"...Ah, so that's how it is."

Having thought that far, Subaru grasped a truth he'd kept sealed away, unexamined, until now.
Rem, whose existence and memory had been devoured from the world by the power of Gluttony. She now remained only inside Subaru; the world retained nothing but an empty shell.
The traces of her involvement — the physical evidence, the memories — were disappearing, vanishing away.

Then what became of the things that had happened because of what she'd done?
—Most likely, everything she had done was being treated as though someone else had shouldered it instead, with the awareness of everyone around adjusted, scene by scene, into whatever form required the least strain to accept.

"The people Rem's actions saved are directing their gratitude for that girl at me instead."

Now that he thought of it, the reason Petra adored Subaru so unconditionally, too, was probably because the world had half-forcibly etched into her the belief that Subaru was the one who'd risked his life to pull her out of the Witchbeast forest.
Otherwise, how could she trust, this thoroughly, some outsider of dubious appearance and origin whom she'd only spent a handful of short days with?

In other words, Subaru — unconsciously, yes, but once again — had been lounging atop Rem's actions, taking it for granted as though it were his due.

"...Even I disgust myself, honestly. The stupidity of it, the sheer scumminess."

Spitting the words out, Subaru came to fully realize that everything blessed about his current circumstances was the achievement of the girl now sleeping within him.
The blue-haired, beloved girl who still left something warm lodged in his chest. Even with the world having forgotten her very existence, was she still devoting herself to him regardless?
Even that, perhaps, was nothing more than a sentimental, self-serving delusion on Subaru's part.

"————"

"Hm, oh, yeah, don't worry about it. I'm fine. ...You too — you're way too good a girl for someone like me, you know that?"

Patrasche glanced back at him worriedly. So as not to disturb the thoughts of the master riding on her back, her movements, even while carrying speed, remained remarkably smooth. Setting aside whether or not the Divine Protection of Windshield was active, it was clear she was running with that same high level of awareness.

Riding solo on Patrasche's back like this, rather than having her pull a carriage, was something he hadn't done in a week — not since the incident with the White Whale and the Witch Cult.
Perhaps there was some pent-up dissatisfaction from being made to pull heavy loads for so long, because with the cargo unloaded and only Subaru on her back, Patrasche's gait seemed, if anything, a touch lighter.
And yet, she too had been the first to worry over Subaru losing an eye, licking his face all over as if to comfort him with that stiff, rough tongue of hers.

Recalling that little rendezvous with Patrasche, Subaru glanced back over his shoulder.
A covert evacuation using six Dragon Carriages. In reality, the creaking of the carriages, the sound of their movement, couldn't be entirely concealed, so this was a situation far from truly covert. Still, they were at least doing what they could — keeping to the lowest possible speed, mindful of avoiding detection.
Most of the drivers handling the Dragon Carriages were people who'd taken on the job of evacuating the villagers from the Witch Cult purely for pay — people who, under normal circumstances, would've had no need to get tangled up in this conflict. Subaru had worried there might be considerable dissatisfaction among them because of that, but seeing them grip their reins with steady focus, that worry seemed to be unfounded.
At the very least, these weren't the kind of people who'd cause trouble out of raw emotion at a moment this critical.
—And then,

"Natsuki-san, Natsuki-san."

Just as he'd thought that far, a call suddenly came from the lead carriage. Turning that way, he found Otto leading the line of carriages, skillfully waving him over while steering his beloved Frufoo.

"What's up. Evacuation's going smooth, I'd say."

"Yes, almost too smoothly, so far. But we've got a problem."

Lowering his voice, Otto leaned toward Subaru, who'd pulled up alongside the carriage. Covering his mouth with a hand so as to keep the other carriages behind them from seeing, he said,

"Natsuki-san, listen carefully."

"Hm?"

"—The forest is stirring. Something 'tremendous' is coming."

It was a vague, overly blurred way of putting it, and Subaru had no reaction available besides furrowing his brow. But Otto's expression as he said it was dead serious, in a way that left no room for laughing it off.
Subaru caught his breath, glancing back and forth between Otto and the carriages behind them.

"This 'tremendous' thing — is it coming soon?"

"The words of the trees are often unclear on the specifics, so I can't say anything for certain, but at our current speed, there's a chance we collide with it. At the very least, if we could pick up the pace a bit more..."

Otto proposed, a bit of cold sweat beading on his forehead. Sensing something certain behind that desperate expression, Subaru made the call to instruct Patrasche to pick up the marching speed. If they could cut through the remaining distance to the barrier at full speed, then whatever came after that—

"—Yo. Takin' a big ol' stroll this late at night, and you didn't even invite me? That's cold."

Just as he was about to shake off his hesitation and break into a run, that voice sliced through the forest's silence and shook his eardrums.
Patrasche's legs stopped as though gouging the earth, and the black Earth Dragon lowered her head, baring her fangs, growling with the same measure of hostility toward the figure standing before them as she had toward the one she'd clashed with before.

Watching the Earth Dragon settle into that battle-ready stance, the blond young man twisted the corner of his mouth in amusement.

"Ha. Not flinchin' even after gettin' hurt that bad — you really are one hell of a woman. 'The brighter it shines, the more the finger shrinks back from it,' ain't that how it goes.'"

Saying that, baring his white canines, the Sanctuary's guardian stood in their path —
flickering the threat of something "tremendous," even now.
