Arc 5 Chapter 17: "A Well-Worn Suit of Armor"

——Though things had settled into some kind of shape, there was no restoring the atmosphere of the banquet room to what it had been.

Having heard Felt's fierce retort, Priscilla, looking thoroughly satisfied, swept out of the inn with Al in tow. From her perspective, she'd accomplished most of what she'd come for and was perfectly content.
Considering the damage Subaru and the others had sustained, it was a self-serving act in the extreme.

In the end, each faction hastily cleared away their own meal, and with no real hope of resuming pleasant conversation, the dinner party simply dissolved. Everyone bore the deep marks of the shock and the wounds they'd taken, and the emotional toll on Reinhard and Wilhelm in particular was more than any bystander could fully grasp.

Even so, it said something about the two men's fortitude that neither let it show on their face.
Of course, the reconciliation between grandfather and grandson that had seemed on the verge of happening had now been postponed—leaving Subaru with a regret that showed no sign of fading.

"It's a real relief Garfiel wasn't there for that."

So said Otto, as he left for the Muse Company after the meal broke up.
He was right, certainly—if Garfiel and the rest of the hot-blooded crowd had been present at that dinner, there was no telling what disaster might have resulted. It wasn't hard to imagine Garfiel lunging at Heinkel and the bloodbath that would have followed.
Only the more level-headed members had remained in the room. ——Though perhaps even that much was simply another card played from the palm of Priscilla's hand.

"...As if she's that omniscient. It was luck. Pure luck."

Priscilla might boast of her strong fortune, but it was undeniable that luck had, in fact, blown in her favor this time.
Frankly, it was a way of thinking equivalent to searching for the least terrible option amid the worst of circumstances—but as a small mercy amid misfortune, Subaru needed to acknowledge at least that much, or else his insides would keep boiling over.

Emilia's and Beatrice's hearts had surely ached no less than Subaru's—likely more.
Even with Felt behaving with restraint, it was Subaru who'd become the most emotional in that room. He felt he owed the two of them an apology, since they'd had to keep watch over both friend and foe alike.

Emilia and the others should have returned to their room by now, taking a short rest before the promised stroll.
Subaru used the free time to try to walk off his restlessness. His feet came down on the wooden floorboards somewhat harder than usual, if he was honest with himself.

The creak he felt beneath his soles seemed to echo the creak in his own heart.
Thinking such thoughts, Subaru stamped his foot again, if only to make himself aware of that inner creaking. And then—

"You oughtn't stomp on the floor like that, Subaru. You'll trouble the innkeepers."

Subaru, who'd been glaring down at his own feet, looked up at the voice that came from beside him.
He found that, without quite noticing, he'd wandered onto the veranda overlooking the garden. There, Julius stood in the garden itself, catching the breeze, watching Subaru make the floor groan.

With one hand resting against his faintly wavy purple hair, catching the cool wind, he looked strangely picturesque.
Subaru, who could hardly help approaching Julius from a place of envy to begin with, clicked his tongue once at the man's handsome bearing before dropping down to sit on the veranda.

"Are you not with Lady Emilia and Lady Beatrice?"

"You can see I'm not, can't you. They're not children either. They're at an age where they want some private time, and I've got just enough delicacy to respect that. I've got a date properly promised with the two of them, anyway."

"There were a few words in there I've never heard before, but I gather the gist. Even you, it seems, have learned to be considerate of others."

"Guh—you—!"

Even though Subaru had been the one to pick the fight, it was he who ended up bristling first at Julius's remark. But his irritation dissolved the moment he saw Julius's expression.
Julius gave a small shake of his head.

"Forgive me. If you were the sort of man incapable of considering others, you wouldn't have raised your voice at the vice-captain the way you did back there. ...If anything, I ought to be grateful."

"There's nothing to be grateful for. I just couldn't stand the guy, so I lost my temper. It was pretty pathetic, honestly, getting worked up like that when everyone else stayed calm."

"That isn't so. Because you showed such rash behavior, the rest of us were, if anything, able to remain composed. Myself included. Your recklessness has its uses, at times."

"You're not actually trying to compliment me here, are you?"

Subaru scowled at the sheer audacity of the remark.
Julius's words always carried a faint, subtle irony directed at Subaru. It wasn't as though Subaru himself was any better about it, so he wasn't exactly in a position to complain—but it was hard to take the compliment at face value all the same.

"I know, I know. Be calmer, more composed—that's supposed to be knightly, right? Even now that I'm technically a knight, I know I'm still lacking in composure. It was written on my report card practically every term back in elementary school."

"That's... true enough, I suppose. If knightliness—and the conduct it demands—is the standard, then your behavior back there was hardly praiseworthy. But—"

In front of a sulking Subaru, Julius suddenly cut himself off.
Then, seeing what Julius did next, Subaru's eyes widened in surprise.

"What are you playing at?"

"Exactly what it looks like."

"If it's exactly what it looks like, then it looks to me like you're bowing your head to me."

Before Subaru, Julius had bent at the waist, his head lowered.
It wasn't a knight's salute. Nor was it the etiquette of a nobleman. What stood there was a plain, unadorned bow that disregarded both formality and rank—behavior utterly unlike Julius.

"My thanks. I thank you. In my stead, for giving voice to righteous anger back there—for that, I thank you."

"...I have no idea what you're talking about."

"If one values knightliness, then one is expected to conduct oneself as a knight regardless of the circumstance. Even if a friend is disparaged, even if he is subjected to a humiliation no human being should have to bear, one must never act on raw emotion. But you did not hold back."

Still bowed, Julius layered his words of gratitude atop Subaru's recklessness.
Subaru could only stand there, bewildered by a reaction he hadn't expected in the least.

"Torn between knight and self, I checked my anger before I even knew it. But seeing you rage the way you did, I felt ashamed of myself. So I wanted to thank you."

"So, what, 'thanks for getting angry in my place'?"

"————"

At the sound of Subaru's voice, one that seemed to have arrived at some understanding, Julius finally raised his head.
Having laid his heart bare in that one statement, his eyes held something bright and unclouded—but seeing it, Subaru twisted his mouth into a deliberate scowl.

"Don't talk nonsense. Quit messing around."

"...Quit messing around, is it."

"Obviously. Why the hell would I have gotten angry in your place? I got angry because I was pissed off. It's not like I was trying to punch that bearded old man on someone else's behalf. You think I've got the finesse to pull off some noble little proxy-rage act like that?"

Subaru genuinely couldn't believe what Julius was getting at.
As for Subaru's own anger, he wasn't confident it was anything so lofty as righteous indignation. What Reinhard and Wilhelm felt was something only the two of them could ever truly understand.

Subaru, standing on the outside, had simply imagined it was something like *this*, and when that imagined thing was defiled, he'd charged in, huffing and puffing. That anger belonged to no one but himself.

"If you were pissed off too, you should've gotten angry yourself. That old man was acting all high and mighty because it was just me alone, but if you'd joined in, he would've folded and run a lot faster."

"He is, nominally, the vice-captain of the Royal Guard. It isn't so easy a thing, to show such disrespect to a man who is, in name at least, my direct superior."

"He's not your direct superior right now, and besides, you just said 'in name at least' yourself. Don't think so narrow. Have you been so wrapped up thinking 'knightly, knightly' that even your heart's put on a suit of armor?"

"————"

At Julius's silence, Subaru propped his chin on his hand atop his knee and let out an exaggerated sigh.
It was a stupid argument. Unable to accept the thanks with any grace, Subaru had rebelled against it and taken it out on Julius instead.
And the thought that it had all started with Heinkel made it that much more infuriating.

"Even my heart's put on armor, is it. ...Ha. That one stings, I'll admit."

"I think it's a pretty cool line myself, but just let it slide. It's nonsense."

"No, I'll take it to heart. To think there are things I can learn from you—it's rather pleasant to consider. A year ago, this would have been unthinkable."

"For the record, I still get nightmares about you sometimes, you know."

Even now, he still dreamed of standing across from Julius in the training yard, getting beaten senseless with a wooden sword.
Remembering the physical pain of it was bad enough, but worse still was how faithfully his memory reproduced every bit of the mental anguish that had come with it. That unwanted vividness of recall had burned the moment into him, and it played out like a film, holding its late-night showing night after night.

Of course, the duel with Julius wasn't the only feature on the program, but as far as things that had carved a scar into Subaru's heart—rivaling several of his own deaths in sheer pain—it ranked among the very worst.

"I'd appreciate it if you'd stop, if you could. Thinking that you and I keep meeting again in your dreams every night isn't the most pleasant notion."

"Take it up with whoever's in charge of my dreams. Given the choice, I'd obviously rather have some giggly, lovey-dovey dream with Emilia-tan."

"That you'd rather rely on dreams than your own efforts there is, I think, very much in keeping with your character."

"Oi, don't just suddenly bounce back and start calling people spineless! What about you, huh? What about you!"

"Lady Anastasia is a captivating woman. To serve so close at her side is an honor beyond compare. I consider my position in life a happy one."

At Julius's utterly unbothered retort, Subaru let out a low, cat-like growl of "fffff—"
Having promptly forgotten all about the humble mood of his bow, Julius had already returned to his usual bearing. Feeling a certain relief at that shift in attitude, and annoyed with himself for feeling it, Subaru scowled, cleared his throat, and dragged out another topic.

"About that guy earlier, the bearded old man... the vice-captain thing—that's for real, right?"

"It's understandable to find it hard to believe. But it is fact. That man is indeed the vice-captain of the Royal Guard of the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica—Heinkel Astrea himself."

"Is it a case of nobody having any judgment, or is there a reason for it? Isn't there a problem with that? Hasn't anybody raised any doubts?"

"There is an answer to every one of those questions. Naturally, there are those—both above him and within the Royal Guard—who question the vice-captain's fitness for the post. In fact, the position itself is essentially ceremonial; I doubt anyone has ever seen the man actually perform its duties."

At Julius's answer, delivered with a shake of his head, Subaru pictured the image of some retired bureaucrat parachuted into a cushy post-retirement job—drawing a fat salary for a title without doing any real work. That was about the closest thing Subaru's mind could conjure, and Heinkel's position wasn't far off from it.

And on top of that, everyone around him understood he was useless, and he still carried on the way he had.

"Don't tell me he's just riding on the fact that he's the Sword Saint's father."

"...That, too, isn't entirely off the mark. To openly denounce the vice-captain would, naturally, reach the ears of his son, Reinhard. Reinhard's impartiality is a matter of public record, but how he would judge things if family were involved is another matter. Not even that much can be trusted with full confidence."

"I don't buy that Reinhard would break his principles for that father of his."

"Even so, he is still his father. Whatever he may look like to outsiders, to Reinhard he remains blood kin all the same. No one can measure what he truly feels."

Julius, ever composed, tried to placate an increasingly heated Subaru.
Subaru clenched his back teeth, a low growl of frustration rising in his throat.
Julius was right. No matter how worthless a man his father might be, whether Reinhard could sever that bond was something only Reinhard's own heart could know.
As a knight who valued impartiality, he shouldn't let that parent-child relationship sway him. And yet, as an individual, Subaru didn't want Reinhard to be so thoroughly resolved that he could cut the tie easily, either.

If even bystanders couldn't settle on what they wanted from Reinhard, who had any right to tell the man himself not to waver? It was presumptuous in the extreme.

"You said 'that, too' a minute ago. Meaning there's more to it than that—other reasons too. What else..."

"That man is the head of House Astrea, and Lord Wilhelm's son. Put plainly, he is the head of the kingdom's foremost house of martial prowess, and son of the previous Sword Saint. Give a man like that no important post, shunt him off into an idle sinecure, and there's no telling if he might come to harbor treasonous feelings toward the state."

Julius's answer came quickly, in a tone deliberately stripped of feeling.
Hearing those words, Subaru thought for a few seconds, and then understanding struck him all at once.

"The kingdom—! Doesn't trust Reinhard! Or Wilhelm-san either! Is that it?! If Heinkel ever showed the slightest sign of turning against the crown, the Sword Saint's whole family might turn hostile...! So they're keeping him close, coddled, like some live bomb they're just letting sit there—is that what this is?!"

If that were true, what an insult it was—to Reinhard, and to Wilhelm.
Having seen the character of those two men, did they truly still believe they might betray the kingdom?

Subaru's fury rose to a pitch that nearly rivaled what he'd shown standing before Heinkel himself. Standing before him, Julius placed a hand on Subaru's shoulder and shook his head.

"Your anger is justified. But the kingdom must prepare for every possibility that could arise."

"What possibility?! There's no way something like that could happen!"

"...Lord Wilhelm was once captain of the Royal Guard."

"—?!"

Subaru, in the middle of trying to shake the hand off his shoulder, froze at those words.

"Fourteen years ago, there was an incident at the royal castle—a member of the royal family was abducted by persons unknown. At the time, Lord Wilhelm was captain of the Royal Guard, and also the man responsible for the search for the missing royal."

"So what. I've at least heard that much of the story."

The abducted royal—whether that royal might be Felt—was the burning question at the center of things right now. Subaru personally found the whole notion hard to credit as real, but he had no idea what Julius meant by digging it back up now.

"I know the missing royal was never found. But so what? Are you saying Wilhelm-san was made to take responsibility for that, and quit the Royal Guard because of it, and that gives him some reason to resent the kingdom? But that's—!"

"The 'Great Conquest'—the campaign to subjugate the White Whale, which included the previous Sword Saint among its ranks. It took place while Lord Wilhelm was away from the royal capital, occupied with the search for the missing royal."

"————"

At the words Julius had just delivered, Subaru's mind went blank.
And into that blankness slid, unbidden, something Wilhelm had once said.

Wilhelm had told him.
That when his own wife had died, he hadn't been able to be at her side.

"...Because of the abducted royal, he wasn't there when his own wife died. Is that what you're saying—that that's why Wilhelm-san resents the royal family, resents the kingdom?"

"I don't know Lord Wilhelm's true feelings. But it is fact that after the search for the royal was called off, and the Great Conquest itself ended in failure, Lord Wilhelm left the Royal Guard. If Captain Marcos hadn't devoted himself afterward to rebuilding it, the Royal Guard would very likely have lost its function entirely."

"I don't care what happened after! I'm talking about Wilhelm-san! What do you think—what do you think about it?! Are you saying Wilhelm-san is the kind of man who'd hold some twisted grudge over his wife against the people around him, and then—and then—!"

To harbor resentment for everything, and perhaps turn against the kingdom itself.
Was that truly how one could see Wilhelm van Astrea—a man who had loved so purely and unwaveringly, who had given up everything for that love? How could anyone look at those eyes, that back, and think such a thing?

Had they never seen the unclouded blade of the Sword Devil?

"How can you not understand that he's not that kind of person?!"

"————"

Shaking off the hand on his shoulder at last, Subaru shoved Julius square in the chest. Julius staggered back a step under the force of it, and Subaru rose to his feet, glaring down at him.
The yellow eyes that met his gaze looked back at Subaru's fury almost with envy.

He knew. He knew perfectly well that this anger was misdirected.
What Julius had told him was, at most, that such a view existed—nothing more. Julius's own feelings on the matter had already been made plain by everything he'd done up to now.

Because it was Julius who, a year ago, had comforted Wilhelm at the end of the battle against the White Whale.
Comforted a man who had spent fourteen long years avenging his wife, and finally seen it through.
There was no way that same man could actually doubt Wilhelm's loyalty to the kingdom.

"...Sorry. I'm an idiot."

"No. You're not wrong. You're right. I'm the one who's wrong. ——The one who thinks the wrong side is right—that's me."

Both dropped their gaze, closing their eyes.
Neither of them could shake the sense of futility that hung between them.

The soil in which doubts about Wilhelm's heart had taken root had been laid down in a way neither of them could undo. Neither Subaru nor Julius could accomplish anything simply by hurling their own feelings at it.

"Is Reinhard the same? Is it like that for him too?"

"...By the same logic, one could say he too has reason to resent the kingdom—for dragging his grandmother into a plan that failed so utterly, for letting the previous Sword Saint die."

"Then—"

"But Reinhard is different. The kingdom does not suspect him of disloyalty. If anything, the one they suspect is Lord Heinkel."

At the mention of Reinhard's father's name, Subaru's eyes went wide for the third time.
Honestly, it wasn't a name he wanted to hear—but if the man was involved, he couldn't very well cover his ears. If anything, the mere mention of that name lent an ugly plausibility to whatever came next.

"What's Reinhard got to do with his father? No—I mean, in terms of blood relation, there's nothing more direct than that, obviously."

"There was a period when Reinhard was, essentially, under Lord Heinkel's thumb. As parent and child, that much is only natural, in a way—but... there was a time it went beyond what was natural."

Averting his eyes from Subaru, Julius spoke with what seemed a note of bitterness.
For Julius to call it excessive—what facts lay behind that word were hard to guess at from here. But Julius, it seemed, had no intention of going into detail, and quickly brought his gaze back.

"With Reinhard's own independence as the turning point, that sort of thing inevitably stopped being visible. Still, without ever knowing the decisive reason why, the suspicion lingers that Reinhard might yet lend an ear to Lord Heinkel's words."

"...So that's why the kingdom treats Heinkel so well—so he won't go giving Reinhard some crazy order?"

"Or something worse still. This is, admittedly, no more than rumor—but I'll tell you, since you're the one who got angry back there, as Reinhard's friend."

With that unsettling preamble, Julius glanced briefly around them, checking that no one was in earshot, before stepping in close beside Subaru.
And then—

"The vice-captain is suspected of involvement in the royal abduction, fourteen years ago."

"—?!"

"There's no proof. But it is fact that he's been under such suspicion, and questioned about it more than once."

"Is it true, though? That he's involved in the kidnapping."

"The truth of the matter is beside the point here. The point is that a man under such suspicion is in a position that might allow him to move the kingdom's greatest military asset. That's what's being treated as a problem."

The glittering honor carried by the name 'Sword Saint.'
And yet, the more its reality came to light, the more it began to seem to Subaru less like an honor than a curse.

"But if he really is involved in the kidnapping, then Heinkel's the reason his own father and mother never got to see him at their deathbeds."

"...It goes further than that, even. At the time, Lady Theresia had already set down her sword and retired from active duty. I've heard it was Lord Heinkel who recommended her as a participant in the Great Conquest."

"He threw his own mother onto the front line against the Witchbeast?!"

"This much is recorded as nearly indisputable fact—that in exchange for declining to take part in the Great Conquest himself, the vice-captain recommended Lady Theresia in his place."

There was nothing to do but fall silent, speechless.
Unlike the vague grounds from before, Julius said there was proof for this one. If it was there in the record, then unless every single person at the time had been deceived, it was fact.

Heinkel had offered up his own mother in his place, to fight the Witchbeast.
And his mother had died in that battle; his father, unable to be there at her deathbed, had taken up the blade of vengeance; and he himself, shielding behind his son's talent, had gone on living out his days in comfort and ease.
It was impossible. There was no way a person capable of such a thing could actually exist.

"It's got to be some kind of mistake..."

He didn't want to believe it.
It wasn't that he wanted to believe in Heinkel's character—anyone who'd exchanged even a few words with the man in person could tell how rotten he was.

But he didn't want to admit that the man could be worse than rotten—vile, monstrous, obscene.
He wanted to believe, needed to believe, that there was some limit past which no ethics, no shred of humanity, could fail to hold a person back.
That such a thing could actually happen in reality—even imagining it felt like a sin.

"...I'm sorry. I shouldn't have laid all this on you without any warning."

At Subaru's speechlessness, unable to find words, Julius murmured, his voice heavy with gloom.
If Subaru, only listening, felt this way, then the man who'd spoken it could hardly have stayed composed either. It was unlike Julius—a man who always made a point of staying rational—to behave this way.

"I—I'm the one who wanted to hear it. It's not your fault. ...Though it'd almost be easier if I could say it was."

"It was hardly commendable conduct on my part. To speak of another house's affairs as though I had witnessed them myself, mixing in hearsay and preconception—it was thoughtless in the extreme. As a knight, it's conduct I should be ashamed of."

"But you have seen it, haven't you? You were Reinhard's friend."

To Julius, voicing his own self-reproach, Subaru offered those words.
Julius raised his head and looked back at him. Subaru gave him a nod.

"I don't know how long you and Reinhard have been friends, but I get that you're worried about him. So I get why you lost your temper too. I'm not gonna say that's weird, or that it's none of your business. I don't think it's right to just quietly stay out of it just because it's someone else's family stuff, either."

"...Is that how you see it?"

"If you hear crying coming from the house next door, you go barging in, don't you? If you see your friend looking down through the window, you call out to him. The fact that you cared about Reinhard—I don't think that's weird. I don't want to think that's weird."

If Julius had been peering in out of some cheap curiosity, Subaru would have looked down on him for it, too.
But in Julius's words, his conduct, and in everything between them up until now, Subaru couldn't find a trace of that kind of ugly sentiment.

"Like I said before—you don't have to keep clinging to some perfectly well-behaved idea of knightliness. Seriously. It's not so bad to shed the armor once in a while and be Yuri instead. Loosening up like that might make things go smoother than you'd think."

Yuri was the false name Julius had used when he'd joined the fight against the Witch Cult.
Unable, given his standing, to join the mercenary company as Julius, he'd taken on that hastily improvised, useless alias, hiding his desperation behind an air of elegance. In the end, no one—not even the man himself—had kept using the name. But back then, Julius hadn't behaved like a proper knight at all.

"Yuri, is it. That's quite the nostalgic name to dredge up."

"It only got used for, like, one moment, not even a whole 'temporary' stretch. I oughta pat myself on the back for even remembering it on the spot."

"Telling me not to be bound by knightliness—you ask difficult things of me, Subaru. It isn't as though I'm unaware of what I'm called, you know."

"That's exactly why—going around all stiff about being 'the most excellent' or whatever—your body and your heart both end up locked up tight. Take the armor off when you're taking a bath, and stretch before you put it back on."

Subaru bent forward right there on the spot, pressing his palms flat against the ground to demonstrate his flexibility. Before he'd learned parkour, his body had been stiff as a board, and flexibility had been the very first thing he'd worked to correct once he'd started training his body in earnest.
And then, in front of Subaru showing off that flexibility—

"If you think you've beaten me with that, I can only lament how shallow your understanding is."

"Whoa?!"

With that, Julius performed a beautiful front split right there in the garden. Stretching his long legs out completely, settling his hips onto the ground with effortless ease—it was flexibility worth marveling at.
Or rather—was he trying to casually outdo Subaru at absolutely everything?

"Ngh... but, but! When it comes to playing the Lyulyre, or sewing on appliqués, there's no way I'm losing that one...!"

"I see little value in winning at those in particular, but I do have some passing skill at playing instruments myself, as it happens. Sewing, admittedly, is beyond me."

"Guh! There it is! 'Passing skill'! Coming from a guy like you, 'passing skill' means basically top-tier! I am never starting a band with you. You'd steal the vocals!"

Running the motion of his stretched legs in reverse, Julius rose smoothly back to his feet. To Subaru's retort, he gave his forelocks a quick, casual brush, then, with a triumphant smile, turned his face up toward the sky.

"I see. So this is what it felt like—the sky Yuri looked up at, the wind Yuri felt."

"Huh?"

"Now that I think of it, even then, I remember the color of the sky seeming somehow different from usual. That's all I meant."

"I have no idea what you're talking about. Don't get all poetic on me, pretty boy."

Pushing at the shoulder of a man still lost in his own mood, Subaru plopped himself back down onto the veranda. Julius gave a wry smile at Subaru's attitude, narrowing his eyes as if dazzled by the sunlight.

The awkward mood left hanging by their conversation was chased off, somehow, by another mood entirely.
Of course, nothing they'd talked about was going to vanish from memory, and there was no denying the knot that remained lodged in both their chests. Still, they could at least manage, together, not to let it drag on forever.

——Seen from a distance, the two of them would have looked, just then, like nothing more than a pair of ordinary friends.
