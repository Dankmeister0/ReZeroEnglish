Arc 4 Chapter 121: "Save Him"

Emilia wiped away her tears and slapped her cheeks.
She smoothed her hair down where it had come mussed, and carefully pressed the wrinkles from her clothes.

Was she making an unsightly face right now?
Normally, Puck would have been there to comment on every last detail of her appearance, but he wasn't. Still, the misshapen crystal stone Garfiel had tossed to her glowed faintly, as if affirming her.

"...I guess I won't get to see his face again for a while."

The crystal stone clutched in her hand drew Emilia's mana up through her fingertips where they touched.
Even simply keeping an unContracted great spirit contained within an Anchor required an enormous amount of mana. Subaru, who had been carrying it, and Garfiel, from whom it had drawn mana without hesitation — if either of them had spent a full day dealing with this drain, they might well have been left utterly parched.

Still, the pace at which it drew mana was negligible compared to the vast reserves Emilia herself held.
Now that she had regained her past memories and remembered that she could use magic without borrowing a spirit's hand, she found that when she consciously turned her attention to the Gates within her body, she could sense an almost absurd circulation of mana.
It had been Emilia, after all — no one else — who had originally manifested Puck's presence in this world. He'd always claimed, half-joking, that he gathered mana from the atmosphere, but in truth he'd surely been drawing the bulk of it from Emilia's own unnoticed stores.

All of it, so that Emilia would never have to face the memories she had unconsciously buried.

"Honestly, you're such an overprotective worrywart."

Smiling faintly, Emilia flicked the tip of the crystal stone lightly with her finger.
As if in protest, or perhaps in wry amusement, the crystal blinked in response.

"...Okay. Right. I'm fine now."

Her feelings had settled quite a bit.
Among the memories she'd regained, thinking of Fortuna and Juice still made her chest ache, and even now, if she let her guard down for a moment, she felt like she might cry.

But she couldn't stay curled up sniffling forever.
There were things Emilia had to do. And surely those were exactly the things Fortuna and Juice had hoped for, had expected of her.

She stepped out of the Trial chamber and made her way down the stone corridor toward the outside.
Two Trials remained. Clearing the first alone hadn't been enough to open the door at the back of the small chamber — that door, presumably, would only open once all three Trials had been overcome.
The conditions for the next Trial to begin weren't clear. Entering and leaving, perhaps, or the passage of time. Either way, while Emilia had been in the small chamber shedding tears in mourning for the past, the next Trial had shown enough sense not to begin thoughtlessly. If re-entry was the trigger, though—

"What if Echidna decides to be mean to me...? I feel like she was really, really angry there at the end."

When told she was hated, Emilia had shot back on reflex that she "didn't dislike her that much."
It had been payback for all the venom Echidna had spat at her throughout, but perhaps she'd shown a little too little restraint toward the Witch who held the reins on how the Trials proceeded.

"Please, don't let her be too angry with me."

Praying that Echidna wasn't so petty, Emilia headed out of the tomb. The end of the corridor announced itself with moonlight spilling into view through the entrance.
The moment she saw it, Emilia set aside all thought of Echidna and lifted her face, her spirits bright and clear.

The past she had recovered had been anything but gentle.
She had no clear sense of it yet, but it was surely something that would shake the very foundations of who Emilia was as a person.
But right now, she wanted to tell the people who had believed in her that she had accomplished the Trial.
To the one who had told her she could do it, she wanted to answer that she had.

And so, narrowing her eyes against the brightness of the moonlight, Emilia passed through the entrance—

"Welcome back, Emilia-sama."

At the place where she emerged, she was greeted by Ram, waiting alone, bowing — and Emilia tilted her head in surprise.

—Time rewinds slightly, to just after Emilia set off to face the Trial.


On the way to the building where Roswaal was convalescing, meaning to confirm his true intentions, Subaru caught his breath and stopped in his tracks.
Garfiel, walking beside him, likewise had his eyes wide with obvious shock. For him, this was surely a bolt from the blue.
Even Subaru, who'd had some inkling of what to expect, couldn't hide his surprise. Sparing a thought for what must be going through Garfiel's mind, Subaru let out a breath toward the figure standing in their path, blocking the way.

"I figured you were probably out there somewhere... but seeing it like this, it still stings a little."

"Well now, isn't that interesting to hear. According to Roz-bou, Su-bou has eyes that see through everything there is to see."

"That's just him overrating me. I have no idea how highly he's decided to value me."

For Roswaal, who knew of Subaru's Return by Death — no, his "do-overs," as he called them — and relied on that power, it must indeed seem like a god's own miraculous power to bend all creation to one's will.
But in reality, this power was nowhere near that omnipotent. It did nothing to help him take back into his own hands the precious things he might lose. It was an inconvenient, limited power like that.

Narrowing her eyes at Subaru's answer, the girl with long, pale pink hair — a figure who was the spitting image of the Ryuzu he'd parted with in front of the tomb, another duplicate bearing the will of Ryuzu Meyer — curved her mouth into a wily, knowing smile.
Subaru, who had anticipated her presence, reacted with little more than a slump of the shoulders, but beside him, Garfiel's reaction was far more dramatic. Eyes bulging, he glared at his grandmother's face.

"Wh—why the hell is there another hag here!? There's only supposed t'be one who can talk, and the rest that look the same as her can't move a muscle without my say-so—"

"There are exceptions to everything. I am... let us say. If the Ryuzu Gar-bou knows, who plays the figurehead of the Sanctuary, is the overseer of this place, then I am the administrator of the Sanctuary's functions. I carry on Ryuzu Meyer's will — the will to keep protecting this place."

"In other words, you're the anti-faction within the system opposed to lifting the Sanctuary's barrier. I thought it was strange that there wasn't an opposition faction within Ryuzu-san egging Garfiel on. And the one secretly taking on that job was... you. — Ryuzu Omega."

Subaru called the Ryuzu who maintained her unreadable composure "Omega."
It wasn't the Ryuzu addressed who reacted to the name, but Garfiel beside her. "Omega?" he repeated, turning back with a suspicious look.

"Boss, the hell's that supposed t'mean?"

"With multiple Ryuzu-sans around, it's hard to keep track without distinguishing them. So for convenience, we've been calling the four Ryuzu-sans we know Alpha, Beta, Sigma, and Theta. And now this fifth Ryuzu-san who's just come to light — she's 'Omega.' Not to your liking?"

"Nah, it's just — that's way too cool a name for a hag... not fair."

"Don't say that. When you multiply, I'll give you an equally cool one."

"Ain't no way in hell I'm multiplyin'..."

Subaru's knack for sharp, provocative wit seemed to strike a chord deep in Garfiel's sensibilities. Omega let out a breath, watching the two of them discover this unexpected point of common ground.

"I don't much care what I'm called, but you're getting a bit too chummy while leaving me out of the conversation, aren't you. Since when did Gar-bou and Su-bou become such good friends?"

"Trade fists under a setting sun, and men are friends for life. Even if it's after getting the stuffing beat out of you four-on-one. Right, Garfiel?"

"Still don't sit right with me, Boss."

Apparently not quite convinced by being beaten through sheer numbers, Garfiel's response came a touch grudgingly. In any case, this was as far as they could stall for time with banter while gathering their thoughts.
Subaru turned back to face Omega, who smoothed her long hair with one hand.

"Your eyes have changed, it seems. A hard boy to satisfy."

"Being called 'boy' is a nice fresh change, actually. So what about you, Omega-san? Different from the other Ryuzu-sans — no shift rotation or anything? Because I really don't need a Gamma, an Ampersand, a Dollar-sign, and a Pound-sign showing up too."

Garfiel's eyes lit up at every symbol Subaru rattled off from memory.
Deliberately ignoring the fourteen-year-old's starry-eyed admiration, Omega placed a hand against her own flat chest.

"Rest easy. I alone bear the role of watcher. In truth and in full, I am the last Ryuzu with a will of her own in this Sanctuary."

"You're not lying to me, right? The guy I know of, the same face shows up in like twenty thousand copies max."

"Surely the scale is far too different. This Sanctuary couldn't conceal a number like that."

Subaru relaxed, apparently relieved that his worst-case imagination hadn't come true.
Then he furrowed his brow, puzzled by Omega's composed demeanor.

"It's nice that you're spilling all your secrets like this, but... what's going on? You've been playing your cards close to your chest this whole time, running circles around all our guesses — why suddenly show yourself now?"

Bold, if you wanted to call it that, but there was no clear reason for the boldness.
To Subaru's question, Omega offered a weary smile. "It's simple, really," she said.

"First, there's the matter of Su-bou and Gar-bou's reconciliation exposing my existence. It wasn't that I was caught red-handed, but the moment I was even suspected of 'maybe' existing, I'd already lost. So I came out quietly like this, prepared to be judged."

"'Judged' — bit dramatic, isn't it. ...But that's not all of it, is it?"

If Omega had truly meant to carry out her role to the fullest, there would still be plenty of moves left to make. Even in a state of "maybe" existing, as long as she wasn't discovered, she'd hold the advantage.

"Guerrilla tactics or whatever else — if you wanted to obstruct us, you could've done just about anything. Isn't that the whole point of Omega-san being the joker in the deck? Roswaal went to such lengths to keep your existence hidden for exactly that reason..."

"That very attitude of Roz-bou's is the other reason I came out so quietly."

"Roswaal's... attitude...?"

Eyes widening, Subaru watched Omega shake her head slowly.
There was something ironic in her manner, and something faintly resigned as well.

"If you'd seen Roz-bou's state right now, holed up in his room, the reason I want to throw in the towel would be plain as day. As administrator of the Sanctuary, I lent my hand because Roz-bou held to a consistent will to see the Sanctuary run properly. ...When that's gone like this, well."

The disappointment in Omega's words struck Subaru, who knew the other Ryuzu, as unusually harsh. Garfiel seemed to feel the same, saying nothing against the severe judgment of Roswaal.
Unlike Alpha and the others, Omega alone had been entrusted with the role of Sanctuary administrator. When she had taken up that role, Subaru didn't know, but her active time was surely far longer than Alpha's and the rest. Her present form might well be something those years had shaped.
In any case—

"Whatever you've done up to now, can we take it you don't mean to get in our way anymore?"

"By the standard of proper administration, there's still plenty I'd like to say. The Sanctuary's liberation runs counter to Ryuzu Meyer's wish, but... this too is the times. If the times no longer need a place like the Sanctuary, then my role too is unneeded. What I've striven for up to now has been something like stubbornness — not wanting to be left behind."

Omega's voice carried a note of loneliness, something like anxiety about her role coming to an end peeking through. For a long, long time she had devoted herself to fulfilling a duty she'd taken upon herself.
Subaru had no way of knowing what feelings filled Omega now as she looked back over her days. A sense of unfinished purpose, some kind of relief — perhaps both, mingled together — the weight of years ill-suited to her youthful appearance made him think so.

"Stubbornness, huh. Ain't nothin' wrong with that. That's somethin' worth having, hag."

It was Garfiel, listening in silence, who broke in on Omega's quiet reminiscence.
Omega looked up at his words, watching Garfiel cross his arms and click his fangs together.

"Same with me. I was holdin' onto the same kinda stubbornness as you, Omega-hag. Nah, worse than yours, prob'ly. But the Boss went and smashed it, by force, with numbers on his side. Honestly pissed me off at first... but now, somethin's cleared up in here."

"Gar-bou..."

"Boss told me — even if the Sanctuary's barrier disappears, it don't mean the world we live in vanishes with it. Once the Sanctuary's gone, everywhere outside becomes the Sanctuary. And there, there's still somethin' for me an' you to do too, Omega-hag."

At Garfiel's answer, Omega looked down, seeming to mull it over.
The anxious color and the anxious furrow of her brow from before had vanished from her expression. In their place was something considering, weighing his words, and the golden-haired boy nodded, satisfied.
Subaru, who'd watched the whole exchange, clapped Garfiel on the shoulder.

"Garfiel, you... you don't have a fever or something, do you? You just said something really smart, and it's kind of embarrassing."

"Boss. I got enough brains in this useless head t'squeeze out a lil' wisdom now an' then, y'know? Besides, eighty percent'a that was straight outta your mouth anyway."

"Seriously? I said that? Oh no, oh no no no, way too embarrassing now that I think about it."

Ears burning, Subaru crouched down, and Garfiel let out an exasperated breath. Then he turned back to Omega, still lost in thought, and waved a hand to clear the way.

"Got your point, hag. Heard what needed hearin', I guess. As for the rest of the scheming — I'll hear it straight from the bastard who cooked it up."

"Do as you like. ...What should I do, I wonder."

Omega murmured, stepping aside to let them pass. At her words, Subaru gave an understanding "ah."
Up to now, Omega had kept her existence hidden. Even Alpha and the others, who hadn't known of her covert dealings, likely hadn't noticed she existed. In other words, only now that her role had ended was Omega finally free to show herself before others.

"If you head to the tomb, Ram and Alpha-san — Theta-san now, I guess — will be waiting there. They've probably both got some inkling of what's going on. Go meet them and talk."

"Those two got a clue about this, seriously? I didn't notice a damn thing."

"That's just your circuits running a lap behind, is all."

It would be strange not to catch on, given the flow of that conversation. Across countless scenes throughout the loop that had started with the Sanctuary, it was easy to imagine how often his own instinctive behavior had gotten in the way.
Either way, Omega's anxiety was surely needless worry. Theta, who knew there were many copies of herself, and Ram, who presumably knew of the duplicates' existence itself, would accept her.
So the remaining problem was—

"A little sit-down with the mastermind behind the scheming, boss clown himself."

When he stepped into the room, Subaru had the momentary illusion that it wasn't the room he knew.

Within the Sanctuary, Roswaal's presence had been in this room from the very start. It was the room Subaru had walked to every time he'd meant to see him face to face. A room whose every last piece of decor he'd surely committed to memory by now.

"Oh-ho, if it isn't Subaru-kun. Wha-a-at brings you here? You must be busy, no?"

To Subaru, standing speechless with a hand still on the door he'd pushed open, Roswaal turned and said this in his usual breezy tone. Shrugging his shoulders in a playful gesture, Subaru had to suppress the momentary urge to pretend nothing was wrong, and faced Roswaal properly.

The room had been thoroughly, savagely wrecked.
Bookshelves overturned, white sheets torn to shreds. The bed had been stomped through at its center, splinters scattered everywhere, and the Warlock standing at the center of the destruction had blood dripping from his hand.
Watching him pull a wood splinter from the wound in his hand, Subaru understood — he'd smashed the bed with brute force.

"Boss..."

Garfiel, seeing the same wreckage, stepped half a pace forward, positioning himself to shield Subaru. It wasn't only Subaru who felt a sense of danger at Roswaal's abnormal state.
Garfiel's golden eyes were plainly wary, his body leaning forward, ready to seize Roswaal instantly should he make any suspicious move.

"Wha-a-at a fine, obedient pet you've become, Garfiel."

Roswaal, noting Garfiel's behavior, said it in a mocking tone. He smiled, twisting his lipstick-lined mouth into a wicked shade of vermilion.
Still smiling, Roswaal closed one eye, reflecting Garfiel in his remaining yellow one.

"I must say, I'm surprised at how quickly you've turned coat. Shielding Subaru-kun like that means you too have joined Subaru-kun's merry band of friends, doesn't it. Casting aside, so-o easily, the love for your mother that bound your very will, for so long, so long."

"Roswaal, that's not it. It's not that Garfiel betrayed his feelings and decided to side with us. He just changed his way of thinking a little—"

"Tha-a-at's exactly what I mean by cheap. Surrounded by a mob, beaten down, lectured a bit, and his whole stance changes. If it's a resolve that wavers just from losing a fight, then your feelings were only ever worth that much. Something so thin, it can be changed that easily."

"Roswaal!"

At Roswaal's stringing together of such cruel words, it was Subaru, not Garfiel, who flared with rage.
Having ended his fierce battle with Garfiel, Subaru had come to know the feelings Garfiel had carried, one edge of the wound in his heart. He knew it was never cheap, never something to be taken lightly.
That was exactly why he couldn't forgive Roswaal for trampling on Garfiel's feelings.

"Take it back! You don't have the right to mock Garfiel's feelings!"

"Calling the soft, soft. The brittle, brittle. Is there some fault in saying so-o? An overreaction only strengthens the truth of my words all the more. A cheap sentiment, trying to affirm itself with a cheap bond — a third-rate play. Truly... unbearable to watch."

"——!"

At Roswaal's derision, Subaru flared with fury and moved to close the distance.
But it was none other than Garfiel who stopped him. Garfiel, who ought to have been the most wounded by Roswaal's insult in this room. Wondering just how deep that wound must run, Subaru glanced at his profile in trepidation.

But—

"Your words are light, Roswaal."

Arms crossed, looking bored, tilting his head slightly, Garfiel said this to Roswaal.
The attitude was a surprise to Subaru — and equally so to Roswaal.
Words that, from the Garfiel of a little while ago, would surely have provoked a furious lunge. He brushed them off now as easily as if he'd been bathed in a lukewarm breeze.

"If you're sayin' I'm half-assed, can't deny it. I was on your side up till this mornin', an' now I'm with the Boss. I'll take the criticism for switchin' sides fast."

"Ideological apostasy, and now shameless defiance, is it? That is quite a departure from the strength you've affirmed all this time. Your ten years — hardly a short span of time — overturned by a mere few days' acquaintance, and you change your creed just like that."

At Garfiel's answer, Roswaal shrugged his shoulders and shook his head.
His mismatched eyes, one on each side, glared at Garfiel, clouded with turbid emotion.

"That's what I call cheap. If it were truly love, feelings would never change their shape. Are your ten years, are Emilia-sama's hundred years, things to be handled so cheaply?"

"——"

"A mere few days is all you've spent with Subaru-kun. How much could have happened in that time? Have you and he built up anything comparable to the feelings you hold for the one you love? There's no way! Nothing piled up beside it, however much, however great, could ever measure up to the feelings held for the one you love! It couldn't possibly compare! That is what it means to love someone above all else!"

Raising his voice, Roswaal kicked a fragment of the bed's wreckage lying on the floor. It went flying toward Subaru and the others, but missed, striking the wall behind them instead.
Listening to the pattering sound of falling wood splinters, Subaru held his breath at Roswaal's assertion.

—Strip away everything except the one, most precious thing.

To Roswaal, that was what it meant, and could only mean, to love something.
Beyond the one thing he'd decided on, he made nothing else precious, needed nothing else. And precisely because of that, his devotion to that single thing was unshakably firm, and he wouldn't even hesitate in seeing it through.
Roswaal believed that this was what "feelings" were.

The instant Subaru understood his philosophy, Roswaal, as if noticing it too, looked at him.
Deep within those yellow eyes swirled an overwhelming heat of love, one that threatened to swallow him whole.

"Do you remember the terms of the wager? The one you proposed yourself. By that wager, binding your own greatest advantage, what can you, as a mere ordinary human, accomplish? Nothing. Because you — you are inferior even to an ordinary human!"

"............"

"The only reason you can be the trump card against all creation is that power, and that power alone. Cast it aside of your own will, become a mere ordinary human, and you won't even manage the ordinary struggles of an ordinary person within your limited time! Not for anyone! If you're going to live the same span of time as everyone else, you can never surpass feelings carved deep by months and years! You mustn't! It cannot be allowed!"

Garfiel's ten years, clinging stubbornly to the Sanctuary, twisting his love for family all the while.
Emilia's hundred years, leaving behind a past she wished she could forget, and the guilt that sprang from it.
And then—

"Ten years, and a hundred years, and then my four hundred years! To have that overturned by none other than you — a mere ordinary human — how could I allow such a thing!"

"Is it because feelings never change?"

"That's right!"

"Because it's a feeling you've held onto for so, so long?"

"Yes, that's right!"

Every one of Subaru's questions, Roswaal affirmed without exception.
That feelings could never be overturned by anyone. That feelings never, ever changed or bent.

Now, at last, Subaru felt he understood. At last, he felt he understood Roswaal.

Roswaal wanted his own feelings affirmed.
He wanted to believe that this was what feelings were, by affirming feelings other than his own.

That was why Roswaal had wanted Garfiel to stay weak.
He'd wanted him to remain a boy clinging desperately to his own feelings, guarding with everything he had the one unchanging thing — that was who he'd wanted Garfiel to stay.

"Why is it, Roswaal?"

A single feeling for the one he loved.
To affirm it, Roswaal clung with particular obsession to the sight of others loving someone too.
Even though Roswaal, more than anyone, should know that someone can love another.

"Why do you only ever see the weakness in loving someone? If you understand that loving someone, always, is a strong feeling, why do you only ever look at the weak part of that idea?"

"—Because I believe in it."

To Subaru's words, Roswaal answered as if wringing the voice from his throat.
His eyes blazed with overwhelming passion, as if glaring at the most hateful thing in this world.

"Because you! Believe in someone's strength, and expect it of them, just like that! I believe that everyone remains weak, always! Weak, fragile, tiny beings who can accomplish nothing with their feelings except cling to the one precious thing they have! That is what I believe!"

"——!"

"For four hundred years, I have never once forgotten a single woman! Far longer than the days we spent together, even through time we could never touch again, her figure remains seared into the depths of my heart, refusing to leave! On that day of parting, my heart shattered to pieces, and I have not changed one bit since!!"

Roswaal stepped forward.
Garfiel blocked him as he moved to close in on Subaru. But Roswaal pressed a hand to Garfiel's chest, looking down at his face, and said—

"It was easy, wasn't it? For ten years, listening to the cry of the 'feeling' within you, holding to it stubbornly, living on and on — you got to immerse yourself in being someone who kept loving another, didn't you?"

"—! You bastard..."

"That's fine, that's how it should be. Everyone ought to be that way. No one, no one can live without loneliness! Living on, always loving someone — that alone should be enough... and yet, why do you try to change your feelings? To betray them? Did you not love her at all!?"

"That's not it! I—!"

"What changed you!? Did the body you kept training lose a fight, and you lost your foothold, is that it!? Your fangs get broken, and that's enough to bend a resolve that was your ten years? Then it was you yourself — no one else — who smeared mud on your ten years, who spat on them!"

At Roswaal's fierce words, Garfiel shook off the hand pressed to his chest. In the same motion he tried to shove Roswaal back, but Roswaal twisted his body to dodge, seizing Garfiel's arm with wide eyes and throwing him.

"Gah—!"

But as he was thrown, Garfiel planted his feet against the ceiling at the peak of the throw, using the added momentum to avoid slamming down on his back. Twisting at the hip to half-rotate his body, he landed on three points, leaving the captured arm behind. In return, he pulled the arm still gripped, dragging Roswaal in close, and drove a headbutt into his torso.

"Guh, uh...!"

"Ha! If I hadn't heard from the Boss that you do body arts too, you'd have got me good with that throw."

Looking down at Roswaal, now on one knee, Garfiel lightly rolled his freed arm.
Then he bared his sharp fangs.

"Roswaal. My dumb head can't make heads or tails of what you're on about. Talkin' 'bout four hundred years — you ain't even thirty yourself, are ya? Even countin' me at 'bout half that."

Reaching out, Garfiel grabbed Roswaal's collar and pulled the clown's face close to his own.
Looking at Roswaal's pained, twisted expression, Garfiel wrinkled his nose and said—

"An' another thing. I ain't with the Boss just 'cause I lost a fight. Losin' did sting, sure. Like you said, my stubbornness took ten years to build up. Ain't soft enough in the head to flip that over just from that."

"Then why is it that you now stand here, before me...?"

"The Boss — well, Ram, really. Told me, after I lost. Told me to go into the tomb an' look at the Trial. So I went an' saw, ten years later, where the feelin' from ten years ago started."

"Wha—!?"

Face still close to Garfiel's, shock ran across Roswaal's expression.

"Impossible... there's no way you could face the past again!"

"Don't matter if there's a way or not. Did it anyway, saw what I saw. An' then, I understood."

"............"

Roswaal glared at Garfiel, who shook his head. His silent stare waited for what Garfiel had understood to be put into words.
But Garfiel only opened his mouth wide at him.

"What I figured out... ain't tellin' you. Too good to waste on you."

"What did you say...!?"

"Just gonna tell you one thing — the deciding reason I'm with the Boss."

Casting Roswaal off, letting him fall on his backside, Garfiel turned toward Subaru. Seeing him flinch, Garfiel let out a small breath.

"Given a choice between someone believin' I should stay weak, weak, just as I am, or someone tellin' me I'm needed 'cause I'm strong — obviously I'm gonna go with the second one."

Delivering this thoroughly obvious conclusion, Garfiel turned his face away from Roswaal. Then, passing by Roswaal, still slumped on the floor, he came to stand beside Subaru, arms crossed.
Subaru glanced at him repeatedly at this behavior.

"Wha."

"...Nah, it's nothing. I'm counting on you."

Saying this to Garfiel, who screwed his eyes shut with a sour look, Subaru then crouched down in front of Roswaal. Chin tucked, looking down, Roswaal wouldn't meet Subaru's eyes.

"Roswaal."

"............"

"Garfiel saw the past. As a result, maybe his stance changed, but it doesn't mean his feelings for his family over those ten years got weaker or wavered. The strength of his feeling didn't change at all, and still he changed. Can't you believe that?"

Even without his stubborn devotion to the Sanctuary, Garfiel's feelings hadn't weakened one bit. Knowing that his one-sided love for his mother had actually gone both ways — Subaru had no way of knowing what a shock that must have been to him.
But looking at Garfiel now, who could call that "weak"? Who could lament that he'd "wavered"?

"You're the same. We're not asking you to twist the feelings you've held onto for someone this whole time. We just want you to change how you show them. In a form that sacrifices nothing else, we'll even help you."

"...That is precisely what I cannot bear. And what does it matter that Garfiel alone has had a change of heart? For you and me, the one who truly matters still remains."

Even with Subaru's hand extended toward him, Roswaal wouldn't take it.
Shaking his head, stubbornly refusing to give up, Roswaal spoke of Emilia, even now facing the Trial.

"Emilia's not going to go the way you think, either. She's going to overcome it."

"There's no way she can. Crushed beneath the regrets she carries, mourning that she ever harbored the ambition to 'change,' sobbing and clinging to you... that would suit her."

"There's no woman a crying face suits, you know that? Besides, have you ever even seen Emilia cry?"

Subaru remembered Emilia before their argument in the tomb.
Emilia, grieving the heavy burden she carried and the loss of her bond with Puck. Tears she couldn't hold back spilling over, and the look on her face as she glared at Subaru.
Remembering it, a fire lit in Subaru's chest.

An unbearable flame of anger burned through his whole body.

"I've never once seen a woman cry that badly at it in my whole life...!"

"Being hurt, being scorned — that is the fate of those half-elves. Sharing an origin with the Witch of Envy is a curse borne from birth. Being reviled as a 'witch' is inevitable."

"Cut the crap. Where's she a witch at all. Your so-called witches — there's no such thing anywhere."

Crouched, Roswaal kept his head down.
Grabbing his collar, Subaru forced his face up, meeting his gaze with eyes narrowed in fury.

Reflected in Roswaal's mismatched eyes was Subaru, burning with unbearable anger at this world.
That's right. Right now, Natsuki Subaru found himself furious at everything in this world.

Where was Emilia a "witch"? There was no such thing as a "witch" anywhere.
If there was one, it was—

"If you're saying that girl is a 'witch,' then it's you — all of you ganging up on her — who's making her into one! Saying it's only natural she's weak, only natural she's scorned, that it's just because of the birth she couldn't help — you're the ones turning her into a 'witch'!"

He remembered the Witches' tea party.
Flashing across his mind — the sight of, in the past, the witches who bore the names of the Deadly Sins.

Minerva, Sekhmet, Typhon, Daphne, Carmilla, Echidna.
And then, the moment that dream-space shattered, the face of Satella, who had sent Subaru off, came back to him.

As if he could forget.
That face — it had been the spitting image of Emilia's.

"Has even one of you ever told her — even once!? That it's all right to cry when things are hard, when things are sad! That if she can't wipe away the tears she's shed, someone by her side will wipe them for her! That there's someone who'll do that for her too! Has anyone ever told her that!?"

However painful an experience, she accepted it as only natural.
There must have been times her chest tightened with suffering, times she felt crushed by grief.
And yet no one had ever let Emilia cry — that must be why she was so bad at it.
Holding back the sound of crying, hiding a crying face, covering up the self that was crying — everyone learns those tricks through crying, and crying, and crying again, over and over.

Because she'd never known that.
Because she'd been left to come this far never knowing it, that girl was bad at crying.

—A world that let that happen, that made that happen — right now, Subaru hated it to the point of madness.

"If it's the natural order of this world that no one takes her side, then I'll change it — my very existence will change it! I'll show you, you who believe a four-hundred-year curse can never change!"

"——"

Before Roswaal, his eyes wide with rage, Subaru thrust his right hand toward the heavens.
By strange coincidence, it was the very same pose Emilia — even now facing her Trial — had shown the Witch who had hurled words of malice at her—

"My name is Natsuki Subaru! Silver-haired half-elf, Emilia's knight!"

Once, Natsuki Subaru had proclaimed this without any real resolve behind it, and been mocked by a crowd for his reckless determination. Looking back at himself then, he still fell short of even that lacking self now.
But there was one thing different from that time.

No matter who laughed at him for it now, Natsuki Subaru would never be ashamed of it again.

"Emilia's coming, Roswaal. The girl you so stubbornly believe is weak — she's coming to cut down your last hope."

"There's no way..."

"And when she's peeled away, one by one, every weakness you're clinging to, and speaks to whatever's left of you at the end... I believe you'll finally lend an ear."

Even hearing Subaru's declaration of resolve, Roswaal's heart did not yield.
Just as he'd said himself a moment ago — what four hundred years had built up was not something that would change with a single word.

Just as Garfiel's ten years, Emilia's hundred years, only moved through the accumulation of both words and action.
So too would Roswaal's four hundred years only be reached when Subaru and the others acted, and strung word upon word.
For now, he wanted to believe in that.

"...No matter who does what, my feelings will not waver."

As Subaru rose, Roswaal crawled past him. Reaching out a trembling hand, he pulled a black book from beside the broken bed and clutched it to his chest.
The true Gospel, with the future written within it.

Garfiel, Emilia — breaking free of the weakness Roswaal believed in.
Ryuzu Omega, Ram — parting ways with Roswaal to press forward on the paths they themselves believed in.

And so the final "hope" left in Roswaal's hands was the Gospel.
Only when its writing was lost could Subaru finally exchange words with the bare, unguarded Roswaal.

"The snow... will fall..."

"Do as you like. I'll relentlessly crush every one of your schemes."

Answering Roswaal, who muttered as if in delirium, Subaru turned toward the door. Tucking his chin at Garfiel's meaningful look, he brought him along and left the room.
At the last, Garfiel looked back at Roswaal, left behind alone in the room, and — whatever he saw in that abandoned figure — muttered, low and quiet,

"Stupid bastard."


※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※


And so, having finished his talk with Roswaal and stepped outside the building, Subaru let out a breath.
Deep, deep, wringing every last bit of air from his lungs, he exhaled completely and turned around.

"Ugh. I was trying to get him to call off the attack on the mansion, and instead I backed him into a corner he can't back down from."

"He was sayin' some nonsense 'bout makin' it snow an' shit. Didn't look like he was in any state t'talk sense anyway... ain't really your fault, is it?"

"No, that was completely unnecessary provocation on my part. I could sort of tell the moment I walked in that Roswaal wasn't quite right in the head, and I go and do that anyway. What was I thinking?"

Holding his head, Subaru replayed the scene, in which the two of them had done little more than throw their arguments at each other.
He felt he'd finally, truly come to understand the real meaning behind Roswaal's convictions. And in response, he felt he'd managed to state his own feelings, his own stance, too.
Throwing Emilia's victory conditions in the face of a Roswaal who kept scorning her — that, he thought, had been necessary to make Roswaal admit defeat.
But still—

"Doing that means one crucial thing slipped through the cracks..."

"Don't go gettin' all down 'bout it. Not like I wasn't wonderin' what you were gonna do while I was listenin' either, but there weren't nothin' wrong with what you said."

"Well, yeah, that was the idea, but..."

"An' besides that... that speech was pretty damn cool!"

Grinning at the somewhat rueful Subaru, Garfiel jabbed a finger sharply toward the sky.
Honestly, given how much flak that pose had earned him ever since coming to this other world, it was nothing but a relief to find someone whose sensibilities lined up with his.
Garfiel was probably trying to comfort Subaru in his own way. Probably. Surely.

"—Natsuki-san! Garfiel!"

And then, a voice called out to the two of them as they talked.
Turning around, Subaru saw a young man raising his hand and running toward them — Otto.
He'd been acting separately from Subaru and the others, and now came to an abrupt stop before the two of them.

"Looks like your talk with the margrave is over. How'd it go?"

"Yeah. Picked a proper fight with him."

"That was supposed to be the point of going there!?"

The main issue had been the final trap Roswaal had set within the Sanctuary, and getting him to reconsider. Omega's existence had come to light on the way to see Roswaal, but Roswaal's head was far too stubborn for a change of mind. So the negotiations had broken down.

"Yo, kid. Don't be too hard on the Boss. That was a damn fine, spirited bit'a smack-talk, for what it's worth. Felt good just listenin' to it."

"Do you two even remember what you went there to do? This isn't going to make a funny story, you know."

Otto's rebuke left Subaru with nothing to say, deep in self-reflection.
But Garfiel roughly clapped Subaru's drooping shoulder, and flicked a finger against Otto's dissatisfied forehead. Looking down at Otto, who yelped "Nga!?" and doubled over,

"Sure, the talk went south. But that was always the backup plan anyway, weren't it. — I'll sort out the problem with Roswaal's mansion, one way or another."

To Otto's silent, teary-eyed reproach, Garfiel opened his mouth wide and laughed.
Baring his sharp fangs, clicking them together loudly, he said—

"Leave it all t'me. — I'm the strongest there is."


※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※


"And that's how it went — the three idiots had a clever Earth Dragon pull their Dragon Carriage and rode off to the mansion."

Finishing her account, Ram pressed a hand to her forehead with a somewhat weary look.
It was rare for Ram to let emotion show on her face in front of Emilia, and it surprised her a little.

"I see. ...Well, can't be helped, I guess."

"...That's all?"

"That's all. Well, um, they didn't wait for me to come out, so there's just a tiny bit... a really, really tiny bit, of a something I feel about that."

After all that back and forth, they'd sent Emilia off to the tomb.
And now the very person who'd done so had gone off somewhere out of earshot without even waiting to see the outcome — what was the meaning of that?

"But I guess that means it never even occurred to them that I might fail."

If Subaru had truly been more worried about Emilia than anything else, he would have stayed here. That he wasn't here meant that someone who needed his help even more than Emilia existed somewhere other than here.

Knowing Natsuki Subaru, one could believe that meant he trusted her.

"Do you really think he loves me? Don't you think so too?"

"Barusu thinks of Emilia-sama more than anyone else in the world."

"...Fufu, thank you."

To Emilia seeking agreement, Ram affirmed it, though with few words. As Emilia pressed a hand to her mouth and smiled, Ram lowered her eyes, seeming to mull something over.
Then, after several seconds of silence, she raised her face.

"Emilia-sama, I apologize."

"What's wrong?"

At Ram's sudden apology, Emilia's eyes went wide.

"It's really, really rare for Ram to apologize."

"Ram thinks so too. ...But this is the first time Ram has bowed her head to Emilia-sama from the heart."

Confessing plainly that all her bows up to now had been mere formality, Ram left Emilia with a wry smile.
Facing that wry smile, Ram looked straight at Emilia with her pale red eyes.

"Ram... did not believe that you would rise, Emilia-sama. With your heart broken by the Trial, even losing the great spirit who was your anchor... and learning that Barusu had hidden his true feelings from you as well — how could you possibly stand after all that?"

"............"

"And yet, no matter how much was piled upon you, you never once broke. When Ram realized you had left your bed and gone to the tomb... Ram finally understood how much she had underestimated you."

Even so, at the point Ram had noticed, Emilia hadn't yet pulled herself back together.
It was only that she hadn't tried to abandon facing the Trial. That much, at least, she could say with certainty had never even crossed her mind.

"So is that why Ram lent a hand to Subaru and Otto-kun?"

"It's only that Ram thought the future that lending a hand led to was worth seeing. If it looked as though Ram lent her hand to those two, that would be wrong. The one Ram lent her hand to was you, Emilia-sama."

"...Maybe that's true."

For Emilia to have stood up there, Subaru's words had been indispensable.
And for Subaru to prove those words true, defeating Garfiel had been necessary. And to defeat Garfiel, Otto and Ram's cooperation had been essential.
So, looking only at that outcome, one could say Ram had contributed for Emilia's sake.

"Why did you do that for me?"

"—Because when making a request, one cannot fail to show sincerity from one's own side first."

"——"

Saying this, Ram quietly knelt before Emilia.
Up until now, whenever Ram had shown even formal respect to Emilia, it had followed the manner of a servant — pinching the edge of her skirt, bowing politely.
But this bow was different. This was the deepest bow of respect, the one everyone living in this world used to show the utmost respect they could offer to another.

"I humbly beg you, Emilia-sama. — Please save my master, Roswaal-sama."

"...Roswaal?"

"That man is possessed by an obsession. A long, long obsession, like a curse binding his heart. And even so, Ram was content. Even if he never looked at Ram, even if he thought of Ram as nothing more than a tool to fulfill that obsession — even so, it was fine."

Still holding her deepest bow, Ram laid bare what was in her heart to Emilia.
It was perhaps the wish she had carried all this time, watching Roswaal from behind an expressionless mask.

"But that obsession can no longer take shape. The world has already diverged from the writing of the Gospel, which was the foundation of everything, and Roswaal-sama clings only to the letters on the page... please, break it for him."

"Is Roswaal going to be all right, if it's broken?"

"He will not be all right. He will surely be thrown into confusion. He may lose the meaning of his own life and collapse. But there is no one else. It is only Emilia-sama who might, walking on beyond a world that has diverged from the Gospel's writing, allow Roswaal-sama's obsession — his feelings — to be fulfilled."

Bowing her head, Ram pleaded with Emilia.
Even half of what she was saying, Emilia couldn't quite grasp clearly.

The Gospel Roswaal held must be the black book he had shown her. That its contents and the world were already diverging was something Roswaal himself had told her.
With the world becoming different from what was written in that book, what would Roswaal do? What did it mean that Emilia could become the hope of a Roswaal left with no other recourse?

"What should I do?"

"—Please, ascend the throne."

"——"

"When you, Emilia-sama, sit upon Lugunica's throne. When that happens, Roswaal-sama's feelings will be fulfilled. Please, teach him — even in a world diverged from the writing — that a day will come when his feelings bear fruit. Please give him a reason to live, today, tomorrow."

To the silent Emilia, Ram kept adding words.
Never before had Emilia seen Ram speak her feelings so freely, so at length.

Was that why?
Was that the reason?

That such an inexpressible emotion welled up in the depths of Emilia's chest.
That she, who had seemed to stand alone, relying on no one, felt a nearly unbearable emotion at being relied upon like this in turn.

Before the speechless Emilia, Ram raised her face.
Her pale red eyes glistened, brimming with a love that filled her small body to overflowing.



"Please, Emilia-sama. — Save him."



At those quiet words, Emilia's whole body trembled, unmistakably.
Once, twice — a sensation like her insides being shaken by a hand, and something like a vibration spreading through the blood coursing through her whole body.

After that trembling had run its course through her entire body, what remained within Emilia was one thing.
A single, burning sense of purpose, setting the depths of her chest ablaze.

"Honestly, I don't understand why my becoming queen would save Roswaal."

"............"

"And I probably still can't really understand Ram's feelings either, not in the true sense."

"............"

"But."

Meeting the silent gaze of Ram's eyes fixed on her, Emilia drew a breath.
The confusion had vanished from her chest. No anxiety remained in her mind either.

Her soul was burning brighter than it ever had before.

"This is the first time Ram has ever asked me for anything."

So,

"All right, Ram. You believed in me — and now, I want to answer that."

It was, surely, a moment when what Emilia ought to do and what she wanted to do aligned so perfectly there was no need to hesitate at all — and so she said it, smiling.

"I bet that's what I have to start doing, from here."
