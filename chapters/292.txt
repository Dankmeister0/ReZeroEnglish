Arc 4 Chapter 123A: "Bowel Hunter VS Sanctuary's Shield"

This time it's a simultaneous update. This is Subaru's side, Route A.












"Alright? There are four people who need rescuing in the mansion. All of them are girls."

Riding atop the running dragon carriage, Subaru held up four fingers as he explained.
The scenery blurred past at high speed, the road rough and broken. And yet the carriage didn't shake, and no wind buffeted them. Some part of his mind noted, as it always did no matter how many times he experienced it, what a strange sensation that was, even as Subaru nodded seriously toward the two watching his raised fingers.

"One is Frederica. Garfiel's big sister, as you well know. If attackers do show up, she's about the only one we can count on to buy us time."

"Sis, huh...? Honestly, it's been ten damn years since I've even seen her face."

Looking uncomfortable, Garfiel scratched at his short blond hair.
Garfiel had stayed behind in the Sanctuary, stubbornly digging in his heels the whole time. It made sense he'd find it awkward to face Frederica, whom he'd decided had abandoned the Sanctuary to go out into the wider world.

"Are you serious, you haven't seen her in ten years? From what the Margrave and Ram-san said, there seemed to be plenty of back-and-forth between the mansion and the Sanctuary."

"Sis had her own reasons to feel awkward about it, I guess. Never once came along with that Roswaal bastard... though seems like she sent letter after letter."

"'Seems like'?"

"Never read 'em. Handed every last one over to the old hag."

Garfiel looked away sullenly. His attitude toward his awkward big sister was, by this point, thoroughly childish. When the two of them finally reunited, it would surely be a touching scene.

Subaru let out a breath, and Otto — their final companion, apparently struck by a similar sentiment — pulled the reins and spoke up.

"So then, the second one is Petra-chan?"

"Right. Roswaal Manor's promising new maid, a little precocious — Petra's the second. This girl's a completely ordinary village girl through and through, no hidden depths, so if she gets targeted, it's a hundred percent bad news."

In fact, as things stood, Petra's death rate in the timeline of the assault on Roswaal Manor was a flat hundred percent.
Of course, the other three's death rates were uniformly high as well, but in Petra's case, lacking any combat ability, she tended to get finished off early in the pattern.
If they meant to protect her, they'd need to find her fast.

"The other one is Rem. Ram's younger sister. Though you probably don't remember her."

"Even now I'm only half convinced, boss. Twin sister who looks just like Ram, you say — but for yours truly, with as long as we've known each other, to just plain forget somethin' like that? That happen?"

"That's exactly the kind of 'curse' that even her own other half — Ram — forgot her too. How to fix that's a whole separate conversation... Anyway, worrying about Rem isn't urgent. She's not on the hit list of the assassin attacking the mansion — Elsa. Probably because Rem's existence wasn't known about when the job was contracted."

"Even so, if Elsa-san finds Rem-san sleeping in the mansion, that won't end well, will it?"

"...No, that's for sure."

That was just how Elsa was.
Even if Rem wasn't part of the job, if Elsa found her, she'd reach out just for fun. In fact, though Subaru hadn't seen it with his own eyes, there had been loops where Rem ended up killed.
All they could do was pray that Rem, who wouldn't be moving on her own, wasn't in whatever room Elsa happened to open.

"Either way, relying on the other side isn't much of a strategy, is it."

"Relying on you guys, and relying on the enemy too. This is the Natsuki Subaru school of strategy — I call it 'Reverse Wind, Forest, Fire, Mountain.'"

"Th-that's badass...!"

Garfiel's eyes lit up as he clenched his fist.
Getting this much enthusiasm for such an off-the-cuff remark stirred an unusual pang of guilt in Subaru. He resolved to properly teach Garfiel the real Wind, Forest, Fire, and Mountain some other time when he had the chance.
With that resolution made, Subaru knit his brows and looked at Garfiel. "But still..."

"You've been kind of terrifying to watch this whole time — is that really effective in that state?"

"Can't be helped, we're in a hurry, right? If yours truly had a better way to do it, he'd rather use that, y'know."

Garfiel responded with a dissatisfied look to Subaru's slightly hesitant words.
What Garfiel said made sense, but Subaru's concern wasn't unreasonable either. After all, right now Garfiel was clinging to the outside of the dragon carriage, talking to Subaru and the others through the window.
Gripping the window frame and hanging on, Garfiel was running alongside the carriage, dragged along as his feet scraped the ground beside the rapidly spinning wheels.

For Subaru, who had once defeated a hated enemy by shoving him into a wheel, the sight — one slip of the hand away from recreating that exact scene — was not something he could watch with a peaceful heart.

"If you turn into confetti from some mix-up here, that's instant PTSD for me, and we'll have no way to handle things at the mansion either."

"Come on, boss. You're awfully worried, ain'tcha. I'm tellin' you, it's fine. Look, look, look! Look, look, look!"

"Stop it! You'll die! I'll die before you do!!"

Using the window frame he gripped as a pivot, Garfiel began spinning around and around with nothing but the strength of his arms. It was an acrobatic feat only possible because they were within the Divine Protection of Wind Evasion, combined with Garfiel's inhumanly powerful grip. Indeed, the window frame Garfiel gripped was warped and creaking from how hard he squeezed it, and Subaru could easily picture Otto, the carriage's owner, later grieving over the damage.

Still,

"The thing about the Divine Protection of the Earth Spirits is, it doesn't kick in unless his feet are on the ground. Since Garfiel needs to arrive at the mansion at full strength, or close to it, I suppose we just have to accept this as a necessary measure."

"I get the logic, but seriously, from the outside this looks like you're driving at full speed to shake off some guy trying to climb aboard the carriage. And what's actually happening is you're throwing a fourteen-year-old boy out of the vehicle and dragging him along the ground at high speed."

"When you put it that way, both how it looks and what it actually is sound completely deranged!!"

As the one holding the reins, Otto naturally wanted to avoid being seen that way from the outside. Though the two Earth Dragons pulling the carriage without mercy, Patrasche and Frufoo, kept right on running, utterly indifferent to their driver's feelings.

The reason Garfiel was running his little acrobatic act was, in short, exactly that.
The bulk of his wounds had already been healed by Emilia's magic back at the Sanctuary, but the mana and blood he'd lost from within his body hadn't recovered.
The distance from the Sanctuary to the mansion was about half a day. Even with Patrasche and the others tearing along at full speed the whole way, there was only so much room to let Subaru and the others' stamina recover.

Garfiel, gathering strength from the earth through the Divine Protection of the Earth Spirits, remained their trump card no matter what. All Subaru and Otto could do was set the stage for him to fight at full power.

"So-o, anyway, the conversation stopped, boss."

"Huh?"

"What we were just talkin' about. Four people who gotta be saved, and we're only at three. Haven't heard the last one's name yet. Who is it, exactly?"

Garfiel lifted his body to peer into the carriage. He turned the same question he'd thrown at Subaru toward Otto, but Otto shook his head and shrugged.

"Unfortunately, I haven't met that last person myself. I was only at the mansion for about a week or so... and in that time, our paths never crossed."

"Someone hatin' you so much they won't even let you see their face, even though you've never met — you sure that's fine, bro?"

"I'd like to believe that's not actually the reason we never met!"

Under Garfiel's look of pity, Otto argued back with a desperate expression.
Watching the two of them go at it noisily, Subaru let out a breath as he pounded the seat with his fist.

"The last one is... Beatrice. Probably, only I can bring her out."

"――――"

At Subaru's words, both Otto and Garfiel fell silent and looked his way.
The seriousness in his voice must have convinced them without needing to hear the reason. What reassuring companions he had.

"I'll bring Beatrice out. I'll bring her out for sure. It has to be me."

No one else — it had to be Subaru who did it.
Even if, on the surface, Beatrice acted like that wasn't what she wanted.

"If boss says so, then it's so, I guess."

"We should probably evacuate the people of nearby Arlam Village too, if we can. To avoid confusion, why don't I handle that smoothly?"

To Subaru's resolve, the two of them each showed their support in their own way.
Subaru would play his part. And they would play theirs.

Honestly, truly — what dependable guys.

"Thanks, you two idiots."

"Can't you say thanks without adding an insult, one idiot!?"

The battle had all at once grown fiercer, scattering destruction throughout the opulent mansion.

"――Ohhhhhh!!"

"Ahhhh!!"

The clash of steel on steel, sparks scattering amid the screech of friction, the shock of deflected slashes and blows — all of it tore apart the moonlit nighttime calm of Roswaal Manor.
Windowpanes burst with a light, tinkling sound; the carpet billowed up under the impact of the shattering floor; a painting on the wall was smashed to pieces with a roar.

"That's it, you're wonderful."

"Don't get much happier hearin' that from anyone but Ram!!"

The arm gripping the shield launched forward, passing beside the dodging woman and burying itself in the wall. Chasing after the woman as she slid sideways, Garfiel spun around with the momentum of his swung right arm and drove his left arm, backhanded, at the woman's shadow.

"Too bad."

"Ain't over yet!"

Faster than the dodging woman could raise her blade, Garfiel — having driven his left arm home — spun again and pulled his right arm from the wall to unleash another blow. The woman broke off her downward slash, using the recoil of raising her arm to somersault backward — Garfiel's strike shattered the empty space her legs had just passed through.

"Raaaaah――!!"

Left backhand.
Right heavy fist.
Left back roundhouse kick.
Right straight thrust.
Left spinning body kick.

Spinning as he went, Garfiel unleashed attack after attack without loosening his pursuit of the retreating woman. Eventually the woman, fully occupied just dodging Garfiel's barrage, noticed her retreating feet had reached the end of the corridor and lifted her face.

"Get crushed already!!"

Stepping forward, Garfiel released both drawn-back arms at once.
The launched fists tore through the air, silver violence reflecting moonlight in the dark corridor, closing on the woman. A beast's mighty arms, more than enough to turn a living human into mincemeat on direct impact. The woman, back against the wall, bent her knees slightly and planted the sole of her right foot against it.
Then, taking aim at the closing fists, she thrust her blade out as if to drive its tip straight into them. Steel screeched again as the blade wedged itself into the gap between the two shields. But—

"You think that trick's gonna work on me――!"

The woman's plan was surely to wedge her blade into the join between the two shields and skewer the charging Garfiel outright. But Garfiel's raw strength was not something a woman's slender arm alone could overcome.
The curved blade, caught vertically at its tip, failed to break through his guard and bent, warping until it snapped.
And just before that—

"Then, shall we layer on one more trick?"

Using the foot braced against the wall as a pivot, the woman's body spun vertically in place.
In that instant, her left leg — rising for the backward rotation — kicked the base of the knife whose tip was caught in the shield, prying the gap between the shields open just slightly wider.
Through that opened gap—

"And this is the real one."

"――!?"

The woman's left hand, now upside down, gripped a different knife from the one that had created the gap in the shield. This was already the third curved blade of its ominous shape — just how many knives did she have hidden on her?

Through the sliver of a gap between shield, shield, and blade, the slim knife slid easily through.
The lethal blade closed in without even a sound of cutting air, aimed precisely at Garfiel's throat. Even in a snap Beastification, that was the most vital of vital points, certain death if struck.
But Garfiel met it with a vicious countermeasure of his own.

"Lovely."

"――Bein' pwaithed don't make me happy none!!"

Right before the woman, murmuring in rapture, Garfiel thrust his face forward.
His sharp canines had clamped down tight, literally stopping the blade in the woman's left hand. Blood dripped from the slightly cut corner of his mouth, and the distinctive iron smell of the knife assaulted his nose.

"Gross!!"

Clenching his jaw, Garfiel bit through the knife in an instant.
Spitting out the shattered fragments, he still drove his toes upward from directly below at the head of the still-upside-down woman. Against the merciless kick that would have burst her skull, the woman instantly offered up her already-drawn-back left arm.

There came a sound like a wet rag slapped against a wall, and bright red blood sprayed across the corridor. Wiping the blood off his face with his sleeve, Garfiel let out a deep breath through his nose and turned around.
Several meters away stood the woman, having escaped the dead end with no way out. Except now her left arm — its bones from wrist to shoulder shattered many times over — hung twisted at a grotesque angle.

"Gettin' away with just one arm, huh, not bad. Ah, damn, my mouth hurts."

"...Fufu, thank you. Ah... it hurts, it hurts. I can really feel that I'm alive."

"The hell? You like gettin' cut just as much as cuttin', huh? Can't understand that at all, me. Well, never had any intention of understandin' you anyway."

Even as she bled steadily, the woman smiled in rapture, and Garfiel, feeling a visceral disgust, clashed the shields on his arms together. Then, noticing something further behind the woman, he spoke up.

"Yo, sis. Still just standin' there frozen, huh? Like you saw, sorry, but I ain't gonna be able to show off my cool side the whole time. Go ahead and do what needs doin', quick."

"...Y-yes, that's right."

Stammering, Frederica somehow managed a nod at Garfiel's words.
To be precise, at that moment Frederica hadn't been silently watching by choice — she simply couldn't move. That was how high a level the battle between Garfiel and the woman was operating at.
At the very least, if Frederica had joined the fight now, she'd have been knocked out within the first few exchanges. Both combatants' skill was that far beyond her.

Frederica glanced at the woman's back, then looked toward her destination — the room where Rem slept. In terms of distance it was only a few meters, yet even though she herself should have been much closer to the room than the woman was, she couldn't picture reaching it before the woman did.
If only she could reach the room, she could carry Rem out through the inner window and escape.

"No need to be so wary, dear sister."

"...Eh?"

"Right now, I'm completely head over heels for your little brother. Whatever room you have business in, whatever you're planning to do there — none of my concern. All my interest, every bit of it, is elsewhere."

"――!"

Without even turning around, the woman guaranteed the safety of Frederica's actions.
There was surely no lie in her words. She didn't seem the type to deceive an opponent that way, nor did she have any need to. More than anything, the sincerity of what she'd just said came through to everyone who heard it.

The woman's attention right now was entirely, fully fixed on Garfiel.
Frederica no longer even registered as a passing thought to her.

And yet, from the woman's very stance, an aura of bloodlust seemed to threaten to swallow the entire mansion — so dense and violent a killing intent that the malevolence she'd shown when they first faced off seemed like child's play by comparison.

"Sis."

"――I believe in you."

To Garfiel's short words, Frederica offered her trust in equally short words.
Frederica pushed through the space filled with the woman's killing aura, feeling a pressure as though swimming through water, and reached her destination, slipping into the room—

"――――"

—but not before glancing once more, one last time, at Garfiel.
Watching her go, Garfiel let out a long breath.

"Lettin' my sis go — that ain't you bein' careless or somethin', is it?"

"Do I look like a fickle woman, able to stay careless in front of such a fine specimen as you? Right now, it's only you. ――Ah, I can't stand it."

A woman's smile, fragrant with alluring charm and thick with the stench of blood and killing intent, all at once — a ghastly, magnificent thing.
Bathed in a gaze so heated it could almost be called passionate, Garfiel spread his legs and lowered his stance.

"Honestly, all it does is give me the creeps. Gonna bite you to pieces and tear you apart."

"And I promise to pull your intestines out without so much as a scratch on them."

Left arm hanging limp, the woman spun the knife in her uninjured right hand.
Then, leaning forward until her chest nearly touched the floor—

"I am the 'Bowel Hunter,' Elsa Granhiert."

"...The strongest shield there is, Garfiel Tinzel."

The instant the names finished being spoken, the woman moved first.

The woman — Elsa's — smile dissolved as if melting into the darkness, and she launched into a run at godlike speed, showing no trace of her injuries. No sooner had the first sound of her foot striking the floor rung out than sounds like walls being punched through followed in rapid succession, from above, below, left, and right.

Kicking off the floor, off the wall, off the ceiling, Elsa's figure closed in on Garfiel through repeated leaps. A speed that refused to let him fix his aim, movements unlike any creature Garfiel had ever faced before. No being that moved with such nightmarish motion could exist in human or beast form.
Most astonishing of all was that her speed was clearly faster now than before she'd been injured.

"Interesting――!!"

Baring his fangs in a grin at the leaping shadow, Garfiel took action.
If his opponent was going to come at him with tricky movement, then Garfiel's counter would match it. Against a foe repeating leap after leap, Garfiel, all four limbs on the floor, detonated off his hind legs.

Down the mansion's straight corridor, Garfiel tore through like a human-sized mass of shot.
Shield held forward, the shockwave of his tiger-like charging force blew apart the shattered window glass and wall debris all at once.

"Gaaaaah!!"

Without even watching the result, Garfiel let out a battle cry and jammed his arm into the floor to brake forcibly. Instantly reversing his stance, he took the beast posture again, and his hind legs shattered the floor.

The explosive sound shook the mansion, and the carpet caught up in the destruction scattered and billowed into the air. Garfiel flew with a scrap of red cloth caught on his body — the instant—

"――――!!"

"Ahahahaha!!"

Elsa's blade, diving down off the ceiling as a foothold, met Garfiel's shield flying straight down the corridor. The impact tore through the air, and the violent sound pierced eardrums, sharply shaking the moonlit corridor.

Elsa, laughing as she was knocked back, spun sideways at high speed. Garfiel, his course bent by the force of the slash, plowed head-first into the wall beside him, punching through the stone wall and tumbling into a guest room.

"Bah!"

Trailing white smoke, the tumbling Garfiel instantly grabbed the leg of a nearby bed. His arm muscles swelled, and he lifted the nearly hundred-kilogram bed with ease, hurling it back through the hole he'd just smashed his way in through. A roaring crash, a clean split — from the far side of the bed, now cut clean in two, the black-clad woman threw a silver blade.
Garfiel deflected it with his left shield, and moved in with his right shield to try to crush Elsa's face as she closed in. But it only grazed the braid she'd been too slow to pull back as she ducked. The instant the tip of her black hair tickled the end of his nose, following the chill running up his spine, Garfiel threw himself forward with no regard for form. He barely avoided a slash sweeping up from below his crotch, his back grazed by the arcing blade, and burst through the door, moving the battlefield back to the corridor.

With no time to catch his breath, Elsa leapt out after Garfiel in pursuit. Garfiel unleashed a kick aimed at her slender waist. A direct hit — no sensation of impact. The woman twisted her body with abnormal flexibility, redirecting the force of the kick along a path that merely brushed the surface of her stomach, evading it. In turn, with Garfiel's leg extended and his movement momentarily stopped, the tip of her curved blade closed in, slicing through the air with a shriek.
Unlike the earlier slash launched from an awkward stance, this one, if he tried to stop it by biting down, had the speed and force to split his head in two instead. Garfiel's judgment was instantaneous — he slid his right shield into the blade's path to deflect it, and as it flowed off, his left shield caught it and redirected it further.
A grinding screech. Red and yellow sparks scattering. Black eyes widening in astonishment, and the woman's torso left open. Garfiel let out a battle cry, slammed his kicking leg into the floor, set his stance, and sank his fangs into Elsa's side, meaning to live up to his outrageous epithet and spill her guts out.

"――――!"

It was instinct alone that made him cut short that bite and, using the same momentum, snap his head back down.

Too slow to fully dodge, Garfiel's left ear was blown off, and he rolled through a mist of blood in evasive action. Planting a foot against the wall, he escaped the pursuing slash by fleeing up to the ceiling. Dodge, dodge, dodge completely.
Driving his arm into the ceiling, he cracked it open, collapsing part of the upper floor to create a gap in Elsa's pursuit, using the moment to disengage. Hands and feet planted on the carpet, Garfiel pressed his palm against the bleeding upper half of his now-vanished left ear.

Breathing hard, Garfiel gritted his teeth against the burning pain. Then, cutting through the billowing smoke, he watched Elsa step out and raised the corner of his mouth.

"You... I made damn sure that left arm of yours was useless."

"That's true. It hurt. But wounds on a person's body heal, don't they."

"Not tryin' to be rude about my own narrow-minded common sense, but a mangled arm healin' in two minutes ain't somethin' that happens to a human body."

More than that — it went beyond the bounds of any living creature.
Even with Garfiel's Divine Protection of the Earth Spirits, making a shattered arm usable again required hours of rest. That was choosing land rich in mana and lazing about at full leisure to achieve it.
In the middle of a battle, and in this short a time no less, a wound fully healed like that was unthinkable.
He'd heard beforehand from Subaru that she "wouldn't die even if killed," but now the inference Garfiel had drawn from that at the time was starting to feel real.

"If that's the case, then this is simple. You ain't human. Don't know what you were born as, but at the very least, that much I'll say for sure."

"You're sharper than you look."

"Don't get much happier hearin' that from anyone but Ram, I said. And besides, I got an idea about that freakish healing constitution of yours."

Pointing a finger, Garfiel voiced his own theory.
Garfiel was, surprising as it might sound, someone who liked books. That was because, in the tedious Sanctuary, with no one around who could match him in strength, reading had been an important way to pass the time.
That said, what Garfiel favored were adventure novels, myths, and legends — unfortunately, his interest never extended toward accumulating actual knowledge.

"In the books I read, they were overflowin' with monsters and heroes you couldn't even tell were real or not. And among 'em, there was one with the same kind of body as you."

"...I'd rather not be lumped in with the delusions found in picture books, if it's all the same to you."

"Ain't a picture book, it's a proper book, full of words. ...Though it does have a few pictures here and there, but whatever. And this ain't somethin' I can just call a delusion, either."

Elsa listened to Garfiel's words with a composed expression.
The way she stuck around to hear him out, at least until he finished talking, made her an odd match for the murderous look she wore in battle.
He'd change that expression on her face now.

"See, way back, there was apparently a witch who had someone with the exact same kind of body."

"――――"

The motion of the blade tip, swaying gently side to side, came to a stop.
Elsa's black eyes looked at Garfiel with an idle motion. Pointing a finger at her, visible to those eyes—

"That body — that's what they call a 'Vampire,' ain't it!"

"I don't drink blood or anything, though."

Hearing him finish, Elsa's body, breathing out, kicked off the floor.
With both her left and right arms — the left now completely healed — she raised curved blades in both hands and closed in on Garfiel. He caught the diagonal slash with both raised shields, simultaneously thrusting his right leg up in a straight kick — Elsa launched a kick along the same trajectory, soles of their shoes colliding and sending them both flying backward.

"Don't like this! Your left hand's completely back to normal too!?"

"And your ear's finished healing too, while you were busy buying time with all that talking, hasn't it? So we're even."

*Caught me*, Garfiel silently clicked his tongue.
While he'd kept talking, he'd used his left hand — which had been pressed against the wound — to cast healing magic, and the injury to his left ear was already treated. As for the missing part, he'd just have to hope it grew back gradually, but even so, taking as much damage as Elsa had, Garfiel's healing magic could only manage first aid.

"You not denyin' it — that mean Vampire's a hit?"

"What people around me choose to call it is their own business. I don't drink blood, and I eat normally too. Even out under the sun, all that happens is the guards make a fuss — nothing more to it, really."

"Then that bowel-huntin', bowel-huntin' talk is 'cause you're a vampire?"

"That's just my personal taste. I simply like the sight of vividly colored intestines, and touching warm innards."

"That's way scarier, that."

In front of Garfiel, Elsa cast off her cumbersome black cloak.
Seeing that, judging that it only meant Elsa's motivation had risen further, Garfiel clacked his fangs together and clashed the shields on both arms.

"World's a big place... it's gonna be a bit rough, but do it well, boss."

Saying that, as Elsa charged at him once more, Garfiel too let out a roar and swung his shields down to meet her.

――When the door opened, the smell of a huge quantity of paper drifted out from within the room.

That choking, overwhelming smell — was it the weight of the days and months spent in that place over such a long time? Or, if one followed the room's nickname, "the room where time stopped," did the passage of months and years have nothing to do with it at all?

"On that note, it just so happens I had a lot to think about back in the Sanctuary. I'd like to hear your answer on that too, if you don't mind."

"――Why."

Without waiting for the room's mistress to grant permission, Subaru strode brazenly into the library.
The air was, as ever, a mixture of stillness and gloom. No window let in sunlight, not even a small vent for airing the room out. Stay too long, and it was certain to turn your stomach and weigh on your spirits.

All the more so given the face of the girl looking up at Subaru as if to greet him — utterly worn out.

"Why is it, again, that you managed to reach this room? I don't recall calling for you."

"Sorry, but showing up without being called is just the kind of man I am. Back in middle school, there was a time I showed up to a friend's birthday party I wasn't even invited to and made the whole atmosphere awkward — I still can't forget it."

Even Subaru, hopeless as he was at reading the room, had thought to himself that he'd hold back from then on, after that incident.
Though, having decided "well, today's fine!" anyway, and gone home having made more noise than anyone else there, the result was that he stopped getting invited to anyone's birthday at all after that.

"My chest's about to burst with how bittersweet that is, so let's leave that topic be."

"Leave it be — as if you didn't go and bring it up yourself. Honestly, you do whatever you please, no matter what, I suppose."

"Yeah, I do what I please. That's why, no matter how much you hate it, I'm coming here."

Right in front of him, he could tell the girl had caught her breath.
As if to make sure it reflected in those eyes, Subaru bowed his head respectfully right there, and then—

"I'm taking you out of here, Beatrice. ――This time for sure, I'm going to drag you out under the sun with my own hands, and we're going to play until that dress of yours is caked in mud and you're black from head to toe."

Hearing Subaru's declaration, the girl — Beatrice — hugged herself where she sat, as always, atop her stepladder.

In her arms, still clutching the black-bound book, her trembling eyes stared fixedly at Subaru.
