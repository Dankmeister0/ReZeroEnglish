It had the roles turned completely upside down. To an outside eye, it would have looked like a scene that made no sense at all — but it was, without a doubt, a sight that was distinctly Subaru.

"—Ah, geez, what are we gonna do with you, Subaru-sama."

Subaru heard someone say that, as if the words had burst out of them. That set it off, and the urge to laugh spread through the crowd around him just like that, and—

"Well, if you're going to try that hard just to say you'll protect us, what choice do we have."
"Oh, honestly. I don't know why, but my chest's gone warm for the first time in a good while."
"Subaru-sama really is a hopeless one, isn't he."

Timidly, Subaru raised his face. The dazzling morning sunlight burned into his eyes from beyond the ring of people, and for just an instant his vision washed white. Shading it with his hand, he turned his eyes to the world that opened up before him, and—

"...Thanks, everyone."

"—That's our line, Subaru-sama."

Surrounded by troubled, smiling faces, Subaru laughed with an expression on the verge of tears.

※※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※

—And so, the persuasion of the villagers reached an amicable resolution, through the strangest of conversational turns in all the world: the ones meant to protect, bowing their heads to the ones being protected.

They gave the merchants the same explanation, with the "bandits" scenario, and directed everyone to split into two evacuation groups, one bound for the Sanctuary and one for the capital. With Dragon Carriages and villagers assigned and everyone about ready to board and set off—

"There's just one favor I'd like to ask."

—So Subaru raised his hand and drew the villagers' attention. Even with the earlier discussion having wrapped up on relatively good terms, receiving their eyes on him again made Subaru hesitate to voice his next proposal. Honestly, it was something he himself wasn't entirely sure was even a necessary request. Still, there were two reasons he wanted to see this proposal through. They were—

"Emilia, at the mansion... I'd like someone to ride along with the Half-Elf girl."

At Subaru's proposal, the bustle that had filled the air a moment ago fell silent in an instant. As an awkward hush began to settle over the village once more, Subaru struggled not to let a feeling of disappointment — a resigned *I knew it* — show on his face, and said:

"A proclamation went out in the capital, so I think you've all already heard about the Royal Selection. That our lord, Roswaal... sama, is a supporter of one of the candidates, and that the candidate he's supporting is that Half-Elf girl."

—

"I know everyone's uneasy about it. I understand there's plenty that's hard to swallow, too. But even so, I want to ask this of you. Don't let her ride in the Dragon Carriage all alone."

He wasn't confident his voice hadn't carried a trace of anxiety or grief. Hearing Subaru's words, the villagers exchanged glances and wore thoughtful expressions. Still, that they were even considering it at all felt, in itself, like something different from before. Even so, sensing the atmosphere wasn't one where he could expect a positive answer, Subaru was quick to raise his hand to cut his losses with a "sorry about that," when—

"—Petra?"

Before Subaru's raised hand could stop things, a small palm had already gone up. Startled, Subaru looked at the owner of that hand, and she puffed out her red cheeks, looked around at the surrounding adults, and said:

"Why won't anyone listen to him? Subaru's so troubled, asking with a face like he's about to cry, and you won't even try to help him?"

It was a terribly honest, unadorned thing to say — and precisely for that reason, it cut straight to the heart. Free of every entanglement the adults carried, it was the single strongest blow in the world, one only a child was permitted to deliver. The adults grimaced with guilt even greater than before and searched for words to soothe the girl, but—

"The big sister from the mansion — that's the big sister who's always wearing white clothes, right? She's the one who brings the stamp during radiooo caaalisthenics."

"Huh, ah...! Yeah, that's right. The stamp big sister."

Looking back, remembering those peaceful days, Subaru's mouth curved into an unbidden smile. Back when everything had been tranquil, when he could simply enjoy the ordinary days. Every morning, he'd bring Emilia down to the village, do radio calisthenics with the villagers, and stamp everyone's cards with the potato-carved seal he'd made — that had been his daily routine. Yes, memories of happy days — and beside him in every one of those days, she'd been there too.

Perhaps some fitting image had surfaced from a corner of their memory, because the adults too began to catch the meaning behind Petra's words. And in the gap left by that hesitation stopping their mouths, the children around Petra began raising their hands one after another, saying:

"Me tooo! I'm riding with her tooo!" "If Petra's riding, it can't start without me, right?" "No way I'm letting my big brother be the only one who looks good — I'm going with Petra too!" "Subaru looks like he's gonna cry, so let's help him out!" "Yeaaah, let's do thaaat!"

The children stirred and clamored all at once, and at the center of it all, Petra smiled proudly. At an expression far too cute to really call smug, Subaru placed a hand over his chest, feeling as though he'd been saved.

"Can I ask this of you? That girl acts tough and stubborn and pig-headed, but she's lonely underneath, and I worry if someone's not there watching over her."

"Well, I guess it can't be helped — I'll watch over her in Subaru's place."

"Whew" — a breath of relief slipped from Subaru's mouth. Then Subaru walked over to Petra, who'd made him that promise, and held out his pinky finger in front of her. Smiling at the bewildered-looking girl, he said:

"Like this — you hold out your pinky, and we hook them together. Then you sing 'pinky swear, cross my heart' while you cut it off — that's how they seal a promise back where I'm from, nice and now. Think you can do it?"

"I-I'll do it!"

The reply came back with far more spirit than he'd expected, and Subaru laughed, "That's the spirit," and hooked his finger together with the girl's. Then he slowly taught the still-flustered Petra the little rhyme:

"Pinky swear, cross my heart—"
"—if I lie, I'll swallow a thousand needlesss—"

"—Pinky's cut!"

Their fingers pulled apart, and a promise — a compact — was sealed. Petra swore to Subaru that she'd never leave Emilia alone, and Subaru swore that he would, without fail, protect the safety of her and the village.

—This promise, this time, no matter what, he would never break.

"This time, let's go without a single regret trailing behind us. Let's do this, everyone!"

Thrusting his fist into the air, Subaru raised his voice, and everyone there thrust their fists up too. Just as it seemed like a rousing cheer was about to burst out at full volume—

"—Ah! This is supposed to be a covert operation, everyone quiet down, quiet down!"

—and so everyone found themselves scrambling to clamp their mouths shut, which rather undercut the moment.

※※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※

—As an aside, though.

"Ehehe..."

Subaru, who'd called out the order, was stroking the girl's head with his free hand, the one not clasped in the pinky swear. That the girl being stroked had her cheeks flushed red, and was gazing up at Subaru with intensely heated eyes — no one noticed, save for the cat-eared knight and the pink-haired maid.
