Arc 5 Chapter 59: "Regulus Corneas"

*Impossible, impossible, impossible. What is going on, none of this makes any sense. Why do I have to go through something like this? Who do you think I am? I am the Sin Archbishop of "Greed," Regulus Corneas. The most fulfilled being in this world! The most fully established as an individual! A being with nothing to shake it, body or soul! That's what I'm supposed to be, so why do I have to go through something like this?! Don't screw with me. This isn't funny. Why does every single one of them just accept this incomprehensible absurdity like it's a matter of course? Something's wrong with them. That man, that woman, that knight — they got carried away just because I showed them a little mercy. If I'd gone all out from the start, they'd have been smashed to pieces, blown apart — do they seriously mistake that for their own strength? That's exactly why I hate dealing with other people — because they can make such a laughable mistake without a shred of shame! Bothersome, irritating, infuriating, aggravating, hateful, repulsive, contemptible, sluggish idiots, every last one of them. I've done well, all this time, always. For years, for decades, for over a hundred years, I've kept at it like this, more faithful to being a Sin Archbishop than anyone. The first time I was chosen by a Witch Factor and got hold of this Authority, I slaughtered every one of them — the father who barely earned a living but was always drunk, the mother who did nothing but whine and complain day after day, the greedy siblings who kept their beady eyes fixed even on my share. I tore apart the villagers who looked at me like I was something to sneer at, the townsfolk who'd shoved me into that hopeless village and house in the first place, and the incompetents running the country who'd left that whole town and village to rot without lifting a finger. I destroyed all of it, got rid of every last bit of it, and only then did I finally realize how I, truly I, was meant to live! I don't need anything. Everything is just a bother. I'm fulfilled. It's not that I didn't have anything — I didn't need it. You presumptuous trash, I never needed a single thing. And yet, if something gets given to me anyway, doesn't that mean I'm being told, without words, that from the outside, from your point of view, I look lacking, insufficient, pitiful — something to be pitied? Wipe out every single one of the people who push things I never wanted onto me — the only people who should exist in this world are the ones who say nothing to me, fulfilled as I am. Every single one of you keeps saying whatever you please. Damn you all. No one has the right to pity me. No one has the right to make me despair at being pitied. I will never let anyone do that to me. I don't need anything, I don't want anything. A father who barely earned a living, always drunk, but who sometimes brought home a little souvenir — screw him, die. A mother who did nothing but complain day after day, but who kept saying obvious things like "sorry for making things hard on you" — screw her, die. Greedy siblings who kept their beady eyes fixed even on my share, but who'd split their own portion with me when I knocked my plate over — screw them, die. Stop it, you bastards, being kind to me without permission. Being kind to someone means looking down on them, doesn't it, means thinking less of them, doesn't it? Anyone who looks down on other people is trash, and people who look down on their own family, not even strangers, deserve to be despised as less than human. They deserve to die. I'm not wrong. I'm not wrong at all. You're the ones who are wrong. You, you, you're the ones who pity me, feel sorry for me, leave me all alone. You should get a taste of what it feels like to be made to think you're the most hopeless being in the world. The only ones who should be near me are the ones who don't pity me. Every single reason for anyone to pity me should just disappear from this world. I hear laughter. They're looking at me, aren't they. They laughed at the sight of me, didn't they. What's so funny about me. What did they see that made them laugh. Every single one of them, smirking away, trash with no power at all who are only good with their mouths. Why do I have to let trash like that shatter my heart like this. Don't stand in front of me, don't get in my way, don't pity me — the pitiful ones aren't me, they're you — powerless, ignorant, and yet "Greedy" all the same! You're the ones who have to crawl through your whole lives trying to fill up what's missing in yourselves — you're the greedy ones who deserve pity! I'm different, I'm not like that, I don't want anything at all. I who want nothing am superior to you who are lacking. Don't pity me. You're actually envious of me, jealous of me, you admire me, but since you can't reach what I have you just talk yourselves out of it as sour grapes. That's right, isn't it, that has to be it, it must be. Wait. Wait, wait a second. Stop it. Don't look at me, don't say my name, don't talk about me. Whether it's good or bad, stop it, don't pay attention to me. Just leave me alone. If I could just be complete in myself, my heart would never be trampled on — so why do you people keep trying to reach out and touch it. We'll never understand each other. You and I are different people. Paying a risk to go after some return makes no sense however you look at it, it's not rational, it's wrong. You're all out of your minds. If you just calmed down and thought about it clearly you'd see it. Every single person besides me is simply feverish, out of their heads. You should be able to see that wanting other people is nothing but useless, futile, meaningless. All those words you keep repeating like a broken record — love, romance, friendship, trust — they're all illusions. The act of reproduction itself is the single most disgusting thing there is. It makes no sense. What's the point of it. Spouse, children, dress it up with the word "family" all you like — they're still beings separate from myself, so what difference does it make to me whether such things live or die? Even if they go on living, if I die, I'm finished. Even if they die, as long as I'm alive, I simply go on. Love and romance can't make two people into one. A person was only ever one to begin with. Out of consideration for people who cling gratefully to that illusion, I gathered spouses. Since it'd be idiotic to be despised by others, I collected women with pleasing looks. Since nothing is more foolish than being betrayed by a chosen partner, I made sure every one of them was a virgin. What more do you people want from me? Don't spout such selfish nonsense. After violating me this much, what more do you want from me? After all this! After making me bend my own thinking this much! And you still keep pressing demands on me? How much more do I have to do before I'm free of your pity? "The most pitiful person in the world," you say! I have no reason to be told that by some vulgar whore ruled by her own "Greed" to be joined with the man she loves!*

"Raaah!!"

Up, up, wrapped in a raging gale, Regulus's body was sent hurtling into the night sky.

The instant the impact struck from below, between his legs, Regulus activated "Lion's Heart," stopping the beat of his own heart and entering his invincible state. As a result, the damage from the blow was nullified — but.

"Guh, gubb—!"

He let out a breath, groaning as his vision flickered with agonizing pain.

The longest Regulus could stop time — including his own heart — was five seconds at most. Within that span he could act exactly as he had when he'd entrusted "Lion's Heart" to a bride, without any trouble. But if he demanded a stop any longer than that, his body would never return from the stoppage.

On top of that, it couldn't cover the agony of his stopped heart suddenly lurching back into motion once "Lion's Heart" was released. Pain, suffering — he hadn't felt either in over a hundred years.

"Don't you dare... screw..."

Spitting out hatred as if coughing blood, Regulus, still rising, couldn't even speak properly. His body, sent flying, was beyond his control, and however much force had been packed into the blow, the momentum carrying him upward showed no sign of fading, until he reached an altitude high enough to take in the whole of the city of Priestella at a glance.

The water gate city of Priestella — the place where, upon reading the passage in the "Gospel" that said the empty seat among his brides would be filled, he had done nothing but thank his good fortune.

"How could... such an idiotic turn of events...!!"

He had lost every single bride he'd gone to such trouble to gather, his very standing as "Greed" was under threat, he'd been reviled by some brat who was only good with his mouth, and he had been pitied by the shameless woman he'd chosen as his own.

There was no humiliation greater than this. He couldn't recall ever tasting such disgrace. Hadn't he become a Sin Archbishop precisely so he'd never have to feel such an irritating emotion? And yet, to be put through something like this — that wasn't the deal.

"Enough, enough, enough...!"

He would not think of holding back anymore. This was as far as his mercy would go. It made no difference that his opponents had seen through the Authority of "Lion's Heart," or that that inhuman Sword Saint was among them.

Given five seconds of a stopped heart, Regulus had any number of ways to kill them. He wouldn't get to see their faces twist in despair, wouldn't get to hear their dying screams — that was the only reason he hadn't dared to do it before.

While in his invincible state under the effect of "Lion's Heart," Regulus could, if he chose, act while ignoring every physical law of this world entirely. Faster than the wind could keep up with, in an instant on a scale beyond common sense, with overwhelming power that seemed not of this world — he was fully capable of erasing them from existence.

He could simply use the Authority of "Greed" to rise into the sky himself, then scatter sand so it reached across the whole town, and that alone would be enough to annihilate it. Other Sin Archbishops had come to the city too, but whether those fools lived or died was none of his concern. At this very moment, was there anything more important than freeing himself from this humiliation? He would paint over the faces of those smug, triumphant fools with terror.

The moment this absurd upward momentum finally stopped and he fell back to the ground would be their final moment. Until then, let them revel all they liked in their shallow, surface-level victory—

"—Agh!?"

Regulus, still chanting his litany of resentment, cried out as an impact struck his back.

Looking down, he found his upward momentum had abruptly stopped, and he was pinned forcibly in midair, as if something up there in the sky had kicked him.

"In a proper duel, this is the point where I'd sheathe my sword, once you'd lost the will to fight."

The voice belonged to someone standing calmly in midair, one foot planted on the back of the face-down Regulus, speaking with utter composure.

Regulus understood in an instant just who this being was, standing in midair with a foot on his back. And the moment he understood, he was struck with horror. Did this person have any idea how high up they currently were?

How could anyone reach this height faster than Regulus himself, who he himself had launched?

"Not to brag, but I do have confidence in my jumping ability. I've leapt from the ground straight onto the back of a Flying Dragon soaring above the clouds before."

"You... monster...!"

"That's right. I'm a monster who hunts monsters. — And now, it's time for you to accept your fate too."

Reinhard's foot lifted from his back.

The instant after he spoke, Regulus sensed a fighting spirit bearing down on him. Regulus, who over the course of his life had faced strong opponents time and again, and had learned nothing whatsoever from any of it.

And yet even so — the starting point of the attacks from the strong opponents he'd crossed blades with while stifling yawns, a realm his conscious memory hadn't retained — that memory made him react.

—"Lion's Heart" activated, and in the same instant, the attack came.

"Guh, aaaaaaah—!!"

Reinhard's downward hand-blade strike landed square in the middle of Regulus's back.

Struck by a blow whose sharpness might even have surpassed that of a famed blade, Regulus, still trailing the lingering effect of his invincible state, took only the impact — and was sent slamming straight down toward the ground below.

The ground rushed closer and closer, and Regulus slammed face-first into the cobblestones. But the effect of "Lion's Heart" was still active, and his body carved into the ground as if being swallowed by it.

Regulus's body punched straight through the cobblestones in a single line, passed through solid bedrock, and drove into the earth. As he continued this unresisting excavation of the ground, Regulus suddenly realized something.

If this momentum didn't stop, his body would keep falling all the way to the bottom of the earth. He'd never once considered whether the earth even had a bottom. But this world's land did have an edge. This world, surrounded on all sides by the Great Waterfall, surely had some far end where that great waterfall's falls poured over.

If his fall continued like this, would he end up reaching that place?

"There's no way I could ever accept something like— guh!?"

Literally choked with a bottomless terror, it was at that moment that his heart reached its limit.

Five seconds had passed. A warning signal blared, and Regulus wavered in his own judgment. He had never stopped his own heart for longer than five seconds. What was the maximum — surely not ten. And even if he did extend the time, all that would do was extend the distance he burrowed.

But what would happen if the ability were released now, in the middle of tunneling into the ground like this?

—There was no time to agonize over it. Dying from stopping his own heart too long would be idiotic beyond all reason.

"Ngh, nngghhh, ngghhhhh—!!"

Clenching his back teeth to keep them from chattering, Regulus steeled himself.

Heeding his heart's insistent demand to resume beating, Regulus released the effect of "Lion's Heart." His invincible state unraveled, the strength of his body and the laws of physics returned to normal—

"Bwuh—!?"

Every bone in his body — shattered.

That was how it felt — a merciless impact tore through Regulus's body.

Of course it did. He had driven into the ground at a speed far exceeding free fall, and with not a fraction of that momentum killed, his body had gone on burrowing through the earth. The only reason his body hadn't been blown to pieces was, precisely, that the tunnel he'd carved through the earth left no room for the pieces to scatter.

That said, while it couldn't spread side to side, up and down were another matter entirely.

"Ah, ahh..."

A hollow voice leaked out, and Regulus wept tears of blood from eyes now utterly crushed. The impact had run straight through his body lengthwise, destroying every part of it.

Without exaggeration, every bone in his body had suffered damage well beyond mere cracks, and the contents of his stomach had been churned into mush. His white hair, untouched by anyone until now, was soaked in blood and mud, and from his lower abdomen, its function lost, filth and waste leaked freely.

What remained there now was nothing more than a lump of flesh, its human shape lost.

And yet, astonishingly, that lump of flesh still drew breath.

"Ohh, ahhh..."

A dreadful, almost horrifying attachment to life — no, this should not be called attachment. It should be called a curse.

It was not an attachment born of wanting to live. What remained was only resentment toward the living who still lingered above. Even reduced to this, what drove him on was nothing but empty vanity.

*If I go all out — you're all — that's all there is to it.*

"Ehh, uh."

But do not underestimate that obsession.

A being who had devoted his entire life solely to never being pitied, without losing a single fragment of the twisted nature he had spent over a hundred years refining and warping, arrived at the optimal judgment for his own survival.

Activating "Lion's Heart" repeatedly for extremely short intervals, Regulus began digging through the earth. Once in his invincible state, damage to his body was of no concern at all. In a state where he felt no pain, moving his wounded body caused no further harm. With his bare hands, Regulus dug through the dirt.

His body, which had been buried head-down, turned within the round hollow he'd carved in the earth. Once his head faced upward, all that remained was to dig his way slowly back up to the surface. Once he reached the surface, those maggots, so deluded and full of themselves, would surely still be up there, giddy with excitement over having beaten Regulus.

Unforgivable. There was no way he could forgive this.

Being scorned, being looked down on, being pitied — every one of them was unbearable agony. Slander while he lived was one thing, but how could he forgive it after his death? Ah, yes. He should have simply done it the quick way from the start. Kill everyone — whether they were in sight or not — and no one would be left to speak ill of him. He should have done that from the very beginning. He wouldn't make that mistake again. Once he climbed back to the surface and killed those three, he'd take care of everyone else after that.

"————"

No longer even crying out, Regulus went on digging through the earth, powered by nothing but his own resentment.

He looked forward to seeing them beg for their lives once he reached the surface and went all out at last. Especially that woman, the one who'd kept making a fool of him — her, above all, he had to humiliate thoroughly. His seventy-ninth wife, the one who was supposed to be. The woman who should have occupied that empty seat had, to begin with, been an Elf woman living in some desolate forest, and there, too, had been that infuriating Petelgeuse—

—.
——.
———.

Aaaaaaah. Aaaaaaaaaaah. Aaaaaaaaaaaaah.

He remembered. He remembered, just now.

*That woman! It was her. No — the brat from back then!*

*When he'd gone to collect his seventy-ninth wife, it was that brat who'd kept scampering around her, wailing! That brat back then had grown into that woman now!*

He understood now why he'd wanted to give that empty seat to that woman the moment he laid eyes on her. It was simple. Since she was taking her mother's place, of course that girl should be the one to sit there.

She was the brat who had cherished his seventy-ninth wife, who had made a fool of him, and that sluggish fool Petelgeuse. Why hadn't he realized it sooner? No — how fortunate that he'd realized it now.

If he'd killed her without ever realizing it, the resentment in his heart would never have been settled. It was precisely because he now clearly recognized their crimes that killing her was worth doing. There was a sense of achievement in it, enough to answer for this humiliation. For the first time in a long while, he was consciously aware — there was meaning in satisfying this desire.

*I'll defile her, seventy-ninth wife. I'll take her away, Petelgeuse.*

*That girl you both cherished, the one who pitied me, who felt sorry for me.*

"Ah, hehe."

An impulse burst deep in his throat, and Regulus seethed with delight.

With no teeth left, he laughed through shredded lips. Hope for living had welled up in him. There was joy in the thought of plucking away the one thing those who had insulted him had desperately left behind.

*Claw his way up, claw his way up, claw his way up, and then—*

"—?"

Digging his way upward, Regulus suddenly noticed something touch his fingertips. He lowered his right hand — he could no longer tell where his fingers even were — and looked, with eyes that could no longer see, at the lump of blood and mud it had become. The surface was faintly damp with something that wasn't blood.

He tried licking it. It tasted of bitter mud, but it seemed, somehow, to be water.

Water. Water. The instant he understood it was water, Regulus became conscious of his own thirst. A single drop wasn't enough. He wanted enough water to soothe his throat, to fill his stomach. With the effect of "Lion's Heart" interrupted and time flooding back into his body, Regulus, after a gap of nearly a hundred years, found himself craving the chance to eat.

Water alone would do for now. The most exquisite taste imaginable was right there. The instant he thought so, just as Regulus wished, a trickle of water began flowing down from above.

He sucked greedily at that earth-flavored water. Even with his teeth gone, his tongue torn, and blood pouring endlessly inside his mouth, the water was undeniably delicious. He felt, for a moment, fulfilled.

—It was right after that, that the volume of water pouring in suddenly surged, and Regulus's tunneled-through body was dropped back down to the very bottom once more.

"Ah, uh, ohh?"

It poured in. It kept pouring in. Into the earth with no way to escape, water flowed in without end.

There was no spare space beneath the ground. In no time at all, Regulus's body sank into muddy, foul-smelling water, and he lost all freedom of movement.

—Regulus surely had no way of understanding what was happening to him now.

This was the water that had been flowing through the canals of the city of Priestella, up above his head.

Regulus, driven by Reinhard's single blow straight through the streets and down into the earth. Into the tunnel his own body had carved into the ground, the water from the canal — which he himself had destroyed and flooded — came pouring in. It assailed him without pause, drowning the madman beneath the water.

As if it were the very anger of the city, and of the people living in it, whose beautiful streets had been destroyed.

"Gogh, bwah."

Of course, Regulus, drowning at this very moment, had no way of noticing any of that.

Tortured by water in the ground, Regulus, terrified by the pressure of water forcing its way even into his lungs, thrashed desperately. But there was no space in the earth to thrash in. All he could do was curl up in the filthy mud and shut himself away with "Lion's Heart."

While "Lion's Heart" was active, he didn't have to endure the agony of suffocation. The same was true for the pain of his shattered body.

But "Lion's Heart" never lasted more than five seconds. The moment he felt his heart reach its limit, the fear of death dragged Regulus back into the hell of being tortured by water once more.

Invitations to death, arriving in alternation.

There was no way he could choose either one. He had to reject both. But Regulus had no means of doing so. All he had was resentment at this absurdity.

His time limit kept ticking away.

"Lion's Heart" could be triggered again and again, but breathing didn't work that way. And to reactivate "Lion's Heart," he needed an interval of several seconds.

Cardiac arrest.
Drowning.
Cardiac arrest.
Drowning.
Cardiac arrest—.
Drowning—.

Pain and suffering pressed in without end, without pause, as if it might go on forever.

Regulus opened his mouth. Into that open mouth, water and mud came flooding in. As it invaded his lungs and organs, Regulus screamed. He kept screaming, in a voice that made no sound.

There was no answer. No one was beside him.

Still he kept screaming, pouring resentment into it, wishing that this scream would kill every person in the world.

*He refused to be mocked after his own death.*

*He refused to let that girl go around gleefully saying she'd avenged her mother and Petelgeuse.*

*Just the thought of that girl rejoicing at Regulus's death, leaping for joy, made him want to vomit.*

*She would surely act as though she'd achieved the goal of her life, the very sustenance that kept her going.*

*She would no doubt spout some deluded nonsense about how Regulus's death would set her own life in motion, make it shine.*

*He could not bear the thought of that girl being fulfilled by a joy so misdirected, so misguided, so utterly unreasonable.*

*That his own death should leave such a great, enormous mark on that girl's heart—oh.*

Regulus Corneas, who had shattered the cobblestones and sunk into the earth.

Into the grave that madman's own body had carved out, a great volume of water from the water gate city poured in. There was no telling how far down that madman had sunk. But given the limits of his Authority, it was unlikely he had tunneled clean through to the far side of the world.

Nine times out of ten, the effect had run out somewhere underground, and he had been crushed by his own momentum. Even if, by some chance, he hadn't been crushed, the water pouring in would never let the madman escape.

A madman drowned in his own powerful Authority would meet his end drowned, in payment from the very city he had destroyed.

"...Emilia-tan, you've got a face like something's not sitting right with you."

Emilia kept staring intently at the hole where Regulus had sunk. Seeing a faint trace of unease in her profile, Subaru spoke up.

There wasn't a single thing about that madman worth sympathizing with. Emilia surely agreed on that point too, and there was no reason for her to be troubled over his fate buried in the earth — and yet—

"It's a good thing that you're kind, Emilia-tan, but I think it'd be a mistake to share even a bit of that with a guy like this. Some people really are just hopeless, no matter what."

"...Thanks for worrying about me. But that's not it. That's not what this is."

"Hm?"

To Subaru's considerate words, Emilia slowly shook her head.

Then, after a moment of silence, lowering her eyes framed by long lashes, she said,

"It's about Regulus... ever since I first saw him, I've had this feeling that I met him somewhere before."

"You mean it wasn't the first time you'd met? Then when was it?"

"That's the thing — I can't remember."

At Subaru's question, Emilia tilted her head.

By strange coincidence, it was the very same moment that Regulus, sunk in the earth, let out his silent scream.

A madman who, with a scream that would never reach her, had prayed that Emilia would not rejoice at his death.

His own existence, bound up in both her mother's death and her benefactor's madness. A wedge driven into her life that the girl could surely never forget — he did not want his own death to become something that fulfilled her.

And so that madman's final wish, one that never reached the surface, was —


"—I wonder where I met Regulus before."


Regulus Corneas had left no impact whatsoever on Emilia.

And in that ironic way, his wish was granted, fully and completely.
