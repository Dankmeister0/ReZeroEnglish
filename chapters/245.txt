Arc 4 Chapter 78: "The Sound That Makes You Want to Cry"

"Getting all small and stubborn like that... You're just like a child, aren't you. It's painful to watch, I can hardly bear it. *sigh*"

Hearing Sekhmet's words comparing him to a child, Subaru — curled up small within his own shell — thought, *Maybe she's right.*

Stubborn, convinced this was the only way, deaf to the voices around him, digging in his heels — but this way was the surest, and it caused the least harm.

If he leaned on the world's repetitions, he could be granted chance after chance, and so long as he kept paying with his life as the price, he could try again without end. Along the way, no doubt, his heart would wear thin, again and again, fraying almost to nothing.

But even so — in the moment he seemed about to shatter into brittle pieces — Subaru already had words enough to steel himself.

'—Subaru-kun is Rem's hero.'

That's right. For Subaru, that alone was enough.

If those words, and everyone's faces, lay somewhere beyond the point where his soul wore itself away, he wanted nothing more than that.

What was wrong with that?

"—Baru, are you cryin'?"

Suddenly a young child's voice rang out into the silence, and the witches all drew a faint breath.

A small palm stroked, soft and light, over the head of Subaru curled into a ball. Glancing up through a vision blurred with tears, Subaru saw, faint in his sight, a girl with dark tan skin.

The Witch of Pride met Subaru's feeble gaze, and—

"You got made to cry like this, poor thing. ...Who made you cry?"

Typhon, who had been kneeling, rose to her feet and swept a glare around the circle of silent witches.

The girl, a dangerous light kindling in both eyes, looked over the witches' faces one by one, and at last noticed Satella and raised her brows.

"Tera? Tera's here? How come? It's been a while, huh."

She raised a hand and waved it at Satella in greeting, but the combative glint in Typhon's eyes never once faded. The first to speak to Typhon in that state was Sekhmet, who let out a weary breath and pushed herself upright.

"Typhon... *hah*. We're a little occupied right now, *hoo*. Don't go provoking the boy, *hah*. Come over here, *hoo*."

"Ha, did you do somethin' bad to Baru? Ha, are you a baddie?"

"Your mother, *hah*, doesn't have the energy to be a villain, *hoo*. I've no will to lift a finger myself, let alone make you lift one, *hah*."

Typhon gave a small nod at Sekhmet's answer, but showed no sign of obeying and leaving Subaru's side. Instead she turned to look at Minerva next.

"What about Ruva? You're not... bullyin' Baru, right?"

"Why weren't you even going to ask me that? That's rude! It's not like I'm always, always just healing people — sometimes I might let the violent feelings smoldering in my heart take over and hurt... hurt someone too, you know, maybe!"

"A girl who turns pale just imagining it hardly seems the type to start a fight herself."

Echidna shrugged and needled Minerva over her less-than-convincing claim. Minerva's gaze sharpened into a glare, and drawn along by it, Typhon turned to look at Echidna too, twisting her young face into a scowl.

"It's Dona. Dona, did you do somethin' bad again? Dona, are you a baddie?"

"I wonder what it means that you're so certain about me in particular. I'd love to hear the details from whoever raised you — what do you say?"

"That'll be your everyday conduct catching up with you. *hah*"

Sekhmet pressed a hand to her forehead, looking exhausted. Typhon, meanwhile, refused to leave Subaru's side, watchful and worked up in her determination to root out whichever "baddie" had made Subaru cry.

Echidna narrowed her eyes at the young witch's behavior and murmured, "Even so—"

"—with even Typhon showing her face here, that's nearly everyone gathered. If Daphne turns up too, it'll be just like four hundred years ago..."

"Did somebody caaall for Daphne juuust now?"

As though responding to Echidna's remark, a black-painted coffin abruptly appeared in the meadow.

Inside the coffin, her whole body sealed in a straitjacket and even her eyes bound shut, was the Witch of Gluttony, Daphne.

She gave a small sniff of her nose, seeming to identify everyone present by scent alone, and said—

"Never mind Subaru-n, is Tera-Tera even here toooo? Now isn't that somethin'. All seven Deadly Sin witches together, and on top of that even a Sage candidate..."

"Daphne. —He hasn't reached that far yet."

"...Ohh, my baaad, sorry about that. Still, though... *sniff sniff*. There's a salty smell in the air — is somebody cryin'? Is it Neru-Neru?"

Her oblivious, languid drawl knocked softly against the tension in the air.

With Satella included, the dream castle now held all seven witches gathered together — a sight so rare it had scarcely occurred even in the witches' heyday four hundred years past.

Just seven women who had once plunged the world into chaos — and with some of them now on the verge of coming to blows, the scene held power concentrated enough to reshape the very form of the world, depending on how things went.

The Witch of Pride — to pass judgment on whoever had made the shrinking boy cry.

The Witch of Wrath — clenching her fist tight, so that the wish of the witch dear to her heart standing at her side might be fulfilled.

The Witch of Sloth — keeping languid watch over everyone's movements, poised to crush anything the instant it became necessary.

The Witch of Lust — maintaining an air of complete indifference, ready only to protect herself the moment anything happened.

The Witch of Gluttony — no longer caring in the least how the situation changed, agonizing instead over whose finger would best satisfy her hunger.

The Witch of Greed — regarding one particular witch's presence with distaste, and yet her eyes shining with curiosity over how this uncertain tea party would turn out.

And Satella, who was not the Witch of Envy—

"I love you. —Because you gave me light. Because you took my hand and showed me the world outside. Because on the nights I trembled with loneliness, you stayed by my side and held my hand the whole time. Because when I was alone, you kissed me and told me I wasn't. I received so much — too much — from you. ...That's why I love you. Because you — because you gave me everything."

"————"

Not one word of Satella's whisper held any memory for Subaru.

He didn't know her. He knew nothing. He had never once met Satella, never exchanged words with her, never shared warmth with her. What she spoke of was nothing but the product of delusion. A woman driven mad by obsession, laying out fantasies that had never happened, seeing an illusion of Subaru that wasn't real.

That should have been the truth — and yet 'Natsuki Subaru' knew it.

"Why... What is this, inside me? I don't want this feeling. Don't bind me with memories that never happened... I—I don't... I don't feel that way about you... about you...!"

All he had to do was tell her, in a single word, that he hated her.

He only had to thrust it in the face of the one directing such feelings at him — that he held not one shred of affection for her in return. It would be a sight to see, the face she made, the self-centered woman who tried to twist another's feelings to suit herself. No doubt she would contort her face magnificently, wracked with heartache.

—How could you possibly do that to her?

"Baru?"

"Boy..."

"Th-that child..."

"You...!"

"Subaru-n?"

"—Ah. That, too, is one choice, Natsuki Subaru."

Each of the witches called out Subaru's name in turn, and Echidna gave a small nod at the outcome of that call.

"—Guh, blugh—"

—Still crouched on the ground, Subaru had bitten clean through his own tongue.

Cornered by the witches, he had lost all sense of anything.

And in a situation where even the shape of his own heart seemed about to be twisted out of true, if not even his will remained his own, what was left to Subaru at all?

If he couldn't even hold to his own stubbornness, and was left simply denied — that alone would still have been bearable.

But if being near Satella meant he came to accept even his own denial — that was what terrified Subaru.

—If he lost his life here in the dream castle, what would even happen?

Right now, Subaru's physical body should still be inside the tomb in the Sanctuary.

What had been summoned here was only Subaru's spiritual form — his soul, one might say. If that spiritual form died here, would it feed back into his physical body? Or would it simply be the death of his soul?

It didn't matter either way. If dying meant starting over, he'd already resolved himself to that.

Without relying on the witches' help, paring himself down further than ever before, purging every excess element, if he simply gave everything he had with single-minded effort, surely a path would open — and then, he would—

"You— idiot——!"

The instant she realized Subaru had harmed himself, Minerva rolled up her sleeves and surged forward, breaking into a run to strike Subaru and heal him with a fist brimming with restorative power. But before she could reach him, Typhon, who had been standing at Subaru's side, cut in between them.

The young witch spread her small body as wide as it would go and stood blocking Minerva's path.

"Baru chose it himself! Ruva can't get in the way, no way!"

"Self-harm, suicide, hurting others, killing others — I won't allow a single one of them in front of me! I don't care about anguish of the heart! Wounds I can't see are none of my business! So instead — the wounds I CAN see, I will never overlook!"

With a single step that caved in the ground, Minerva's wind-piercing fist closed in on Typhon's face.

The force packed into that driving fist held enough power to shatter a mountain, but the instant it struck a living creature, the destructive energy converted into healing energy. That said, the shockwave and the sheer impact of the blow still tore straight through whatever it struck.

A thunderous boom rang out, and Minerva's fully extended arm sent Typhon flying with a solid, direct hit.

The undeveloped body of the girl blew away like a leaf, spinning high up into the meadow's illusory blue sky. It was a merciless sight — but Typhon was not the only one who paid a price.

"————!"

Minerva's right arm, having followed through on the swing, shattered from the shoulder down like a crystal of ice.

It was the result of her act having touched the judgment of the Witch of Pride and being ruled 'evil' — and so, denied.

Minerva threw her head back at the pain of losing her arm, opened her mouth wide, and let out a scream—

"IT'S NOTHIN'——!!"

None.

The Witch of Wrath, so keenly attuned to the pain of others, endlessly puts her own pain last.

In that, she was a mirror of Natsuki Subaru's own way of living, conveniently set aside — putting herself last.

"Anyway, with this——!"

With the obstacle gone, Minerva thought, and leapt toward Subaru, twisting her remaining left arm. Her mighty arm came crashing down at Subaru from directly above—

"The next obstacle is me, *hah*."

Golden hair fluttering, in a single instant Minerva had been slammed down flat onto the ground.

Her whole body pressed into the earth, leaving a person-shaped hollow in the meadow, Minerva lifted her face — red with fury — and shouted at Sekhmet, who sat there hugging her knees.

"Stay out of this——! Sekhmet——!"

"Afraid that won't do, *hoo*. As far as my feelings go, I'm on that boy's side, *hah*. And I might add, I'm on Typhon's side too, *hoo*. There's no reason for me not to interfere, *hah*."

At Sekhmet's declaration of opposition, Minerva bit her lip in frustration and looked around.

But Daphne and Carmilla held a neutral stance toward this conflict, and Echidna, for her part, was merely an observer watching to see how it would turn out. And Satella—

"Ah... ah..."

—her voice trembled at the sight of Subaru, who had collapsed to his knees, blood pouring in a torrent from his mouth.

Gurgling, his throat clogged with overflowing blood and his torn tongue, tasting the sensation of drowning, Subaru caught Satella's state at the edge of his awareness.

Was she crying?

Watching Subaru's 'death,' she looked shaken beyond anything he'd seen before.

"Why don't you notice...? Among everyone you wish to save, you should be there too — it's such an obvious thing."

Why would she think of Subaru that way?

Just how much, within her delusion, had Subaru supported her heart?

"Just like the people gasping for breath in fate's dead end, it's come for you too. It's only that you alone have the chance to overturn it... Even you are someone who deserves to be saved. So why—"

All of that was wrong.

Subaru was a hopeless case — he hadn't even followed through on the things he thought were within reach, hadn't managed to save the very people he wanted to save, hadn't fully broken free of being a half-hearted man.

Hadn't he sworn to break free of that, to stop being half-hearted?

Hadn't he decided to make himself look good, to be someone worth looking up to?

—Inside himself, the weak self and the self that refused to be weak were locked in struggle.

Natsuki Subaru could no longer show his weakness to anyone.

He had to live strong, noble, resilient — the way a hero ought to be.

One girl wished that of him. It was a curse Subaru himself had placed on her, and he bore the responsibility to repay the girl he had bound with that curse. No — it wasn't about responsibility. It was simply that, if she believed in him that way, he wanted to be someone worthy of that belief.

Because she had told Subaru she loved him, he wanted to remain someone she could keep telling that to.

Ah, that's right.
Yes, that's right.

If there were anyone who would even hypothetically grieve Subaru's 'death,' it would be her.

Choosing 'death' would mean betraying the girl who believed in him. Of course, Subaru had no intention of ending things with mere 'death.' He was resolved to make even 'death' itself a stepping stone, in order to sweep away the reason for that death, in order to win everything back.

But — for the person left behind by a Subaru who had chosen 'death,' what would that mean?

He mustn't think about it.
He mustn't know it. That was a dangerous way of thinking.
It was fine. This was fine, for Natsuki Subaru.

He mustn't think that anyone would grieve over him.
He wasn't worth that much. Subaru's life was a consumable. Use it, use it, use it up — that was all, so long as it got him to the end. That was all it should be: a consumable.

Cast aside all appearances, put dying to good use, and don't face his own 'death' at all.
Draw the line and be done with it. There was no need to think about anything. It was simply the decision to throw away what had to be thrown away, in order to pick up what he wanted to pick up. Everyone did it. Subaru could do the same.

If he could just save the precious people who deserved saving, that would be enough.
If he could do just that much, then Subaru—

"In the two Trials of the Tomb... what exactly... did you see...?"

Trial. —Trial. Trial, 'Trial.' Trial trial trial, 'Trial' trial trial trial, trial——?

Oxygen deprivation and shock had slowed his thinking to a crawl.

Even his sight had grown unreliable at last, the world beginning to flash red, static crawling across it like a television full of snow, and Subaru thought, hazily, that the end was probably coming soon.

The end arrived, slowly.

How many times was this, facing 'death'? Even counting felt like too much trouble — but that was fine too.

Sooner or later, he would have to repeat death so many times that even counting would sicken him.

He couldn't imagine he'd be able to keep the kind of mind that could still tally up his deaths.

A heart of steel.
A heart of steel, unshaken by anything at all, he would carry with him—

And just like that, Subaru's consciousness slowly, slowly slipped away,
until at last, it vanished,

* * *

'I'm counting on you, son.'

There was a sound.
From beyond the static, amid the chaotic scatter of noise, an oddly clear sound reached him.

'—Take care out there.'

He heard it again.
A different sound, this time. But what it stirred in his chest was the same — a sound, he heard.

'I wanted to call you a friend.'

A different sound, one that changed what he felt.
A sound that made him terribly restless. And yet, a sound that was somehow pleasant too.

'Subaru-dono... I'm sorry... I'm so very sorry...!'

Another sound, yet again.
What welled up in his chest was a loneliness, and something like longing — a sound that made him feel sorry.

'I know you're not... that person... but...'

A sound that squeezed his chest tight, he heard.
Hearing that sound, he found himself unbearably unable to hold back. A sound on the verge of tears. A sound he mustn't let cry. A sound he had to protect. A sound. A sound. A sound.

'Please, show me something cool, Subaru-kun.'

With a lurch, something burst inside him, a sound answering the sound.
Heat spread through his whole body. A sense of purpose drove him on. This sound was one that had supported him all along.

And then—

'Thank you, Subaru.'

There was a sound.

'—For saving me.'

—A sound, announcing the beginning of it all, there was.

* * *

Would he end up crying?

The people Subaru held dear — if they learned he had died, would they grieve for him?

In the world Subaru had selfishly broken away from by experiencing 'death,' the irreplaceable people left behind there — had they mourned his death, grieved it?

Just as Subaru, regretting his own insufficiency, repeated 'Return by Death' in search of the best outcome — had they, too, who lost Subaru by a single step falling short, regretted that moment?

There were people he thought of as precious.
There were people he believed he had to protect.
There were people he could wish, with his whole heart, to save from fate's dead end.

—Was he worth being mourned by those precious people?

Was it all right to be conceited?
To believe that even someone like him could be precious — that the people precious to him thought of him that way too.

Was it all right to believe it?
That even someone like him was needed by the people he wanted to protect, needed enough that they wanted to protect him too.

Was it permitted to wish for it?
That even someone like him had people who would weep at his loss, that he was worth someone reaching out a hand to save.

—Was it all right to think it, even so?

That he didn't want to die.
That he didn't want to give up and tell himself it was the only way.
That he hated the thought of becoming nothing but a foundation stone, vanishing away to protect the future of the people he loved.

That in the future he managed to protect, he wanted to be there too, alongside the people precious to him.
Was it all right to think that way?

Did he have the right to think it?
If he did—

"I don't... want to die..."

A clot of blood spilled out with a sound, and along with the hiss of escaping air, his voice came out.

His throat, which should have been sealed shut by his bitten-through tongue, opened, and his mouth worked open and shut, gasping, grasping for oxygen. His lungs swelled, oxygen reached his brain, and the blurred edges of his vision began to return.

And then—

"That's your real feelings, isn't it...!"

—Right there before his eyes, red in the face, the Witch of Wrath who had healed Subaru with a headbutt had lost both her legs too, and reached him anyway through sheer will.
