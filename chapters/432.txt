Arc 6 Chapter 29: "Loser"

—By the time he'd sprinted up that long, long staircase and finally arrived, it was already too late.

"—————"

Breath ragged, lungs screaming in protest at the abuse, he'd wrung out a shout from somewhere deep inside.
But voices, words — they rarely carry enough power to change a situation. In a scene where swordsmanship itself was being contested, that shortfall in the power of words was all the more glaring.
Though whether it could even be called a contest of "swordsmanship" was itself doubtful.
It had been far too one-sided, and no matter how you dressed it up, one combatant's weapon was nothing more than a stick.

—A blinding white light painted over the already-white space with an even fiercer white.

The principle behind it was unknown. Whether it was because the blade itself boasted of cutting even light, or simply the doing of that unorthodox swordsman's sheer nature, the slash came with a shockwave that swept the space clean.
The figure caught in the line of that strike was likewise swallowed by the light and sent flying, helpless to resist.

Then, in the literal blink of an eye, the light vanished, and what the clearing white revealed was the tall frame of a red-haired man with one sleeve shrugged off — and, sprawled like a corpse, the figure of a purple-haired swordsman.

"Yo, if it ain't the minnow. That you?"

It was the red-haired man who called out so casually to Subaru, struck speechless by the sight. His face showed no trace of the sword clash just moments before — not even a hint of exertion — as he grinned like a shark.
Then he pointed at the fallen swordsman — Julius.

"You're late. Already wrapped up here, and he's in the way, so haul him on outta here already."

"...Reid Astrea."

"What's that, huh? Don't go diggin' around for a guy's name like that. Here I was tryin' to look cool by not introducin' myself, and you go and ruin it."

The "Stick-Swinger" — or rather, Reid — soured at having his name spoken.
At that off-the-mark complaint, Subaru felt his own irritation rise, but he didn't let it show outright. Slowly, without ever taking his eyes off Reid, he made his way to where Julius lay fallen.

"I ain't gonna eat him or nothin'. No need to stare me down like that."

"Sorry, but where I'm from, common sense says you don't look away from a bear. Julius—"

Keeping his wary gaze fixed on Reid, Subaru leaned down to check Julius's breathing. He'd lost consciousness, but the hand Subaru held to his mouth caught the faint reaction of breath.
Even after taking that strike, it seemed his life hadn't been taken. Subaru breathed a sigh of relief.

"For a guy who said he wouldn't hold back next time, you're showin' some mercy."

"Not really. Don'tcha think gettin' killed by chopsticks is less lame than losin' to chopsticks and runnin' home with your tail between your legs? I sure do. Better off dead than showin' a pathetic sight like that. So I'm beatin' him with chopsticks and sendin' him home cryin'."

"I take back what I said about mercy, you bastard."

"Ha! Nothin' a minnow says is gonna land on me. Besides, I got no more testin' to do today. Come at me if you want, and I'll flatten you — just like the guy at your feet."

Scratching his belly with his right hand, Reid pointed the chopsticks in his left, alternating between Subaru and Julius.
And as things stood, no matter how furious Reid's attitude made him, Subaru had no way to make him take back his insolence.

"Damn it."

"Yeah, yeah, that's the spirit. Just shut up, sling him over your shoulder, and mutter some sore-loser grumblin' on your way out. If that clears your head, it's easier and smarter, y'know. Boring, though."

Plopping himself down, Reid narrowed his blue eyes with cold amusement as he spoke. Exposed to that victor's cruel pleasure, Subaru somehow managed to hoist the fallen Julius up.
Just as he had barely an hour before, he forced Julius onto his back. An unconscious body was heavy, and Julius was taller than Subaru besides. It made for an awkward, difficult carry, but there was no helping it — needs must.
Leaving Julius behind here alone was never an option to begin with.

"—Next time bring some pretty girl along, will ya. That drop-dead gorgeous one'd do fine."

To the very end, without once calling anyone by name, Reid gave a fluttering wave of his hand.
In the face of Reid's attitude, tantamount to a bad joke, Subaru quite literally had nothing to say, and could do nothing but flee back the way he'd come.


* * *


"...Haah, haah."

Step by step, stair by stair, he made his way down, testing each one as if to be sure.
The stairway passage was fairly wide, but the sight of it hemmed in by stone walls left an inescapable sense of confinement. This was the second time he'd gone up and down this staircase, but as for the structural oddity of a straight staircase that never broke through the tower, or any way to unravel that mystery — he had zero clues so far.

"Thinkin' stupid stuff like that... is the only way I can... keep goin'...!"

The agony of descending a long, long staircase while carrying another person on his back needed no explanation.
Especially since this great staircase had as many as four hundred and forty-four steps (per Emilia's count). Subaru practically never harbored any ill feeling toward Emilia, but this once, having a precise number attached to this brutal staircase, he felt the urge to pull her cheeks and scold her for it.
Of course, cheeks pulled or not, Emilia was surely still cute—

"Gotta get him back quick... Emilia and Beako must be worried too."

When Subaru and the others had noticed Julius's absence in the green room, they'd all split up to search for him throughout the Trial Tower. Some headed for the third floor, others hurried to each room on the fourth, and Shaula was sent down to check on Giant and the dragon carriage below — all manner of approaches.
Everyone was worried about Julius. Defeated by Reid, he'd vanished, leaving behind his broken knight's sword — and what state of mind he must be in—
That the women's hearts would ache with concern over this was, by now, beyond question.

"But, well..."

—Only Subaru was different. Only Subaru had understood at once.
Where Julius, having set down his knight's sword, had gone, and for what purpose.
That was something, surely, only Subaru—

"—It sways, doesn't it."

"—! You're awake!"

At the voice reaching him from his back, Subaru stopped the foot he'd extended toward the floor below. At his call, Julius, borne on his back, stirred with an "Ah," and—

"Wh-where..."

"Vaguely speaking, partway down a staircase. A bit more specifically, partway down a long staircase. And with even more specifics added, partway through fleeing back down the stairs between the fourth and second floors."

"How roundabout... Am I... being carried by you?"

"That's right. And let me tell you, this is the second time in this short a span. You have any idea how I feel, gettin' made to do this again just thirty minutes after swearin' I'd never do it twice?"

"No wonder the ride's so uncomfortable..."

"You wanna get thrown off?"

The faint sense of Julius laughing, breath escaping softly, reached him through his back. Even as he protested the sarcastic remark, Subaru let a little of his tension ease.

Honestly, Subaru hadn't been able to predict what Julius's first words upon waking would be. To speak his true feelings without reserve, it wouldn't be an exaggeration to say he'd been afraid of it.
So he felt relieved that the exchange springing from Julius's first waking words hadn't been born of self-destructive despair.

"You actually remember what happened?"

"...It's a pathetic story, I admit. Struck down by the enemy with ease, and then shown mercy on top of it, all while causing trouble for Lady Anastasia and you—"

"...I'm not enough of a devil to blame you for gettin' wrecked by that guy."

At words so painfully like him, Subaru sighed and resumed his descent.
With his consciousness restored, Julius was far easier to carry than before. And with the anxiety that had weighed down his steps lifted, it seemed the remaining stairs could be tackled with momentum.

"...Is Lady Anastasia safe? I saw her collapse, and I saw her being treated in that spirit's room, but—"

"No danger to her life — that's the diagnosis so far. You're the one who came a lot closer to dyin', goin' up against that guy... Actually, you're gonna be shocked when you find out who that eyepatch bastard is."

"—Reid Astrea."

"—————"

At the voice so full of certainty, Subaru's breath and footsteps both stopped for an instant in surprise. But quickly, as if to cover it up, both his breathing and his steps resumed.
Choosing his words so his back wouldn't betray that unease—

"Well, well, look who figured it out. On our end, Shaula knew him — makes sense, since she'd be an acquaintance of his from four hundred years back. Apparently that whole thing about her bein' scared of him was real, too. Passin' out the second she saw his face — that's why, apparently."

"It's nothing. There was ample room to notice. Hair red as flame, blue eyes. Swordsmanship so far beyond the pale... though whether it can be called swordsmanship, I wonder. I never even made him draw a blade against me. Let's just say he's a man of overwhelming skill. And 'Stick-Swinger' — that's an expression found in the records describing him as well."

"Don't tell me — he's been called the 'Stick-Swinger' since way back because he fights with those chopsticks?"

"To be precise, it carries the meaning that he doesn't choose his weapon. The moment he called himself the 'Stick-Swinger,' I had my suspicions... but I couldn't be certain. I'm sorry I didn't say anything."

Subaru said nothing in response to the apologetic note in Julius's voice.
Julius said there had been room to notice, but Subaru thought that was far too harsh a standard to hold himself to.

Reid Astrea, the man held to be the first Sword Saint, was a person who should have died four hundred years ago.
Encountering a great figure of the past whose name lived on in fairy tales and legends — that wasn't the kind of leap you could easily make just because a few features matched.

If there had been any possibility of noticing, it was Subaru who should have possessed that imaginative leap.
He and Emilia had nodded in agreement that the "Trial" conducted at this Trial Tower bore some resemblance to the Trials of the Tomb he'd undergone at Echidna's tomb.
If so, it was Subaru who should have given more thought to the nature of the "Trial" held in this tower, laying out every possibility he could conceive of.
Neglecting to do so was what had led to their rout on the second floor, "Electra."

"Heh. An encounter, by whatever principle, with a legendary swordsman revived from the past... is it. By rights, I suppose I ought to rejoice at this miraculous encounter, but..."

"I feel for you. A legendary hero turnin' out like that in person — that's peak disappointment. Between him and Shaula's whole 'Sage' reputation, this tower's got way too many letdowns."

"...You feel for me, do you."

There was something hoarse and self-mocking in the tone of his voice.
Hearing those words Julius let slip so close at hand, Subaru gritted his back teeth, realizing he'd lacked consideration. But without touching on it, he only said, "Anyway,"

"If I remember right, the guy engraved on the gold coin was supposed to be the first Sword Saint, but he was way different from the real thing too. Shaula bein' a totally different gender, sure, that's just mistaken identity, can't help that — but this guy's got a pretty huge gap too. The one on the coin looked a lot more like an old geezer..."

"As a matter of historical record, Reid achieved the feats that earned him a place among the Three Great Heroes at a considerably older age. The figure depicted on the coin is likely accurate. It's the him up above who's younger than the historical record."

"Now that you mention it, Reid didn't seem to recognize Shaula at all..."

What was meant to change the subject ended up tying into one of his questions in an unexpected way.
Cross-referencing it with Julius's account, indeed — for two people who supposedly knew each other, there had been far too much of a temperature gap between Shaula's side and Reid's. If, hypothetically, that had been "the Reid from before he met Shaula," then the discrepancy in their recognition of each other made a certain amount of sense.
Though granted, Shaula's reaction had been excessive, and given Reid's personality, there was a fairly high chance he simply didn't remember people's faces or names — which weakened the theory.

"If that's right, then it means he fought the Witch after his prime had already passed. And we've gotta get past him in his young prime."

"It's starting to sound less like a rocky road ahead and more like an impossible task."

"No question it's rough. But if we work out a strategy, there's bound to be a way through. In fact—"

Even as he groped for some thread toward beating Reid, Subaru hesitated over what to say next.
Something in him balked at whether, in this very moment, he should tell Julius that.
But that was a step too late.

"In fact... what?"

"No, well..."

"Subaru."

An excuse like "I saw a breakthrough by observing Reid" would be seen through in an instant. In truth, no method to get past Reid right now, no weakness, had actually been found.
So, called by name so briefly, Subaru gave up trying to dodge it.

"...After you and Anastasia went down, Emilia cleared the 'Trial.'"

"—————"

"Only, well, it's not like she just flat-out beat him in a straight contest of strength. A bunch of coincidences worked in her favor, and... Emilia's, well, kind of a special case."

Calling it a simple victory was, in a way, difficult given the result.
As a "Trial," the closest description was that Emilia had made Reid, the examiner, acknowledge her resolve and her ability — but the actual circumstances were hard to explain to anyone who hadn't seen it firsthand.
Even if you told someone to win the same way, it wasn't something anyone but Emilia could pull off.

"Anyway, with a bunch of complicated factors tangled together, Emilia cleared the 'Trial.' Except, apparently he only lets the person who cleared it through — so for all of us to get upstairs, everyone still has to win, same as before... if anything, it's worse."

"—————"

"So, yeah, we need to work out a strategy. Never mind those of us who can actually fight — someone like me won't get anywhere unless I can talk him into a tag-team fight with Beatrice. And Meili doesn't even have a reason to take on the 'Trial' in the first place. Gotta talk all that through and get everyone on board... though just thinkin' about it's exhausting."

"—————"

"So, um, it's not like there's no hope for you either, if you go for a rematch. Though, I mean, not the way we did it this time. We'd size him up properly first, work out his patterns and countermeasures. Just this once, follow my style and—"

"—————"

"...Hey, you listening? Julius, hey?"

While explaining in a rush, he grew suspicious of Julius's lack of response. Calling out to the man on his back like that, after a few repeated calls Julius let out a faint, held breath, and—

"Ah, yes, I'm fine. I'm listening, of course. ...I see, so Lady Emilia—"

"That part's slightly earlier in the story, but... yeah, that's right. Which is why this isn't some hopeless Trial to clear. Don't get too worked up about it, okay?"

"Worked up? No, there's no need to worry about that. —Everything is as you say. If Lady Emilia was able to overcome the 'Trial,' then he... the Sword Saint Reid, is by no means an insurmountable obstacle. Learning that is a great gain."

"R-right, exactly. That's it exactly. ...As long as you get that much, we're good."

Julius accepted the news that Emilia had overcome the "Trial" with unexpectedly graceful ease, and Subaru, who'd explained it all fearfully, felt almost let down. —No, this was fine.

Being told that someone else had already cleared the obstacle he himself had stumbled on.
Worrying that this fact might grate at his heart was, for Julius, entirely unnecessary. Perhaps Subaru had expected a bit too much of a fragile side from him. Or perhaps he simply shouldn't have tried to measure a knight like Julius Juukulius by his own yardstick.

Subaru's musing and Julius's long exhale came at the same moment.
Then, in a light tone, Julius spun out the word "Well then,"

"Would you mind setting me down soon? I feel like I'll get carriage-sick if you keep carrying me forever. Unlike an Earth Dragon, you don't seem to have the Divine Protection of Wind Evasion, after all."

"Just put up with the swayin' and the wind and be grateful you're gettin' carried. It's rough goin', sure, but I'm not so heartless I'll make an injured guy walk on his own. Emilia-tan would chew me out."

Shaking his head at Julius's request, Subaru gave his body a jostle and resettled Julius on his back.
In truth, Julius's condition wasn't in good shape. He'd only been in the green room for a matter of a few dozen minutes, and no matter how capable the spirit there was, it was doubtful how much treatment could really be given to a body beaten as thoroughly as Julius's.
And after his second defeat, Julius hadn't received any treatment at all.

If there'd been lacerations or broken bones, Subaru would have put his Clind-brand first-aid skills to work, but unfortunately Julius had no such clear-cut external injuries.
Reid's attack had been unleashed with the singular aim of breaking Julius's spirit.
Of course, there was no doubt that this had accumulated damage in a form different from physical wounds, and letting him push himself was out of the question regardless.

So Subaru had resolved to carry Julius the rest of the way, roughly two hundred more steps, all the way down on his back — but—

"—No, I can't put you through that much trouble. If I'd stayed unconscious, that would be one thing, but fortunately I've woken up. I can manage stairs on my own."

"Don't be stupidly stubborn about it. Besides, puttin' up a front now is way too late. If you're embarrassed about people seein' you carried, everyone who was there already saw. ...Well, except Shaula, who was out cold, and Anastasia."

"Then that's exactly the reason. I can't let those two — especially Lady Anastasia — see me like this. Put me down."

"Don't say something so obviously tacked-on. In the first place—"

"—I said put me down!"

—The outburst came without warning.

"Whoa!?"

The instant that taut, strained voice struck his ear, Subaru's shoulder slammed into the stairwell wall.
The cause was Julius, on his back, forcibly wrenching his body. It was only because Subaru had instinctively turned toward the wall that he was saved — he could easily have fallen down the stairs.
But because protecting himself had taken everything he had—

"—Gh."

"You... you idiot! What the hell are you thinking!"

Leaning against the wall, Subaru turned to find Julius sprawled on the steps a little way below. He'd fallen from Subaru's back and slid some distance down the stairs.
Face-down, propped on his elbows, gasping painfully — the color of agony was thick on him. Clearly, it wasn't only the fall down the stairs that had caused it.

"Told you so! Hey, stay right there, idiot. I'm comin'—"

"Don't come here!"

"—————"

"...I can stand on my own. There's no need to borrow your hand."

Startled, the foot Subaru had rushed to send down the stairs stopped.
With his elbow still on the floor, Julius had thrown out a hand to stop Subaru from rushing over. He let out a deep breath, his profile stiffening, and somehow hauled himself upright.
Then, leaning his back against the wall, he slowly, slowly raised himself as if scraping his back along it, straightened his knees, and — leaning against the wall all the while — stood up.

"Just as I said, wasn't it? Standing on my own is nothing at all."

There was something discarded, cast-off, in the ring of that voice, and Subaru found no words came to him at once.
Julius turned his body around as he was, leaning the right half of it against the wall, and at a pace no faster than a crawling infant, began, sluggishly, to descend the stairs.
One step, then another, pressing down as if to test each one—

"It'll probably take a little time, but there's no need to trouble your hands with it. More than that, I'm worried about the women downstairs. It's a bit shameless of me to say this while shelving my own conduct, but I can't imagine you're the only one searching for me."

One step, and another.

"If you could, would you go on ahead and explain things to them first? Though, of course, the detailed account and my own excuses ought to come from me. All you need to do is tell them I've been found, and set their minds at ease."

Slowly, slowly, one step at a time.

"...I'll admit having to explain myself weighs on me. But it's a road I can't avoid walking. If you could smooth even a little of that rough road ahead for me, I'd owe you an enormous debt of gratitude — though you may well feel you've already lent me more than enough as it is."

Without turning his face this way, Julius, trying to descend the stairs alone, all by himself, kept talking.
Even at his sluggish pace, the gap between him and the stationary Subaru steadily widened.
It was a distance that could be closed in an instant, if he tried. Even to grant Julius's wish, he'd need to overtake him at least once. —So Subaru moved his feet.

"So I just go tell Emilia and the others first, right?"

"...Yes, that's right. If Lady Anastasia happens to be awake... no, never mind, let's not. In any case, I'm counting on you."

Hurrying down the stairs, Subaru caught up to Julius with ease. At the sound of footsteps echoing on the stairs, Julius let out a breath that resembled relief, urging Subaru to go on ahead.
—No, not "go ahead." What he'd been urged to do was "please go ahead."

"—————"

Subaru believed he understood, at least a little, what was in Julius's heart when he said that.
The reason he could understand it was the same reason that, unlike Emilia and the others, only Subaru had immediately grasped, on instinct, that Julius had gone off to challenge Reid—

It had to be something that resembled, somewhere, a feeling Subaru himself had once carried.
That was why, back then, Subaru had—

"—! Ah, damn it! Damn it, damn it, damn it! You idiot! Both of us, me and you, we're both huge idiots! Damn it all!"

Spitting the words out in irritation, Subaru kicked off down the stairs toward Julius.
Not to overtake him. He grabbed the left arm of the man leaning against the wall, his footing unsteady and faltering, and roughly slung it over his shoulder to support his body.

"Wha— Subaru, what do you think you're—"

"Shut up! What do you mean you can stand on your own! Your legs are shakin' so hard it's obvious from a mile off! There's no way I could swallow leavin' a guy like that behind and just walkin' off! Forget Emilia yellin' at me — I'd disgust myself!"

"But, I—"

"If I really didn't need to lend you a hand, believe me, I wouldn't. Both my hands are already full with all kinds of other stuff as it is. If you seriously don't want to lean on me, then don't go stumblin' around lookin' so pathetic that I can't stand it!"

"—————"

Spittle flying, Subaru shouted this out, and Julius fell silent.
The instant Subaru saw the strength Julius had used to try shaking him off drain away, saw him hesitate to keep resisting, Subaru forced the shoulder-lend to stay and started walking.
Once he'd set the pace like that, Julius had no room left to object.

"I'm not gonna talk like I know what's really down in your gut."

"—————"

"But right now, there's no need for you to walk down this staircase, this ridiculously long staircase, all on your own, all by yourself. I'll lend you my shoulder, and I don't even count it as a debt."

Talk of debts and favors was absurd, honestly.
If it came down to that, how much did Subaru himself owe Julius, in the first place?
The very first debt of all had surely begun back at that training ground at the royal castle.

—Subaru understood why Julius had thrown himself at Reid, knowing full well he couldn't win.
It was the same as back then — the same as Subaru back then.
Back then, Subaru had thrown himself at Julius even knowing he couldn't win. No matter how many times he was knocked down, no matter how many times he was struck, he'd gotten back up, undeterred, and kept challenging him.
Because there had been no other way to let out the raw, surging emotion welling up from deep in his chest.

And in that moment, at that place where everything had ended, that place where he'd broken with Emilia after their quarrel, Subaru had been left "alone" — and it had hurt. He'd wanted to cry.


—So there was no way he'd leave Julius alone, all by himself, on this staircase.


"—————"

The pit of his stomach burned. Just like back then.
Unlike back then, though, he had no idea where to let this surging emotion out.

"—Subaru."

"What."

"...I'm sorry."

"Shut up."

He answered, hoping it hadn't come out sounding like he was taking it out on him.
And so, slowly, the two of them descended the stairs together, making their way back to the fourth floor.

—It was some ten-odd minutes later that Emilia found the two of them and pressed a hand to her chest in relief.
