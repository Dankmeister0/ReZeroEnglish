Arc 4 Chapter 109: "The Wrong Choice"

"Just as I explained beforehand, we'll draw Garfiel out while getting the villagers evacuated. Natsuki-san said buying time would be enough, but I was thinking of being a little greedier than that... So, would you be willing to cooperate?"

After parting from Subaru, before setting off with the two decoy dragon carriages, Otto and Ram stood facing each other at the edge of the settlement, exchanging words.

At Otto's question, asked with a distinct lack of confidence, Ram — arms crossed, back against the wall — kept her eyes shut. Faced with Ram's continued silence, Otto grew impatient, all too aware of how little time remained before the operation began.

"Um, sorry... honestly, we don't have a lot of time."

"Men with no patience are disliked, you know. As I said before, it still depends on the terms."

Opening one eye, Ram flatly cut down Otto's pleading gaze.

The "before" she spoke of dated back three days from this moment. It was the night Subaru had proposed his gamble to Roswaal that Otto had first made his offer of cooperation to Ram.

When Subaru had come to visit, Roswaal had urged Ram to leave the room so the two of them could be alone. It was then, once Ram had stepped outside the house, that Otto — working in concert with Subaru — had made his approach. His read had been that if the matter touched on something as grave as the Gospel, Roswaal would keep Ram at a distance, and his prediction proved correct; he'd managed to catch her during that brief window when she was free.

Frankly speaking, three days ago the connection between Otto and Ram had been far too thin for comfort. The number of times they'd exchanged words could be counted on one hand, and to begin with, Ram held not the faintest sliver of interest in any man other than Roswaal unless something truly extraordinary was involved. Aside from those tied to Roswaal Manor, about the only people she exchanged words with in this Sanctuary were Ryuzu and Garfiel.

And so Otto had first had to build some kind of rapport with a woman who looked at him — a man merely trying to strike up conversation — the way one might regard a pebble on the roadside.

"Er, good evening, Ram-san. Fine night, isn't it?"

"――――"

"Um?"

"――Oh. I wondered who it was — the man who's always fawning behind Barusu. Without him around you're so faint an impression I couldn't even tell what kind of creature you were."

"Below human!? That one stings. No, I mean, I understand being seen as sort of an accessory to Natsuki-san, but..."

"There's no value in a man who's content being someone's accessory. Go away."

"Harsh!?"

Faced with Ram's utterly unapproachable attitude, Otto — seeking friendly contact — was already in tatters. She showed not a shred of interest in him, leaning her back against the doorframe with her arms crossed.

"Um, would it be alright if I talked with you a bit?"

"Whether I bother remembering or not is a separate matter, but if you're going to speak to someone, shouldn't you introduce yourself first? Whether I bother remembering or not is a separate matter."

"Why did you press the 'whether I bother remembering' point twice? ...Haah. My name is Otto Suwen. Just a humble traveling merchant — I'd appreciate it if you'd remember at least my face and name."

"That depends on how interesting the matter you bring is."

To Otto's feeble introduction, Ram refused to yield the initiative even a little. While adopting the posture of one willing to listen, she made it plain that a worthless topic would be cut off immediately. Otto steeled himself anew at his negotiating partner's unexpected angle of attack.

"It concerns the very matter Natsuki-san is discussing with the Margrave right now... Ram-san. Would you have any interest in cooperating with Natsuki-san and me to free the Sanctuary from its barrier?"

"――There's nothing to discuss there. Ram's wish is for Roswaal-sama's long-cherished desire to be fulfilled. If I respect Roswaal-sama's will, then freeing the Sanctuary by that method means nothing at all."

"But that's only if things proceed exactly as the Margrave envisioned... isn't it? You already know that things have strayed from that course, don't you, Ram-san?"

"――――"

It was Otto who, after discussing it with Subaru, had proposed drawing Ram into their camp. Subaru had resisted right up to the last moment, but from what he'd heard, Otto had judged it a wager with more than enough odds in their favor.

Opening her closed eyes, Ram fixed Otto with an emotionless, pale-pink gaze. Uncrossing her arms, her lowered right hand's fingers came to rest faintly against something — perhaps the wand tucked beneath her short skirt. Depending on his choice of words, he might well touch a nerve, and her anger would come wrapped in blades of wind.

Swallowing, wetting his lips with his tongue, Otto smiled the same fearless smile he wore when he threw himself into a deal. The stiffness drained from his body, and the rapid beat of his heart shifted into a pleasant rhythm.

Now then — just like always, it was time to haggle.

"A course diverged from the Margrave's intentions — one in which Ram-san's true wish could, for the first time, come to fruition. I believe Natsuki-san and I can help with that."

Otto watched Ram's fingers, which had been trembling as if uncertain, drift away from the wand — and judged that his sales pitch wasn't such a bad one after all.


Having heard Otto's proposal and learned the details of the plan, Ram set out several conditions.

Honestly, revealing the full scope of the plan to Ram was, for Otto and the others, a considerable gamble. If they misjudged the wavering of Ram's heart even slightly, everything they intended would be exposed to Roswaal. And yet Ram's change of heart was worth that risk.

Having drawn out the terms of her cooperation, Otto judged them not unreasonable and agreed. The finer details of those conditions, however, Otto did not share with Subaru — because to secure her cooperation to the fullest, the action they would ultimately take had to be kept secret from him.

If told, he would try to stop them. But if he let sentiment hold their actions back, it might well narrow the odds of the larger plan succeeding.

"You've got an unfortunate temperament. I don't know what your trade is, but you're not cut out to be a merchant."

"You definitely remember perfectly well that I'm a traveling merchant!?"

At Ram's dismissive snort in the face of Otto's secret resolve, a wail of anguish echoed through the sky of the Sanctuary.

Before the overwhelming intimidation of that ferocious beast, Otto found it strange that his body wasn't trembling.

The bizarre form standing before him was a savage beast with a colossal body easily surpassing four meters in length. Warped, razor-sharp fangs jutted like swords from a mouth too enormous, and its claws traced the vicious curve of a scythe — the kind a god of death might use to reap life. The golden fur covering its entire body looked like so much wire, each strand thick enough that a blade of middling sharpness might well get tangled trying to cut through it.

Only the glint of those golden eyes, the same color as the fur, seemed to retain any trace of the face from before the transformation — and the sheer paucity of anything else in common made the scene feel all the less real, as if it might vanish the instant Otto blinked.

Garfiel stood there, having shed his human body and bared the form of a ferocious tiger. A growl mingled with the rank breath leaking from its throat, and a bone-chilling pressure — the kind that reached down into one's very core — swept to the farthest edges of the forest.

"――――"

Otto tried consciously to force a smile to his lips, tried to crack some joke to distract himself. But his throat, frozen solid, released no sound, and even the cheeks that should have been smiling refused to obey his will, locked stiff.

And belatedly, Otto understood the reason his body wasn't shaking.

It wasn't that he felt no fear. Faced with the absolute presence of "death" itself, Otto's body had preemptively abandoned the very functions meant to keep him alive.

The trembling that fear brings is a defensive instinct — the body stoking the will to survive in order to keep the soul alive. It's a phenomenon that occurs so the body can call out to the soul, telling it not to give up. Place a person in a situation so hopeless that such a call serves no purpose at all, and it's only natural the body wouldn't bother trembling.

He'd heard the stories. So he'd at least imagined it. He'd heard that Garfiel, born of mixed Demi-human and human blood, had the ability to beastify.

But reality tore straight through Otto's flimsy imagination, manifesting a being beyond human comprehension and devouring his spirit whole.

Faced with this — with this presence — had he really said something like "buying time is fine, but it wouldn't matter if we ended up defeating him"? Of course it mattered. There was no way. None at all.

And yet—

"Garf's beastified... that means all the conditions have been met."

The small girl standing beside him spoke, her voice unchanged even before that overwhelming presence.

He couldn't even manage to ask what she meant. Even so, turning his head with a mechanical creak that seemed almost audible, Otto somehow managed to look at the girl — Ram.

Ram loosened her lips just slightly, showing Otto, for the first time, something that resembled a smile.

"Garf clearly made the wrong call. ――This fight is Ram's — ours to win."

"――――"

*You're kidding, right?* Otto thought inwardly, forgetting even his manners.


※※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※


――The conditions Ram had presented to Otto for her cooperation numbered, broadly, three.

"Roswaal-sama's ambition... that even straying from the contents of the Gospel, Roswaal-sama continues to hold onto some form of will to keep living in this world."

This one had come to fruition through Subaru's wager with Roswaal. Having once been on the verge of throwing everything away, Roswaal had donned his war paint once more, resolving anew — eyes brimming with vitality — to challenge the world.

"Separately from that, that Emilia-sama does not lose the resolve to face the Trials. If this world, having strayed from Roswaal-sama's intentions, is to continue regardless, then it's unavoidable that she'll have to stand and face them. ...Before the day of execution, we'll arrange a place to test that, on our end."

This condition had rested entirely on Emilia, and by extension on Subaru, who cared for her — but so long as Ram had at least been willing to hear the matter out, she seemed to consider it, within her own reckoning, satisfied. When Otto had heard that Emilia had vanished, he'd thought everything was completely over — but Ram apparently didn't see it that way. He hadn't pressed the matter deeply, fearing that asking for details might change her mind.

And then—

"If those two conditions are met, Ram will lend her hand to your side as well. ...Though to cooperate with the plan to be kept secret from Barusu, a few more detailed conditions are needed."

"If you'd let me hear them, I imagine I might personally see an angle for how to pull it off."

"If Ram is to join the fight against Garf, it'll only be after the odds of winning have been widened, even a little. First — seal Garf's sense of smell. Deal him a certain amount of damage, enough to strip away his composure. I won't lend a hand until it reaches a decisive moment, so get that far on your own. Given you're bringing me a plan like this, you must have at least one trump card up your sleeve, no?"

"That is... well, more or less."

"Naturally. Taking that into account, here's the last condition."

"Yes."

"Make Garf beastify. ――That is the final condition for securing victory."


※※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※


"His sense of smell — still crushed, I trust?"

"…………"

"Pathetic."

"Guh!"

At Otto, too cowed to answer even a simple question, Ram drove her elbow in without mercy. A sharp pain gouged into his side, and Otto, letting out a groan of pain, finally remembered to breathe, gasping.

"A-ahh... I thought I was going to die... just now, I thought the pressure alone would kill me...!"

"A thin, soft shield of flesh is still better than none at all — pull yourself together. His sense of smell — you crushed it, correct?"

"I'll let that unforgivable remark slide, but yes, it's crushed. Macoil pollen is an extremely potent pest repellent — inhale it up close and even a human would lose consciousness."

"All the more so for Garf, whose sense of smell is many times sharper. That neutralizes one of the advantages of beastifying. What's left is time, and the damage from before the transformation... both, as expected."

Letting out a light breath, Ram rose lightly onto her toes where she stood, then lowered her heels — repeating this simple leg exercise a few times. Otto knit his brows at her bizarre behavior. "Um?" he asked, tilting his head.

"R-Ram-san? What are you doing?"

"Warming up. Loosening the body a little before starting. Irritating as it is, Barusu was right — it's efficient."

"No, that's not what I meant... What is that kind of exercise even for?"

"Simple."

While Otto's voice still hadn't fully shaken off its tremor, Ram finished her up-and-down motion and closed her eyes. Then, opening them, she stepped forward — with a light, easy gait, almost as if heading out for a stroll — and began closing the distance to the giant beast before her.

"Wai—!?"

"Be quiet."

Otto's eyes went wide at her audacious action. But Ram rejected his voice, and without slowing her pace, advanced straight for the ferocious tiger. Lowering itself, glaring down at the prey before it, the tiger froze for just an instant, as if caught off guard by the small creature's behavior — but quickly judged it something like an insult to itself.

Fury blazing in its golden eyes, it reared back a foreleg now thicker than Ram's entire torso and lashed out, claws sweeping like a scythe. A single claw, thick as a slender girl's arm, carried enough force that a mere graze would turn the girl's limbs to mincemeat in an instant.

The wind howled as the claw-shaped scythe of the death god tore forcibly to wrench Ram's life from this world.

――An instant before—

"Too soft, Garf. ――Who do you think you're dealing with?"

Ducking, letting the mighty arm pass over her head, Ram spoke to the great tiger as if pitying it.

The tiger's body, its all-out swing having gone wide, staggered off balance, and Ram's small body slipped into the opening. Bending her knees, she cocked her fist as though stretching her whole body into the motion, and drove it upward—

"Has there ever, even once, been a time you beat Ram in a fistfight?"

"――――ッ!"

The fist that buried itself in the beast's torso launched a body weighing no less than several hundred kilograms straight up from below. The force of the blow bent its body double, and something like a pained groan leaked from the giant beast's slack, gaping mouth. A shockwave tore through the forest, and Otto heard the crack of bursting air against his eardrums.

"N-no way."

A girl who was probably a full head shorter than himself had struck, with her bare fist, a beast so massive that looking up at it still wasn't enough to take it all in. Without pause, Ram, fist still pressed against the tiger's torso, slammed her other hand into its body as well. Alternating blows rained onto the massive frame, and the great beast screamed and staggered backward. Baring its fangs, its powerful jaws lunged for Ram, but she spun aside, planting a foot on the tiger's snout and, using her leg strength, stamping the beast's face down into the ground.

Gouging the earth, its charge crushed, the great beast twisted its body, flailing limbs in an attempt to smash the girl toying with it — but Ram's body, dancing like a leaf caught in the wind, evaded the beast's movements as if mocking them, driving sharp blows one after another into the thin-furred spots on its body.

A heel to the throat. A thrust-hand strike to the flank. A fist punching through the back of a paw, and a kick landing square on the exposed face.

The sight of her overwhelming a ferocious beast through pure close-combat prowess gripped Otto's heart with the unreality of watching some illustrated legend unfold.

What, exactly, was he watching right now?

Seeing Garfiel cast off his human form, Otto's body had tasted despair deep enough to make it give up on living. And yet — what was this in front of him now?

Each time the girl swung her arm, the great beast's body reeled from the force of the blow, and a frame so massive its roar alone might kill a living thing could only flail uselessly, unable to land a single strike on the girl. Its thrashing arms mowed down trees at random, gouged the earth, violently reshaping the forest — but Ram paid no mind whatsoever to the ever-shifting footing beneath her.

*We can win. This — we can actually win.*

He felt foolish now for having doubted Ram's words before the fight began. Her declaration that she would deliberately make Garfiel beastify now carried unmistakable credibility.

Garfiel's presence as a great beast was overwhelming, but that trampling might of his was the sort best unleashed in a many-against-one fight. Of course, the difference in sheer mass was itself a weapon, and under normal circumstances he shouldn't fall behind even facing a single opponent. But when that single opponent possessed combat strength beyond human bounds, it was another matter entirely.

A thick, fleshy body became a giant target; a mighty arm capable of felling great trees became a weapon with enormous openings; and a strength massive enough to trample everything underfoot bred a sluggishness too lopsided for either attacking or retreating well.

Analyze the situation calmly, and one could see all this. But the reasoning that made it seem so easy to defeat only held once the premise of an "overwhelming individual" was already in place. Against a beastified Garfiel, securing this trump card — more effective than anything else, and hard to come by — for their own side was Otto's single greatest contribution to this battle.

"It's working... it's actually working! At this rate, Garfiel...!"

Clenching his fists, Otto cheered at the sight of victory finally coming into view. As if to bear out that hope, Ram's fist struck sideways across the great tiger's face, and the beast's body, kicking up a cloud of dust, went flying dramatically across the ground.

And then—

"――Buh."

Letting out a groan she couldn't suppress, blood pouring heavily from her forehead, Ram's body swayed violently.


※※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※


Feeling her vision dye red with blood, Ram fought with all her strength, her footing barely holding, to keep her body from collapsing.

A sharp pain shot through her like something driving in — just above her forehead, at the center of her head. From that point, where the faint trace of a white scar remained, agony radiated outward, and Ram clicked her tongue, feeling something detestable well up.

Behind her, she could tell that Otto had let out a shriek, his voice cracking. Noisy. That the situation had swung sharply in a bad direction was something Ram herself, more than anyone, understood perfectly well. It wasn't that Garfiel's attack had actually connected. A wild, thoughtless swing that telegraphed, riddled with openings like that — it would have been harder to get hit by it. The screaming was noisy. She wanted to smack him.

"――Hah."

Letting out a short breath, she kicked off the ground with legs threatening to give way. An instant later, the tiger's claw gouged and sent flying the spot on the ground where Ram had just leapt from. Clods of earth sprang up, and Ram, scattering fresh blood, danced through a spray of mud like buckshot to dodge.

A visible time limit had been imposed on her, but somehow, some way, her body still moved. She kicked the wide-open jaw of the tiger from below, and as it groaned in pain, followed up with her other foot against its face. Using the momentum of the kick to spin backward, her body, putting distance between them, lost its footing at the moment of landing.

"――Ah."

"――――ッ!"

A hairsbreadth escape. The claw, arriving before her delayed dodge, sheared off a lock of her hair, pink strands scattering through the air. This dodge had been the product of pure, complete chance. Had she not happened to slip, there was no doubt Ram's head, from the neck up, would have burst like a piece of red fruit.

Tasting the sensation of near-death up close, feeling something indescribable race up her spine, Ram let her red lips curl and gave a small, savage laugh.

The exhilaration of battle. The scar on her forehead throbbed, sending a sensation somewhere between itch and pain coursing through her whole body. A broken horn. The mark of an Oni. To draw out the full potential of Ram's body, the organ that is the horn must gather mana from its surroundings in limitless quantities. Having lost that organ, Ram's body could bring out barely a tenth of its original specs.

Forcing her body to this extent, she knew, would bring the backlash crashing down on her immediately.

Even so, she'd meant to arrange the conditions so that, so long as it stayed a short exchange of blows, she wouldn't lose.

"――You've gotten stronger, Garf."

Murmured softly, the emotion overflowing in Ram's voice was something she rarely let anyone else glimpse. It was the kind of emotion — one that Otto, and even Subaru, had only rarely seen — that she used to show toward blood kin now erased from her memory.

Still wearing that soft smile, the fist she drove forward crushed the great tiger's muzzle without mercy. The hard hide's texture snapped back against her knuckles; with the hardening effect gone, the impact rebounded directly into her fist, and she could feel the bones of her fist shatter. A pleasant sensation. She was fighting. She was alive. The exhilaration of a fight that might kill or be killed. An overwhelming euphoria dominated her entire body. More. More still. There was further to go. There was a dimension still further to reach.

Her right fist shattered, no longer able to even close into a proper fist. The tiger, its muzzle crushed, let out a roar. Its breath surged toward her from the front like a gale, and Ram split the wind with a left-hand blade strike, gouging into the thin hide of the tiger's throat. Blood sprayed; she tore free the flesh caught between her fingers, and fresh blood gushed out. Droplets scattered across cheeks turned starkly white, and the tongue that licked them up brought with it the sweet taste of iron and a wave of intoxication.

With her shattered right hand unusable, she wrapped her arm around the beast's neck instead. A throat far thicker than her own torso couldn't possibly be encircled by a single arm. Leaping, grappling, using her legs too, she went for the great beast's artery, dodging the claws closing in to tear her off. Putting her full weight into it, she snapped one of its fingers. Its shriek was pleasant — it felt like coming home.

"Buh, fuh, ahbuh."

Even while continuing so delightful a dance, her mind growing hazy irritated her. The bleeding from her forehead showed no sign of stopping, and now blood had begun flowing from her nose and mouth as well. With movements pushed past their limit, her body's collapse had begun. The flesh, unable to receive mana's reinforcement, simply couldn't keep pace with the potential of the nerves that governed Ram's body.

Still, Ram's body, moving with furious speed, continued to dodge Garfiel's claws. An overwhelming combat prowess that hadn't let a single blow graze her. And yet the malevolent hand of collapse was steadily eating away at her body, aiming unerringly for its end. Before claw or fang could reach her, the end would catch her first.

"――U, fuh."

Drawing a deep breath, letting it out deeply, an instant later something welling up spilled from her mouth in a great rush. A clot of blood struck the ground with a sound, and as if that expelled mass had been Ram's very vitality itself, all the strength drained from her body. Her shoulders dropped; her knees buckled. That instant, the great beast would not let slip a second time. Its face, battered and ragged from repeated blows, bared its fangs and swung an arm down at Ram's slender body.

But then—

"Uwaaaaah! Dona!!"

A cry rose in a cracking voice, an incantation, and an earthen wall thrust up from the ground as if erupting. It wedged itself between the descending tiger's arm and Ram's body, dampening its force for the briefest instant before being instantly shattered to dust.

But in that single instant, Ram's body was drawn in by the forceful motion, then thrown, with all the force behind it, backward.

――This felt like the second time, in a battlefield, she'd been thrown bodily by someone like this.

Flying through the air, gazing vacantly up at the sky, Ram found herself entertaining that oddly out-of-place sentiment. Then, striking the ground back-first and having the breath knocked out of her, she instinctively raised her head to grasp the situation.

In front of her — in the spot where Ram had just been — the one who'd thrown himself into the gap instead was Otto, who should have been trembling and curled up in fear. It seemed that, seeing Ram's condition turn strange and judging the situation unfavorable, he'd leapt in. Not that there was anything he could actually do—

"――――ッ!!"

The great beast, enraged that a powerless being had interposed itself in the fight between it and Ram, let out a roar that shook the forest, intimidating the pale young man. If he froze up, he'd become fang-fodder then and there, no question. In a moment demanding instant action, Otto — for whatever reason — clenched his fists, planted his feet against the ground, and—

"――――っ!"

Let out, from his own slender throat, the exact same roar the great beast had given.

It was the power of the Divine Protection of the Soul of Language he'd mentioned beforehand. The power he'd once boasted could let him converse with any sentient creature — down to Earth Dragons, small animals, even insects — had proven capable of communication even with a beastified Garfiel, a "beast that had lost its reason."

What Garfiel's roar sounded like to Otto's ears, no one could say. What Garfiel felt at Otto's roaring reply, no one could say either.

But the clash of roars, before claw and fang could be unleashed, produced the faintest of gaps. For Ram, that span of time was enough to make her realize the true intent behind Otto having thrown her away.

"――――ッ!!"

"――ッ! Argh! My throat's had it...!"

Unable to endure what was, quite literally, a scream fit to draw blood, Otto clutched his throat and coughed. Finding a punctuation to the exchange, the great beast reared its arm and aimed a sweeping blow at Otto. Facing it, Otto brought both hands up in front of him and, with a face set in resolve, shouted, half in desperation:

"El Dona!!"

A thick earthen wall rose up in front of Otto, and before it could even see how it fared, Otto leapt backward. As if giving chase, the beast's arm shattered the wall, kicking up a cloud of dust, and closed on the fleeing Otto's body — a direct hit, sending his slender frame flying as lightly as a dead branch kicked by an adult, plunging without stopping into a thicket of trees.

Whether he lived or died was entirely a matter of Otto's own endurance now. Ram spared no attention for the outcome of his choice and action. Judging that not doing so was the most fitting way to repay his effort.

"Garf. ――After all, beastifying was the wrong choice."

She spoke to the great beast, which — having sent Otto flying — was turning back to take aim at Ram next this time.

Leaning against the trees, Ram, freed at last from the euphoria of bloodlust, gradually regained her composure, and pointed the tip of the wand held in her barely-functioning left hand toward Garfiel.

The mana began to shift in nature.

"If Garf had even a scrap of judgment left, he wouldn't have let this chance slip by."

"――――"

At Ram's words, the great beast wrinkled its muzzle warily. But the tiger could take no further action. It didn't understand what was about to happen. To the eyes of a beast that had lost all reason, the meaning of Ram's figure — standing amid a white light — was beyond comprehension.

Having beastified and, in the process of assaulting Ram, thoroughly ravaged the earth, the trees, the forest itself, the great beast had become — to the forest's inhabitants — nothing more than an unrepentant, rampaging villain.

"Ram doesn't make the same blunder that man did."

"――――!"

Sensing something, the great beast lowered itself and charged at Ram.

Too slow.

"――Alfula."

The mana, gathered from the fury of the forest's inhabitants, took residence at the tip of Ram's raised wand, and light erupted.


――And so the battle in the forest of the Sanctuary came to its end.

Thinking he'd heard the roar of a beast, Subaru stopped and turned around.

"Nah, no way."

Far too soon, no matter how you looked at it — that was Subaru's simple impression. It had probably been about twenty minutes since he'd parted from Otto, whom he'd asked to buy time. It was doubtful whether Otto's dragon carriage had even departed yet; the situation couldn't possibly have progressed that far already.

He concluded that his anxiety was giving him auditory hallucinations, but that thought was, if anything, the more frightening one — a measure of just how much, unconsciously, he was dreading this situation.

"Believe, believe. Otto said he'd stick to buying time. I told him, if he gets caught by Garfiel, to come clean right away... and the people of Arlam Village haven't been told anything extra either, so they shouldn't become targets for attack."

By Subaru's reasoning, the reason Garfiel had attacked the villagers — and by extension Subaru himself, trying to flee outside — traced back to "the time he talked to Garfiel about Ryuzu's duplicates." In other words, Garfiel didn't want word of the duplicates carried outside. What reasoning that was rooted in, Subaru didn't know, but it seemed clear that unless he wiped out anyone who might know of it, Garfiel couldn't feel safe.

In an earlier loop, the case where Garfiel had massacred all the villagers besides Subaru was, most likely, because Garfiel had had no way of judging to which of the people accompanying Subaru's escape he might have leaked the information.

How much Garfiel would believe Otto's flat denial that he'd told anyone was a gamble, but there was a strange confidence that, argued sincerely, it would get through. Garfiel didn't seem the type suited to complicated thinking, so maybe the idea was simply to hand him a conclusion that spared him from having to think at all.

"From what Otto said, he'd secured Ram's cooperation, too. Worst case, unless things get really desperate, that guy shouldn't raise a hand against Ram..."

If there was a point of concern, it was the possibility of Ram provoking Garfiel more than necessary. The two of them, with their long history together, shared emotional baggage from the past that Subaru wasn't fully privy to. If that became the spark for a widening rift, things could take an unexpected turn.

Still, the reason Ram had gone along with this plan, this time, was surely so that she herself could pave the way for Roswaal to go on living in a world that had strayed from the Gospel's account. At least, that was how Subaru had interpreted it, and achieving that made cooperation with Subaru and the others indispensable. Even if she had complaints about how Otto ran things, surely she'd fall in line.

"I'm counting on you, sis. However unreliable Otto might be, please hear him out."

Pressing his palms together, Subaru offered his utmost prayer for the safety of the two of them. Unaware that, somewhere beyond his knowledge, the two of them had made a decision daring enough to cross the line between life and death on their own, Subaru finished his round of prayer, and—

"Well, then — time for me to play my own part."

Slapping his cheeks as if to fire himself up, Subaru shook his head and, steeling himself, stepped forward at last. The instant he set foot through the yawning entrance ahead, Subaru felt a sensation of floating through empty air — a nauseating feeling, as if his internal organs were rising up, coursing through his entire body.

"Ub..."

Pressing a hand to his mouth, Subaru forced down the nausea welling up and pressed forward. The floating sensation wouldn't fade. With every step, in stages, the feeling churned his insides further. His blood seemed to flow backward; an overwhelming discomfort, as if his eyeballs were being licked over by the very air, assailed him.

Tasting a rejection so total it felt as though the world itself, with all its might, refused Natsuki Subaru's presence, Subaru repeated deep breaths, put a hand against the wall, and dragged his feet forward, his face gone pale.

"C'mon, don't be so cold... it hurts, you know, even for me..."

He'd emptied his stomach beforehand, anticipating this. Even so, the nausea wringing at his insides demanded that stomach acid spill from the corners of his mouth. Forcing it down, prying his eyes open, Subaru pushed on through the darkness, crawling forward with all his desperate might.

And then—

"Ah, thank god. ――Finally found her."

Having crossed a short distance that had felt endless, agonizingly long, Subaru let his shoulders drop in relief.

In front of him, leaning against a weathered wall, sitting in a passage soiled with mud and dust, knees drawn up, a girl looked back at him with a vacant, dazed gaze.

"Suba, ru...?"

That her voice, however halting, had called his name — that alone satisfied him.

Then Subaru sat down beside the seated girl, and—

"Well then — shall we talk, Emilia-tan?"

The same opening line he'd once used, back when he'd been unable to fully own up to his own mistakes, Subaru now offered up again — this time, from his own initiative.
