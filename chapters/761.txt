Arc 10 Chapter 4: "You Are Welcome"

---

"Forget steam engines — what needs developing, and fast, is something like a magic stone engine."

Gripping the reins from the driver's seat, eyes fixed straight ahead, Natsuki Subaru said as much. At his words, Beatrice — perched snugly on his lap — furrowed her brow.

"...There you go again, saying something bizarre. What in the world is a 'magic stone engine,' I wonder?"

"It's tech this world doesn't have yet. Like, if we used magic stone energy to build something similar to a steam engine, I figure we could pull off a locomotive."

"A 'locomotive'?"

"Basically an iron dragon carriage that doesn't need an earth dragon and just keeps running as long as the magic stone holds out. It can only go where there are rails, so it's not very flexible, but once you've laid track somewhere, you can travel back and forth at a pretty good clip. What do you think?"

"What do I think? I suppose it sounds convenient enough — so long as I overlook how far-fetched it is, I wonder."

At Beatrice's answer, dripping with barely concealed exasperation, Subaru pursed his lips to voice his displeasure.

He thought it was a pretty solid idea, honestly, but without ever having seen the real thing, it was hard to get the image across. Then again, when it came to not knowing much about actual locomotives, Subaru was about on par with Beatrice. If only he knew a bit more about how a steam engine actually worked, he might have been able to make the case sound more realistic.

"——!"

"Whoa, sorry, sorry, Patrasche. I'm not trying to brush you aside — not after you came all the way to the Empire to fetch me. It's just a matter of using the right tool for the right job..."

"——!"

"Unfortunately, that excuse doesn't seem to be working on her, I suppose. If you put Patrasche in a bad mood, our trip home is only going to get further away, I wonder."

"That's a situation I want to avoid no matter what... Come on, cheer up—"

Even if he hadn't managed to precisely convey the image of a locomotive, she'd clearly understood that its role would compete with hers. Scratching his head at the protest from his beloved dragon, Subaru took the reins back up and narrowed his eyes at the unchanging sea of sand.

—Having sealed Al away at the Pleiades Watchtower, Subaru and the others had set out straight for the royal capital, determined to learn the facts that had come to light during the loop that never happened, and the circumstances they needed to understand.

The shock of Al's betrayal had hit them hard, and to swallow it down, the group had left the tower with a forced, almost overcompensating burst of energy — but the road to the royal capital that truly mattered was a long one.

After all, the distance was nearly the full breadth of the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica, from its easternmost point to its westernmost.

Even borrowing the running strength of the spirited, superb Patrasche, it looked to take a considerable number of days — and the more fired up they were, the more the frustration piled up.

Given that painful state of mind, it was only natural, in a way, that Subaru's thoughts would drift toward a kind of escapism — dreaming up new modes of transportation.

"If a locomotive or a train actually became a reality, I wonder how much travel time it'd cut down... No breaks needed, and it'd definitely move faster than an earth dragon, that much's for sure."

There was absolutely no room for it right now, but he thought that once things settled down a little more, maybe he ought to bring it up more seriously with Roswaal or Otto.

The coupled dragon carriages devised in the Empire were close in concept, but those, too, ultimately depended on the running strength of mounts like earth dragons, so a magic stone engine should be the more convenient option.

"If I could actually pull it off, I feel like it'd blow Abel's mind. Just picturing him grudgingly asking me how I did it makes it feel worth doing."

"If it involves magic stones, Betty might be able to help you think it through, but if the whole point is some petty desire to one-up that Abel, Betty won't lend a hand, I suppose."

"Yeah, fair. Wanting to see Abel's sour face was a pretty dumb reason. Forget I said it, forget I said it."

Waving away the vague image of Abel's scowl that had risen in his mind, Subaru rested his chin on top of Beatrice's head and let out a small sigh. Naturally, Beatrice became aware of the sigh above her, and then of the hard sensation against the back of her head — the black orb hanging from Subaru's neck.

"Subaru, that forbidden art—"

"It's the one you figured out for me. ...Sorry I can't explain it any better than that."

"It's not as if Betty's blaming you, I wonder. What's bothering Betty is the abnormal precision in how that magic was structured, and in how its targets were chosen... Betty could sense a trace of Mother in it, I suppose."

"Mother... Echidna, huh."

"...I suppose so."

Beatrice gave a small nod, and Subaru thought of the "Witch of Greed" he'd encountered in the Sanctuary.

As far as that infuriating face went, he'd also seen it during the Vollachian Empire's "Great Disaster," when he'd come face to face with Sphinx, who'd taken on her likeness. Given the depth and sheer number of scars the "Witch of Greed" had left behind, Subaru had lost all faith in the reliability of what had passed between them in the mausoleum.

What's more, the very existence of the "Witch of Greed" had been erased far too thoroughly, especially compared to the scars she'd left scattered across every corner of this world.

That wasn't something that had simply faded with the passage of time — it made far more sense to think it had been deliberately erased from history. The question was who had done it.

"Well, Echidna was dead, so it's not like she could've done it herself. Although if it turns out she's been sneaking into people's dreams and causing trouble even after death, that gets a little shaky too."

"That much shouldn't be possible, I suppose. Even a Dream Arts User skilled in Dream Arts shouldn't be able to freely wander into people's dreams after death, I wonder."

"Hm, a Dream Arts User? Dream Arts?"

"It's magic that uses dreams as a medium to interfere with someone's mind, I suppose. It's a type of Forbidden Arts too, and its practitioners were almost entirely wiped out, I wonder."

"That is definitely the kind of thing that has survivors! Interfering with minds? Sounds like whoever did the wiping-out just planted a convenient memory that says 'all taken care of!' while the real target got away."

"Y-you're being far too pessimistic, I suppose! ...Ahem, anyway, I wonder. The approach Mother used when you and Emilia encountered her in the mausoleum seems to apply the principles of Dream Arts, but strictly speaking it's something different, so she shouldn't be able to cross between the living the way one crosses between dreams, I suppose."

Subaru nodded along as Beatrice wagged a raised finger, lecturing him in her own adorable way.

He didn't understand the finer principles, but apparently Echidna's dream castle was, at its core, anchored to the mausoleum as a special place, and didn't extend beyond it.

That being the case, at least the worry that Echidna had done all of this entirely on her own was put to rest.

"But in that case, it means someone who inherited Echidna's will has been diligently working to cover things up, and the most likely candidate for that is Roswaal..."

"If it turns out to be an inside job, I suppose we'd have no choice but to swallow our tears and turn him in, I wonder."

"Which is exactly why I can't turn him in until the Royal Selection's over, and honestly, I'd rather not even after it ends. Also, it's a classic twist for entertainment purposes that the pretty girl act our Bea puts on in front of me is secretly a cover for scheming as Echidna's disciple behind the scenes, you know?"

"The plot where Betty turns out to be the final boss got scrapped ages ago, I suppose. It hasn't a shred of persuasiveness or consistency — just a lousy idea desperate to be a twist, I wonder."

"I think so too, but wow, you're really laying into it..."

She seemed to refuse to even entertain the idea of turning against Subaru, even as a joke in passing. He decided to take that, too, as proof of just how much she cared about him, and let his heart warm at it.

So then, assuming it wasn't Roswaal, and of course not Beatrice, who'd been going around erasing Echidna's traces—

"—the Witch Cult."

"—."

Beatrice shifted faintly and held her breath. Her tense reaction was proof that, deep down, she agreed with Subaru's murmured guess.

The "Witch of Greed" — no, not just information about Echidna, but every "Witch" bearing the name of a Sin other than the "Witch of Envy" seemed to be having her existence erased from the traces of history.

And the Sin Archbishops who'd all appeared at once in the floodgate city of Priestella had, each of them, made selfish demands of the city in exchange for the lives of its residents, claiming there was something they wanted. Some, like Regulus, had simply been out to satisfy their own desires — but mixed in among the rest were demands for things that hardly seemed like something the archbishop in question would actually want for themselves.

"And there's something Otto told me before. No matter where in the world it is, if something connected to a 'Witch' turns up carelessly, the Witch Cult comes to steal it away... something like that."

In fact, the Vollachian fortress city of Garkla, which Subaru and the others had visited, was said to have fallen into a state of ruin as a result of Regulus running rampant there. He kept ending up remembering someone he'd rather not remember, which put him in a foul mood.

Anyway—

"The rampage in Priestella feels like it backs up the rumor Otto mentioned. If that's the case, then their rampaging isn't just cult terrorism — there's another motive behind it. Maybe they're trying to hide the 'Witches' from this world entirely."

The Witch Cult worships the "Witch of Envy" and holds up reverence for her existence as its whole purpose.

Because that superficial piece of common knowledge had become accepted as fact, it might be concealing the truth — that they were working to erase the traces of every "Witch" besides the "Witch of Envy."

"So instead of hiding a tree in a forest, you hide a 'Witch' within the story of the 'Witch of Envy,' huh. ...But if that's true, then whoever created the Witch Cult—"

"—it can't have been Mother, I suppose."

Following the same line of thought as Subaru, Beatrice cut him off with a denial before he could reach the conclusion himself.

With her lips pressed tightly together and her face turned forward, Subaru couldn't see Beatrice's expression. But what was packed into those words was a prayer far too fragile and feeble to be called conviction.

Thought through logically, it should have been clear that Subaru's reasoning made sense.

But the fact that Echidna was her creator, a family member she'd spent precious time with, would never let Beatrice accept it.

Subaru understood that painfully well, and at the same time, it stirred his anger toward Echidna.

"Making my cute little Bea go through something this hard..."

The "Witch" who had ordered Beatrice to wait for "that person," leaving her behind in the Forbidden Archive for four hundred years.

Subaru knew that the true reason behind Echidna's instructions was a horrifyingly wicked curiosity — she'd simply wanted to see who Beatrice would choose as "that person." Of course, he could never tell Beatrice that directly. He had no intention of ever letting her know.

Denying the affection Beatrice held for Echidna just to overwrite it with himself — that was a vulgar, unseemly way of thinking. There was no need for that; Beatrice could go on cherishing Echidna just as she was, so long as Subaru became an even bigger presence in her life than that.

"Bea, I love you."

"—?! Did the conversation just take a sudden turn, I wonder?!"

Overflowing with affection and a sense of purpose, Subaru wrapped his arms tight around Beatrice from behind. At the embrace, her wistful expression flushed bright red. That's it — that's the one.

It wasn't as if this would let him sweep the conversation under the rug and leave it unresolved, but still—

"We can't get an answer here, not yet. We need to get to the royal capital and... huh, guess it all comes back around to that. Dammit, the sooner that magic stone locomotive gets developed, the better...!"

So he muttered, frustrated, clutching at the black orb hanging against his chest.

The road ahead was still long, and the royal capital they were making for lay far, far away—

And yet, Subaru's impatience would end up being resolved by unexpected means.

It began with—

"—Well, well. I'd about given up waiting. So you've finally come back, have you."

—starting with the surprise of being greeted, in the waystation town of Mirula reached after crossing the sea of sand, by a bald-headed giant with arms as thick as logs crossed over his chest — Old Man Rom, waiting for them there.

△▼△▼△▼△

"—It's a surprise that there are still survivors of the Giant Tribe, I suppose. Same as the Oni Tribe, Betty had thought their blood was all but extinguished by now, I wonder."

"Unfortunately, I myself have never once crossed paths with another giant. As it happens, I may well be the last giant left alive in this world."

"Seriously? That's way past 'countdown to extinction' territory."

Rom, the giant himself, answered Beatrice as she gazed up at his massive frame with open curiosity. Hearing this, Subaru frowned at the hopeless circumstances of the Giant Tribe.

After all, if the very last survivor was none other than the extremely elderly Rom, there was no hope of descendants, and extinction was the only path left. If the last survivor of the race had at least been young, there might still have been some thread of possibility left to hold on to, but—

"Bah, it stands to reason that a race that's fulfilled its role goes extinct. There's no logic in keeping the likes of the Giant Tribe or the Oni Tribe alive into ages yet to come. Every folk lives as it pleases and dies as it pleases... that's about the way of it, I'd wager."

"I dunno, that kind of... fatalism, I guess? I'm not real fond of it. Though I know saying that after it's already too late doesn't really do any good."

"Quite the admirable thing to say. Seems you've picked up a bit of thoughtfulness yourself, boy."

As Subaru poked the inside of his cheek with his tongue to express his frustration, Rom, cheeks twisting into a grin, clapped a huge palm on his shoulder.

In fact, this was the first time Subaru had properly talked with Rom like this since the very day the Royal Selection was announced, back when the two of them had honed their wits together scheming their way into the castle.

He'd heard Rom was serving as a sort of brain trust for Felt, but only now, meeting face-to-face and exchanging words after so long, did that fact finally feel real.

"Looking back, guys like you and Felt were people I ran into in this world even before Bea, so it's kind of moving. ...Though if I think about it too hard, I met the old fruit-shop guy before even Emilia-tan, so that kind of ruins it."

Honestly, once he started ranking out the order he'd met everyone, even Tonchinkan got thrown into the mix and his brain short-circuited.

Regardless, reuniting with old acquaintances was a joyful thing for Subaru — all the more so when it was someone he'd met on the very first day he'd been summoned to another world.

And so—

"—You're one of them too, Reinhard."

"Good. Your conversation with Rom-dono was going so well that I was sure I'd been forgotten."

"Forgotten? You? You've actually had that experience before?"

Surely not — Reinhard had a presence so overwhelming that even if the Authority of "Gluttony" devoured his "Name," there was no way anyone could forget him. Or perhaps, in his case, things would never even get to the point of his "Name" being eaten in the first place.

At Subaru's remark, Reinhard gave a wry smile and shrugged.

"Fortunately, I haven't had the occasion, not yet. Though that comes with its own weight to bear, in its own way. My own deeds can never be undone, after all."

As he spoke, Reinhard's gaze flicked briefly toward something behind Subaru — Garfiel, standing there with imposing dignity.

Barely bothering with a greeting for their reunion, Garfiel silently fixed his sharp emerald eyes on Reinhard. Bathed in that gaze, Reinhard narrowed his own blue eyes slightly and said,

"You seem to have grown a great deal stronger since before. That's quite something, in so short a time."

"Tch, easy for you to say. I've been leveling up the hard way, coming this close to dyin' over and over again, and I still can't even guess how deep your limits go."

"Won't you challenge me the way you did back in Priestella, then?"

"Ain't no path to winnin' if I try now. Besides, if I go off and lose on my own account, that ranking's just gonna end up causin' trouble for Emilia-sama."

Turning his face away with a click of his tongue, Garfiel forced down his own fighting spirit.

It wasn't overthinking on his part. In truth, given how far his skill as a knight fell short, it was Garfiel, as the Emilia Camp's military officer, who'd inevitably be compared to Reinhard. If word got out that Garfiel had lost to Reinhard in a head-on match, even though the ranking was already common knowledge, it could easily leave the impression that the camp lacked strength.

That much, Garfiel had worked out for himself, and so he'd held back.

"Garfiel, you've really grown up...!"

"Ahh?! What's your problem, boss, don't go pattin' my head! I just finished sayin' somethin' pathetic about not bein' able to win, didn't I?! Ain't no reason to be praised here!"

"There is, there is, there totally is! Hey, everybody, let's praise Garfiel!"

"R-rawrgh!"

Blushing red with shy embarrassment, Garfiel found himself surrounded by Subaru along with Beatrice, Petra, and Meili, all showering him with "there there, good boy" praise.

It was exactly the reverse of the pattern from when Subaru had been comforted back at the Pleiades Watchtower.

With Garfiel thoroughly doted on in that manner—

"—So, what business do the two of you from Felt-sama's camp have with us?"

The one to properly get down to the actual matter at hand was Petra, the most level-headed member of the group.

Seeing her small back, hands planted on her hips, brimming with the spirit of the camp's own negotiator, Subaru felt an unexpected surge of reliability, and his chest warmed.

Unexpectedly, it felt as though he could sense a fragment of the lost loop — just how desperately she'd fought to bring him back during the battle with Al.

"W-why are you making such a sentimental face, Subaru?"

"Nah, I think I might just be a little teary-eyed lately. Bea, will you hug me for a bit?"

"I-I suppose it can't be helped, I wonder. You really are a spoiled little thing, I suppose."

Though seeming reluctant about it, Beatrice, who'd been holding his hand, switched to clinging tightly to Subaru's waist instead. With that settled, Subaru turned his focus back to the conversation at hand.

Here, at last, was the reason Reinhard and the others had gone out of their way to wait for Subaru's group—

"Of course, we came to meet you. —A situation demanding urgent attention has arisen in the royal capital. Felt-sama, judging that Emilia-sama must be beside herself with worry, gave us her instructions."

"Emilia-neesama? But then why would Felt-sama..."

"The two of them have become friends. So Felt-sama said she wanted to lend a hand for her friend's sake."

"Oh my, so Felt-chan's gone and fallen for Emilia-oneesan too, has she~. I know just how that feels~. Maybe I could get along with Felt-chan too~."

That was Reinhard's answer to a puzzled Petra, and hearing it, Meili gave a mischievous laugh. Though, phrased the way she'd put it, she'd basically admitted that she herself had been won over by Emilia — Subaru wondered if Meili was even aware of that. He said nothing.

Besides, Subaru himself was surprised too. Of course, he'd never gotten the impression that Emilia and Felt were on bad terms, but the idea of Felt actually moving on Emilia's behalf...

"Can't imagine that happening back when she first stole your emblem..."

"Let me tell you now, boy, we'd rather that story not go spreading around in public. If you lot are going to go about scattering that kind of bad reputation, we'll have to resort to some countermeasures of our own."

"If there were nothing to it, sure, but that one's a mess of her own making... Well, we're not gonna go digging that up, since it's not just you two who'd be in trouble if we did."

To Rom's warning shot, more a formality than anything, Subaru just gave a shrug.

That whole emblem-theft incident in the royal capital tied back to Roswaal's scheme, having secretly hired Elsa to handle it. And since Elsa's business tied in with Meili standing right there, it was actually the Emilia Camp that would rather not have people poking around in it too much.

Besides, it seemed unlikely that Emilia would bring up the emblem theft at this late date anyway. All the more so now that the two of them had become friends.

"Honestly, there's even a chance Emilia-tan's just plain forgotten she ever had her emblem stolen by Felt... nah, that'd be pushing it, huh?"

"For my part, I choose to believe that Emilia-sama, having moved past that history, still wished for friendship with Felt-sama."

"Yeah. I like that version of Emilia-tan better too. But..."

The problem was the plain fact that Emilia had been driven into a state where she couldn't sit still.

Wondering just what on earth had happened in the royal capital, Subaru suddenly hit on the possibility that he himself was the cause of Emilia's current state of mind.

Maybe it was the effect of the "message" he'd asked Flam to deliver before leaving the Pleiades Watchtower—

"No, I've also heard about what happened with Priscilla-sama, and the matter with Al-dono. Emilia-sama's grief over it was considerable, but if anything, she was more worried about you and the others. So that isn't the direct cause."

"I see... that's good—well, not good, but I get it. But if it's not that either, then what the hell did happen?"

"—I suppose you could call it a matter that shakes the very foundations of the Royal Selection."

"—!"

At the weight in Rom's voice, every one of them changed expression.

A matter that shakes the very foundations of the Royal Selection — just hearing that made it clear how enormous a matter it was, and the fact that Reinhard didn't deny it either was proof there was no doubt at all in that assessment.

Whether that was because of the death of Priscilla and her elimination as a Royal Selection candidate, or whether there was some other cause entirely, he couldn't say—

"Regarding that matter, the various camps will need to discuss quite a bit among themselves. That's why I, having taken on the task of fetching Rom-dono, who'd remained behind in his territory, went straight on to come fetch you and the others as well."

"Then you'd want to get back to Felt in the royal capital as soon as possible too, wouldn't you..."

"Rachins and the others are in the royal capital. Following Felt-sama's orders took priority in this case. I truly did want to come all the way to the tower to fetch you, but I also wanted to avoid the risk of heading into the Augria Sand Dunes without Meili's help and missing you along the way."

"And so, that's the long and short of why we were waiting for you lot in this town."

Hearing Rom wrap it up that way, the questions in Subaru's mind finally settled.

So that was the circumstance behind it — Reinhard and Rom had been waiting here in Mirula for Subaru's group to arrive. Even as he felt grateful for that, he also found himself growing afraid of just what kind of problem could drive them to go this far.

Still—

"Can't exactly not ask, though. Time's precious. Let's talk while we move."

"Yes, let's do that. There's a great deal I'd like to hear from Subaru's own mouth as well — what happened in the Empire after we parted ways in Priestella, and so on, whatever you're able to share."

"There's nothing I can't tell you. I'll lay it all out. Besides, I've got plenty I want to ask someone carrying a name badge as big as 'Sword Saint' myself."

"—. If it's something I'm able to answer, then anything at all."

With a mutual nod, consensus was reached between Subaru's group and Reinhard's.

For Subaru, who'd felt frustrated enough with their pace of travel to go pining after a locomotive that didn't even exist, having Reinhard's strength to rely on wasn't just a stroke of good fortune — it was practically getting handed the Millennium Falcon.

And so, before setting off once more toward the royal capital—

"Oh, right, Reinhard."

"Hm? What is it?"

"You're probably not gonna get what I mean, but just hear me out. —Thanks."

At Subaru's words of thanks, Reinhard, who — just as forewarned — surely hadn't understood the meaning, raised his eyebrows. Subaru's lips curved into a smile at having caught the "Sword Saint" off guard, and he went on,

"I don't know the details myself, but I'm just dead certain I put you through some serious trouble. So, thanks."

"With nothing to go on, I hardly know how to respond."

"Then just go ahead and say 'you're welcome' or something."

"Understood. —You are welcome."

"Damn, your pronunciation's really good."

Grateful all over again to Reinhard for responding that way even to such an unexpected thank-you, Subaru stroked the head of Beatrice, still clinging to his waist, and began preparing to move out.

Since Reinhard had come rushing over a distance that should have taken days, covering it all in one go, he was probably about to be hit with some outrageous method of travel. Better brace himself mentally for it, Subaru thought.

"—."

Unaware, all the while, that Reinhard's eyes — his hand resting on the hilt of the sword at his hip — were fixed on Subaru's back as he made preparations to depart with his companions, watching him as though taking his measure.
