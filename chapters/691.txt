Arc 8 Chapter 72: "Sun Princess"

"—You have your uses, it seems. You'll make yourself useful — for the sake of fulfilling my ambitions."

That was the first thing that man ever said to her, and it never once changed, right up to the very end — the symbol of the relationship between herself and that man.

She had spent several decades together with that man.
In all that time, she had never been granted freedom; she had been all but imprisoned, chained in an underground cellar.
There had been caretakers to keep her from dying, but the cautious man had periodically silenced and replaced them, so that man alone could be said to have had contact with her across those decades.

—Leip Barielle.

A noble of Lugunica, the Kingdom Beloved of the Dragon, and, to put it plainly, a man ruled by an ugly ambition.
At the time of the Demi-Human War, House Barielle had held the rank of Viscount and been called into service, but in the midst of that civil war, they were made to answer for a defeat, stripped of their title, and demoted to Barons.
That humiliation and anger must have been the driving force behind Leip's actions — but if so, just how long could a human's rage and hatred go on burning?
Leip was forever chewing over that decades-old humiliation as though it had happened only yesterday.

"In time, you will be made to serve me — without fail. You live only because I allow it. Never, ever forget that."

Among the humans she knew of in her stored knowledge, there was something called affection.
The kind where, even with someone whose personality or relationship one would never willingly choose, spending ten or twenty years together would soften one's attitude, thawing the thorns of how one dealt with them.
But Leip had none of that. From him, she always felt a hostility as fresh as the day it began.

Not once had he ever spoken a warm word to her.
She knew nothing of Leip's personal history, his family, or any such matters concerning House Barielle.
Day after day, she passed long, quiet, idle hours as a captive, hearing only — on the rare occasions Leip visited — of the kingdom's present circumstances and of the unchanging days of lying in wait.
Only once did she and Leip ever speak of something different.

"You conspired with those demi-humans and drove the civil war. What, exactly, was your aim?"

More than twenty years had already passed since the Demi-Human War — it was far too late a subject to be raising now.
Valga and Libre, who had fought alongside her, were already dead, and by then most everyone who could be called a key figure of the Demi-Human Alliance was surely gone as well — it was at such a time that Leip voiced that question.

She did not ask why he suddenly wished to know.
She had a feeling that asking in return would touch Leip's temper and cut the topic off entirely. So she said nothing unnecessary, and since she felt no need to hide her purpose either, she answered plainly.

That her cooperation in the Demi-Human War had been for the sake of fulfilling the purpose she had been created for.

"—How pointless."

Upon hearing that the Demi-Human War had been one part of the trial and error toward a created purpose she had pursued for centuries upon centuries, Leip spat out those words with terrible ease.
That reaction of Leip's stirred nothing like anger or sorrow in her. She had, after all, always been that sort of being, and his reaction was, if anything, exactly what she'd expected.
To this ugly, ambitious man, surely every wish besides his own was worthless.
But—

"Though you were born as yourself, you wish, of all things, to become someone else? To wear another's name and way of life like a mask, and let your own name die?"

The conversation she'd thought cut short and finished by that one dismissive line went on after all.
As if to say it was, from the bottom of his heart, an idea worth spitting on, Leip turned toward her eyes clouded with negative feeling, more irritated than she had ever seen him.

"Your purpose is worth nothing. In the end, you exist to be used by me. Even fulfilled, it's a wish without a shred of value. If it's to go to waste anyway, hand it over to me."

"You—"

"A 'created purpose' — how pointless. If you can't live without someone else's instructions, then you may as well be used for my ambitions."

There was not a trace of righteous anger or compassion in Leip's eyes as he said all this.
What dwelt in Leip's clouded eyes was rage at those who had dealt him a humiliation that never faded, resentment and hatred toward an age and a world that refused to acknowledge his worth, and an ambition like a black flame, vowing that he would surely raise himself to the position he deserved.

It was terribly violent, as though trampling her underfoot, with the roughness of being doused in filth — and it truly did drive a blade into the being that was herself, tearing her open, drawing cold blood.

—To fulfill the purpose of her creation, given to her by her creator.

That alone was the meaning of her life, the purpose she had walked toward for so long.
But had she ever once felt a burning heat for the sake of that purpose? No. Not once had she ever held any passion for it. What ruled her was a heatless inertia and compromise — the sense that, having been given something, she must simply carry it out because it had been given.

But surely, in truth, isn't this what a wish, a desire, is supposed to be?
To fiercely crave something, to strive to make a wish you long to fulfill come true — isn't this the right way for it to be?
Wasn't the very way Leip Barielle existed the correct method for making a wish come true?

"—————"

More long years passed. Through it all, her relationship with Leip did not change.
They exchanged few words, and no occasion ever came for the use he'd promised to make of her, the service he said she'd render — time simply flowed on, moment by moment.
And then, at last, something changed.

"At last, it seems my chance has come around."

So said Leip, his eyes gleaming with a mad brilliance — though his ambition had not withered, his face and frame had been eaten away by an old age he could not resist, wasted thin.
And yet, in the figure of him declaring that the time had come, there was a joy at last rewarded — the joy of one who had forbidden himself even to blink, determined without fail to seize the thread dangling before him.

As she came to learn, over these past decades Leip had wormed his way into the favor of the Lugunican royal family and been entrusted with the management of a prophetic tablet called the Dragon History Stone, bestowed upon the kingdom by the Divine Dragon.
Leip had buried the truth carved into that tablet — a calamity soon to strike the Kingdom of Lugunica, a plague to spread among the royal family — and had instead set his ambitions ablaze for the battle over succession to the throne that would follow.

"I will find a candidate before anyone else. I will take her as my wife, send her into the Royal Selection... and without fail, seize the throne. You, too, will be made to serve."

Clenching a fist of nothing but skin and bone, Leip declared this with fierce resolve.
Now that "make yourself useful," the phrase she had been told over and over for so long, had come attached to something real, even she — chained for decades — felt a small stirring rise within her. She had — hoped.

The next time he came, Leip brought with him the candidate he had found and ordered the preparation of a technique to break her heart. He would fashion the candidate into a puppet and rule the next kingdom with his own two hands.
That he could scheme so brazenly, plot to make it real with such extraordinary tenacity, and in fact bring it right up to the edge of realization — Leip's ambition was, in every way, darkly dazzling.

She wanted it fulfilled. She wanted it carried through. Just as that passion demanded.
She wanted him to fulfill that ugly ambition everyone would look away from, to use the kingdom exactly as his desires dictated, to use even the Witch herself as a stepping-stone for his own wish, and see his obsession through to the end.
She wanted him to show Sphinx that this — this — was what she had been lacking.

And so she waited. She kept on waiting.
She looked forward eagerly to the moment Leip would bring the candidate for the Royal Selection — the wife he had married for his own use — and break the heart of the woman offered up to him.

She waited, and waited, and kept on waiting, and Leip never came.
To learn the reason he never came, she undid, on her own, the bonds that had gone unloosed for decades, and stepped outside.

And upon learning the reason Leip never came, she understood, for the first time.

"—Ah."

—This, this was the "heat" of wanting to accomplish something.

"Countermeasure required— no. Go on and stop it, if you can."

The trump card, the secret weapon, the hidden trap of the Sphinx who had spoken those words — all of it, overturned.
Reflected on the wavering surface of the water mirror was the desperate fight of those who, one after another, were overcoming the schemes the Witch had laid, driving off the empire's supposedly certain doom.

"—————"

Narrowing her crimson eyes, Prisilla, bound in chains, looked from the mirror's surface to Sphinx's pale face.
Yearning, drowning, Sphinx had placed the greatest possible burden upon herself in her drive to see her own wish fulfilled. What that wish entailed was surely a hateful thing to the world, to the empire, to Vincent — but to Prisilla, it was not, on the whole, something to be despised.

It was not that she wished for the ruin of the empire and the world, or for the collapse of the ceaseless effort of her own elder brother, Vincent.
It was only that anyone who threw everything they had into what they wished to see happen was, without exception, beautiful.
Even one who directed at her a hatred close to unwarranted resentment was no exception to this.

Everything has what suits its station.
One who does not know the size of their own vessel and craves more than it will hold usually meets ruin. But Prisilla had no love for those who, speaking wisely of having known their proper place, consoled themselves with having filled themselves only with what fit within their vessel.
She wanted to love the way of life of the fool who sought what did not suit their vessel, who walked a road that might lead to ruin, who scorched their wings reaching for the sun — to love it regardless of whether the challenge succeeded or failed.

"How lovely."

Every last soul risking their life on that battlefield would surely doubt their own ears at Prisilla's words.
But it was Prisilla's true feeling, without exaggeration. To begin with, Prisilla had no taste for scheming against or deceiving others in word or deed. She wished no such way of living upon herself.
And so, it was praise from the very bottom of her heart.

Everyone on this battlefield who took up arms, lent their shoulder to another, and scorched their soul while bleeding — all of them, regardless of whether they lived or died, seemed dear to her.
If it could be granted—

"—How unlike me, such sentimentality."

*Beautiful.*

It was something she'd copied from her brother, who had done it since childhood whenever he fell to thought. Vincent, who understood all too well the weight of the duty laid upon him, would not close both eyes even in sleep.
That stance was admirable, but overdone. —Sometimes there are those one can only meet behind closed eyelids.

"Brother, dear, you ought to find someone with whom you can close both eyes while still in their company."

Even while paying respect to the way Vincent had, blade of the Sunlight Sword in hand, once burned away the scheme of the Witch leading the Great Disaster, Prisilla spoke thus.
Then, within the water mirror, Prisilla's eyes fell on those who stood out above all others.

—Aldebaran and Natsuki Subaru.

Her plucky fool of a jester, and the maddeningly endearing half-witch knight whose name stood among the Royal Selection candidates.
Just as Sphinx recognized them as a threat, Prisilla's eyes too understood that their desperate struggle had bent some destined star that was no ordinary thing.

"You two — what exactly are you deceiving? The Witch's... no, heaven's?"

Sphinx, who led the Great Disaster, had laid schemes upon schemes that were no half-measures.
The army of the Undead conjured through the Sacrament of the Immortal King; the use of the Rock Mass Muspel to make it possible; her return as the Witch of Greed, having once slipped past the flame of the Sunlight Sword; the murderous intent turned on Arakiya, whose fate had become bound to the empire's very earth; the starlight and the Magic Crystal Cannon; the subsequent runaway of the Magic Core, the unfurling of the magic circle meant to exploit the magic crystal stone, the staged crisis at the Fortress City—.

Who could have foreseen that a scheme this thoroughly prepared would be crushed, every last piece of it?
It was something impossible even for the Wise Emperor Vincent Vollachia, even for his right hand the White Spider Chisha Gold, even for Prisilla Barielle herself, of all people.

The ruin of the Vollachia Empire should have been unavoidable.
If not for those who broke through that dead end of fate with an impossible power.
And so—

"—One more move."

Watching the scene floating on the mirror's surface, Prisilla's lips wove out those words.
The vast water mirror that had formed over the imperial capital projected the dire straits of the far-off Fortress City, meant to break the fighting spirit of those in the capital. Starlight descended upon the Fortress City, seeking to destroy it as if mocking those fighting desperately there — only for that flame of ruin, once swallowed by the void, to snuff it out.
The starlight was cancelled out by the capital's own proud Magic Crystal Cannon, and the water mirror shattered.
At last, it seemed that every scheme the Witch had prepared had been broken through.
But—

"Not yet."

Even with the Fortress City's starlight snuffed out, the Witch's countless schemes were still not exhausted.
The Witch, watching with her own eyes as the great calamity she had prepared was warded off, used the very fact of its being warded off as a stepping stone, and set a trap.

—The water mirror shattered in the sky above became raindrops, pouring down over the entire imperial capital.
Like a passing shower, it soaked the capital, its droplets striking the living and the dead alike.
And it was a blade of water, harmful, disguised as harmless.

Slowly, the droplets sliding over the skin of those struck by the rain began to tremble, becoming blades. It was a danger bearing down equally on everyone except those who had been indoors, or the transcendents who had instinctively avoided the rain.
It was, by now, a fatal flash there was no longer any way to guard against.

"—————"

And so Prisilla closed her eyes.
She did the same thing she had just been thinking about, regarding Vincent. —Schult, Heinkel, Emilia, Crusch, Felt, Anastasia, Serena, Arakiya — in the darkness behind her eyelids, Prisilla saw face after face, soul after soul.
Vincent was there too, and Lamia, and Rem, and Aldebaran as well.
Every one of them, someone lacking just one more move.

"—The world is made to suit my convenience."

So Prisilla murmured, and Sphinx's motion, as she moved to make the rain-blades flash, stopped short.
She looked at Prisilla through the water mirror, her black eyes wide open.

In the next instant, the alien space made to hold Prisilla captive collapsed. —Burned away, unbearably, by the scarlet flame brought forth by the Sun Princess imprisoned within it.

The instant the light of the star gleamed above the Fortress City, everyone who looked up at the sky lost their words.

"—————"

It happened just after the fact that they had succeeded, moments before, in defeating an assailing Blight Dragon, driving off a formidable foe as one, had raised the morale of every soldier braving the siege to its very peak.
But before word could spread to everyone that the Undead army's greatest fighting force had been destroyed, that beautiful annihilation came bearing down from overhead, and many forgot the beating of their own hearts.

Of course, even under such circumstances, there were those who tried to exhaust every last bit of strength they had.
The Spirit Knight wrapped in polar light; the women warriors who knew nothing of losing their fighting spirit; the soldier harboring an Insect within himself, and the giant frame armored in gold; the war band gathered beneath the light of their own star — all of them too.
Even the wise ones without the strength to fight raced through countless thoughts in the merest sliver of time, begrudging not a single second in trying to shatter this desperate, hopeless crisis.
And yet, even so — just as a swarm of ants cannot stop a giant's foot — everything should have ended trampled underfoot, everything mowed down.

—Until the flame of ruin that came flying from the far sky collided with the light of the star, and the world flashed white.

"—————"

No one could grasp, in that instant, what had happened.
Only this much was clear: the retinas of those who had tried not to look away had been seared white, and before their sight returned, the annihilation that could have rained down at any moment — did not.
And then—

"—!"

The white of the star and the red of the flame, the two lights mingling, and a shockwave blanketed the sky above the Fortress City.
A beat later, a raging wind whipped up and scattered in every direction, shaking even the city's sturdy ramparts, blasting living and dead alike off their feet.
On top of that, the ferocious wind even split cracks into the great mountain the city bore on its back, and the rock face, giving way in collapse, peeled loose — a great falling boulder swallowed a corner of the great fortress with a thunderous roar.
And that spot was, as ill luck would have it, the very place designated as the aid station where the wounded were carried—.


"—Rem! Hey, Rem!"

Called by that desperate voice, Rem blinked several times.
For a moment her thoughts had been flung off into a white beyond, and it took her time to grasp what had happened.
But she quickly recognized the voice frantically calling her as Katya's, and remembered that the aid station had been caught in a collapse from falling rock.
Seeing the cracks spreading across the wall and ceiling, Rem had instinctively shoved Katya aside, into the collapse.
And yet—

"Am I... alive?"

"Of course you're alive! Seriously, cut it out! If you died protecting me, this time my heart would really, truly die..."

Buried in rubble, Rem, having confirmed she was still alive, was struck by Katya's tearful voice. That forceful grief was cut short by the sight of Rem pushing aside the rubble on top of her own body.
She must have been startled — the rubble that had piled onto Rem was considerable in weight, not something even Rem's strength, apparently that of the Oni Tribe, could easily push aside.
And yet Rem had been able to do it, because—

"R-Rem, your... your eye!"

Pointing at Rem's face, Katya let out a voice that cracked like a scream.
What Katya was pointing at was her own left eye, and Rem guessed that a flame must be burning there. Because the same flame was burning in Katya's eye too.
—No, not only Rem and Katya.

Everyone else who had been in the aid station, everyone besides Rem and Katya caught up in the collapse, rose to their feet with no more than scratches, without any serious wounds.
And in one eye of every single one of them, a flame could be seen flickering and burning.

That flame, that welling strength, was what had granted Rem her power.
And that was—

"—Lady Prisilla?"

Feeling the painless heat of the flame that dwelt in her own eye, Rem murmured that.
She had no proof. She didn't know the reason. But as to whose flame this was, Rem followed her own heart, and was certain.

"Katya-san."

"W-what!? Water... it's water, right!? Quick, quick, we have to put it out with water—!"

"No, I think this is a fire that mustn't be put out. Besides—"

"Besides?"

To Katya, flustered and confused, Rem, now on her feet, found the overturned wheelchair, and, since it had narrowly escaped being broken, lifted Katya up and set her in it.
Then she exchanged glances with the people rising to their feet throughout the aid station, and nodded.

"I feel like I'm being told something. —That we've still got one more push in us, don't we."

—The water mirror shattered, and from a sky scattering droplets, a crimson woman came falling.

Everyone, their awareness stolen by that overwhelming presence, looked up at the sky burning with the sunset.
And naturally, the Witch was no exception.

"—Prisilla Barielle!"

At this moment, Sphinx the Witch, manifested in forty-four bodies by taking in part of the vast mana released through the breakdown of the magic crystal stone via the incomplete magic circle then in operation—every single one of them turned their palms to the sky, and light was born around the falling Prisilla.
But—

"No looking away allowed, and no touching the dancing girl either!"

"How careless. That there's a bad move."

Transcendents who did not let that moment slip past interfered, cutting forty-four down to thirty-six in one stroke.
Even so, orbs of light — each one more than threat enough on its own — were gathered all at once toward Prisilla. A lethal attack from every direction, moving to snatch away the sun that had escaped its captivity—but—

"I won't let you, oh no I won't!!"

In answer to that cry, thick with the color of deep affection, the writhing streets of the imperial capital shot out like thrown spears.
They blocked the depth-charges of light trying to reach Prisilla in midair, and the explosions and blast winds born one after another colored the sky while protecting her.

"Match me, Beatrice!"

"As if you needed to tell me, I suppose!"

Piercing through the blasts of light, around Prisilla, still falling, a violet-indigo brilliance was born. It was a disc-shaped crystal, and the superlative teamwork of spirit and mage deflected the encroaching orbs of light, becoming a great shield that protected Prisilla, protected her, protected her through to the end.
But—

"Not yet!"

The Witch cried out with feeling, as if coughing up blood.
Immediately after, the disc, struck fiercely, groaned, and at last the cracks spreading across it widened, and it was shattered all at once.
The Witch, her white hair streaming, who had done this, rose into the sky, and moved to shower Prisilla — falling now with no defense left — in a light that would tear life apart.

"Nnngh!!"

"I won't allow it!!"

Into the torso of that Witch, who could see nothing but Prisilla, a golden-haired girl who had teleported from the ground and a Deer Human girl who leapt with enough force to shatter the Crystal Palace both drove a blow at the same instant.
The Witch buckled, and the beam of heat she loosed missed its mark.

"Bait required."

Even as the buckled Witch was shattered by fragments of earth, she said even this was part of her scheme.
In that instant, the multiple Witches on the ground clasped hands together, and — possible only because each was the very same being — with perfect synchronization, shortened the construction of the spell formula, and a great storm was born.
It became raging water, wind, and light, and swallowed up Prisilla, there in the sky.
In the next instant, Prisilla's wretched form, torn utterly to shreds—

"Icicle Line—!"

The storm of destruction burst from within, and what appeared next was not Prisilla torn apart, but a flower of ice, blooming in the heavens as if to protect her, or perhaps to adorn her.
The beautiful, great-blossomed petals sealed the light away, keeping it from ever reaching Prisilla.
And toward Prisilla — who had refused every last one of the countless deaths, destructions, and endings bearing down on her — a shadow came fiercely closing in from the ground.
It was—

"—Princess!!"

It was Aldebaran, who had heaved up the ground of the imperial capital beneath his own feet, stretching it into the sky as a crooked, misshapen pillar of stone and earth, closing in on Prisilla.
The pillar stretched on and on and on, and the distance between the falling Prisilla and the rising Aldebaran shrank. It shrank. It shrank, and shrank, and at last — became zero.

"—!"

Atop the unsteady pillar, his outstretched right arm forcibly caught the falling Prisilla in his hold. Right at the point where the two of them might well have fallen together, Aldebaran locked both feet firmly onto the pillar's tip as his footing, and, staking his life on it, protected Prisilla from the fall.

At that desperate act of Aldebaran's, Prisilla narrowed her long, slanted eyes, and—

"Well done."

At those few words from Prisilla, Aldebaran bowed his head, his heart overflowing with feeling. Then he raised his face, his voice trembling,

"Princess, Princess, my princess...! At last, again... ow!!"

"Fool. Who said I was yours?"

Prisilla rapped the head of Aldebaran, who seemed unable to hold himself together, hard with the hilt of the Sunlight Sword. With enough force to dent his iron helmet, Aldebaran instinctively crouched down. But with one hand still supporting Prisilla, he couldn't even rub the spot she'd struck.

To that pitiful Aldebaran, Prisilla gave a small "hmph" through her nose, and said—

"Still, for someone like you, you've done well. I'll grant you my praise."

"T-that's an honor, real grateful for it... What about you, Princess? You okay? Hurt anywhere? Did they rough you up somewhere? Actually — for someone who was held captive, aren't you a little too spotless?"

"Don't pile foolishness upon foolishness. As if my beauty would falter merely from having been held captive for some time. Mind your tongue, Al."

"—————"

Held in his arms, told this by an exasperated-sounding Prisilla, Aldebaran — Al — held his breath.
Then, once more, he confirmed the reality of the Prisilla before him, and nodded deeply. At that reaction of Al's, Prisilla, still held in his arms, gazed around them, and said—

"There now — the star has made her entrance. The rest of you had best raise a grand cheer."

In Prisilla's field of view as she said this, the scene visible from atop the stone pillar held the forms of many who had staked everything on this battle, and in every one of their eyes, a flame was burning.
In the eyes of those Prisilla Barielle deemed worthy of her love, the flame of the soul burned.
And that, of course—

"Princess?"

Right beside her, she lifted with a finger the cheek-guard of the helmet Al wore, and peered inside. —In his right eye, the bare face he showed to no one but Prisilla, a flame was likewise burning.

Seeing that, Prisilla smiled, in high spirits, and said—

"It's nothing at all. —After all, this world is made to suit my convenience."
