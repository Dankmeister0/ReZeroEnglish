Arc 7 Chapter 102: "The Wall of Resolve"

——Todd Fang did not think of himself as being particularly cautious or thorough.

By single-mindedly pursuing what was simply "obvious," he closed off every loophole he could think of, covered as many of his available options as possible, and thought about things as pessimistically as he could in order to minimize mistakes.
Even after doing all that, if an opponent had prepared some scheme, action, or trump card beyond the scope of his imagination, he'd be finished off without even being able to lift a finger.
That much, he understood, was the compromise between the ability he'd been born with and reality itself.

Fortunately, he had never yet run into such a being — or, when he had, he'd managed to avoid any decisive confrontation — and so he had survived to this day.
That said——

"——Lately, my luck's just been rotten, plain and simple."

Looking back on the short span of the past month or two, Todd lamented his own fate in just those words.
The bad luck had started when he'd been assigned to a unit dispatched from the imperial capital to the eastern lands. Just when he thought being torn away from his fiancée Katya was bad enough, along came that worst of pickups.

The man and woman who'd been swept down the river near their campsite — never mind the woman, but the man could fairly be called the trigger for every misfortune that had befallen Todd since.

As stated before, Todd never once thought of himself as "special," but that man had come completely unhinged on that front. He was mediocre, and yet abnormal.
They'd exchanged few words and spent little time together, but the man himself seemed utterly unaware of it.

Both before and since, Todd had seen any number of people who could be called exceptional.
Every one of them seemed to have some awareness that their own existence lay outside the bounds of the ordinary, that they had no choice but to walk a path all their own — but among them, that man alone was the exception.
That was what made him dangerous. So Todd had resolved that, if the chance ever came again, he would kill the man no matter what.

But once it became clear that goal wasn't going to be realized, Todd abandoned his kill-on-sight policy at once and chose instead to keep his distance from that walking plague god. Not getting involved was best.
Sacrificing Jamal, who'd served as his umbrella against the rain, to force through the rescue of the captive Arakiya had been part of that same plan. If he could win the favor of Arakiya, who possessed far greater strength and authority, he could escape the unwanted position he'd been placed in. ——That scheme had missed the mark by a wide margin.

For the sake of avenging Jamal, a task he'd never even wanted, he ended up leaving Katya behind in the capital after all, and while making moderate use of Arakiya — empty-headed, without a shred of self to call her own — found himself thrown headlong into the vortex of the great rebellion against the Emperor that had erupted across the whole of the empire.

Nothing, absolutely nothing, was going his way.
It seemed to him that the root of every last one of these grievances traced back to that plague god. So at the very least, he wanted this much of his plan to go right——

"——Well, that's a bust. I was hoping to take care of all of you in one go just now."

He'd slipped deep into enemy territory for a surprise first strike.
Having dealt with the armed soldier in his opening move, Todd sized up the enemies still standing and grumbled as much.
His opponents numbered three — two young girls and one soft-looking man. He'd managed to take down the one proper soldier among them first, but Todd didn't consider that the best possible outcome.
If anything, he took it as having secured only the bare minimum of results.

Originally, the reason Todd had left his post to push into enemy territory was to eliminate a particular troublemaker in this Battle for the Imperial Capital — someone displaying extraordinary skill in the war of information.
In a large-scale group battle like this one, the accurate handling of information was a lifeline.
Honestly, Todd believed that was the biggest reason the Imperial Army — despite its solid walls and its Nine Divine Generals, each worth a thousand men — was being pushed so hard by rebels whose only real asset was sheer numbers.

Put in terms of the human body, the commander of a group was the head or the heart. Crush either and it was a fatal blow, and because everyone understood that, of course they'd try to protect it. But no matter how hard the head or the heart worked itself up, the body couldn't function if the vessels that carried the flowing blood didn't do their job.
In that sense, the one bearing the role of deciding this battle's flow was whoever was thoroughly playing the part of the "blood vessel" on this battlefield — the enemy that needed to be eliminated above all else.

"It'd be easy to answer that women and kids have no business on a battlefield, but by that logic, the people of the capital are catching way too much collateral. Besides, that'd be a little too convenient, wouldn't it."

Exchanging words he had no business exchanging, Todd sized up his opponents.
The three facing him were as described above, but as to which of the three corresponded to the "blood vessel" Todd was wary of, he had nothing that could pass for real confirmation.

The two who looked most out of place on a battlefield, at a glance, were the two young girls, still children.
They appeared to be in their early teens — not so different in appearance from Madelyn Eschart, the Flying Dragon General, but unlike her, whose different dimension as a living being was obvious at a glance, both of these girls were probably human.
Still, the very fact that beings who shouldn't be here were here was itself grounds to suspect that these girls were beings who needed to be here.

"If you were just noncombatants stranded on the battlefield, your logic just now might hold up. But I don't count anyone doing a job on a battlefield as a noncombatant."

Meanwhile, the soft-looking man trading words with Todd head-on — he didn't have a warrior's face either, but of the three, he was, for now, the closest match to Todd's mental image of the "blood vessel."
In terms of raw physical weakness he gave off much the same air as the girls, but there was something off about his eyes. Behind that narrow face lurked the shrewd, calculating gaze of someone who could treat his own life as a tool without flinching.

Still, he couldn't afford to let that soft-looking man steal his attention and drop his guard against the two girls.
One of the girls had eyes with an unusually steady nerve behind them, and the other unmistakably carried the color of a warrior in her stance and footing. It wasn't unheard of in Vollachia, but that latter girl had probably already taken a human life.
If she had killing experience, then child or not, she'd already climbed over the wall of resolve.
In other words, she wasn't someone he could afford to underestimate.

"Who knows. But my gut's telling me something. That you three are the very root of the mischief being worked in this war. And my gut's telling me something else too."

All three of them, each in their own way, were opponents that put Todd on guard.
There were also signs, even as they talked, that they were watching for — or scheming to create — an opening in Todd's guard, and that he didn't care for. ——Though he still lacked the decisive proof of who deserved top priority.

"——That you three aren't the kind I should be giving any time to, either."

That every one of them was a target who needed killing — that was Todd's unshakable conclusion.

——Otto Suwen often found himself cursing his own carelessness.

The prime example was the whole situation of Natsuki Subaru and Rem going missing, landing him here in the Vollachia Empire — but there was plenty else to be self-critical about besides that.
The matter Petra had just scolded him for, too, was something that called for serious reflection.

Using the Divine Protection of the Soul of Language to dominate the theater of this Battle for the Imperial Capital.

Having made that grand declaration and carried it out with Petra's help, this policy had — bias and self-flattery aside — completely transformed the shape of the battlefield.
The combination of the power of the Divine Protection of the Soul of Language with Petra's Yang magic amplifying Otto's hearing — it let him grasp the battlefield's ever-shifting situation and formations moment by moment, and keep feeding information in real time.
Of course, gathered information was a wasted treasure if it couldn't be put to accurate use, but — his personality aside — in terms of sheer competence, Abel had lived up to Otto's expectations and wielded it brilliantly.

Precious few people actually understood just how much Otto's efforts were contributing to this battle, but Otto wasn't hung up on getting credit.
Whatever happened here in the Vollachia Empire couldn't be breathed a word of outside it anyway.
If this were happening within the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica, it might be worth trumpeting openly, but getting credit for his work in the Empire would be nothing but needless risk. So recognition was a secondary concern.

"What's needed is just one result — bringing Natsuki-san and Rem-san home. ...And doing it without any casualties."

If, in the course of bringing Subaru and Rem home, someone from their own side ended up a casualty, that would defeat the entire purpose.
That was the bare minimum premise of this journey, and at the same time, it was also the premise beyond which there was nothing more. ——No, to put it precisely, it wasn't so much "beyond which there was nothing more" as "there was nothing else."

That was why he'd gone into this battle resolved to lay every card he held out on the table, and Petra had called him out for that recklessness.
He'd taken that criticism seriously — but it still hadn't been enough.
The result of failing to fully make up for that carelessness stood before him now: an imperial soldier, axe in hand.

"——Well, that's a bust. I was hoping to take care of all of you in one go just now."

Gripping his long-hafted axe, the imperial soldier with a bandana wrapped around his head fixed his gaze on them.
Facing a man who showed every sign of thorough ruthlessness and realistic judgment, Otto — shielding Petra behind his back — cursed his own carelessness with everything he had.

He'd entrusted the use of the gathered information to Abel, who he judged could make the best use of it.
But the heart of any war of information lay, from the very start, in how you obtained the information to be used in the first place. In most cases, no one paid attention to that part — it was only human nature to want to target the body handling the information instead.
But the man in front of him was different.

"It'd be easy to answer that women and kids have no business on a battlefield, but by that logic, the people of the capital are catching way too much collateral. Besides, that'd be a little too convenient, wouldn't it."

The true intent behind this man's appearance before Otto and the others was obvious — not to go after the main camp, where Abel was handling the gathered information, but to target Otto and the others, the ones doing the gathering.
They were lightly guarded, with few defenders, but taking their heads would achieve the same result. Once there was no information left to work with, the precision of Abel's command would drop. There was no reason not to do it.
Otto himself, in the same position, would probably have done the same.
So——

"If you were just noncombatants stranded on the battlefield, your logic just now might hold up. But I don't count anyone doing a job on a battlefield as a noncombatant."

While privately agreeing that the man's remark made perfect sense, Otto studied him minutely.
There were two major questions: how had the man appeared here without being caught by Otto's Channel, and how had he pinpointed Otto and the others' location?
He guessed that the answer to both questions was probably one and the same.
Some peculiarity of the man's had let him evade the Channel while also letting him track down the source of its signal.

"Who knows. But my gut's telling me something. That you three are the very root of the mischief being worked in this war. And my gut's telling me something else too."

He'd wanted to fish for a clue from the conversation, but just as his first read had told him, the man was thorough about it.
He understood that saying little was, in itself, a way of protecting himself. Not the type so common in the Empire who crushed opponents through sheer overwhelming force, but one who carried himself with cunning instead.
An enemy like this was formidable. ——An opponent who believed himself weak was a threat.
Because of that——

"——That you three aren't the kind I should be giving any time to, either."

Raising his long-hafted axe high, the man came charging straight at them.
Gritting his teeth against it, Otto poured every ounce of himself into dodging that first blow. He poured himself into it, then braced — wondering just how far he could go against this man with nothing but the bare minimum of preparation.

——Petra Leyte, every time she found herself in adversity, chafed at her own lack of polish.

Petra was often spoiled by her companions in the camp on account of her youth, but she'd set it upon herself that settling into that position and accepting her own immaturity was simply wrong.
If this had been some failure in her everyday duties as a maid back at the mansion, maybe she wouldn't need to take it this seriously.
But that kind of slackness — depending on the situation — was the sort of thing that eventually led to a major failure.

She wasn't saying she had to be tense every single moment.
But she had to be able to deliver the results expected of her, always. That was how Petra thought about things — the ideal that her still-immature self at least wanted to live up to.
So——

"——That you three aren't the kind I should be giving any time to, either."

Against the imperial soldier charging in, axe in hand, she held back the trembling in her limbs and lifted her face.
At the edge of her vision, blurring with welling tears, she could still see the messenger's corpse — burned black, still being consumed by the raging flames.
If Otto hadn't instantly pulled her back, she would have lost her life the very same way. That fact cracked something in Petra's very soul, and at the same time——

"Petra-chan!"

——became a deep trust in Otto, who'd called her name like that, and it was what moved Petra to act.
Otto's voice, casting aside his attitude toward the temporary role of "young lady" and shaking the real Petra instead, loosened the stiffness that fear and regret had begun to lock into her body.
And then——

"——Number Seven!"

At Otto's signal, Petra's arm rose on reflex.
In that instant, a flicker of anxiety crossed her mind. This was a role she'd been entrusted with after declaring she could handle it, but there was no denying it had ended up as trial-by-fire.
She had practiced this endlessly. She was self-aware enough to know she had a knack for coming through in the moment that mattered.
All that was left was——

"——Subaru."

As if kicking away that flicker of anxiety, she simply called the name of the boy she loved and gritted her teeth.

"——Jwald!"

From the fingertips of Petra as she chanted it, a single thread of white light shot out.
It was one of the few offensive spells in Yang magic, a spear of light that pierced whatever lay in its path — though that was true only when wielded by a first-rate user, and inexperienced Petra fell short of that standard.
Petra's Jwald was, at best, capable of inflicting a burn — let alone killing a living creature.
But that was fine.

——

The white light released from her fingertips headed — not toward the charging man, but toward the ground diagonally behind him. It was the trap disguised as "Number Seven," rigged just enough not to look unnatural.
The goal wasn't to burn the man. ——It was to ignite the rigged charge.

"Wha——"

One of Otto's specialties: a trap buried in the ground using fire-attribute magic stones.
Ten traps, prepared in case Otto — who scouted the enemy by opening what he called his "Channel" — and Petra, who assisted him, were ever targeted.
Between the limited quantity of magic stones they could carry and having to keep moving while scouting, it couldn't be called a flawless setup. Even so, Otto's preparations — thorough to the point of being almost sinister, all on the reasoning of "you never know what'll come in handy" — bared their fangs, and the ground behind the man erupted.

"——!"

In that instant, the man's guard shifted, just slightly, toward his own back.
What Petra had blown up on instruction was a spot well removed from directly under or near the man's feet, so the blast's shock and heat never reached him. But it had drawn his attention slightly back.
That alone was enough for the girl who'd been watching for her chance to pounce.

"Hyaaaah——!!"

Swinging the cutlass in her hand, Medium closed the distance and dove straight into the man's guard.
The blonde girl, not much taller than Petra, cut in boldly with a ferocity unlike Petra's own, going for the arm that held the axe, aiming to lop it clean off.

"Tch."

Having had his attention pulled clean away, the man clicked his tongue, twisted his wrist, and instantly knocked Medium's blow aside with his axe. Both their opening strikes parried, the fight seemed poised to reset——

"Not done yet! Not done yet! Not done done done yet!!"

——or so one might have thought, but Medium wasn't the least bit faint of heart about it.
Even with her cutlass knocked back by his attack, she used the momentum of the rebound to spin around and loose a fresh strike, carrying that momentum straight through. If that one was dodged, she rode the momentum of the dodge to spin again and unleash another.
A sword-dance whose momentum wouldn't stop, like the swollen waters of a flooded river——

"Number Four!"

"Yes!"

Otto clapped the shoulder of Petra, who'd nearly been caught staring, and the next instruction flew from him.
When the signal came, she fired her magic at the designated spot without thinking about anything else. ——That was the condition Otto had impressed on her as the most important one, back when they'd made their pact to move through the battlefield together.
So when it came to that instruction, Petra had resolved to forget her fear and everything else and simply obey.

"Jwald!"

The spell fired again, this time blasting the ground at a point closer than before.
The man might have assumed that, since he and Medium were locked in combat, they wouldn't catch her in the blast too. But that was a naive assumption.

"If it's necessary, we'll catch Medium-san in it too."

"I'll heal you up properly afterward, I promise!"

"Whoaaaa! I don't really get it, but I'm not stopping!"

The explosion, bursting only a few meters to the side, betrayed that naive assumption and struck at both of them.
It might be cold-blooded, but Petra agreed with Otto's thinking on this. The reality of all three of them surviving, wounds and all, mattered more than the ideal of all three coming through unscathed.

Even with her skin singed by the blast's heat, Medium's momentum didn't stop.
Feeling both gratitude and guilt at that, Petra, together with Otto, poured everything she had into driving off this assassin——

"Hey now, give me a break."

——and just as she narrowed her eyes to focus, the man said that amid the roar of the blast.
Strangely clear as it was, it wasn't laced with anger or impatience at having his momentum checked. It carried something stranger, something out of place.
What was packed into the man's voice was exasperation.

"Guess we're all thinking the same thing, huh."

The moment she heard it, gooseflesh rose over every inch of Petra's body.
Even without understanding the meaning, it was the sort of remark whose intent she could grasp.

"Number Two!!"

At Otto's shout — his grasp of what lay behind Petra's shiver a little more precise than her own — Petra abandoned all thought and obeyed instantly.
The Jwald she loosed conveyed its heat to a magic stone hidden in the ground, just as before. ——Except this time, the spot her finger pointed toward was the ground right beneath their own feet.

——

White light raced along the ground beneath them, then transformed into red light as a shockwave erupted.
Only after doing it did Petra recall that this was the trap set up for emergency evacuation — the one Otto had prefaced by saying, "It hurts, so I'd rather not use it if I can help it."
And sure enough, the overflowing light kicked back off the soles of her feet, launching Petra's small body into the air. But she wasn't sent flying defenseless.

With a grunt, an arm reached out and caught her, and Petra's body was pulled into Otto's slender embrace.
Of course, Otto's own body was being thrown by the same blast, but he'd chosen this — both to avoid the disaster of the two of them being flung apart and left unable to support each other, and to use his own body to minimize the harm Petra took, at the very least.

What Petra could do in response to Otto's decision wasn't to complain or freeze up, but to cast her Yang magic as fast and as strong as she could, to make Otto's body at least a little more resistant to the impact.
So she did. Both their bodies went flying. And immediately after——

——

A violent boom went off at the very spot Petra's own blast had just come from — that is, the exact spot where Petra and Otto had been standing moments before.
The source of that explosion, which she only barely glimpsed, looked to Petra's eyes like the very same flame that had burned the two messengers.
That thing had been aiming to burn Petra and the others alive, coming from behind them.

"With the axe..."

He'd made them think he meant to cut them down with the axe, when the real threat had been that fire, rigged behind them all along.
That refusal to meet an enemy head-on was clearly the man's specialty, and the fact that it had, in a sense, mirrored Otto's own preparations was probably the true meaning behind the man's muttered remark.

"Ugh!"

Petra's thoughts arrived at that conclusion at almost the same moment Otto, still holding her, tumbled to the ground.
Groaning at the pain of rolling across hard ground, and having caught the blast wind as well, Otto gritted his teeth. But he immediately rose onto one knee right where he was, pulling Petra upright with him as he turned back to face the man.
Petra, too, braced Otto's body and glared at the man, now some distance away.

But contrary to their wariness, the man didn't immediately press the attack.
The reason for that became clear soon enough.

"Wish I didn't have to do this much, honestly."

Grumbling as much while keeping his axe leveled at them to hold them in check, the man turned his head.
He tested the feel of the ground where the explosion had gone off beneath his own feet, sniffed at the scent of grass burning and sending up black smoke, and then——

"There, and there. And there too."

No sooner had the man murmured those words than Petra's eyes went wide at what followed.
Where the man had turned his gaze, the ground exploded. Number Six. Then Nine, then One, and in rapid succession, Five and Eight — one after another, the rigged charges went off.

"Wh-why..."

"...Don't tell me — a spirit?"

That he'd seen through where the traps were hidden was startling enough, but how he'd managed to set them off was beyond her.
The man showed no sign of casting magic the way Petra had to trigger her own blasts, and at his utterly baffling method, Otto's words made Petra catch her breath.

If he was a Spiritual Arts User, that made him the same kind as Subaru and Emilia.
For Petra, there was a strong reluctance to accept that the man in front of her was the same kind of person as those two, who in so many ways meant so much to her——

"But there's no spirit anywhere around..."

"There's more than one way to make one obey. There's also the kind of deal where you do it because you don't want to get eaten."

"Huh?"

"Not that I'm trying to get back at him, but I don't think we should be listening to a word he says."

At an answer she couldn't make sense of, Petra froze, eyes wide. Otto immediately clapped her shoulder and snapped her back to her senses.
Whether the man's remark just now had been meant to create an opening in their guard, or whether he'd actually let slip a fragment of the truth, she couldn't guess either way.
But one thing she could say for certain.

"I don't like you at all!"

"Same here!"

Clenching her small fist tight, Petra shouted it, and a small shadow leapt in answer.
It was Medium, who — wary of the man's unfathomable technique, but watching all the while for her chance to strike — had used the smoke kicked up by the man setting off the traps one after another as cover, and now lunged out from within it to aim her cutlass at his back.
Patience piled on patience, her decisive strike flew straight toward the man, seeming certain to connect——

"Out of the three of you, you're the easiest to read."

"——Ah."

Tilting his body aside, the man slipped clear of the cutlass's downward arc.
Without missing a beat, he spun and swung his axe at Medium behind him, saying——

"You're the only one of the three fighting Imperial-style. Makes me want to puke."

"Medium-chan!!"

In Petra's field of vision, as her scream trailed out behind her, the man's merciless axe flew straight toward Medium's face, her eyes flown wide.

——Medium O'Connell didn't think deeply about things.

Strictly speaking, Medium did think about all sorts of things.
She always worried about her brother Flop O'Connell, while also trusting him completely. Hearing that he'd been carried off from the walled city had left her chest fit to burst with shock and worry, but then again, her own body had shrunk too, and if he knew about that, her brother would probably be flustered right back.
So she figured they were even on both the worrying and the trusting.

"Little sister! I have a feeling that thinking with the heart draws out much better answers for us than thinking with the head. You especially seem to have that sort of air about you, so keep it in mind!"

Those words, said to her by her brother a good while back, were tucked away inside Medium as an important guidepost — kept safe in her chest both before it shrank and after.
Thinking things through with her heart tended to come out illogical, or led to wildly out-of-place remarks that people sometimes disliked. Abel, especially, seemed to hate it.
But her brother's words were amazing. In fact, that way of doing things had worked out for Medium so far.

"My big bro really is amazing!"

Of course, if everything went perfectly by that method, her own body wouldn't have shrunk, her brother wouldn't have been carried off to the capital, Rem wouldn't have vanished, Natsumi wouldn't have been flung off to some faraway place, Chaosflame wouldn't have been destroyed, and Emilie and the others wouldn't be running around in a panic.

Not everything went right.
But within the limits of what actions Medium herself could take, it was the way most likely to make the best thing happen.
So even in the moment her surprise attack from behind failed and the axe came crashing down on her wide-open face, Medium — even as she thought, *Ah, I'm dead* — didn't panic.

It was closer to a feeling of: since she'd taken the best action she could, since she'd done what needed doing, if she lost here anyway then there was nothing to be done about it, and no regrets.
Of course, she didn't want to die, and not being able to see her brother again was painful, and not being able to help new friends like Rem and Natsumi was a real shame too.

But thinking things through with her heart, Medium had made the best move available to her.
Whatever result came after that was, to put it bluntly, up to the will of heaven — and if that will didn't fall her way, having her life taken was simply the way of the world, the Vollachian way.

However——

——

An instant before it buried itself in her face, the thick axe blade was knocked aside in a shower of sparks.
Catching a faint whiff of steel, Medium let out a small breath and said——

"My big bro really is amazing. I haven't died once yet, doing it this way."

"——Oh, now that's something to envy. Me, I've lost count of the times I should've bought it."

To Medium, remarking that the method she'd learned from her brother had saved her once again, the person who'd knocked away the axe meant to take her life answered so.
What he'd swung was a green dragon blade with an edge every bit as thick as the axe he'd deflected — and the one gripping it was a figure of remarkably strange appearance, one that, in Medium's book, rated pretty highly.

"You're..."

Looking at the figure who'd cut in, the man whose surely-certain kill on Medium had just been thwarted moved his lips, sounding displeased.
To that man, the one who'd cut in — a one-armed drifter with his face hidden behind a jet-black helmet — tossed back the words, "Hate to interrupt you right when you're on a roll, but——"

"——I've been wondering where the real danger was hiding, and turns out it's true what they say, it's darkest right under the lighthouse. Man, an inch ahead being pitch black is a real problem, and I mean for real."

——

Quietly, wariness settled into the bearing of the man who'd come to finish off the noncombatants.
But it wasn't the stance of a lion who wouldn't go easy even on a rabbit — it was the posture of a hunter wary that the rabbit had turned into something else entirely. Seeing that, the drifter — Al — shrugged his shoulders.
And then——

"Can't do a thing about the road ahead going pitch black. After all, you can't even see the Stars from down here."

——and with that, he declared he was entering the fight.
