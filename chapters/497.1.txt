Scorpion Tale Prologue: "The Days Spent Waiting for You"

Sparkling, sparkling, they twinkled.
Sparkling, sparkling, they shone.
Sparkling, sparkling, they dazzled.
Sparkling, sparkling, sparkling — everything glimmered, vivid and bright.

"—Mm~, guess there's no wake-up call from Oshi-sama again today-ssu~"

Sitting cross-legged on the hard floor, she stretched her body with a good, hard pull, murmuring the complaint through a yawn.

The voice of her beloved, the one she longed for without end — if that were the very first thing she heard upon waking, it would be perfect. That was what she always hoped for, but sadly, this wish was, without fail, betrayed every single time.

Every day, every morning, the same disappointment repeated at every waking — a dull, uninspired routine, but there was nothing to be done about it.

"Asking me not to expect anything from Oshi-sama — now that's the real impossible thing-ssu."

Next time for sure. This time for certain. Or — no, wait, maybe it would land on some anniversary, or the exact round number of times she'd hoped for it, and because of all the times it had been dragged out before, there'd be some big payoff waiting at the end of it all. Such dreams and hopes for a surprise swelled bigger and bigger the more her love for "him" piled up.

"Same as ever, today included — Oshi-sama really is such a tease-ssu."

Saying this with a sulky pout, she hopped up to her feet.

"One-two, three-four," she counted, swaying and stretching her supple body — a gorgeous woman with long, slender limbs and a knockout figure to match. Dressed in a two-tone bikini-and-mantle outfit whose colors matched her beloved's own, her long, tan hair bound up with a large ribbon into a round-knotted scorpion tail, she was, in every sense, a great blossom in full bloom upon the desert.

"This alluring body, made for Oshi-sama's sake, is firing up in fine form again this morning-ssu."

She jabbed a finger sharply forward, declaring it to no one in particular.

A declaration of war with no one to hear it — but echoing through the dry stone chamber, it was meant for her own ears. Pointing and confirming facts and goals mattered. Speaking your thoughts out loud worked wonders too — someone had told her that once, with utter confidence. Who was it again, exactly?

"Well, one thing's for sure — it wasn't Oshi-sama-ssu. There's no way she'd forget a single thing he ever said-ssu."

And if it wasn't him, then it didn't matter who'd said it at all.

"But-ssu, since she still thought of it as words of wisdom, that counted for nothing against her-ssu. If anything, since she still remembered the content of what was said, that was practically a plus in her favor-ssu. Yataaa — victory is mine-ssu! I won't let anyone complain-ssu!"

She tapped her feet in high spirits right where she stood, even breaking into a little humming.

Waking up had betrayed her expectations as usual, leaving her genuinely blue — but the little pep talk that followed had done its job, and her mood was excellent now. Today was shaping up to be a good day — and right then, her stomach growled.

"Mmm," she muttered, rubbing her bare, navel-exposed belly, and tilted her head.

"Now that she thought about it, when was the last time she'd eaten anything...ssu?"

Between not being the type to fuss much over meals, and not being the type to fuss much over anything in general, her memory on the subject was pretty hazy — hazy, foggy, fuzzy-vague.

Left to her own devices, she could go days, weeks, even months without eating a thing and think nothing of it. That had once been praised as an advantage — a benefit, even, for survival.

"But Oshi-sama didn't like that sort of thing-ssu. Something about how it was hard on him to watch, seeing only her not eating when everyone else was having a meal...ssu. Guh — his thoughtfulness really gets to her-ssu!"

So she'd made a point of doing her best to take part in mealtimes, even if it meant deliberately choosing energy-inefficient behavior. Wasting away the energy-saving ability that let her go for literal years without moving, she tried to keep herself as busy and bustling as possible instead — and that made every meal something she no longer minded.

"He," too, had been more than pleased about it, so it had truly, truly been worth doing.

"Though Kaka-sama did grumble something about how it was ruining a perfectly good trait-ssu."

Anyway, her body seemed to have gotten thoroughly used to that routine along the way. Wherever that energy-saving physique had gone, before she knew it she was constantly hungry, constantly sleepy — honestly, unlike back when she'd been with "him," now that she was alone, she found it more of a nuisance more often than not.

But when "he" kept his promise and came back, she didn't want to have gone back to watching meals like they were somebody else's business, and disappoint "him."

"After all, it's Oshi-sama's whole theory that a woman who eats heartily is charming-ssu... ah, Oshi-sama, no-ssu! Don't look at me like that when my mouth's full of meat and beans...!"

Pressing both hands to her reddened cheeks, hips swaying, she pictured "him" inside her head, smiling refreshingly at her as she stuffed her face with food.

For now it was nothing but a daydream, but image training was essential if she wanted to properly meet that day when it came.

And so, to be ready and waiting for that day's arrival, today too she would keep up her hunger-prone body, burning through energy in a flashy, busy way, and then finally get herself a meal.

For starters, toward that end—

"—Guess she'll just go ahead and knock out her daily routine, bit by bit, like always-ssu!"

—The accumulation of days makes the self, and that becomes the real image reflected in others' eyes.

Something like that — some fussy, hard-to-parse line — who had said it again? That, too, she absolutely could not recall. Well, since it wasn't "him," the usual conclusion applied: no need to remember.

"It sounds theory-heavy, so maybe Kaka-sama-ssu...? Though honestly, what Kaka-sama even looked or sounded like is a total blur-ssu. Like... white? Black? Might not even have the color right-ssu."

In exchange for a healthy sensuality, exceptional energy efficiency, and an alluring, drop-dead body that could slay even a tease like "him," what she'd gotten was a pretty shoddy set of contents up top — that was who she was. With ninety-nine percent of her scant memory capacity devoted entirely to her love for "him," everything else was left hazy.

"Aaand, once this wall's had its day, this room's getting sealed up too-ssu~."

As she said this, she surveyed all four walls of the room she'd frequented for so long for her "daily routine," and once she'd finished checking the routine carved into the stone walls, she decided to close the room off.

Fortunately, there were still plenty of empty rooms left. At this rate, rendering one or two rooms unusable wasn't going to cause any real inconvenience. Besides, she'd had management work she wasn't even suited for foisted onto her in the first place, so a little abuse of authority like this ought to be overlooked.

"So, with the master authority granted to me — this door is close the door-ssu. My memories sealed up in here are don't-touch-me for anyone's eyes-ssu."

She shut the stone door with a rumbling groan, and clapped her hands as if to say a job well done. She'd need to pick out another room for tomorrow's "routine" onward, but that could wait until tomorrow.

What could be done tomorrow could be done tomorrow. She'd forgotten whose words these were too, but they were good words all the same.

"Well then, guess it's about time to go 'hunting'-ssu..."

She said it with a faintly meaningful tone, grinning to herself, then immediately reconsidered — "or would 'hunting' have sounded better-ssu?" — even as her feet carried her toward the hunting ground: the balcony.

Striding down the corridor on her long legs, she passed through the doorless entrance and stepped out into the open air, where a sky so hatefully clear and blue welcomed her as if to embrace her.

Right, left, and straight ahead — no matter where she looked, an unbroken expanse of blue sky stretched on.

Baring her porcelain-white skin to the streaming sunlight, her bound hair stirred by the sweeping wind, she made her way to the railing at the edge of the balcony, about waist-high. One careless step over that railing meant a straight, no-stopping plunge of several hundred meters to the ground below, with no safety line. She hopped up onto that very railing with an utterly casual air.

"Hmm~, wind's a bit strong today, maybe-ssu~."

The railing she casually stood on had long since become her regular spot, the stone worn down into the shape of her feet, fitting them perfectly and holding her firmly in place. No matter how strong the wind blew, it wasn't going to be sweeping this dynamite body away.

"Not that she was ever the soft, flimsy type to be swept off by wind in the first place-ssu. For her, the perfect woman shaped by Oshi-sama's own guidance, it's through fire and water alike-ssu. Ah, but... the lonely, howling dry wind — that's the one thing that gets right down into her bones-ssu..."

Playing at being tough yet a touch lonesome — a bit of hard-boiled roleplay — she used the tip of her wind-tossed scorpion tail of hair to check wind strength, pressure, temperature, humidity, and everything else, all at once. Factoring in even the murkiness of the drifting miasma, today was shaping up to be an exceptional day for sniping—

"Forget a hundred hits out of a hundred shots — might even manage a thousand hits out of a hundred today-ssu, don't you think? Scary, scary-ssu! My own potential, it's terrifying! Help me, Oshi-sama!"

Hugging herself tight, tight, tight, she bottled up inside her the heat that welled up whenever she thought of "him." Longing for the day she could someday unleash all this heat on "him" once he returned, letting her affection simmer and boil like magma — that was her daily routine.

Yes, that was her unchanging routine, an ordinary that was anything but ordinary — the everyday of her days. And this day, too, was supposed to be just that.

"Hnn?"

Even while carrying on a conversation with no one to chime in or call her out, she'd kept surveying the world around her — the blue that filled the upper half of her vision, the dead-leaf brown that filled the lower half. Now her eyes narrowed.

Staring hard, her eyes — marked with a distinctive pattern — focused in on the cause of the disturbance.

What she saw was a sea of dead-leaf-colored sand, and cutting through the whirling clouds of dust upon it, a single dragon carriage tearing along at a furious pace. Pulled by a green earth dragon, it had burst out of the sandstorm and pushed through into "this side" of the dunes, barreling forward across the sand at full speed.

"Oooh, ooooh! Rare-ssu! It's been a while-ssu! Who knows how long it's been since anyone broke through the sandstorm-ssu! Who in the world is making their grand entrance now?! !!"

She raised a hand to shade her eyes, cheering, her expectations tickled despite herself by the scene below.

Reckless challengers to the sand sea never ceased, but almost none of them ever made it through to this side of the sandstorm. The only ones who could reach this side were either those who knew the way to cross the "Sand Time" barrier the sandstorm produced, or those blessed with rare, limited luck even without knowing.

"Could it be Oshi-sama—"

Which one it was this time, she couldn't say — her voluptuous chest bounced with excitement as she stared, riveted. Maybe, just maybe, today was the day.

Maybe he'd finally come back at last, to keep his promise.

She'd been kept waiting an awfully long time, but if he'd properly come home, she was willing to wipe the slate clean on that grudge. No — actually, maybe this was the moment to press her advantage on his guilty conscience and demand double in return.

Eyes bright with anticipation, she focused in on the racing dragon carriage. Her field of vision zoomed in with a low grind, and she made out several figures in black robes aboard it. Unfortunately, she couldn't see clear into the carriage itself, but she saw no reaction to her gaze, no gesture meant as a signal.

"—Aww. A miss again, huh-ssu."

In an instant, the bubble of expectation burst, and the flutter in her chest scattered away, fleeting.

No waving hands, no waving flags — none of that "look over here" appeal. That was the attitude of someone who had no idea she existed: the administrator of this sand sea, its ultimate sniper.

When that happened, her hope flipped right over, and in its place came the cold face of the archer—

As an archer, what she needed to do was clear. But it didn't look like there'd be any role for her to play.

Looking closer, the furiously racing carriage was desperately fleeing from something — countless Witchbeasts, chasing after the carriage that had broken the sandstorm, bursting out from it in turn. The swarming Witchbeasts were of every kind imaginable, so many that it almost looked as if every single species living in the sand sea had marked the carriage as an enemy.

That carriage probably hadn't set out to challenge the sand sea alone, either. And now, only a single carriage had made it through to the far side of "Sand Time." And even that one wasn't going to reach the goal.

"Scrambling and flailing, so desperate-ssu~."

She murmured it like someone else's business entirely, her voice drained of expectation and interest alike.

Even as she watched, a Witchbeast leapt onto the carriage as its wheels caught in the sand, and one of its passengers was thrown off. The unlucky figure tumbled across the sand, and the starving Witchbeasts all lunged at once, tearing pitilessly into their prey — and the next instant, a roar and a burst of flame went up.

"Wahoo — tamaya-ssu!"

At the blast that echoed off the blue sky, and the great crater it left gouged in the sand sea, her once-lost interest flickered back to life just a little, and it slipped out of her mouth as a cheer.

A self-detonation. The figure who'd been thrown off and swarmed by Witchbeasts had blown themselves up, taking their surroundings down with them. The blast slammed sideways into the pack of Witchbeasts chasing the carriage, flame licking at their haunches. A life traded for time bought at the cost of throwing oneself in harm's way to protect one's companions — and it didn't stop at just once.

One after another, a second, a third such explosion followed, and each time the carriage lost one more passenger, ten times that many Witchbeasts or more were blown away in turn.

And even so—

"—Well, that's that, I guess-ssu~."

Their valiant struggle came to nothing in the end; a Witchbeast that finally caught up got tangled in the carriage's wheels, and the carriage flipped over spectacularly in a spray of blood-mist. Amid the dust rising over the sand, the first victim was the fallen earth dragon — its death cry rose as the Witchbeasts' fangs closed on it, and behind it, a survivor crawled out of the wrecked carriage. Having escaped with what strength they had left, that survivor, perhaps out of sheer panic, began running while crying out — drawing the Witchbeasts' attention. Needless to say, a cruel fate awaited that pitiful lamb.

"Oh?"

But behind the sacrifice of the earth dragon and the lamb, two figures were quietly slipping away. It seemed the lamb had deliberately played the fool to let those two escape.

"Now that's what you call guts-ssu. —Well, either way, the result's the same-ssu, though."

Sitting on the railing with one knee drawn up, she rated the challenger's final moments as worthy of an honorable-effort prize. Call it bad taste if you like — but it was simply something she had to do, as her duty as the Watcher.

Whether those who tried to challenge this sand sea — no, this "Tower" — made it back or ran out of strength along the way, seeing to it that she witnessed what became of them was her duty as its Watcher.

Now, of the two survivors who'd slipped free of the carriage, one had thrown themselves forward to let the other escape, and their final self-detonation had produced one last blast of wind. Caught in that blast, the last surviving one tumbled backward onto the sand. And there, mercilessly, the Witchbeasts crept closer.

"—Hm."

With that last death right before her eyes, something happened that made her lips twist despite herself, unbidden.

It was hesitation, born the instant she got a clear look at the figure of that last challenger, surrounded by Witchbeasts, checkmated completely with their life on the line.

Could it be? No — could it really be? Wasn't it just a trick of the eye? And if it wasn't—

Such endless thoughts spun round and round and round in her head—

"—Hell's Snipe."

"—!"

In that instant, the head of the Witchbeast lunging at the last survivor vanished.

The bear-like Witchbeast's head was blown clean away, and not a drop of blood sprayed from the severed stump of its neck. The wound, cauterized by light, didn't let even a single drop of blood soak into the sand, and the other Witchbeasts froze stiff at the suddenness of it.

In a sand sea ruled by the law of the strong devouring the weak, that pause was an act of sheer foolishness.

"—Bang, bang, bang. Ba-ba-bang, bang."

Her tongue clicked against the air, and the light little sounds it made brought forth flashes of light in the sky. For all their lighthearted ring, they trampled lives underfoot with merciless, overwhelming force.

The sight of lives pierced by light, shattered, snuffed out with nowhere left to run, felt almost unreal. And in fact, the person witnessing it firsthand stood stunned, eyes wide, utterly dumbfounded.

"Well, it's easier on me if you just stay put like that-ssu."

"⁠ ⁠"

The voice must have come as a surprise, because the other party, still lost in blank shock, gave a start, thin shoulders jerking. Slumped on the sand, they turned around fearfully, and their eyes met hers, standing right behind them.

A woman. Probably. Brown hair cut straight at shoulder-length, with prominent whites showing all around her irises. Dressed in a loose black poncho-like garment that hid her figure, with no particularly striking features — an entirely ordinary appearance.

Not that she had any interest in the woman's looks to begin with — aside from being the sole reason she'd found herself leaping down from the Tower's balcony into the sand sea and slaughtering every last Witchbeast just to save her life.

"Sheesh, guess I'm losing my edge-ssu."

Grumbling, she swung her arm, and an instant later blew the head of a Witchbeast — a sand earthworm trying to rear its massive body up out of the sand beyond the woman — into splinters with a burst of magic light.

As the huge creature's flesh and blood splattered wetly through the air, she shrugged.

And then—

Unsteadily, on the sand now empty of Witchbeasts, the unconscious woman collapsed.

None of the woman's companions had survived. The pack of Witchbeasts that had been hunting them also sensed the tide had turned against them and scattered, fleeing in every direction. Which meant there was no one left to carry the woman lying on the sand.

No one, that is, except the one who'd so plainly drawn the short straw.

"Ugh, honestly, this isn't even for Oshi-sama's sake, and it's turned into such a hassle-ssu."

Sighing deeply, she reluctantly gathered the fallen woman up in both arms. Emotionally speaking, she'd rather have grabbed her by the ankle and dragged her back, but doing that risked undoing the whole point of having saved her.

Who had told her this? — No, unlike those other sayings, this one she remembered perfectly well.

Without a doubt, it was one of the things "he" had told her. Even if someone was a challenger to the "Tower," they had to be treated with proper care.

Because—

"—Pregnant women are to be treated with care. Good thing your belly's swollen-ssu."

With her lips pursed and grumbling complaints under her breath, the belly of the limp, unconscious woman in her arms was swollen.

Not just a heavyset woman — a pregnant one. No wonder the rest of the carriage's passengers had fought so desperately to protect her. They, too, had spent their lives by the very same logic "he" had once taught her.

"That said, my kindness only goes so far-ssu. This doesn't count as clearing the challenge, so don't go getting the wrong idea-ssu."

She offered the warning to no one in particular. Being misunderstood would be a real problem.

After all, for someone who was neither herself nor "him" and his companions to set foot in this "Tower" — the Pleiades Watchtower — was an event that hadn't happened in roughly several hundred years.
