Arc 5 Chapter 20: "Feelings That Elevate Each Other"

Barely managing to keep himself from sinking to his knees right there, Subaru let out a ragged breath.
Around him, Emilia, Beatrice—and even Liliana—were casting worried glances his way. Priscilla, looking utterly uninterested, was fanning her own face with a light, idle motion.

Bathed in their concerned voices and glances, his awareness gradually began to regain its grip on reality.
Feeling his thoughts slowly pick up speed, Subaru began, bit by bit, to chew over and understand just what he had experienced only moments ago.

The scenery had changed abruptly, as if someone had switched the channel on a television.
The smells, the tastes, the sights he'd been sensing only a moment before had all shattered into fragments, replaced wholesale by something else entirely—a sense of losing reality, no, a sense of reality's sheer abruptness.

His eyes, ears, nose, skin—everything had already adjusted to the new world, and yet his awareness alone kept dragging the old channel along behind it, an unshakable wrongness.
Only once he'd swallowed that feeling down, chewed it, ground it up, and forced it down his throat did the switch finally complete.

"—!"

Gritting his teeth, Subaru got to his feet.
He shook his head and looked around. There it was—a park bathed in gentle sunlight, a fountain, green lawns, a resting place framed by beds of vividly colored flowers.
And around him stood four beautiful girls—Emilia, Beatrice, Liliana, and Priscilla. What was more, Liliana's last words rang a bell—for the *second* time.

"Are you okay, Subaru? Your face just went a terrible color."

"...Emilia-tan. Did Liliana, by any chance, just say something like 'I'll sing another song'?"

"That's what you remember!? This Liliana cannot hide the shock, the dismay, and the wounded heart of being ignored all the way back to that point! Compensation! I demand compensation!"

Subaru shook off Liliana, who'd grabbed his sleeve and seemed ready to file a lawsuit, and ignored the songstress as she went flying with a startled "Ehh?" He turned to Emilia and asked with his eyes instead. Emilia, too, seemed to sense from Subaru's manner that this wasn't some joking question, and said,

"That's right. Liliana was just about to sing us the song we missed hearing before. And then you started up some secret little whispered conversation with her..."

"So it's this exact moment, huh. Got it. Thanks. ...Thanks."

The instant the word "thanks" left his mouth, an indescribable revulsion welled up inside him.
Emilia furrowed her brow in confusion at Subaru, who'd reflexively pressed a hand to his mouth.

It wasn't Emilia's fault. There was no reason for a word of thanks to taste bitter, either. It was only that it reminded him of that world just before—of that indescribable malice.

"I see. So I..."

He didn't say the words *I died*, but only once he'd thought that far did it finally begin to sink in.

—Natsuki Subaru had died.

He had died, and come back again, starting over from the time this world had lost. Even as relief washed over him at having made it back, frustration surged in like heat.

He was supposed to have been ready for this—so ready, so resolved.
In that forest a year ago, in the Sanctuary, in the Trials of the Tomb, Natsuki Subaru had sworn, so firmly, to reject "death," to resist the sorrow that came with being born anew.

And yet he'd died. So easily, so anticlimactically. Far from resisting, he hadn't even sensed anything wrong.
What had that been? It was a fact that it had been abnormal, and yet Subaru had no idea what had actually happened. He had felt it, experienced it firsthand, and still it made no sense.

That obviously abnormal space—Subaru hadn't felt it was strange at all. And not just him. Everyone else present had surrendered themselves to the same madness he had.
To watch a boy bound in chains, sobbing and screaming, and cheer him on with a smile—that was out of one's mind. And to greet a boy being thrown to his death with applause—calling that madness would be putting it mildly.

What could possibly have to happen for a situation like that to occur in the first place?
And when, exactly, while watching it unfold, had Subaru died?

There shouldn't have been any hint of "death" right up until the end. No—he couldn't even say that for certain anymore. It was possible the signs had been there and he simply hadn't recognized them as dangerous.
He couldn't trust his own senses at all. The bare fact of the abnormal situation leading up to "death," and what had happened, were both plain enough—but not a single one of the steps that led there was clear.

How had he died?
Had something like explosives been fitted to the body of that boy who fell to his death? Had it detonated the instant he hit the ground, blowing everything around him apart? That instant of instant death—the memory felt something close to that.
It wasn't clear. The memory of the moment of "death" was heavily colored by Subaru's state of mind at the time. No matter how calmly he tried to sort it out now, there was nothing to be done about the fact that a memory formed while he wasn't calm would carry that same haziness forward.

If Subaru had, in fact, been out of his mind, then untangling a memory formed in that same deranged state, whole and intact, was for all practical purposes impossible.

"Are you *really* okay, Subaru? And Liliana's making this really sulky face, too."

"I'm not sulking at all. It doesn't matter one bit to me how Natsuki-sama thinks of me, not one bit. I—I'm certainly not sad about it or anything, so please don't get the wrong idea!"

"See, she's acting exactly like someone who really, really wants you to worry about her."

Liliana's bluster was transparent enough that even the naturally oblivious Emilia had seen through it—but that exchange reminded Subaru of the position he was expected to be in.
At this point in time, while Liliana bought time with her singing, he and she had been off running an errand to fetch sweets as a peace offering. And it was right after finishing that shopping trip that he'd run into that monster of "Wrath."

—Less than fifteen minutes remained before that nightmare of a speech began.

"You've gotta be kidding..."

He couldn't recall a Return by Death ever landing this close to the actual moment of his death before.
Every previous Return by Death had granted him anywhere from several hours to several days. But this time, it was far, far too short.
What could Subaru possibly do with a mere fifteen minutes?

Time kept passing even as he agonized. In this case, simply avoiding death was easy enough—all Subaru had to do was not go anywhere within reach of that speech. If that abnormality, that death, had been caused by some kind of bomb, then as long as he didn't go there, he wouldn't be caught up in it.
Surely they hadn't used a bomb powerful enough to level an entire city.
So if saving his own skin was the priority, all he had to do was not go there. Sirius surely hadn't singled Subaru out and appeared at that spot specifically for him.

It was purely indiscriminate malice, and Subaru had simply happened to be there. Which meant even without Subaru present, Sirius's crime scene wouldn't change. In other words, that speech would begin and end regardless of whether Subaru was there or not.
Whatever the outcome, at the very least, the boy who was thrown down to his death would die for certain.

"I have to stop it... Damn it, I have to stop it...!"

Clawing at his own hair, Subaru made his decision.
There was no way he could abandon him. That boy—Lusbel—had done nothing wrong. He was a poor kid who'd been excited for his coming baby sibling, and who'd been put in that horrific position as a stand-in for his childhood friend. How could Subaru possibly be so cowardly as to save only himself and leave him to die?

"Beatrice, come with—!"

He caught himself right before the words *come with me* left his mouth.

"With Subaru, what?"

At Subaru's tone and expression, Beatrice asked with a serious face.
With an emergency plainly bearing down on him, bringing Beatrice along was the obvious choice if he wanted to bolster his own fighting strength.
Not bringing her meant, in effect, halving his combat power. And yet Subaru hesitated all the same.

It wasn't out of some sentimental reason like not wanting Beatrice to have to fight.
Of course, he wouldn't claim that thought wasn't somewhere in his heart at all—but here, now, it wasn't the reason.

—It was Emilia. Emilia was here.

"—"

The Witch Cult was in this city.
They—there was no telling yet if there were more than one, but at least one Sin Archbishop of Wrath was here. Whether Sin Archbishops acted alone was something Subaru couldn't say, since so little was known about the Witch Cult's true nature.
But the anxiety of leaving Emilia behind in a city where the Witch Cult was known to be moving in the shadows kept flitting across his mind, refusing to leave.

The terror of leaving someone precious behind in a place he himself wasn't.
The terror of the Witch Cult's grasping hand reaching some precious person, somewhere beyond his sight.

That was a fear that had lodged itself, murky and unmoving, at the very foundation of Natsuki Subaru's heart.

Then should he bring Emilia along to face "Wrath"? Out of the question. Letting Emilia and the Witch Cult meet could only produce tragedy. That much, at least, was certain.
He only had to remember what had happened with Petelgeuse to understand. Subaru must never let Emilia and the Witch Cult come near each other. It wasn't a question of why. It simply was.

"Beatrice, come with me and..."

"With Subaru, and?"

"...get the same sweets as before. Sound good?"

"...?"

Beatrice knit her brow at Subaru's words—no doubt because it hardly seemed like something worth building up to with such urgency. Subaru nodded at Liliana as she face-planted spectacularly, then turned his eyes to Emilia.

"I'm gonna make a quick run and grab something sweet we can snack on. Emilia-tan, you just sit tight, all elegant and relaxed, and listen to Liliana's song while you wait."

"Um, okay. But are you sure it's fine if I don't come with you?"

"It's fine, trust me. I'm gonna protect you."

Emilia blinked her big eyes, then flushed and nodded at Subaru's words. Subaru then beckoned to Beatrice, who was eyeing him suspiciously, and leaned in close to whisper in her ear.

"Protect Emilia. If I need your power, I'll call for you right away."

"...Something Betty can't be told about again, is that it, I suppose."

"If I do call, you can just assume it means things have gone bad, no question about it."

Subaru poked the tip of Beatrice's discontented nose with a finger, raised a hand lightly, and took off running. Feeling the four pairs of eyes watching him go at his back, he sprinted at full speed without looking behind him.

Following the road, it wouldn't take even five minutes to reach that same plaza as before. Except this time his start was a little later than before to begin with. With only a sliver of time allowed him this time around, even that small difference could prove fatal.

"Still, I didn't do the shopping this time, so I should have close to ten minutes left..."

Skidding to a stop on the cobblestones, Subaru looked around the plaza he'd reached.
Last time he'd been too fixated on looking up to spare any attention for his surroundings, but at least there was no sign of any suspicious group or individual in black robes anywhere obvious.
Could he take that to mean the Sin Archbishop was acting alone?

"The problem's what to do from here. Once that speech starts, there's a good chance I'll get dragged right back into that whole incomprehensible situation."

As long as he didn't understand the principle behind that abnormal space, there was no telling whether he could keep his sanity if he got swallowed up by it again. What made that brainwashing space so terrifying, Subaru figured, was precisely that it made you lose the ability to recognize the abnormal as abnormal in the first place.

"Evacuate everyone from here...? Same as with Petelgeuse... no, I don't have the hands for it, and if I make a clumsy scene out of it, Sirius might make his move."

Should he evacuate the crowd here so they wouldn't be caught up in it? But how? To begin with, if there was no special meaning behind Sirius holding his speech in this particular spot, then Sirius might not even need to restrict himself to this location.
If there was no one here to hear the speech, he could simply give it somewhere else. Moving the victims wouldn't stop anything—it would only set the stage for a different set of victims.

"Then there's nothing for it but to take out the source...!"

If anything, now, knowing exactly where Sirius would appear, was the chance. This matched what he'd concluded back when he'd defeated Petelgeuse, too. The Witch Cult couldn't be left alone. It wasn't just about preventing the Witch Cult's harm before it happened—unless the root cause itself was eradicated, they would just keep repeating themselves.

He'd been slow to arrive at this conclusion.
If he'd reached it right away, Subaru wouldn't have come here alone in the first place. No—really, the mistake had been made the instant he was sent back to that park at all.
If only he'd been sent back as far as the inn, he could have borrowed Wilhelm's or Julius's strength—

"No use wishing for what I don't have. Anyway, right now, it's just me. Once the speech starts, his location's gonna be pinned down for sure, but... no, wait, there's a much faster way than that!"

Reaching his conclusion, Subaru glared at the base of the Time Tower—the white tower where that accursed speech would take place. Sirius had entered from there, passed through a window, and given his speech.
Which meant he ought to already be inside, preparing for the speech that would take place in just a few more minutes. And even if he wasn't inside yet, there was a chance Subaru could rescue Lusbel, still bound in chains.

Subaru ran while checking for watching eyes and reached the base of the Time Tower. He put his hand on an inconspicuous door tucked away beside an alley, pushed the iron door open, and slipped his body inside.

The inside of the Time Tower was dim, filled with cold, dusty air.
The absence of any sound at all was because, unlike a clock tower, there was no need for gears or other mechanisms inside. The Time Tower's method of marking the hour was solely the glow of its mana-inscribed crystals, which merely reflected, through changes in their color, the natural, faint shifts over time in the mana saturating the air.

—Which meant any sound heard from within the Time Tower could only be a sound someone was making on purpose.

"...Nnngh."

"No crying, no screaming, no fuss. You really are a good boy. Such a strong boy. I'm sure Musuran Papa and Ina Mama, and the little brother or sister you haven't met yet, are all so proud of their strong big brother. Wonderful, truly."

That horrid voice, drifting up.
And the groaning voice of the boy, terrified by that horror, drifting up along with it.

From above the spiraling staircase came what sounded like sobbing. It sounded like a curse, and like a blessing; like hatred, and like love, all at once.
It was warped. Not sane in the least. The kind of thing that could not be endured with one's sanity intact.

"—"

The moment he was certain of their presence, Subaru let out a long breath and stilled his breathing.
Pressing a hand to his chest, right over his pounding heart, he counted his heartbeats as he crept toward the stairs. Fortunately, they were stone. With sufficient care, he could avoid making any footsteps. And besides, his opponent should be fully focused on the child in his hands.

Keeping himself ready to summon Beatrice at any moment, Subaru climbed the stairs slowly, killing every footstep. Above, the voices grew closer little by little, and the tension mounted.
The Time Tower itself soared high enough to have to crane your neck to see the top, but there was nothing notable inside on the way up. A great central pillar rose through the middle to support the structure, and the spiral staircase followed the building's outer wall toward the summit.

And the demon's voice, and the valiant one's, were coming from the very top of the stairs. The one window connecting to the outer wall—probably meant as an access point for adjusting or inspecting the mana-inscribed crystals fitted into the Time Tower. The voices were coming from the space in front of that window.

It felt less like a loft and more like an attic room.
Peering up quietly from the foot of the stairs, Subaru could make out two figures moving fitfully in the darkness at the back of the space, exchanging words.
There were no other figures. It seemed safe to rule out any horror-movie scenario of silent Witch Cultists lurking unseen.

—All that remained now was to watch for an opening and land a single strike.

"—"

He shouldn't even entertain the thought of taking him alive. There was a world of difference between an enemy ending up alive as a result and setting out to capture him from the start—all the more so when the enemy was the kind of person who might do anything if left alive.

Subaru crouched down and reached a hand around to the small of his back.
He gripped the handle fixed there firmly and drew the weapon from the holster at his waist.

What Subaru held in his hand had a handle long enough to have some length left over even gripped with both hands, with a supple length of specially-woven fiber extending from its tip—a whip.
It was the kind commonly called a bullwhip, famous for being the tool of choice of a world-renowned movie archaeologist while raiding ancient ruins.
Subaru's whip had an even longer reach than the one that professor of archaeology carried, and was a harder type to handle. Over a year of practice, he'd honed his skill to the point where Clind had granted him full mastery certification.

Of every weapon out there, Subaru had chosen the whip as his primary equipment for a very specific reason.
It offered a wide range of uses distinct from a sword, hammer, spear, or bow. And above all, in the case of a sword, the level Subaru could hope to reach in a mere few years was painfully limited.
Having already witnessed the battles of those who stood at truly extraordinary heights on the path of the sword, Subaru understood all too well that he couldn't muscle his way in there with some hastily acquired skill.
So instead of a sword or a spear, Subaru had chosen the whip.

To begin with, he could only fight through cunning, footwork, and inventiveness.
So it made sense to go all in and pick a weapon that suited who he was—one he could actually put to use.
And besides, with a whip—he could strike from mid-range, too.

"—"

The distance from the top of the stairs to the shifting figures was roughly four meters. That was right around the limit of Subaru's reach. To land a hit for certain, he'd want to close in one step—no, even half a step would do, before cracking the whip.
Either way, a whip's killing power wasn't enough to guarantee a one-hit kill. To pull off a one-hit kill with that weapon, he'd need to rely on some other factor.
And in this case, of course, there was nothing for it but to make use of the height.

—In that case, there was only the distance where he could be certain of a hit.

"—"

He drew a small breath, exhaled just a little. And then held it.

Rising up, Subaru pulled his whip-hand back as he climbed the last of the stairs. The instant he reached the top floor, the figure at the back still hadn't noticed him. He could strike first.
He stepped in half a pace and swung his arm in a circle over his head.

Cutting the air, the whip's tip lashed out, tearing sound apart as it lunged for its prey.
The sideways sidearm swing set power aside, prioritizing the speed of striking first and the specific purpose of this particular whip attack.

Like a serpent's head writhing through the air, it snapped at the unguarded back before it—to coil around that neck, and cast this embodiment of malice down into the Abyss.
But—

"Why are you so angry?"

The vague, hazy voice came from the figure, its back still turned to him.
An instant later, the shadow whipped its right arm around at terrifying speed, and with the chain wound around that arm, canceled out the force of the whip Subaru had thrown.

A writhing serpent, intercepted by a serpent of silver.
For an instant, Subaru was stunned by the sight—but the moment he judged that the tip had made solid contact, he wrenched his arm through in a diagonal sweep.

"Oh my?"

The figure—Sirius—stumbled, thrown badly off balance, staggering backward.
The chain dangling from his right arm had indeed canceled out the blow from Subaru's whip, but the whip's tip had caught on a link of that chain. Yanked by it, Sirius lost his footing.

"Hraaah!"

As Sirius reeled back, Subaru pulled his arm in further and closed the distance in a single rush. The bandage-wrapped figure spun halfway around as his body was dragged off, and Subaru dove in shoulder-first, slamming into him with a full body check. That surprisingly light body went flying easily, unable to withstand Subaru's mass.

"Eek—yipe!"

Letting out a thin shriek, Sirius's body bounced off the floor and carried straight over the edge of the platform, sent tumbling down to the floor below exactly as Subaru had first intended. From this height to the ground floor was over ten meters—high enough, at least, that a child falling headfirst would be crushed like a piece of fruit.

"Are you okay, Lusbel!"

Without waiting to see how Sirius's fall ended, Subaru rushed to the other figure still remaining in the back. The small shadow was, as expected, Lusbel, who looked up at Subaru with a terrified face, chains still held in both hands.
The chains he held seemed to be connected to the chains wound around the boy's own lower body—and that alone told Subaru everything he needed to know about the sick nature of Sirius's little game.

"That bastard...! He made him wind the chains around himself...!"

Little by little, literally forced to strangle himself with his own hands—how much terror must Lusbel have endured? Even now, with it interrupted halfway through, his expression bore a brand of fear that would surely never fade.

Sensing that malice, Subaru felt a surge of visceral fury. He immediately clapped the boy on the shoulder and forcibly wrenched the chain from his grip.

"That's enough! It's okay now. You don't need to do this anymore. You're getting out of here with me!"

"B-but... if I don't k-keep my promise, Tina's... Tina's...!"

Eyes brimming with tears, Lusbel's lips trembled.
Seeing that, Subaru's throat caught despite himself.

The boy had accepted this devil's bargain in exchange for his childhood friend's safety. Even amid this much terror, Lusbel was still more worried for the girl than for himself.
Even with his legs shaking, his teeth chattering out of rhythm, his vision blurred faintly with tears, and even his words refusing to come out straight—

"It's okay. There are... a lot of people... in this town right now, people you can count on..."

His ragged, broken breath got in the way, and the words wouldn't come out the way he meant them to.
He needed to give the boy strong, reassuring words. Right now, this town had the Sword Saint in it. It had the finest knight in the kingdom, too. There was even the greatest healer in the whole royal domain—an assembled cast of allies capable, between them, of leveling a fair-sized city.

So there was nothing to be afraid of. Evil never prospers in the end. That's right. Exactly right.
There was nothing—nothing at all—to be scared of. So—

"So... *stop it already*, you damn shaking legs!!"

In front of Lusbel, whose eyes had lost focus from sheer terror, Subaru struck his own buckling knees and desperately forced his voice out.
Even his voice cracked, tinged with an anguished ring that raised gooseflesh. A revulsion, like something unknowable was riding on his back, coiled around his entire body and refused to let go.

"—Guh."

With a bursting, bubbling sound, Lusbel retched up yellow bile. His body convulsed as if seized by a fit, and he collapsed forward onto his own vomit. As Subaru tried to lift the boy back up, he too succumbed to a nausea like his own guts were being churned inside out, and emptied his stomach's contents.
The Daisukiyaki he'd stuffed into his stomach that morning came back up shapeless, the sour reek of stomach acid and bile pouring out along with it. Gurgling, tasting the misery of nearly drowning in his own vomit, Subaru kept right on retching, spectacularly, without end.

No matter how much he threw up, his head kept spinning, his teeth kept chattering, and his whole body wouldn't stop shaking. It wasn't that he felt cold. It was a sensation Subaru knew well—as if some unseen hand were wringing his stomach out, shuffling every one of his organs around without pause.
This was, unmistakably—

"—The fact that you're this afraid is proof of how kind you are."

The gentle voice came from behind him, and Subaru retched again.
Nearly drowning in the stomach acid overflowing from him, he too fell face-first onto the floor, soiled with vomit. The sticky, slick sensation of liquid against his cheek. With his face pressed into the filth pooled on the floor, yellow bubbles popped audibly with every shallow breath he took.

Sirius watched this scene of misery—the kind that made you want to look away—with a fond, endeared smile. Watching Subaru and Lusbel, heads lying side by side, desperately drawing breath amid their own vomit, their whole bodies still overtaken by uncontrollable trembling.

"People can understand one another. People can become one. Kindness does not exist for oneself. It exists for others. Kindness only shines when it's given to someone else. Being kind to yourself is nothing but selfishness—something altogether too different from true kindness! And so, the kindness you're showing right now, like this—it's the real, genuine radiance of thinking of another! Ah, it is 'love'!"

"Guh... ah, hh..."

"Please feel it to your heart's content. Please show it to me, to your heart's content. Your 'love.' That boundless kindness of yours. The nobility that made you want to save Lusbel-kun!"

Before the two of them, smeared with vomit and eyes rolled back to white, Sirius did a light little tap-dance step.
Then the monster crossed both arms, pointing at Subaru and Lusbel alternately with each hand while swaying his hips. As if dancing. As if singing praises.

"Lusbel-kun's fear, felt together by you, in your kindness. The fear of Lusbel-kun's that you've felt, felt in turn by Lusbel-kun, through you. The fear Lusbel-kun felt through you, now felt once more, in turn, by you, from Lusbel-kun. The fear you've felt once again, piled once more atop Lusbel-kun's own fear. The fear of yours that Lusbel-kun has piled on top, felt by you together with everything piled beneath it up till now. The fear you've felt all together as one whole, felt by Lusbel-kun together with a fresh, brand-new fear, and the fear brimming with Lusbel-kun's freshness, inherited by you, becoming true fear. The true fear that you feel, taken up by Lusbel-kun, giving birth to a second, a third fear, terrifying Lusbel-kun anew, and the next fear born within Lusbel-kun, wrapped by you in your own fear, becoming the greatest fear of all..."

It feels like something is being whispered right into his ear. Delusions, nonsense, closing in with overwhelming force. Subaru has no room left to make sense of any of it now, because right now, for Subaru, everything reaching his ears, everything reaching his eyes, is nothing but an object of fear—if breathing itself is fear, then even blinking feels like nothing else but fear, and yet if he doesn't blink he won't be able to bear the pain of his eyes drying out, and even that pain, for Subaru right now, is nothing but a symbol of fear, and feeling pain means contemplating the possibility of feeling the next pain, which makes Subaru feel an endless, never-ending, infinite fear, so he cannot not blink, and yet if he blinks, even for a single instant the world will be shut away into darkness, and in that instant of seeing nothing at all he has no way of knowing what's happening, of course nothing is happening, that goes without saying, but he has no means whatsoever of confirming that for certain, and the inability to confirm it is fear itself, and if the inability to understand is the very thing that calls forth fear from instinct, then to live is precisely to overcome fear through understanding, and to begin with, fear is an emotion that springs from weakness, the kind that only reveals itself in a living creature when its life is under the most extreme kind of threat, and to be capable of feeling it means, in equal measure, that the creature possesses something it is capable of fearing, and this function of feeling fear shares something in common with the function of feeling pain, which means that in order to sustain life, the instinct to sense danger can never be severed from it, and to be numb to fear is, without exaggeration, to be numb to the value of life—

"Oh my? It seems he's gone and lost his mind. Those who are deeply loving and highly sensitive sometimes become terribly fragile. Ah, it's because of 'love' that people suffer. But it's also because of 'love' that people can go on living. Such a difficult thing, isn't it. Well then, let's have Tina-chan lend us a hand. Lusbel-kun, you did wonderfully—thank you for your hard work."

—to be numb to the value of life is to run counter to a living being's inborn function to survive, which means fear is a necessary thing, and so to be feeling fear like this, right now, is something to take pride in rather than something to be ashamed of, of course none of that means anything at all, it's nothing but a hollow hypothetical, but by repeating this thought experiment, over and over, resisting the fear that has, in this moment, taken over his entire body, and struggling, striving to overcome it—isn't that the last, the greatest resistance there is left to break through this situation—that's what Subaru is thinking, while in front of him Lusbel's convulsions were gradually subsiding, and from the boy's white-rolled eyes the flame of his life was gradually slipping away, and however wretched that made him feel, his heart, his heart alone, must never be allowed to break, his heart will not break, he will keep fighting, that's what Subaru swore, just as he had, in that terrifying, agonizing, sorrowful trial a year ago, because if not, then what had Subaru been suffering for, grieving for, still, even now, living on with everything he had for scared scared everything's scared everything is scared living's scared blinking's scared breathing's scared it stinks scared feels sick ubebe ubebebebe bababababa ababababababababa radaggab bahbababa.

"For our little chat after the song, don't you think it might be nice if Natsuki-sama went and prepared some snacks? I'm sure if you went and got some sweets ready, everyone's hearts would just leap right up, and it'd bring us all so much closer, don't you think? Don't you think?"

The instant he blinked, the world swapped out from under him, and Subaru lost his footing as he listened to the voice. The face of the girl in front of him, mid-clumsy-wink, came rushing closer, and their foreheads collided.

"Gwahh?!"

"Ow—?!"

A hard crack rang out, and sparks scattered across his vision.
Subaru reeled back from the sharp pain, stumbling backward. In front of him came the sound of something collapsing onto the grass, but with his hand pressed to his forehead, he couldn't confirm it right away.

"Wh-what just...?"

"'What just' nothing. Subaru, you just headbutted Liliana out of nowhere. You can't do that—if you don't like something she's doing, you have to say something first."

"That's right, I suppose. Before resorting to violence, maybe you ought to have warned her first—quit that hideous wink or I'll flatten you—rather than going straight to force, I wonder."

"Was it really that hideous!?"

Liliana leapt up, her voice full of indignation.
At her words, Emilia and Beatrice exchanged a look that said everything and nothing at once. Liliana, shocked, crumpled right back down where she stood.

"What a ridiculous farce, I say. Fool, do not be so rough with my little bird. There won't be a next time."

Perhaps displeased at seeing violence done to Liliana, Priscilla, unusually, had something to say about Subaru's conduct.
Subaru gave a vague nod in response, and reoriented himself to confirm just where he was, and—

"...He's disgusting."
