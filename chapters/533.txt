Arc 7 Chapter 35: "A Night of Conversation"

"The place me and my bro were at, man, it was a real rough place."

At night, gathered around the campfire for their encampment, Medium said this in her usual easy way.
Always bright and cheerful, Medium didn't know how to lower her voice. What came from her mouth this time was the story of the facility she and Flop had once been in, or so Subaru had heard.

Subaru had already heard from Flop that the two of them had grown up in poor conditions.
The siblings had been raised in an institution that took in orphans, beaten every day, he'd said. And he'd spoken of his fury at a world where unhappy adults struck unhappy children, and of the vow of revenge he'd sworn because of it.

"I don't really get all the hard stuff my bro says, not all of it anyway. But when he's walking around with his chest puffed out and taking big strides, I just wanna cheer him on."

"Even if it's revenge against the world, you mean?"

"Yeah, yeah! Well, I don't really get how he's gonna do it, though."

Grinning bashfully as if embarrassed, Medium sat cross-legged with a plop, combing Rui's hair from behind where the girl sat on her lap.
The topic of her facility had come up in the first place because of how deftly she handled the younger girl.
It might be an odd thing to say, but Medium generally gave the impression of doing everything in a rather rough-and-ready way, so it had been surprising to see her looking after Rui so attentively.
When Subaru had asked about her practiced touch, that was how the story had come out.

"There were other kids at the facility besides me and my bro, see. Some even smaller than me. It wasn't much fun, so I figure the least a kid should get is to let their hair grow out nice and easy."

"So that's why you're so good with the younger ones. You do have a knack for looking after people, Medium-san. That makes sense."

"Uheh, you think so? Well, glad I could be useful, then!"

Lit by the red glow of the campfire, Medium's beautiful blonde hair sparkled. Rui, sitting in front of her, had the same hair color, and the two of them, so affectionate together, looked just like sisters.
If you counted Flop's alter ego "Flora" as well, you could call them a trio of beautiful sisters.

"...This won't do. Thinking of pushing such a debt onto Flop-san and Medium-san."

"—? Did you say something, Natsumi-chan?"

"No, it's nothing. I was simply realizing, once again, how much we're being helped by having you here, Medium-san."

Setting aside the inappropriate arrangement between the O'Connell siblings and Rui, what Subaru said to cover her slip was nonetheless the unvarnished truth.
Both in terms of fighting strength and in terms of morale, there was a great deal Subaru's group owed to Medium and her presence.
Without Flop and her, Subaru's pilgrimage through the empire would have been a far bleaker thing.

"On that note, don't you think you ought to be a bit more grateful?"

"——"

Narrowing her eyes as she spoke, Subaru directed the remark at Abel, who sat warming himself by the fire.
In this respite after dinner, Abel had surprisingly not retreated at once to the carriage, but had lingered on in silence. Even so, his attention hardly seemed bent toward idle chatter, so Subaru's words were nothing more than sarcasm and mockery.

"Medium. Where are you and your brother from?"

"Huh?"

And yet, without responding to Subaru's words, Abel posed that question to Medium.
The content of the question followed the flow of the preceding conversation.
Caught off guard, Medium opened her large eyes as wide as they would go and let her mouth hang open. It seemed she was startled both that Abel had spoken to her at all, and that he'd been listening to the conversation.

"Abel-chin, you knew my name?"

Apparently that wasn't the only reason for her surprise.
In any case, faced with a form of address every bit as liable to offend His Imperial Majesty as anything Al might come up with, Abel let out a small sigh and said,

"I remember names, at least. Spare me your pointless admiration. Answer my question. Where are you and Flop from? And who oversaw that facility?"

"Oversaw, you mean like the headmaster? I forget his name. But me and my bro were in a little town called Averyk."

"Averyk... a western town. I'll remember it."

"—? What're you gonna remember it for?"

"For appropriate measures. Whether I see to it myself is another matter."

Abel's answer was concise, but its true meaning didn't reach Medium.
A question mark floating over her head, Medium's expression became one of someone who'd stuck their head into an even larger cloud of confusion. And the unhelpful Abel made no effort to untangle the mystery for her.
Subaru, too, hadn't grasped the whole of his thinking, but—

"Listening to the honest voice of the common people, so you can put it to use in governing right away, is that it?"

"I'm not so virtuous as that. I told you before. It is a matter of due reward and due punishment."

Reward for effort; punishment for folly.
That, it seemed, was Abel's creed as a ruler, an immovable article of his character.
Thinking back on it, even at the Lifeblood Ritual in the Shudrak settlement, with Subaru on the verge of death, Abel had desperately pressed her to voice a wish. Behind that desperation lay the same creed.
Which was to say—

"—You're the sort who can't stand to see other people walking away empty-handed, aren't you."

"Most beings are born possessing nothing. What they grasp with their hands, what they hold as they die, that is the whole of their life. To gain the right to hold something and then let it slip away — that must never be allowed."

"Being born possessing nothing — hearing that from the mouth of His Imperial Majesty is rather galling, you know."

Most people were born with nothing, and however much they lamented their own lack of talent or strength, all they could do was struggle on. Even knowing that was simply life, hearing it spoken from the position of the privileged left one with nowhere to stand.
But to Subaru, who'd taken it as sarcasm, Abel replied without so much as meeting her eyes,

"—Nor am I an exception."

"—? What do you mean?"

"A position carries a burden to match. Bear a weight you cannot carry, and a fool is simply crushed beneath it. Bearing and pride alike can only be polished through daily self-recognition."

Answering Subaru's question, Abel's gaze slowly turned toward her.
He looked her over — Subaru warming herself by the fire, her black hair, her clothing — as if inspecting them, and said,

"A false, manufactured self-image will eventually reveal the base metal beneath. You seem quite practiced at your disguise, but all the more labor it will take to smooth things over once it peels away."

"——"

"So long as you produce results, I have no interest in others' tastes and preferences. I don't intend to go back on that. But it is unbearable to watch someone prop themselves up on an illusion. Sooner or later, the whole foundation will tilt."

Bathed in the heat-laden wind, Abel's gaze was as piercing as the cold of night.
The words he chose showed no consideration for others, no room made for understanding. And so their true meaning reached Subaru only by half.
All that remained was the ache of having had her heart violated with terrible bluntness.

"...I've said too much. The rest, I leave to you."

Leaving those words behind, Abel rose and vanished into the carriage.
The sound of the heavy door closing followed, and left behind around the fire were only Subaru, Medium, and Rui, who was passing the time none the wiser about what had been said.

"...What is with that man."

The words slipped out, and Subaru caught herself, pressing a hand to her own mouth with a bitter expression.
It sounded exactly like the sour-grapes muttering of some villainess in a melodrama. Natsumi Schwarz, the iron-blooded lady strategist, was supposed to be sturdier and more supple than that.
The heart of steel Subaru so longed for — the image of the strongest woman who possessed such a thing.

"Natsumi-chan, you okay?"

As Subaru wrestled with such conflicting feelings, Medium's hand gently stroked her head.
Still cross-legged, Medium had deftly hoisted herself up and shuffled over to sit beside her. Her palm was sized to match her tall frame, large enough to easily cup Subaru's whole head.
That large palm, gentle and soft, wrapped Subaru's heart in warmth.

"Yes, I'm fine. Honestly, that man, so full of insinuation... Medium-san, did you understand what he was trying to say?"

"Nnn-nope, not a clue! But I could tell Natsumi-chan looked like she was having a hard time, and this is about all I can do."

"That's not—"

"It's fine, it's fine, I know! I'm the one in charge of doing my best with this big body of mine — the hard stuff, I leave that to my bro and everyone else."

Grinning a good-natured grin, Medium kept stroking Subaru's head, and there wasn't a shred of falsehood in her expression.
Aware of her own shortcomings but never letting them weigh on her, her whole way of being was so forward-looking, something that resonated with the people Subaru admired most.

"You're quite the grown-up, Medium-san."

"Nobody's ever said that to me before! I've been told I'm a good big sister, or that it feels nice watching me eat a lot, but never that."

"That's also one of your charming points, Medium-san."

"Uwehehe~"

However many words she spent, Subaru couldn't fully convey her gratitude and admiration, and it frustrated her. But Medium happily accepted even Subaru's rather empty compliments.
Curled up on her lap, even Rui seemed cheerful, as if caught up in the good mood.

Just for this one moment, everything — every hardship, every anxiety — felt cut off and far away.
It was such a peaceful stretch of time that it felt almost like an illusion.


△▼△▼△▼△


Flickering, watching the swaying flames of the campfire, time flowed on gently.

"——"

Amid the crackling pop of burning wood, the world was very quiet.
There had been a time when Subaru hated stretches of empty time like this. Left to drift idly, she used to be tormented by an unidentifiable restlessness, driven to feel as though something were chasing her.

Did she think she was allowed to simply pass the time in a daze, doing nothing?
Some formless black shadow would creep up behind her, sling an arm over her shoulder with false familiarity, and needle her, clinging and insistent.
Even with eyes shut and ears covered, that nightmare could not be kept at bay — and to escape it, Natsuki Subaru had taken up every hobby and pursuit under the sun.

Sometimes she was praised for having many hobbies, for being deft and versatile.
But all of it had been nothing but an excuse to flee from responsibility and guilt.

A papier-mâché figure built up out of piled excuses — that was Natsuki Subaru, and—

"—I'm just a little relieved that it didn't end as nothing but an excuse."

"Is... that sO..."

Having finished listening to Subaru's story, Taritta nodded, her head lowered.
Beyond the swaying flames, Taritta, seated with one knee raised off the ground, had her face lit red by the fire. The flickering light of the flames suited her tan skin well.
Speaking of suiting her — her outfit, meant to hide the fact that she was Shudrak, suited her too.
With the white markings the Shudrak bore on their bodies erased, and dressed in clothes befitting a civilized person, Taritta, with her slender height added to the effect, looked every bit the beautiful woman in men's clothing.
Though, standing watch as night sentry, she'd shed her jacket and rolled up her sleeves, an outfit that left an impression of wildness rather than smartness.

"——"

In the brief silence that fell, Taritta was lost in thought.
Subaru's own story, told at Taritta's urging — of her original world, and of darker, heavier things kept deliberately vague — what effect had it had on Taritta?
The trigger for it had been Taritta watching Subaru tend to her nails and asking, "Where did you learn to do makeup?"

Makeup, and cross-dressing besides — neither had come from any grand impetus.
Subaru, having spent a miserable middle school life, had simply taken it on as a bit of a lark, a way to make a splashy comeback in the high school life to come.

"It's just, I do have a rather obsessive streak... once I decide to do something, I go all the way, wanting to produce something I needn't be ashamed to show off."

Whatever the path, the refinement of a skill rests on the effort of countless who came before.
Trying to show an attitude worthy of that legacy, Subaru's high school life had come to an end. Since then, she'd sworn never to cross-dress again — and yet, here she was.
You never know what will prove useful in life until it's over. In Subaru's case, it never really was over even when it ended, which made her feel that all the more.

"Not that it's much of a laughing matter—"

"I envy Natsumi'S attitude."

"—Wait, wh-what!?"

"W-why are you so surprised...!?"

"Ah, no, it's simply not something I'm often told..."

It had been quite a genuine shock, and it seemed to have startled Taritta rather badly in turn.
Even so, the shock in Subaru's heart, hand pressed to her false chest, didn't fade easily. Objectively speaking, Subaru didn't think her own position was something to be envied.

Even setting aside her own perspective — ever since coming to this other world, and if she looked back, even before that — Subaru's life had been an unbroken string of failure and disappointment.
Honestly, without meeting Emilia, she doubted there would have been any chance of things turning positive at all.
Every encounter and happiness since, she believed, all traced back to that alley, to Emilia trying to save Subaru from being kicked around by thugs.
—Though the memory of that moment no longer existed within Emilia herself.

"I grew up hiding behind my sister, always. My sister's the sort of person you know, so no one ever doubted she'd become chief someday. Not even me."

"Taritta-san..."

Haltingly, Taritta began to speak of her own circumstances.
At first, Subaru had meant to stop her, thinking that if this was Taritta returning the favor of confession with something she didn't wish to say, it wasn't necessary. The fact that Subaru had ended up telling her own life story after being asked about the source of her makeup and cross-dressing skills was simply a matter of how Subaru had steered the conversation. She had no intention of forcing this on her.

But hearing the tremor in Taritta's voice, seeing her damp eyes as she spoke with her head bowed, Subaru let that thought go.
It seemed to Subaru that Taritta was speaking because she wanted to speak.

"My sister and I are three years apart. But the reason my sister feels so much bigger to me isn't just those three years. Because, isn't that so?"

"——"

"Whatever my sister could do at ten, I still can't do at the same age. That's not a difference of years. It's a difference of something else entirely. I don't know what."

Every word Taritta spoke pierced straight into Subaru's chest.
It was a story she'd heard somewhere before, and at the same time, one she'd never confided to anyone.

An inferiority complex toward a brilliant older sister — that was what Rem had carried.
An inferiority complex toward a back she'd once admired — that was what had tormented Subaru.

"Why did my sister choose me as chief? I can't imagine I'm suited for it, not at all."

"—So, you really are that man's child after all."

Disappointment in herself, and guilt at the expectations placed on her by someone precious.
On the verge of being crushed by it, Taritta had nonetheless joined this journey. Mizelda, who had been chief, had sworn her cooperation, joining Abel's road toward reclaiming the throne.
That journey might be, for Taritta, a chance to look at herself anew and come to accept herself — and at the same time, a means of fleeing the crushing weight of expectation laid upon her.

"By the end of this journey, I have to find an answer. No — I have to make up my mind."

"Make up your mind... you mean, to accept succeeding your sister as chief?"

"——"

At Subaru's question, Taritta drew in her slender chin and nodded.
Not "find an answer," but "make up her mind" — the appropriate word choice revealed the fate she saw before her: how she viewed the role of next chief entrusted to her by Mizelda.

Taritta believed that inheriting the position of chief was unavoidable.
That she had no right to refuse it. That it was the duty of a younger sister, chosen by a great elder sister, to lead the next generation of the tribe—

"Running away..."

"Eh?"

"Running away is fine too, I think. I wouldn't blame you for it."

The look on Taritta's face, eyes going wide, spoke of how unexpected those words were.
Even if the conversation had led them there, Taritta had opened up about what was in her heart. Subaru could see the situation she was in, the anxieties she carried, the burden placed on her slender shoulders.
And could see, too, how her strong sense of responsibility, her tendency toward self-blame, made her desperately try to shoulder all of it alone.

Perhaps what Taritta had wanted, here, was for Subaru to tell her "pull yourself together," to give her the push she couldn't give herself toward the resolve she couldn't quite reach. Perhaps, seeing Subaru cross-dress so boldly, without a trace of shame, she'd hoped for reassurance that a lack of confidence was foreign to her.

But what Subaru said was words that betrayed that expectation.

"If you feel you can't bear it, if you think there's someone better suited than you, then you can pack your things and leave the carriage, and we won't blame you for it. At least, I won't."

"B-but if I'm gone, our fighting strength—"

"Of course, that's a real worry. But we'll manage somehow."

"——"

*I'm saying whatever I please*, Subaru thought self-mockingly to herself, though she kept it from showing on her face.
If Abel heard this conversation, he'd surely be furious. Just as Taritta said, she was a precious asset to a camp short on fighting strength.
That held true for the group as a whole, and for this traveling party in particular.
And yet here Subaru was, saying it would be fine even to let her go.

"You mean I'm... not needed..."

"No, that's a great misunderstanding. I, too, want you to stay with us, Taritta-san — for your character as much as for your strength in battle. But isn't that my own selfishness?"

"Selfishness..."

"Because I'd be telling you to kill your own heart, just so that I can go on living."

As someone who had experience with this, who was of the same kind, Subaru couldn't force that on Taritta.
To Taritta, who was older, and who might have far more seniority than Subaru when it came to this particular anguish, Subaru — having only just been given the chance to climb out of that same darkness herself — spoke with the air of a senior all the same.

—One cannot become someone else.

However much you admired them, envied them, however deeply the image of them was burned into you until it scorched — even if they were kin, even if they were close to you, you could not become them.

"We can't become anything other than 'ourselves.'"

And so, at the very least, become the "self" you can come to like, that you can accept, that you can have confidence in.
Repeating disappointment and discouragement, and just a small measure of accomplishment, over and over — like a butterfly emerging from its chrysalis.

"...How pretentious of me."

She murmured it in a voice too soft for Taritta to hear.
What she'd tasted while speaking was the same theory Abel had voiced a few hours earlier around this very fire. That many who possessed nothing lived by grasping at something with their empty hands.
The essence of that argument seemed, to her, to overlap with what she herself had wanted to say.

"——"

Hearing Subaru's words, Taritta's eyes wavered, her uncertainty growing stronger still.
Which way that uncertainty would settle was something only Taritta could decide, but whatever she chose, Subaru felt it ought to be respected.

Subaru herself had once fled from the expectations placed on her, and from the guilt of being unable to meet them.
She'd even wondered, at times, whether her being summoned to another world had been some meddler's way of indulging that very urge to flee.
Of course, that was no excuse for never having said goodbye to her parents — her real parents—

"Being able to run away was, in its own way, a kind of salvation."

At the very least, if she'd remained shut up in her room, forever caught between expectation and guilt, she never would have caught up to the way Natsuki Subaru's heart felt now.
She'd have remained the Subaru who was too consumed with her own troubles to spare any thought for doing something for someone else, for wanting to help someone.

Running away, until you're ready to fight, isn't a bad thing.
Or perhaps, choosing not to fight at all ought to be an option too, in a world like this.

"Confidence, resolve..."

"Eh?"

"Confidence, resolve — I lack those too. But it's not only that..."

Hearing Subaru's account drawn from her own real experience, Taritta murmured, her lips trembling.
Her voice was so faint it seemed it might be swallowed by the crackling pop of the fire, and yet it carried an emotion even more urgent than when she'd spoken of her own powerlessness.

"If... if I had committed some great wrong... how could I make up for it?"

"A wrong, and making up for it... you mean you, Taritta-san?"

"——"

Asked back, Taritta let out a small "Ah."
The regret that flickered through her wide eyes seemed, much like the wrong she'd just spoken of, to be regret at having let this slip to Subaru here.

Apparently, there was some great guilt Taritta was carrying, over some wrong she'd committed.
Beyond the sense of powerlessness from comparing herself to her sister, that guilt too seemed to be a factor keeping her from being able to decide for herself.

"What a strange thing to say... please, forget it."

What followed, in the end, was a conclusion of leaving an untold story untold.
Taritta's voice and expression made clear she'd by no means arrived at an answer, but Subaru understood that she couldn't force any more out of her than this.
If, someday, Taritta wanted to let it out, Subaru only hoped she could be there for her then—

"—Brother, it's about time to switch."

Just as Subaru reached that conclusion, a voice called out from the direction of the carriage.
Rolling his thick neck, it was Al, walking slowly toward the fire. Night watch rotated every three hours, and it seemed Subaru's turn had come.

"I do object to Abel and Rui being excluded from the rotation..."

"Nah, don't even start. If Abel-chan gets back to the imperial throne and finds out he got executed because he was pulled for night watch duty, that'd be pretty bad, right?"

"If we're going to start talking about grudges, I think we'd lose track of how many of my own heads would need to be lined up."

"If you're self-aware about it, rein it in, would ya. Being around you makes my heart race with worry."

Catching Subaru's grumbling, Al voiced a reasonable complaint.
Even so, Subaru's stance of wanting to speak up about Abel's attitude wasn't something she could let go of going forward. If no one ever said anything to him, that was exactly why Abel got away with being such an arrogant, insufferable tyrant.
Some correction of his character needed to happen now, while he was a fugitive without real power, however small.

"Otherwise, even if he returns to the throne thanks to our efforts, another rebellion will spring up right after, and this time his head really will roll."

"Ahh, I'll leave that one to you. Like you can tell from how I am with the princess, I'm basically a live-and-let-live, hands-off kind of guy."

Waving a hand airily, Al gave up on trying to change Subaru's mind.
Sighing at Al's unreliable manner, Subaru turned once more to face Taritta. Calling out "Taritta-san" to her as she sat gazing into the fire, lost in thought, Subaru said,

"I'll be retiring now. If Al's sexual harassment or dreadful jokes become unbearable, do let me know at once."

"Sexual... harassment...?"

"I don't pull that kinda stuff with the princess! I keep it appropriate, matched to distance and closeness. TPO, you know."

"That's exactly the sort of thing I'm no good with."

Taritta answered in a faint voice, while Al loudly proclaimed his innocence.
Glancing between the two, Subaru rose, brushing the dirt from her hem and backside, and quietly leaned in to murmur to Al.

"Taritta-san might come to you for some life advice. If she does, please guide her as a seasoned elder with plenty of experience."

"'Seasoned elder with plenty of experience' — doesn't that feel like the exact opposite of how you'd describe me? I can't take responsibility for anyone else's life, that's exactly why I've spent my whole life actively running from that kind of thing."

"It's a chance to overcome something you're bad at. Same as with Peppir."

Even Emilia and Beatrice, who weren't fond of Peppir, never lost their bold fighting spirit in trying to shed their likes and dislikes, striving to overcome him in various ways.
So far, neither of them had won a single time, but Subaru believed that as long as they kept fighting, a day of victory would come eventually.

"So, do your best too, Al."

"Am I the only one who feels like that didn't land at all just now..."

At the very least, Subaru's own conclusion was that the words just now met all the conditions to be inspiring — meaning the problem lay with Al, for not being moved by them.

"Natsumi, until tomorrow... then."

"—. Yes. Until tomorrow."

Yielding her spot by the fire to Al, Subaru turned to head back to the carriage when Taritta called out to her.
That faint but certain promise of tomorrow made Subaru hope — even as Taritta took in her words — that tomorrow, they would still be able to face each other.

Running away isn't a bad thing. There's a strength to be gained from running away, too.
But there are surely also people who decide to fight without running, and who manage to win that way.
Subaru found herself hoping that Taritta would be one of them.


"——"

Returning to the carriage, the dark, hushed air within greeted Subaru.
The interior was partitioned by a simple curtain, dividing the sleeping areas for men and women, front and back. The front for the men, the back for the women's group — and yet the whole party, asleep, was surprisingly quiet.

Resting now were Abel, Medium, and Rui, three in all — but while Abel's silence was to be expected, it was rather surprising that Medium's and Rui's breathing was so quiet too.
When Subaru had once asked, despite knowing it was a breach of manners to bring up someone's sleeping habits, Medium had answered cheerfully, "If you snore too loud, people nearby figure out where you're hiding, y'know."
It was only some time after hearing that that Subaru realized it must have been from her days traveling as a peddler, or perhaps from her days at that supposedly wretched facility.

A skill acquired out of necessity.
In that sense, the reason Abel too slept so quietly was probably the same—

"...Even asleep, that's an infuriating face. Maybe I ought to draw on it."

"—What is it, staring at me so rudely."

"—!"

Partway through the carriage, in the middle of the allotted space, Abel lay sleeping. Peering down at his sleeping face, Subaru was startled by his sudden voice. Nearly letting out a shriek, she managed to hold it back and asked, voice cracking, "You were awake?"

"...Here we are, doing our best on night watch, and I'd have thought you might at least fully benefit from it."

"Sleeping with one eye open is a custom of the Vollachian imperial line. I am no exception to it."

"One eye open — this isn't a movie, there's no way— wait, are you serious?"

"Why would I bother telling you a pointless joke."

Countered by Abel, calmly keeping one eye open just as he'd declared, Subaru held her breath.
The light in that black eye proved his words weren't a lie.

There had been an old movie where an assassin was said to have the habit of sleeping with one eye open.
She'd heard that, structurally, the human brain made that difficult — but seeing Abel doing exactly that before her eyes made clear it wasn't impossible after all.
But that was a different matter entirely from accepting it.

"Why can't you sleep properly? Do you think we'd slit your throat in your sleep?"

"Even the smallest possibility exists. One cannot afford to lose one's life through carelessness."

"Carelessness...!"

Hearing his flat reply, Subaru's voice trembled, and she pointed out the window.
Visible through the carriage window was the glow of the campfire a short distance away. Al and Taritta stood there as night watch, keeping guard against approaching danger.
Of course, it was also to protect their own safety, but—

"—Who exactly do you think is doing that, and for whose sake?"

"——"

"Even in a situation like this, you still can't close both eyes in front of us?"

Natsumi Schwarz's skin peeled away, and from beneath it, twisted with expression, emerged Natsuki Subaru.
Witnessing it firsthand, Abel's piercing expression didn't waver in the slightest. As ever, he kept to his established habit, blinking with his right and left eye in alternation.
Even the number of those blinks was kept to the bare minimum — for the same reason he couldn't sleep with both eyes shut.

It had been the same when the two of them had risked their lives together at the Lifeblood Ritual, the same when they'd infiltrated Guaral disguised as dancers, and the same on this road now, riding in the carriage toward their destination.

Subaru sympathized with the position of someone who had to stay perpetually on guard, in a situation that allowed no room for failure.
But then couldn't he, at the very least, make some effort to let his guard down before heading into what might well become enemy territory?

At Subaru's words, eyebrows raised as she said so, Abel closed his eyes. —One of them.
Closing one eye, crossing his arms, leaning back against the seat, he gave a snort.

"Do not flatter yourself that you might warp my way of being. Know your place."

"——"

"The Divine Capital draws near. See to your own role. I ask no more of you than that, and I will permit no more, either."

Faced with that rejection, offering no foothold at all, Subaru clicked her tongue.
She'd thought, perhaps, there might have been some small room to close the distance between them, but no, it was hopeless after all.

"Fine then... oh, fine! I'll be sure to snore loud enough for you to hear."

Her voice rose with irritation, but Abel gave no response to it.
Face twisted with bitterness, Subaru settled into a bed as far from Abel as she could manage, loosening her clothes and lying down carefully so as not to damage her wig.

She half-considered staying awake out of sheer defiance toward Abel, but couldn't see the point in doing so, nor which — sleeping or staying awake — was the "correct" answer, and her consciousness faded into white before she could decide.


The carriage journey continued on, carrying with it all manner of problems, anxieties, and tangled relationships.

—Their arrival at the Divine Capital, Chaosflame, was now close at hand.
