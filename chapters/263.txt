Arc 4 Chapter 95: "Sigma"

"Barusu. Are your preparations for your scheming coming along?"

He'd left Emilia, cried out and exhausted, and stepped out of the building, when Ram called out to him with those words.

Still with his hand on the door, Subaru narrowed his eyes at Ram, who apparently had been waiting outside for him.

"Scheming, huh? That makes me sound way worse than I am — I'd appreciate it if you dropped that word."

"You two men have been sneaking around, plotting and hiding all sorts of things from everyone else. Since Roswaal-sama has already given his approval, I won't say anything about that merchant having free run of the Sanctuary, but..."

The "merchant" Ram referred to was likely Otto.

Even though they spent their days in the same Sanctuary, Ram devoted most of her time to caring for Roswaal, so the time she'd actually spent with Otto was quite short. At best, there might be a chance to exchange a few words while waiting outside for Emilia as she faced the Trials.

Since Subaru had brought Otto along on his own initiative, it was only natural that Ram—who knew nothing of his character or his track record—found him lacking in trustworthiness.

Even to Subaru, Otto's character aside, his actual ability as a merchant remained something of an unknown quantity. When Subaru had introduced him to Roswaal, it had looked to him as though Otto had been valued less for his promise as a merchant than for his aptitude as a straight man for jokes.

Still, it was a fact that Roswaal had permitted Otto to stay in the Sanctuary. That alone meant Ram couldn't openly reject him, and so her eyes remained coldly critical of Otto.

"Well, that's a part he's gotta work hard at himself, so I'm not gonna bother setting the record straight for him."

"That's a poor thing to say about a friend. He worries about you so much—shouldn't you be the one repaying him a little?"

"A friend, huh... Well, I can't really argue with that, but wait—you actually see me and him as friends?"

"The way you two carry on together, so familiar, cutting loose like that... and you're saying you're not friends? If you could act that way toward a total stranger with no connection to you at all, that would be far more unsettling."

Ram hugged her own shoulders, giving a little shudder for emphasis. At her words and reaction, Subaru's cheeks loosened slightly and he chuckled low in his throat.

Perhaps finding Subaru's suppressed laughter unsettling, Ram, still hugging her own shoulders, took a step back to put some distance between them, and said,

"Now then, Barusu, I'll repeat my first question. —Are your preparations for your scheming coming along?"

"'Coming along smoothly' would be a stretch — there's a lot of parts that are hitting snags."

Whatever the details, it was only natural that the true intent behind his scheming had been seen through.

Ram stood, in a sense, as one of the cards in the hand of Roswaal—the man Subaru was in a contractual standoff against. Subaru couldn't say how much stock to put in it, but Ram was surely serving as Roswaal's eyes and ears while he lay injured and unable to move.

It followed naturally that she'd be keeping tabs on Subaru's movements. That she'd come right out and ask him about it so bluntly, though, was very much like Ram.

"You're one to talk—didn't you come here for Emilia's sake? No point wasting all your time talking to just me, is there?"

"The fact that you've come out here means you at least saw her off to sleep, didn't you? After screaming and crying that much, she's not likely to wake up again so soon."

"...That's a pretty harsh opinion."

"It's an objective, straightforward opinion. Take it to heart."

Saying so with utter indifference, Ram glared at Subaru's downcast profile.

Cowed by the pressure of her gaze, Subaru swallowed a sigh inwardly.

—The Contract between Emilia and Puck had been severed about two and a half hours ago now.

Having heard it from Puck beforehand, Subaru had gone in with some understanding of, and readiness for, the shock, confusion, and grief Emilia would feel once the Contract broke—and yet, when he actually saw her, the ache of something like homesickness that swept over him was beyond words.

Emilia, having lost her sole means of support, wailed and sobbed, half out of her mind.

She thrashed her beautiful silver hair into disarray, dug her nails into her white skin, and flung whatever was within reach in every direction—baring her raw emotions like a child's tantrum.

That the option of rampaging with Magic hadn't occurred to her in her deranged state was, perhaps, the one small mercy amid the misfortune—both for those around her and for herself.

In any case, having known in advance of the shock awaiting Emilia, Subaru had waited outside the room, and the moment he heard her scream, he'd rushed in and gathered her tightly into his arms.

For the two and a half hours since, he had taken on, alone, the full brunt of Emilia's broken, fragmented voice, her grief, and her feeble destructive impulses, and now, having left her asleep, he'd stepped out.

His encounter with Ram, right as he was thinking of leaving the changing of clothes and wiping down her body to her, was likely because Ram herself had been waiting there with the same intention. The sight of her holding a basin of water and a cloth gave him something of a saved feeling.

To Subaru, feeling this way, Ram said in a low voice,

"Barusu. —Do you truly have hope for that woman?"

"................"

"Anyone who's seen how Emilia-sama has been up until yesterday knows there's not a single bright sign that she'll overcome the Trials. And on top of that, a situation I thought couldn't possibly get any worse has gotten worse still. The Great Spirit has left her side, hasn't he?"

"...You've figured out that much already."

"If she's been screaming that loudly, over and over, loud enough to be heard outside the building, even a fool would notice. If even Barusu can notice, then of course Ram can too. Obviously."

"Feels like there's a formula there equating me with 'fool,' but I can't exactly deny it. And that Emilia's situation has gotten even worse—that's an undeniable fact too."

Honestly, Ram's concerns were more or less a direct expression of Subaru's own anxieties.

It wasn't that he'd swallowed Puck's words whole without question, but it was a fact that Subaru had supported his proposal.

Puck's claim that his own presence was acting as a stopper preventing Emilia from ever facing her true past. The doubts Subaru had harbored, not a few of them, about the changes from loop to loop and about the Gospel in Roswaal's possession. Beatrice's outcry, Echidna's testimony, Puck's very existence.

Various elements had intertwined, and Subaru had backed Puck's decision, throwing Emilia into a cage of solitude. As a result, for the first time since she was born, she was truly, in the fullest sense, alone.

What she needed to climb out of that cage was time, and a trigger.

But Emilia currently lacked both. The circumstances gave her no grace period in which to calm down and face things, and the trigger that would serve as the detonator existed only within her.

As for that latter trigger, only Emilia herself could find it. Ram understood this too, and it was likely why she couldn't bring herself to believe in that detonation happening.

Except for that last part, Subaru was in complete agreement with Ram.

"Even so, I have hope for that girl, and I believe in her."

"...What a bright, cheerful face. I wonder just how one manages to arrive at such an easygoing conclusion."

"I decided that I'd believe in the things worth believing in. Things like a 'friend' who works hard for my sake, or my beloved dragon—being saved by their existence has let me start believing in myself a little too."

"So how exactly does that connect to believing in Emilia-sama...?"

"Once you can believe in yourself, you start wanting to believe in whatever you've pointed your feelings at, right? I like Emilia, and I want to be of help to her. Sure, part of why I like her is that she's totally my type looks-wise... but the part I'm serious about is somewhere else entirely."

Meeting Ram's dubious gaze, Subaru shrugged.

The reason Subaru had first become conscious of Emilia was, of course, none other than the fact that she'd been the very first to show him kindness, back when he'd just been summoned to this other world with no one to rely on and no hope to cling to.

His life saved, and through his subsequent contact with her, Subaru had come to know the girl named Emilia, and had come to want to be of help to her. There had been the pretext of repaying a debt of gratitude in that too, but the truth was, at the time, he himself hadn't really understood his own feelings.

After that, he'd lost her right before his eyes, redone the world through Return by Death, and even while losing the memories of what should have been their first exchange, Subaru had rescued her from the fate of death and changed the future.

Everything—what happened at the mansion, the battle against the Witch Cult that began in the royal capital—all of it was the result of him continuing to run, driven ever onward by the fire at his back, the passion that had started from that very first feeling.

His life had been saved, that first time, in the royal capital.

At the mansion, his heart, on the verge of breaking, had been saved again.

With his life and heart both saved, he'd wanted to repay her for it, and in his self-righteousness, he'd wounded her.

He'd been given time apart, a rift had grown between them, and through the chance to reexamine his own conduct, he'd risen back up.

Why had he brought down the White Whale, defeated Petelgeuse, and why was he still here now, doing all this?

Why, having become aware of the intimate ties binding himself and the Witch, having swallowed down his own past grudges through parting ways with his makeshift parents, was he still being driven onward now by some incomprehensible force?

"Because I love that girl."

"........."

"The girl I fell for is a hard worker, stubborn, not honest with herself, the type who can't even say she wants to cry when she wants to cry... and she's a girl who can throw herself wholeheartedly into helping others, without a moment's hesitation."

"That might just be Barusu's own presumption, mightn't it. It's true Emilia-sama has a nature that puts others before herself... but isn't that because doing so was how she protected her own heart? Wasn't it a means of shielding herself, given her looked-down-upon origins? Don't you worry you've been thoroughly taken in by that trick, and are simply being used to her convenience?"

"Nope."

To her long, questioning speech, met with a curt, one-word reply, Ram faltered.

Feeling a certain satisfaction at her rare reaction, Subaru turned only his head back toward the building.

Picturing, even now, Emilia lying on the bed.

"If I'm just being used to her convenience, that's fine by me. Even if, somewhere in her heart, Emilia calculatedly thinks of me as no more than a tool she can wear down without it ever breaking—that's fine too."

"You're saying you have no complaints about being a tool?"

"That's not it. I'm just glad that she's still got the will to stand up and keep walking, even if it means using a tool like me to do it. As long as she's got the spirit left to lift her head up like that... there's plenty I can do to help. She can use me all she wants."

"........."

At Subaru's declaration of his stance, Ram narrowed her eyes as if displeased.

If anything, Subaru found something fresh in her lack of expression. Her reaction to Subaru accepting the word "tool" without reservation—it was almost as if she herself were the one who—

"If anything, I figured you were the one who'd already made peace with that kind of thing."

"—Why would you think that?"

"Because I'm aware I've seen your attitude and behavior up till now, and it's been... pretty on the edge, at times. I figured you'd understand where other people's stances come from too."

"Anyone would feel something like this, seeing in someone else a problem they haven't been able to fully digest within themselves. All the more so upon learning that someone else has managed to chew through a problem you yourself have been trying, and failing, to bite clean through."

Having said this in a rapid rush, Ram looked away, as if ashamed of what she'd just voiced. Then, with a small sigh, she waved a hand at Subaru, who still stood before the door, gesturing for him to move aside, and said,

"Enough. If you're a tool, then act like one and do your utmost for your owner. As for Ram, Ram will do as Ram pleases. That's Ram's own prerogative, isn't it?"

"Well, sure. You do you. —Just one thing, though."

He called out to the back of Ram's neck as she passed by him and put her hand to the door. Ram, stopping, glanced back at Subaru with only her eyes, waiting for what came next, and he nodded and went on,

"I'm not devoting myself to Emilia out of some selfless feeling, you know."

"........."

"I've got things I want from Emilia, for my own sake. What I want can't happen without her cooperation. Just now I said it was fine even if she's using me with some calculation involved... but the truth is, I'm trying to make calculated use of her too, same as her."

It might sound bad put that way, but having ulterior motives when interacting with someone is unavoidable in any relationship with another person.

Taken to its extreme, the future Subaru hoped for was one where everyone present here made it through together. Which meant, in the end, that when Subaru accomplished that future, it could just as well be said he'd used every single one of them to the fullest.

Being used by Emilia was exactly what he wanted. Because for the sake of the future Subaru wished for, he too intended to use Emilia, cling to her, and hold her tight by force if he had to.

"........."

Now entirely silent, Ram stepped into the room without another word to Subaru.

Just as the door was swinging shut, right before her small frame was cut off from view,

"It's already diverged from what's written in the Gospel. —In this world, Roswaal is free now."

Whether that last call reached her or not, there came no reply.

The door shut with a sound, and Subaru could no longer tell what was happening inside.

Still, there was little chance of Emilia waking and turning violent on Ram. Partly because he judged that Emilia, having cried herself to exhaustion and fallen asleep, wouldn't have that much energy left in her—but more fundamentally, her body would need far more time yet before it woke.

"My real shot is tomorrow. The backup day's the day after... either way, it's cutting it close."

Once the time limit arrived, Roswaal would bring down the snow, and the Great Rabbit would come. When that happened, the Sanctuary would be finished. This attempt would be crushed halfway through, and worse still, Subaru was bound by the Contract.

In accordance with his Contract with Roswaal L Mathers, Natsuki Subaru would be forced to live his life "choosing any means necessary for the sake of just one thing."

—Thinking about it that way, he also thought: a bit late for that, wasn't it.

"Even now, I still can't give up on that one single future, same as ever."

—At present, Subaru's battle within the Sanctuary had entered its third day.

His deadline under the Contract with Roswaal ran through the sixth day. By the sixth day, the calamity threatening the Sanctuary and the mansion would surely have closed in to the point of being unavoidable, so the effective deadline was the night of the fifth day.

In other words, counting tonight, Emilia had only three more chances left to challenge the Tomb.

That said, as for the first of those precious three chances—

"Tonight, I guess we've got no choice but to write it off."

Having learned of Puck's loss, Emilia had broken down in tears from that morning, crying herself into exhausted sleep just before noon. Since then, she had remained deep in slumber, with no sign yet of waking.

Even supposing she woke before nightfall, how much time would it take for the waking Emilia to once again come to accept the reality of Puck's absence? The bond between her and Puck surely wasn't so short or shallow that a few hours would be enough to fully digest it.

It was always when there was no time to spare that everything happening demanded the passage of time. Even knowing it was pointless to curse the malicious hand of whatever god was dealing the cards, he wanted to curse it anyway.

"Being cried over that much just for disappearing... I'm a little envious of you, and about a hundred times more resentful, Puck."

Picturing the little gray cat's form in his mind, Subaru gave a small shake of his head and turned to face what lay ahead.

As things stood, there was nothing Subaru could do for the sleeping Emilia. At most, holding her hand was as much as he could manage. If that could serve as some strength to protect her from nightmares, he felt he'd gladly keep doing it for hours on end.

But just as Emilia had no grace period of time, neither did Subaru.

What he'd stacked up to wager was still insufficient.

It was a plan tangled up with a number of uncertain factors, but scraping together and gathering up whatever showed the slightest promise, adding as much as possible to his hand—only then did the odds reach a fifty-fifty split. And even that was being somewhat generous to himself.

"So I've got high hopes for this talk, Ryuzu-san."

"I've got my own hopes pinned on you too, Su-bo. ...Seeing as the other two before me made sure to press the point."

The sound of gravel being kicked came, and a small figure—Ryuzu—appeared at the meeting place.

She made a scowl that didn't suit her childlike appearance, and after glancing all around the meeting place Subaru had specified, went on, "But still..."

"To choose this place of all things for a meeting spot... You've got a rotten streak in you, Su-bo."

"I think it's a pretty good choice, in the sense that nobody's likely to interrupt us. In front of the crystal, I feel like I'd end up blurting out something I shouldn't, and anyway, no matter how many times I go there, I can't picture a future where I get used to that nose-bending stench."

He pinched his nose, expressing his distaste for the laboratory whose stink seemed to invade one's nostrils and soak into the entire body. Ryuzu found her own birthplace mocked in the process, but she probably agreed with the sentiment herself. She gave a small laugh at Subaru's words and nodded. "Well, that's true enough."

"Though I do wonder about choosing Gar-bo's secret base as the alternative. If it's a hideout you wanted... surely there were other candidates."

"Inside the village, there's no telling who might be listening in. Garfiel and Frederica's house of memories wasn't a bad idea either, but you and I are both in the same boat when it comes to not wanting to be overheard, right, Ryuzu-san?"

"Can't argue with that."

At Subaru shrugging his shoulders in appeal for agreement, Ryuzu's expression softened and she gave a small nod. Then Ryuzu stepped into the misshapen little hut, cobbled together out of scrap and decaying lumber.

"Sorry, but there's no comfy sofa waiting for you. As someone brimming with respect for my elders, it really pains me not to have even one chair to offer up."

"Well, well, making an old woman stand and talk, is it. Working an old body to the bone like this—kids these days just don't have any manners."

"Ha, now that line reeks of old age. You're not letting up on playing the elder card, huh."

Subaru gave a wry smile at Ryuzu, who patted a back that wasn't even bent, putting on airs of being creaky all over. Then he guided her to the middle of the hut, folded his arms, and leaned back against the wall himself.

"Though as far as my own feelings go, I wouldn't mind just keeping up the small talk with a cute girl like this."

"Listen to you. I'm a bit too far along in years to be called a girl, wouldn't you say."

"Speaking of age, my main heroine's plenty older than me too, you know. Well, it's just recently come to light that her appearance and her mental age don't quite match up with that."

Actual age, around a hundred. Apparent age, eighteen. Mental age, fourteen.

Emilia's genre was wonderfully complicated—truly, she never bored Subaru. Just when he thought she was a "loli-baba," it turned out the truth was more like a "baba-loli."

He finally understood why she was, for her looks, so childish, so fragile, so high in her ideals, and why her remarks occasionally had a whiff of an old lady to them. There was no way any of that could shake his resolve.

"For that Emilia's sake too, I want to do what I can do. —So, I'm gonna have you tell me all sorts of things, Ryuzu Sigma-san."

"Shig...what now?"

"Sorry, I've just been calling you that in my own head. With all four of you named Ryuzu, it's hard to tell you apart, so just as a convenience, in my own mind—Alpha, Beta, Sigma, Theta."

"................"

At Subaru stumbling right out of the gate with that remark, Ryuzu put a hand to her mouth as if lost in thought.

Frowning slightly at how thin her reaction was for someone he'd apparently offended, Subaru, before she could press him further, held up a finger toward Sigma and said,

"Anyway, I'm grateful you agreed to talk with me. Si—given how Ryuzu-san's been positioned lately, refusing to deal with me at all would've been a perfectly reasonable choice too."

"If it's hard to say, Sigma will do just fine. Seeing as we're talking about each of us individually, I agree it's better to have a way to tell us apart. Though up till now, there was never any need for such a thing."

"Yeah? Alright, I'll take you up on that then. If you'd rather have something cuter, I could swap names with one of the other three."

"—No, Sigma's fine. No, wait—Sigma's good."

Ryuzu's answer carried a slightly different nuance the second time than the first.

Subaru blinked, but Ryuzu apparently had no intention of continuing this topic any further. Hinting at a change of subject with an "Anyway," she said,

"I think I understand well enough... but what is it you want me to tell you, Su-bo? What is it about the circumstances surrounding the Sanctuary that you want to know?"

"What I want to know is basically everything I don't already know... but right now, straight to the point, it's what Sigma-san saw in the Tomb. I heard from Alpha-san the day before yesterday that two Ryuzus challenged the Trials. Sigma-san is one of them, right?"

"That's right. I'm one of the two Ryuzus who entered the Tomb. Though I only went in the one time, and only briefly at that... I merely stepped in to bring back Gar-bo, who'd ignored the rules and gone into the Tomb himself."

What Ryuzu Sigma told him was the same story he'd once heard from Frederica.

The story of how Garfiel, aspiring to free the Sanctuary, had entered the Tomb, and when he failed to return from the Trials, his sister had turned to Ryuzu for help. And it seemed the Ryuzu whom Frederica had relied on at that time had been this very Sigma standing before him now.

"'Merely stepped in,' huh... but still, once you were inside, you must've seen it too, right? Your own... past, I mean, Sigma-san."

"........."

"If the Tomb hated you completely, like it does Roswaal, your body would reject you the moment you tried to step inside. I heard Roswaal's body nearly burst apart, and even Patrasche, who came in to help me, ended up covered in wounds. For someone without qualification to dive in there ought to take just as much resolve as challenging the Trials itself."

"Couldn't it be that I'd already resolved myself to be the one who gets hurt?"

"That'd be a beautiful story in its own right. ...But then I can't figure out the real reason you oppose the Sanctuary's liberation, Sigma-san. The pieces don't add up."

"........."

He'd voiced information that hadn't actually been confessed to him—that Ryuzu Sigma and Theta both opposed the Sanctuary's liberation—but Sigma chose silence rather than denial.

Which was, in effect, as good as a silent admission.

"You must have seen your own past in the Tomb, Sigma-san. And because of it, you're averse to the possibility of the Sanctuary being freed. What exactly did you see?"

"................"

"If there's a possibility, it'd be around the time you were born, Sigma-san. Maybe it was the moment you emerged from the crystal, or maybe..."

"You mean, perhaps, Ryuzu Meyer's past?"

Getting ahead of Subaru's words, Sigma cut straight to the heart of it.

Silently pressing his lips together, Subaru, wordlessly, felt certain that was exactly right.

They were the four lead replicas who, ever since their creation, had gone by the name Ryuzu Meyer and acted as representatives of the Sanctuary. If they had a past worthy of regret, Subaru had thought it more likely to lie not in the time after their creation, but before it—before they became the Ryuzus of today.

That his guess wasn't entirely off the mark could be seen clearly enough in Sigma's reaction just now.

"If what you saw was the past of the real Ryuzu-san inside that crystal... then I can kind of understand why you'd be afraid, Sigma-san. Given the reason she was sealed in the crystal in the first place."

"................"

It was the Witch of Greed, Echidna, who had sealed Ryuzu Meyer within the crystal and incorporated her as a device for generating replicas.

If Sigma had relived that "moment" of being sealed by the Witch's own hand, it would be more than reason enough to give up on ever passing the Trials. But—

"Just how much do you actually know, Su-bo?"

"................"

"Even within this Sanctuary, only a precious few know what happened to the Ryuzu Meyer inside there. And I can't imagine any of them telling it to you, Su-bo."

Something in Sigma's profile hesitated to let the words spill further. Without turning her gaze toward the wordless Subaru, Sigma looked up at the hole-riddled ceiling.

"Neither Roz-bo, nor the other Ryuzu besides me who knows the Tomb... I can't imagine either of them telling you that. So then, Su-bo, wherever could you have heard it from, I wonder."

"........."

Here, Subaru hesitated over what to answer.

It seemed like a harmless enough question, and yet it was nothing so simple.

He could feel a change in the air, a prickling sensation along the back of his neck. It was the same air of a high-stakes gambling den he'd tasted so many times before—different from an actual exchange of life and death, and yet it was something that would decide the very "arena" on which any later exchange of life and death would play out.

It was a sensation similar to the one he'd felt at Crusch's mansion, when he'd pleaded for aid before the battle with the White Whale.

Which was to say, nothing less than that the way this conversation, right here and now, tipped one way or the other was a matter grave enough to sway the very future of the Sanctuary.

"........."

Falling silent once more, Subaru sank into deep thought.

Whatever answer Sigma was seeking from him would greatly determine what came after.

To begin with, Subaru wasn't exactly quick on the uptake when it came to other people's emotions. If anything, he was hopelessly obtuse about such subtleties. Turning his head over wasn't enough—he'd have to burn it until it practically caught fire just to reach an average person's level of perception.

The answer Subaru ought to give, here in this moment, was—

"I heard it from Echidna. Inside the Tomb."

"—From... the Witch."

At the Witch's name being spun out, Sigma's expression stiffened slightly.

Just how much weight Echidna's name carried, Subaru had come to painfully appreciate over his time spent here in the Sanctuary. While Roswaal disliked calling Echidna "the Witch of Greed," it seemed to him that Garfiel and the Ryuzus avoided the name "Echidna" itself.

The name Echidna was probably taboo to them—the sort of thing that, for better or worse, was bound to stir up the past.

What would come of touching this taboo was a gamble, but Subaru chose to make his decision.

"I've had my qualification stripped away now, but for a while I actually held the qualification to challenge the Tomb. So I know from firsthand experience, more or less, what happens during the Trials. And what Echidna's scheming, why she made this Sanctuary in the first place, and why she's producing replicas like you and the others, Sigma-san."

"...I did think your understanding ran too deep for something you'd only been told by Roz-bo."

"So yeah, when it comes to the circumstances, I figure I know things deeper than you'd expect, Sigma-san. I hope that's material enough for you to judge whether I'm someone you can safely tell things to."

"Listen to you."

At Subaru's remark—stepping back a pace at just this moment—Sigma gave a wry smile. Then she pressed her small palm to her forehead and let out a long sigh.

It was the ritual her decision required.

"If you already know about the Witch-sama's connection to the Sanctuary, and even about me and Ryuzu Meyer... then it'd honestly be more unnatural for me to keep hiding it..."

"Then—"

"Don't rush me. I understand your feelings well enough, Su-bo... but this isn't so simple a matter. —What you said you wanted to ask me about was the past that Ryuzu saw during the Trials, wasn't it."

Subaru began to say "That's right" in confirmation, but a faint sense of unease made him hold his tongue.

Sigma had just now deliberately used the word "Ryuzu" instead of "I." Subaru frowned, wondering at her true meaning, and at the very same moment Sigma murmured, "Sharp-eyed, aren't you."

"If you're asking about the past I saw in the Tomb, then my answer is 'I don't know.' After all, I never underwent the Trials of the Tomb myself. Though it's true enough that it was I who came back out of the Tomb."

"...What exactly does that mean?"

"It's simple enough. Didn't it strike you as strange to begin with, Su-bo? There was only one occasion when a Ryuzu entered the Tomb, to bring Gar-bo back out. And yet there are two Ryuzu Meyer replicas who entered the Tomb. That mismatch, between the one occasion and the two people."

"Ah..."

Only once it was pointed out did Subaru realize just how thick he'd been. Indeed, now that she mentioned it, it was exactly as she said. Two Ryuzus had entered the Tomb, and yet there had been only one occasion to enter—if there was an answer that resolved this contradiction, there could only be one.

"The Ryuzu who went in, and the Ryuzu who came back out, are different people..."

"That's it exactly. To put it your way, Su-bo... the Ryuzu who came out was me, Sigma. And the Ryuzu who went in was Theta. It was Theta who saw the past; I merely hoisted Gar-bo up and carried him back outside. As for my own position, it's less that I oppose the Sanctuary's liberation, and more that, by process of elimination, I fall into the neutral camp."

And so, Ryuzu went on, and seeing Subaru on the verge of disappointment, she said,

"—If there's anything I can tell you from my own mouth, it would be fragments, so to speak, of the past that Gar-bo has been so desperately hiding, the very thing that boy witnessed with his own eyes."
