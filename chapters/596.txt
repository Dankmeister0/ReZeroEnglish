Arc 7 Chapter 91: "Racing Across the Battlefield"

She shed the garment she'd worn and stretched her limbs — the ones she made a point of keeping ladylike every other day of her life — as far as they'd go.

"――――"

Kicking hard off the ground, she flung her supple body forward. The sensation of golden fur cutting the wind was pleasant, and even as she trampled grass and tore across the field, she scolded herself inwardly.

This was a battlefield, a place where countless lives collided and were spent, the terminus where lives scattered and ended.

Even knowing that in her head, the scent of blood slipping in through her nostrils, the innumerable fighting spirits carried to her on the air, kept trying to make Frederica's beastified heart soar.

"How disagreeable..."

Without looking away from that surge of excitement, Frederica murmured with an emotion held apart from instinct.

Whether it should be called fortunate or unfortunate, though she'd stood at the edge of the great whirlpool as a member of the camp, Frederica had not been struck by any conspicuous calamity of her own. If anything, the only such moment had been the burning of the old Roswaal Manor, where she'd been reunited with Garfiel after over a decade apart, and her battle then with Meili and her partner. That was about the only time — the only truly hopeless battlefield where her own life had genuinely been on the line.

Because she'd spent her time in what was, in a sense, relative peace, she was forced to realize it now, whether she liked it or not.

That the blood flowing within her, the mingled nature of human and beast, was a lopsided thing. That unless she kept that awareness, that consciousness, firmly in place, she would all too easily lose sight of something important.

What troubled Frederica was something like a fate tied to her half-beast body.

The instinctive urges that her younger brother Garfiel controlled unconsciously, at a high level — her own inability to fully master that same nature was, to Frederica, a deeply vexing thing. The wellspring of blood that shaped who she was seemed dreadfully base, dreadfully merciless—

"—This is no time to be pitying myself. Not while Garf and Lady Emilia, and Petra and Otto-sama, are all doing their utmost."

Cutting short that dialogue with her inner self, Frederica growled through her beastified limbs and faced forward.

Both the excitement that swept her up and the anxiety over a situation whose outcome she couldn't foresee were negative feelings born of Frederica's own uncertain place, her unstable standing within the camp.

—Naturally, the one who represented Frederica and the others was Emilia. Everyone in the group offered their own opinions on policy, but in the end, it was Emilia who decided. On top of that, Emilia herself was a powerful Spiritual Arts user, someone reliable even in the thick of a melee.

Garfiel possessed more than sufficient skill as a military officer, and Otto, even here in a neighboring country plunged into a state of war, showed them the way forward with his excellent judgment. Granted, both Garfiel and Otto had shown a slight tendency to overexert themselves in Subaru's absence, but the former sought an outlet for that on the battlefield, and Petra had handled the latter well.

"If everyone becomes too reliable, even I might start to feel a bit lonely."

She murmured that as if sulking, but the growth of the girl she doted on like a little sister was a source of pride for Frederica. The touch of loneliness that came with it was something like indulgence.

Using the "Divine Protection of the Soul of Language" to listen to the voices of every living thing surrounding this battlefield, Otto was probably gathering more information than anyone else in this great upheaval.

At his side, Petra — with the sun magic she'd only just learned, magic that tended to enhance mind and body — was surely casting it on herself and Otto, easing the backlash from overusing his Divine Protection while also lending her strength to sorting through the flood of information.

Over this past year, Petra, who had trained under Frederica as a rookie maid, had achieved unbelievable growth thanks to her quick memory and knack for things, on top of her own tireless effort. Judged by degree of growth alone, she probably surpassed even Emilia and Subaru — first in the camp.

Still young, still immature, and for that reason forbidden from stepping onto the battlefield, she had nonetheless refused to sulk, sought out her own role, supported the precarious Otto, and fulfilled a duty that was hers alone.

It made Frederica proud. And at the same time, it made her turn a critical eye on herself.

She couldn't fight like Emilia or Garfiel. She had neither the tactical cunning nor the supporting abilities that Otto and Petra wielded. All she could do was suppress her own boiling instincts and race across this battlefield in a beastified body — and to what end?

"――――"

To use his Divine Protection at full strength and dominate this battlefield — that was Otto's resolve, and Frederica believed it was, in fact, coming true. Leaning on Petra's support and keeping his forward planning to the bare minimum, Otto's efforts saw information gathered and carried one piece after another to Abel at the main camp.

And through Abel, who grasped these unfolding events and changes in real time, the tactics being assembled expanded, and the rebel army's fight kept shifting — steadily, toward the advantage.

Of course, Abel's directives didn't reach every corner of the rebel forces. The other insurgents, who had begun fighting without waiting for the arrival of this group — which included Emilia's party, who had joined from the fortress city — showed not the slightest inclination toward coordination or cooperation.

Still, it was Otto and Abel's assessment that this frustrating state of affairs wouldn't last forever, and the first signs of that change were already beginning to show.

Carried by the momentum of the uprising, the insurgents who had charged headlong toward the imperial capital in one fierce rush were met by the "Monsters" — hand-picked elites answering directly to the Emperor who ruled this empire. Having that strength thrown at them head-on must have felt like a bucket of cold water to the insurgents.

If they could just pull free of the state where only the enemy before them and the glory beyond mattered, there was hope they'd start lending an ear to reasoned words. Until that happened, what Frederica could do in this frustrating interim was—

"—Found you! Lady Taritta!"

"—Ngh!? What the—!?"

In an instant, amid a battlefield shifting at a dizzying pace, Frederica spotted her target and dashed over. At the sight of a four-legged beast charging fiercely through the grass, the one who turned — Taritta — widened her eyes. But that shock lasted only a moment; with terrifyingly cold eyes, she leveled her bow, and the notched arrow's tip swung toward Frederica without hesitation—

"Wait, Taritta. Ally."

The one who saved Frederica from being shot through was Mizelda, pressing down on her sister's bow from above. Seeing Taritta's eyes widen at her sister's words, Frederica landed while calling out loudly, "My apologies!" and said,

"Forgive my appearance like this. I am Frederica."

"I see, so it was you. —A beautiful beastification. Were it anyone but you, I'd want to skin that pelt and hang it in the settlement."

"I-I'll take that as a compliment, shall I? Ahem. There's something I must report. —Word from the main camp, from Lord Abel."

At Mizelda's praise, arms crossed, Frederica, a little flustered, got to the point. The reason she'd beastified and been racing across this battlefield thick with flying arrows and clashing blades was to serve as a messenger — bringing word of the information Otto had gathered, with Petra's support, and Abel had refined.

"From Abel... what does he say?"

Taritta, who'd been wary of the enemy forces guarding the five points of the star-shaped fortress and hesitant to press an attack for fear of getting drawn into a chaotic melee with insurgents who'd jumped the gun, leaned forward.

The People of Shudrak's combat strength was a formidable thing, but the true worth of that strength lay in group combat — situations that amounted, essentially, to a hunt.

On open, level battlefields, they couldn't make full use of that strength. If they could get the Shudrak over the wall, the imperial capital's defenses would drop sharply all at once. That was why choosing which wall to bring down mattered so much.

And that wall was—

"—The third vertex. The main camp's judgment is that it's the weak point most worth striking."

"Third... that one, huh."

Receiving the relayed message, Mizelda turned her eyes toward the wall in question.

In the distance, the insurgents who'd taken the vanguard clung to the wall one after another, attempting to break through the sturdy fortifications protecting the imperial capital of Lupugana. But the impact sounds and screams that cut through their fervor mocked their reckless, headlong charge, hinting at a mercilessness that cut them down without hesitation.

There was no doubt some powerful force guarding the capital was stationed at each of the five vertices. But as for why the third vertex functioned as a weak point—

"—A stone... a person of stone,"

"The Steelfolk? But the 'Nine Divine Generals' should only number one."

"――――"

Frederica was left speechless by the sisters, who conversed calmly while gazing at the distant wall. Frederica, too, was half-beast, and in her current beastified state possessed sharper eyesight than usual. And yet, all she could see on the wall the two of them were watching was billowing dust and nothing more. Hunting peoples or not, she found herself wearied anew at falling short.

"Frederica, what did Abel and the others say about the doll?"

"—Ah, yes, well, according to Lord Abel, it's the war-puppet under the command of Moguro Hagane, one of the 'Nine Divine Generals.'"

"'Nine Divine Generals'..."

Hearing that phrase, Taritta, still scanning the distance, stiffened her cheeks. The faint trace of unease mixed into her murmur — Frederica understood the reason all too well.

Naturally, in setting out to join the decisive battle for the capital, Taritta must have already considered the possibility of clashing with one of the "Nine Divine Generals." But even having considered the possibility, an unease that couldn't be wiped away still remained. It was the same as how knowing the "Sword Saint" existed did nothing to make one's fear of her vanish.

"Don't worry, Taritta."

"Sister..."

But at her younger sister's tense manner, Mizelda grinned. Her sharp, piercing eyes gleamed brightly as she gave her sister's shoulder a hearty slap.

"Even if the enemy is one of the 'Nine Divine Generals,' we won't fall behind again. If it's that same 'Nine Divine General,' we'll settle the score. If it's a different one, we'll take it out on them instead. Any problem with that, hm?"

Shrugging, Mizelda showed off her right leg — the one below the knee replaced with a wooden staff — and posed the question to Taritta. Whether those were truly words of reckless conviction, or simply meant to encourage her sister, Frederica couldn't tell—

"—No, no problem at all."

Blinking once, then twice, Taritta nodded, and in the next instant, with a speed too fast to follow, drew an arrow, notched it, and loosed it straight up above their heads.

A moment later, hearing a pained cry overhead, Frederica looked up to see a Flying Dragon come tumbling down, spinning through the air, landing on the earth a short distance from the three of them. The arrow that had struck beneath the dragon's jaw had driven clear through to its head, killing it instantly. While Frederica stared in astonishment at that overwhelming archery, Taritta let out a breath and said,

"When the moment comes, we'll cross the wall ourselves. Once we break through, the fight is ours."

"Now that's the chieftain of the Shudrak. —Frederica, what will you do?"

Mizelda, satisfied, nodded at Taritta's resolved expression and turned around. Frederica, who'd been half swept up in the sisters' mood, snapped back to herself at the question.

"I'll carry what I've just told you to the others as well. If those willing to listen gather in one place—"

"One hole brings down the dam, is it."

"Yes. —A breach will open."

That was precisely the Emilia Camp's policy, and precisely what Frederica ought to be doing. Because she understood all too well that she still occupied an uncertain place within the camp, she wanted, at the very least, to fulfill the role entrusted to her as fully as she could.

"Lady Taritta, Lady Mizelda, I wish you good fortune in battle. Please, do not throw your lives away needlessly."

"Yeah, you take care too, Frederica."

"If you die, your pelt gets passed down through generations of Shudrak."

"Regrettably, I have no intention of handing it over!"

With a touch of the tension eased from her, Frederica took off running, seen off by the sisters. Kicking through the grassland like a gale, she raced onward to deliver Abel's instructions. She couldn't let Petra and Otto's teamwork go to waste. And beyond that, if it could contribute to Garfiel and Emilia's hard-fought battle, and to Subaru and Rem, who might well be somewhere in the imperial capital—

"For now, all I can do is run."

Leaving only a faint afterimage in the swaying grass, Frederica's beastified form vanished.

A golden leopard — bearing no resemblance at all to her original tall, beautiful form, and yet a refined four-legged beast whose beauty even a hunting people would have to praise.

Following the information Frederica had brought them, Taritta and the others were resolved to rally the Shudrak and move to take the third vertex. Just as that resolve had settled—

"—Tch, can't cut through! Too many of these damn things in the way!"

Falling from the sky with a coarse shout came a man with an eyepatch, twin swords in hand. Deftly wielding the long blades in both hands, he sheared off the wings of a Flying Dragon, then, as it fell alongside him, dove in beside the beast and severed the neck of the dragon as it tried to bite him.

The one who turned around after delivering that brilliant sword strike was—

"Jamal. Seeing your face satisfies nothing."

"The hell's that supposed to mean! Damn it, this is maddening! If I can't force my way into the capital somehow, I've got no way of knowing if Katya's safe... that bastard Todd better be protecting her properly."

Grumbling and cursing under his breath, Jamal raked a hand through his dark brown, unruly hair and snarled. He was an imperial soldier taken prisoner at the fortress city, and one of the rare few who, upon learning Abel's true identity, had thrown in with them — a man whose skill matched his unusual position. Unfortunately, his face, marked by that same coarse temperament, didn't seem to be to the taste of Mizelda, who had a preference for good looks.

Still, given that he apparently had family left behind in the capital, Taritta could well understand the restless anxiety driving him. After all—

"Us aside, the other insurgents will take whatever ground they can get."

"I know that already! That's why I want to cut off the fake Excellency's head as fast as possible and put an end to this ridiculous farce."

"Good news for someone so eager. We're heading for the third vertex next. Word says that's the target."

At Jamal, stamping his feet impatiently, Mizelda revealed Frederica's earlier message. Hearing it, Jamal scowled suspiciously. "Huh?"

"Target, huh? Who the hell said that..."

"Abel, apparently."

"Say so from the start! Oi! You lot, get ready! We're heading for the third vertex!"

Quick to shift gears, Jamal barked that out roughly and immediately began rallying the soldiers who shared his circumstances — those who'd joined their side back at the fortress city. Loud voice, clear intentions, and genuinely strong in a fight — perhaps, unexpectedly, he had the makings of a "general." Watching Jamal's back, Taritta moved as well, unwilling to fall behind.

"Sister, we should also call Kuna and Holly and get ready to... huh?"

Just as she called out, Taritta found herself holding her breath without meaning to. Eyes wide, she reached toward something drifting and falling before her. On her gently outstretched hand, something white vanished in less than a second.

Slowly, dancing through the sky above a battlefield raging with the fires of war, drifted a white light — no, cold grains of ice. Snowflakes.

"Why is there... this..."

"—Ah. That's right, you haven't seen it, have you, Taritta."

"Sister?"

Beside Taritta, stunned at the sight of snow for the first time in her life, Mizelda nodded. Unlike Taritta, Mizelda, wearing a knowing look, seemed to have some idea — more than that, some familiarity — with this sight.

"Emiria."

"Emily...?"

The name Mizelda gave was that of the silver-haired girl in the same camp as Frederica. Was the girl who, not long ago, had spoken of being a Half-Elf, the very one causing this snowfall? And to what end?

Perhaps because she was experiencing something beyond her imagining, Taritta felt a faint shiver run through her, and quietly wrapped her arms around her own shoulders.

"Truly, the world is a wide place. The Shudrak's chieftain and former chieftain, both getting outdone at every turn far too often. This snow is the same. And then there's—"

Mizelda, who had been looking up at the snow-falling sky, turned her gaze elsewhere — toward the grass Frederica had just been treading, the direction in which she'd vanished. Thinking of that outrageous messenger, tirelessly carrying Abel's fresh information back and forth across this vast battlefield without ever running out of breath—

"—A beautiful beast keeps us alive. However far it goes, that's the very height of a hunter's fortune."

So she said, praising a companion she had, once again, far too greatly underestimated.
