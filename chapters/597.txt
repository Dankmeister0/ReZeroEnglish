Arc 7 Chapter 92: "The Rekindled Grudge"

—The air screamed, and the world slowed, little by little.

Heat ruled the battlefield. —No, it wasn't the battlefield that had been seized, but the Empire itself.
Heat governed the whole of the Empire's land, and the earth had been swallowed by chaos the likes of which had never been seen.

Countless voices rose, weapons were raised, ideals were proclaimed, and the heat climbed higher still.
It spread to all around, growing bigger, wider, faster, stronger, hotter.
That heat was something that had smoldered endlessly within the hearts of the people of Vollachia.
Heat denied any outlet, stuffed endlessly into a box. But even shut away, that heat kept building, until at last it found its chance and exploded.

What had come to pass as a result — that, in itself, I have no intention of condemning.
Where there are many people, there are many ways of thinking. All the more so when nations differ — common sense differs too, and at the root of every way of life lie different soils, different waters, different airs.
Say what you like without knowing any of that, and no one will lend you an ear.
So talk of right and wrong has no place here.

In a place where so very many things were tangled together, if there was one thing that could be said, it was this.
It was—

"—I really, reeeally hate it."

That murmured voice, quite literally, changed the very air of the battlefield.
White snow began fluttering down over the farmland and grassland ringing the imperial capital of Lupugana, over the green expanse of earth — a sight that should have been impossible in an Empire known for its warm climate.

"What... is this..."

The voice raised in unease at the sight of snow for the first time belonged to a Weaponkin collapsed upon the ground.
Born with parts of their bodies that could turn to metal, reforging those parts into weapons to charge across the battlefield — living weapons, one might call them — they had thrown themselves at the capture of the imperial capital, only to be shattered by a towering foe.
Even so, they had regrouped, and those still able to fight had steeled themselves to challenge the enemy once more — and it was then that the white scene slowly began to unfold.

For an instant they were struck dumb, but they quickly wrenched their awareness back to the battlefield.
Had that instant's opening been seized, it would have been no surprise to lose their lives for it. But the enemy, it seemed, no longer regarded the Weaponkin as a threat at all, and the opening went untouched.
Storing up that humiliation, pouring their fury into blade, spear, and axe, they meant to swing them down and, on their race's pride, finally cut the enemy down this time — and it was as they tried to do so that they realized:

—the bodies they tried to move had gone stiff with cold, refusing to obey them.

To say it again: the Vollachian Empire was a country where a warm climate held throughout the year.
Of course, for those whose homeland lay in the high mountains, along the borders with neighboring countries, or near the Great Waterfall, there were occasions to taste a different climate — but the Weaponkin were not among them.
That being so, surely no one could blame them for it.
Who, after all, could have been on guard against this?

Who could have guarded against a wind so freezing, in the midday battlefield where blue sky and sunlight still peeked through, that bodies flushed hot enough to sweat were chilled all at once, their circulation grinding sluggish?

"Everyone! Get away from here! I'm going to try reeeally hard, so leave this to me!"

The voice struck the astonished Weaponkin as they forced their unresponsive bodies to move.
Through that cold air, in which merely gripping a weapon had become difficult, a figure could be seen advancing in long strides across the grassland, silver hair streaming behind her.

White skin, violet-blue eyes, a profile of frighteningly perfect beauty — the girl stepped forward with commanding presence, treating the scene around her as though it were the most natural thing in the world.
Even as they gazed at her, entranced, the Weaponkin realized — whose doing all of this was.

"—You again, is it."

The Weaponkin's shivering astonishment continued still.
The voice that fell upon the advancing silver-haired girl came from a small figure standing atop the ramparts — a Dragonkin with two black horns growing from her head: Madelyn Eschart.
She reigned over the seat of the "Nine" among the Empire's Generals, a "monster" who had shattered the Weaponkin's battle line with a single sweep.

In the hand of Madelyn, who glared down with irritation, was gripped the Flying Winged Blade, a weapon far too large for her stature — and she was its wielder, handling with ease a weight far greater than it appeared to carry.
As a Dragonkin — a rare breed even in an Empire teeming with Demi-Human peoples — she carried a pressure that pinned one in place simply by standing opposite her, enough to buckle the knees of anyone weak of heart.
And yet—

"Yep! It's me again, Madelyn! Just a passing Spiritual Arts user — Emily!"

The girl who leveled a finger straight at her — Emily — paid no mind whatsoever to the Dragonkin's golden glare.
From that fearless exchange alone, the Weaponkin understood — the difference between the stage they themselves stood upon and the one where Emily and Madelyn stood, the difference in the height of the vantage each could see from.

"—"

Disappointment and despair at the reality forced upon them — but immediately after, a surge of exhilaration.
It was anticipation for the battle about to unfold before their eyes, a battle beyond human comprehension.

It was about to begin — a clash between heights and heights, a realm reachable by nothing so simple as being born strong.
Quite apart from the blood cooling with the falling temperature, a warrior's shiver of thrill ran through them.
And then—

"What an infuriating girl. In our last battle, you should've learned you can't beat a dragon—"

Madelyn's expression — one that could almost have been called adorable — twisted into something fierce enough to strike terror.
Raising the Flying Winged Blade in her grip, the enraged Dragonkin poured strength into her slender arms, utterly unbothered by the bitter cold that hindered the Weaponkin's every movement.
They sensed it instinctively — that the instant that Flying Winged Blade was swung, a battle for the history books would begin—

"—Hyah!"

—In the next instant, at the point where Emily's outstretched hand pointed, a block of ice as large as a castle plummeted from the sky, and Madelyn's form atop the ramparts was swallowed, along with a thunderous roar, beyond a curtain of shattered ice.

As the great block of ice slammed into the ramparts, Emilia nodded inwardly. *Good.*
With the battle begun between the rebel army surrounding the capital and the regular army trying to defend it, Emilia's head had become quite thoroughly scrambled.

Try her hardest, and together reduce the number of people who ended up dying.
That was the policy Emilia had set — a goal her companions had accepted without objection, even knowing how difficult it would be.
And yet, by the time Emilia and the others actually arrived at the imperial capital, the rebel forces that had gotten there first had already begun the fighting.

"From the capital's point of view they're all the same rebels, but each tribe has its own agenda. This was never going to be a battle that started neatly, in step. It's a matter of reason you needn't even think hard about."

So Abel had said, rather unkindly, to a flustered Emilia — but the group that had joined from the walled city secured their own position and, belatedly, joined the battle as well.
Among Emilia's group, the two mainly taking part in the fighting were herself and Garfiel. Beatrice and Petra couldn't be made to fight, and Otto and Frederica had their own duties.
It was frustrating, being able only to fight, but Emilia had learned how important a division of roles was, so she left everything else to whatever each of them was best at.

"The imperial capital, Lupugana, is a city ringed by star-shaped ramparts. Easy to defend, hard to attack — a city that won't fall easily. Taking it requires seizing the five points."

"The five points?"

"The star-shaped ramparts. From these five points, one can grasp the battlefield in depth and at distance. Which is to say, seize them, and you cripple the enemy's forces considerably. To that end—"

"To that end?"

"We work out the enemy's force disposition from the rebels who engage first. If they whittle down even a little of the enemy's remaining strength, that's a win."

"But that means the people who go in first are in danger! That's absolutely not okay!"

Even granting that devising a strategy to win was Abel's role, Emilia couldn't accept that approach. And so she decided to sortie out herself, to take the needed points of the star.
Honestly, she hadn't found any decisive way to know which point was best to rush to, though.

"Emily, I'll tell you where to go. —I have a guess."

So Otto said, lending his help to Emilia just as she was about to dash off.
Otto's expression at that moment somehow looked to Emilia like the face of someone about to do something reckless, and she hesitated over how to answer, but—

"It's all right, Emily. Leave it to me."

So Petra told her, and with that, Emilia could put her full trust in it and run off.

"Emily! I'm headin' this way! Let's blow a hole clean through 'em!"

She and the boisterous Garfiel swore mutual good fortune in battle, then parted along the way, each heading for their own point.

"Emily... no, Lady Emilia, I too shall run to my duty. I wish you the best of fortune in battle. For Master Subaru's sake, please don't push yourself too recklessly!"

Running alongside her, Frederica underwent Beastification and tore across the battlefield at a speed too fast for the eye to catch.
Every single one of them was desperate to fulfill their own duty.

"I have to try reeeally hard too."

With firm resolve tucked away in her heart, Emilia reached her point to carry out what she had to do.
She shut her eyes tight and laid her left hand over her right, pressed to her chest. Before the battle began, Beatrice — regretting that she couldn't take part in this scene — had gripped this hand and told her:

"It's infuriating, I suppose, but Betty's counting on you to stand in for her. When it comes to Subaru, Emilia's probably the only one who can try as hard as Betty, I suppose."

"Yeah. I really, really want to believe that's true."

Whether it was thanks to the feelings Beatrice had entrusted to her, or because everyone was cooperating with her, Emilia's mind and body were in shockingly perfect condition.
The heart that had been panicking, the head that had been scrambled — both had settled, and she was perfectly calm.
Very calmly, Emilia was holding both her duty and what she wanted to do together, at once.

The duty Emilia had to fulfill was to defeat Madelyn, who guarded the point.
What Emilia wanted to do was to keep as many people as possible from dying.

She was about to fight Madelyn. And she would do it so as not to let many people die.

"—"

Opening her eyes, Emilia focused her awareness on the Gate deep within her body, released the Mana she'd stored up all at once, and began dropping the surrounding temperature at a furious pace.
Vollachia was a warm country, so many people would be bad with the cold — that was studious Petra's opinion. Flying Dragons in the sky were also weak to cold, animal-loving Otto had told her too.
And that when it got cold, one wanted to curl up — that was the opinion of the siblings Garfiel and Frederica.

Weighing all of that against her own experience, none other than her own, Emilia had realized something.
It was—

"When it gets reeeally cold, nobody's in any shape to keep fighting."

Once, back at Roswaal Manor before it had burned, before Puck had fallen asleep, there had been a time when he'd had trouble with insufficient Mana release, and the area around the manor had turned into a complete snowscape.
The trouble had spread to nearby Arlam Village too, and it had become quite the commotion — but for Emilia, who had never once in her life felt cold, it had remained a strange sort of memory.
That experience and knowledge from back then came into solid use now.

"I know everyone's opinions, and the feelings they've put into this fight — those aren't things that bend easily. I'd like to really talk it through and understand each other too, if I could, but there's no time."

So Emilia had decided.
To force everyone down — no, not with strength, but with cold.

Fingers going numb until they couldn't grip a weapon, knees shaking until not a single technique could be used, teeth chattering until one's gaze wouldn't settle, breath turning white enough to startle.
Do that, and everyone would be in no state to keep fighting.

And the one who would face whoever still insisted on fighting anyway, with strong body and strong heart, was—

"—I'll be your opponent!"

"You'd best cut it out with the jokes already—aaah!!"

Emilia's resolute cry was answered — nearly drowned out — by a furious roar thundering from the block of ice that had fallen on the ramparts.
That a lump of ice bigger than a house could score a direct hit and still get back a lively shout in return surprised everyone around her — but not Emilia. It had been the same when they'd clashed in Guaral, a few days before.
That time too, the battle had begun the same way, with hurling a block of ice.

"No imagination, this one."

A shrill sound rang out, and in the next instant, the enormous block of ice split clean in two, top to bottom.
Emerging from beyond the scattering ice shards, hale and hearty, Flying Winged Blade held aloft, was Madelyn.
Glancing sidelong as the shattering ice broke into fragments, then into particles, then reverted to Mana, she narrowed the pupils of her golden eyes.

"Even after a beating like that, nothing's changed, has it, half-Demon..."

"A beating... that last part sure was a shock, but Priscilla and I were just about to win. Don't lie."

"—Ngh, don't you underestimate a dragon, half-Demon!"

Eyes blazing with anger at Emilia's retort, Madelyn bent her knees to leap down from the ramparts. Emilia's eyes went wide at the motion, and after a quick glance around—

"Hyah!"

She swung her arm, sending a block of ice raining down onto Madelyn, just as before.
Naturally, it didn't so much as give her pause — Madelyn's weapon, raised with annoyance, batted it aside and shattered it, letting it accomplish nothing.
But—

"Hyah! Hup! Still not done! There!"

"Wha—"

One after another, she conjured blocks of ice into the sky, dropping them down onto Madelyn on the ramparts as fast as they came.
Partway through she began prioritizing speed over power, so the ice grew much smaller than the first block — at most, about arm's-width across.
Even so, a hit to the head from one of these would mean unavoidable, tremendous pain — and Emilia conjured them by the tens, twenties, fifties, hundreds, raining them down upon the ramparts.

Hailstones of ice — though "hailstones" was far too cute a word for this storm.

"Don't! Get carried away—!"

Against that raining storm of ice, Madelyn, its target, fought back with wild ferocity.
Working her whole body, unbothered by the cold, she used the Flying Winged Blade not by throwing it but as a weapon to swing and swat, knocking down every last one of the falling blocks of ice.

Impacts and shattering sounds chained one after another as the ice swarmed toward Madelyn, tearing across the ramparts.
Smashed, dodged, deflected, evaded — spinning round and round with the footwork of an acrobat or a dancer, Madelyn wove clean through the whirling dance of ice.
That mobility, too, was eye-popping, but landing a hit wasn't Emilia's aim. This was an attack premised on being dodged — its purpose was to buy time.
Namely—

"Everyone, get away! It's dangerous around here once Madelyn and I start fighting!"

Even as she pounded the storm of ice down, Emilia called out to the rebels around her.
The battered Weaponkin huddled close together, seeming to look for a chance to lend a hand, but with many of them shivering from the cold and having lost their weapons, it was an unreasonable ask.
That moving their own limbs had become such a struggle was something Emilia herself had caused, and she felt very bad about it — but she wanted them to push those limbs into moving anyway, and pull their comrades back.
If they did that—

"—! Not good!"

Weaving through the gaps of Emilia's attack — one that, prioritizing volume, had grown monotonous — Madelyn's Flying Winged Blade tore through the finely shattered ice and was unleashed.
Roaring furiously as it cut through the air without a whisper of wind, it came flying at her, and Emilia leapt aside on reflex — but the whirling Flying Winged Blade circled the battlefield with a thunderous roar and came back around with terrifying precision.
That whirlwind of death, mowing down everything in its path, would cut down anyone besides Emilia without mercy. So, to keep the slow-moving Weaponkin from being caught in it, Emilia broke into a run.

"Ngh, hyaaah!!"

She cut in front of a Weaponkin whose body couldn't keep up with the approaching whirlwind of death, who hadn't managed to dodge.
With a kicked-up leg she sent the blade of the Flying Winged Blade upward. The impact made every bone in her body groan, but she gritted her teeth and bore it through sheer stubbornness, striking it upward with all her strength, and the blade passed clear over Emilia's head, over the heads of the Weaponkin, and away behind them.

"With that..."

"You think it's over, do you."

"—!"

Before she could even breathe a sigh of relief, a voice came from behind.
Beyond her own streaming silver hair, Emilia's violet-blue eyes caught sight of Madelyn, who had snatched the kicked-away Flying Winged Blade out of the air and was already winding up with it.
If she dodged the blow about to come crashing down, everyone around her would be in danger.

"Ice Brand Arts!!"

With no option left but to block, Emilia brought a sword of ice, conjured on reflex, up to meet the Flying Winged Blade.
It didn't become a contest of blades locked together. The moment it touched the enemy's blade, the ice sword cracked and shattered pitifully. But that was only the first. She conjured a second in her other hand, a third in the hand that had lost the first, and — shattered again and again, never giving up — Emilia's chain of ice-sword strikes roared on.
Spinning her whole body around, she struck the flat of the Flying Winged Blade, struck it again, barely managing to deflect it, and it fell.

"—Kyaaah!?"

The instant the knocked-down Flying Winged Blade struck the earth head-on, a shockwave slammed into Emilia's entire body.
Battered by the same force, the Weaponkin who hadn't managed to flee in time were sent flying spectacularly too. A Dragonkin's arm strength was outrageous — stronger, far stronger, even than the strong Emilia.

"Even so..."

Springing back up from her tumbling roll, Emilia slapped her own cheeks and faced forward.
Madelyn, pulling the Flying Winged Blade out of the ground, glared this way with displeasure, taking in Emilia — still hale and hearty — and then the surroundings.

"Just you, is it. What happened to that woman."

"That woman? ...Oh, you mean Priscilla?"

"You think a dragon bothers remembering human names? I mean that red woman."

"That's Priscilla. I'm Emily. Priscilla said there's someone she wants to see."

While Abel worked out strategy back at their base and Emilia and the others charged onto the battlefield, Priscilla and Al, too, were treading the same battlefield's soil.
Unlike Emilia and the others, who wanted to bring Subaru and Rem back, or Abel and his side, who wanted to somehow take down the Emperor, Priscilla's purpose wasn't at all clear.
Even asked, she hadn't given a proper answer.

"And she won't even tell me what she talked about with Yorna..."

They were mother and daughter reunited after being apart, so there was probably a lot pent up to talk about.
Thinking of the day, someday, when she too would be reunited with Puck once he woke inside the mana crystal hanging from her neck, Emilia had wanted to hear what they'd talked about, for reference.
Of course, there was also simply wanting to hear a happy story from Priscilla, too.

"Someone she wants to see..."

"Yeah, they seem to be somewhere in this battle. She said it was her own business, so I went and prioritized my knight instead... ah! Um, Lady Petra's! Knight, maybe!"

"—"

Madelyn eyed Emilia with suspicion as she hurriedly corrected herself, remembering that in the Vollachian Empire she needed to keep up a disguised identity.
It felt like she was being doubted, but Emilia was no good at lying. Trying to smooth it over seemed likely only to make things worse, so she gave up and let her shoulders slump.

"No, wait, that was a lie. Subaru's my knight. Sorry for trying to trick you."

"I never even asked in the first place!"

"Was that... how it went?"

"Whatever a little girl like you thinks, it's no business of a dragon's. —It's infuriating that red woman isn't here, but if that's how it is, all the better."

Even as she spoke, steam slowly began to rise from Madelyn's whole body.
In the ever-colder air, just as breath turned white when exhaled, her body — its heat continuously climbing — began, simply by standing there, to send visible fighting spirit rising off it.

Bathed in that visible fighting spirit, Emilia conjured twin swords into both hands on reflex.
Emilia readied them, on guard, and at the sight Madelyn smiled ferociously,

"One by one, I'll make a bloody sacrifice of every last one of you who made a fool of a dragon."

Her golden eyes blazing bright, she charged straight at her.
With a stride that exploded the freezing earth beneath her, she closed the distance between them in an instant, and Emilia braced herself against Madelyn, who raised the Flying Winged Blade high.
And then—

"Priscilla might not be here, but I'm not alone!"

"What are you—"

—about to say, Madelyn had likely meant to roar.
But a sudden figure, and the impact of something pinning down the raised Flying Winged Blade, wouldn't let her finish. Madelyn's eyes went wide in shock, and she stared at the figure that had leapt onto the Flying Winged Blade.

It was Natsuki Subaru, made of ice, throwing his entire body into blocking the attack—

"Wha—!?"

"Hyaaah!!"

Into Madelyn, her movement halted, Emilia's twin ice blades came slamming down.

At each point of the star-shaped ramparts ringing the imperial capital, battle had begun, and the very nature of the war was changing.
The many being routed by the few — and mostly by a single fighting force at that — was, in its way, almost comical, and at the same time seemed a microcosm of this land called the Vollachian Empire, where the strong were revered.

No matter how many weak were bundled together, one swing from the strong could snuff out their hope with ease.
In fact, this battle so grandly billed as the decisive fight for the imperial capital, if left to run its course, would see the rebels cut down one and all, and those still smoldering with fighting spirit would likely be quieted for years to come.
One might even suspect that flushing out such rebellious elements was precisely the Emperor's calculation this time.
Though, mind you—

"—that would only hold, if His Majesty Emperor Vincent Vollachia hadn't been driven from the throne."

Surveying the battlefield with a pipe in one hand, Yorna Mishigure breathed out words mixed with purple smoke.
Deliberately setting a small fire, and working to tighten up thinking gone slack with peace — that was exactly the sort of thing the Emperor might do, but not something he'd do to the extent of surrendering the throne himself.
Had she not known the truth of this affair, and been summoned instead as one of the Nine Divine Generals reigning over the Cursed City — which side, she wondered, would she have taken?

"But there is no time to steep in such useless sentiment. —I have my own duty to attend to."

Setting aside a possibility that could never have been, Yorna quietly faced forward.
Vincent Vollachia, the true Emperor, had laid peace and stability across this Empire. What sort of future the false Emperor who had toppled him would rule over, where it would lead — that darkness ran thick and deep.
And so, Yorna thought.

"My own Beloved Children, and the people living on the Empire's soil that His Majesty loved."

The Beloved Children who, foolishly, had lost the Cursed City, who had failed to show the authority she ought to have shown, and who still went on adoring her regardless.
The people of the Empire, loved by a man she had once loved and who still, even now, had not lost an ounce of her affection.
And, no less, a world worthy of the child with whom she had shared her very Soul, the child who lived on in it.
That was—

"—I would sooner have the peace the true Emperor maintains, than the future the false one would build."

Even as she spoke, twirling her pipe, clumps of earth around Yorna slowly began to swirl, tracing a spiral. It was Yorna's own technique — granting the Soul Marriage Technique to inorganic matter and bending it to her will.
A technique no one else could ever replicate, owing to the sheer capacity of Soul it demanded — though unlike the Cursed City, where she had spent long, intimate time steeping the ground in her feelings, the earth of the imperial capital moved sluggishly.
No doubt Yorna's own complicated feelings toward the Empire weren't entirely unrelated to that, either.

"Forgive me for not being at my very best, but I shall be the one to face you."

Straight ahead, Yorna's gaze, lifted toward the point of the ramparts, rose higher still, past even the walls.
The reason was simple and plain: the one her gaze needed to pierce was higher than the ramparts, flying freely through the sky above the battlefield.
Her legs from the knee down transformed into flame, ruin concealed within that slender body — the Spirit Eater—

"—Yorna."

"General Arakiya. Fighting as flashy as ever, I see. I could tell it was you at a glance, even from a distance."

A brown-skinned Dog Human with a tree branch in one hand and her left eye covered by an eyepatch — Arakiya.
She turned wary eyes on Yorna, who had appeared below, and, her very skin scorched by that hostility, Yorna looked around, narrowing her eyes at the farmland turned to burnt wasteland.

Of all the rebel attacks, the flashiest counterstrike by far had come at this point — that is, from Arakiya.
Of course, part of the reason was simply the sheer scale of her own power, but there was likely also a meaning in putting on a show for those around.
Yorna wasn't the only one to work out, at a glance, that it was Arakiya guarding this point.
Any warrior of note, even without pinning down that it was specifically Arakiya, would easily have sensed the overwhelming skill at work.
Though that was less Arakiya's own aim than—

"Has someone been putting ideas in your head, Arakiya?"

"—!"

"You..."

From behind, a voice came from someone treading across the blackened, scorched grassland, and it shook the battlefield.
Both Arakiya and Yorna alike caught their breath, eyes going wide. A voice that shouldn't have been possible to hear had appeared, bold as could be, and Yorna's brow knit.
And the footsteps, without Yorna even needing to turn, came to stand at her side,

"What is it, Mother? Even having died once, can you still not let go of your child?"

So saying, snapping open the fan in her hand with a sharp sound, red-eyed Priscilla smiled cruelly.
Caught off guard both by her unexpected arrival and by what followed, Yorna let out a breath.

"Priska, it is only natural you cannot forgive me, but..."

"Unfortunately, the girl who bore that name is dead. If it troubles you so, go visit her grave. I am Priscilla Barielle. See that you do not mistake the two, ever."

"...Abel was meant to have said he'd leave this place to me, was he not?"

"And I said, in that very same conversation, that I would not become Abel's puppet."

Answered without mercy at every turn, Yorna found herself at a loss before Priscilla's attitude. But Priscilla scoffed with a "hmph," and the smile at her lips vanished.
Her crimson gaze turned, just as it was, up toward Arakiya overhead.

Arakiya, ranked among the strongest of the Nine Divine Generals — Yorna had been ordered to face her precisely because everyone understood that no one but Yorna herself could match her.
And yet the thoughtful Priscilla had still come all the way here, apart from any question of combat strength or tactics, because she had her own reason to stand against Arakiya.
As proof, Arakiya had clearly been shaken by Priscilla's appearance.
Even before Yorna, her somewhat otherworldly expression hadn't wavered — but the instant she laid eyes on Priscilla, that mask had crumbled.
That was, beyond doubt, proof of some special relationship between Priscilla and Arakiya, and—

"Arakiya. Has your heart been decided?"

Priscilla's profile as she asked was cold and clear, her gaze sharp enough to nearly slay the one it pierced. And yet the words her lips wove were, somehow, gentle — one could even sense a kind of mercy in them.
A tender question dressed up in severity — Arakiya caught her breath at it, and nodded.

"I'll... get the princess back. —Kill His Majesty, and the princess becomes... the real Emperor."

"You wish for a proper conclusion to the Imperial Selection Ceremony, then. Very well."

Hearing Arakiya's answer, Priscilla closed the fan in her hand. Tucking it away into the valley of her chest, she raised her now-empty hand to the sky and drew a crimson jeweled sword out from the space itself.
Radiating heat enough to make the very air shimmer around its blade, it was the Sun Sword, a weapon permitted only to a Vollachian Emperor to wield — and even Yorna found herself, for a moment, entranced.
For it felt, for an instant, as though the beloved man who had once wielded that crimson jeweled sword had risen up again behind her closed eyelids.

"This is no time to be staring, Mother."

"...From start to finish, going on and on however you please... Even granting I never raised you, however did you turn out this way?"

"Who's to say. There's no reason for I to be I. And besides — there's one thing I forgot to mention."

Watching Priscilla ready the Sun Sword out of the corner of her eye, Yorna too tensed her whole body, entering a battle-ready stance. And it was just as she settled into that stance that the addition came, making Yorna turn a suspicious eye on her daughter.
Without so much as glancing back at that look, Priscilla pointed the tip of the Sun Sword at Arakiya,

"That one is my milk-sibling. After Mother's death, she was raised alongside I, near as good as a sister. Now, it seems, her purpose is to see I seated upon the throne."

"Wha—"

"Here she comes."

Yorna's eyes went round at the revealed truth almost the exact same moment as the merciless warning.
Swelling flame blotted out the sky, falling as an immense mass of heat meant to burn the earth, already a scorched wasteland, into ash and ruin all over again.
Beyond it, dyeing the whole world a single shade of red, Arakiya cried out.

"I'll get the princess back, with my own power!! —That's... what I was taught."

Watching from afar as the flames Arakiya unleashed scorched the world in dramatic fashion, Todd Fang sensed that the mood of the battlefield was shifting along with the wind.

"—No mistake, that opening move worked..."

Overwhelming firepower to break the nose of any rebel foolish enough to charge in — the one entrusted with striking first was always someone whose ability had been recognized.
No group whose members were burned to ash without recourse could possibly keep up its morale.
For that reason, among the five points guarding the star-shaped ramparts, he'd read that the point assigned to him — the one with Arakiya — would be the safest, and the one where the enemy's assault would weaken fastest.

And indeed, Todd's read had proven correct: after the Cyclops Tribe who'd charged in were wiped out, the rebel attacks, fearful of Arakiya, had thinned out, and it had turned into something practically indistinguishable from a won battle.
What had changed that mood was the arrival of a second wave, joining the first wave of rebels that had already been repelled.
Struck on the head and stripped of its fighting strength, the first wave had seemed on the verge of total collapse — but it had been absorbed into the second wave that arrived afterward, and was now attempting to regroup its forces.

Of course, a pack of beaten dogs banding together shouldn't have much effect on the outcome — and yet the latecomers had managed to pull together men whose morale should have been utterly broken, and had held the damage to a minimum.
There had to be an unusually sharp-eyed commander in that second wave — thinking that far, Todd shuddered at a terrifying possibility.

"Don't tell me — even arriving late was part of the plan?"

A rebel army out of step with itself, charging in on nothing but its own self-assertion, empty-headed — a first wave made up of nothing but such men had taken its beating, and the battle had been on the verge of ending in a total rout for the regular army.
But the first wave, having lost its strongest fighters, had had cold water thrown over it, its hot blood cooled down, and in the end had lost, root and branch, every troublemaker incapable of listening to reason.
Once that happened, in order to salvage a win from the battle they'd picked, those men had no choice but to fall in under whoever had stacked up a real chance of victory, performing desperately for whatever scraps they could get.

"So the real threat isn't the vanguard full of fools, but the second wave."

The fact that they'd even challenged Arakiya was likely a countermeasure prepared by that real threat.
It was hard to imagine Arakiya actually being brought down, but Todd didn't believe an opponent capable of calculations this precise would come at the capture of the imperial capital empty-handed, without a plan.
Not just against Arakiya — there would be some preparation readied against the guardians of the other points too.

What's more, aside from those guarding the points, the regular soldiers, whose job should have been nothing but hunting down stragglers, had — while not exactly struggling against the rebels' movements — begun to take longer than expected.
He sensed a troublesome quality to it, as though some diabolical enemy mind had been fitted out with eyes and ears that could see far, and clever, dexterous limbs to match.

Strong enemies could be left to Arakiya and the other guardians.
But the truly troublesome enemy in a war, Todd thought, lay elsewhere.
And so—

"—I've got to go deal with whoever's causing the real mischief."
