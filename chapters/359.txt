Arc 5 Chapter 43: "Circumstances Before the Rendezvous"

"I'm sorry I couldn't lend my strength when it mattered most. I can only reflect on my own faithlessness."

Under the gaze of everyone in the room, Reinhard offered his apology.
At the sight of the Sword Saint bowing his head, everyone lost their words for a moment. It would have been easy enough to throw back words of denial at his apology. But even a hollow reassurance was, in all honesty, hard to manage.
The fact remained: for these past several hours, when they'd wanted his strength badly enough to reach out and grab for it, Reinhard had been missing, his whereabouts unknown.

If only his strength had been there when they'd fought to take the city hall — that thought was impossible to shake.
So even a perfunctory denial didn't come easily from anyone's mouth.
Except—

"Damn right it's true, you idiot. You have any idea how much trouble we were in without you?"

—except, that is, for Subaru, who walked up to the crimson hero and gave his chest a poke.
With a fist pressed to his chest, Reinhard turned an apologetic look on Subaru. It wasn't like him — looking scolded and dejected — and Subaru snorted at the sight.

"Besides, if you were gonna show up, you could've come fifteen minutes earlier. Thanks to you, I ended up giving some big speech that didn't even suit my character. That was supposed to be your job too, you know."

"I'm sorry. ...But it was a fine speech, very much you. Even if I'd been asked to do the same, I couldn't have delivered a broadcast that stirred up that much courage. It had to be you."

"I think what's expected from your broadcast and mine are pretty different things, you know."

Subaru poked Reinhard's chest again as the hero managed a bitter smile. Then he leveled a finger right at the downcast hero's nose.

"Reinhard."

"...What is it?"

"Having you here is worth a hundred men — no, a thousand. That's the kind of expectation I get to put on you, right? I'm counting on you."

"――――"

His was a strength equal to tens of thousands of reinforcements. Even Subaru's estimate of a hundred, a thousand men, was far too modest.
At Subaru's question, so full of expectation, Reinhard blinked his blue eyes. But his bewilderment faded quickly, and his lips eased into a smile.

"Yes, I want you to rely on me. If that's what you wish, I'll answer for it."

"Could you not phrase it in a way that's gonna delight a certain crowd of shippers? Eh, whatever. ...Anyway, that's how it is, so everybody else better say what they want to say now while they can."

Subaru grinned at Reinhard, whose face had lost its earlier burden, then turned around. He swept his gaze over the others who'd been unable to say anything until now, and pointed at Reinhard.

"Being handled with kid gloves is way harder to take at a time like this. Besides, you don't get many chances to chew out a Sword Saint who's just begging to be scolded. Go on, get it out of your system."

"――――"

"And once you've all had your fill of giving him grief, let's talk. —About how we're going to save everyone."

Subaru said it with his chest puffed out.
At his attitude, someone drew a quiet breath. But only Garfiel and Otto, familiar with Subaru's bluster, let their faces relax into something like a smile.
Well, having one or two people see right through him was about the right balance.

After all, he'd only just given a speech about not needing to shoulder everything alone.

* * *

After that came each person's individual words for Reinhard (details omitted), and then the discussion for retaking the city resumed in earnest.
That said, aside from Reinhard and Otto joining them, there wasn't much notable improvement. The number of enemies they still had to deal with, and the plan for simultaneously taking the control towers, remained the same.

"By the way, where were you and what were you doing during all this, Reinhard?"

They'd formed a circle to begin the discussion, and it was then that Subaru suddenly brought it up.
At the question, Reinhard's expression clouded over. It had been that kind of face all day.

"Felt's not with you either, and there's a lot I wanna ask. Oh, I'm not blaming you, okay? I figured that was already water under the bridge after what everyone just said."

"Setting aside how Natsuki-kun puts it, that's somethin' I'm curious about too. I don't figure the 'Sword Saint' was hidin' away out of fear or anything, but I'd like a reason that makes sense."

Anastasia piled onto Subaru's question. From the way she glanced sidelong at them, it seemed she too had already gotten a report from Julius.
A report about how, just before the uproar broke out, Reinhard and Felt had made contact with Heinkel.

The way Reinhard's expression had clouded at the question was proof enough of that.
Wilhelm was the same way — House Astrea's manner of dealing with Heinkel was always terribly evasive. It's an odd thing to say, but their approach was just like—

"...like elderly parents who can only handle their shut-in son of over a decade, now a full-blown professional NEET, as if he were something painful to the touch..."

"Sorry to interrupt while you're off in some strange delusion, Natsuki-san, but what should we do? If it's hard for Reinhard-san to talk about, I could explain instead."

Otto raised his hand and cut in on Subaru, who'd been picturing something like an evening news special on shut-ins. Otto's concerned gaze was fixed on Reinhard, and he spoke as though he were well informed on the matter.

"Now that you mention it, you and Reinhard came in together — don't tell me you were together this whole time too?"

"No, it's not like that. I only linked up with Reinhard-san at the very last moment... Even so, I've got a pretty good grasp of the situation."

"Thank you, Otto. But this is a problem in my own family. It is admittedly difficult to speak of, but it's only right that I be the one to tell it."

Reinhard shook his head, turning down Otto's considerate offer. Then he closed his eyes, and after a beat, straightened his posture.

"First, I've said it many times already, but let me apologize once more. I, who ought to have been the first to lend my aid, failed to join you for hours. I'm taking that deeply to heart."

"...On that point, our view was already conveyed earlier. We can't simply say there's no problem and forgive everything outright. But you're needed in the battle ahead. If you wish to reflect on yourself, then prove it through your actions on the battlefield."

"I intend to, Julius. ...When the Witch Cult's first broadcast came, Felt-sama and I had been summoned to a corner of the second district. The one who called us there was Vice-Captain Heinkel."

Reinhard, beginning his account, called Heinkel "Vice-Captain" in a rigid voice. That the two were father and son was common knowledge. And yet the fact that he addressed his father by rank alone was more than enough to make clear that whatever lay between them could not be resolved as a simple parent-child relationship.

"After that falling-out, Felt actually agreed to a summons?"

"In her heart, Felt-sama surely wanted to refuse. But once the matter was framed as concerning the authority over House Astrea's territory, refusing wasn't an option. We had no idea what would be demanded of us... but Felt-sama brought me along and went to the meeting place."

"So, how'd the talk go, what got decided..."

"I'm sorry, but that touches too closely on the inner workings of the Royal Selection. I'd appreciate it if I could avoid speaking of it. I'll only say the talk didn't go smoothly."

Reinhard's voice dropped, letting them sense, without words, just how badly the negotiation had gone.
Even without that, Felt was impulsive and had made no secret of her hostility. Combined with Heinkel's base character, it wasn't hard to imagine how the talk had descended into chaos.
And in the middle of that discussion—

"—the Witch Cult's first broadcast came. I couldn't believe my ears, and at the same time I knew I had to move at once. In fact, I'd kept myself ready to act the instant something urgent happened. I'd told the other retainers how to call for me if it was needed."

That method of calling him was, of course, the same signal from before — a burst of magic launched into the sky.
When Sirius had appeared, Rachins had launched that signal, and Reinhard had reached the scene in about thirty seconds. There was no lie in his words.
And yet, even hearing the blatant malice of the Witch Cult's broadcast, Reinhard hadn't moved. He hadn't rushed to the scene — instead, he'd kept silent for hours.
Just why was that?

"You must've known the broadcast came from the city hall. At that point, you had the option to rush to the city hall yourself. ...Why didn't you come?"

"――――"

He wasn't trying to blame him. At least, that was the intent, but the voice pressing for an answer came out rigid.
There was, at the very least, something to be said about it. If Reinhard's presence had been there for the fierce battle to retake the city hall — Crusch, even now struggling against her suffering, might never have been harmed at all. Subaru's right leg, too, might never have had to accept that inexplicable affliction.

Without meaning to, it wasn't only Subaru whose gaze hardened.
Under the gaze of everyone in the room, Reinhard, who had been speaking so fluently until then, fell silent and lowered his eyes once more.
His eyes, framed by long lashes, betrayed his hesitation over the words that were to follow.
Still, the hero wrestled down his inner conflict in a matter of seconds and spoke the rest of what he'd left unsaid.

"—Vice-Captain Heinkel took Felt-sama hostage."

"…………"

"It was an unforgivable failure on my part. Felt-sama was seized, a blade held to her throat, and I was left unable to move. That's why I couldn't act."

It was now clear just why those words had cost Reinhard so much to say.
His liege, to whom he owed his loyalty, had been taken hostage by none other than his own father's hand.
How deeply must humiliation and shame have tormented Reinhard's heart.

"...So, what, then? Was the Vice-Captain some kind of Witch Cult agent?"

Reeling from the shocking confession, Subaru murmured the question while trying to guess at Reinhard's state of mind.
What a cruel truth that would be. He'd heard how the Witch Cult lurked hidden among ordinary people, how there was no telling who might be a believer. But he didn't even want to imagine one appearing within someone's own family.
Now that he knew the true nature of the Sin Archbishops beyond just Petelgeuse, he felt that all the more strongly.
Because the Witch Cult, root and branch, to the very bottom of their hearts, was made up of nothing but human garbage.

"—If that were the case, I wonder. What would my heart have been, then?"

"What?"

But to Subaru's conclusion — the reasoning that roughly half the room found convincing — Reinhard responded with words that seemed to catch somewhere.
Subaru eyed his attitude with suspicion. But the other half of the room — faces like Anastasia's, Julius's, and Otto's — wore expressions that suggested they'd reached a different conclusion.

"The Vice-Captain has nothing to do with the Witch Cult. ...At least, nothing in what he said after restraining Felt-sama suggested anything of the sort."

"That's... no way. I mean, fine, maybe not 'no way'... but then why? Why take Felt hostage? What would even be the point of that—"

—he was about to finish, when Subaru caught himself.
Reinhard's gloomy expression, and the helpless looks on Otto and the others' faces — catching sight of them out of the corner of his eye, he wanted to laugh off the conclusion forming in his mind.
He couldn't laugh it off. It was beyond hopeless. If so, then Heinkel's actions were—

"Don't tell me — he wanted to keep you pinned down there?"

"――――"

"Once the Witch Cult's broadcast made it clear the city was in danger... he wanted the strongest fighting force close at hand, the quickest way to protect himself?"

"...The Vice-Captain said it himself. 'Your precious liege and your father are both right here. Would you abandon us to go running off to people whose faces you don't even know?'"

"And that's supposed to be something a father says?!"

Boiling over, Subaru slammed his fist against the floor.
All day, since morning, he'd been coming face to face with this same fury again and again. But he never imagined he'd feel this kind of anger toward someone who had nothing to do with the Witch Cult.

"I had no words to answer with. Of course, Felt-sama insisted it was a bluff — that I should forget about her and go help everyone else. —Even so, it was my own decision to stay there. I'm certain I'm the one who deserves the blame."

"How do you figure that?! Everyone in this room knows perfectly well whose fault this really is!"

"Even so, the choice was mine to make."

Reinhard wouldn't yield an inch of the blame, even as Subaru shouted at him.
Stubborn as he was, Reinhard clearly had no intention of bending on that point no matter what was said to him. Subaru clicked his tongue in irritation and forced himself, roughly, to calm down.

"In any case, that's how we ended up at a standstill. Nothing much happened after that. I couldn't respond to any of the later broadcasts either... and I believe I received quite the scolding from Felt-sama."

"So what happened to Felt-chan, seein' as she's not with you?"

Anastasia asked, as Reinhard managed a wry smile.
Toying with her scarf, her expression unreadable, she seemed concerned about the whereabouts of the absent Felt.

"Bein' a hostage for hours like that, she must be worn out somethin' fierce. Seein' as you're sittin' here now, Reinhard-kun, I'm guessin' things got sorted out."

"Yes, that's right. Felt-sama has since been reunited with her retainer at a shelter. As for the Vice-Captain, he's been restrained, and Felt-sama and the others are keeping watch over him."

"Restrained... you caught him?"

"That much, he'll simply have to accept. Though I don't think it would have been easy to manage without Otto's help."

"Otto's name's coming up here?"

Subaru frowned at the mention of Otto's name, which hadn't so much as hinted at appearing until now. As for Otto, he gave a light cough to draw attention, seizing his cue.

"That's how it was. Though I only arrived at that scene through a pile-up of one coincidence after another. Still, I already understood the relationship between the three of them, so just seeing the situation was enough to grasp roughly what was going on."

The trouble within House Astrea, and the matter of Felt's territory.
On top of that, he'd witnessed the scene of Heinkel holding Felt hostage to keep Reinhard in place. Even with poor circulation to the brain, anyone could understand what that meant.

"I understood right away that not being able to borrow Reinhard-san's strength against the Witch Cult was about the worst situation there could be. The blood drained right out of me, and at the same time I knew I had to do something about it, somehow."

"So Otto drew his attention and somehow got Felt free, and that's what let Reinhard move, and that's how we got here... is that about right?"

"Fortunately, there was also Natsuki-san's big speech, so I didn't have to worry about where to meet up. It would've been better if I could have moved sooner, but I had my own complications too."

Brief as it was, Reinhard gave a nod at the mention of Otto's contribution.
Once again, Otto had quietly done his part where no one was watching — a true unsung hero.

"But hold on, what's Otto-nii been doin' this whole time? Honestly, with how strong Otto-nii is, wanderin' around the city like that's basically suicide, ain't it?"

"That's actually a rather roundabout story... but, all right, let me tell it."

Otto cleared his throat and pointed out beyond the city hall.

"As planned this morning, I went alone to the Muse Company. I meant to ask Kiritaka-san to resume our negotiations. He'd had a night to cool down, so it looked like I'd be able to secure a promise to renegotiate without much trouble... but—"

"But?"

From the way he trailed off, Subaru assumed the broadcast must have started right then. But Otto shook his head and said—

"A member of the Witch Cult — a Sin Archbishop — appeared in the third district's main plaza, right where the Muse Company is. That person caused a disturbance, and that's how the Witch Cult's attack came to light."

"A Sin Archbishop...?! You mean before the broadcast?"

"Yes. That's right, before the broadcast."

Otto nodded at Subaru, who leaned forward in surprise. But thinking about it, it wasn't such an impossible turn of events. Sirius and Regulus, too, had been Sin Archbishops who'd begun acting before the broadcast.
Aside from Capella, who had presumably attacked the city hall, it was entirely possible that every free Sin Archbishop had been active somewhere in Priestella—

"Wait, Otto."

"――――"

At Subaru's lowered tone, Otto's gaze took on a grieving cast.
His careful, concerned manner confirmed the very thing forming in Subaru's mind.

Sirius and Regulus were both impossible — Subaru and the others had already run into them. Capella, too, must have taken part in the attack on the city hall.
That left only one candidate for the Sin Archbishop who had appeared before Otto.

"The one you ran into — was it the Sin Archbishop of Gluttony?"

"...Yes. That's what she called herself. There'd be no reason to lie about it, so I believe that's exactly who it was. The Sin Archbishop who gave that name in the plaza looked, to me, still like a child."

Otto's testimony matched the Roy Alphard Subaru had seen. He didn't particularly want to know the selection criteria for Sin Archbishops, but Gluttony was, at least in appearance, a child — one dressed in tattered, filthy rags, with an unpleasant way of laughing.

"At first, someone nearby thought it was just a disrespectful child and warned it off. He'd been guarding the Muse Company, so I believe he was one of the White Dragon's Scales. He was the first one Gluttony overpowered. Literally flattened."

"…………"

"When a person gets flattened, there's no choice but to believe it, like it or not. The White Dragon's Scales mobilized at once and surrounded her, but it wasn't even a contest."

Pale-faced, Otto described the carnage in few words. Gluttony had apparently mowed down the mercenaries who came at her carelessly, as though dancing. And more terrifying still was Gluttony's sense of smell — a nose that let no prey escape.

"Given the situation, people on the street tried to flee for their lives. But she wouldn't allow it. Honestly, I don't really understand what she did. But even people some distance away got caught by Gluttony's attacks. The same was true even inside buildings."

"What was she even after?"

Subaru asked before he could stop himself, then realized it was a meaningless question. There was no consistency to be found in the Witch Cult's actions or aims. Sure enough, Otto had no answer for him either, and only shook his head.

"Who knows what it was. In any case, it was a disaster. Even trying to flee, the moment you turned your back, her attack might reach you. It was a bit of a hopeless situation. ...If it hadn't been for Kiritaka-san, I don't think I'd be here right now."

"Kiritaka did something to help you?"

"I suppose he was a cautious man to begin with. In the chairman's office of the Muse Company, there was a hidden passage leading down to an underground waterway. I used it to escape outside by small boat. As I fled, I saw Kiritaka-san's back get cut."

"――――"

"From the third district, I fled the rest of the way in a pretty sorry state. After that, the Witch Cult's broadcast came, and I couldn't move carelessly around the city anymore, so I made my way through town as carefully as I could. And that's how I finally came across that scene from earlier."

That meant the scene where Reinhard's movement had been sealed.
With the story now connected, Subaru understood at the same time that Otto, too, had somehow survived his own ordeal.
Still, there was something in the story that nagged at him.

"Why would Kiritaka go that far for you? From what you just said, it sounds like he sacrificed himself to get you out. That's how it sounded to me, anyway."

"...Yes, that's right. Kiritaka-san threw himself in the way to let me escape. The reason he got cut at the end was because he shoved me under the floor to shield me."

"Why would he go that far..."

Subaru barely had any impression of Kiritaka to speak of. What little image he did have was fixed on the scene of the man, pale-faced and voice cracking, flustered enough to hurl a magic stone at him. Of course, he didn't think that was the whole of who the man was.
But he never would have pegged him as the kind of man strong enough to put his own body on the line for someone else.

"It might partly have been a merchant's pride — you were a guest he'd invited into his company, after all — but I'm sure his real reason, deep down, was something else. Something simpler."

"Simpler how?"

"You don't get it? It's you, Natsuki-san."

Otto stated the answer plainly to a bewildered Subaru. Subaru faltered at the sudden appearance of his own name.
At Subaru's reaction, Otto lowered his eyes, looking somewhat frustrated, and said—

"Faced with a Sin Archbishop, watching his own men get cut down — I can only imagine what was going through Kiritaka-san's mind. But I think he also felt, at the same time, a sense of duty that something had to be done. And you, Natsuki-san, became his hope."

"…………"

"From your meeting the day before, Kiritaka-san knew you were staying in the city. He knew about your feat of defeating Sloth as well. So it's only natural his hope would tie back to that. The very reason he prioritized getting me out first was surely because he saw me as someone who could connect back to you."

Otto's explanation was reasonable, and it settled neatly into Subaru's chest. But that was only true on the level of logic.

He understood it in his head. And there was no mistake that he'd resolved to shoulder it.
But was this happening again? Was everyone hoping this of him, of all people?
Were they placing the fate of the entire city on the shoulders of some tiny, hopeless good-for-nothing?

"Natsuki-san, don't get the wrong idea, okay?"

That voice stopped Subaru just as a cynical smile threatened to rise on his face. Otto was looking straight at him, peering into his eyes. Seeing Subaru's expression stiffen, Otto gave a shrug and said—

"Kiritaka-san is one of the city's people in charge, so I'm sure a lot went through his mind. And I understand you getting cold feet about shouldering the fate of the whole city and all that. But I'm sure what Kiritaka-san actually hoped for from you was something much simpler."

"...Like what?"

"Isn't it obvious? This is the city where the woman Kiritaka-san loves lives. Before anything about protecting the city, what he hoped for from you was the safety of the woman he loves."

"――――"

"That's what he said, when he shoved me under the floor."

In the end, what Otto meant to say was that Subaru was overthinking it, taking it too seriously. It was a remark born of watching Subaru stagger under a weight he'd taken it upon himself to carry — the fate of the whole city. And it struck, harder than expected, at the conceit that had crept into Subaru's heart.

His face grew hot, and Subaru felt ashamed of himself.
The fate of the city, the hopes and expectations of the masses — how ridiculous.

How foolish, to see what needed to be carried only in the shape of something as abstract as "the city."
What Subaru needed to protect wasn't some rigid word on the surface like that, but the individual people who made it up. One life, and then another.
And each of those lives had someone precious to them — it was that web of connections he needed to save.

"Does it feel a little lighter, thinking of it as carrying countless small things instead of one big one?"

"Damn. It's just a different way of looking at it, but somehow it actually feels real now."

Otto's support skills were simply too good, and Subaru genuinely couldn't hold his head up around him. Subaru's face, which had been bowed the whole time, lifted at last, and Otto looked satisfied with the result.

"Well now, seems like you two got a good thing goin'. Watchin' you was almost enough to make me jealous."

Seeing the conversation reach a lull, Anastasia teased them like that. Subaru and Otto, who'd ended up speaking almost face-to-face despite everyone sitting in a circle, felt a bit chastened by it.
Then, as everyone's faces turned back to face one another again, Anastasia tilted her head.

"Still, goin' by what you just said... is the Muse Company done for, then?"

"Honestly, I don't know. I saw Kiritaka-san get cut, but I couldn't tell whether that wound was fatal or not."

"Is it pointless to even try to think about Gluttony's goal? I simply cannot believe she committed such an outrage for no reason at all."

It was Julius, his face stern, who cut in. The oddly certain way he said it was bothersome.

"You're awfully hung up on this. You got some kind of basis for it?"

"...No, I can't say I have a clear reason. Call it an impression from crossing blades with her at the city hall, perhaps. I don't think we should conclude she's simply acting for thrills."

Subaru agreed with that much. She wasn't simply acting for thrills — she was a malicious one at that. Though it seemed his impression pointed to a different answer than whatever Julius had in mind.

"According to the broadcast, Gluttony's demand was for the artificial spirit, right? Could it be she's looking for it, by any chance?"

"As a possibility, sure. But if there's a bigger question than that, it's whether this 'artificial spirit' even exists to begin with. Does something like that really exist?"

Reinhard cut in on Subaru, who had voiced the motive that occurred to him.
His doubt was surely one that had crossed the minds of many who'd heard that broadcast's demand.
The answer to that, in this room, only Subaru and Anastasia possessed. Subaru couldn't help glancing at Anastasia's face, but she, for her part, wore her usual practiced, composed expression. Did that mean she had no intention of revealing it?

"—Sorry, just one thing."

But before Subaru could decide what to do, Otto raised his hand, drawing everyone's attention. A trace of unease showed on his face as he let out a sigh tinged with resignation.
And then—

"As for that artificial spirit, I can't say either. But there's another demand — the one from Wrath, about the 'Book of Wisdom'..."

He paused there, hesitated slightly, and then said—

"I'm sorry. —I'm the one who brought it into this city."
