Arc 4 Chapter 127B: "Don't Give Me 'That's Enough'"

There was nothing in the room save the coffin in which the pale-haired woman slept.

"So then... this thing this person's sleeping in is... the mechanism for stopping the barrier?"

Having circled the room once, Emilia tilted her head, troubled, at the conclusion she'd reached.
There was no obvious lever, no magic stone she could simply smash. The transparent magic stone in which the woman slept carried a faint glow, and she could tell it was drawing in mana.
At the very least, this coffin was the only thing functioning.

"Who could she really be...? Could she be, I don't know, Echidna's mother or something?"

Emilia recalled the face of the Witch who had expressed such displeasure toward her.
Like the woman sleeping in the coffin, she too had had white hair and worn black clothing, and it was still fresh in Emilia's memory that she'd possessed remarkably refined beauty, even to Emilia's eyes.
The Witch's face as Emilia remembered it and the sleeping woman's face shared quite a few points of resemblance, somehow.

The placement of the closed eyes, the line from the bridge of the nose down to the lips.
Whereas Echidna had appeared to be in her late teens, the woman in the coffin looked to be somewhere in her mid-twenties. Rather than a mother, perhaps an older sister was the more likely guess.

"There's no name carved anywhere... But this is supposed to be Echidna's tomb."

Now that she'd come inside, the woman sleeping in the coffin was not Echidna.
Should she call this a case of the tomb's name being a lie? Or if not that, then—

"What if this is Echidna, and the girl I saw in my dream isn't?"

That was quite the outlandish conclusion, Emilia thought, shaking her head at her own words.
Setting aside what Echidna herself claimed, if that were the case, it wouldn't be strange for Sekhmet to have said something about it. And at this point, it was hard for Emilia to think of that girl as anyone other than Echidna.

"So... I guess the conclusion is that this is Echidna's tomb, but someone else is sleeping here?"

If that were so, wouldn't the name need to be changed?
The gravestone said "Here Sleeps Echidna," yet if someone else was actually sleeping there, that posed all sorts of problems. She'd even have to prepare different offerings.

Even as she arrived at a rather muddled answer, Emilia examined the coffin without touching it.
Lightly tracing the flow of mana, it seemed the coffin as a whole was absorbing trace amounts of mana from the tomb and the earth connected to it, using that mana as the driving force for some kind of spell formula.
Despite drawing only truly minuscule amounts at a time, it managed to sustain a barrier operating on quite a massive scale — surely because the area over which it gathered mana was simply so vast.

"The tomb and the earth connected to the tomb" meant exactly that, literally.
Presumably, the entire stretch of forest enclosed by the barrier served as the mana supply source sustaining the tomb's function — of course, only in trace amounts small enough not to affect the source.

"Amazing... It's so amazing I have no idea what it's even doing..."

A spell formula that drew in the flow of mana to sustain a magical function.
Emilia herself could write a simple spell formula or two, but the complexity of the formula acting on the barrier surrounding the Sanctuary was far beyond her comprehension.
Once its function was stopped, she had the feeling it could never be started again.
Not that there was ever any need to start it again, of course.

"Found it. If I cut off the flow here, that should stop the supply."

Tracing the flow of mana, she located the activation point of the barrier, centered on the coffin.
At the center of the coffin — right above where the sleeping woman's hands were folded over her stomach — was the point where mana flowed in. If Emilia interfered there with her own mana and disrupted the formula, the tomb's function would stop completely.

"――――"

For just a moment, she hesitated.
Stopping the tomb's function would presumably also cripple whatever triggered the Trial. And if that happened, it would also mean losing her means of reaching the dream castle.

*—The tea party, whose message she'd entrusted to Echidna, would go unfulfilled too.*

The Witches — or at least Sekhmet — had seemed to know something about Emilia's mother.
Vaguely, alongside her awe at Sekhmet's overwhelming presence, there had been something like a nostalgic sense of closeness. Part of her wanted to find out what that meant.
The means to confirm that would slip further away if the dream castle vanished. That—

"—Honestly, I really am hung up on things, aren't I."

With that murmur, the mana running along her fingertips disturbed the faint flow where it touched the coffin.
A change arose in the flow of power that had sustained the Sanctuary's function and formed the barrier. It interfered with the very root of the spell formula, and the small change, as it propagated, swelled into something immense.
Eventually the particles of light dissolved away, and the coffin of magic stone lost all trace of the spell formula. The light flickered once, faintly, as if slipping free, and what remained the instant after was exactly what met the eye: pure magic stone, and the woman sealed within it.

"...I guess that's it?"

Seeing that no more visible changes were occurring, Emilia looked around timidly. The flow of mana that had been strung throughout the tomb until moments ago was gone now; the tomb should have changed roles into nothing more than a massive stone structure.

Emilia let out a small breath and leaned back against the coffin.
Presumably, this meant people without qualification would no longer be rejected upon entering the tomb. She should probably bring Roswaal or Ryuzu, who might know something about all this, and have them confirm who this person sleeping in the coffin was.

"It's over... yeah, it's over..."

Emilia repeated the words, as if confirming the fact aloud, trying to find some sense of reality in something that didn't yet feel real.
She recalled the bold words she'd flung at Roswaal before challenging the tomb.
Roswaal had told her: "Just produce whatever result your heart desires."

She couldn't guess what Roswaal had been feeling then, but he had probably not wanted Emilia to overcome the Trial — even though he was the one who'd invited her here and recommended her as a Royal Selection candidate in the first place. It was a way of thinking she couldn't understand.

"Teacher... that's what he called them, right?"

What came to mind alongside that was the person Roswaal had once referred to, almost in passing, as "Teacher."
Even Roswaal, who as a mage possessed power that placed him near the very summit, had — quite naturally — had a teacher of his own. His teacher, this someone called "Teacher," had begun this business of the Sanctuary together with Roswaal.

"Could it be... that's you?"

Somehow, touching the coffin, that was how Emilia felt.
If there really was a person called Teacher, someone who must have been irreplaceable to Roswaal, then it felt fitting that it should be this pale-haired woman.

"—I need to tell everyone."

Shaking her head, Emilia tore her eyes from the coffin, trying not to leave any lingering attachment.
Whether or not to say anything about the woman in the coffin, everything else came first for now. According to what Subaru had told her, something terrible would happen unless she left the Sanctuary sometime after tomorrow night — that is, by the morning of the day after tomorrow.
If any conspicuous change occurred in the Sanctuary, he'd wanted her to flee at once, without a second thought.
Even though there was a full day's margin, there was no telling what unexpected turn of events might occur.

She hurried out of the small chamber and into the passage, heading for the tomb's exit. If things were as they'd been when they saw her off, Ryuzu and the people of the Sanctuary should be waiting for her.

Her shoes clicking sharply, Emilia dashed down the passage and emerged from the dim tomb into the great hall.
And then,

"—Eh?"

Facing a Sanctuary lashed by a raging blizzard, she let out a ragged, white breath.


* * *


A snowscape blanketed the world before her eyes.
The gusting wind was almost violent, and the snow striking her body stole her warmth without mercy. Breathing white breath, blinking her violet eyes, Emilia's throat had frozen solid with shock.

*—What in the world had happened!?*

"—Lia-sama!"

The wind roared, and her ears, rapidly losing heat, ached.
Lightly dressed, Emilia felt as though the frigid wind were slicing into her body as she stepped toward the voice reaching her from beyond the blizzard.

The snow had already piled up nearly to Emilia's knees, and her feet threatened to be swallowed up with every step. Fighting to pull her feet free and push through the snow, she saw, beyond the white curtain of the blizzard, a number of shapes huddled together.
The people of the Sanctuary, perhaps? If so, that would mean they'd been waiting for Emilia out here in this blizzard, without taking shelter inside any building—

"Everyone! Why are you all out here in snow like this...? Eh?"

Emilia's words trailed off, the picture she'd imagined — of people huddled together warding off the cold — falling apart before her eyes.
With Ryuzu at their head, the Sanctuary's residents numbered around forty gathered there. That alone would have been a considerable number — but she had also spotted figures who had no business being there.

"Emilia-sama! Is the Trial over!?"

The one who called out to her was a young man with a close-cropped haircut.
Emilia knew him. They'd exchanged words even before coming to the Sanctuary — and even if he hadn't recognized her, she certainly recognized him.

He was one of the young men from Arlam Village's youth corps.
Emilia's eyes went wide at the sight of him — he was supposed to have boarded a dragon carriage under Otto's guidance and left the Sanctuary hours ago. And he wasn't the only thing to surprise her.

With the close-cropped young man at the fore, she could make out here and there the faces of other Arlam Village residents. They'd brought dragon carriages with them, and Sanctuary folk and evacuees alike were sheltering from the snow together, huddled inside and behind the carriages.

"W-why is everyone here...? Weren't you supposed to have evacuated?"

"Yes, we did evacuate. On Subaru-sama and Otto-san's instructions. We were told the Earth Dragons remembered the way, so we should flee without worrying about appearances."

"Then why!? Weren't you all told this place would become dangerous?"

"We were told. And we were given instructions, too."

At Emilia's words the young man clenched his teeth once, then raised his head and pointed beyond the forest.

"We were told to wait outside the forest, and if a signal came from the Sanctuary, to come back and collect the residents."

"Eh...?"

"Whether it'd be Ram-sama, who was staying behind, or someone else — either way, they'd send magic up into the sky. If we saw that, we were to return to the Sanctuary, collect the residents by carriage, and leave the area. Those were our instructions."

"Instructions from who!?"

"Otto-san told us that."

At the mention of Otto's name, Emilia recalled the face of that meek-looking merchant.
But for all his appearance, he was Subaru's friend. Every time she saw the two of them chatting amiably, Emilia had revised her opinion of him upward — clearly he was someone who could hold his own alongside Subaru, close as they were.
There was no doubt that, in this whole Sanctuary affair — starting with winning over Garfiel — Otto had been scheming behind the scenes in all sorts of ways as Subaru's chief strategist.
If so, then perhaps these instructions carried real significance too.

"B-but this is reckless! I mean, with a blizzard blowing this hard... you should've been able to tell this was too much, even in the middle of it!"

"..."

"What's wrong?"

At Emilia's words, the young man looked away awkwardly. Emilia didn't miss that reaction, and pressed him on what had happened.
Pierced by Emilia's violet gaze, the young man put a hand to his forehead and let out a white breath.

"Otto-san told us that if things got truly dangerous, snow would fall before any signal came. If snow fell, that meant time was up... and we were told to leave the forest immediately."

"So snow falling was also... no, never mind that. Then why, why are you here!?"

"—Because the snow fell."

To Emilia's near-tearful voice, the young man straightened his back and answered clearly.
The strength in his gaze left Emilia at a loss for words.

They had originally been told that if snow fell, it meant danger.
And when they actually saw snow falling on the Sanctuary, they understood that the dangerous time limit had run out. In the end, the choice they made was to come here.
To rush to the side of the Sanctuary's people, now exposed to danger as the snow fell.

"It just felt like something Subaru-sama or Emilia-sama would do."

"..."

The young man gave a wry smile and answered the question that had been rising in Emilia's throat.
So the evacuees guarding the dragon carriages behind him were indeed people from Arlam Village's youth corps. Since the purpose was to bring the Sanctuary's residents out, they'd presumably set down everyone but the necessary personnel and had them evacuate separately. Though, of course, those set down were now forced to evacuate on foot.
They must have accepted that as the necessary cost, which was why they were here now.

"Emilia-sama, if the Trial is over... can you leave now?"

"Y-yes... that should be the case. But with this blizzard and snow..."

The young man glared down at his own feet and clicked his tongue in frustration.
His reaction said it all. The snow made even a short walk a struggle. Naturally, the dragon carriages' wheels couldn't move through this either — there was nothing to do but sit stranded.
If only there were somewhere a large group could warm themselves and wait out the cold—

"If we can't make it to the cathedral... let's go into the tomb. In there, mana keeps it warm, and there's no risk of the building collapsing under the weight of the snow."

"Can we get inside?"

"I stopped the tomb's dangerous mechanism, so it's fine now. More importantly, can you get everyone to the tomb? I want the Earth Dragons unhitched from the carriages and brought inside too."

The six Earth Dragons had understood the youth corps's resolve and carried them all the way back here. Even now, dozens of people were being saved thanks to the carriages the dragons had pulled.
Abandoning them was simply not an option.

At Emilia's words, the young man nodded and said, "Without fail."
For the moment, that would do as a response to the snow. The problem was the danger that "something" would arrive now that the snow had fallen.

"Ugh, if this is how it was going to be, I should've properly heard the whole story!"

She regretted not having had time for a proper talk with Subaru before facing the Trial. Probably, Subaru simply hadn't wanted to leave Emilia with any extra cause for worry while she confronted the Trial.
That consideration touched her, honestly — but it was no good if it meant her response was delayed.
Someone else, like Subaru and the others, who might know about this snow — three candidates rose in Emilia's mind. Roswaal, Ram, and—

"Emilia-sama, well done returning to us."

"Ryuzu-san!"

Stepping down onto the snow from one of the dragon carriages and reaching a hand toward Emilia was the girl with long, pale pink hair — Ryuzu. Being as short as she was, snow that only reached Emilia's knees came up to nearly her thighs. As she pushed through it looking thoroughly put-upon, Emilia likewise forced her way through the snow to close the distance.

"I finished the Trial, properly! Is everyone here!?"

"Every resident of this Sanctuary, and every human who came back for us. Only..."

"Only?"

"Little Miss Ram and Roz-bou aren't here. The two of them left together before the snow even started falling, and haven't come back."

Emilia turned her head, scanning the carriages and the people around them.
There was no sign of the tall, eccentrically dressed figure she knew so well, nor of the dependable pink-haired girl.

"I have to go find them... Ryuzu-san! Do you know — if we stay here in the Sanctuary while it's snowing like this, what happens?"

"..."

From Ryuzu's stiffened cheeks and downcast eyes, Emilia understood instinctively.
Ryuzu knew the circumstances. She knew exactly what that danger was.

"Tell me, Ryuzu-san. We have to stop it, whatever it is."

"But the timing's off. By Su-bou's account, or by Roz-bou's plan, the snow wasn't supposed to fall until tomorrow night. This snow falling now, this way — it must be some kind of mistake..."

"Mistake or not, the snow's already fallen! So! We have to do whatever needs doing once the snow falls! Ryuzu-san!"

Placing a hand on her thin, small shoulders, Emilia tried to reason with the hesitating Ryuzu.
Then, the reluctant look on Ryuzu's face changed, and she stared back at Emilia with an expression of blank astonishment.

"W-what's wrong, Ryuzu-san?"

"...Emilia-sama. Did you enter the building deep in the Sanctuary's forest?"

"Deep in the forest...? No, I haven't gone there."

At a question she had no memory relating to, Emilia tilted her head, wondering what this was about. At her answer, Ryuzu blinked, murmuring "That can't be..." and then looked past Emilia, at the tomb behind her.

"Could it be... was there something inside the tomb? Something special. For instance... something like a huge magic stone?"

"—There was. A really, really big magic stone. I was planning to have you and Roswaal come confirm it later, actually."

Glancing around warily, Emilia leaned close to whisper in Ryuzu's ear.
Careful not to let the people behind her, already preparing to move the Earth Dragons, overhear.

"Actually, there's a woman inside the magic stone. I don't know who she is."

"—!"

Ryuzu's expression changed dramatically at that piece of information.
Within her childlike features, Ryuzu's eyes went wide as she stared at Emilia. Then she let out a long, long breath, murmured "So that's it..." and nodded, as though something had finally clicked into place.

"Now I understand. Emilia-sama, please, ask me anything you like. I have a duty to answer. A duty to obey the order that's been handed down."

"An order, that's..."

"Just listen. What Emilia-sama touched in the tomb is a magic stone that chooses the one qualified to command 'Ryuzu.' Just now, that qualification passed from Gar-bou to Emilia-sama. I... no, all of us, shall obey Emilia-sama. Command us however you wish."

Solemnly, Ryuzu tried to lower herself in the snow that reached her thighs. Emilia panicked, realizing this gesture was meant as kneeling, and that at this rate Ryuzu's whole head would end up buried.
She caught her by the shoulders on reflex,

"Wawa! I get it, I get it! So I'm in a position to ask you for things, then. All right, then, please. Tell me what happens to the Sanctuary once the snow falls."

"...By Su-bou's account, once snow falls on the Sanctuary, the Witchbeast known as the Great Rabbit comes. Drawn by the mana of the weather-altering spell formula powerful enough to make it snow... or so I'm told."

"A weather-altering spell formula... you mean someone is making this weather happen!?"

Startled, Emilia asked back, and Ryuzu nodded wordlessly.
Magic on a scale large enough to manipulate the weather. If Puck went all out, he could probably have pulled off something like this easily. In that sense, he was the prime suspect — but tracing Ryuzu's attitude and the order of events, Emilia quickly identified the real culprit.

"...Roswaal did this?"

"Probably. I believe Little Miss Ram went to stop him. But the fact that the snow fell anyway means either something happened, or else..."

"Stop. I don't want to imagine that. Anyway, I have to find the two of them. Ryuzu-san, I'll go search the village for them myself. If you have any idea where they might be..."

"There's no need for that, Emilia-sama."

Ryuzu cut in with startling confidence, interrupting Emilia's words.
Her manner suggested she had some plan in mind, and Emilia caught her breath faintly.
And then,

"Within this Sanctuary, we Ryuzus have eyes everywhere, watching. —We'll find Roz-bou and the others in no time and guide you to them."

So she declared, guaranteeing it with total assurance.


* * *


Unconscious, Ram looked as though she were merely sleeping.

"...Ram?"

Roswaal gathered up the girl who had collapsed limply and called out to her, shaking her body. But there was no response from Ram to his call.
Ram, who normally set Roswaal's every word above all else, no matter what.

Of course there wasn't.
Ram was, right now, teetering on the very brink of death. And every bit of it was Roswaal's own doing.
Seeing the Gospel burned, the blood had rushed to his head in an instant. Everything before his eyes had gone red, he'd lost all sense of what was happening, and all he'd felt was that he could not forgive Ram for what she had done—

"..."

He'd hurled the mass of flame he'd conjured, and Ram, defenseless, had been blown away by the impact.
Her body, already having overworked her incomplete Oni power and borne the same burden mere hours earlier, had reached its limit for continued movement.
And into that, he had driven a fireball without mercy.

Her life now hung by the thinnest of threads.

"...Ram."

He couldn't recall what he'd been thinking as he walked toward her fallen body, up until the moment he touched her. Even now, holding her small frame and gazing at her young, sleeping face, nothing came to mind.
To begin with — why, for what reason, had Ram turned against him?

To Roswaal, Ram had been a truly convenient, useful pawn.
The circumstances by which she'd come under him were what they were, and the Contract they'd formed had become a bond maintaining an extremely straightforward relationship for both of them.
To Ram alone had he revealed his true feelings; to Ram alone had he spoken his goal. All of it, believing it to be repayment to his accomplice — handing his own person over to her once that goal was achieved.

And yet, midway along that path, Ram had betrayed Roswaal.
Certainly, what she'd said was correct — thinking in terms of the Contract, her raising a flag of rebellion against Roswaal, who had failed to carry events forward according to the scenario, was a revenge long since decided upon.
So he had no intention of blaming Ram for that. If he had to say anything, it was only that he'd wished she would have put off her revenge a little longer, at least until she'd seen this world through to its result.
There was also the matter of the wager with Subaru. Garfiel had turned out to be more of a soft touch than expected, and Emilia had gone back to face the Trial spouting words as if she understood everything — but in the end, it was nothing more than the delusional talk of the weak.

There was no way to defy a future that had already been fixed. Even if the path taken to reach it changed, the future always arrived at its predetermined end. If one strayed from the path leading to the correct ending, what awaited was the end of one's natural life.
And yet here they were, trying to defy it — it was almost laughable. And laughable too, was his own weakness, fearing their actions even while understanding all this.

Why try to change? He couldn't understand it.
Every feeling ought to continue on, unfading, from the very moment it reached its peak. If one had ever loved someone, if there had been a time when one's heart burned for that person, then that heat, that brilliance, ought to be eternal.

Even if the vector of that feeling wasn't romantic longing — hatred, anything at all, it was the same.
A feeling wished for, long and long over time, ought to be sublimated into something real. A feeling that had endured through time was unshakable, unyielding to anyone. It had to be that way.

Garfiel's heart, hating the outside world, would be shattered.
Emilia's sorrowful past, the past she loathed, would be accepted.
And Ram's vengeful hatred for Roswaal, which should have been inexhaustible—

*"Ram loves Roswaal-sama."*

"—!!"

A confession of love, like a curse, still seared into the depths of his ears even now.
Words that should never have been, spoken from the mouth of the girl now lying with closed eyes in his arms.

He could have understood it, if only while the Contract still bound her heart and Soul. Her blazing desire for revenge, converted into subservience to Roswaal; hatred transformed into love itself.
That was precisely why Roswaal had been able to trust Ram, more than anyone, both to assist him toward his goal and to handle matters once it was achieved.

All of it, because he had believed in the hatred that could be swapped for love, and in the vengeful desire to kill Roswaal.
Because he had believed in the hatred of that young girl, who from the very first meeting had looked at Roswaal with eyes distilled down to pure murderous intent.

—And yet, Ram had betrayed her own thirst for revenge, and sung of love instead.

"Why, Ram... I don't understand..."

The faint sound of her breathing grew more distant, and Roswaal sensed the end of Ram's life approaching.
His heart pounded faster, something inside him screaming that things couldn't go on like this. His right eye throbbed. Terribly, terribly, it throbbed. Stop. Don't assert your presence. He, himself, was ceasing to be himself.
What should he do. What should he do. He didn't know what he had to do, what he mustn't do. He couldn't remember. He couldn't think.

He looked around. What he sought was nowhere to be found. The Gospel, which had shown the future and told Roswaal the correct path, had been thrown into the flames and lost its form. No one would tell Roswaal anything. What he had to choose, now — no one would tell him.
Then, well, there was nothing else for it.

"—Winds of lamentation summon clouds, and the light that fills the earth returns to the sky. The droplet becomes silence given form, and untimely white grieves for the eternal world—"

An incantation.
The incantation, hummed like a song, gave direction to the power surrounding Roswaal L. Mathers. The vast, stockpiled mana interfered with the spell formula he had been refining all along, and dark clouds began to gather over the forest night.
A frigid wind blew, and cold sharp enough to freeze one to the marrow swept through the Sanctuary.
Snow clouds covered the entire enclosed forest, and white snow crystals began falling one after another onto the earth.

—The power of the ultra-large-scale spell formula magic, "Artemilion."

"—Ugh... hnh."

The incantation finished, the power of a spell so long in the making was released.
An enormous quantity of mana was drawn out of his body all at once, and even Roswaal, who boasted a superlative personal mana reserve, felt a faint dizziness.

Ordinarily, magic on this scale would require months to stockpile enough mana, and even then would be used within a far more limited area. It was Roswaal himself who was abnormal — preparing in a mere two days or so, and interfering with an area more than double the usual range.
And the mage who had accomplished this feat let out a long breath, and then found himself at a complete loss.

"Just as the Gospel said, I've made it snow... now, what should I do?"

By this point, Roswaal had forgotten the fact that he'd made it snow a full day earlier than the Gospel's account. No — even the matter of the wager no longer remained anywhere in his mind.
For Roswaal, the process no longer registered at all. All that mattered was the very last part of the events surrounding the Sanctuary. The snow falls, the barrier is undone.
Once that was accomplished, once that was accomplished— what came after, again?

"Ram... ah, that's right. Ram."

Ram, whose breathing could no longer be heard at all.
Looking down at her face, Roswaal gently laid a palm against her forehead. From the white scar where a horn had once been, blood was seeping under the influence of her Oni transformation. Wiping the blood away, Roswaal poured in colorless mana — a composite of every attribute — just as he always did for Ram.
A ritual he had carried on all this time, so that Ram's body wouldn't succumb to her own native Oni blood.

It wasn't that he had any particular thought guiding him.
He simply understood, unconsciously, that to keep Ram's life going, there was nothing to do but stake everything on the vitality of her own Oni blood. Nor was there any doubt in his mind about wanting to save her.
Ram had to live. Both for the sake of Roswaal achieving his goal, and for what came after that goal was achieved.

"Teacher... Teacher... I... what should I do! Teacher... Teacher...! Please, tell me... please guide me again..."

Roswaal's mind had reached the height of confusion, and he no longer understood even the part of himself that was voicing these whimpering pleas.
Even as he tried to keep Ram's life going, his anger at her betrayal hadn't faded either. Even understanding he'd lost his guiding light, he still reached for the glow he'd once seen.

The snow fell, mercilessly covering both Roswaal's and Ram's bodies in white.
Everything covered in white, vanishing, disappearing.

And yet, for some reason, not even the faintest sliver of a thought that this might be fine took root within him.


* * *


Following the one who led her, kicking through the snow, Emilia ran on, breathing white breath.

"Huma! More, Huma!"

She cried out, continuing to chant her ice magic.
It was a measure to avoid the time lost from her feet repeatedly sinking into the piling snow. By deliberately hardening the snow with ice magic, she created an improvised foothold and ran across the top of it.
For some people this would risk slipping and landing them in worse danger,

"Hyah! Hup! Oof!"

but Emilia, an ice mage herself and someone raised in the Elior Forest, was thoroughly at home on a frozen footing. She dashed across the Sanctuary's icy snow footing as if it were her own backyard, chasing after the small figure leading the way.

"Are you sure this way's really okay?"

Falling in alongside her guide, Emilia asked between short, panting breaths. In answer, the low-eyed girl glanced her way, but only gave a small nod of the chin, saying nothing.
Communication was possible, but she basically wouldn't engage in conversation. Just as the original Ryuzu — the representative one — had told her beforehand.

Ryuzu, who had so suddenly begun treating Emilia with reverence in the plaza before the tomb.
She had told Emilia that she was a being born from the Soul of a girl who had once existed, named Ryuzu Meyer, and had also explained that multiple beings sharing that same standing existed within the Sanctuary. Those Ryuzus, working as "eyes" throughout the Sanctuary, would find Roswaal and the others within the settlement and guide her to them.

In this world, there existed — though extremely rare — a form of magic called replication magic.
She'd never heard of it working on living creatures, but perhaps at the level of the Forbidden Arts, such a possibility existed. Holding back all the things she wanted to ask, Emilia relied on Ryuzu's duplicates, tearing through the Sanctuary in search of Roswaal and Ram.

"I have to hurry... or the Great Rabbit will get here...!"

The Witchbeast known as the "Great Rabbit."
Even Emilia, unworldly as she was, of course knew that name.
One of the Three Great Witchbeasts alongside the White Whale and the Black Serpent, its existence, like the other two, was said to be a calamity in itself.

Each individual was a weak, fragile little rabbit Witchbeast. But the Great Rabbit was a colonial creature that was, at once, an individual and a swarm. Not each single rabbit, but the entire species gathered together, formed the singular calamity called the Great Rabbit.
An inexhaustible appetite and overwhelming numbers devouring everything that stood in its way, still never satisfied, cannibalizing itself as it wandered the world — truly a calamity.

What made it truly terrifying was its mysterious, supposedly infinite capacity to multiply. Ordinarily the Great Rabbit kept its numbers low, cannibalizing itself to stave off starvation when there was no food. But once it found prey to stir its appetite, there was no stopping it. It multiplied without limit, devoured its prey down to extinction, and after reducing a place to scorched earth, dwindled in numbers again and moved on. Such was the creature.

Against that superlative-class Witchbeast, Emilia had resolved that there was nothing to do but stand and fight.
If the Great Rabbit was going to attack the Sanctuary, then the time to flee had already been stolen from them. The piling snow hindered any escape; Emilia and the others had no choice left.

Hide the non-combatants in the tomb, and hold a defensive line before its entrance.
There was no other way to stand against the Witchbeast. Emilia, and Roswaal. Ram too, if possible — every fighting force the Sanctuary had needed to be gathered together.
And so—

"..."

A snapped tree trunk, a building bearing the aftermath of battle. Gouged-out earth, snow piled in unnatural drifts. —And, huddled together in the shadow of a tree, a man and a woman.
Finding Ram sleeping limply and Roswaal sitting in a daze, Emilia cried out.

"—Roswaal! Ram!!"

Leaving the silent Ryuzu duplicate behind, Emilia slid across the frozen snow. Manipulating the soles of her shoes freely, scattering shards of ice as she moved like a fairy of the snow, she grabbed the shoulder of the unmoving Roswaal, half-buried in drifted snow.

"Are you listening!? Roswaal, Roswaal, come on! It's an emergency right now! We have to save everyone! This is no time to sit here frozen!"

"..."

The shaking knocked loose the snow that had piled on Roswaal's head. His expression, hidden until then, came into view, and Emilia made a small sound in her throat.
Because Roswaal's face, gazing at Ram with eyes drained of all will, was far too weak, too fragile.

"Roswaal...?"

"........."

Roswaal said nothing. He gave no sign of even noticing Emilia.
His lack of response frightened her, and Emilia dropped her gaze — not to him, but to what lay in his arms. There, the pink-haired girl lay still,

—and the snow falling onto her cheek showed no sign at all of melting.

"—! Ram? Ram!"

Emilia called to Ram in Roswaal's arms, trying to wake her.
But there was no response worth the name from Ram, eyes still closed. No answer, of course, and far from waking — not even so much as a trembling eyelid came in reply. The cheek she touched, the lips, were abnormally cold. Almost as if—

"There's no way... !"

Denying such a weak thought, Emilia gritted her teeth and slipped her arm inside Ram's clothing. When her hand brushed over her chest, she felt, faintly, a weak heartbeat.
A frail heartbeat, as if it might vanish at any moment.

"—She's alive! It's okay! We're still in time, Roswaal!"

Finding hope, Emilia cried out and turned to Roswaal. But Roswaal, still holding a palm to Ram's forehead, only wore a blank, unfocused expression.
Then Emilia noticed something.
From Roswaal's palm, resting against Ram, a vast quantity of mana was being poured into her. And that this was the lifeline that was, just barely, keeping Ram's life going.

"You're... saving Ram, aren't you..."

"..."

"—!"

The moment she realized that, Emilia also grasped a painful truth alongside it.
Ram was in a critical, unconscious state, and Roswaal had no choice but to devote his delicate healing skill entirely to treating her. There was no way he could lend his strength to a fight.
Which meant Emilia would have to face the coming Great Rabbit alone.

"........."

*—Can I do it?* The question flitted through Emilia's mind.

A monster counted among the Three Great Witchbeasts, one that had lived on for four hundred years, since the age of the Witch of Envy herself.
Surely there had been countless others before her, hardening their resolve just as she had now, who had challenged it. Could Emilia alone stand against a Witchbeast that all of them, together, had never managed to destroy?
With no Puck at her side — Emilia, entirely alone.

"Even now, if..."

Should she choose to flee? But in that case, what if they were caught?
If they encountered it out in the open, with nowhere to shelter and nowhere to hide, Emilia would have no way to protect the residents from the Witchbeast. Defending a place like the tomb offered the best chance.
The loss of Roswaal and Ram's fighting strength stung, but she couldn't give up on resisting.

"Roswaal. Anyway, bring Ram and come with me. Everyone from the Sanctuary is at the tomb... yes, everyone's taken shelter there. I'll definitely protect that place, I promise. So don't give up on treating Ram either, Roswaal..."

"It's already... hopeless."

Lowering herself to meet his eyes, just as Emilia was about to declare her resolve and her firm course of action, Roswaal's hoarse murmur cut her off.
Still with hollow eyes, Roswaal stared fixedly at Ram's face.

"It's hopeless. Everything, now... The future — I don't know it anymore. I don't even know myself anymore. ...This world is finished."

"You're saying that again...! What's a book got to do with anything! What does it matter what's written in some book some maybe-important person wrote, when it comes to what we're going to do!?"

At Roswaal voicing his resignation, Emilia could no longer bear it and raised her voice.
Why was this happening? The Roswaal Emilia knew wasn't like this.
Always playing it composed, tossing out bold decisions without a second thought, grinning with an air of knowing everything. Wasn't that who Roswaal was?
Then who was this — this cornered man with the face of a lost child, this weak man who had thrown everything away?

"Roswaal. I don't know, right now, how you're feeling, or how deeply you're hurting. I want to understand, but I don't have time to learn that right now. ...So I want to make that time. And for that, I need your help."

"..."

She didn't understand Roswaal. And staying like this, she never would.
But there were things you could come to understand by exchanging words, by opening your heart to someone. Things you could never understand any other way. Things that could never be conveyed without taking the time.
She had to make the time needed to bring their hearts closer together.

"So please. Stand up, Roswaal. Neither you nor I can end here, not now. And I won't let Ram end either. Let's go to everyone, together..."

"It's already..."

Emilia pleaded. Still, Roswaal wouldn't meet her eyes.
Still looking down at Ram, he moved his lipstick-painted mouth and said,

"It's enough already..."

It was a hoarse voice, one that seemed as though it might vanish at any moment.
In fact, it was swept away by the cold, driving wind, and barely made a sound at all.
Murmured only within his own mouth, a voice even Roswaal himself might not have heard.

But that small voice of surrender had, without doubt, reached her.
And so, Emilia—

"—Don't you dare say something so selfish!!"

Grabbing Roswaal by the collar, Emilia's voice trembled with anger.
His head jolted from the motion, and Roswaal gave a small groan of pain. Into his face, Emilia kept hurling words like she meant to bite him.

"'Enough already'!? What do you mean, 'enough already'!? Nothing is 'enough already'! Not one single thing is 'enough already'! Don't you dare give up on your own like that! Don't you dare decide by yourself that it's over! For me, for Ram, for you — there can't possibly be a single thing that's 'enough already,' can there!?"

"..."

"I finished the Trial! The past I was too afraid to look at! The happy present that could have been! The sad future that might still come someday! I saw all of it! And even so, I chose — I decided to walk this path, right here, right now... and only because I decided that, I can finally walk it!"

She roared it out.
A surge of fury was rising in her, more intense than Emilia herself could recall ever having felt before.
That's right. Ah, that's right. What a weak voice, what a pathetic notion, what spoiled, coddled resolve. Could you really call it living, a life that could end the moment you gave up?

Roswaal's cheeks stiffened at Emilia's shouting, and he tried to look away — not so much out of concern for Ram, but simply the posture of fleeing, averting his mind from something he didn't want to see.
Emilia caught his chin and forced him to face her.

"When you're talking to someone, you look them properly in the face while you do it!"

"—!"

"You can't know what someone's desperately thinking unless you look them in the eye, can you? You can't make them understand why you want what you want unless you look them in the eye either. So look at me, properly, in the eyes. Listen to my voice. And then stand up, and come with me."

Roswaal's mismatched eyes blinked, as though something had just occurred to him.
His lips trembled faintly. But no sound came from them.

"—Ah."

"I won't let anyone say 'that's enough.' As long as you're alive, there's no such thing as 'enough already.' So — I'm not going to let anyone die here!"

Emilia rose to her feet and turned around.
Without her even having to call, more than ten Ryuzu duplicates had already gathered. Every one of them knelt solemnly on the spot, waiting for the command of a single person.

Drawing a breath, Emilia shouted her command out into the white air.

"Bring Roswaal and Ram, and let's head back to the tomb. I will protect every one of you — that's a promise."

With commanding presence, even knowing full well that the qualification behind it had come to her purely by chance, Emilia led the obedient Ryuzus and set off running through the snowbound Sanctuary.
The Ryuzus supported Roswaal and Ram together as a group, taking turns clearing the way as they followed after Emilia.

—In the feet with which Emilia ran, there was no longer the faintest trace of hesitation.


* * *


And so, the story—

"You don't have to push yourself that hard, Emilia-tan."

To the side of the girl who had resolved to protect everything, to carry every feeling forward, the boy who cherished her returned,

"Leave the rest to me — you can hang back if you want. —After all, I've got that first-battle bonus going for me."

"Sorry. I have no idea what you're talking about."

After their usual exchange, the girl smiled — and yet, bracing her body with sheer will against the urge to collapse, she followed with her eyes the white shape stepping forward.

Two shadows stepped forward, shadows with joined hands.
Two voices could be heard as well.
Those voices carried a certain buoyancy, and somehow it felt to the girl as though she too had been waiting for the moment those voices would overlap — her heart quickened, she couldn't say quite why.

"Well then, I won't be responsible for whatever happens now."

"Yeah, let's make it work out somehow. —You and me, together!!"


And so, the story overlapped once more into one, and their wishes overlapped as well, moving toward the final curtain.


In the snow-blown, blizzard-wracked Sanctuary, a knight and a princess stand facing the Witchbeast.
The knight, insufficient alone, brings a mage to his side, to offer up victory.

—The final battle for the Sanctuary begins.
