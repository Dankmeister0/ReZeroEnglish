Arc 2 Chapter 19: "The Fruits of Reading and Writing"

"From what Ram and Rem said, you'd come straight here, so I was thinking you were taking a bit long."

Laughter finished, at the edge of the garden, Emilia looked at Subaru and said this.
Traces of her laughter still lingered as drops of tears in her eyes, and she scooped them away with a finger as she spoke.
Subaru, having been laughed at uproariously, casually toyed with Puck in his hands.

"That was, well. Yeah, I was trying to stage a reunion with some real impact, so I went to a lot of careful trouble, Mr. Fluffy."

"Uh-huh, but flying out of the terrace and landing in the flowerbed might've been overdoing it a little."

"Sorry, that was force majeure. I'm not that much of a reckless brat, you know."

It would be a problem if she thought he was some troubled kid who dove out windows whenever he got worked up. Getting impaled in the flowerbed every time and then hosed down by Puck's flash flood was not something he wished to repeat, thank you very much.

"By the way, you saying you thought I was taking a while — does that mean you were waiting for me?"

"...N-no, that's not it, okay? I mean, sure, I was thinking I needed to thank you, and I thought it'd be bad if I went looking for you myself and we missed each other, and I did think a little about how it wouldn't be great to make someone who just got over an injury wander around looking for me, but me staying here was more just... a coincidence, kind of."

"Right, just a coincidence, Subaru. She came up with all sorts of excuses to drag out grooming me, and brought up the same topic with the minor spirits over and over till the poor things were worn out... she did all that, but she totally wasn't waiting for you. Right?"

Emilia's self-inflicted blast set off a chain reaction from Puck, who seized the opportunity.
Emilia turned her gaze, still smiling, toward Puck as he kept up his teasing.

"Puck~?"

"You could just be honest, you know. That's the part of my daughter that's so cute... don't you think so too, Subaru?"

"Totally think so! Emilia-tan is the best! I'm already weak in the knees!"

"Now even you're joining in on Puck's teasing... and also, what's with that 'tan'? Where did you even learn that from?"

The question about the nickname was fast becoming a regular part of their exchanges.
Up till now Subaru had gotten away with vague dodges, but this time he teamed up with Puck to push the matter along instead. The two exchanged a look, making eye contact.

"You don't know it? This is the highest honorific to express affection and endearment toward another. You'll understand if you try saying it slowly, savoring it. Yes — like this: Emilia-tan."

"Eh, is that so...? But I've never heard of anything like—"

"Well, it's a word from a very, very old era, all but forgotten now. It's no wonder Lia doesn't know it. Even I took a little while to remember it myself."

With a distant look in his eyes, Puck countered Subaru's fabricated conversation with a fabricated memory of his own. Subaru nodded solemnly at his words.

"It's like some old, moldy fairy tale that's already vanished from history. But its spirit still lives on today. Quietly."

"But there are fewer and fewer people who know of it now... Could it be, Subaru. Are you perhaps connected to that lost kingdom...?"

"Connected... no, more like a party directly involved, you might say. Haven't you heard? The royal family of that lost kingdom bore black hair and black eyes, a bloodline that..."

"You two are smiling way too much for this!"

"Gyaaaah!!"

Just as things were heating up and the conversation was getting fun, a chunk of ice came flying from Emilia's direction, forcibly shutting them down.
It was a block of ice about the size of a sugar cube, but it flew in such volume as to fill his whole field of vision and plaster itself all over his face — a rather passive-aggressive sort of retort.
Once stuck, the ice would hurt his skin if he tried to peel it off by force, so there was nothing to do but leave it until his body heat melted it. And having his whole face lose heat like that actually hurt quite a bit.

"It stings in this subtle, awful way! It's subtly brutal! Right in the weak spot! Super effective!"

Rolling around on the ground to raise his core body temperature and speed up the melting, Subaru writhed across the lawn. Watching him from above, Emilia let out a troubled sigh.

"Honestly, you always joke around like this right away... you won't even let me say a proper thank you. That's just not fair."

"This suffering right now is pretty genuinely real, though. Ah, if I peel it off wrong it'll bleed, it'll actually bleed."

Emilia didn't seem to quite grasp the seriousness of the situation, while Puck watched Subaru's undignified display with obvious enjoyment. In the first place, why was it that Puck — who should've been his accomplice — wasn't taking any damage at all?

"Well, you see, I can parry magic on Lia's level with one paw, easy."

"Oh-ho."

"I just had whoever was standing next to me take the full brunt of every attack I parried."

"So that's why I got this excessive damage?! You're seriously awful underneath that cute face!"

Having rubbed his face and finally melted all the ice off, Subaru glared and shouted, while Puck said "aww, well" and rubbed his own head with an embarrassed grin.
Deciding that engaging further would just tire him out, Subaru let it go and happened to turn toward the manor, when—

"Oh, Ram and Rem are..."

"They really are. I wonder why, is it breakfast time already?"

It was probably rare for the two of them to set foot in the garden in the middle of their routine duties. Watching Emilia tilt her head in puzzlement, Subaru inwardly nodded in understanding — "ah."
At this time on the first day, it would be right around when Roswaal returned. The twins had likely come for that very summons.

The twins approached the group in front of Subaru, and together, in unison, bowed politely.

"'Master Roswaal has returned. Please come to the manor.'"

Stereo audio that never failed to impress no matter how many times he heard it.
Emilia shrugged, resigned, at their words, and Subaru rose to his feet, stretching and bending to check his condition before turning to face the twins.
The twins, in turn, looked Subaru up and down.

"Sister, sister. In the short time we haven't seen him, our guest has become a drowned rat covered in mud."
"Rem, Rem. In the short time we haven't seen him, our guest has become a filthy rag covered in grime."

"You don't have to tell me, I already know. Where's my jersey?"

Responding to their scathing remarks, Subaru looked up at the manor's silhouette.
Change clothes, tidy himself up, and then face Roswaal properly once more.

—Because this time, he intended to try a different approach than before.

After greeting the returned Roswaal in the manor's entrance hall and getting through their third "first meeting," being shown to the dining hall for breakfast followed the same flow as the second time around.
For Subaru, now on his third go, it was a development that felt almost like familiar routine. As for why this sequence kept repeating itself, Subaru suspected it was probably due to some strong will on Roswaal's part intervening.

On the very first day he'd been summoned to this other world — the day he met Emilia, that is — even across those four repeated days, events that could occur due to strong will almost always repeated without fail.
Felt's theft of Emilia's insignia was one such case, and so was the harebrained scheme in the back alley to strip Subaru of everything he owned.
There were small variations scattered in between (individual whims, or Subaru's own actions), but the fact that the broad flow never strayed was likely due to such intentions.

So it followed that the topic at this first breakfast would inevitably turn to Emilia's insignia, and from there eventually shift to the matter of her being a candidate for queen, and then to a reward for Subaru's accomplishments.
Though he couldn't deny he'd steered the conversation there a little on purpose.

"So, well, that makes me Lady Emilia's patron. Which means, if you want to keep the information about the insignia theft hidden, I'll let you pay me a corresponding price for it, o-o-okay?"

"A price, huh..."

Steering the conversation smoothly there, Subaru closed his eyes as if searching for a landing point.
The first time, and the time before that, the reward here had settled into the form of "being hired on as a servant." Last time, that had been done with the goal of following the first loop's precedent — but this time, Subaru had absolutely no intention of tracing the previous loop as-is.
So the reward Subaru would ask of Roswaal here would be something different.
Namely—

"Well then, Roswaal old pal. Just let me stay at the manor for two or three days. After that I'll be heading somewhere else, so it'd help if you could spare a little travel money."

"Hmm, and by 'a little,' you mean... enough to build two or three houses, perhaps?"

Reading too much into Subaru's request, Roswaal narrowed his eyes and said this. Subaru waved a hand and gave a wry smile in response.

"No hidden agenda like that, seriously. Just, plainly, enough that I won't have trouble staying at inns and eating meals for about ten days. After that I'll manage on my own."

"Just as Lady Emilia said, my, my... how unambitious a request."

"It's fine. I'm not asking for anything that outrageous... ah, unless Emilia-tan says she can't bear to part with me, in which case, that's a different story!?"

"I do think it's admirably modest of you... but I'm sure Subaru has his reasons too. It must be something important, so it wouldn't be right of me to try to stop you."

"Cast aside via a charitable interpretation!"

Subaru had no idea what kind of idol he'd become in Emilia's mind, but the negotiation ended with a reply that seemed to peg him, absurdly, as some kind of stand-up guy.
Watching Subaru clutch his head, Roswaal straightened the collar of his kabuki-esque attire.

"Well, if you insist that much, I suppose it can't be helped, I'll grant it. I'll ha-a-ave Ram and Rem prepare things — the stay was three days, correct? Is there anything else you need, while we're at it?"

"Right, yeah. In that case... lend me some paper, a quill, and a storybook."

Roswaal raised an eyebrow slightly at Subaru's request.
Subaru scratched his head at him, catching the twins in the corner of his eye.

"I made a promise to keep practicing reading and writing every night. I may not look it, but I'm the type who keeps his promises, y'know?"

He winked and signaled the twins. The twins exchanged glances with each other, blinking in bewilderment at this one-sided promise being sprung on them.

—Effectively, this was the third week spent at Roswaal Manor.

In this, the third iteration of the repeat, what Subaru prioritized above all else was pinning down the cause of his death and the identity of the assailant who'd carried it out.

"Keywords are magic and chains... but that's still nowhere near enough to go on."

What he knew for certain was just this one fact: there would be an attack by someone in the dead of night on the fourth day.
If he brought this to Roswaal now, he might get some measure of countermeasures in place, but with Subaru unable to explain where the information came from, it would be a considerable gamble. If he could at least learn the assailant's appearance and weapon, that alone would make a huge difference.

"So, I'll accept it this time. This time, I'm betting everything on gathering information. If the conditions for 'Return by Death' are the same as before..."

At the very least, he should still have one more chance left to try again.
That was the best plan Subaru had managed to come up with at this stage.

Regarding the previous loop, three deaths and a breakthrough on the fourth attempt had been confirmed. Taking that as a given, he should always be able to come back at least once more.
And then, using the information gained in this loop, he would aim to break through in the fourth iteration of the world.

"Honestly, it feels like a strategy I don't want to pick, since it's basically giving up from the start..."

Given how limited Subaru's available means were, this kind of sacrifice was, without question, a necessary cost. And besides, he had not the slightest intention of just passively watching the situation play out.
Even having steeled himself to try again, a situation where he wouldn't need to try again was still infinitely preferable. Which was exactly why, if he could clear the loop this time around, he fully intended to do so.

"I've already given Puck a heads-up, sort of, to protect Emilia-tan."

After their exchange in the garden, Subaru had pulled Puck aside, out of Emilia's sight, and issued a warning.
He hadn't been able to get into specifics, but he'd asked Puck to keep an eye on her safety at night. Puck had tilted his head at the lack of concrete details, but the cat who could read emotions had probably judged that Subaru's seriousness wasn't feigned.

"I'm not convinced, but it does seem to be true that you're worried about Lia."

And with that, Puck had accepted Subaru's forceful way of framing things with magnanimous grace.
He could consider Emilia's safety reasonably secured for now, at least. In their short acquaintance, Subaru had learned that Puck wasn't the type of cat to break a promise. It was a promise made at the cost of giving up his precious petting privileges, no less. A cat like that wouldn't treat it lightly.

Relieved that Puck hadn't pressed him further, Subaru had felt a weight lift off his shoulders because of it. Why he'd felt relieved that he hadn't been pressed further, though, was something that hadn't even occurred to him to think about, at the time.

"Now it's just that loli, and Roswaal, sort of, subtly... subtly how, though? That's not something you ask of someone with low social skills."

He roughly scratched and mussed his hair, tucked the quill under his nose, and straightened his back.

Just thinking about the sheer number of problems waiting for him made his head hurt.
Even granting that this time was a "no loss if it fails" attempt, there was no way choosing to treat the girls as expendable stones wouldn't come with pain attached. If at all possible, he wanted both Ram and Rem to make it past the fourth day safely too. Roswaal and Beatrice, naturally, belonged on that list as well.
Even if the summit didn't look all that high from here, that was no reason not to make the climb.

"Now then, what to do about..."

"Excuse me, guest."

Leaning his weight back against the chair, making it creak, a voice came from outside just then. Before he could even answer, the door opened and revealed the figure of the elder of the vividly peach-haired maid sisters — Ram.

She held a tray with tea on it, and upon seeing Subaru at the desk, she knit her brows.

"Oh my, so you really are studying, guest."

"You know, that's incredibly rude. I am, at least nominally, currently a guest of this manor, you know."

"A guest in name, a freeloader in fact. That is how Ram understands it, guest."

Utterly unrepentant, Ram pushed her way into the room, set the tray on the desk, and held out the steaming tea with a "here you are."
The tea in this world resembled the "black tea" of his original world in both appearance and taste. To be blunt, Subaru wasn't fond of tea-leaf-based drinks in general, but he'd more or less resigned himself to the fact that hoping for cola or some other soft drink in this world was a pointless wish.

Besides, his time spent living at Roswaal Manor now added up to about ten days in total.
Even though his position had shifted between injured guest, visitor, and servant, the taste of the tea served to him during that time hadn't changed one bit. It was proof — one only Subaru, having repeated all this, could perceive — that Ram, for all her curtness, wasn't slacking off.

So today too, Subaru brought the offered tea to his mouth and sipped it slowly, savoring it.

"Yeah... it's terrible, as always."

"That's a rather sacrilegious comment about the finest tea leaves served in this manor."

"Bitter is bitter. No good, it just tastes like leaves to me, no matter what. And drinking it with someone like you across the table, I've got no reason to put on airs about it. Tastes like a plant."

"That's rather familiar of you, guest."

As she spoke, Ram picked up, as though it were the most natural thing in the world, a second cup that had for some reason also been on the tray, and sat down without hesitation on the bed behind the desk, raising it to her lips.
Wasn't this just slacking off disguised as serving? Subaru glared at her with narrowed eyes.

"Blatantly slacking off in front of a guest — I've got no words for your sheer nerve."

"Don't mind Ram, guest. Ram doesn't particularly want anything from you, guest. It's just that using tea as a pretext lets her kill some time."

"While her little sister's working hard the whole time, big sister's living the good life, huh."

"Not as much as the guest who's the reason that little sister's workload increased in the first place."

Oh-ho-ho, they each raised a hand to their mouth, exchanging a expressionless little laugh of mutual checkmating.
Trading barbs with her like this, Subaru couldn't help but feel something didn't quite sit right.

Since this third loop had begun, things had already progressed into the night of the second day.
During that time, aside from the hours spent chasing after Emilia, Subaru had spent nearly all his time shut up in his assigned guest room, focused on learning his letters. Though he had made sure to properly set aside the occasional bit of time to go tease Beatrice.

In other words, in this loop, he hadn't interacted with Ram or Rem beyond the roles of guest and servant. Their acquaintance ought to be even thinner than in the first loop, let alone the second.
And yet, here was Ram, making excuses to keep coming to Subaru's guest room like this. The tea-serving was one thing, but she also dropped by from time to time just to check on him, interjecting as if to verify the fruits of his studying, arguing with him a bit, and then leaving the room. Over and over.

If anything, Ram's attitude here felt even more familiar than it had in the first or second loop.
That was gratifying in its own way, but for Subaru it was also a relationship that gave rise to a pang of conscience.
Enough so that, for Subaru — who had already, in his mind, more or less resolved to treat this loop as a mere waypoint — it made him regret the thought of simply passing this world by, letting it go unseen.

"I don't get it... maybe it's that. This dignified, quietly-devoted-to-my-letters, so-called literary-young-man vibe I've got going on from behind — did my back view make some maiden's heart go pitter-patter?"

"A literary young man..."

Ram reacted to Subaru's mutter. Still seated on the bed, she looked him over, scanning Subaru up and down as if licking him with her gaze, and then—

"Ha."

"You do that sometimes, and it seriously feels awful, you know."

Sneered at with an utterly condescending expression, Subaru's cheek twitched despite himself.
Before him, still with the same unchanged demeanor, Ram shrugged.

"That obviously sinewy, muscular body of yours, those eyes that are less sharp than simply mean-looking, and a spirit about as far from delicate as it gets — and you call yourself a literary young man. Perhaps this 'literary young man' exists nowhere but inside the guest's own head."

"You're going that far?! I mean, sure, back home, Natsuki-san's boy Subaru-kun was famous in the neighborhood for looking every bit the athlete but actually being a total shut-in, but still!"

Every time someone said as much, his mother would come report it to him at his bedroom door, barely holding back her laughter. Remembering the humiliation of those times made him want to roll around on the spot right then and there.

Face red, fighting off the urge, Subaru struggled. Ram rose from the bed, slipped past him, and glanced at the notebook on the desk.

"And then there's this filthy handwriting. — 'Literary young man' would laugh to hear itself described so, guest."

"I've never met a maid who says 'guest' as flatly as you do... even a maid at a maid café would put on more charm than that."

He'd never actually been to one, so it was only secondhand knowledge, but apparently the hospitality there was something else entirely. Not that he'd know, never having gone.

Subaru's resentful remark was breezily ignored, and Ram flipped through the pages filled with writing, her expression utterly uninterested.
Glaring at her unmoving profile, Subaru suddenly picked up the storybook that had been left untouched all this while.

"Speaking of which, this book, written mostly in 'I-script' — it's probably about time I could read it, huh."

On the reddish-brown spine, in this world's script, a title reading something simply like "Collected Fairy Tales" was printed. The book's construction itself closely resembled that of his original world — first a table of contents and page numbers, followed by the stories arranged chapter by chapter.
At a glance at the table of contents, this book contained only about ten or so tales. The book wasn't especially thick either; if he sat down to read it, an hour or so would easily suffice.

"In other words, the time has come. About time I felt the fruits of my studying, too."

"They're all the sort of commonsense tales that would embarrass you not to know. As a self-proclaimed 'literary young man,' these are things you should have down cold, naturally. Right, guest?"

"Fine, fine, quit dragging it out. What, did it bug you that much that I acted all literary-young-man? You got some kind of intense fetish for literary young men or something?"

Without dignifying Subaru's harassment-adjacent question with a response, Ram tilted the cup still remaining on the desk and drank it down. That happened to be Subaru's half-finished tea.

"Hey now, I've never heard of a servant drinking all the tea she brought."

"You're going to make that awful face while drinking it anyway, so what's the difference? Besides, tea deserves someone with a proper palate to appreciate it, don't you think."

"That's why I told you it tastes like leaves... ah, whatever. I'm going to bury myself in this book, so go back or kill time however you like, your call."

Waving a hand dismissively to shoo Ram away, Subaru settled his weight into the chair and, at long last, opened the storybook to the first page.
Right at the start, there was an author's preface compiling this collection of fairy tales, but he immediately stumbled — it was suddenly riddled with characters that weren't in 'I-script.'

"All I can make out of this is something like 'blah-blah does blah-blah for the sake of blah-blah'... damn it, I could probably just skip it, but it bugs me."

What if he hit an unreadable character right at a good part of a story?
Worried about that, Subaru closed one eye and flipped lightly through the book, skimming ahead while trying to avoid spoilers.
Having confirmed at a glance that the main text didn't seem to contain anything but 'I-script,' he turned to tackle the book once more.

"Okay, let's see... 'once upon a time'—"

So every world's fairy tales started that way too, he thought, as he read on through the story.
Being fairy tales, the narrative arcs were remarkably clear and concise. And precisely because of that, there was plenty of room left for imagination — no different from the many fairy tales of his original world.

"They've even got that subtle moralizing-to-children flavor going on, same as back home. There's even one that's basically a dead ringer for 'The Crying Red Ogre.'"

If asked which fairy tale he liked best, Subaru would put "The Crying Red Ogre" at the top of the list. And if asked which fairy tale he disliked most, Subaru would, just the same, put "The Crying Red Ogre" at the top of that list too.
A story he loved and hated in equal measure, "The Crying Red Ogre." He fondly recalled how, back in kindergarten, he'd added his own original ending to every single copy of the picture book in the whole school.

"The blue ogre deserves better, damn it! Isn't it just so lonely, huh?"

"I have no idea what's going on, being suddenly told that with tears in your eyes. It'll look bad, so please don't cry in front of Ram."

"How cold, toward a boy's tears!"

Cut off flatly, Subaru rubbed his eyelids to evaporate the tears. Then, with Ram still in the room, he gave the cover of the finished tale a light tap.

"Finished it. Surprisingly good, actually. There's something to enjoy in the subtle differences in common sense, you know — like, exactly like a cross-cultural exchange? Maybe I should study art here and spread their stories back home. Think copyright follows you across worlds?"

"I didn't understand about half of what the guest just said. ...So, that's your impression after finishing it? Was there nothing that left an impression on you?"

Plagiarism's no good, huh, Subaru mused, his creative conscience wavering, when the question came at him. He looked up, flipped through the storybook, and answered.

"What caught my attention was, well, that dragon story right in the middle, and the witch story at the very end. No matter how you look at it, those two are clearly treated as being in a different category from the rest."

Of everything in the collection, those two tales had left the strongest impression on Subaru. One was treated as clearly a cut above the rest, and the other was, if anything, more like—

"The witch story, I dunno, it's got this half-hearted, 'we couldn't NOT include it so we tossed it in' kind of vibe. It totally ignores narrative structure and everything, just a bare outline."

"...The witch tale can't be helped. As for the dragon tale being treated specially, that's only natural, given that this is Lugunica."

"Ah, the 'Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica,' right? I finally get where the name comes from."

Setting the storybook on the desk, Subaru rested a hand on its cover and nodded.
The great nation Subaru was apparently currently staying in, called the "Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica." Looking at a world map, this country, positioned at the very eastern edge of the world, had good reason to be called the "Dragon Kingdom."

It was simple, really. This nation had, since long ago, been aided in its prosperity by a Covenant sealed with a Dragon.

"Famine, plague. And wars with other nations — the Dragon is said to have lent its power in all manner of crises like these, to protect Lugunica."

"And so the name they landed on was the 'Dragon Kingdom,' quite the grand claim. According to the fairy tale, it says something about a Covenant between the royal family and the Dragon, so this is more like a legend than a mere fairy tale..."

"That's right, because it's true. Even now, the noble Dragon watches over this nation's peace from far, far away — beyond the Great Waterfall. Until the day the promise exchanged with the royal family is fulfilled."

Subaru listened, swallowing hard, as Ram solemnly declared this.
A promise exchanged with the Dragon long, long ago. — The fairy tale didn't describe its contents, but it was a promise that had saved the kingdom from crisis time and time again.
Something of corresponding weight must have been exchanged between man and dragon to seal it.

Thinking that far, Subaru suddenly realized something.
If it was the royal family of the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica that the Dragon had made its Covenant with, then—

"Wait, hold on, didn't the family that made the promise with the Dragon... just die out recently?"

"That's right. Rather abruptly, too."

"Isn't that bad news? I mean, I have no idea what exactly would be bad about it, but..."

A dragon that had done this much for them, in keeping to a promise — the price for that must have been considerable too. And yet, if the royal family who ought to be paying that price had been wiped out entirely, it wouldn't be strange at all for the dragon to fly into a rage demanding, "what about all my back wages, then?"

"No, no, negative thinking's no good, me. If anything, let's push the opposite theory here — that the royal family already finished paying the Dragon's wages in advance. That way the kingdom's safe, the Dragon's got no problem, and we all get to celebrate together."

Rhyming as he tried to talk himself out of his own anxiety, Subaru wanted Ram to deny it. But Ram rejected his plea with a single shake of her head.

"What the Dragon wants is, as the fairy tale says, unknown. How the Dragon will act given the current situation is something only the gods—"

She stopped there, said "no" and caught her breath once, then continued.

"—only the Dragon knows, that would be the phrase, guest."

Subaru held his breath, feeling sweat trickle down his forehead despite it not being hot at all.
Chewing over Ram's words, swallowing them, letting them churn in his stomach, at last he managed—

"Then the pressure riding on Emilia-tan is just unreal, huh."

"Indeed. Bearing an entire nation, carrying its fate on her two shoulders, negotiating with a dragon who could preserve or destroy this country on a whim — just thinking about it, it's practically a tale fit for this very collection."

Hadn't it been the last night of the second loop when Emilia had worn a complicated expression looking at this storybook? Only after dying did Subaru come to understand why her hand, mid-page-turn, had stopped.
The magnitude and weight of what she carried far exceeded anything Subaru had imagined. Just thinking about how much weight rested on those slender shoulders of hers, about the burden on her heart, made something rise in Subaru's chest — a scream that was neither anger nor grief, but both at once.

"It can't be helped."

"—Huh?"

"Everyone is born with certain innate qualities, and with them, a corresponding responsibility. Lady Emilia was born under such a star. That's why, no matter how steep the path may be, Lady Emilia must endure the pain and climb it."

"By making one girl carry it all alone?"

"I do think it's fine if there are people who help carry the load together with her. But at the summit she eventually reaches, it must, without fail, be her figure standing there."

Anger of unclear origin made Subaru's voice tremble. And in answer, Ram's voice was cold and rational.
Sensing in it a deliberate, conscious coldness, Subaru's flaring temper suddenly regained a shred of composure.
To begin with, there was no point getting angry at her over this. Ram bore no responsibility for any of it — she had merely put into words her resolve to accept it, unconditionally.

"Right, Ram. There's one more thing I wanted to talk about..."

Apologizing felt somehow wrong too, so Subaru pointed at the storybook to change the subject.
In the middle of the book, clearly treated as a cut above the rest, was the dragon tale; and as if forming a pair with it, at the very end, described in just a few short pages, was that other story.
Its title was given as "The Jealous Witch." But—

"This witch story, though..."

"I don't want to talk about that story."

She cut him off flatly, even more sharply than she had with the dragon story.
Before Subaru, who couldn't help widening his eyes in surprise, Ram briskly tidied up the cup and tray.

"I've stayed too long. I can't put too much of a burden on Rem, so I'll be heading back now. Guest, I'll come call for you again at dinnertime."

"Ah, y-yeah..."

"Very well, then."

Turning her back with an air that brooked no argument, her small frame left the room. Left to see her off in silence, Subaru watched until the door was fully shut, then let out a breath and, all the strength draining from his body, collapsed onto the bed.

"What was that about, that attitude..."

Muttering that, Subaru lifted the storybook up in front of his face and flipped through its pages.
The final story, "The Jealous Witch," was a short tale of just four pages.

"The fearsome witch, the terrible witch. So fearsome that even to speak her name was frightening. Everyone called her thus: 'The Jealous Witch'..."

With no narrative arc to speak of, the content did nothing but relentlessly convey the sheer terror of the witch. Precisely because it was written in characters simple enough for a child to read, the eeriness came through all the more starkly and plainly.

"After all that studying just so I could finally read this book..."

Whatever sense of accomplishment, satisfaction, or even a pleasant lingering afterglow he might have had, it all felt ruined in one fell swoop.

Subaru rolled over on the bed, setting the contents of the fairy tale aside from his thoughts for the moment. Instead he turned his mind to the trial and error of this loop, with only two days left remaining.
Turning things this way and that in his head, wringing out whatever wits he had, before long Subaru's consciousness melted away, dissolving into the soft embrace of the bed and sheets, and slipped into nothing.
