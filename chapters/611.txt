Arc 7 Chapter 106: "Chaos at the Summit (Part Two)"

When Berstetz Fondalfon, noticing the disturbance, returned to the throne room, the doors to the most venerated chamber in the Crystal Palace stood firmly shut.

That "shut" did not mean a simple opening and closing of the doors.
In this case, "shut" meant, literally, the doors' complete isolation from the outside world. Undeniably, it expressed the will of the palace's master, one who would allow no outsider entry.
Except—

"—Tell me, in this case, whose will do you suppose the master's will belongs to? Which do you think it is, my dear Chancellor?"

Standing before the closed doors, the effete man spread both arms in a clownish gesture, and Berstetz's thread-thin eyes narrowed further still as he came to a halt.
The man lingering there with a faint smile was one granted, by virtue of his nature as a "Stargazer," a peculiar exemption allowing him free passage in and out of the Crystal Palace—an unaccountable presence who declared himself neither friend nor foe.

"Lord Ubilk, is the throne room presently occupied by—"

"Oh, I'm not one for cruelty, so I'll give you the truth of it. His Imperial Majesty is within. The genuine article and the counterfeit, both present, having it out face to face, as it were."

"...I can't say that sits well with me."

It was an answer he'd more or less anticipated, but hearing it spoken aloud all the same, Berstetz brought a hand to his chin.
As he'd said—it didn't sit well—there was no mistaking that the situation was not one easily swallowed. At Berstetz's reaction, Ubilk tilted his head. "Doesn't sit well?"

"What is it you find so hard to accept? The method by which you brought the genuine article here? If so, well, I was guided by the whispers of the stars, you see..."

"Even upon a battlefield, you can choose a path where no arrow or bullet falls. You can pick your way through even as soldiers clash blades at your very side, treading somewhere untouched by so much as a spray of blood—was that not the claim?"

"Yes, that's exactly it. Though that's not all there is to it."

Grinning loosely, Ubilk nodded without the slightest attempt at concealment.
It was an absurd tale, but Berstetz had confirmed Ubilk's abnormality with his own eyes before now.
Ubilk had, quite literally, once strolled leisurely through a hail of blades and a downpour of arrows without so much as a scratch. He claimed to have simply followed the whispers of the stars, but whether that was truth, or merely a lie told by a man concealing superhuman combat prowess, Berstetz could not discern.

If there was one thing that could be said with certainty, it was that whether it was the whispers of the stars or Ubilk's own ability, some power beyond human comprehension surrounded him.
And precisely because that power was useful, neither the genuine Vincent Vollachia nor the false one had ever been willing to let Ubilk go.
All of it—

"—as a guide, meant to ward off the coming 'Great Disaster.'"

"Oh-ho, and not for my sterling character?"

"Those summoned to the Crystal Palace in appreciation of their character—that would be Marshal Goz, and Marshal Goz alone, I should think. Everyone else was brought here for their capability. I myself am no exception."

Personal attachment, from the standpoint of running a nation, was nothing more than the buzz of an insect's wing—beneath notice.
Such was Berstetz's belief, and he could say with confidence that it held equally true for either Vincent Vollachia.
It was not a matter of good or bad, of like or dislike, but a question to be discussed purely in terms of necessity.

On that score, Berstetz himself was, in the end, merely a gear needed for the present moment—and should he ever become a position not worth keeping, unnecessary and unneeded, he would feel no resistance whatsoever to being removed.
Ubilk too, whatever his resolve, surely understood that the role demanded of him did not extend beyond that.

"Is it not precisely because you recognize that, that you turned a blind eye to the plot between His Majesty now seated upon the throne, and myself?"

"You don't suppose my actions amount to betrayal, do you? Tha-at would be a stretch. After all, to betray someone, they have to trust you first. Do you trust me?"

"No. Not in the slightest."

"I thought as much. Still stings to hear it, though."

Pressing a hand to his forehead, Ubilk spoke of being wounded while looking thoroughly amused. Whether that was composure or something else entirely, Berstetz had never once seen the man's expression crack.
Until now, he hadn't found that quite so displeasing—but in this moment, for the first time, he found it an eyesore.
Now that the genuine Vincent Vollachia—the one driven from the Crystal Palace, judged to have forfeited his qualifications as Emperor—had been brought beyond that door, dragged into a decisive confrontation.

"Allow me to answer one of your questions, Chancellor... I haven't changed my position, you know."

"—Your position, meaning?"

Berstetz pressed him, demanding to know which he meant to claim—friend or foe. At that, Ubilk pressed his palms together before his chest, producing a sharp clap of air, and said:

"Why, as one who wishes to repel the 'Great Disaster,' and to see the Vollachia Empire endure in peace, of course."

"...And to that end, this confrontation beyond the doors is necessary?"

"Yes, yes, exactly so. I'm doing all of this for that very purpose. —This beating heart, these lungs swelling and shrinking with every breath, this blood coursing up and down through me, all of it, everything."

"——"

Ubilk pressed his clasped hands to his own chest as he continued, and Berstetz fell silent.
The unchanging smile, the unwavering demeanor—and yet somewhere in Ubilk's gaze there burned something close to desperate intensity, which struck Berstetz's eyes as sane, and in earnest.
Whether that sanity and earnestness were not simply a glimpse past the far side of madness, however, was another matter entirely.

"—Your Majesty. What will you do?"

Picturing the two Emperors surely facing one another beyond the great doors Ubilk stood guarding—the very man he himself had driven into exile—Berstetz murmured.
Should this neck be struck from his shoulders, should his soul be seared to ash, should he be made to taste whatever cruel execution awaited, Berstetz would not mind in the least.

If Vincent Vollachia—he who stood among the wisest emperors in the Empire's history—truly meant to become Emperor in earnest, then he did not mind at all.
And so—

"A man loose of hip and loose of tongue. A 'Stargazer' who's entrusted his loyalty to the heavens is not something to be relied upon."

"I never expected loyalty from that one to begin with. If we filled our ranks by measuring devotion alone, maintaining Vollachia as it has stood to this day would be impossible. Though, mind you—"

"—if my fall came of failing to question the merits and faults of a hidden ambition, then it stands to reason that my treading these palace floors again would grow all the more distant."

Treading the blood-red carpet, arms folded, Abel fixed his gaze on the man before him and posed his question.
There was no room for debate as to whose hand had made Abel's presence in this place possible. That man, who had honed the nature of being an anomaly to its very extreme and poured heart and blood into realizing the will of the "Observers," had no equal when it came to walking the world beyond the board.

A kind of projectile weapon. Though one that could not be pried from its post unless certain conditions were met.
That projectile weapon, capable of cutting both ways once set in motion, and pinpointing the conditions needed to pry it loose from its post, had been a task of the utmost difficulty. And yet, it had been accomplished.

The proof of it lay in the fact that he now stood once more in the throne room from which he had once been driven out.
It would not be an exaggeration to say that all the scheming and quiet endurance up to now had existed solely to seize this opportunity.

"——"

Seated upon the throne he gazed upon, the man receiving his question wore a face Abel had seen countless times.
His own face. Though to call it merely "familiar" would fall short of the truth entirely.
A face that, to anyone else, would surely appear to be Vincent Vollachia himself—but to Abel, who had known for years the man who'd mastered the art of wearing that face, it looked like nothing more than a poorly made mask.

But even a poor mask is still a mask.
A mask worn covers the true face, serving to keep one's true feelings hidden in shadow. And so Abel did not rely on his gaze, but placed his question upon his words instead.
A question, moreover, that could not be dodged or deflected—one that struck straight and true.

"—Did allying with Berstetz and driving me into exile grant you what you wished for?"

The question Abel voiced was of the sort that, to anyone else who heard it, might well have provoked fury.
The exile drama that had begun in this very chamber of the Crystal Palace had already sent ripples across the entire Empire, and even now, at the walls enclosing the Imperial Capital, Imperial soldiers and rebels clashed, lives continuing to scatter and fall.
The people living within the capital, too, had staked their very lives on the outcome of that battle.

Amid all that, Abel's question was of a kind that could scarcely escape censure for its leisureliness.
And yet Abel spoke it. That the rebel who despised all waste, who had spun countless webs of scheme and strategy to arrive at this point, would speak such a thing—it was because it was necessary.
In order to determine what he—no, what the genuine Vincent Vollachia—ought to demand, in the dialogue yet to come with the false Vincent Vollachia.
And then, after a pause too long for hesitation, yet too short for deliberation—

"—No, not yet. I have not yet obtained the result I seek."

In a voice identical to the very tone in which the question had been asked, came the false Emperor's answer to the true one.

"——"

To that answer, Abel too required a moment's pause.
Sandwiching in something that could be called neither hesitation nor deliberation, Abel allowed himself the space of a single breath.
And then—

"Not yet obtained what you seek, is it."

So he murmured, closing both eyes. —Defying a habit born into him.
Abel never closed both eyes at once. He had to keep at least one eye open at all times; for an Emperor who ruled over an Empire that could not survive so much as a single blink of inattention, anything less would be woefully insufficient preparation.

For Abel, who through training and self-discipline could keep one eye open even in sleep, half his consciousness ever alert, the arrival of a darkness in which both eyes closed was not something that had happened in years.
The act of doing so, and the very fact that he was able to do it, Abel made into a declaration of his own intent.
Namely—

"Deception."

From the moment he had stepped into this throne room, neither anger nor disappointment had colored Abel's voice or gaze. That held true even now, facing the very man who had betrayed him, stabbed him in the back as surely as any blade. A self-restraint like tempered steel had made it so.
Into that voice of Abel's, thoroughly stripped of emotion, color now mixed in for the first time.

The color of contempt, no longer concealed, directed at the man wearing a face meant to be his own.

"——"

At those words, the false Emperor warming the throne kept his silence.
Kept it, yes. If what that silence guarded had been some trifling pride, there might still have been some hope of redemption.

"You drove me from the throne, disposed of Goz once he learned of it, schemed to preempt and crush my strategies after my flight, and played your part in the destruction of the Magic City besides. The spreading spark has blazed across the entire realm, and at last granted the rebels' treasonous ambitions crude, unrefined entry into the forbidden ground that is the Imperial Capital itself."

"And if I had remained the one on the throne, you suppose none of that would have come to pass?"

"Had I not stepped down from the throne to begin with, this present tableau would never have been drawn. As it stands, the great conflagration you invited has scorched the Empire. However—"

There, Abel cut himself off for a moment, reaching a hand toward the demon mask that covered his own face.
And then—

"—There is, even now, a means to douse that fire."

As he spoke, he peeled the mask clinging to his face away, baring his true face to the outside air, and to the gaze of the man before him.
The countenance looking back at him, identical down to the last detail—between two such faces, the two Emperors stood face to face. The genuine and the counterfeit, mirror images whose difference no outsider could ever discern.

"——"

A perceptive man. Abel's actions and words, their intent, had surely been conveyed with perfect clarity.
Having come this far, he understood well enough both his own disadvantage and the difficulty of seeing his scheme through to completion. This was the moment when the surging waves of reason, against which no struggle could avail, would sweep away the strategy he had built.
If the obstacle they both faced, the "Great Disaster" they both had to resist, was one and the same, then that too was only natural.
And so—

"I shall—"

He meant to declare his decision to return to the place he belonged.
He meant to issue an edict none could resist, and bring this foolish, misbegotten conflict to its close.
It was in that very instant—

"—Your Majesty."

That single word halted the words on Abel's tongue.
A word that should never have been spoken, in that form, in that tone. A foolish utterance that betrayed a complete loss of awareness of one's station, a debasement of oneself before someone beneath—unthinkable.
The moment it registered, Abel's words caught, stalled for one beat.

That, perhaps, was the second time in this Crystal Palace—the second fatal moment in which Abel, no, Vincent Vollachia, found his intentions betrayed.
The first had been his exile from the throne. And now, this second time—

"——"

Slipping into that single beat's gap, the false Emperor rose from the throne.
Lifting his heavy frame, the height difference that already looked down upon Abel widened by the barest margin further still. But that impression vanished in an instant, rendered meaningless.
Because—

"—Your one oversight was in surveying the board from too great a height."

Even as he spoke, the figure closed the distance in a single breath, bearing down upon Abel until he was right before his eyes.

—In the Crystal Palace of the Imperial Capital Lupugana, the true and false Emperors closed the distance to where their very breaths mingled.

In that same instant, changes erupted simultaneously across every corner of the Battle for the Imperial Capital.
Each arose from a different will, a different conviction—but on one point, every one of these scenes shared something in common.
Not a single one of these changes, in any of these scenes, was something to be welcomed.


"—El Fura."

Swinging the staff in her hand, Ram conjured wind into the parched, taut air of the battlefield.
Ordinarily it was magic honed to slit an opponent's throat with minimal effort and precise accuracy. But Ram had come to realize that against the enemy on this battlefield, it would not serve as an effective blow.

Massed together, barring the way, were stone-bodied puppets that showed no sign of possessing life.
They seemed to lack anything resembling self-awareness, mechanically meeting whatever approached them, and though humanoid in shape, they possessed nothing resembling a vital point on a human body.
Sever the head, cut off the limbs—whatever remained would simply take up the severed parts as weapons and lunge at the enemy still.

And so, the tactics Ram excelled at bore no fruit here.
And yet, she was not so sweet a maiden as to throw up her hands and declare it unwinnable simply for that.

"Loose—!!"

In step with Ram's advance as she glared down the horde, pressing the front line forward alongside her was a line of dark-skinned warrior maidens. The "People of Shudrak," advancing across the battlefield without fear, drew their bows, nocked their arrows, and loosed a strike upon the stone obstacles pressing in.
Wrapping her own wind about every last one of those arrows, Ram forced her way through the problem by sheer brute strength.

The wind-wrapped arrows, granted speed and spin, struck the stone puppets dead-on, and the instant the arrowhead bit in, the wind burst forth, and the resulting piercing force scattered the puppets to pieces.
The arrows, their force undiminished, went on to pierce the stone puppets standing behind in a chain reaction, wreaking the same destruction and widening the toll.

A single loosed arrow, felling two or even three stone puppets at a stroke.
And on top of that—

"Fura."

A delicate incantation, whisper-soft, conjured a wind of a different wavelength from the wind meant to destroy, and swept across the ground littered with the scattered stone shards, as though stroking it.
At once, the arrows that had fallen to earth after destroying the stone puppets lifted back into the air, returning to the hands of the running Shudrak, nocked once more, loosed once more, felling stone puppets once more. And so it repeated.

"Fura, El Fura, Fura, El Fura."

Alternating incantations, one spell cast after another in rapid succession, the delicate motions of magic of the same lineage.
In the Vollachia Empire, left behind by the advancement of magic, and among the Shudrak, who held nothing but reverence and admiration even for well-honed martial skill, no one could grasp the extraordinary nature of what she was doing.

Closing one's eyes, threading a needle without using one's hands. And more than that—it was a feat of god-like skill, threading that same number of threads through ten, twenty holes, all at once, simultaneously.

With Ram's participation and the effect of her wind magic, the Shudrak's breakthrough power swelled several times over.
The women warriors whom Zikr Osman's conviction and sentiment had left behind now used that preserved strength to smash apart the forces meant to seal off the Third Peak.

"Ahh, how delightful! To take the breath away of friend and foe alike!"

So spoke Mizelda, dashing across the battlefield, a blackly gleaming dagger gripped in her hand.
Though she bore a prosthetic in place of a lost leg, her stride showed not the slightest hesitation, no sign of the loss at all. Charging through the very front line of the battlefield, where her allies' arrows flew without mercy, wielding the blades in both hands, Mizelda shattered the stone puppets like a storm, tearing a hole through their ranks.

"Sister moves as she pleases! Do not stop your hands! Ride Ram's wind with the force of our spirit!"

Bow in her own hands, loosing three shots to every other Shudrak's one, Taritta watched her sister rampage at the front and shouted encouragement to her kin.
In answer, as the Shudrak's arrows dealt telling blows to the horde of stone puppets, Zikr and the others, having reclaimed lives they'd been ready to throw away, charged in to break the enemy formation.

"Out of the way, out of the way, out of the way! You stone rabble, don't you dare tear up this battlefield!!"

At the head of the charge, shouting in a coarse voice quite at odds with his elegant swordsmanship, the eyepatched man cut down the stone puppets in sweeping strokes, and the battlefield was leveled in an instant.
An overwhelming advantage—so much could be said from the description thus far.
And yet—

"Fall back—!!"

Astride his beautifully coated Galewind Horse, Zikr raised his voice, and the group racing at the very front scattered instantly. A moment later, a "wall" fell from above onto the very center of that group.
A thunderous roar and violent tremor wrapped around the earth—a phenomenon that, without exaggeration, was like fighting a fortress itself. The threat that was Moguro Hagane, fused with the city walls, showed no sign of weakening no matter how many stone puppets were shaved away.

Literally, with a single swing of Moguro's arm, the ground they had fought to gain was swept back in an instant.
It was not a one-step-forward-one-step-back struggle, but one step forward, two steps back.
But then—

"—What?"

Handing arrows to the Shudrak, having the Shudrak's arrows pierce true, focused on the one-step advance of the battlefield, Ram narrowed her pale pink eyes, suspicious of the change that had occurred.
It was only that Ram had been the first to notice the signs of it; before long, it became a change plain to every eye, one that shaped the entire battlefield around the Third Peak.
That change was—

"—The hell are you doing?! Don't turn your back on the enemy! And you call yourself a Marshal?!"

At the back of Moguro Hagane, whose body had swelled to unprecedented size, a coarse curse was hurled.
Yes—at his back. At the sight of Moguro Hagane turning his back on Ram and the Shudrak, on the great host of warriors he faced upon that field, and taking his great first step toward the Imperial Capital instead.



"No! She's not waking up at all!"

Shaking her shoulders, calling out to her, giving her cheek a light pat—none of it worked, and the Dragonkin girl limp in her arms, Madelyn Eschart, would not open her eyes.
Emilia, who had recovered the girl after she collapsed motionless in the snow, had been doing everything she could to make the frantic battlefield even a little better, but the results simply would not come.

"Where's Mezoreia..."

Cradling Madelyn, who remained stubbornly asleep with her eyes shut, Emilia turned around, her silver hair streaming in the cold wind, and there, before her, unfolded a clash between two beings each beyond the ordinary.

One, by its very nature, was a Dragon wreathed in cloud, utterly removed from the realm of ordinary living creatures.
The other, in appearance and manner of speech a small child, was a blue-haired boy darting about the battlefield with fighting prowess that put both adults and even Emilia herself to shame.

The battle between the "Cloud Dragon" Mezoreia and Cecilus Segmunt had already become something out of a legend.

"Shwa!"

Pressing the sole of his plain, unremarkable sandal against a wall of ice, Cecilus's body ran across the sky at an angle parallel to the ground.
With nothing to hold onto, a person's body would ordinarily simply fall to earth. And yet Cecilus ignored that plain fact, using the towering, towering wall of ice as a foothold to close in on the Dragon high overhead.

Kicking off hard with his final step, Cecilus's body caught up to Mezoreia at the speed of lightning.
Flapping her wings, trying to put distance between them, Mezoreia was thrown into disarray by his maneuver, her swung claws dodged, and in the end took an undefended slash of an ice blade to her exposed throat.

『—Gyaaugh』

An anguished cry rose, one strikingly un-draconic for a Dragon, and a shrill sound carried word of the ice blade's fate as it shattered against the Dragon's throat. Whether it was the resilience of the "Cloud Dragon's" scales, or the speed of Cecilus's swing, that had shattered the iron-hardened ice blade, was unclear.
Either way, the shattered ice blade had served its purpose, and Cecilus, unguarded there in midair—

"Take your pick, feast your eyes! No restrictions, no tricks needed!"

A voice as sharp and high as the shattering ice, a mark of Cecilus's natural voice and the swell of his own excitement.
Cecilus's words, spun in a tone pleasant to the ear, were followed by a second sound of shattering ice—no, not just two, but three, four, in rapid succession.

"Chai chai chai chai chai!"

He'd seemed unguarded in midair, but the leaping Cecilus had left nothing to chance.
He had, one after another, pulled the countless ice weapons Emilia had conjured—the weapons of the Icicle Line—and stuffed them into his own clothes. On his back, at his waist, between his thighs, all those he'd gathered on his way to catching up with the fleeing Mezoreia were, one after another, wielded by Cecilus's hand now aloft in the sky, stripping away Mezoreia's scales.

Blades, axes, spears, hammers of ice raged furiously, and against that fury, the "Cloud Dragon" was reduced to pure defense. Or perhaps, since she wasn't managing to defend at all, even that description was mistaken.

"Amazing..."

From a distance, in a position safely out of the fray, Emilia could follow it with her eyes—but if Cecilus moved like that right in front of her, she was sure she wouldn't even be able to keep up with the afterimages.
Watching that sheer astonishment unfold, Emilia thought that maybe, even if Madelyn never woke up, Cecilus might finish off Mezoreia all on his own.
It wasn't as if some part of her didn't think that would be fine, if it came to that.

"You're fighting for someone important to you too, aren't you?"

Looking down at Madelyn's unconscious sleeping face, Emilia's violet eyes softened at the corners.
Madelyn had been hostile toward her all this while, was furious with her, wouldn't lend an ear to anything she said—but Emilia didn't know her well enough to hate her for it.
What she did understand was that the reason for Madelyn's fury lay in feelings for someone important to her, and that Mezoreia had come down from the sky to lend strength to just such a Madelyn.

If Mezoreia were to die while she slept, what state would that leave Madelyn's heart in?

"Madelyn, wake up! Come on, wake up!"

They were in the middle of a battle. And more than that, Cecilus had saved Emilia's life when it had hung by a thread.
She couldn't ask him to hold back, couldn't ask him not to kill Mezoreia—such selfish requests were not hers to make.
And so, it had to be Madelyn. Only Madelyn herself might be able to bring this battle to an end without costing the life of either Madelyn or the Mezoreia who had come to save her.

"Ah, I see! The base of the wings is the weak point, isn't it!"

Contrary to Emilia's hopes, Cecilus's analysis of the battle he was so gleefully cheering on continued apace.
Looking back on her memories of fighting Volcanica, Emilia hadn't the faintest idea what a Dragon's weak points might be—but Cecilus, it seemed, was different.

Never falling from the air, matching his dodges to the attacks of wing and tail meant to knock him away, continuing the aerial battle with the Dragon's own body as his foothold, Cecilus's blade struck, exactly as he'd said, at the base of the Dragon's wing.
In that instant, the pitch of her scream changed, and blue blood dripped down onto the white-powdered snow.

Proof that the strike had passed through, beyond the sturdy scales.

"If a Dragon were to lose her wings, I wonder what the difference would even be, compared to an Earth Dragon? Did you ever get the chance, across your long life, to learn to fight while crawling along the ground?"

There was no mockery in it, no contempt.
The tone of Cecilus's voice never wavered; if anything, he spoke that way to psych himself up. And yet, Emilia found herself certain that what he'd just said would become reality right before her eyes.
If even Emilia could be certain of it, then Mezoreia, on the receiving end of his blade directly, must have been all the more certain still.

A Dragon, her wings severed, falling to the earth.
Just how unbearable a thing that would be, Emilia, who had neither wings nor was a Dragon, could not begin to imagine. But she understood well enough that it would spell the end of any chance Mezoreia had of winning.
Because there was no way a Dragon who couldn't be caught even in the sky could possibly be chased down on the ground.

『—A Dragon!!』

In that instant, as if to cast off the humiliation looming right before her, Mezoreia's low voice erupted.
Kicking off Mezoreia's flank, Cecilus leapt up, his slash bearing down on the base of her wing. An instant before it landed, she spun in a corkscrew, reversing her posture in midair from face-down to face-up.
Facing down the Cecilus who had aimed for her wing, Mezoreia's draconic arm swept out in a wide slash.

Claw or scale, whichever caught him, a human body would be torn apart in an instant.
Even Cecilus, fast as he was, would surely be no exception—Emilia nearly screamed. But the scream that rose from Emilia's throat was not for Cecilus's death, but for a different sight entirely.

"Whoa, that was close!"

The swung draconic arm had indeed caught Cecilus, there in midair.
And yet Cecilus pressed the sole of his foot against the arm that struck him, and ran clean along the very arm that had delivered that furious blow.
Starting from around the elbow of the Dragon's arm, he was launched from the tip of her draconic claw as a springboard.
Converting what should have been the force to send his body flying on direct impact into propulsion to run and leap free, he escaped, with that outrageous speed of his feet, even from a death that should have been unavoidable.

"Beautiful lady!"

"Oh, yes!"

Called a beautiful lady, Emilia forgot even her usual words of modesty at the address.
Sensing by instinct just what sort of call that was, Emilia, as Cecilus leapt free from the tip of the Dragon's arm and landed atop the ice wall, cast new ice weapons anew around him.
Cecilus snatched them up in an instant, turned to face the surely-pursuing Mezoreia, and bent his knees, bracing for another leap.

The distance that had opened between them, the blink of an eye it would take to close it—that was Mezoreia's chance, the one moment where Cecilus's attack absolutely could not reach her.
Naturally, Mezoreia would pour everything she had into this. —Or so it should have been.

"Huh?"

Instantly bracing for the attack, dropping his hips low, Cecilus tilted his head.
By unspoken agreement, he had been yielding the first move—and yet the attack that should have come never came.
The very same doubt that had gripped Cecilus gripped Emilia too. That this was the final line dividing victory from defeat—even Emilia, pushed somehow to the sidelines without quite realizing it, understood that much.
And yet Mezoreia did not move. More than that—

『——』

An instant before, Mezoreia had been on the verge of swinging her draconic arm to obliterate Cecilus. That motion stopped, frozen in midair, and her white eyes—where the black of a pupil ought to have been unclear—fixed upon a single point.
Not on Cecilus, who had cornered her, meant to make her taste humiliation.
Not on Cecilus, bracing atop the ice wall, nor on Emilia, cradling Madelyn—a presence painting this battlefield white, one Mezoreia herself could not afford to ignore.

Mezoreia, frozen still in midair as if pinned in place, turned her gaze toward the far higher sky.
Toward a sky far, far higher than her own, she remained, unmoving.

"...Is something... flying up there?"

Drawn along by Mezoreia's gaze, Emilia squinted toward the gray, snow-laden clouds.
At a position higher still than the sky where Mezoreia's enormous body floated, at the very edge of what even Emilia's eyesight could make out, some shadow was flying.
Something flying through the sky—Emilia's only guesses were the Dragon right in front of her, one of the many Flying Dragons crowding the battlefield, or Roswaal, too lazy to bother with travel time.
And then—

『—No way』

Murmuring that, Mezoreia beat her wings.
The Dragon's body, which had been still, resumed motion. Only, it was not the motion meant to unleash the decisive attack that should have come the moment before, but—

"Ehhh?! Wait wait wait, hold on, you can't be serious!?"

The instant he witnessed that motion, the greatest tremor yet ran across Cecilus's expression.
That face, which had looked delighted no matter what was done to him until now, went wide-eyed in a moment of pure panic. And no wonder. The opponent who should have been closing in for the kill had, of all things, turned her back on him.

『——』

Paying no heed to Cecilus's voice, Mezoreia flipped her wings, cutting through the sky.
Once she'd resolved to fly and set herself in motion, the Dragon's speed was nothing short of extraordinary; the momentum with which she wheeled about and climbed into the sky was as swift as an arrow loosed with all one's strength.

"I won't let you, will I!!"

Determined not to let the fleeing Dragon escape, Cecilus, knees bent, detonated the strength in his legs—not to intercept her, but to give chase to the fleeing Dragon.
A launch unbelievable from so small a frame cracked fissures into the thick, massive wall of ice at the point where the sole of his shoe struck, and shattering the ice like a collapsing cliffside, Cecilus's body leapt.

Straight as an arrow, Cecilus's figure outpaced even the Dragon's speed, closing in on her wings. Closer. Closer, and closer, and closer still, and then—

"—Ah, no, this one's not gonna reach, huh?"

However fast Cecilus's feet were, however tremendous a distance he could cover in a single bound, he could not erase the distance opened up by a Dragon who had started out already airborne.
Pitifully, Cecilus's body failed to catch the retreating Mezoreia, losing momentum at the very limit of his leap and flipping over backward. Had the "Cloud Dragon" turned back at that moment to strike at him, perhaps even he would have been in real danger.
But Mezoreia did not turn back. Without turning back, she climbed higher and higher still, cutting through the sky.
And then—

"—She's heading... into the Imperial Capital?"


△▼△▼△▼△


—Among the changes occurring across every corner of the Battle for the Imperial Capital, two developments in particular loomed largest.

The very instant those unfolded outside the Crystal Palace, within the throne room, the two Emperors stood face to face, their gazes crossing at a distance close enough for their very lashes to brush.

"——"

Having been outmaneuvered by a single move, Abel, ringleader of the rebels, nonetheless shifted his thinking at once.
Leaning in to strike the best move—no, the move that would follow the best move—against the opponent bearing down before him, wearing his own face—

"—!"

A sharp impact struck his left collarbone, and at that stab of pain, his thoughts scattered red.
Looking, he saw that what had struck his throat was the iron war-fan in his opposite's hand—a familiar object, the weapon the man wearing his own face favored using.
As a weapon it belonged to an unusual category, one whose true destructive power Abel had often wondered about, questioned many times before.

"——"

He consciously excised from his thoughts both the fact that his past question had now been answered, and the pain stabbing into his brain.
He pictured in his mind the matters that demanded priority in this very instant, and set immediately to devising countermeasures to address them. Feasibility weighed against effect, priorities sorted out even amid his injury.
But—

"This isn't a game played on a board. That, I'd wager, is precisely why you could never quite become a warrior."

Faster than the countless options surfacing in his mind, faster than he could choose among them, the warrior unveiled a technique steeped not in the head, but in flesh and blood.
Without mercy it wrenched Abel's arm, and as his grip loosened, stole the thing he held—and in the next instant, his field of vision was shut off for but a moment.

"——"

Had his eyes been gouged out, or was this some manner of blinding trick?
That momentary consideration was disproven when his obstructed vision returned an instant later. Then what had been the true intent behind his opponent's action? Even as he wondered, he realized it.

—That a sensation, once familiar, had been fitted back over his own face.

"You—"

His lips, moving faster than hand or foot, let slip a sound as he glared into the black eyes before him.
At those words from Abel, now wearing the stolen demon mask once more, the false Emperor before him—no, Chisha Gold—twisted his lips into a shape not his own.
That it was a bleakly decadent smile, Abel recognized as his own face, and his eyes went wide.
In that instant—

"——"



—A shaft of white light, boring through the wall of the throne room and hurtling within, pierced through the chest of Vincent Vollachia, pinnacle of the Empire, from behind.
