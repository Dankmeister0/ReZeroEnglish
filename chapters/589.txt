Arc 7 Chapter 84: "Tea Room"

――Across the Vollachia Empire, the flames of rebellion spread, and with each passing day the air of unrest thickens.

Nine-odd years had passed since Vincent Vollachia ascended to the throne, and never before had the domestic situation grown so scorched with tension; the hearts of the people were greatly disturbed.
The history of the Vollachia Empire is a history of war.
Even should an era of peace unmatched in its annals arrive, it could never fully forestall the conflicts that arose beyond the reach of the imperial capital's eyes and ears. And so, not all the people could rest easy.

And yet, even so, the people who lived in the imperial capital of Lupugana held a certain peace of mind.
Only in the capital, the ground beneath the emperor's own feet, that magnificent city, did strife never occur. Such was the comfort born of Emperor Vincent Vollachia's authority — but that, too, was a thing of the past now.
The assassination attempt on the emperor two years prior — that had happened in none other than the capital itself. The emperor had been wounded, his life endangered, and the one who had carried it out was, of all people, a member of the Nine Divine Generals.
Since that incident, the people had learned that true peace would never come to the Empire. And having learned it, they had also come to hope.

That the emperor's bearing — unshaken even by the treachery of the Nine Divine Generals, the very pinnacle of the Empire's martial might — meant that no matter how violently the Vollachia Empire shook, it would never crumble.

"――That peace and hope among the people has, of late, been greatly shaken."

A report that a crack had appeared in the great premise laid down over the Empire.
Had this been spoken to one who brushed aside all words unpleasant to the ear without mercy, it might well have cost the speaker his head — a report over which hesitation would have been no surprise. Yet it was declared boldly, aloud, in the throne room itself.

The civil and military officials arrayed there, gathered at the capital's Crystal Palace — the very keep of the Vollachia Empire — were, whatever battlefields they might otherwise call their own, soldiers all the same. That even they could not help but falter came from this: to be judged weak-willed here was to be judged, in effect, dead.

None of them feared death itself. What they feared was a death without meaning.

To meet an end unbecoming of a Vollachian warrior — that was what they feared.
And so the officers held the white-haired sage who had made that report, Chancellor Berstetz Fondalfon, in high regard. And they waited on the response of the one to whom the chancellor's report was addressed — Vincent.

"――――"

The throne, vast enough to seem to swallow whole that slender frame, was itself a symbol of authority passed down unbroken since the time of the first Vollachian emperor.
Behind the throne flew the national flag, the wolf pierced by a sword — the nation's crest — gazing down valiantly upon the assembled officers and men. With that sword-wolf at his back, Vincent sat in unhurried repose.

Leaning easily against the throne, Vincent bore no air of martial prowess.
Indeed, no one had ever heard so much as a rumor that this emperor, possessed of such profound cunning, excelled at arms. No one had seen him wield a blade; no one had seen him so much as indulge in the hunt.

An emperor holds the throne, and is required to govern the whole of the Empire.
Even in an Empire that prizes strength above all, its summit — the emperor himself — is not required to be skilled in arms. For the peerless strength he commands in his service is itself the emperor's blade.
And yet――

"――っ"

Silent, merely seated, and still the emperor pressed down upon the many gathered officers and men.
There was no reason he should win a duel of blades; no reason it should take more than a few seconds to close the distance to the throne. If it is the natural order that the strong oppress the weak, then the officers had no reason at all to fear this emperor.
And yet this distance, and the irresistible presence of the emperor, remained unassailable.

"The people's peace of mind, is it."

Abruptly, breaking the silence that had reigned until then, the emperor's lips moved.
At once, the tension that had filled the throne room seemed to loosen — only, instead of easing, it thickened, and pressed down upon the hearts of the assembled officers and men.
Narrowing his long, dark eyes, Vincent turned them upon Berstetz, who still held the posture of a vassal's salute, palm pressed to fist before his chest.

"Since when has the emperor of my nation taken to gauging the moods of the common folk?"

"……I understand how Your Excellency might see it so. And yet it is a fact that unease over Your Excellency's reign has risen among the rumors of the common folk. Left unattended, this poisoned blood, bearing its sickness, will circulate through the whole of the Empire."

"And so you counsel me to bleed the poison out entirely."

"Forgive my boldness, but even His Excellency who rules the Empire would lose his life were his head to fall. To spare the limbs out of reluctance, only to lose the head in the end — would that not be a grave misjudgment?"

"――――"

"Naturally, if it can be checked while it is still a matter of fingers, ears, or nails, nothing would be better."

Adding a bow to that final remark, Berstetz set forth his opinion so.
Such unreserved words — put another way, words spoken without the slightest deference — made the assembled officers tremble. And yet, at the same time, they could marvel that he had spoken aloud what needed saying.

Berstetz's words were, in truth, the consensus of the officers regarding the rebellion spreading across the Empire.
Those who had thus far resigned themselves to Vincent's rule now watched with fury as, once one voice had dared to rise, others leapt upon it, raising the cry of rebellion one after another across the land.
Had it been only that first man, he might have qualified as a valiant enemy worthy of the stage. But what of the shamelessness of those who followed after?

To fight, to conquer, to seize — that is the basic principle of the Empire's people.
Too many mistook the creed that the Empire's people should be strong and mighty, twisting it to their own convenience. Was it not only by crushing such men that the people could truly embody that creed?
And yet, against these rebellions, Vincent had done nothing but commit to defense by the garrison troops of each region, taking no active countermeasure. Though, to be fair――

"Dispatching generals one by one to nip the seeds of rebellion will not resolve the matter at its root."

"――Quite the silver tongue you've developed, Berstetz. Winning over the assembled officers to your side to stand against me — you wear the very face of one who might himself lead the rebellion."

"A jest, surely. This old servant harbors no ambition to lead a revolt and drive Your Excellency from the throne."

"Hmph."

Vincent gave a short snort through his nose, letting Berstetz's protest pass unremarked.
And yet one could understand why Vincent spoke so. For until just now, every word from Berstetz's mouth had been, in truth, the voice of the officers' own hearts.
Including the point about the dispatch of a single general being insufficient, raised in advance as a caution.
As for the sharp, blood-edged exchange that followed――

"Your Excellency, this rebellion in question——"

"I have heard your counsel and taken note of it. But——"

"――――"

"――I too have a plan."

With a single utterance, one eye closing, Vincent spoke thus, his gaze tracing over Berstetz and then the assembled officers, and the mistrust toward the emperor that had begun to rise was broken.
When the summons had first gone out and the officers gathered in the throne room, each of them had harbored some private thought concerning Vincent's passive stance toward this rebellion.
Indeed, it had been Berstetz who spoke in the officers' stead, but the sentiment he conveyed had lived equally in every officer present, and hearing it voiced had only swelled it further.

That sentiment, which had in a sense threatened to spread like the rebellion itself, was now extinguished.
As a great fire, doused by wind and water, has its force slackened, weakened, and snuffed out.
And then――

"Or do you doubt my word?"

So asked the emperor, possessed of profound and unfathomable cunning, and Berstetz's brow trembled.
Deep behind those thread-thin eyes, what emotion stirred there, invisible to any outsider, none present could say for certain.
Only one thing was certain.

"「――Never, not for a moment!」"

The officers spoke as one, answering the emperor's question with valiant voice.
Stamping their feet, the military officers drew the swords at their hips and raised them high. The civil officials pressed palm to fist before their chests, each offering the highest salute befitting their station, answering His Excellency's question thus.

The thoughts of Emperor Vincent Vollachia — no one could ever unravel them.
And yet, ask whether that which cannot be understood should be dismissed as untrustworthy, and the answer is no.

If what trust requires is words and deeds proven, then Vincent had shown deeds.
It began with the Imperial Selection Ceremony by which he claimed the throne, and the skill with which he had since realized a reign free of great upheaval stood as proof enough to merit that trust.
And now the emperor who had shown such deeds had spoken, too, the words that trust requires.

――Do you doubt my word, he had said.

"My handling of this rebellion, too, proceeds from a plan of my own devising. Must I explain it to you from beginning to end before you can even manage to obey?"

"「――Never, not for a moment!」"

"Then, before you strain your ears and let your eyes wander, do what must be done. I have not clothed those unable to fulfill their duties in the garments of rank."

The emperor's manner of speech was cold and cutting, and it was precisely for that reason the officers felt it close to them.
There was, in Vincent's gaze and voice, a power to command the heat of another's soul. That uncanny quality, capable of turning both hot and cold, on this occasion set the officers' hearts ablaze.

What was he thinking — such unease and doubt had clouded the officers' eyes.
No concrete answer had been given to dispel it. And yet the clouded eyes of the officers cleared. — Because their emperor had declared plainly that his deep and far-reaching schemes were at work.
That alone was enough for many of the Empire's soldiers to fight believing in victory.

"Might Your Excellency reveal even a fragment of what lies in your heart to this old servant, and to the officers?"

"To what end? To reveal it would cast a shadow over my strategy. And in exchange, what would I gain but the peace of mind of you and the officers, fretting over what lies ahead?"

Not worth the comparison, Vincent said, brushing Berstetz aside with cruel indifference.
And yet the officers stood behind that very answer of Vincent's. The feeling from moments ago, that Berstetz's words had spoken for them, had already vanished.
If anything, they now felt something like anger, even irritation, toward Berstetz's counsel. Vincent had revealed that he had a plan of his own. Was that not enough?

"There will be discontent, I am sure. Nor do I intend to explain everything away with the Empire's authority alone."

Fixing his gaze on the silent Berstetz, Vincent added further words.
At the emperor's tone, one meant to instruct a party who could not be satisfied, many of the officers shook their heads inwardly. No further words were needed. And yet the emperor continued.

"But it is as I have told you. I do not intend to lay bare my intentions. In their place, I give you but a single word."

"Your Excellency……"

"Let the Empire's people be strong and mighty."

"――――"

"――A battlefield befitting that sword-wolf's nature — that, I shall prepare for you."

With a deep nod, Vincent made that vow to the officers beyond Berstetz.
A beat late, a searing passion caught like fire and spread through every officer's body. It was a blaze no less fierce than the fires of rebellion spreading across the Empire, a conflagration wrapped in ferocity.
If rebellion was a flame of distrust toward the emperor, then what now burned the officers was a flame of trust in him.

"――――"

Amid the officers, their fervor quietly building, Vincent and Berstetz exchanged a silent glance.
Emperor and chancellor, both standing at the Empire's summit by dint of their cunning — the meaning behind the look that passed between the two of them, the surrounding officers could not discern.
Only, Berstetz added no further remark likely to displease the emperor.
Instead――

"――Your Excellency, forgive my boldness, but there is one more matter I would ask."

"Piling question upon suspicion, are you? Unlike before, those at your back no longer look upon you as their ally."

"Whether or not I have allies is hardly grounds on which one in the chancellor's position ought to decide whether to speak or hold his tongue."

"Glib as ever. Say it, then."

Jutting his thin chin, Vincent bade him continue.
At this, Berstetz prefaced his words with "Then," and went on.
It was this――

"――The black-haired 'Crown Prince.'"

"――――"

"The figure that those raising rebellion in various regions champion as their banner. A boy with black hair and black eyes, said to be — by birth — Your Excellency's own child, though never publicly acknowledged."

Like tossing a fire-imbued magic stone charged with mana, Berstetz's act left the officers, whose vision had been narrowed for better or worse, once more struck speechless.
That rumor — the officers, too, had heard tell of it. Its truth or falsehood was, to them as well, a matter of gossip they could not honestly claim indifference to. And yet none had possessed the courage to confirm it.
Toward Berstetz, who had confronted it head-on, the officers — whose feelings a moment ago had leaned toward resentment — now turned, as if by a flip of the hand, back toward expectation.

Now, the existence of the "Crown Prince," on which the whole of the Empire's people had fixed their attention.
How did it appear in Vincent's eyes, how did his ears hear it, how would his mouth speak of it?
After a moment's silence, Vincent called out, "Berstetz," to the chancellor.
And then――

"Do not let yourself be swayed by worthless rumor. I have no child. If anything, seize upon the source of that gossip and bring this so-called heir before me. I shall keep him as a jester, for my amusement."

So the black-haired emperor declared, with even a cruel smile playing on his lips.

"――Truly, the 'Crown Prince' is not Your Excellency's own child?"

The same question, posed a few hours earlier before a great assembly.
And yet the color of feeling woven into the voice now was subtly different, and it carried that much more weight for it. A question meant in earnest, in a tone whose meaning only the listener could grasp.
And well it might, for to the old man who posed it, this was quite literally a matter of life and death.

The place was neither the throne room nor the Crystal Palace, but the chancellor's residence within the capital.
Though the two of them together conducted the Empire's governance, an ever-present, unrelenting tension stretched taut between Vincent and Berstetz, and it was generally understood by those around them, put plainly, that the two did not get along.
All the more reason, then, that anyone who witnessed this exchange would be greatly astonished.

That Vincent Vollachia should secretly visit the residence of Berstetz Fondalfon, and here, in a single room, sit facing him thus.
Though, in terms of true astonishment, the substance of what passed between them would be far more astonishing still.

"――――"

Narrowing his black eyes at the question, Vincent fixed his gaze upon the other, falling silent. It was less deliberation than the emperor taking a pause meant to press his counterpart — and Berstetz, for his part, did not hurry.
An old man well versed in the effects of both silence and pause.
In truth, for all he called it a "matter of life and death," there was not the faintest trace of unease, agitation, or self-preservation in Berstetz's manner as he posed the question and awaited the answer.
Indeed, not a trace of self-preservation — and that was precisely what made this old man most worthy of wariness.

"Naturally."

"――――"

"The answer does not change. I have no heir. All of it is nothing but idle nonsense."

"As I have told you before, nothing spoken here leaks beyond these walls. Not even General Olbart could catch so much as a whisper of it here. You know this."

Allowing ample pause, Berstetz answered Vincent thus.
The room prepared within Berstetz's residence, called the "Tea Room," was a small fortress outfitted with every manner of ceremony solely for the purpose of exchanging secret counsel. It was said to employ a barrier woven with magic — a rarity in Vollachia — along with unknown Curse Arts and even something akin to a Meteor.
By what he had heard, this "Tea Room" alone had cost enough gold to purchase an entire city.

"I receive stipend enough for the purpose. Upon my death I shall bequeath it to you; I trust you will put it to good use."

"Do you imagine a room named a 'tea room' suits the Crystal Palace?"

"The name and furnishings matter little; redecorate it as you please. What matters is its function……here, there is no need even to wear a mask of falsehood."

Rather than leaving his meaning unspoken, Berstetz came, with blunt words, to strip away the mask directly.
At that, Vincent closed one eye. Not deliberation — the answer was already fixed. This too was merely silence meant to draw out the pause.
He knew well enough that such an effect held no sway over the old man before him, and yet he did not hold back.
He had no intention of bending what he had already decided. An old, easygoing friend had once told him bluntly that this left no room for play — but it was simply his nature.

"There is no reason I should submit to your manner of speaking. You and I are, in the end, allies bound only by a coincidence of interest. Do not mistake that."

"Indeed. All the more reason, then, that I would have you thoroughly dispel this old servant's doubt."

"――――"

"I ask again. His Excellency the Emperor has no child, does he not?"

"――. The answer does not change."

To the repeated question, Vincent gave the same repeated answer. At this, Berstetz replied only with a short "I see," his expression betraying neither disappointment nor relief.
Given this old man's aims, Vincent could well guess what retort would follow — and yet, in connection with the matter at hand, he himself put a question back.

"Suppose, hypothetically, that I did have a child. What would you do?"

"Were Your Excellency to have a child, it would mean Your Excellency had the intent to fulfill the duties of emperor. I would promptly secure the child's person, and see the true Excellency returned to the throne."

"Heh. And what, then, would become of me?"

"You and I both know full well what end befalls a traitor, do we not?"

Berstetz's manner, answering matter-of-factly as though it were self-evident, was thoroughgoing.
If anything, this posture of utter selflessness in service to the Empire's proper order was almost refreshing. Precisely because it showed not a single cloud of doubt, the abnormality of it stood out all the more.
Setting aside Berstetz's thoughts on the matter, though――

"――The rumor of the 'Crown Prince' is likely something the very one who keeps fleeing has spread himself."

"Believing it would serve as kindling for rebellion in every region, you mean? Could it not be that even you, even General Cecilus, simply were never informed of a child's existence?"

"Impossible."

"Even you cannot claim to know everything about His Excellency."

"――It is impossible."

To Berstetz, who seemed intent on hammering at every last detail, Vincent shook his head.
This was not wishful thinking, nor desire, nor the posturing of one claiming to understand another. What cannot happen, cannot happen. He could declare it with not a single cloud of doubt.

True or false, Vincent Vollachia has no child.

That man must surely have been thorough in leaving no room for such suspicion, not even the barest possibility. To eliminate that doubt entirely, he had likely never once shared a bedchamber with a woman.
An iron will that never once closed both eyes before others — that was what let him hold to that role without fail.
And so――

"――Vincent Vollachia has no child. What you do is just."

"Just? If this be justice, then it is I who ought, with these hands, to seize the throne. That I cannot do so proves it is not justice. And besides――"

"――――"

"――These withered, old-tree arms of mine cannot protect the Empire's authority."

A madness-tinged obsession seemed, if anything, to seep all the more through the old man's flat tone.
Most people act believing what they do to be right. Otherwise, they cannot fully exert their strength, and naturally cannot even affirm themselves.
Just how many people could knowingly commit wrongs and still walk on without hesitation? Walk on without hesitation, and bring back results such as these?
And then――

"――――"

――And then, at the end of the path walked while committing such wrongs, what awaited there?

"Once the doubt is dispelled, what this old servant must do does not change."

Setting aside Vincent's private musings, Berstetz let the words fall flatly.
He had likely never held out much hope for the "Crown Prince's" existence in the first place. Having heard from his closest confidant that the possibility did not exist, it was the sort of doubt that could, for now, be set aside.
And so Berstetz's interest, and the topic, swiftly moved on to the next matter.

"Now then, as for the handling of the rebels who would turn their blades upon your person——"

"Was our exchange in the throne room not sufficient to satisfy you?"

"I deliberately took on the role of speaking for the officers, but my position differs from theirs, who trust wholly in Your Excellency's discernment and authority. ――I have dispatched General Arakiya and General Madelyn to various regions to nip the seeds of rebellion, but that alone will not keep pace."

"――――"

Resting his cheek upon his hand, Vincent listened to Berstetz's report in silence.
In truth, Berstetz's conduct in the throne room had not been arranged beforehand. And yet, considering the Empire's circumstances and Vincent's position, it could be called the finest possible stroke.
It had, at least, deflected the officers' discontent and suspicion. And yet Berstetz, who knew the throne itself stood empty, would not be satisfied by the mere authority of one who had simply seated himself upon that vacancy.

"This is, properly speaking, a situation calling for the command abilities of General Chisha and General Goz. As those two are difficult to move, the next best option would be……to recall General Groovy, perhaps?"

"――The movements to the northwest smell foul indeed. Whether it is Kararagi, or some other design at work, I cannot say, but at present there is no option of moving him from the border."

The original duty of a general is twofold: defense of domestic order, and deterrence against foreign threats.
This present affair, though it was Vincent and Berstetz who had conspired to bring it about, would defeat its own purpose were it to imperil the ground beneath the Empire's feet — and so, self-serving deployment would only tighten the noose around their own necks.
The dispatch of Groovy Gumlet, too, was an indispensable stronghold for the Empire's defense.

"Then what of General Olbart?"

"I would also avoid rashly removing him from the capital and letting him make careless contact with the rebel forces. Better he be kept in the capital, deployed only at key points — at least while he remains, for now, inclined to obey."

"There is also the matter of the arm he lost in the affair at the Demon City, still in recovery. It should not be difficult to find pretext to keep him in the capital. Then what of General Moguro?"

"He has a role only he can fulfill."

Tapping the floor with the tip of his toe, Vincent dismissed Berstetz's suggestions one after another.
Each of the Nine Divine Generals listed had been given, and placed in, a role not easily reassigned. Though there was, if one, among the Nine, who did not fit that pattern.

"General Cecilus remains, I take it, an unknown quantity."

"To have him distinguish friend from foe is a task beyond difficulty. His convictions shift not with cause or principle. And so I have removed him from the board."

Their acquaintance could be called a long one. And yet not once had Vincent felt he understood the man.
Likely, no one could understand Cecilus save Cecilus himself. However certain his swordsmanship, there was no room to spare for one whose standing was so uncertain.
For a man who longed to stand upon the grand stage, this would no doubt seem a grievance indeed.

"In any case, there is no likelihood of his return to the board. A piece that cannot be wielded, and which only causes trouble if put to use, would merely throw off the calculations. Likely the rebels view the matter much the same."

"――. If there is no likelihood of his return to the board, this old servant has no objection. Then, as for the forces to meet the impudent 'Crown Prince' who falsely claims to be Your Excellency's child, the rebels gathering momentum, and General Yorna——"

"Arakiya, Olbart Dunkelkenn, Madelyn Eschart."

"――――"

"Should that prove insufficient, add Chisha Gold and Moguro Hagane as well."

At the word "insufficient," Berstetz shook his head, saying, "Surely not."
It was hard to know how sincerely to take this, but to assemble five of the Nine Divine Generals to meet an enemy was extraordinary beyond measure. Among the remaining generals as well, there were no few who might be called near-general in strength, Kafma Irulux foremost among them.
And so――

"――We shall meet the rebels head-on, here in the capital of Lupugana."

"Should the rebels gather in sufficient numbers, might they not change their manner of fighting?"

"Between factions using the black-haired 'Crown Prince' to gather forces, each flying a banner of deceit? Each claiming the truth of an heir who does not exist — hardly likely they could ever march in step. In the short term, it is a fine stroke to fan the flames of rebellion higher; in the middle and long term, it only breeds more flaws."

Most of the rebels who had raised their voices in this opportunity had set up a false Crown Prince as their banner.
Even should the rebel armies of every region gather together and converge upon the capital, there was no way they could ever march in unison. Though of course, a matter so simple could hardly escape Vincent Vollachia's notice.
Even so, some measure of uncertainty remained. And speaking of uncertainty――

"――A decisive battle at the capital, then. Not since the founding of the Sacred Vollachia Empire has a horde of rebels ever pressed upon the capital in such a way — much like 'Magrizza's Guillotine' itself."

"Berstetz."

"Yes."

"You seem to be enjoying yourself rather a great deal."

At Vincent's remark, Berstetz responded with an exceedingly rare look of bewilderment — "Eh?" But then, touching his own cheek as if pinching it between his fingers, he seemed, for the first time, to become aware of the emotion himself.
Carefully probing at the faint trace of delight that had seeped through, he sought its source, and said——

"My deepest apologies. That I of all people should do such a thing — I offer my sincerest regret."

"No apology is needed. What amuses you so?"

"Not amusement, exactly. ……I merely thought, ah, of course."

"Of course?"

"Of course — that it is precisely amid a scene of swirling war and chaos that the Vollachia Empire is most truly itself."

Had anyone from outside the Empire heard it, they would surely have laughed it off as absurd, coming from the mouth of an old man such as this.
And yet it was, most likely, a sentiment held by nearly every man, woman, young and old, so long as they were of the Vollachia Empire — Berstetz was nothing special in this regard.
――No, of course, that one should still give voice to such a thing even after attaining this rank was rare indeed.

"So it is, being thus, that makes it the Vollachia Empire?"

"Each of us carries our own picture of the future we envision. Your Excellency…… no, you as well, surely."

"――――"

Within the Tea Room, though there was no chance of being overheard, he had spoken rather more than was his wont.
This unwonted talkativeness, unlike Berstetz, might perhaps be proof that even one such as he had human blood flowing in his veins. If so, then perhaps it was he himself who had no human blood flowing in his.
For――

"――You speak too freely, Berstetz. Who do you take me for?"

So he answered, quietly — and in those words there was neither the exaltation nor the grief that, by rights, ought to have been there.
