Arc 6 Chapter 85: "Good Loser"

It took courage to call himself the "Sword Saint's Superior Knight."

There was pride to be had in being called that by others, in being praised so.
But he had never once called himself "most" or "superior" of his own accord.

He had confidence in the effort he'd spared no pains for, in the days of discipline he'd spent.
But being untalented and unripe as he was, whenever he looked upward there was no end to the superior forerunners, the comrades worth revering, the astonishing juniors who surrounded him.
He had thought that both a source of frustration and a source of happiness.

Being acknowledged by someone ought to be the reward that comes at the end of striving and diligence.
And if one meant to be acknowledged by *everyone*, then that striving and diligence had to be the kind of discipline so tremendous that its mere existence made everyone stand in awe.

*—Had he, in fact, made efforts worthy of that?*

He did have confidence, certainly, in the effort he'd spared no pains for, in the days of discipline he'd spent.
But had he surpassed his limits? Had he polished himself, day after ordinary day, until his strength gave out? Had he been spurred on by the striving of others to vow ever more effort toward his ideal?

Let him answer that question himself.

Julius Juukulius had done all of that.
He had surpassed his limits, polished himself until his strength gave out, and vowed ever more effort with the striving of others as his model.

—And so, standing before the very summit of the "sword," he could hold his chest out with pride.

"—I am the Sword Saint's Superior Knight, Julius Juukulius. I am the Kingdom's sword, and I will cut you down."

"——"

Gripping the hem of his cloak, Julius bowed, and before him the Sword Saint fell silent.
The eye not covered by his eyepatch—that eye, too, he closed, and he did not look at Julius. He simply crossed his thick, powerful arms in quiet and pondered something. But that pondering did not last long. Even in their short acquaintance so far, it was plain enough that thinking things through simply wasn't in his nature.
And so—

"Ahh, ahh, ahhhhh, ahhhhhhhhhhhhhh—screw it!!"

Scratching roughly at his own head, the Sword Saint Reid stamped his foot hard once against the floor.
That single stomp made the white floor of the second level shudder as if it had burst. Echidna, watching the two face off, flinched, but Julius, standing straight, didn't so much as sway.
Seeing that, Reid clicked his tongue.

"Puttin' on airs, puttin' on airs, puttin' on airs... ahh, so it's airs, is it. Just like I thought, you talk just like that lackey of mine. You're a real irritating bastard, you know that?"

"I regret I have no idea what you mean, but I can't help but sympathize with this lackey fellow."

"Ahh? Who said anythin' about a fella. First off, draggin' some guy around wouldn't be any fun. The lackey I'm talkin' about's a woman. Good face on her, but mouth full of theory."

"A woman... then what do she and I have in common?"

"Ahh? Don't make me say it again."

Wrinkling his nose, Reid bared a savage, shark-like grin.
Then, uncrossing his arms, he slapped his own cheek lightly with one hand.

"Smellin' of theory, and havin' a good face."

"——"

"Tch, not even a flicker of irritation. No charm to you at all. ...Well, fine, I guess."

Snorting at Julius's meager reaction, Reid gave his neck a loud, satisfying crack. Then he observed Julius with his blue eyes—no, not Julius himself, but what surrounded him.
The faint lights swaying about Julius were shining brighter than before.
More than that, this was the first time he had shown them off before Reid at all.

"Something the matter with my buds—no, my lovely maidens?"

"Hah, nothin's the matter. A good woman don't care what species she is. Shame is, I got no interest in a woman I can't hold. —You'd have been stronger if you'd broken outta that shell of yours, y'know?"

"If you say so, then perhaps that path existed as well."

Advice to a junior—such a whim was inconceivable from Reid's character.
That he'd offered it at all was probably because Reid was simply moody by nature, and because he found it a waste to see Julius clinging so desperately to the sword with no other choice.
If you're going to cling to the sword anyway, show a posture that doesn't care how it looks. That was the attitude and resolve Reid had wished for in Julius—and it, too, had been a path that could have been.
But—

"I have decided to walk this road. Perhaps, as you say, breaking my shell and baring whatever 'true self' lies beneath would make me stronger."

Julius himself was aware that, left unchecked, that was exactly what would happen naturally.
In an instant's decision, in a knife's-edge exchange of blows, in a moment where a hair's breadth decided life and death—the true face of Julius would surface.
But that was only so if he did not consciously guard against it. —And now, he would not waver.

"Let me declare it plainly. I will pursue myself as a knight, to the very limit. And in doing so, I will become, in every respect, superior to the man you would have led me to become."

"Hah, and what kinda logic gets you there, huh?"

"It's simple. —The knight I believe in is the embodiment of an ideal. Pure, righteous, and above all, strong. If I call myself a knight, then it wouldn't do for me to be anything less."

"—Ha."

Even Julius thought it a reckless piece of logic, an argument with no coherent thread, an arrogance worthy of ridicule.
And yet, hearing it, Reid showed neither open anger nor contempt and disdain—he simply bared his sharp teeth and laughed.
And then—

"I'll make you cry."

Saying that, Reid tossed away the chopsticks in his hand and, before the wide-eyed Julius, leapt back a great distance. Then, slowly, he stretched out his hand to the side.
What his large palm seized in a firm grip was the Sword of Selection standing planted in the white floor of the tier—

Reid Astrea, who by rights should only have been lending his existence as the tower's examiner.
By some twist of fate, through sheer overwhelming self-awareness he had wrenched himself free of the tower's mechanisms, and finally, by overwriting the body of the invading Sin Archbishop of Gluttony, had even achieved a kind of pseudo-resurrection.
Having become such a being, now under no obligation to obey the tower's "Trial" at all, it was only now that Reid, for the first time, drew the Sword of Selection according to its true purpose.
Which was to say—

"—Let the fool who has reached the Heavenly Sword earn its bearer's forgiveness."

"That's supposed to be my line. ...Well, I forgot it, since it just slipped clean outta my head."

"I thought as much, so I said it in your stead. —Allow me to make my challenge."

"Like hell I'll allow it, idiot. I'll make you squeal."

Before Julius, who held his knight's sword raised before him, Reid pointed the sword he'd drawn carelessly in his direction.
There was no opening whatsoever. A natural stance, honed to the ultimate degree—the pinnacle of swordsmanship—

—The summit of all who wielded a blade, the Sword Saint, Reid Astrea.

"Come, then—"

"However you like."

"—Here I come!!"

Believing in the way of the knight that shaped who he was, Julius Juukulius threw himself at the Sword Saint with everything he had.

Both body and heart were light.
Without exaggeration, that was the power of the exhilaration wrapping Julius's whole body as he swung his knight's sword.

Needless to say, the stability of one's spirit has enormous bearing on a fight.
Thinking on it, ever since arriving at the Pleiades Watchtower—no, ever since his "name" had been taken from him in the Water Gate City—Julius could be said to have remained in an unstable state the entire time.

Of course, Julius had, to the best of his ability, kept himself in check, restrained himself, and never let it show.
Self-restraint that others, hearing of it, might call an iron will—but it was nothing to be proud of.

The result of hiding his own unsteadiness, of deceiving his comrades and even himself, had been this unbroken string of defeats since arriving at this tower, culminating in that humiliating loss.

Julius should first have trusted others.
Flustered by the shock of having his very existence forgotten, pitying only himself for having been erased from another's world, he had overlooked the one thing he most needed to believe in.

Were the people Julius trusted, the people he'd sworn loyalty to, the people he'd entrusted his back to—were they the kind of people who would slight a Julius Juukulius who had, through no fault of his own, been erased from the world?

—He could say, without a shred of doubt: absolutely not.

Then there had only been one thing for Julius to do.
He should have pleaded sincerely, shown his affection himself. —Just as he had managed to do with his buds.

"A severed bond need only be tied anew. One who is no one can become anyone... I myself, of all people, was living proof of that!"

A commoner child who had been no one at all had become the most dashing knight this world had to offer.
Julius, who had likewise become no one, should also have been able to become anything at all.
And so—

"However many chances I am given, on that day I shall long again for a boy burning like flame, see my ideal again in a back that proclaimed itself a knight, and challenge you, the summit of the sword—!"

"Yappin' and yappin', you're givin' me a headache, you bastard!!"

At the strike that carried Julius's resolve on its blade's edge, Reid roared, meeting it with a strike of his own.
Battered from head to toe by the wind and pressure of that blade, Julius narrowed his yellow eyes in admiration.

This made the fourth time fighting Reid, counting the restart.
The first challenge and the defeat right after. Then the delaying action once the whole watchtower had come under threat, and now, this fourth time, after having renewed his contract with his buds.
Among all of these, this was the first time Reid had wielded any weapon besides his chopsticks—though whether chopsticks even qualified as a weapon was a matter of considerable doubt.
And now, with the Sword Saint, the summit of the sword, finally taking up a blade in earnest, Julius thought—

"His swordpower hasn't changed at all from when he used chopsticks...!"

"That's what I been tellin' ya. I ain't strong 'cause I can swing a sword. I'm strong for the simple reason that I'm just strong, that's it."

He caught the carelessly swung-down blow overhead, his knees creaking, and immediately a follow-up strike came from directly below. He barely parried it at the guard, and the impact sent Julius flying backward.
Reid didn't even need to lunge to close that distance—he simply followed at a walking pace.

One might suspect some special footwork technique, but there was nothing special about it at all.
He simply thought, *catch up to the guy who backed away*, and took a step—and in doing so effortlessly achieved footwork that entire schools of swordsmanship would take generations to develop. That was simply what Reid was.
As he himself had said, it was nothing more than his simply being an "existence" beyond all standard measure.

"Feelin' like cryin' yet?"

"—. No, my heart soars at the reality of challenging a legend!"

That wasn't bravado—Julius answered driven by genuine feeling.
Yes, that was exactly it. Standing before him was Reid Astrea. How many times had Julius's own heart raced, his eyes shone, longing over the legends of this man?
Having met the man himself, he had been surprised by his personality, certainly, but his strength was the ideal he'd longed for, made flesh.
And precisely because of that—just how wasteful had he been, all this time?

Even having had the chance to exchange words, to cross swords, to exchange philosophy and conviction—

"Ha."

Crossing swords with the "real thing," Julius let out a breath at a thought that suddenly crossed his mind.
It was wildly out of place, and yet the notion made his heart leap with anticipation—it was, frankly, delightful.

"What're you laughin' at?"

"No, I merely had a thought. —Once I've accomplished my purpose here and returned home, I intend to challenge my friend, Reinhard."

At Reid's question, Julius spoke the notion aloud.
Never once, until now, had Julius competed with Reinhard in swordsmanship. In fact, until circumstance placed them in opposing camps in the Royal Selection, he had never once thought to compete with him at anything.

—He had regretted never trying to stand as his equal.
That, too, was one of the reasons Julius had come to serve Anastasia and enter the Royal Selection.
But even without that regret, Julius would surely have been captivated by Anastasia's great vessel, would have wished to share her dream, and would have stood in the same place regardless.

If so, there had never been any need, from the very start, for pointless excuses or roundabout pretenses.
He should simply have taken up two wooden swords from the beginning and gone to Reinhard's side.

Once, in the past, Reinhard had crossed swords with the strongest swordsman of the Vollachian Empire.
On that day at the training grounds, when everyone had been swept up in the heat of that swordsmanship, Julius's own heart had burned as well.
That, in itself, had been the answer—

"Hah, don't know the name. Who's that, some nobody?"

"He is your descendant. And the Sword Saint of this age, and my friend."

"Kah! A brat's brat's practically a stranger by now. Wouldn't even recognize him if I passed him on the street."

Mixing kicks in with his swordwork, Reid snorted and spoke his irresponsible philosophy.
Julius, opening his mouth to offer a slight objection to that argument as their swords met—

"I'm gettin' sick of talkin' about other people already. You wanna have a nice chat with me or somethin'?"

"—. I won't deny it, but I'll deny it anyway."

"——"

"Given the time, I would gladly exchange words with you for two nights, three nights even. But right now, that time is precious to me. I'm being urged to hurry, as it were. And so—"

As the distance between them opened, Reid watched Julius and curled his lips upward. In his blue eyes was reflected the figure of Julius, glowing ever brighter.
The swirling six-colored light mingled together, gradually beginning to trace an aurora, a rainbow.
And then—

"—Al Krauzelia!!"

—The unleashed rainbow light swept the white world toward Reid.

Even Julius himself was taken aback by the power and range of the unleashed light.
It had become a spiritual art on a scale of magic so extraordinary that treating it as the same as before would practically be disrespectful—

"——"

Buds—no, having literally opened the talent once folded within them, he could no longer call them buds. Beautiful, lovely, valiant, dignified, radiant, having achieved a growth that felt almost divine, they were no longer buds but maidens.
Six of them, each displaying her own charm—to monopolize them, all by one man, might make him a sinner greater even than a Sin Archbishop.
And yet—

"Even should you forget me, I love you."

Chasing after the light he had unleashed, Julius stepped forward.
The rainbow light wrapped in six attributes broke through every defense and shattered its target. Faced with that rainbow radiance, an opponent had only two options—stand and receive it, or dodge.

"—Ha."

And by nature, Reid Astrea did not dodge rainbow light.
Against the rainbow light surging toward him head-on, Reid swung his powerful arm, cutting through the tip of the light with the Sword of Selection he held.
A "swordsmanship" of pure violence, relying on no special magic or divine protection—it wiped away, with a single swing, the full-powered great magic Julius had unleashed.

But Julius, pressing forward, had already accounted for that.
Leaping out from behind the vanishing light, Julius borrowed the strength of his sword, and of his maidens.

"Ia! Aro!"

At that instant, the red and green spirits who answered his call amplified each other's power, and wind woven with blazing flame swirled up, a scorching tornado erupting beneath Reid's feet.
Feeling that heatwave through the soles of his zōri, Reid escaped upward before he could be burned.
But the coordination of the spirit knight, of the "Sword Saint's Superior Knight," showed its true worth from here on.

"Kua! Iku!"

The yellow spirit raised a ridge in the white floor, pushing Julius's body upward. Simultaneously, the blue spirit's light froze the moisture in the air, hindering Reid's ascent as he leapt upward.
Clicking his tongue, in a superhuman feat of kicking off thin air, Reid reversed his posture, planted his feet upside-down on the ice ceiling that had formed midair, and glared down at Julius.
Strength gathered in Reid's knees—the counterattack against the ascending Julius was coming—

"In! Ness!"

At the instant the Sword Saint's counter closed in, the white and black spirits each interfered with the world in their own way.
White light lent strength to Julius's whole body; black light shaved away, even if only slightly, the strength of Julius's enemy. The small difference born in that instant would be the deciding factor in what followed.

"——"

The ice ceiling used as a foothold shattered, and Reid's figure blurred with speed as he shot forward.
The careless stance of the Sword of Selection was a kind of ultimate form that gave no hint of vertical, horizontal, or thrusting attack—but rather than feel threatened by it, Julius braced himself and met it head-on.

Meeting a blade with a blade only invites being knocked aside by the greater force.
And so Julius opened his eyes—the eyes that had always, ceaselessly, watched those above him, those ahead of him.

"——"

Killing all concept of sound and light, Reid's single stroke split the very space.
Let it be declared plainly: whether it was the Sword of Selection or a pair of chopsticks, everything that lay in the path of that stroke would, without exception, be cut down.
Because it was the very manifestation of the concept called "sword" itself.

A "sword" is a thing made to cut.
And swordsmanship is the general term for the techniques of cutting things with that sword.

If so, then a single stroke that cuts anything in this world is the ultimate essence of both "sword" and "swordsmanship."
Whatever is cut by it can never, for all eternity, forget the fact of having been cut.

Which is why the scar Julius Juukulius received beneath his left eye will never, for all eternity, disappear.
That was the price of dodging the Sword Saint's stroke at a distance close enough to graze him.

"——"

Abandoning any stance of receiving the blow, he read his opponent's sword in an instant's exchange.
Blood spurted from the cut socket of his eye. But his eye did not close. Fixing his gaze on his opponent, he swung his arm.
Abandoning defense, pouring everything into the offense—one stroke—

"—Al Clarista."

The greatest sword strike of Julius Juukulius's life traced a rainbow arc.
It caught Reid Astrea's savage, shark-like grin—

—The eyepatch covering the Sword Saint's left eye, pierced by the knight's blow, spun through the air.

"——"

When his toes touched down on the white floor, the sound of his own footsteps felt strangely, unbearably loud.
The instant he became aware of it, the heart that had forgotten to beat deep in Julius's chest hurried back into motion. Pushed by that surge of blood, he let out a deep breath.
Then he turned around, looking at the powerful back turned toward him.

"——"

His long red hair swayed; the back stood motionless.
The great man held the Sword of Selection in his right hand and pressed his left hand to his own face. And where that left hand searched was the place where an eyepatch should have been—but was there no longer.
The eyepatch that had covered the Sword Saint's left eye now lay fallen at Julius's feet.

"...It reached him?"

Looking down at the eyepatch fallen on the white floor and the knight's sword in his own hand, his voice trembled.
He murmured it as if to confirm what had happened, but it felt utterly unreal. It felt as though it might slip through the gaps of his fingers like a bubble of a dream and vanish somewhere, anywhere.
But—

"——"

With no concept of words, six lights praised the feat Julius had achieved.
A warm flower named affection bloomed, trying to fill the emptiness born in Julius's heart.
And along with the praise of those blossomed maidens—

"—Julius."

A faint voice reached him, and Julius's gaze turned toward it.
The great man with his back turned, the maidens conveying their praise—from a position different from either of those, someone had watched the fight from above and called Julius's name: a woman with pale purple hair.
An artificial spirit who had lived through the ages, who wore the same face as the precious lord to whom he'd sworn to devote this sword, with whom he had, without either of them knowing it, been caught in a relationship of mutual injury—

Whether she had shown Julius consideration out of care for Anastasia, or as a result of the Divine Protection of Gathering Spirits at work, he would not pry into the answer.
He only felt gratitude that the presence of someone bearing witness to Julius Juukulius's resolve had lightened his heart this much, and he raised his knight's sword.

"——"

Silently, he raised the knight's sword to the sky.
The lingering traces bearing the light of the aurora sparkled, and the sword's arc literally traced a rainbow.
It was as if, truly, it were blessing the knight named Julius Juukulius—

"—If this were some damn 'Trial,' you'd have won here."

Saying so, the great man slowly ground his zōri into the floor.
There was no wound on Reid's face as he turned around. The tip of Julius's blade had reached, correctly, only his eyepatch. But Reid was not so shameless as to claim it hadn't reached him at all.
More than that, what he'd just said wasn't sour grapes—it was simply the truth.

If this were a continuation of the "Trial" of the Pleiades Watchtower's second level, "Electra," then the moment that single strike landed, Julius would have earned qualification to pass and challenge the upper level.
But the fight between Julius and Reid was no longer a matter of the tower's "Trial."

It was a fight between one knight and one swordsman, between one man and another, to settle things between them.

"——"

Having lost his eyepatch, both eyes now open, Reid gripped the Sword of Selection with both hands.
He gripped the hilt with a forward grip and took a middle stance. —Yes, the "Sword Saint" had truly taken a stance.
Not swinging a stick carelessly, but properly holding the sword to cut down an enemy.

"Don't go complainin' if you disappear without a trace."

"Even if I wished to complain, it would be quite difficult without a mouth to do so with."

"Hah! Can't even laugh at a joke, this guy. What was your name again?"

Asked his name by a legend, Julius raised his eyebrows.
He must have given his name before this man countless times by now, and yet it hadn't stuck. But whether it had stuck or not didn't matter in the least.
Because that question told him, for the first time, that Reid had properly acknowledged Julius.

"Julius Juukulius. It's an easily-forgotten name, so I'd appreciate it if you'd remember it."

Voicing a joke he couldn't have laughed at not long ago, Julius, too, pointed the tip of his knight's sword at Reid.
And once more, he asked for the help of the six spirits who had just finished one job for him.
With himself and them as they were now, they could surely reach an even greater height of rainbow aurora.

Al Krauzelia and Al Clarista.
Borrowing the strength of six spirits, bundling the power of six attributes into a rainbow of aurora light—the great magic Krauzelia that unleashes it, and Clarista that lets it dwell within the knight's sword.
Beyond that lay a secret technique that, due to his own immaturity, he had never once succeeded at—

"—I'm coming."
"—Come, then."

At that instant, aurora light filled the white space, and a band of rainbow challenged the crimson giant.

The rainbow spiritual art Julius Juukulius had devised on his own, the secret of secrets.
Not unleashing the rainbow light bundled from six attributes, nor letting it dwell in the sword, but wrapping it around himself, becoming the aurora itself, a killing blow to strike down his opponent—

"—Al Cranvel."

That ultimate strike of the spirit knight was met head-on by a single white flash.

From beginning to end, it was too high a level of exchange for an amateur eye to follow.

Let alone the clashing sword strikes—the dizzying shifts of position and footwork, which side had the advantage and which the disadvantage, none of it could be grasped even by pale blue-green eyes.
That had nothing at all to do with the fact that this body wasn't originally her own.
It was simply that there were beings who competed for life and death on that plane of existence, and compared to them, the world she lived in was, simply, too low a plane.

If the value of a living thing lay in its strength as a creature, then she was worthless, weak beyond measure.
And that, too, was proof that she had spent hundreds of years idly, turning her back on the path of bettering herself.

From the moment she'd first become aware of herself, she'd had a premonition.
That the purpose of her own unnatural existence had been exhausted the very instant she was born.
If pressed, being born itself had been the purpose, and the purpose had been fulfilled at that very moment. And so she had been left alone, wandering the world without purpose, forced into a blank of hundreds of years.

She wouldn't have minded dying. But there had been no reason to die.
So she had dragged out the ending of her own story indefinitely, long, long, gorging herself on idleness.
And in that drawn-out lifetime, she had met a girl.

Captivated by the way that girl lived, so vividly, her chilled-through life had gained warmth.
What that girl, so small and yet uttering such impossible grand boasts, would become—or would fail to become—she had longed to see through to the end.

At some point, that mere curiosity had stopped mattering—

"—I don't want to lose you, or the children you hold dear, either."

She knew that the passage of time was both gentle and cruel.
Time heals wounds, but it also lets feelings grow stale.
Having lived so long, so very long, she thought, for the first time—*I don't want to make this a thing of the past.*

"Though that, too, is an unreasonable wish, I know."

However much she pleaded for it to stop, time did not stop, and moved ever onward.
Lives that were mortal, that ought to be small and fragile, showed every kind of change within that time.

As if to prove that the nameless knight—robbed of his "name," left behind by everyone's memory—was, indeed, one human being named Julius Juukulius.

—The knight wrapped in rainbow light dove straight into the white light.

Against Julius, who had unveiled his secret technique, Reid Astrea's action was terribly simple.
Swinging up the sword and bringing it down—the swordstroke most repeated, probably, in all this world—became a light that cleaved the world at a diagonal and annihilated everything in its path.

It was no special magic, no special technique.
Merely swinging a sword, and the world was scorched along with the light. It made no sense.
Was Reid Astrea simply beyond all standard, or was every single "Sword Saint" like this?
The one certain thing was—

"—Julius."

The wish to do everything in her power so the aurora would not lose against that unreasonable white light.
That was undeniably her true feeling—but to directly interfere in this battle would be, before even suicide, an act of tastelessness for which her soul could be shattered and she'd have no grounds to complain.
And she—Echidna—already knew that there was nothing she herself could do.

There was nothing she could do.
If there was anything she could do, here, for Julius Juukulius who had become one with the aurora—

"——"

She placed a hand on her thin chest, aware of the presence sleeping within.
This body's original owner, the reason she would not wake from her deep slumber—that was what Echidna had come to this sea of sand, to this tower, to seek. But that had been a deception.
Echidna already knew why she wasn't waking.

A girl who called herself greedy, who boasted she wanted to gain absolutely everything.
Once she took something into her arms, she would never let it go for anything—hating, above all else, to lose something or to let something be lost—and so there could only be one reason.

"Having handed her body over to me, and temporarily hidden yourself within your own Od, you're in a state untouched by outside interference. —Because an Od is a kind of world unto itself."

And she was shutting herself up there of her own will.
The reason was plain. —Step outside the Od, and she would be affected. Affected by the horrifying Authority of the Sin Archbishop of Gluttony.

Made to forget what she didn't want to forget, made to let go of what she didn't want to let go of.
Anastasia Hoshin would forget Julius Juukulius.
And so she— But—

"It seems everyone who came to this tower is a stubborn one through and through. —None of you have enough charm to just stay lost."

For nearly these two months, she'd tried imitating that girl's ways in her own fashion, but it was about time to move on.
And besides, she'd watched, from close by, both the good and the bad of him, this nameless knight fumbling through it all—so even if that girl forgot him, she could tell her about it herself.

"Ah, I see."

An artificial spirit created with no purpose, whose role had ended the moment she was made.
That was the role she'd thought she had, but as it turned out, it wasn't necessarily one that simply ended there.

A bridge between a precious girl and a precious knight, two people missing each other.
What a heavy responsibility that was.

Heavy enough that she could almost laugh, thinking she'd been born just for this.
And so—

"—Not watching your knight's coolest moment—now that would be a waste, and not like you at all, you cheapskate."

Even with all of his own strength, and the borrowed power of the six spirits under their renewed contract, he was still being pushed back.
He had unveiled the secret of secrets, while his opponent had simply, earnestly, in complete seriousness, swung a sword. —Truly, that unreasonable, standard-defying nature left him utterly exasperated.
And yet, at the same time, there was a part of his heart thrilled that it *had* to be this way—he, too, was beyond saving.

Swing a sword, and the world splits.
That was the ultimate technique of the "Sword Saint," one that also occurred when Reinhard swung his blade.
In the middle of this life-and-death struggle, Julius suddenly thought—

If Reinhard and Reid were to fight, which would be stronger, he wondered?
Legend against legend, Sword Saint against Sword Saint—a battle that would never happen, but which way would the verdict fall?
Unfortunately, the chance to find that out would probably never come.

"Then there's nothing for it but for me to confirm it with my own body."

If there was anyone who might get the chance to cross swords with both Reinhard van Astrea and Reid Astrea, it could only be among those who had reached this tower.
And of those, the only ones capable of it were Julius, and that woman, Emilia, who had gone on to the upper level. —He had no intention of handing that role to anyone else.
So all that remained was to win.

Push aside this white light, strike down Reid Astrea with rainbow light.
For that, with everything he had, one more step, more swordpower—

If even a shred more of pride and power could dwell in the "Superior Knight's" blade edge—

"—Julius."
