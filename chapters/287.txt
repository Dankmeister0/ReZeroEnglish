Arc 4 Chapter 118: "The Day the Heike Star Laughed"

Along a game trail surrounded by tall trees, Emilia advanced with easy, unhurried steps, familiar with the way.

She walked through the grass, pressed her feet into the earth, and took care not to step on the roots of the great trees or the flowers hidden here and there. Emilia thought it strange that she could feel the hard packed soil beneath the soles of her feet, even though this was supposed to be a dream.

Whatever an ordinary dream might be like, in this world she could feel the rough texture of tree bark, the sweetness of flower nectar's scent, and the warmth of the wind.

"How is it that I can feel all these things properly, even though this is a dream world?"

"'Dream world' is nothing more than a figure of speech. In truth, this ought to be called a separate world altogether — a space that has drawn in only your consciousness, in order to recreate the memories of the one undergoing the 'Trial.' Since it's your memories, as the challenger, that have been drawn out, it's only natural that you yourself have a sense of contact with the world. In exchange, no matter how much I touch the ground or the trees, I get no sensation from it at all."

"I see... What if I threw a huge fit and tore this whole forest to pieces?"

"A truly savage, witch-like notion. Indeed, you have the sensation of touch, but you cannot interfere with anything. What's more, you can't even make contact with the creatures recreated in this world. Though if this were the other form the 'Trial' can take, that would be possible."

"What do you mean, another form?"

"All you do is ask. You ought to learn to think for yourself, at least a little. Ask and you shall receive — though for someone who's lived her life leaning on others and currying favor, that may be asking too much from the start."

"Mmn..."

The 'Witch of Greed' followed behind Emilia, who walked ahead, keeping a fixed distance between them.
Echidna, lacing her lecture with poison, mocked Emilia's ignorance with an expressionless face. And yet, though it rankled her, what Echidna said was reasonable enough.

Emilia put a hand to her mouth and thought.
The difference Echidna had spoken of, between memories that could be touched and memories that couldn't. A way for Emilia — whose consciousness alone had been pulled into this place — to make contact with the people walking within those memories.

"I thought about it, but I don't get it. Tell me the answer."

"............."

"What's wrong? Does your stomach hurt?"

"That stomach-turning attitude of yours. Even if it's only displeasure, you're one of the very few — aside from him and his friends — capable of stirring this much feeling in me."

"Echidna, you have friends?"

At Emilia's murmur, carrying a faint note of envy, Echidna let out a sigh.
It seemed her words hadn't been taken in a very good light, and Emilia hesitated over how to rephrase them so they'd come across properly. Then—

"The regret one glimpses in the 'Trial' isn't always a single scene, depending on the person."

"Um?"

"There's the kind of past where a single moment is cut out, and that moment alone is what's regretted. Different from that, there's also the kind of past where something ongoing is regretted — a relationship with someone, for instance. In the latter case, rather than a single scene being cut out, what gets recreated is 'that person' as they exist within the one undergoing the 'Trial.' Exchanging words, making contact, even loving one another — all of it would be possible."

"...I see. So that's how it is."

Emilia nodded, satisfied with Echidna's explanation.
Indeed, 'regret' was the sort of thing that admitted such a distinction. Some people regret having quarreled with someone; others regret the time that slipped by while the quarrel went unresolved.
Which of the two needed overcoming was, in the end, a matter for the person themselves.

"But you go and properly answer the questions of someone who properly hates you."

"Do not, under any circumstances, mistake me for a sooo nice person. There is nothing more humiliating to me than being thought well of by you. That I end up answering when asked is simply a matter of my own disposition, nothing more."

"Uh-huh~"

She didn't feel like laughing, but she'd more or less learned how to get along with the distant Echidna.
There was no doubt Echidna loathed Emilia like a viper, but Emilia, for her part, couldn't bring herself to hate Echidna. She simply didn't know her well enough for that.
Conversely, that meant Echidna knew Emilia well enough to hate her this much — though the chance to ask about that wasn't going to come, at least not here.

"—Fufu! Ahaha! Over here! Over here!"
"Eep!"

Emilia, whose attention had drifted behind her, was startled by the sudden high voice of a girl.
A small girl swept past right beside her, circling around where she'd stopped. Emilia was shaken that she hadn't noticed the girl's presence even at such close range, but she quickly realized the cause wasn't her own carelessness or complacency.

The girl who'd passed her ran about, her silver hair — long enough to reach her back — tousled loose.
Violet-blue eyes, worn old children's clothes. Running fearlessly through the forest, laughing innocently — it was a face she knew well.
What stood there was herself as a small child — Emilia of days gone by, who had not yet come to know 'regret.'

"Even granting that she doesn't know anything yet, that's an exasperatingly carefree face."

"Don't go complaining about me even from back then. Besides... whether that's a bad thing or not is something we're about to find out."

Leaning against a tree, Echidna offered that thoroughly biased assessment of the frolicking young Emilia.
Emilia, voicing her objection to it, grimaced as she felt something like a numbness run through the temple of her consciousness-only self.

With her Contract to Puck severed, memories long sealed away were reviving one after another in Emilia's mind.
The days spent with Mother Fortuna. The group led by Jusu that had brought supplies into the settlement. The matter of the seal, and the "fairy" who had helped her sneak out of her princess room. And meeting Jusu, whom she was never supposed to meet, and becoming his friend.

"Why did I go on living as if it were only natural to forget all this..."

Even though her memory was riddled with holes here and there, Emilia had lived filling in those blanks as if nothing about it were strange.
What would have happened if, apart from the 'Trial,' she had noticed those holes and begun to doubt herself? It would have become irreversible. Because he understood that, perhaps Puck — who, more than anyone, must have known of the abnormality within Emilia — had said nothing at all.

The many memories now reviving still lay sleeping behind a half-opened door.
She hadn't been able to peer all the way through before taking on the 'Trial,' but even so, it was enough.

Now, within this 'Trial,' every memory Emilia had sealed away would surely be laid bare.
And she could well imagine that, upon seeing it, something within her would change decisively.

"But I'm not going to be afraid of it anymore."

"Crying, wailing, clinging to a father figure or some man. Are you giving up on the decision so fitting for a woman as unseemly as yourself?"

"I'm sure I'd be forgiven even if I did that... but I don't want to disappoint myself or Subaru by doing it. I don't want to be someone who, being weak, just brazens it out and stays weak."

"...Do as you please. All I'll do, at most, is keep one of the outcomes in mind."

No matter how much malice was layered on, it could not shake Emilia's resolve now.
Perhaps sensing that from their exchange so far, Echidna fell silent, her face resigned.

Once the Witch's meddling had spent its fury, Emilia let out a breath and turned her attention back to the past.
Before her, young Emilia dashed about with an innocent face. And then—

"Please wait, Emilia-sama. If you run about so, it's dangerous."

"It's not dangerous, I'm fine. Isn't it you who's going to skin your knee, Jusu?"

"It matters not how much I get hurt. Your injuries, Emilia-sama, are what matter. Should your fair skin be scratched, I could not atone for it even in death."

Chasing after the mischievously bounding Emilia was a tall man in swaying black vestments — Jusu. Upon his stern features he wore a clear gentleness and affection, mildly admonishing Emilia as she frolicked, heedless of his warnings.

"Jusu. The way you just said that sounds sooo indecent instead."

"I meant nothing of the sort... To look upon you in such a light, Emilia-sama — never, never."

The one who called out to Jusu was a woman following even further behind him as he chased Emilia — a sharp-eyed beauty sweeping back her short-cropped silver hair.
Seeing her, Emilia's breath caught in her throat, and—

"Mother Fortuna..."

At the sight of the woman who was as good as a mother to her, alive and well, Emilia couldn't hold back the welling urge to cry, even knowing this was only a memory.
She had loved her so much. Respected her more than anyone. Even now, Mother Fortuna was family just as precious to Emilia as Puck.

Fortuna came up beside the troubled-looking Jusu and looked up at his tall profile.

"Not just toward Emilia — toward anyone, surely. You're getting on in years yourself, Jusu."

"Age doesn't mean very much to me. In the sense of living through a very, very long span of time, even you, Fortuna-sama, and Emilia-sama, are but children to me."

"So I'm a child to you, Jusu... is that it."

At Jusu's answer, Fortuna lowered her eyes and muttered, sounding displeased.
Jusu knit his brows in consternation at her attitude, but Fortuna didn't respond. Instead, Emilia came pattering back, puffing out her cheeks at the two of them for not chasing after her.

"Geez! Mother Fortuna, Jusu, why won't you chase me! We're in the middle of a game of tag, aren't we! You can't just stop!"

"Ah! My apologies, Emilia-sama. This Romanée-Conti has committed the blunder of a lifetime..."

"Don't you go spoiling her like that, Jusu. — Emilia, you haven't forgotten why Mother and Jusu ended up chasing you in the first place, have you? Mother reeeally dislikes children who don't reflect on what they've done, you know?"

"Myu!"

At Fortuna, whose smile hid a trace of anger within it, young Emilia's shoulders jumped.
She'd remembered the reason she was being chased, and realized she'd stirred up trouble for herself. Turning pale, Emilia laughed an evasive "ehehe" and spun around to run off—

"Too bad. Emilia's been caught by Mother Fortuna."

"Fwah! I'm sorry, Mother Fortuna! It's not — it's not what you think. The fairy said to come play, to go outside, so..."

"Mother also dislikes children who blame the fairy instead of themselves. Do you understand, Emilia?"

Fortuna's voice was almost a whisper against young Emilia, who flailed in a panic at being embraced from behind. The struggling girl stopped thrashing and drooped, crestfallen.

"I'm sorry, Mother Fortuna. It's boring staying in my room, and Jusu and I are already friends, so I wanted to see him, and I snuck out."

"And then, once Mother found you, you ran away, didn't you? That means you knew you'd done something wrong. That's a sooo bad thing to do."

"Yes..."

"Breaking a promise is a bad thing. Keeping a promise is important. A promise is the shape trust takes, so breaking one means betraying the feelings of someone who trusted you. That's why it's no good."

Fortuna spoke to young Emilia, who looked about to cry. Cupping the girl's face in both hands as she tried to look away, she made sure their violet-blue eyes met firmly.

"Emilia, promise Mother. Promise that from now on, you'll keep your promises properly."

"Mm... yes, I will. I'm sorry, Mother."

"Good. Then that's fine."

Hearing the teary-eyed vow, Fortuna pulled her beloved daughter into her arms.
Gently stroking the silver hair of Emilia, who let out little hiccuping sobs, Fortuna accepted her child's growth of heart with a soft, gentle breath. Then—

"Jusu? What are you fussing about over there?"

"I— I just... th-that sight was too dazzling for me... I couldn't hold back my tears...!"

Before an exasperated Fortuna, Jusu crouched in the shade of a tree, pressing a handkerchief to his face, sobbing loudly. It seemed the exchange between mother and daughter had overwhelmed him with emotion.
Seeing Jusu cry, both within the revived memory and within the 'Trial,' Emilia felt she could remember that he'd always been quick to tears, and warmth spread through her chest.

"By the way, Emilia. This 'fairy'..."

Leaving Jusu, who had begun blowing his nose on a tissue, to himself, Fortuna picked out the detail that had caught her attention in young Emilia's earlier account. When the subject of the "fairy" came up, young Emilia lifted her face from Fortuna's arms and, still red-eyed, smiled and said, "Ah, that's..."

"Come here, fairy."

Young Emilia held out her hand, speaking to the world.
Then, as though using her pale little fingertips as a perch, several faint, newborn lights began to gather around her, swaying.
Seeing this, both Fortuna and Jusu looked on in surprise.

"Don't tell me — minor spirits... and this many... how?"

"...? I talk to them, and lots of them come. While I was playing alone in my princess room, they started coming to me."

"To command this many minor spirits at such a young age... it seems Emilia-sama has an extraordinary aptitude as a Spirit Arts user."

"Aptitude... as a Spirit Arts user?"

Having finished his fit of emotion, Jusu had returned to his usual manner. When young Emilia tilted her head at his words, he nodded with a gentle smile, "Yes."

"Those you call fairies, Emilia-sama, are beings known as minor spirits. There are those who commune with these spirits — found everywhere throughout the world — exchange words with them, and form Contracts. Those beloved of spirits, who accomplish the extraordinary by borrowing their power, are called Spirit Arts users."

"Can I become one of those?"

"Yes. If you simply continue to grow up healthy, and remain the sort of person the spirits love... surely you'll come to command many spirits, and even more powerful ones besides."

At Jusu's explanation, young Emilia's face lit up brightly.
But Fortuna, who'd been listening, stood and elbowed Jusu in the side.

"Hey now, Jusu. Don't go saying unnecessary things. Just because she's got a few minor spirits following her doesn't make her a Spirit Arts user... Emilia doesn't need any of that."

"You say that, Fortuna-sama, but Emilia-sama won't remain a child forever. There may come a time when you cannot be at her side. When that day comes, I believe this will be necessary so she may look after herself."

Fortuna and Jusu argued back and forth over how Emilia ought to be raised.
Watching the exchange from the side, Emilia couldn't help but think something.

"Somehow, Mother Fortuna and Jusu are kind of like a daddy and a mommy."

"Wha—!?"

The very thing present-day Emilia had been thinking, young Emilia said aloud with an innocent face.
Watching Fortuna's face flush red, Emilia found herself thinking, of course my past self thought the same thing.

"Now listen here, don't say strange things, Emilia. Mother and Jusu have known each other for a very, very long time — we're not the sort of relationship that ought to be described that way."

"Just so, Emilia-sama. Fortuna-sama and I have known each other a very long time — indeed, since back when your father and mother were still together..."

"—Jusu."

Fortuna, who had been desperately explaining herself, called out to Jusu in a low voice after his slip of the tongue. At that voice, Jusu, his face registering he'd realized his blunder, put a hand to his mouth and apologized, "I'm terribly sorry."

"My father, and mother...?"

"I'm sorry, Emilia. We'll talk about that another time. More importantly, it's about time you went properly back to your room. That's not to say I'm forgiving you for sneaking out on your own in the first place."

"Mmn... Mother Fortuna's being mean..."

Feeling the topic had been dodged, young Emilia puffed out her cheeks in protest. But Fortuna, apparently having no intention of tolerating that stubbornness, pressed both hands to the girl's puffed cheeks and let the air out of them.
As young Emilia let out a "pfft" of breath, her cheeks deflating, Fortuna met her eyes.

"Be a good girl and behave. This isn't the last time you'll see Jusu, either. I'll... make sure there's another chance for that."

"Really? Will you promise? You won't betray me?"

"Oh, this child. Wherever does she pick up this kind of clever reasoning."

Fortuna gave a wry smile at young Emilia bringing up their earlier talk of promises, and gently embraced her.

"Yes, a promise. A sooo important promise, between Mother and Emilia."

"...Okay, then. I'll go back to my room."

Because Fortuna had promised so firmly, young Emilia believed her and nodded.
Once released from the embrace, young Emilia ran up to Jusu before heading back to her princess room. She reached her hand up to Jusu, who looked down at her, and smiled.

"See you, Jusu. A promise, so we'll meet again."

"—Yes, without fail. We shall meet again. I look forward to that time."

Jusu took the small extended hand and shook it.
Her smile answered with a smile, young Emilia nodded again and again, then let go and said her farewell.

And with that, young Emilia turned to head back to her princess room—

"It seems he's arrived."

Echidna, who until then had watched the past unfold in silence, murmured quietly.
Her voice, oddly clear, made Emilia look up, and she turned her head to see what the words meant — and found it.

"——"

It was a white young man.
White hair, pale skin. An unadorned figure in a plain shirt and trousers. His features were tidy enough, but his appearance was mediocre, lacking anything that particularly stood out.
He was a lump of characterlessness who would vanish instantly into any crowd — yet here, now, in this place, he felt like a foreign element possessed of an uncanny presence.

"...Who are you!?"

Within the memory, Fortuna too noticed the young man watching them, and instantly pulled young Emilia close, her voice openly wary.
At her voice, the young man, his hand resting on the trunk of a great tree, brushed back his white hair.

"When you ask someone their name, isn't it only proper to give your own first?"

At that reply, Fortuna's brow twitched.
Seeing it, the young man twisted his mouth into a shape that carried a faint sense of something grim.

"I do think it's a rather trite thing to say when someone asks 'who are you,' but now that I actually find myself in that kind of situation, ah, I understand the urge to say it. This is the first time either of us has laid eyes on the other. In the sense that we're about to build a relationship from here, we ought without question to stand as equals — so why is it I'm being unilaterally interrogated for my name, looked down on from above? I wonder if you're even aware of that. That unconsciously, thoughtlessly, entirely on your own, you've gone and treated me with contempt."

"...For a man, you certainly do seem to like long-winded talk."

"'For a man' — that phrase, too, betrays the prejudice of someone who doesn't seem to know much about men worth comparing. In the first place, what right do you have to measure me against creatures called 'men,' of whom there are countless numbers the world over? That attitude of yours is... honestly, a little hard to overlook. It shows a severe lack of courtesy. It treats me, as an individual — my rights — with contempt."

With every word Fortuna spoke, the young man's manner of speaking seemed to grow more unhinged.
Sensing at last a truly dangerous opponent, Fortuna braced herself, openly wary. But the one who stopped her was Jusu, standing beside her.
He looked up at the white young man and spoke, his expression stern.

"Regulus Corneas! Why are you here!? It was firmly agreed that no one but myself would be involved in this matter!"

"Firmly agreed, my foot — that was an arrangement you unilaterally proposed and unilaterally decided on, wasn't it? Trying to force others to obey your presumptuous say-so — a mere spirit sure has started running its mouth like it's something grand. Restricting my actions in the first place is unfaithful conduct that shouldn't be tolerated at all... and that's a spirit for you? Would you kindly stop violating both my body and my mind?"

"That doesn't answer my question! If you dislike the arrangement, then it's a matter to raise at the church! What have you come here to do!? To begin with, who told you of this place—"

"—That, would be by my instruction."

Against the young man — Regulus — who spoke petulantly, Jusu's own voice trembled with anger.
But cutting into the argument between the two of them was the voice of a woman — one that had not once stirred the air until now.

At that voice, everyone watching the scene reacted, each in their own way.
Jusu's eyes filled with a shudder of dread, Fortuna's eyes burned with fury, young Emilia shook her head with tears in her eyes within her mother's arms, and Regulus formed an ominous smile.

Emilia, glimpsing the memory, caught her breath at the presence behind that voice, while Echidna simply closed her eyes.


A girl came forward.
The figure standing beside Regulus, looking down on young Emilia and the other two, was a girl so beautiful that anyone who beheld her would tremble.

Her long, translucent platinum hair shone with a sweetness, as though sunlight pouring down had been given form, flowing over her slender shoulders and down her back.
Her eyes, framed by long lashes, were a deep blue as though the whole world had been shut up within them, and her too-perfect features held an exquisite, precise beauty that even a god might hesitate to touch.
Her small frame was so delicate that even being embraced by the wind seemed precarious. A single sheet of white cloth was all that wrapped that body, and there was an air about her that made one believe the world itself refused to let anything else touch her bare skin.

A presence that was no ordinary thing, a bearing that could never belong to an ordinary being.
The voice she spun out held a magic-like charm that bound the body and heart of anyone who heard it, and in that sudden moment, not one person present could find further words.

"Whatever is the matter, Bishop Petelgeuse Romanée-Conti?"

The girl tilted her head as she asked.
To be looked at, to be spoken to. Simply having a single one of the girl's actions turned upon oneself, one could not help but be overtaken, from head to toe, by a euphoria one might gladly die for.
Looking up at the girl's presence, Emilia felt her mouth going rapidly dry, even knowing full well this was only the past.

*That thing is dangerous.*

"Why are you here...? No, wait — Regulus Corneas...! Why have you brought this person out here...!"

Jusu's voice, gritted through clenched teeth, as though rejecting the emotion swelling within himself.

*That thing is far too dangerous.*

"'Brought out,' as if I, of all people, would do something so infringing on someone's will? She came along entirely of her own volition. Trying to pin absolutely everything on me is, frankly, taking prejudice too far. I'd appreciate it if you didn't go around passing judgment on me, a fellow human being, all on your own."

"Bishop Regulus. He too is confused. Please, don't be too hard on him."

The girl beside him gently reproved Regulus for his answer to Jusu.
At her words, the corner of Regulus's mouth trembled, and he bent at the waist in a reverent bow, straining desperately not to let his happiness show.

It was abnormal.
Regulus, that overwhelming foreign element — the fact that he could be made to obey her so utterly, so completely at her whim, already made the girl's own abnormality plain beyond any doubt.

Looking up at her, Jusu shook his head, his eyes still trembling with confusion and shock.

"This is... far, far too cruel... Pandora-sama..."

At Jusu's voice, which seemed to escape him like a released breath, the girl gave a faint smile.
A smile that brought a happiness even greater than being blessed by the world itself. Pandora, as she was called, answered the gazes turned upon her from all around with an all-forgiving, all-embracing warmth.
She spread both hands wide, as though to embrace everything within those small arms.

"Now then, shall we begin? — To fulfill the true purpose of we of the Witch Cult."

"Pandoraaaaaa——!!"

Fortuna's fierce scream overlapped with the girl's calm declaration.
Shielding young Emilia behind her, Fortuna thrust both hands forward, and a blue Magic Circle unfurled before her. Ice stakes, manifesting with tremendous force and overwhelming density, hurtled straight at Pandora.

"Oh my."

"Get skewered, and beg my brothers' forgiveness!!"

Fortuna's magic assailed the vacant-faced Pandora, a hand raised absently to her mouth.
Ice stakes, each as thick as an adult's arm, nearly twenty of them in all — generated at an unbroken pace and launched one after another — pierced the vacant girl's body, white steam bursting outward.

The chain of shattering ice never let up, and even as white smoke billowed thicker and thicker, Fortuna didn't ease her assault. Before young Emilia's gaping mouth, Fortuna, her beautiful profile twisted with blazing fury, raised both hands.

"Take thiiis——!!"

As though obeying her hands swinging downward, a massive block of ice fell, mowing down the forest's trees from above. It struck true, crushing the spot where Pandora had stood straight from overhead — a white annihilation carving a gravestone into the forest floor.

At Fortuna's tremendous magical power, even present-day Emilia found herself speechless.
Could even Emilia, borrowing Puck's power, weave magic on that scale? She had never once looked down on her mother, but she shuddered to find her ability had exceeded even what she remembered.

But—

"You know... just now, I was completely out of your mind, wasn't I? And even though I was out of your mind, you still went and attacked in a way that would catch me in it — how does that work, exactly? That means, doesn't it... that you trampled on my life, my existence, my rights?"

Right after that long-winded grievance was heard, the huge block of ice shattered to pieces from below.
Amid the fantastical sight of powdered ice crystals scattering across the forest sky, Regulus stood there unruffled, his figure utterly out of place. And beside him, Pandora, likewise unharmed.

Regulus gave his jacket a light brush with his hand, and despite having taken that much of an attack, he hadn't so much as a scratch, let alone a smudge on his clothes. Pandora was straightening her bangs, only slightly mussed by the wind pressure, with her hand.
Presumably Regulus had stood in front of Pandora and shielded her, but it was all far too abnormal. Emilia had no idea what had actually happened.

"So that is the present Greed. Seeing it with my own eyes like this — quite the improbable encounter, when you think of it. There's something interesting in that."

"Did you understand what just happened?"

Emilia called out to Echidna, who had left the shade of the tree for a spot with a better view. Echidna glanced sidelong at her and narrowed her eyes slightly.

"I can guess, but I'm far from certain. I'd like to say that if we watched a little longer we'd know how it turns out, but... it seems the situation won't allow for that."

"What do you—"

"It's moving."

At Echidna's clipped words, Emilia looked ahead, a feeling of frustration rising in her.

Despite Fortuna having fired the first shot, the battle of the past had come to nothing.
Seeing sullen-faced Regulus take a step forward, Jusu extended his arm.

"Fortuna-sama, please take Emilia-sama and fall back! Against Regulus Corneas, we stand far too powerless as things are!"

"What...!? You're telling me to fall back, with that woman standing right there!?"

"Please, think of the situation! Who is it that you're protecting right now!"

"——!"

Jusu's sharp rebuke struck Fortuna as she braced for all-out resistance. Her cheeks stiffening in shock, Fortuna looked behind her, where young Emilia stood clutching the hem of her mother's clothes with an anxious face.

"M-mother..."

"Emilia——!"

"Please fall back. And send for aid from the village at once. The believers who came with me share my convictions. They will surely be of help to you."

Jusu spoke in a calm voice to Fortuna, who drew the clinging young Emilia into her chest. Hearing this, Fortuna rose to her feet with Emilia in her arms and looked at Jusu with anxious eyes.

"And if we do that, what will you do?"

"—Please, rest assured. I don't intend to remain behind without a plan."

To Fortuna's troubled gaze, Jusu answered with a smile, tension showing through it all the same.
Seeing his smile, Fortuna shut her eyes as if to tear herself away, and—

"I will come back to help you, without fail."

Leaving only those words behind, Fortuna, holding young Emilia, ran off into the forest.
Struggling in her arms, young Emilia poked her face out beside Fortuna's head,

"Jusu——!!"

"——"

At young Emilia's cry, Jusu turned back and raised a hand, his expression somehow relieved.
After that, as Fortuna and young Emilia dashed deeper into the forest, Jusu's figure vanished from sight.

"...That's strange. I was carried off like that, so I shouldn't have seen what happens next."

"Don't underestimate the world of memory I've constructed. It's rooted in your memories, but the construction draws on my own arts and on what's recorded in the Book of Wisdom. Filling in, to a degree, scenes your consciousness never saw is easy enough. Although..."

Echidna answered Emilia's question. Beside the bewildered girl, she followed the departed Fortuna and the others with her eyes.

"In terms of overcoming your 'Trial,' there's no doubt that following them would be the correct choice. What do you want to do? Will you stay and see how things unfold here?"

Echidna's unspoken message was that Emilia ought to follow Fortuna. Her words were logically correct. Indeed, since the 'Trial' concerned Emilia's own past, what ought to take priority here was what young Emilia would go on to see and do afterward. And yet—

"Echidna... somehow, that sounded like you wanted me to go over there just now."

"............."

"I don't think... I'm overthinking it. The way you said that, and how you acted — it was strange."

"...Think whatever you like. Besides — this side is about to move too."

Without answering Emilia's question, Echidna, her expression blank, stepped back slightly. Perhaps she withdrew to avoid being caught in the aftershocks of the battle about to begin.
No matter how much devastation occurred, it wouldn't touch Emilia or Echidna directly. Even so, should the terrain itself change, there'd be no avoiding some effect on their footing.

"Quite the fine display, Petelgeuse. But say, who gave you leave to go and do a thing like that? What do you suppose I came here to do, anyway? However you look at it, my business isn't with you — it's with her, over there. So getting in my way like this means obstructing what I ought to do. It's a violation of my rights."

"Say what you like, Regulus Corneas. But I, on my very existence, will not let you advance one step further than this!"

"Big talk. From you, of all people — one of the founders of the Witch Cult or whatever, sitting in that seat of yours for no better reason than some minor contribution you made once, long ago. How could you possibly have the confidence to stand against me — someone rightfully chosen for this rank — and win?"

"That... I shall show you, starting now."

Jusu answered quietly as Regulus's anger mounted on the strength of his self-serving logic.
Reaching a hand into his vestments, his expression was one of resolve — to Emilia, it looked exactly like the face of someone resolved to die.

"No... Jusu, what are you doing...!?"

Through reliving the past, Emilia's affection for him had come flooding back to mind.
At the sight of him in a state dire enough to make him ready to die, Emilia instinctively reached out to stop him. But there was no way for the present Emilia to interfere in the story of the past.
Her outstretched hand passed straight through; the palm that should have clasped his in childhood never made contact.

"That is..."

The small black box Jusu drew from within his robes.
Regulus knit his brow at the sight of it, but his eyes soon went wide, as if he'd realized what it was. Jusu pierced the young man — showing surprise on his face for perhaps the first time — with eyes charged with resolve.

"You should be able to feel it. You, too, once held this in your hands."

"Of course I know. And precisely because I know, I'm at a loss for words at just how stupid you are. Maybe you thought you were keeping it hidden as some kind of trump card, but the very fact that you're able to keep it on your person at all — why can't you see it? That you! Have no right to hold that thing! That's a decision made by the thing itself, and no one else!"

"...Indeed, I am not compatible with it. That is why, all this time, I have merely held what was entrusted to me. But that, too, was for a moment such as this."

"Bishop Petelgeuse Romanée-Conti."

Jusu returned a quiet answer to the enraged Regulus. Into the exchange between the two of them cut Pandora, who had not moved from her original spot.
When Jusu, hearing his name called, raised his face, Pandora, her expression serene, said—

"A good journey to you."

"——"

It was a blessing, plain and simple — free of malice, ill will, hostility, or any ulterior motive whatsoever. And that was precisely why Emilia, hearing it, couldn't stop the chill running through her, and Jusu felt exactly the same.
Jusu bore the pain with an expression as though that blessing had just cut his whole body to ribbons, and then turned the small box in his hand and removed its lid.
Inside the box that fit in his palm was a black, writhing "Something."

"Forgive me. Flugel-sama."

So saying, Jusu pressed the black "Something," box and all, against his own chest.
In the next instant, the "Something" burst across the surface of Jusu's flesh where it touched, splattering like water, and swelled explosively in volume to engulf his entire body.
At the sight — exactly like some viscous creature swallowing him whole — Emilia let out a voiceless scream. The "Something" covered Jusu's body and constricted around it.

"What a fool."

Spitting the words out, Regulus, for the first time, offered a brief, curt verdict.
At the end of his contemptuous gaze, Jusu, his whole body swallowed by the "Something," raised both hands to the heavens, mouth open in a scream — as though his very being were being churned up by some emotion other than pain, other than pleasure, something else entirely.

"——"

Suddenly, a strange sound mixed into the screaming. It was the sound of someone clapping.

"Magnificent."

Murmuring the word and offering applause was the platinum-haired girl, Pandora.
Watching Jusu, still gasping as he was swallowed by the torrent of emotion, her cheeks flushed with rapture.
Her breath came slightly quickened, for no reason other than that the sight excited her.

"Pandora-sama?"

It wasn't only Emilia who found her behavior strange — Regulus did too.
The white-haired young man frowned at Pandora's clapping. Pandora then turned to him, cheeks still flushed, stopped her applause, and pointed at Jusu.

"Bishop Regulus Corneas."

"Yes."

"Here it comes."

An instant later, Regulus's body was suddenly flipped upside down and hurled high into the sky.

"Huh——?"

It was a violence crude as a child's, like grabbing a doll by the leg and flinging it roughly into the air.
Even Regulus himself, thrown as he was, seemed not to understand what had happened, letting out a hollow sound as his body reached the peak of its arc — only to plummet rapidly straight back to the ground. Clearly exceeding the speed of free fall, it looked exactly as though he'd been hurled down "while still gripped by the leg."

Utterly helpless, Regulus was slammed headfirst into the ground.
The earth burst apart with a thunderous roar and a shuddering tremor, and trees caught up in the shockwave toppled one after another toward the point of impact. Buried beneath the follow-up shock, Regulus lay pinned, and silence fell over the forest.

Emilia lost her voice, her thoughts blank as she scrambled to trace back what had just happened.
She hadn't seen a single part of it clearly. But if there was one thing, and only one, that she understood, it was—

"I did say so... DESU."

The man in black vestments, kneeling there, gazed ahead as he wept tears of blood.
Glaring through gaps in the trees thick with billowing dust, breathing hard, was a man who had won his gamble in exchange for his resolve.

Freed from the agony of being swallowed by the black "Something," he rose to his feet.

Jusu — no. That man was Petelgeuse Romanée-Conti.



"Those two, I won't let anyone chase after... Past this point, I absolutely will not let you through — DESU!!"
