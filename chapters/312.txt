One Day II Part 5: "Annerose's Trap and the Sibling Relationship"

Was it really wise to go along so obediently with the schemes of a nine-year-old?
Subaru eyed the small back leading the way down the hallway, frustrated with himself for failing to come up with a better plan.

—Annerose Miload.

Being a branch family of House Mathers, Roswaal's own house of birth, she shared many physical traits with him. Most notable, perhaps, were her deep indigo hair and blue eyes.

Her long hair was braided and wound in a coil around her head, and since Subaru didn't know the name for that hairstyle, he privately dubbed her the "Braid Loli."

She was a girl possessed of a crisp intelligence unbecoming a nine-year-old, and her sharp eye for things and quick wit certainly spoke to her being Roswaal's kin. But the moment her "Roswaal-ness" showed through most clearly was none other than this—

"Emily, might I hold your hand for a moment?"

"Eh? Sure, that's fine, Anne."

"Then I shan't hold back. By the way, Emily, might I hug you for a moment?"

"Eh? Sure, that's fine, Anne."

"Then I shan't hold back. By the way, Emily, if you could just carry me like this..."

"That's enough of that."

Subaru pulled Annerose—hand held, hug given, and now trying to get herself picked up—away from Emilia.

Emilia blinked in surprise, while Annerose, showing no sign of disappointment, brushed off her knees and then theatrically dusted off the shoulder Subaru had touched.

"How terribly uncouth of you, Subaru, keeping apart two people who both wish to touch one another."

"Where'd you pull the statistics for 'both wish to' from? Looked to me like a survey of exactly one Miload walking around asking herself questions."

"Unable to overlook a child's cute little mischief—it says a lot about the size of your Vessel, Subaru."

"If it actually looked like cute little mischief, I wouldn't be saying a word!"

Annerose, with an air of perfect composure, tried to justify her own conduct. And yet even as she spoke, she was looking for any opening to take Emilia's hand again—there was no letting one's guard down.

Annerose: kin to Roswaal, and possessed of a precociousness quite unlike a nine-year-old's.

Her most extreme trait: for whatever reason, she liked Emilia far too much.

Ever since the day it was decided they'd be staying at this mansion and they'd exchanged greetings, Annerose had shown an inordinate amount of affection toward Emilia. Emilia, guileless and utterly incapable of suspecting anyone, seemed to take it as an adorable display of fondness. Subaru did not.

After all, she was Roswaal's relative. Even though the truth behind his supposed "Demi-Human fetish" had been overturned by his actual thing for Echidna, that didn't necessarily mean his relatives were any different.

In fact, many of the servants working in House Miload were Demi-Humans. Apparently, these were Demi-Humans Roswaal had gathered from all over, people who'd experienced persecution, and by Roswaal's own design, House Miload had become something of a sanctuary for Demi-Humans with that kind of past.

For Annerose, born and raised in such a household, the presence of Demi-Humans was simply a familiar part of life. As a result, she held no prejudice toward Emilia as a Half-Elf either—but her friendliness went well past reasonable bounds.

To put it plainly, Subaru was determined not to let Annerose steal Emilia away.

And Annerose, in turn, was determined not to let Subaru monopolize Emilia.

In other words, the two of them were rivals in love over Emilia.

Or so it seemed, at least.

"Honestly, Subaru. I don't know what's got you so worked up, but getting mad at Anne like that when she's still so little—it's not very grown-up of you."

"'Worked up,' huh—don't hear that one much these days... Anyway, that's not it, Emilia-tan. To be specific, the look in Annerose's eyes isn't the kind you can just wave off as 'she's still a little kid'—"

"No excuses! Sorry, Anne. Subaru's still a little on edge, staying in someone else's mansion and all..."

"Don't talk about me like I'm some pet you dropped off who still hasn't settled in!"

Since their stances toward Annerose were so different, Subaru and Emilia's arguments about her attitude never went anywhere—always two parallel lines.

Why couldn't Emilia notice the way Annerose's gaze practically dripped?

"It's probably that only someone who has ulterior motives toward Emilia-tan themselves can spot someone else with the same ulterior motives...!"

"Emily. Subaru just confessed he lusts after you. How filthy."

"Your way of putting it is what's filthy! Are you seriously nine years old!?"

Unlike Beatrice, whose lack of charm was only skin-deep, Annerose's lack of charm went all the way through. Take that one word, "filthy"—coming from Beatrice it would've just been sass, but from Annerose it somehow carried real conviction.

"Honestly, why can't the two of you get along? It's a real mystery..."

"That's because both of us... about Emilia-tan..."

"About me?"

"—Gnh, guh."

Faced with Emilia's tilted head, Subaru couldn't bring himself to finish the sentence.

It was, of course, feelings he'd already confessed any number of times, but somehow, saying it out loud in front of others over and over made it feel increasingly cheap. And blurting it out now, on impulse, at a moment he hadn't even meant to—that was cheap plus embarrassing.

At the edge of his vision, he caught Annerose smiling triumphantly.

"Well, I suppose that's enough teasing Subaru for now. And here we are at my room already—let's continue this conversation inside."

Annerose said this to Subaru, whose face had gone red under Emilia's innocent gaze.

He realized that, without noticing, they'd walked the length of the hallway and now stood before an overly ornate door, just as the girl had said. Annerose's private chambers—Emilia had apparently been invited many times before, but this was Subaru's first time coming this far.

Taking Emilia's hand, Annerose moved naturally to usher her into the room. But Subaru put a stop to that.

"Wait. Something about your room feels suspicious, so I'm going in first."

"—Hmph. Very well, go ahead. Do as you like."

That brief pause before her answer was a little concerning, but Annerose yielded the way to Subaru with nothing more than a sigh. Hand on the doorknob, Subaru entered the room, feeling somehow a bit tense.

And then—

"We have been awaiting you, Master Natsuki. Tea and accompaniments have been prepared. Please, take a seat and make yourself comfortable. Pleasant conversation."

He was greeted by Clind, bowing politely in the posture of a butler welcoming a guest.

Stunned, Subaru turned back around, and found Annerose wearing an expression of perfect nonchalance, as if this were only natural.

"Huh, Clind? Didn't you go to the dining hall with Beatrice and Petra just now?"

"As Lady Emilia says, so I did. However, partway there, it seemed the young mistress had decided upon a tea party in her room, and so I, Clind, made preparations beforehand. In great haste."

"I see, so Anne said something like that."

"Indeed, the young mistress 'thought' as much. Keen insight."

Emilia peeked her face in beside the frozen Subaru and exchanged these words with Clind inside. But there seemed to be a subtle discrepancy in their exchange.

He'd said "thought," not "was told," hadn't he.

"If you try to make sense of Clind's peculiarities with reason, you'll lose your mind. It's mentally easier to simply accept that he's just that sort of being."

"I always endeavor to stay one step ahead of merely coming when called. My grand ambition."

Was that really something you could manage through sheer effort of will?

Subaru couldn't help but wonder, but since both Annerose and Emilia sat down without a second thought, he joined the tea party too, still scratching his head inwardly.

"Now then, Clind's tea is ready, so let us continue where we left off."

"The repair of the relationship between Frederica and Master Garfiel. The matchmaker."

"Clind, are you perhaps the type of butler who's actually several people?"

"Surely one Lady Ryuzu is more than enough for that sort of thing. A stale rehash."

Perhaps judging that the conversation wouldn't progress if he kept joining in, Clind, having prepared tea and sweets for everyone, straightened his spine at the edge of the room and stopped moving entirely, like a statue.

His gaze bored straight into Annerose, but the young mistress deflected it as though well used to it, and said—

"As Clind just said, the early repair of Frederica and Garfiel's sibling relationship is our shared concern... shall I assume that's correct?"

"Yes, that's right. We want to do something about it too, and we've been racking our brains, but we just can't come up with a good idea. We're at our wits' end."

"Even flustered, you're adorable, Emily. —And so, having gone around asking everyone in the mansion involved, and hitting a dead end, you've come to me."

"Don't slip your real thoughts in there like some subliminal message."

Annerose, wearing an utterly unbothered expression, let Subaru's jab roll right off her.

In any case, she seemed to already grasp their current situation, which saved them the trouble of explaining.

"Still, what's gotten into you, wanting to help those two make up? Everyone else just keeps saying 'time will solve it, time will solve it.'"

"Isn't that tendency stronger in people who are used to giving up, or to waiting? The people the two of you went around asking were, without meaning any offense, largely that type, weren't they?"

"You don't mince words, do you... Well, I did ask Otto too."

"Then let me add 'a man unacquainted with success' to his file as well."

"That's brutal!"

The sadness of it, that even Annerose, who'd known him barely a week, had formed that impression of Otto.

Though the fact that Subaru couldn't quite bring himself to deny it—that was best left unsaid.

"'Time will solve it'—I won't deny that either. The ten-year gulf lying between those two... give it the same amount of time again, and it will surely resolve itself. But ten years is far too long. Ten years is about how long it took my father and mother just to kiss."

"Hmmm?"

In the middle of a remark quite unbecoming a nine-year-old, she'd suddenly arrived at a conclusion that was very nine-year-old indeed.

As Subaru groaned, unable to keep up with the sudden downshift, Clind, beyond Annerose, raised a finger to his lips in a "hush" gesture.

He couldn't quite grasp it, but was it possible that this was the one area where her knowledge really was nine-year-old level? He'd rather not get into it in detail here, since Emilia, laboring under the same misunderstanding, was also present.

"What was that strange groan just now, Subaru?"

"It's nothing. Just some phlegm and a bit of hopeless despair caught in my throat."

"I see. Puberty must be difficult. ...Setting that aside, I have no intention of letting those two take that long."

"We feel the same way, but do you have some idea, Anne?"

"What did you have in mind, Emily?"

Answered with a question in return, Emilia knit her well-shaped brows at Annerose's query and pressed a finger to her lips. "Um, well," she began.

"I'm sure they both want to make up, deep down. Garfiel seems to be trying to make time to talk on his own, and even though Frederica seems awkward about it, I don't think she doesn't want to talk either."

"Yes, that's true. And?"

"So I thought, if we just locked the two of them in the same room, wouldn't that make things simple?"

"Emilia-tan, that's a pretty violent method you're picking there!?"

Taken to its extreme, Subaru himself thought there wasn't really any other way, but hearing that opinion come out of Emilia's mouth was still pretty startling. For what it's worth, Subaru agreed with the basic idea, but he had a slightly more refined take on it. The trouble was the refinement itself.

"I mean, sure, throw them in the same room, but if those two team up, they'll bust their way out of most rooms through sheer force. And I'd rather not have the mansion half-destroyed in the process. We're already crashing here because our own place got wrecked—wrecking this one too within a week would be some kind of speedrun."

"Then what do you think we should do, Subaru? Lock the two of them in a room made of ice that I make?"

"I don't think we need to push it to some life-or-death extreme just to revive their family bond! C'mon, more like—give them a shared goal and put them in the same room together, something like that!"

"A shared goal...?"

Emilia tilted her head, looking blank.

Subaru had somehow managed to talk her out of the drastic plan, but the truth was, his own idea had stalled at exactly that point too.

He'd gotten as far as "give the two of them a shared goal," but hadn't connected it to any concrete plan. Throw some monster at them that they couldn't beat without joining forces?

Where on earth was such a conveniently-timed monster-subjugation event supposed to come from?

"Actually, I was thinking the very same thing as you, Subaru."

"Huh? There's a subjugation request for a cyclops or a chimera or something?"

"It seems that was not it, after all."

Under Annerose's scornful gaze, Subaru stuck out his tongue and rapped himself on the head in apology.

The nine-year-old let out a small sigh, her cheeks coloring under the shine of Emilia's expectant gaze, and said—

"On the point of giving them a shared goal, our thinking is the same. But the difference in the information we each have about those two is surely what accounts for the difference between my idea and Emily's."

"The information we have about those two...?"

"Between the two of them, the one you and Subaru know well is, if anything, Garfiel, isn't it, Emily? But believe it or not, I've known Frederica for over eight years. I've been acquainted with that fang-filled face of hers since before I can even remember."

I see, Subaru thought, grasping roughly what Annerose meant.

It seemed Annerose could, through Frederica, fill in the missing piece of a shared purpose between her and Garfiel—the very hole Subaru hadn't been able to fill himself.

"Is that a sure thing?"

"If I can recruit a collaborator. Beyond that, Frederica is no problem, but Garfiel is the issue."

"Garfiel is?"

"If Garfiel is truly the sort of person I've observed him to be these past few days, then it should proceed without a hitch."

How closely Annerose had been paying attention to Garfiel's character, Subaru couldn't say, but at least as far as he knew, Garfiel here had been acting naturally, like himself.

He hadn't been putting up that pointless bravado like back in the Sanctuary, or showing any sign of trying to hide the middle-school-ish immaturity that came with being fourteen. On that point, Subaru could vouch for him.

"On the 'Garfiel being himself' front, I don't think there's a problem."

"Then we should be fine. Now, the matter of a collaborator... could we ask for help from Lady Ryuzu, family to the both of them?"

"Lady Ryuzu?"

If asked who had the deepest connection to the two of them, hers would be the first name to come up.

The only unknown was whether Ryuzu, who hadn't seemed all that cooperative up to now, would go along with it. In any case, Annerose didn't seem to share that concern, and beckoned lightly to summon the butler behind her.

"Clind."

"Yes. As for the kitchen, if dinner preparations are delayed, it can be made free for about two hours. A proposal."

"I see. Very well. Do inform whoever's in charge of dinner accordingly."

"Understood. Swiftly. Promptly."

With the exchange settled between just the two of them, Clind slipped out of the room without a sound. To Subaru and Emilia, who watched him go with startled eyes, Annerose turned a smile while taking a sip of tea, and said—

"Now then, let's make quick work of this problem. After all, there are still more people I need to go meddle with."

And with that, she left Subaru and Emilia looking even more bewildered than before.

The moment Garfiel stepped into the kitchen and spotted that "unfamiliar back," he realized he'd been caught in a trap and sighed.

"...Ev'ry single one of 'em, seriously overdoin' it with the meddlin'."

Even as he grumbled the curse, the corners of his mouth were curled in the faintest smile.

Garfiel's nose was a cut above. His sense of smell, incomparable to any ordinary person's, had caught this scent before he'd even set foot in the place.

That he'd still gone through the motions of not knowing might be called the last stubbornness of a man named Garfiel—or perhaps just a man's petty pride.

"Garf?"

A woman turning around called out to Garfiel, who was scratching his head, in a voice "unfamiliar to his ears."

Standing before him was a woman with long, shimmering golden hair swaying about her. She stood taller than Garfiel, and her build could only be called sturdy. The sharp fangs adorning her mouth, combined with the powerful frame, lent her an air somehow savage and violent.

Were it not for her gentle voice and the soft light in the eyes turned toward him, her true nature would surely go on being endlessly misread by everyone she met.

Frederica Baumann—that was the name his sister went by, or so Garfiel Tinzel had heard. Tinzel was their mother's family name; Baumann, their father's.

Garfiel didn't know why Frederica had deliberately chosen to go by their father's name, and he tried not to think about it too much.

Still, he suspected that the people responsible for putting him in his current situation hadn't given much thought to those complicated feelings of his. Or maybe he was just making too much of it, and from the outside it wasn't really such a big deal.

"Didn't figure on runnin' into ya here, sis."

"That's my line. You, coming all the way here... Dinner isn't ready yet, so there's nothing for you to nibble on."

"Ain't here to sneak a bite. Don't go treatin' me like some kid."

"The way you hate being treated like a child is exactly what makes you one. Besides, you're still at the age where you want to be spoiled a little, aren't you, Garf?"

"Nah, I'm fourteen, past all that by now. Who's a spoiled brat!"

Somehow it struck a bit too close, and Garfiel barked his denial all the louder for it.

Frederica shook her head at Garfiel's overreaction and turned her gaze back to the depths of the kitchen—to her own hands—and said—

"I have things to do right now. Unlike you, Garf, I'm not in a position to spend all my time playing."

"It ain't like I spend every day happily playin' around either. And I didn't stop by here to kill time. ...I figure I got the same reason as you, sis."

"The same as me?"

"Though it looks like whoever put us up to it was different."

From just those words, Frederica seemed to grasp the situation too.

"So that's how it was," she murmured, as though it all clicked into place.

"I did think it was strange. Lady Annerose suddenly announcing, 'If you don't eat a meat pie made by Frederica, you'll drop dead on the spot.'"

"I gotta say, sis, fallin' for a line like that says somethin' about you too."

"And who told you to come here, Garf... or rather, what did Granny say to get you here?"

"That if she doesn't eat a meat pie I made, her mind'll go soft faster..."

"And I have to say, believing that line says something about you too, Garf."

At Frederica's reply, Garfiel fell silent.

Now that she mentioned it, fair enough—but he really had been worried, so it couldn't be helped.

"Might be somethin' you wouldn't get, sis, seein' as you up and abandoned lookin' after the old bag. But Granny, she just ate and then goes askin' 'ain't dinner ready yet?'—that ain't even rare for her. 'Course I'm gonna worry."

In truth, this was just a sad misunderstanding born from the fact that when Ryuzu's representative personality switched, the memory of whether or not she'd eaten didn't carry over—but Garfiel had no idea of that, and Frederica, hearing it secondhand, didn't catch it either.

Between the two siblings, their worry over the mental state of Ryuzu, who was family to them in all but blood, only grew.

But in this moment, Garfiel's words struck at a vulnerable part of Frederica's heart in an entirely different way. Without meaning to, he had thrown a piece of their ten years of blank silence right at her.

"...You're right. In these past ten years, I never once went back to the Sanctuary. All that time, it was you who protected it, Garf. What happened inside, how Granny was doing... I have no right to speak as though I know."

"Nah... I didn't mean it like that. It's just, I..."

"—————"

Turning back once more, Garfiel found himself face to face with Frederica.

That face, even now, still remained "unfamiliar."

Ten years of separation. And the image of his sister that Garfiel had kept picturing in his mind all those ten years hadn't changed at all from her childhood self of a decade ago.

So even now, reunited with the real person after all this time, he couldn't quite accept that fact honestly. Frederica was surely in the same position. That was why she, too, was looking at him with eyes clouded by the same bewilderment as his own.

Still, what caught his attention now was the confusion in his sister's eyes, and another wave of emotion just as strong, rising alongside it.

What in the world was his sister feeling toward him?

"...Ah."

His gaze met and then turned away, Garfiel let out a breath that seemed to drain right out of him.

And then, all at once, the answer dropped into his chest.

He understood.

He'd ended up understanding, whether he wanted to or not, what the emotion flickering across Frederica's eyes was the same as.

It was the same feeling Ryuzu had sometimes worn, back when he was in the Sanctuary. And surely the same as what he'd once seen reflected in his own eyes on the water's surface.

If so, then it was loneliness. Mixed together with a measure of guilt.

"Ohh, right. That's how it was, huh."

In Garfiel's mind, he'd believed the matter of ten years ago had already been settled.

Inside the tomb, Garfiel had relived the scene his younger self had witnessed, that final parting with his mother. Having come to understand the feelings his mother had left behind, he'd believed the matter was closed.

He'd even thought he'd shared that moment of resolution with his sister.

But that wasn't true.

What happened in that tomb was, in the end, nothing more than something that had passed between the tomb and Garfiel himself.

Their mother's feelings, and the emotions too difficult to put into words that she'd carried for their sister—Garfiel alone had confirmed them. His sister, who'd been in some far-off land, knew nothing of it, and none of it had ever reached her.

That was why Frederica still looked at Garfiel with an expression unresolved since ten years ago, why her face still didn't know what to say.

That was why his sister, even now, in a form grown so much since ten years ago, still had the same eyes.

"Sis."

"—!"

"Sorry, for never sayin' any of this. It's alright. I'm... I'm alright now. I think I really do understand, about Mom, and about you too, sis."

"Garf..."

Frederica's eyes wavered, a powerful emotion welling up and dampening them.

Not knowing what words to use, Garfiel, cursing his own poor way with words, searched for something that could properly convey what he felt.

Rifling through his mind, through the books he'd devoured, he hunted for the words he needed to say.

"About you leavin' the Sanctuary, and about not comin' back to show your face even once after that—I can't say I get it... but I mean to try and get it. So... uh, well..."

"Mother... have you come to be able to forgive her?"

"—Ain't nothin' to forgive."

His mouth easing into a faint smile, Garfiel shook his head at Frederica's words.

That's right. There was nothing to forgive.

The love and hatred Garfiel had carried in his chest, pictured over and over for so long, had simply been misdirected from the start. Not knowing the truth, not even knowing his own heart, he'd been throwing his anger at the darkness for the crime of not being able to see anything in it—nothing more than a child's tantrum.

Once you knew the truth, it amounted to nothing much at all.

There had never been anything that needed forgiving, nothing that needed resenting.

"I already know Mom loved me... loved us. I know that now."

"—————"

"So if you've been avoidin' me over that whole thing, it ain't gonna do you any good. Don't matter to me anymore. More than that, I'd rather we talk about somethin' better, sis."

Slipping back into his usual way of talking and address, Garfiel rubbed a finger against his slightly reddened nose.

At Garfiel's attitude and words, Frederica let out a long, deep breath. Then, with a finger, she caught the faint drop welling at the corner of her eye, and said—

"Garf... you've really grown up."

"Is that sarcasm!? Compared to you, I ain't grown big at all! What the hell happened to you, sis! How'd you get so—guh, gwahh!?"

"Family or not, the nerve, saying something like that to a lady. Idiot Garf."

His leg was grabbed, and he was hauled to the ground with full force, cracking the back of his head.

Staring up at the ceiling, eyes spinning, Garfiel's vision was filled with Frederica looking down at him. Her expression had already returned to the shape of a smile.

"Come now, get up."

"You're the one who flipped me over, and now you're sayin' that?"

Taking the offered hand, Garfiel got to his feet.

Then, brushing himself off lightly, he said, "So?" and peered at the countertop where Frederica had been working.

"How far'd you get with the meat pie, sis?"

"I've only just gotten the ingredients ready and started chopping a little. As for you, Garf, I'm surprised you still remember how to make a dish you only ever ate as a child."

"Some meddler made sure to leave the recipe behind proper, so I could keep makin' it even after she was gone. Anyway, I'll knead the dough."

"Then I'll handle the filling."

Standing before the ingredients, Garfiel wrapped a cloth around his head and got ready. Meanwhile, Frederica brought out the utensils for Garfiel's side, and the two of them passed things back and forth in perfect rhythm.

And so the two siblings, standing side by side, began their work as though the ten-year gulf between them had never existed at all—two familiar people carrying out a familiar task, as naturally as anything.
