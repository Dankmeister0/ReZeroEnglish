Arc 7 Chapter 107: "Chisha Gold"

Shoved back, he could tell — even through the demon mask — that his cheek had stiffened faintly.

It was an expression most people would surely misread. The emperor, too clever by half, often omitted words that should not have been omitted — and even his expressions said little. Probably, the only one capable of correctly reading the meaning behind this particular expression was himself, the man who had worked alongside this emperor for so many years.

"Ah, or perhaps—"

If it were the younger sister this emperor revered in every sense of the word, would she see through it too? Either way, the comparison meant nothing.

Because in this instant, in this single sliced-off moment, he alone was present.

"Cheshire Trim, can you die for my sake?"

The first time they met face to face, the first thing asked of him, right after he gave his name, was an answer concerning the use of his own life.

At the time, Cheshire, fourteen, had thought of himself as a precocious child — but meeting this boy, two years his junior, made him reconsider. True precocity was the boy standing before him; his own had been nothing but conceit.

"——"

Having thrown the question, the boy fixed him with a steady stare from those black eyes. Hair black as a cut-out shadow, and eyes to match — neither a common physical trait even in the ethnically diverse Vollachian Empire. The eye color differed, but Cheshire, himself black-haired from birth, could sympathize; he too was often mocked for it.

Though to claim sympathy here would be presumptuous indeed. The peculiarity of a physical trait was a trifling thing next to the qualities this boy possessed. A boy whose origins lay so far from the ordinary that a mere resemblance in appearance became meaningless — even his air of ultimate precociousness had proper grounding behind it. A singular position that demanded he be thus, and that he could not survive otherwise being.

And that was—

"—Prince Vincent Abelux."

That was his name, and the title appended after it was the position he had been born into. The black-haired boy — Vincent — was the true son of Drizen Vollachia, the emperor who ruled this Vollachian Empire, a being granted a share of the possibility of one day standing atop the empire's summit. Granted, more than twenty siblings of his had been given the same qualification, but that did nothing to dilute the noble blood of the boy before him. In any case—

"——"

Why had he, of all people, come to find himself face to face with such an exalted figure, riding along in the same dragon carriage? Cheshire looked back over the chain of events that had brought him here.

The trigger had been nothing more than an act of everyday help. He'd come across a dragon carriage stuck fast, its wheel lodged in a roadside ditch. While the men struggled, pushing and pulling to no avail, he'd wedged a plank under the wheel and used it as a lever, helping the deeply tilted carriage climb free of the ditch. That carriage, as it happened, belonged to the lord of this Abelux domain — and riding along inside was none other than Vincent Abelux, famed for having seized real power through the so-called "Miracle of Abellux."

The "Miracle of Abellux," incidentally, referred to a rebellion staged by a retainer who had long served the ruling Abelux house — a masterful strategy involving collusion with another house — which nonetheless met defeat under the command of an eleven-year-old boy, resulting in the wholesale slaughter of the traitor's clan. Vincent, until then merely one prince among many with no name of particular note, had instantly rallied the scattered household and soldiers, wielded rare skill, and won.

The rebellious retainer's fatal error had been mistaking Vincent not for an enemy, but for a mere trophy whose head was there for the taking — and as a result, his ambitions, ill-suited to his true stature, were shattered, and he was destroyed. Such facts, and the rumors that spread from them, had accrued exaggeration upon exaggeration until the man was spoken of as someone quite extraordinary. Cheshire too, as an ordinary subject of the empire unlikely ever to be entangled with such a figure, had only ever caught the rumors in passing — yet by some twist of fate, here he now stood, face to face with the very man at the center of them.

And he had been asked: could he die for his sake?

"——"

The true intent behind that question eluded Cheshire. To begin with, the only reason he'd been brought aboard this carriage was as thanks for helping free the wheel, and an invitation to the manor. Naturally, Cheshire had no right of refusal. Even reluctant at heart, there had been no room to decline. So he had resigned himself to it as a passing matter and stepped into the carriage he'd been invited into — only to have Vincent's first question thrown at him the moment he did.

It was an inappropriate thing to say to someone who had merely helped a carriage along its way. And yet the other party was a prince born to the role, a man who had spent his life being waited upon by others. Despite that, he had recently suffered betrayal at a retainer's hands and, in his fury, annihilated the man's entire clan. Experience enough for a twelve-year-old boy to develop an extreme distrust of people. Perhaps he could not rest easy without confirming loyalty even from a mere passerby.

The silence had already stretched past ten seconds — disrespectful enough by this point. The other party was not merely a superior but, quite literally, a being above the clouds. To begin with, both what he ought to answer and what the boy wanted to hear were painfully obvious. The desired answer was, of course, a single word: "I can." Cheshire too, as one subject living in this empire, ought to have sworn absolute loyalty and eternal servitude here and now, so as not to touch the reverse scale of a prince who might well become a future tyrant. And so Cheshire bowed his head deeply to the prince facing him across a gap in status neither deserved—

"—My apologies, but I'm afraid I cannot do that."

And gave, of all the answers he must never give, precisely that one.

"——"

Head still bowed, Cheshire cursed himself for the foolishness of what had just tumbled out of his mouth. At the same time, he wanted to clutch his own head for having done it again, for lacking the self-control not to. This same short temper had gotten him into trouble with the powers-that-be back in his home village and gotten him exiled in the first place. He'd told himself over and over that he had no future unless he corrected this trait, and still he could not fix it.

And now, on top of it all, he had given the one answer he must never give to the one person he must never give it to. Ruining himself through his own foolishness — one of the stupidest ways to die there was.

But to be asked, from on high, whether he could die for someone's sake, and to parrot back the desired answer as desired — that hollow a performance, he simply could not manage. If his spirit broke, if the pride that made him himself died, then whether his body lived or not, it amounted to the same thing as being dead. He hadn't much cared for the Vollachian way of doing things, but in that, at least, he was a man of the empire after all.

So he had no regrets over this answer. If he must say anything, it was that he felt sorry for having snapped back — almost out of spite — at a boy who'd developed this mistrust of people for such a pitiable reason. Though that guilt, too, would likely vanish the moment his head was lopped off in a fit of rage—

"That'll do. From now on, serve me with that same attitude."

"—Come again?"

"Don't raise your voice at the end if you mean it as an answer. It sounds like you're appending a question mark."

"It's not that it merely sounded that way by accident — I made quite certain it would. ...Am I not to be executed, then?"

"For what — just now conscripting you? What would be the point?"

Closing one eye, Vincent across from him frowned, displeased. It seemed unreasonable that the prince should take more offense at being questioned back than at the earlier refusal to swear loyalty, but Cheshire calmly reconsidered his own position.

For whatever reason, Vincent seemed willing to overlook his earlier rudeness. More than that — it appeared he actually intended to take Cheshire on as a retainer.

"No, I still fail to see the sense in it. Does the prince, perhaps, enjoy making such tasteless jests to someone whose life he's about to take?"

"You, of all people, cling so stubbornly to wanting to be killed by me. That's the far more incomprehensible thing here."

"With respect, the prince is known far and wide as a man who shows no mercy to those who defy him."

Having said it, he chided himself — this too was the sort of thing that should not be said. But having committed one act of disrespect already, Cheshire reasoned, however far he went now was margin of error, and turned to a kind of reckless resolve, placing his own life on the scale.

Like savoring a book, like accumulating new knowledge, like putting a theory that had just occurred to him into practice — he wanted to unravel what this prince was thinking. If the result was death, then death it would be; nothing to be done about that. To Cheshire, now plunged into a state of mind that might well be mistaken for self-destructive abandon, Vincent gave an "Ah," as though something had clicked, and nodded.

"If you mean the matter of the recent rebellion — the extermination of the clan was a necessary example. A leash was needed, so that no one else entertains the same idea hereafter. Fear is the most effective tool there is."

"Including tearing the first traitorous retainer's vanguard limb from limb and lining them all along the road?"

"If a life must be spent, that life should be put to maximum effect. — People ought to die efficiently."

Vincent answered, propping his cheek on his hand within the carriage. Faced with such a stark philosophy and the will to carry it out, Cheshire quietly swallowed his words. What Cheshire had raised was the first and greatest decisive blow that had let Vincent crush his retainer's rebellion — the massacre of the enemy vanguard, and the brutal treatment of their corpses.

Bodies torn apart alive, left in agony, lined up on the battlefield — and word spread that every captive would suffer the same fate. The other retainers, who had secretly pledged to join the uprising, broke their compacts and stayed still. Only the original traitor, who had no line of retreat, challenged Vincent and his blood-soaked scheme, and tasted the same hell as his soldiers — and died. As did his entire clan.

Naturally, once that deed became known, Vincent Abelux would surely be seen as a savagely cruel, blood-hungry prince—

"—Or perhaps that rumor, too, you spread yourself?"

"At the very least, if it leads you to fear me excessively, that is not contrary to my aims."

"Ha, is that so..."

Cheshire marveled all the more at thinking coming from a mere twelve-year-old. And at the same time, he thought that even if Vincent forgave his rudeness, he himself would likely be unable to fulfill whatever role Vincent wanted of him.

"And what is it you imagine I'd want of you?"

As though reading straight into Cheshire's chest, Vincent tossed the next question at him. Vaguer than the first, and moreover a riddle he'd need to interrogate within himself. Indeed, if he meant to say it was impossible however much he was wanted, he had to reveal what exactly was being wanted of him. And yet — it was a difficult question.

A prince, one who would eventually have to face the empire's unavoidable ceremony — what could Vincent possibly want from Cheshire, a mere ordinary subject of the empire?

"All I'm capable of is helping pull a wheel out of a ditch."

"That'll do."

"My, my — do you intend, then, to keep getting stuck in ditches, again and again, so often that you'll need my strength for it?"

A remark bitingly provocative — disrespect taken to such an extreme that Cheshire almost wanted to congratulate himself. But hearing it, Vincent's expression didn't so much as waver.

"That's right. From here on, I will get stuck in ditches many times over. Not all of them can be avoided. But if one falls into a ditch and cannot climb out, what awaits is death. A means of escaping the ditch one has fallen into — that will be needed, again and again."

"...I feel compelled to ask whether we were still discussing wheels."

"I never intended to speak falsely about the true substance of this conversation, from the very start."

Being told such an outrageous thing, Cheshire felt a chill unlike the fear of having his life threatened — and finally grasped, in full, that he was speaking with a being wholly outside the norm. And that this extraordinary person, for some reason, held him in uncannily high regard. Having taken such a disrespectful attitude, spoken such impertinent words, traded such insolent remarks back and forth — and still his life was not forfeit.

Perhaps that was less an evaluation of Cheshire than a matter of Vincent's own convictions. Either way, it was a development he could never have imagined when he'd woken in a shabby inn that very morning.

"——"

Propping his cheek on his hand, Vincent closed one eye at Cheshire's stubborn silence. An infuriatingly smug attitude, as though he were savoring having gotten the better of him. Concluding that resistance was now futile, Cheshire glared resentfully back at him — and then, suddenly, noticed something.

"The prince cannot close both eyes at once, can he."

"That's it."

"Pardon?"

Resting a hand against his sharp chin, Cheshire had said it without any particular intent, and Vincent nodded at the remark. Not understanding the meaning of that nod, Cheshire tilted his head, and—

"If you want a reason I won't execute you, that's it."

Looking at Vincent, who seemed to feel that explanation complete in itself, understanding dawned. This precocious boy, who wielded schemes that would put grown men to shame, who displayed to the fullest the qualities of a Vollachian prince — was, even so, twelve years old.

—Naively expecting that the world around him was just as clever as he was.

—Chisha Gold.

That was the new name given to Cheshire Trim once he had been taken into Vincent's service and welcomed into the Abelux manor.

"I have family back home, and if my name were to become widely known, it might invite needless trouble for them. Unless, of course, the prince intends to eliminate my worries by killing them all off..."

"You do seem determined to make me out as blood-hungry no matter what. Don't tell me it's your doing that I'm called the 'Crimson Prince' about town?"

"My, I had understood it as a strategy of exploiting fear. From what I hear, parents invoke it to discipline their children — 'behave, or the Crimson Prince will come.'"

"And if I appear, then what? I do nothing."

"Oh, but might you not simply press them into service, as you did with me? How frightful."

"If your mouth doesn't slow down soon, I might just make that false reputation true with your blood."

The exchanges with Vincent were of a sort that could not quite be called either serious or joking. When necessary, Vincent did not hesitate to spend another's life. But when it was unnecessary, he did not. It made no difference to him whether the currency was life, money, or goods — in the time they'd spent together, Cheshire had come to understand that to him, all such things were equally finite resources.

The change of name to Chisha Gold was, as explained, out of concern that those back home who resented Cheshire Trim's rise might bring trouble upon the family left behind. Though even so, he couldn't say he particularly wanted to rush to their aid if something were done to them. Callous as it might seem, that was the distance between himself and his family — the extent of consideration he could offer people who had not protected him when it mattered most.

As for the family name Gold — by some cruel joke, it belonged to the treacherous retainer who had defied Vincent and had his entire clan exterminated. For Vincent, it might have been a small act of spite toward the impertinent Cheshire — but because of it, rumor spread that he had let one survivor of the annihilated Gold family live, keeping him at his side as a humiliation, and that only cemented Vincent's status as the "Crimson Prince" all the more.

As for the given name, when Cheshire requested something with a similar ring, Vincent proposed simply dropping one letter. There was no reason to refuse, and it seemed this, at least, wasn't meant maliciously either, so — thinking it dreadfully lazy, but accepting it readily all the same.

In any case, the days of being reborn as "Chisha," formerly Cheshire, were not bad ones for a man who had always been shunned by others for his bookishness and poor social skills.

If anything, he was blessed with far more books than his home village could ever offer, and no shortage of challenges besides. A life kept at arm's length from farm work and hunting — things he had no aptitude for, and had been unreasonably despised for lacking — was worth a fortune on that count alone. Except—

"Next ditch, Chisha. Prove your worth."

Saying that, Vincent would bring him problems on a scale utterly unlike a carriage wheel, and drag him into endless debate until a solution was found — and it was agony.

The frightening thing about Vincent was his sheer capacity for action. Despite having the same number of eyes, the same number of heads as anyone else, however the world appeared to him, however he grasped it, he was tackling every problem simultaneously.

Being called upon to resolve every problem within the domain was, granted, part of a lord's duty — but the demands placed on a twelve-year-old boy were, in a word, brutal, and the absence of any reliable adult to lean on was almost pitiable. And yet such impressions, viewed from outside, dissolved before Vincent's actual performance.

And Chisha, dragged along in his wake, was likewise required to master knowledge across every field, with no time to stand still. The moment one problem was cleared away, the next appeared immediately. New problems piled on even in the midst of existing ones, and while wrestling simultaneously with a jumble of different difficulties, the Abelux domain changed.

As living conditions changed, the domain's people — initially gripped mostly by fear — shifted their posture toward awe. Terror became reverence; servitude became devotion. Vincent had every qualification to receive it. Though he himself never sought to place any particular value in it.

"Chisha, you've studied water management enough by now. In that case, that incompetent magistrate is relieved of his post. We'll pursue the matter of his embezzlement and have his head."

"Taking his head does seem a touch excessive, in my humble opinion."

"You think his output matched the fortune he lined his pockets with? Is that your claim?"

"...Well, my position there is somewhat weak, admittedly."

"If what he pocketed matched what he produced, I'd overlook it. If not, he'll face the consequences. If repeated warnings of the crisis fail to change him, the hand extended to help him will naturally run out."

Decisive judgment and resolve, with a fastidiousness hidden behind it — the flip side of the expectations Vincent held for others, and the distant cause behind why those who couldn't meet them were branded lazy. Yet Vincent wasn't, particularly, a meritocrat who especially despised incompetence. If anything had to be said, it was probably not that he was a meritocrat but—

"—One need only fulfill the duty befitting one's own vessel."

He wanted everyone, without exception, to give their full effort to living. Once that became clear, the impression of this boy Vincent Abelux, and the background to his near-fastidious perfectionism, began to look different.

Hard to believe as it was, Vincent possessed no firm self-confidence, no pride in himself.

He was always hungry, feeling himself insufficient.
He was always lamenting, feeling himself insufficient.
He was always struggling, feeling himself insufficient.

The driving force that made Vincent who he was — it was not gratitude for having been born into a fortunate position as a prince, but rage at the thought of not fully accomplishing the role demanded of that position. And the reason Vincent came to hold such a fierce conviction was—


"—There exists a man deserving of nothing but contempt: Stride Vollachia."

Some time after entering Vincent's service, Chisha frowned at these words his lord let slip. Fortunately — or perhaps just as Vincent had judged — Chisha apparently had a fair talent for pulling wheels out of ditches, and had managed to keep his position without losing it.

Still, it was a path he'd been drawn onto without much say in the matter. The good and bad aspects of this position had gradually come into focus, and he'd had his own thoughts on how the scale ought to tip between them. It was right around when Chisha found himself in such a mood that Vincent, for the first time, let something resembling an inner thought unrelated to work slip out.

"Stride Vollachia? I confess my studies are lacking, but I don't believe I've ever heard that name."

"It's not a gap in your knowledge. If anything, had you known it, it would be the sort of topic that endangers a life. After all, he is a man erased from existence by the Vollachian imperial family."

"——"

If so, wasn't it dangerous for him to learn of it carelessly now, Chisha thought — but interrupting Vincent once he'd begun speaking was difficult, given his position. More than that, he was interested in this figure who had been cast out of the Vollachian imperial family.

"Though I hardly think it possible to simply leave the imperial family. Even having left, the blood still flows. Surely then the 'Imperial Selection Ceremony' would apply."

The 'Imperial Selection Ceremony,' held to determine the emperor who would rule the empire's next generation. A ritual that might be called the ultimate expression of the imperial way — the emperor's children slaughtering one another to decide who stands atop the empire — and pure, unbroken history since the empire's founding.

The one who becomes emperor is the very last one standing. Otherwise, the proof of the one who reigns over Vollachia, the Sunlight Sword, cannot be obtained.

"One theory holds that renouncing one's right of succession allows one to live on."

"That's a deception. Nothing more than sweet words meant to thin out early those who, while holding succession rights, lack the resolve to fight through the ceremony to the end. So your concern is correct."

"Ordinarily, exile simply cannot happen to a member of the Vollachian imperial family."

"And yet Stride Vollachia received precisely that impossible punishment. And in so doing, was removed from the position that constitutes one factor of the ceremony. Though, given that the 'Imperial Selection Ceremony' proceeded without incident afterward, it seems the man could not escape death either way."

In Vincent's gaze, colder than usual, contempt for that particular imperial figure ran deep. Stride Vollachia — driven from his position within the Vollachian imperial family, his very existence erased from history — was surely someone who touched Vincent's anger in just the right way. He showed no mercy toward anyone who failed to fully carry out the role they were meant to fulfill, the ability they'd been given.

"...But if he's a presence no one is supposed to know of, how did it come to Lord Vincent's knowledge? Did someone of high standing, someone loose-lipped, tell you?"

"As ever, your manner of speaking recklessly straddles the gap in our stations. — A memoir."

"A memoir?"

"Stride Vollachia's memoir. I found it hidden in the archives of the Crystal Palace. Though it's scarcely readable — mostly the ravings of a madman, scribbled down."

Vincent's lips twisted bitterly, as though the disgust ran unusually deep. He was a man who ordinarily received most matters with tolerance, chewing them over before passing judgment — the speed of that process was so fast that to others it looked like snap decisions, for better or worse — but for Vincent to display this much negative emotion was rare.

"What was written there? Might I be permitted to see it as well?"

His interest was piqued. Though he was aware that his true focus had shifted less to the contents of the memoir itself, and more to the fact that it was troubling this difficult master of his so much.

Whether or not he noticed that undercurrent in Chisha's mind, Vincent closed one eye, piercing him with that black gaze, and said—

"I won't show it to you. — Nonsense about 'Observers' and the like, a collection of worthless ravings dressed up as verse."

"Ah, come now, don't you think you're overthinking it, assuming there's some hidden reason behind everything? Nobody else goes around furrowing their brow and plotting conspiracies like you and His Excellency, y'know!"

"—I'd appreciate a bit more care in how you address me, and also, my name is Chisha."

"Ah, whoops, sorry about that! Getting an actor's name wrong is the height of disrespect! I'm properly ashamed of myself. Chisha Chisha Chisha Chisha Chisha Chisha Chisha!"

"——"

A blue-haired boy who spoke, and spoke, and spoke, on momentum alone. This boy, named Cecilus Segmunt, was, like Chisha once was, someone who had caught Vincent's eye and been picked up.

If someone had outstanding ability and the resolve to put it to use, Vincent brought them into service, regardless of birth or station. That approach invited pushback, yet the Abelux domain's governance kept improving year after year. That said, the gap left by education, or lack thereof, was significant, and however much Vincent claimed not to care about background, gathering capable civil officials from the commoner class remained difficult, and Chisha's burden of labor never seemed to lighten much. And yet—

"All a military officer needs is raw strength of arm. Though in my case, I believe there ought to be the added flair of a stage actor besides — and on both counts, I happen to be one of a kind!"

"Quite the confidence... and it appears you have the ability to back the claim up. That it makes things all the more troublesome is neither here nor there, as far as I'm concerned."

A boisterous child — and indeed, six or seven years his junior. From Chisha's vantage at eighteen, that description held true enough, but unfortunately, Cecilus possessed none of the endearing qualities one might expect at his age. Not in looks or manner, but in the sheer skill he so proudly, laughingly, boasted of.

"——"

Chisha himself, taken into the Abelux household, had studied martial arts for the sake of guard duty and self-defense, but he was well aware his own talent there was merely average. He was better suited to moving many other people than to moving his own body. Not that this excused neglecting his training — he would surely need to keep it up for the rest of his life — but even from the modest vantage of one who dabbled in the martial arts, Cecilus's skill was beyond the pale.

Chisha had already encountered someone extraordinary in the realm of strategy. But to now cross paths with someone extraordinary in the realm of raw combat power too — that, he had never expected.

"At this rate, I find myself wondering all the more what Lord Vincent — His Excellency — could possibly want from me."

"Oh, thinking hard about something again, are you, Chisha? Well then, allow me to give you the answer. That sort of thing, you see — is called foreshadowing!"

Pointing a finger sharply at him as he declared this, Cecilus made Chisha blink in surprise. Seeing that reaction, Cecilus withdrew the pointed finger and—

"Perhaps you don't know foreshadowing? You see, foreshadowing is when important information in a story is scattered about without letting it be recognized as such—"

"I am familiar with the definition of the word 'foreshadowing.' The face I am making is the face of a man who doesn't understand why it was brought up here."

"Ah, is that so! Then it's simple. Didn't I just say? That the thing you and His Excellency have in common is worrying that everything, absolutely everything, must have some meaning."

Smiling happily, Cecilus brought his hands together in front of his chest. Then, opening the hands he'd just clapped, he spun on the spot, gesturing at everything around him, and—

"If truly everything in this world has meaning, then anything that can't be unraveled here, in this very moment, is nothing but foreshadowing for developments yet to come! How exhilarating!"

"The flow of funds to unknown ends, or the existence of unexplained hoarded wealth — I would call that not foreshadowing for the future, but evidence of rampant corruption and bribery."

"That's — that's the kind you can already find the answer to right now, isn't it! What I'm talking about is the kind you can't! You're smart, Chisha, so listen properly!"

"——"

His spinning feet came to a sudden stop, and Cecilus, eyebrows raised in offense, protested at him. Not quite understanding why he was the one being scolded here, Chisha nevertheless felt — just a little, just the smallest bit — saved by Cecilus's way of thinking.

Trusted by Vincent, working to meet his demands, there had always been, in some corner of his mind, a nagging doubt: why had he been the one chosen? Even after years had passed, even having come to know Vincent's character, that question remained unanswered. Because it wasn't Vincent's problem to answer — it was Chisha's own.

Continuing to stand at Vincent's side without ever resolving that anguish had been, mentally, quite a difficult thing to bear — and yet—

"...So then, in a manner of speaking, my own existence too is foreshadowing?"

"Ooh? Already putting it to use, are you! As expected of a clever man, quick on the uptake! That's the sort of thing I respect — though I could never do the same myself!"

"If you respect me, oughtn't you attach an honorific when addressing me? I am, after all, your senior in years, in standing, and in experience."

"Aw, come on, calling a friend 'Mister' feels awfully distant, doesn't it? Given what's ahead of us, we're bound together for life. Let's do away with formalities and just wrestle straight past them!"

"Friend..."

Said so plainly, and with a clap on the shoulder to boot, Chisha was left speechless. Part of it was the sheer familiarity of how casually the distance had been closed — but the greater reason was realizing, upon reflecting on himself, that he had no one he could call a friend.

Having abandoned his home village, been picked up by Vincent, and spent the intervening years learning, working, and struggling desperately under him, he had never made anyone he was close to. Certainly there were people he held professional goodwill toward, but setting that aside—

"——"

"What's wrong? Ah, should we start with 'Mister' after all? Starting off distant and gradually closing the gap until eventually we're on equal footing — that's not a bad arc either, I think, so maybe we steer that direction instead..."

"No, that's quite all right. — His Excellency is a man who values speed over caution, it seems."

He hadn't caught the whole of Cecilus's nonsense, but if the destination was the same either way, the faster route there was preferable. Mocking himself, faintly, for having apparently been thoroughly infected by Vincent's own philosophy, Chisha chose to accept Cecilus's way of being rather than reject it. In Cecilus's own style of speaking—

"I look forward to seeing your strength put to use in service of His Excellency's wishes. Naturally, the reason you were picked up too, is..."

"Proudly foreshadowing for being useful, I'd wager! Leave it to me, Chisha. I'm useless at everything except fighting, but when it comes to fighting, I won't be outdone by anyone!"

"That will do."

Puffing out his thin chest, the slight boy's boastful claim — and yet Chisha did not laugh. Following both Vincent's judgment and his own, he granted Cecilus Segmunt the place befitting his vessel. And with that—

"—It seems I too must wait for the day I function as foreshadowing myself."

Muttering that, aware of the role his friend, so much younger than himself, had named for him.

That, as it happened, was an event that occurred barely half a year before the "Imperial Selection Ceremony," in which Vincent Abelux would take part, began.

The death of the previous emperor, Drizen Vollachia, and the "Imperial Selection Ceremony" that began in its wake.

Targeted by his other siblings for his talent that could not be concealed, Vincent Abelux, at one point surrounded and subjected to a concentrated assault. But by the time it was over, just as the initial odds had predicted, Vincent fought his way through the "Imperial Selection Ceremony" with overwhelming strength, living up to every expectation placed on him and every reputation he'd built.

Slaughtering every single one of his brothers and sisters, Vincent Abelux donned the bloody crown of the Vollachian Empire — no, the birth of the seventy-seventh emperor, Vincent Vollachia.

In the blood-soaked path Vincent walked, in the great river of blood he built, the contributions of Chisha and Cecilus, who moved as his subordinates, surely played some part. But within this "Imperial Selection Ceremony," offering counsel toward Vincent's victory was a different matter from the role required of Chisha — pulling wheels from ditches. During this battle, the moment Chisha was called upon for that role came at the very last move—

"—Your sister seems to have escaped safely. A fierce and unrestrained temperament she may have, but surely she understands her own position. I'd call this settled, for now."

The office — a chamber within the Crystal Palace in the imperial capital of Lupugana. In that one room used by the emperor standing at the pinnacle of state affairs, Chisha had reckoned it would take some time before a familiar face looking back at him from within it could be met without disturbance. Though, for his part, the emperor himself showed no sign of his usual composure slipping, even having taken a seat of such authority. Even when certain of the "Imperial Selection Ceremony's" victory beyond doubt, he hadn't let so much as a smile of accomplishment show.

Vincent did not smile. At least, not while he was a prince, or an emperor. In moments when he was neither, his lips might loosen into a smile of poor character. But from here on, such occasions would surely dwindle sharply. He had become emperor. And an emperor's moments of not being emperor — he would not permit himself many of those, surely.

—No, there was one single point where such a moment could still be shared with someone.

"When you sought a means of keeping Lady Prisca alive, I confess my ears doubted what they heard."

With no one else present to overhear, Chisha spoke aloud the empire's greatest secret — one that had arisen within this Vollachian Empire, and one he himself had taken part in.

Alongside the settling of the "Imperial Selection Ceremony," the birth of a new emperor had been announced. But in truth, in a ritual where the imperial family member holding succession rights must be the very last one remaining, that completion had not, in fact, been achieved. — Two remained, at the end.

Vincent Abelux, and Prisca Benedictus.

Prisca Benedictus, spoken of as the ill-fated princess who drained a cup of poison herself and lost her life. In truth, she had drunk the poison, but she had not died. She had not drunk from the cup out of despair, but rather to save an attendant who, worried for his mistress, had clung to some possibility of clemency. The poison stopped Prisca's heart once, and finished everything before it could beat again.

The "Imperial Selection Ceremony" ended, and Prisca Benedictus was laid to rest beneath a grave. Vincent Abelux became emperor, and Prisca Benedictus, become some other existence under a name not her own, continued to live on—

"Had I been born of a house that had served the empire since ancient times, I might have counseled Your Excellency against such a wish as unthinkable. But I am, as it happens, of common birth."

He had no foundation for holding authority or tradition in particular reverence. He paid them a measure of respect, acknowledged their value, but did not mistake them for the highest good. In the Vollachian Empire, such a stance was permitted. Hence—

"In joining this scheme of yours, I confess I hesitated rather less than one might expect."

"——"

The emperor kept his silence; Vincent's inner thoughts could not be guessed at, but his actions were plain enough. To begin with, among his many siblings, Prisca had always been special to Vincent. In terms of sheer brilliance, Prisca had shone particularly bright even in Chisha's eyes, but Vincent's fondness for his much younger sister surely wasn't rooted solely in her talent. Even Prisca, arrogance and pride personified, had shown Vincent respect, and, Chisha believed, treated him properly as a brother who shared her blood.

And it was precisely this — mutual recognition, a mutual approach that nonetheless forbade coexistence — that was the "Imperial Selection Ceremony," the fate of the Vollachian imperial line. That fate, Vincent had broken with his own hand. To Chisha, that felt gratifying — and above all, it brought relief.

Fulfilling one's ability to the utmost within the position and role given — that was Vincent's way. From the day they met to this very day, Vincent had never once bent from that stance. And yet he had let Prisca live — what rational expression of that principle could that possibly be called?

An act of madness: were her survival known, it would endanger his own life, and could well collapse the foundation of trust the emperor had built with the empire's people. As emperor of Vollachia, what argument could possibly justify it?

He couldn't. Of course he couldn't. It hadn't been an instruction handed down through rational judgment. That had been an emotional wish, a longing, a prayer.

Vincent Abelux hadn't wanted to kill his beloved sister. And so Prisca Benedictus had been allowed to live, and Vincent had donned a false crown.

That was what pleased Chisha. Not that Prisca had been spared, but that Vincent had wanted her spared — and, to that end, had needed Chisha to find the means of pulling the wheel from the ditch that stood in the way.

"Foreshadowing — was it."

Cecilus's idle words, spoken before the "Imperial Selection Ceremony" even began, crossed his mind. Things that made no sense in the moment, that seemed meaningless, could later be found to hold some significance after all. Understood, in hindsight, as having existed for that very purpose. His own and Chisha's positions, actions — perhaps they, too, were stepping stones laid toward some future.

"Well, admitting Cecilus was on the mark is thoroughly galling, and if I told him so myself I can just picture him getting insufferably smug, so I shall never say it, not ever."

"It was like that before too, but lately you've been thinking even more than usual. Has all the color drained out of you, and along with it, everything you cultivated waiting on me?"

"It so happens I've been rather sharp ever since wandering the brink of death."

Knowing full well it was disrespectful, Chisha shrugged in front of Vincent. The emperor, whose station had changed, didn't reprimand such a thing so long as it wasn't done in front of the public eye. Vincent referred to himself as "We" now, and the figure of Chisha reflected in his black eyes was, just as pointed out, a white one, thoroughly drained of color.

During the "Imperial Selection Ceremony," when he'd nearly lost his life to an enemy he'd faced, Chisha's black hair, after wandering the brink of death and returning, had lost all its color and turned pure white. Ever since, the wardrobe he'd once kept vaguely black-toned, he now kept in the exact opposite — white, uniformly. He'd even had his beloved iron fan repainted, thoroughly matching everything to white.

Of course, it hadn't been mere whim. Having wandered the brink of death and been forced to lose his own color, Chisha had developed a strange "Noh" ability. Wielding this power to take on the color of others required constant, deliberate awareness. Suggesting to himself, always, that he could be dyed any color — that was the necessary measure.

In any case, the details of that "Noh" he kept concealed even from Vincent. Naturally, he revealed it to no one — not the loose-lipped Cecilus, nor anyone else with whom his connection was thin. A hidden card, a trump card, a last resort — one or two of those were worth keeping in reserve. Though—

"If Your Excellency were to finally deem my service concluded up to this point and release me from my post, such schemes might no longer be necessary, I should think."

"Suppose you were Us — knowing the most vital secret this empire must keep hidden, would you release such a man alive and expect to pass the nights in peace?"

"Building hypothesis upon hypothesis is somewhat poor form, but I suspect this head would fall before I ever left the castle."

"If you understand that much, and still value your life, then you'll go on serving obediently. For as long as you can prove yourself useful to Us, We'll keep that head attached to that neck of yours."

Saying so, propping his cheek against his hand on the desk, Vincent declared it with characteristic arrogance. An exchange of banter neither quite serious nor entirely joking either, but for an emperor so sparing with words to others, having him voice such things aloud and confirm them was, in its own way, necessary. And from it, it seemed Chisha Gold's position and the role demanded of him would remain unchanged even now that Vincent had become emperor — but—

"—The shape of the Sacred Vollachia Empire, on the other hand, may well be about to change."

Already, the gleam of the Sunlight Sword, that absolute symbol of the Vollachian Empire, had been betrayed. Since the "Imperial Selection Ceremony," unbroken since the founding, had reached its conclusion in a form different from all before it, whatever followed from here would surely change its own nature drastically as well.

Walking that path would be Vincent, who had prioritized his own wish and chosen to save his sister. A path unlike any before it, opening up ahead. At that fact, and the expectation it carried, Chisha realized his own heart was, ever so slightly, quickening. But that, he would never let show — not in expression, not in words, not in bearing.

His friend Cecilus's bad influence, he was well aware of that much himself.

—Days passed by in a dizzying blur.

The revival of the old "Nine Divine Generals" system, and the resetting of noble ranks across the empire. Those who, through hollowed-out tradition alone, had been granted high status were swept away, thoroughly instilling both within the empire and beyond it the true nature of the Vollachian Empire, flying the banner of "the wolf pierced by the sword."

The unspoken rule that the strong were absolutely correct, believed thoughtlessly, allowed to run the country ragged under every emperor up to now — that approach was rooted out entirely, and the original principle of honoring the strong was sifted clean. Those who rested on lineage and pedigree had their supports stripped away, while those who had watched and waited, eyes sharp, for their chance were given the opportunity to seize it.

From outside, Vincent Vollachia's rule might have seemed no different from the empire's history up to now. But the reality was entirely different. Those who knew what happened behind the scenes felt it keenly, vividly.

Eventually, many within the empire — and those beyond its borders too — would come to know it. The sheer scale of what Vincent Vollachia was doing: remaking and reforming the empire.

Pointless conflict vanished; irrational bloodshed ceased to be celebrated. Proof of strength could no longer be established through individual martial prowess alone, and those who nursed ambitions beyond their true stature would prove, with their own lives, just how difficult it truly was to fulfill such ambition. It was like watching, step by step, the very standard of what "strong" meant being rewritten. Though—

"I'm 'One,' Anya's 'Two,' Mr. Olbart's in, and Chisha's 'Four.' His Excellency's judgment isn't half bad, but Chisha, you don't look too thrilled about it?"

"Being ranked 'Four' myself does leave the chair feeling somewhat ill-fitting, I confess. As you well know, Cecilus, my own—"

"Muscle's nonexistent, right. Well, well, I get it, sort of, though I won't say I fully get it, but I suppose that's just how rank gets decided in the world His Excellency governs. When the criteria mix like that it does make my head spin trying to work out what's what, but personally I'm fairly in favor of it."

"And why is that? Beyond the fact that you yourself remain the unshakable 'One.'"

"You beat me to it! That's the biggest reason, sure enough. But it's not the only one. I think putting Chisha in that spot changes how people see things."

"See things... meaning, in terms of how a battle would unfold, I take it?"

"More or less. Sure, if you and I went head to head, you'd be dead in the blink of an eye, Chisha — but what if you started off with a thousand men under your command? Might take a thousand seconds!"

"It wouldn't take that long, surely. Though I take your point. While I bought those thousand seconds—"

"His Excellency, or whoever, gets to accomplish the goal, that's the idea! The biggest flaw in me, the star player of this whole world, is that there's only one of me in it."

Even with rank achieved, even with the empire's shape transformed, some men's natures didn't change one bit. And yet Cecilus retained, intact, the very oldest ideal — the reason the "strong" had once earned everyone's respect in the Vollachian Empire, from the beginning of time. That the man himself commanded no particular popularity, that his personality was not one anyone found endearing, that his back was no model for anyone to follow — everyone across the empire knew this well.

And yet, ask anyone who the strongest in the empire was, and every single one of them could puff out their chest as if it were their own achievement to say: Cecilus Segmunt was the strongest being in the empire.

That people believed a thousand men could buy a thousand seconds against Cecilus was, in fact, the very reason Chisha didn't have to stubbornly refuse the position of "Four" among the "Nine Divine Generals." He accepted the honor as an overestimation, all the same, resigned to the role and its position. On the whole, it functioned well enough. If there was a problem, it was—

"—Weell, if it isn't General Chisha. Lovely weather we're having today, isn't it."

A figure who spoke to him with such faint, familiar smiles. Self-proclaimed "Stargazer," permitted entry to the Crystal Palace — the only unsettling presence in a palace where everything else seemed to be turning in his favor, a black speck against Chisha's white self.


—That uncanny sense of his proved, some years later, to have been no mistake.


"—The arrival of the 'Great Disaster' has come down from the heavens. My condolences, Your Excellency."

At the center of the emperor's office, the man who, by rights, should never have been standing there declared it, with an expression as though he truly, sincerely felt it deep in his heart.

Ubilk the "Stargazer" — at those words, Chisha, present at his side, frowned.

"...The 'Great Disaster'?"

An unfamiliar ring to it, and at the same time, a ring that promised nothing good. Called a "great" disaster no less — one could guess it was no ordinary calamity. But what grated on Chisha was the single phrase Ubilk had tacked on beside it. Why had this man appended "my condolences" to whatever he was addressing Vincent about?

"Master Ubilk, I do not doubt the credibility of your 'Stargazing.' You have foretold rebellions, disasters, and matters that might occur within the country time and again. There have been no small number of instances where damage was minimized thanks to your prophecies."

"You flatter me, General Chisha. Buuut, one small correction. I don't make prophecies. I merely relay the whispers of the stars. It isn't my doing."

"...I shall respect Master Ubilk's perspective on the matter. Naturally, preparations must be made for this 'Great Disaster,' whatever it may be — but might I ask what you predict will occur?"

The solemn expression Ubilk had worn until just a moment ago flipped in an instant into a smile as he answered Chisha. Eyeing this man, so expressive yet so devoid of any felt heart behind it, with suspicion, Chisha put forth the question that needed asking.

Ubilk, who claimed the title "Stargazer," filled a role close to that of a diviner or fortune-teller. Though the precision of his prophecies was extraordinary, setting him apart entirely from the sort of fortune-tellers whose theatrics served mainly to boost morale. That said, whether it was related to Ubilk's own personality or not, his manner of speaking tended toward the roundabout, and many details he gave remained frustratingly vague — that much was a flaw.

Even so, as Chisha himself had just noted, there had been cases enough, stemming from Ubilk's counsel, of both man-made and natural disasters being resolved before they could spiral into catastrophe. His permission to move freely within the Crystal Palace stemmed from Vincent's evaluation of that very ability. Chisha, for his part, had never been especially enthusiastic about placing such trust in this unreadable man, but making use of what could be used was very much Vincent's way.

That was, after all, the same reasoning behind bringing into service those who had once served the siblings who died in the "Imperial Selection Ceremony." Keeping the chancellor, Berstetz Fondalfon, so close at hand, however unrivaled his ability, was an act of madness in its own right. Though, to be fair, that particular chancellor happened to hold surprisingly strong loyalty to the empire itself, and so long as Vincent remained Vincent, the odds of him ever baring his fangs were low. In any case—

"If it's grand enough to be called a 'Great Disaster,' repelling it will surely take considerable effort. Fortunately, both Cecilus and General Arakiya have their hands free at the moment... well, those two are dangerous if left apart, so I suppose they're generally free most of the time regardless..."

"—Annihilation, General Chisha."

"Hm?"

Chisha, sinking into a rather weary train of thought picturing the faces of the troublesome "One" and "Two," Cecilus and Arakiya, was jolted back to reality by the sudden weight of that word. Frowning his already-furrowed brow further, Chisha once again gave Ubilk the floor to speak. Taking the cue, Ubilk prefaced with a "So, you see," and—

"What's coming is annihilation, General Chisha. The 'Great Disaster' is the final blow that will bring the Vollachian Empire crashing down. A ruin so complete that not even the light of the sun shall reach it. Thooough, having said that—"

"——"

"The Sunlight Sword was never something that could be fully wielded to begin with. Wasn't it?"

The moment he heard that, Chisha, who had been standing behind Vincent, leapt into the center of the room, drawing his iron fan and pressing it against Ubilk's throat, knocking him off balance. Then, as Ubilk collapsed to the floor, he was about to bring the fan down on the man's head with no mercy whatsoever—

"—Stop it, Chisha. Killing him accomplishes nothing."

"But Your Excellency. This man knows things he should not know. Surely you did not speak of it to him yourself?"

"Fool. As if We would let something slip in front of a jester. No doubt he'll tell you, too, that he learned it from the stars, as you'd put it."

"Ahaha, quite right. Er, was I about to die there, just now?"

Poking at the iron fan that had stopped a hair's breadth from him, Ubilk gave a half-smile. Looking down at him, Chisha spent a moment weighing whether he truly shouldn't finish him off after all, then let out a long breath and stepped back several paces.

"My apologies for the discourtesy. That said, I must ask you to refrain from careless remarks going forward — I cannot guarantee my hand will stop a second time."

"Kehk, that was careless of me, I admit. As expected of one of the 'Nine Divine Generals' — they call you the scholarly type, but your skill with a blade is every bit a warrior's."

"Spare the flattery. More importantly—"

Cutting himself off there, Chisha shifted his gaze from Ubilk, turning it back behind him. There sat Vincent, unmoved in posture since the moment he'd received the "Stargazer," seated before the great desk where he conducted his affairs, watching him.

Facing those black eyes, Chisha suddenly found himself recalling how he'd first met this man. The very first time, face to face with Vincent, it had been about this same distance, inside a dragon carriage. Why had that memory surfaced now, all of a sudden? Probably because the feeling was the same. In the same state of mind as back then, Chisha had to ask Vincent something.

"Your Excellency, you do not appear surprised by Master Ubilk's prophecy just now. Might I ask the reason for that?"

"Um, mine isn't exactly a prophecy, more..."

"Pardon me. But I warn you now — there will not be a next time."

Ubilk, attempting to interject something unnecessary, had his cheek grazed by the iron fan, which buried itself in the wall. A thin line of blood welling from the shallow cut, Ubilk raised both hands and swore silence.

Without so much as glancing his way, Chisha fixed Vincent with a stare — no, a glare. At the intensity of that gaze, Vincent closed one eye and—

"He already reported the sign of this disaster to Us before. That the coming 'Great Disaster' is a calamity that shall bring ruin upon this empire — so whispered the Observers, he said."

"—Why, at that time, was this not shared with me as well... no."

Astonished by what Vincent had just told him, Chisha's tongue stalled mid-protest. Just now, in the words Vincent had spoken, he'd felt an inexplicable chill. Turning that feeling over, Chisha's eyes widened slightly.

"Your Excellency, unless I misheard, did you just say — Observers?"

That was a word he'd heard once before, long, long ago, years past, from Vincent's own mouth. A word said to have appeared in the memoir of the exiled imperial family member Vincent had called a loathsome, contemptible man — "Stride Vollachia." And now, here, Vincent had spoken it himself. As though he'd long since grasped it as a term connected to the "Stargazer" Ubilk, spoken as naturally as breathing.

"Your Excellency, might I take it that this 'Observer' refers to what Master Ubilk calls the 'Stars'?"

"...We hold the same understanding. What Ubilk here speaks of seems to be a kind of notice — something the Observers glimpsed, and relayed."

"—. Then, do you believe Stride Vollachia, too, was a 'Stargazer'?"

"We consider that possibility likely. Though he differs from other self-proclaimed 'Stargazers,' and reading through the contents of the memoir, Stride Vollachia regarded the Observers as an enemy."

As new facts spilled out one after another, Chisha's frustration with Vincent mounted. This man, both before and after becoming emperor, had continually taken on crushing burdens of work of his own volition — and yet, even in matters he didn't share with Chisha, he'd been carrying this kind of trouble too. Concealing it thoroughly from Chisha must have required no small effort on his part.

If he was willing to spend that much effort, he might as well have simply shared everything from the start.

"Do calm yourself, General Chisha. There's a proper reason His Excellency hasn't spoken of this to you. I merely respected that."

"I believe I warned there would not be a next time."

"I know, I know. Buuut, if His Excellency finds it difficult to say, perhaps it'd be better coming from me. That's why he had General Chisha present for this, isn't it?"

Hands still raised, Ubilk looked past Chisha's shoulder toward Vincent. That he behaved as though he understood Vincent's thinking grated on him, but Vincent didn't reject Ubilk's suggestion. If so, Chisha had no standing to reject it either. And besides, he was at his limit for being kept in the dark about whatever remained unspoken.

"I'd recommend choosing your words carefully, and without further delay."

"Much obliged for the consideration. Very well, I'll tell you plainly. — The 'Great Disaster' of ruin that shall befall the empire — that would be—"

Despite having been warned not to drag things out, Ubilk paused there once more. But Chisha's interest in what came next outweighed his anger. And so, Ubilk, without facing punishment for ignoring the warning, continued spinning out his words. And that was—

"—The death of His Excellency Vincent Vollachia. That is the trigger — the heavenly mandate that shall then begin."

The uncouth "Stargazer" withdrew from the room, leaving only the empire's summit and his closest confidant behind in the office. — No, whether that self-designation still held true, Chisha could no longer say with confidence. If his innermost thoughts were never revealed to him, he could hardly be called a confidant. Perhaps, after all, the mocking whispers directed at the "Stargazer," that jester's role, would have suited him better all along.

"Even disguised as Your Excellency and swapping places, the 'Stargazer' never once approached me. I wonder — was that, too, your instruction?"

"Were what We keep hidden spoken of carelessly, the scheme would fail to function. Naturally, We bound his potential slip beforehand."

"I imagined as much. I would have done the same, in your position."

Not quite collusion, but it turned out that Ubilk, who had seemed to move about so freely, had in fact been fitted with Vincent's leash all along, thoroughly. Behind it lay some secret pact concerning the "Great Disaster," exchanged solely between the two of them. But—

"I cannot make sense of it. Why keep even me in the dark about it?"

"—. To spare needless, tangled deliberation."

"Needless and tangled..."

"Knowing, you would think to try to save Us. But that is a meaningless line of thought."

Shaking his head, Vincent declared it without a shred of hesitation. Even with his own life on the line, faced with the fact of it, Vincent showed no sign of flinching. But his own acceptance of it, and Chisha's acceptance of it, existed on entirely different planes.

"Placing Your Excellency's life above all else is only natural. Why call that meaningless?"

"Recall the reports the 'Stargazer' has brought before now — the ones he claimed were heaven's mandate."

"The reports Master Ubilk brought..."

Prompted, Chisha turned the thought over in his mind. If the "Great Disaster" was the only matter Vincent had concealed from him, then every other prophecy Ubilk had delivered, Chisha was already fully aware of. Warnings of natural disasters, rebellions — signs of events that could each have become a great conflagration, and useful ones at that. Thanks to them, damage had been kept to a minimum—

"—Every one of those events occurred regardless, whatever the difference in scale of the damage."

"That's right. However the signs were foretold, not a single instance was ever prevented before it began. Whether man-made or natural, the first blow always lands, without fail. Only the aftermath can be managed."

And Ubilk had said—

"The 'Great Disaster' he speaks of shall occur triggered by Our own death."

"—! Then all Your Excellency need do is protect his own life and defy the 'Stargazer's' mandate!"

"Do you imagine We haven't tried to determine whether that's even possible?"

The reflexive counter-proposal was refuted by Vincent's quiet words. Naturally, whatever emotionally-driven idea occurred to Chisha, Vincent must have already put it into practice himself.

Up until now, with regard to the other prophecies Ubilk had brought, unrelated to the "Great Disaster," he must have tried, again and again, to see whether prevention was possible. Fulfilling the role of emperor to the fullest, all the while, behind the scenes—

"—Your Excellency."

"What."

"A question has come to mind, it seems."

Suddenly, his own voice, cold and sunken, slid into his brain, and Chisha found he'd already said it. He'd said he wanted to ask something, but his mind was numb. Numbed — whether from being made to work in overdrive, or as a form of mental resistance refusing to function, he couldn't tell. But whichever the cause of the numbness, the question had already been voiced, and the emperor had received it. And—

"Granted. Speak it."

What the emperor chose, Chisha could not deny. And so, what the emperor had granted permission for, Chisha likewise could not refuse. And so, tasting the numbness in his own mind, Chisha voiced the question.

"Since when has Your Excellency known the signs of this 'Great Disaster'?"

"—A memoir."

Quietly, Vincent opened the drawer of the desk at which he worked, drew out a single, old, worn book from within, and set it upon the desk. The memoir that had once come up in conversation, but which he'd never had the chance to see — Stride Vollachia's remaining memoir. If it had spoken of this sign all along—

"Your Excellency, everything you have done up to now was—"

Meeting Chisha, once Cheshire Trim, walking alongside him, cultivating time together, picking up Cecilus, facing the "Imperial Selection Ceremony," defying his own way of being for the first time to save Prisca Benedictus's life, and opening a new path as the Vollachian Empire's emperor.

All of it, this journey of Vincent Vollachia's—

"—After Our death, the damage wrought by the 'Great Disaster's' ruin must be kept to a minimum. That is the purpose of the reforms, and of the 'Nine Divine Generals'—"

"——"

"—And of you, Chisha Gold."


△▼△▼△▼△


"I demand you disclose whatever information you hold regarding the identity of the Observers."

Pressing the opened iron fan against his throat, Chisha issued the threat in a low voice. But even bathed in Chisha's fierce, hostile intent, pinned back against the wall, Ubilk's expression remained one of puzzled, carefree confusion, as though he didn't quite grasp the situation. With that same expression still fixed on his face, Ubilk called out "General Chisha" and—

"You spoke with His Excellency several days ago — I'd rather assumed you'd come to terms with it by now, General Chisha, but..."

"It took several days to reexamine whether my premises had been in error. Regrettably, as things stand, there is no way to outright refute His Excellency's reasoning."

"I imagine speaking with me would yield the same result. Though, I must say, as expected of the general who serves as His Excellency's body double — the resemblance really is remarkable."

Spoken to while pinned, Chisha's expression twisted faintly with suspicion. Catching that reaction, Ubilk shook his head and—

"His Excellency, too, asked whether there was any way to bend heaven's mandate. Buuut, my answer is the same now as it was then. There isn't one."

"—. What is it you want, exactly? Master Ubilk, you've claimed the title 'Stargazer' and won yourself a standing within the Crystal Palace by conveying the Observers' prophecies. But myself included, no one but His Excellency thinks well of your presence here. Should His Excellency die as your prophecy foretells, you too cannot escape your own ruin."

"You do speak plainly, don't you — it wounds me, truly. ...Buuut, General Chisha, that's where your premise differs from reality."

"——"

"My own ruin matters far less to me than the fulfillment and completion of heaven's mandate. My purpose is to prevent the ruin that follows the 'Great Disaster.'"

As he spoke, the smirk drained from Ubilk's expression. With the deliberate emotion gone, the warmth stripped from his face, Chisha, catching him up close, felt for the first time as though he were looking at Ubilk's true face. For a moment, he suspected it might be some technique of Ubilk's own — but—

"I don't have any power like that, you know. To show His Excellency I bore no ill will from the start, I destroyed my own Evil Eyes right in front of you, General Chisha, didn't I. Would you like to confirm?"

"—. No, that's quite all right. More importantly, unless I misheard, did you just say your purpose is to prevent the ruin that follows the 'Great Disaster'?"

Answering, Ubilk showed no hesitation, and Chisha could sense the opinion wasn't a lie. To begin with, if he meant to lie here, there'd be no point lying to please Chisha rather than Vincent. Carelessly touching Chisha's temper and getting killed for it wouldn't please even a man like Ubilk, who wore the face of someone who didn't mind dying at any moment.

More than that, it explained why Vincent, despite regarding Ubilk as troublesome and having reservations about his conduct, had never let him go. Vincent and Ubilk were moving toward the same goal.

"Master Ubilk, you don't seem inclined to try preventing the 'Great Disaster' itself. Why is that?"

"Ah, that's simple enough. The 'Great Disaster' cannot be postponed. It's bound to happen regardless — a fixed certainty, of a sort. My purpose is to prevent the ruin that the 'Great Disaster,' once it occurs, would bring about. In other words..."

"In other words?"

"If the 'Great Disaster' doesn't occur, my heavenly mandate goes unfulfilled. So, even if every cause that could trigger the 'Great Disaster' were eliminated, I myself would likely become that cause."

It was a fanatical way of thinking, means and ends utterly inverted. Swallowing hard at the incomprehensible absurdity of it, Chisha pressed the iron fan at Ubilk's throat harder still. Positioned right at a vital point — with enough force, it would slice his throat open. Making Ubilk himself fully aware of that fact, Chisha sharpened his voice.

"Then perhaps it would be best if I removed that cause here, by my own hand."

"I'd rather you didn't, but I won't stop you. Buuut, I'll say this much: even if you kill me, another 'Stargazer' will simply appear in my place. That's how the system works."

"——"

"The four great calamities that shall bring ruin to the world. The chance has come around to stop one of them. We're terminals of the stars' own self-purification and self-defense — there are as many replacements as needed."

Staring intently into those eyes, impossible to pin to any single color, Chisha ground his teeth. That this was neither threat nor falsehood — that Ubilk, at the very least, meant every word of it in earnest — Chisha could feel clearly, unmistakably.

—To prevent the ruin caused by the "Great Disaster," the "Great Disaster" must, without fail, be made to occur.

A declaration so absurd, so unreasonable, and yet one even Vincent had no means of countering — an invitation to a war of attrition, shielded behind an inexhaustible supply of lives. Perhaps, even among the prophecies past "Stargazers" had delivered as heaven's mandate, some had likewise been set alight by the "Stargazers" themselves, playing their own part in bringing the prophecy to fulfillment.

But to confirm that, exterminating every "Stargazer" would require killing every single person who might possibly become one, since the conditions of their emergence remained unknown. That would amount to nothing less than destroying the nation itself.

"His Excellency truly is admirable. I don't tend to feel much personal attachment to individuals, myself, but I take my hat off to His Excellency's way of being. Not being a 'Stargazer' myself, and yet managing that — I could never."

"...Master Ubilk taking his hat off — now that's a joke that isn't funny."

"I mean it sincerely. No one can accept a foretold death without the comfort of heaven's mandate to soften it. And yet His Excellency has made every preparation toward his own death. He hasn't wished to live, but he hasn't given up fighting either. Truly, the Wolf of the Sword."

There was genuine admiration for Vincent in the solemnity of Ubilk's murmured words. The "sword wolf" he spoke of — the wolf pierced by the sword, the crest of the Vollachian nation — was an image honoring a warrior's way of being: one who, even bearing a mortal wound, never flinches. In that sense, as Ubilk said, Vincent's way of being truly was that sword wolf itself.

"General Chisha, I've spent my whole life single-mindedly trying to fulfill my own heavenly mandate. Buuut, that's not to say I think my own mandate is the most wonderful thing there is. If General Chisha finds it unacceptable, testing it out might not be a bad idea."

"Testing it... you mean the annihilation of yourself, and of the other 'Stargazers' who share your position?"

"I said there are as many replacements as needed, but there is a limit. That limit is the number of human lives available. Cooperating with General Cecilus and giving it a try might be one option."

How much of that proposal was genuinely sincere, Ubilk's true feelings were, once again, unreadable. That he'd entertained the idea, even for a moment, that it mightn't be so bad — that alone was proof of how unsteady his own footing had become. As Ubilk said, revealing this truth to Cecilus, guiding him — likely thoroughly baffled by the whole thing — could well result in the empire's people being cut down wholesale.

—No, that too was impossible. Should it reach Vincent's ears, such an outrage would be stopped instantly. Cecilus was a friend, but his priorities within himself were firmly fixed. To Cecilus, Vincent came before Chisha. That, precisely, was why Cecilus Segmunt was "One" — even though his powers of thought couldn't be trusted in the slightest.

"Even so, that the option of confiding in Cecilus never once crossed my mind — even considering it's my own affair, it strikes me as strange."

An out-of-place sentiment carved a weak smile onto Chisha's lips. In his mind, Cecilus's pouting face, launching into furious protest, rose vividly—

"——"

An exchange from some day came back to him, suddenly, vividly.

"Sure, if you and I went head to head, you'd be dead in the blink of an eye, Chisha — but what if you started off with a thousand men under your command? Might take a thousand seconds!"

"It wouldn't take that long, surely. Though I take your point. While I bought those thousand seconds—"

"His Excellency, or whoever, gets to accomplish the goal, that's the idea! The biggest flaw in me, the star player of this whole world, is that there's only one of me in it."

His friend's laughing face, saying whatever came to mind, resurfaced in his memory. And, quite suddenly, a thought occurred to him.

"Master Ubilk, there's something I'd like to ask. — In what manner is the heavenly mandate you receive delivered to you?"

At the question, Ubilk's eyes widened slightly in surprise. Then he tilted his head, and, recovering something of his usual, less solemn air—

"It varies. Comparatively speaking, I tend to receive it in something like a dream, most often."

"Then, the mandate concerning the 'Great Disaster' that you conveyed to His Excellency — was that the same?"

At the repeated question, Ubilk nodded, a puzzled look on his face. Letting out a breath at the answer, Chisha slowly gave shape to the thought that had formed in his mind.

"If the mandate Master Ubilk received is the moment His Excellency's life is lost—"

Bleached pure white, no one in particular, an uncertain self capable of being dyed any color at all. What would appear there was—

"—the Vincent Vollachia who dies there — which Vincent Vollachia would he be, I wonder?"


△▼△▼△▼△


—Chisha Gold, once Cheshire Trim, never thought of himself as a man of especially high loyalty, nor as one who had sworn absolute obedience to His Excellency the emperor.

None other than Vincent Vollachia himself had told him so. When asked whether he could die for his sake, and he had answered flatly that he could not.

Serve me while holding to that same conviction, he'd been told. And so told, he had obeyed, and in that same spirit, Chisha had served Vincent ever since.

So he would not do anything so foolish as to die for Vincent's sake. Though he held no absolute loyalty to Vincent, he did hold ordinary loyalty, the sensible kind. As one subject of the empire, as one of the empire's generals, he carried a proper measure of respect for His Excellency the emperor.

Acting in defiance of Vincent's orders — unthinkable. And so, Chisha's choice here was not made for Vincent's sake — it was, through and through, the same feeling he'd held back at the very start, that day he'd happened upon the prince by the roadside.

That day, seeing a dragon carriage that anyone could tell at a glance carried someone of exalted rank, the many people gathered around had avoided getting involved. If they helped and it went well, fine — but if they failed and drew displeasure, their lives would be at risk. Understanding that reasoning as if he could reach out and touch it, Chisha had walked up to the carriage anyway.

It hadn't been because he wanted to help someone. It was simply that an opportunity had arrived to test the arithmetic, the learning, that his home village had told him was useless — and it seemed a waste to let it pass by.

This time, too, was the same.

That chance to catch a glimpse behind the thinking of Vincent Vollachia, who wielded his schemes as though everything always unfolded exactly as he expected — such an opportunity would surely never come again. And what's more, this outlandishly extraordinary man had been quietly preparing, ever since childhood, to accept that very moment when it came.

In other words, this was a challenge against the greatest gamble of Vincent Vollachia's entire life, staked on his own existence. And when a challenge presents itself, blood runs hot; the larger the enemy, the higher the spirits soar, the soul catching fire over the means of bringing that opponent down — that was the way of a man of the empire.

Chisha Gold was a man of the empire.

With what feelings, what thoughts, Vincent Vollachia had intended, after his own death, to entrust to Chisha — whom he himself had trained to wield the same cunning as his own — Chisha did not know.

He didn't know, and didn't want to know. He'd had quite enough of that blood-draining feeling of learning something he hadn't understood, after the fact.

Feel the same yourself, was all he had to say to Vincent.

And then—

"The ruin the 'Great Disaster' will bring... what a 'Great Disaster,' don't make me laugh."

Bearing a name like "Great Calamity," apparently intent on trampling this Vollachian Empire underfoot and dragging it down into collapse — but to Chisha, it was a joke.

The trigger for this "Great Disaster's" beginning was the death of Vincent Vollachia. A "Great Disaster" that couldn't even begin unless Vincent died — meaning that as long as he lived, it couldn't destroy the empire. Already beaten before it had even begun to destroy anything, wasn't it?

Avoiding Vincent Vollachia entirely, and calling that ruin, calling that a "Great Disaster"?

—The seventy-seventh emperor of the Sacred Vollachia Empire, Vincent Vollachia.

"His Excellency is the Vollachian Empire itself. Trampling the land after His Excellency's passing and calling that ruin — what a laughable, side-splitting farce."

Mocking the yet-unseen "Great Disaster" in a manner not unlike Cecilus's own. Mocking, sticking his tongue out at, a "Great Disaster" he himself would probably never live to witness.

"A dog trying to steal a cheap victory could never kill our sword wolf. — Do not underestimate the Vincent Vollachia that I have supported, that I have helped shape."

Kill him if you can. Take it from him if you dare.

Foretold ruin be damned — don't imagine for one second you could destroy our emperor, our empire.

That day, pulling that wheel from the ditch — it had all been for this moment. Everything, all of it, mocking this very ruin—

"—Foreshadowing, one might say. Though, since it would only make Cecilus insufferably smug, I shall absolutely never say it aloud."


△▼△▼△▼△


—A flash of white light illuminated the throne room brilliantly, and immediately after, a spray of red blood scattered magnificently.

Upon the red carpet, drenched grandly in the spilled blood, stood a slender man facing forward. Wearing on his face a demon mask, almost a joke in itself, the man widened the black eyes hidden behind it, freezing in astonishment at the event unfolding before him.

Before that frozen man, the blood-soaked body collapsed forward, crumpling to the floor.

From behind, an unguarded chest was pierced straight through the back, mercilessly destroying the heart, stripping away utterly everything necessary to sustain life.

"——"

No arm reached to support the falling body; it collapsed helplessly onto the carpet. And there it lay, unmoving, without so much as a twitch — no final words, not even a last breath escaping.

No one pierced through the heart could go on living. And so it was an inevitable event, a fate that arrived precisely because it was destined to arrive.

"——"

The man died.

Cheshire Trim, and Chisha Gold, and then, as Vincent Vollachia — the man died.


—That, was the entirety of what occurred in this single instant.
