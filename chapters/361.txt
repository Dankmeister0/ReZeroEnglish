Arc 5 Chapter 45: "The Inescapable Curse"

The name of a witch he'd never heard of, delivered from Wilhelm's mouth, sent a shudder through him.
The only "Witches" Subaru knew of, aside from Satella, were the six who bore the names of the Seven Deadly Sins whom he'd met in Echidna's graveyard.
That there could exist a "Witch" beyond those was a bolt from the blue.

"So, Wilhelm-san, are you saying this witch, Sphinx, is connected to this Witch Cult attack... that there's a witch out there besides the Sin Archbishops?"

If that were true, the enemy's main force would be four Sin Archbishops plus two of the dead. Add a witch on top of that, and their already dismal odds would become truly hopeless.
But Wilhelm raised a hand against Subaru's concern.

"My apologies. I misspoke. The witch Sphinx's existence was extinguished during that Demi-Human War. There is no room for her to be involved in this attack."

"The witch is dead? Are you sure about that? My impression of witches is that they fake their own deaths, or even when they really do die, they just go on doing as they please anyway."

That was true of Satella, who surfaced whenever Subaru brushed against the taboo, and of Echidna too, who'd been thoroughly enjoying the afterlife in that dream castle.
Being told a witch was dead was no guarantee of anything at all.

"I cannot say what impression of witches Subaru-dono holds, but Sphinx, witch or not, was only ever called that as a matter of convenience. It is only that the Royal Army referred to her that way — she herself never claimed the title."

"She herself — Wilhelm-san, you knew her directly?"

"Several times, during the civil war. One could say the end of the Demi-Human War came about directly because Sphinx's head was taken. Roswaal, Bordeaux, and my wife were the ones responsible for that."

"Roswaal!?"

An unexpected name leapt out, and Subaru's eyes went wide.
At Subaru's reaction, Wilhelm drew his chin in, his gaze growing distant for a moment.

"I had dealings with the previous Roswaal-kyo's predecessor. At the time, I was not especially close with him, but... I was in his debt."

"Predecessor's predecessor... ah, right. The name Roswaal is passed down generation to generation, isn't it."

"Unfortunately, that Roswaal passed away early. After that we grew distant, and the current Mathers-kyo I know only by sight. No, this is beside the point."

The relationship was so unexpected that Subaru's ears had been drawn in, but that certainly wasn't the main topic here.
Subaru nodded, and Wilhelm continued with "So, then—"

"Though this is not Sphinx, I believe the Curse Arts being employed are likely of the same lineage. A technique for manipulating corpses — at the time, they were called Corpse Soldiers."

"Corpse Soldiers... um, do they have any weaknesses?"

"As far as I know, a Corpse Soldier is merely a technique that moves a corpse. It cannot fully reproduce the skill the person possessed in life — it only defiles the dead, reflecting the caster's own vile nature."

"But 'Eight Arms,' and... that..."

He trailed off.
Wilhelm's wife, made into a Corpse Soldier, her death violated. Logically, Wilhelm had accepted that — but even so, Subaru hesitated to say it himself.
At Subaru's hesitation, Wilhelm's lips curved bitterly.

"I appreciate the consideration. But it is necessary. ...Yes, my wife and Kurgan's skill approaches what it was in life. It exceeds what a mere Corpse Soldier should be able to draw out."

"Then isn't there a possibility it's something other than a Corpse Soldier? If so, maybe your wife isn't really dead..."

"My wife died. Because I was not strong enough."

It was Subaru who, weak-mindedly, had tried to cling to hope.
Wilhelm's quiet voice cut that feeling down in a single stroke.
And to the old swordsman's profile, Subaru had no words left to offer.

"Even then, there existed, on very rare occasion, cases that could not be simply declared ordinary Corpse Soldiers. Whether it is some aptitude for the technique, or some other factor, I cannot say... but the prowess of those two should be understood in that light."

"Is there a way to defeat them?"

"Destroy the body utterly and completely. Or else, gouge out the curse-mark that exists somewhere upon it. Do that, and the Corpse Soldier returns to being a mere corpse. It must be done."

Wilhelm's anguished voice was hard to listen to.
He was conscious of staying calm, striving to keep his eyes fixed on what he had to do — even though the tremor in his voice, his clenched fist, his wide-open eyes, hid nothing at all.

"I apologize for keeping you so long. We cannot make Crusch-sama wait any longer. Please, go on ahead."

Wilhelm bowed at the waist, indicating the door directly ahead, now close at hand. The room at the very back of the fourth floor bore a collapsed plate marked "Break Room."
Inside it, Crusch, who had asked for Subaru, was waiting.

Passing by Wilhelm, Subaru walked toward the door, his footsteps echoing.
The distance to the door felt strangely far. The soles of his shoes seemed to stick to the floor, as if something were obstructing his advance.
Subaru knew clearly that this was his own weakness, his cowardice.

"—It's me. Natsuki Subaru. Um, Crusch-san?"

He knocked on the door and called out at a volume uncertain to carry through. There was a moment's silence, then the door slowly opened from the other side.
The face that appeared was Ferris's. Only, he looked utterly changed.

"Subaru-kyun..."

Eyes red and swollen from crying, chestnut hair disheveled. His whole body was stained reddish-black with someone else's blood — he clearly hadn't had the composure to wipe away what had splashed onto his pale skin. Fresh blood clung thickly to his cheeks and neck as well.
At that harrowing sight, Subaru's breath caught in his throat.

"I heard Crusch-san's calling for me. So..."

"Mm. She's inside, on the bed. ...Please, whatever you do, don't do anything unnecessary."

His voice was rigid, and by the end, something like hatred bled into it.
But that hatred wasn't directed at Subaru. It was, in a sense, directed at everything, in every direction. A rage with nowhere to go, hating this entire world, was what now ruled Ferris.

Taking a deep breath, Subaru followed Ferris's back as he returned inside.
Even called a break room, it wasn't a particularly large space. Long tables and chairs stood in two rows, and at the back was a small room partitioned off by a threshold. The bed was inside it.
And on that shabby bed, she lay.

"Na-, tsuki-sama?"

Crusch, still conscious, noticed Subaru entering the room and called his name.
Trying to respond to her voice, Subaru's throat seized up. To brace himself, to feign composure, to offer reassuring words — even something that simple, he found himself unable to do.

"I'm sorry... for such an unsightly appearance..."

"...No. No, that's... not true. That's not true at all."

Seeing Subaru go rigid, Crusch apologized in a weak voice. Flustered by her pained bearing, Subaru forced out words to cover himself.

—Having been drenched in Capella's blood and marked with the curse, Crusch was in a terrible state.

Across the visible skin of her neck and limbs, black blood vessels rose up in mottled patches. It wasn't hard to imagine that the skin beneath her towel blanket and clothes had suffered the same. The black vessels, which carried no blood of their own, pulsed repeatedly, and it was clear they were constricting her slender body as though a snake were writhing around it.
Her pale skin, once without a single blemish, was hideously violated.

Naturally, the damage wasn't confined below her neck.
Crusch's cold, refined beauty, which had always called to mind a slender sword — the left side of it had been eroded by the mottling. By some cruel irony, the right side of her face remained as beautiful as ever. That only made the contrast between left and right starker, a protest against the injustice of something noble being defiled.
An eyepatch had been placed over her left eye, but what lay beneath it was too much even to imagine.

"Is this... the same Dragon's Blood curse as mine?"

If so, nothing could be more cruel.
Precisely because Subaru knew Crusch Karsten, his heartache had no limit.

He looked down at his own right leg. Like Crusch's skin, black vessels ran through it in mottled patches. But aside from that gruesome appearance, Subaru's leg was unaffected. No pain, no throbbing sensation — nothing at all was inflicted on Subaru.
Crusch, though, was clearly different. She gasped painfully, and each time the mottling pulsed, her breath showed signs of enduring pain.

"Ferris..."

He turned to look at the healer, the greatest in the kingdom, wondering if nothing could be done. But Subaru's thoughtless glance only wounded Ferris further, as he ground his teeth against his own helplessness.
Ferris bit his lip, dug his nails into his own arm, and hung his head. He, more than anyone, understood — and regretted — the insufficiency of his own power in this place.

Knowing the bond between the two of them, there was no room to doubt that every method Subaru could possibly think of had already been tried.

"Crusch-san... what do you want from me?"

In a situation this painful, why had she called for him?
He couldn't imagine what he could do. Was there something she wanted to say? To take revenge on the "Lust" who had done this to her, perhaps. Or some words of resentment aimed at Subaru.
Whether he was cursed at, or entrusted with a dying grudge, he would accept it.

At Subaru's question, Crusch opened her mouth, pained.
He leaned in close to her lips, straining his ears not to miss her faint breath.
And then—

"...I'm, glad, you're safe."

"——"

"I heard... you took... the same curse as me..."

Subaru felt a softness settle into her breath, as if she were relieved.
At the same moment, he understood the truth in his own heart, and nearly died of shame at how pathetic it was.

He had thought it would be so much easier to be blamed.
That was why he had doubted Crusch's nobility, why he had debased the loftiness of her heart. All she had done was purely worry, for his sake, that he might be suffering the same agony as her.

"I'm sorry... I'm sorry, Crusch-san...!"

His voice wrung out under a jumble of emotions — doubting her heart, the fact that things had come to this suffering, the fact that he couldn't even take her place in her pain.
His hand shot out reflexively and took Crusch's hand, which lay limply on her stomach. There was no sensation in touching the black blood vessels. That her skin's texture remained unchanged despite such a distorted appearance made it all the more pitiable. Only—

"Fu, uh...?"

"Guh!?"

A breath escaped Crusch, and at the same instant, agony shot up Subaru's throat.
A pain like gripping red-hot iron stabbed through his entire body, originating from his palm. Subaru instinctively released Crusch's hand and looked at his own palm, where the pain had run through.
On that palm, the mottled encroachment had appeared.

"What... is this...!?"

"Let me see, Subaru-kyun!"

Taking Subaru's groaning hand, Ferris examined the encroachment. The light of healing magic covered the mottling, but there was no sign of the pain fading, nor of the encroachment vanishing. But instead—

"Ferris... Crusch-san's hand!"

"Eh...?"

Drawn by Subaru's widened eyes, Ferris looked toward Crusch. His yellow eyes went wide at the same sight Subaru saw.
From the back of Crusch's left hand — the one Subaru had held — the mottled encroachment had thinned, if only as a token amount.
That change, along with a glance down at his own right hand, sent a certain thought flashing through Subaru's mind.

"Don't tell me... it moved from Crusch-san's body into mine...?"

There was no other way to think of it. The change in the hand that had touched and the hand touched were, respectively, plus and minus. There was no room to doubt that the curse dwelling in Crusch's body had transferred to Subaru.

"B-but, there's no change in me at all? I've touched Crusch-sama so many times, over and over, examining her body, and... I... haven't..."

Ferris shook his head at Subaru's hypothesis.
It wasn't the movement of someone rejoicing that a possibility of healing had sprouted — it was the movement of someone doubting whether the hypothesis might be wrong. No, surely his feelings were different from that.

"I can't... give Crusch-sama any peace...!"

"Then I'll check again."

Pushing past the faltering Ferris, Subaru stood once more before Crusch. Crusch, still not understanding what was happening, turned moist eyes toward Subaru as he approached. Bracing himself so his tense expression wouldn't show in the eye blocked by the eyepatch—
He gently touched Crusch's cheek again, to confirm it once more.

"—Gu, uh!"

Immediately, a scorching agony stabbed into Subaru's brain, like magma coursing through his veins. The curse defiling Crusch's body flowed in through his fingertips, searing his nerves.

"Gah, aaaaah!?"

Screaming at the unbearable pain, Subaru's throat shook as he arched backward. He fell backward from the momentum, and the hand touching her left Crusch.

"Ah, hah, haah..."

His lungs seized, his eyeballs twitched.
Like a fish pulled onto land, Subaru opened and closed his mouth, desperately seeking oxygen.

"S-Subaru-kyun... are you okay?"

Seeing his breathing begin to steady, Ferris asked. With enough composure returning that he could feel the hardness of the floor beneath him, Subaru somehow managed to sit up.
Then, looking at Crusch's face on the bed—

"How is it, Ferris? Did it help even a little?"

"Ah..."

Ferris's hips sank as he checked on Crusch's condition, plopping down.
He must have seen it too — Crusch's cheek, eroded by the curse, had been ever so slightly saved from that encroachment. If this treatment was possible, then saving Crusch too, might—

"You mustn't, Natsuki-sama..."

Subaru rose again, ready to try once more. But it was none other than Crusch herself who stopped that action.
Not understanding the meaning of her words, Subaru moved to ask what was wrong.

"Have you not noticed? Your own... hand..."

"—Hand?"

At her words, he looked down at his right hand. And finally understood the change.
Like his right leg, skin marked with black mottled vessels ran across it. That much was fine. That alone wouldn't have shaken his resolve to take on Crusch's curse.
But there was something clearly wrong.

Compared to the mottling he'd taken from Crusch, the range of encroachment was far too large.
What he'd drawn from her body was only from the back of her left hand and her left cheek — a change amounting to no more than a thinning of the black vessels that had surfaced. But meanwhile, Subaru's right arm, which should have taken that on, was covered densely in mottling from the elbow down, on the back side. The density was clearly incomparable.

The exchange rate of the curse being taken on wasn't anything close to one-to-one. Even ten-to-one would be a generous estimate.

"No, but..."

Whether that was reason enough to hesitate was another matter entirely.
There was pain in the moment of taking it on. But once it was actually absorbed into his body like this, there was, so far, no sign that the curse would go on to torment Subaru.
Compared to Crusch, who suffered continuously, whatever pain Subaru endured was momentary. And between a man and a woman, there was no need to even consider which of them the ugliness of this curse would weigh on more heavily.

If it meant saving Crusch, what did it matter if his right leg and right arm turned completely black?

"Natsuki-sama, no. ...I cannot accept that feeling of yours."

"Don't say something so stupid. I can handle being a little uncomfortable. Compared to regretting some tattoo you got on a whim later in life, this is a much better way to mess up your body, if you ask me. I can take the pain too. Strangely enough, it's not hard on me at all. So—"

"Can you be certain that will remain true? ...The two of us — you and I — might both become unable to fight. In the current situation, that would be fatal..."

Crusch's judgment, worrying about the city and others rather than herself, was logically correct. But not everything can be settled by logic alone.

"Ferris, stop Natsuki-sama..."

"I... I..."

"Please. Natsuki-sama is needed now, by people other than me..."

"If Subaru-kyun keeps pushing himself... Crusch-sama's suffering..."

Ferris's bewildered judgment came from placing Crusch at the very top of his priorities. No one could blame him for that. No one here was wrong.
The fact that no one was wrong — was itself the thing that was wrong.

"You mustn't be swept up by a moment's emotion. Natsuki-sama, please..."

"Crusch-san, even so, I—"

"Didn't you say it yourself? —Leave everything else to me."

"—!"

Crusch's pleading eyes held Subaru fast, refusing to let go.
Had he really said such powerful words himself? Having heard them, was Crusch now telling him to make good on them?

"Say it to me too, please..."

"——"

"Leave everything else to me."

A pained smile waited for Subaru's words.
He held his breath, worked his tongue in his parched mouth, and quietly closed his eyes.

Chided for trying to leap at an immediate salvation without thinking of what came after, made to say even words he shouldn't have had to say — so at the very least—

"Crusch-san, please rest easy."

"...Natsuki-, sama."

"You can leave everything else to me."

"—Yes."

He had to at least be able to offer the role, and the words, that were being asked of him.

Hearing Subaru's answer, Crusch let out a long breath, as if relieved.
Her eyelids fell shut without strength, proof that she had been holding onto consciousness by sheer will up until now. Her breathing quickly grew shallow, and the time began again in which Crusch fought the curse's encroachment.

"Sorry, Ferris. I have to go too."

"What should... I do?"

As Subaru pulled the towel blanket back over Crusch and stood, a faint voice reached him. Seeing Ferris this utterly worn down was a first for Subaru.
Honestly, he wanted to keep Ferris by Crusch's side the whole time, without pause.
But the situation, and Ferris's abilities, wouldn't allow it.

"Your power is needed. I won't tell you to leave the city hall. But I'll arrange it so that if anything happens, the wounded are carried here. So, I'm counting on that."

"...The one person I most want to help, is the one I can't help. Funny, isn't it."

"Ferris..."

"Sorry. That was a stupid thing to say. ...Let me have some time alone with her, for a while."

Turning his face away, Ferris sank into the chair beside the bed. Subaru gave his shoulder one last light pat, then stepped out of the break room.
In the hallway, Wilhelm stood just as he had when Subaru went in, waiting at attention.

"Thank you for taking Crusch-sama's feelings to heart."

Wilhelm spoke as Subaru returned. Whether the goings-on inside had carried through, or whether Subaru's face simply told the whole story.

"It's not some fine thing like 'taking her feelings to heart.' It's more like I got a kick in the pants. ...What is my body, anyway."

Taking on Crusch's curse, or the encroachment simply slowing on its own — going back further, even things like the Witch Factor and Return by Death remained entirely ambiguous.
Would there ever come a day when reasons, and conclusions, could be found for any of it?

"Let's leave Crusch-san's care to Ferris. Once everything's settled, I want to try what just happened again."

"That right arm — will it be all right?"

"It doesn't look too cool, honestly. Maybe if I wear long sleeves and gloves it'll look the part. ...If it's to save one beautiful girl, a scar that won't heal is nothing much to worry about."

Even with the reluctance he felt, that was also Subaru's honest feeling.
If there was no other solution, he'd take on all of Crusch's curse. Even if his body turned pitch black for it, Emilia, Rem, and Beatrice would just have to forgive him.

"But that's all a matter for after we get through this crisis. Wilhelm-san, let's head downstairs. They should be discussing the plan to take the control tower by now."

Their forces had likely gathered the best that could possibly be assembled.
What remained was the matter of each Sin Archbishop's abilities and compatibility with them, and the division of roles and timing for the assault. Only about six hours remained until the deadline the Witch Cult had demanded.

"Subaru-dono, regarding that matter, I have a request."

"A request?"

Wilhelm stopped Subaru as he was about to head for the stairs. The old swordsman nodded, his gaze flicking toward the door behind him — the one behind which his lord lay, worry plain in his eyes.

"If possible, I would ask you to recommend that I be given the role of striking down 'Lust.' I am well aware of the might of that Authority, of super-regeneration and mutation. Even so, I ask this."

"Revenge for what happened to Crusch-san, is that it?"

"That, too. But more than that, 'Lust' must be made to tell, while still alive, what was done to Crusch-sama. For that, I would become a devil myself. I will cut him down with my blade, and before I take his head, I will make certain to learn the truth."

The killing intent radiating from the sword demon felt to Subaru like a wave of heat.
Fury, blade-intent — Wilhelm's spirit, unmatched by either, blazed for his lord's vengeance.

"That's the spirit, but... is it all right, about the Corpse Soldier?"

"——"

"It's your wife, someone you knew, isn't it? Whatever happens, you're the one who has to bring it to an end."

"Subaru-dono, Reinhard has arrived downstairs, has he not?"

Wilhelm cut in abruptly on Subaru's raised concern.
Taken aback, Subaru nonetheless nodded. Reinhard's strength was indispensable to the operation. Still, it was certain that the matter of the previous Sword Saint was a sore point for him as well.

"Would you refrain from telling Reinhard the identity of the Corpse Soldier?"

"...Eh?"

Confused, Subaru couldn't immediately grasp the meaning of the request.

"That's... you mean, don't tell him about your wife? Is that what you mean?"

"Yes, that's right. I don't want Reinhard — my grandson — to meet his dead grandmother. He would surely blame himself. Because of me, and no one else."

"Because of you, Wilhelm-san? That's not..."

He wanted to say it wasn't true, but Subaru couldn't bring himself to say it so lightly.
What came to mind was this morning — Heinkel's words that had shattered their moment of peace.

There was no proof to it. But it hadn't been denied, either.
Wilhelm had accused Reinhard, his grandson, of being the cause of his wife's death. That unbelievable past had not been denied.

"Subaru-dono, are you aware that the 'Divine Protection of Sword Saint' is something special?"

"...Honestly, there's more I don't know than what I do. I guess my understanding is just that it's a Divine Protection every person called 'Sword Saint' had, and that having it made you incredibly strong, something like that."

"Your understanding is, broadly, not mistaken. But if there is one point where the 'Divine Protection of Sword Saint' differs from other Divine Protections... it is that it is passed down."

"A Divine Protection that's... passed down..."

Wilhelm nodded at Subaru's murmur.
The old swordsman kept his eyes closed, as if recalling some painful past.

"That Divine Protection has been handed down, generation after generation, ever since the time of the first, Reid Astrea. It dwells within the bloodline of House Astrea, and always chooses the next Sword Saint from within the family. My wife's Divine Protection, too, was one that passed to Reinhard."

"So that's how it works — a Divine Protection passed down within a family... I see, that's it. So when your wife died, it passed to Reinhard—"

Understanding began to dawn, and just as it was about to settle, something snagged in Subaru's mind.
The previous Sword Saint had been defeated by the White Whale, and upon her death, the Divine Protection had passed to Reinhard. A painful past, but in a sense, that could be called a legitimate way for it to be passed on.
That flow didn't match up with the argument within House Astrea this morning.

Wilhelm's grief, Heinkel's mockery, Reinhard's silence — something was obstructing that legitimate succession.
And the answer to that was—

"It was in the middle of the battle against the White Whale."

"Wilhelm... san..."

"—Reinhard inherited the Divine Protection in the midst of the Great Conquest my wife had gone to fight in. During that battle, my wife was forsaken by the Sword God, and was forced to hold the rear guard as nothing more than an ordinary woman, alone."

—That was the truth behind House Astrea's fracture.

During the great expedition to subjugate the White Whale, in the very midst of that battle, the Divine Protection passed to the next generation. And on that battlefield, the result was that the former Sword Saint, now stripped of her protection, was left behind.
The previous Sword Saint took up the rear guard, fighting the Witchbeast to protect the many soldiers there — and vanished without a trace.

"It was I, and no one else, who took the sword from my wife. I was the one who made the woman beloved by the Sword God cast away her blade, made her nothing but an ordinary woman. That is what invited my wife's final moments."

"——"

"The Sword God did not forgive my wife for her betrayal. Stripped of her Divine Protection on the battlefield, forced to rely on nothing but the blade she should have discarded — what she must have felt, I... could not accept it. It is true that I railed against Reinhard, in whom the Divine Protection had come to dwell. My foolish self could not forgive my grandson, who had only just wept for his grandmother's death and been burdened with too heavy a fate. I regret it."

That was the regret Wilhelm had confessed to Subaru last night.
Even knowing full well that Reinhard was not at fault, Wilhelm, grieving his wife's death, had been unable to accept it. As a result, House Astrea had split apart.

"I do not wish to repeat that again. Reinhard bears no responsibility whatsoever for my wife's death. There is no reason at all for my grandson to be blamed."

So he meant to settle it himself, without telling Reinhard.
Hearing all this, Subaru understood that resolve painfully well. If it were possible, he too wished it could be granted. But Wilhelm was carrying far, far too much already.

"Crusch-san, and your wife, and... you're going to break, Wilhelm-san. And even if you keep quiet about the Corpse Soldier, there's no telling where they'll turn up."

"That is needless worry, Subaru-dono."

"Eh...?"

Subaru had been about to point out the lack of certainty, but Wilhelm shook his head.
And the sword demon, his expression twisting into something ominous, said:

"—Because there is no way my wife will fail to come to me."
