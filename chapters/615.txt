Arc 7 Chapter 110: "Words of Blessing"

Beast-claws biting hard into the earth, she launched herself forward with a force that seemed to tear the ground itself loose.
Her comrades were racing about the battlefield, marshaling their wits, swinging fists of martial and magical fury, striving to tip control of this battle toward their own side. To lend her strength to that effort, Frederica Baumann tore across the field.

The beautiful golden-furred she-leopard outran the wind, and among all those who ran this battlefield, she kept up her duties as courier at the "second" fastest speed there was.
Against the imperial regulars, who overwhelmingly outmatched them in quality, the rebels — a mere rabble by comparison — had somehow managed to force a stalemate, and much of the credit for that belonged to the intelligence Otto Suwen gathered, to Abel who put that intelligence to use, and to Frederica, who could carry it swiftly.

Whether or not she was conscious of how much she'd contributed, Frederica was once again kicking up dust with critical information in hand, and the impact that information would have was exceptional.
It was this—

"—There is a sign of some abnormality in the earth. That is the message from my lord and my brother."

Gasping for breath, pushing her exhausted body onward, Frederica arrived at the main camp, and there, having released her Beastification, she gave her report in haste, unconcerned about baring her skin.
Those were the words Garfiel — having survived a fierce battle in the midst of the fighting — had relayed to Frederica with his face drained of color, not even pausing to boast of his own feats.

Battered, bloodied, having weathered a life-and-death struggle beyond Frederica's imagining, Garfiel had come through it all. The urge to praise her brother's valiant fight, as an elder sister should, was put off in the face of his desperate plea.
Fortunately, at Garfiel's side was Roswaal, who had arrived together with the Flying Dragon Squadron reinforcements, and he would surely see to Garfiel properly. Whatever Garfiel himself might feel about being tended to by his rival in love was another matter.

"—Hm. That lacks specificity. What are the details?"

"I do not know, my lady. Only that it is my brother's intuition, granted by his Divine Protection. Only…"

"Only?"

"My brother's intuition — whether for good or ill — is always right."

Call it a wild instinct, though he'd never actually lived in the wild — Garfiel's intuition was as sharp as a beast's. It rarely served him well in ordinary life, where he often made a fool of himself in front of Ram, but when it came to matters of battle, its reliability was second to none.
That was precisely why Frederica, without a second thought, had raced here to the main camp.

"—————"

Hearing Frederica's plea, the one who fell into thought was the beautiful noblewoman Serena Dracroy — an Imperial senior margrave who had brought the Flying Dragon Squadron's reinforcements and joined their side.
From what Frederica had heard, she was the acquaintance Roswaal had gone to seek cooperation from — and being an acquaintance of Roswaal's, she was likely a formidable woman with some flaw of character. Still, that too was a common trait among Roswaal's acquaintances: whatever their personality, their competence was beyond doubt.

That much was clear from the fact that she now stood, here at the main camp governing the movements of the rebels' now-massive army, entrusted as deputy in the absence of the supreme commander.

"Frederica-sama! Something to wear, if you will be so kind!"

As Frederica studied Serena's pensive profile, a red cloak was draped over her shoulders from behind. It was a cloak that only a "general" of the Vollachia Empire was permitted to wear, but the person who had, with such awe-inspiring boldness, draped it over her had no understanding of what such regalia signified.
So, taking pure and simple goodwill for what it was, Frederica smiled, grateful.

"Thank you, Schult-sama. I apologize for the unseemly sight."

"Not at all, it isn't unseemly at all! Frederica-sama worked so hard, running back and forth between so many people! I think that's really splendid!"

Pulling the cloak snugly around herself and giving thanks, Frederica found before her, standing at attention, Schult — a small body packed full of charm.
Left behind at the main camp with nothing to do but watch the state of the battle in anxious suspense, Schult's feelings were surely harder to bear than Frederica's own, even though she at least had the courier's duty to occupy her.
And yet, he still found it in him to offer words of comfort to Frederica upon her return — that alone spoke well of him.

"Mn, Fre good too. Uu wanted to rampage with everyone too."

Beside Schult, Utakata, whose black hair was dyed pink at the tips, stood with arms crossed, nodding.
Flinching a little at her belligerent remark — "T-that's, um, debatable" — Frederica once again turned her attention to Serena.
Serena, stroking the large white scar carved into the left side of her face, said:

"A sign of some abnormality in the land of Vollachia, is it. To dismiss out of hand the counsel of one whom Roswaal holds in high regard — that would be the choice of an outright fool."

"Then…"

"It galls me to say it, but it matches what that man who vanished as a 'Stargazer' left behind as his parting words. He assigned me a great role only I could fulfill — self-serving talk, through and through. As if—"

"As if?"

"As if he himself were His Imperial Majesty. And this from a man who came here to strike the man down."

Arms crossed, Serena let out a sigh, her expression a tangle somewhere between anger and exasperation.
The man she referred to was the one with the demon mask — Abel. He, who had originally been responsible for overall command of the rebels, had left the main camp and entrusted that role to Serena.
What exactly had passed between them at that time, Frederica had not been told the details.

"Before he left this place, what did Abel-sama say to you, Serena-sama?"

"—. 'The resolution draws near.' That was it. However—"

There Serena cut herself off, and this time it was indignation that filled her narrow eyes. Still, the woman known as the "Scorching Duchess" for her fierce bearing gestured toward the battlefield with her well-formed chin, and said,

"The resolution will come without tipping toward either the imperial capital or the rebels — it will simply dissolve into ambiguity, unresolved."

"Unresolved… my lady?"

Frederica, too, felt confusion upon understanding the meaning behind Serena's expression, one that seemed to say the words were distasteful even to speak.
For a struggle on this scale to end in something so unresolved — what sort of circumstance could bring that about? To begin with, as words spoken to the person one was entrusting with command of the battlefield, such a phrasing — one that could be taken to mean their efforts would come to nothing — was surely inappropriate.
One could only conclude that Abel, in leaving those words behind, had shown a fatal lack of understanding for the human heart.
In any case—

"Resolution — does that mean the fighting will end? If so, then Priscilla-sama, and Al-sama, and Heinkel-sama would be coming back too, wouldn't they?"

"Schult-sama…"

His round eyes clouding with worry, Schult stretched his small body up on his toes to look out over the battlefield.
The boy's gaze fell upon a corner of the field that, even amid this extreme situation, was creating a particularly harsh environment — the two battlefields under the red sky and the white sky, places even Frederica had not been able to reach.
Her beast's instinct had given her certainty that merely drawing near would be more than her body could endure.

"—————"

Precisely because of that, Frederica could not casually offer words of false comfort.
Whatever was happening in that space had to be a phenomenon beyond even Frederica's imagining. Feeling the shame of that thought, Frederica's eyes drooped at the corners, when—

"Shu, don't worry. Poo and Yo strong. Good match for Mi and Ta."

"Utakata-sama…"

"Uu fine with -chan too."

"Utakata-chan-sama…"

Chest puffed out, Utakata reached out and roughly ruffled Schult's pink, unruly hair.
It was a childish claim with no basis whatsoever, but precisely because it carried no hesitation, it struck a chord in Schult's heart. Frederica's lips softened into a smile at the sight of Schult nodding his head up and down, vigorously.
Then, turning back to face Serena once more,

"What do you intend to do, Serena-sama? If we are not to set my brother's intuition aside, then we must also prepare for what Abel-sama said…"

"It is a point of hesitation. Naturally, there is no time to hesitate. — Look."

At Serena jerking her chin once more, Frederica let out an "eh?" and turned her eyes to the battlefield. In her emerald eyes, an abnormality on the battlefield visible even from this distance — the rampaging giant humanoid castle wall, one of the "Nine Divine Generals," turned its back and leapt into the imperial capital.

"Is that… heading for the Crystal Palace?"

"All the more, it seems, in line with what that man said. — Though I still cannot fathom the true meaning behind your brother's claim that Vollachia will become the enemy."

Serena frowned, her face showing she had some idea of what lay behind the outrage of the "Steelfolk" Moguro Hagane.
And then—

"Relay this to everyone. — The moment there is any abnormality at the Crystal Palace, all combat is to be suspended. Prepare for the ambiguity that would render this battle, the blood spilled, and the lives lost, all for naught."

—With a shrill ring, the war-blade and the axe, the machete, clashed in midair, scattering sparks.

Steel swung without mercy, meant to take a life.
Her senses had begun to grow numb, but with it now turned upon her, the terror and the reality of a life under threat welled up anew from beneath skin and bone.
She had never thought of herself as fragile in spirit. If anything, compared to children her own age, she had believed herself well-endowed with nerve, given the desperate straits she had already lived through.

And yet, with her skin still stinging from the heat of the earlier blast, with her lungs shrinking tight from tension until she could barely breathe, that foundation of trust in herself threatened to shake loose.
The greatest reason she was made to feel that way was—

"—!"

Even in the thick of battle, that overseeing, contemptuous gaze kept turning her way.
The man's eyes, as if to say he had not forgotten her, thrust at her like a blade — that was the single greatest cause of Petra's breathlessness.

The imperial soldier wielding the axe, a bandana wrapped around his head.
That man, whose name she did not even know, was the true form of the formidable enemy currently tormenting Petra and the others.

To live is always to stand beside death.
No matter the situation, everything could change in the space of a single blink. The man before her seemed a perfect example of that unreasoning truth, and Petra gritted her teeth hard.
She knelt on the ground, supporting Otto, whose breath came in ragged gasps. Otto, who had taken the brunt of the impact from the magic stone used for their emergency escape, bore a heavy exhaustion.
Precisely because of that, on a battlefield where strong and weak alike staked their lives without distinction, Petra could never allow herself to voice weakness.

"Uryaa! Al-chin!"
"Right you are, Miss Medium!"

That, a high voice utterly out of place on a battlefield reeking of blood, and a listless voice, worked in tandem, exchanging blows with the owner of a jet-black killing intent.
Working together with Medium, who used her small body for all it was worth, Al held his own against the imperial soldier. His combat strength wasn't enough to overwhelm the enemy, and a fairly fierce war of attrition continued.
It was unkind of her to think it, but when Al had appeared as reinforcements, Petra had thought him a disappointment.

"If it were Garf-san, or Emilie…"

Not merely because they were allies she could trust, but because their sheer strength would surely have overwhelmed the enemy.
Al's strength was not that — by Petra's estimation, it wasn't especially high. Better than non-combatants like Petra or Otto, perhaps, but she felt he was weaker than any of the Shudrak.

But then again, in the sense that his strength was nothing especially outstanding, the imperial soldier facing him was much the same.
Surely that soldier, too, was far weaker than Emilia, or Garfiel, or those people called the Empire's "Nine Divine Generals." And yet, Petra still found him terrifying.

You don't need elaborate magic to brush a bug off a flower. A flick of the fingers, a little water, and it falls away. In this imperial soldier's bearing, that sort of thinking showed through, faintly but unmistakably.
On this battlefield, Petra and the others were, to him, no more than a slightly industrious little bug—

"—You're a nuisance, you are."

The man's voice slid into Petra's ears just as she shrank back at that thought.
For a moment her shoulders jumped, but the one he'd addressed wasn't Petra and the others — it was one of the two locked in swordplay with him. Al.

"Da! Nuoh! Owaryaa!"

The man swung the axe in his hand, aiming for Al's thick neck. But it was caught on the war-blade in his one remaining arm, and in the same motion the man spun and unleashed a second, third heavy blow in quick succession.
Al somehow managed to weather that barrage of strikes, his handling of the blade unnervingly precarious.
It was so far from reassuring to watch that Petra let out small shrieks — "Kya!" "Waa!"

"Somehow, I want to support Al-san, but…"

Otto seemed to share the same anxiety; there was more than mere exhaustion in his voice, there was urgency.
Al's fight, for all the fanfare of his entrance, was fraught with an unmistakable tension, and though he was fortunately holding on for now, there had already been five exchanges that, had they gone even slightly differently, could have killed him.
Whether he could survive a sixth felt to Petra like a bet with terrible odds.

"Still a nuisance, though."

But it seemed the imperial soldier's assessment differed slightly from Petra's and the others'.
However precarious it looked, the man who had had every attack turned aside stepped back a good distance, then raised the hand not holding the axe and gave it a light wave toward Al and Medium.
An instant later, a fireball formed in midair, and it went tearing across the sky toward the two of them, burning the very air as it flew.

"Hey hey hey hey!"
"Uwaaah!"

Reason unclear, but a spirit being made to obey through some illegitimate method.
Al and Medium clutched their heads and scrambled to flee as the fireball it had loosed bore down on them. The fireball, missing the two of them, caught the surrounding trees and the grass on the ground instead, and the flames swelled up.

"This is bad…"

It was understandable that Otto muttered so.
Looking on, the flames the fireball had brought hadn't burned Al and the others, but had instead set the surroundings ablaze, encircling Petra and the others as they faced off against the imperial soldier, attempting to seal off their escape.
The blazing flames formed a ring, and they were shut inside it.
Even missing its mark, sealing off their escape route this way had likely been the imperial soldier's aim all along, but even as Petra and the others gritted their teeth at the situation, the man cocked his head.

"I don't like this."

At that quiet remark, utterly unsuited to the circumstances, Al let out an "Ahh?"
He gestured with the war-blade in his hand at the blazing surroundings, and said,

"You do all this and then say something like that? Have some respect for resources, resources! Do you have any idea how hard the plants worked to make that oxygen, so humans could just breathe easy and take deep breaths like this…"

"I should have been able to kill you about six times over by now, and I haven't managed it once. Not even when I put you somewhere you absolutely couldn't have dodged. It's unpleasant."

"What a thing to say. Today's just a bad day all around, I swear."

At Al's reply, the man's brow furrowed, his suspicion mounting.
Unable to gauge the true limits of Al's strength, the imperial soldier seemed rather baffled. Honestly, it was distasteful to share the same opinion as that man, but Petra held the very same question about Al.

Al looked like he was about to die, yet he never did.
He was responding to the soldier's attacks, but with precarious movements, somehow avoiding only the fatal blows — only the fatal blows, without fail.
And yet he didn't seem to be holding back his true strength either — that mismatch was deeply unsettling.
So the imperial soldier, too, couldn't find the decisive stroke to cut his losses.

"Compared to me, there's nothing prepared about this — it's improvised, off the cuff. In that sense, the guy behind you there is probably more my type."

"…Are you talking about me? If so, I object, on my honor."

"That's right! Your personalities aren't even alike! You're the more dangerous, sinister one!"

"I'm supposedly on your side, but somehow that still stung."

Unexpectedly dragged into the topic, Otto gave a bitter, wry smile.
At Otto's attitude, Petra felt a flicker of impatience, but it vanished quickly.

"—————"

Glancing her way, Otto gave Petra a wink. Sensing in his expression something beyond mere bitterness — some scheme afoot — Petra turned it over in her mind.
Without speaking, he wanted her to grasp his intent.

"This has taken too long."

Just as Petra sensed that, the imperial soldier, his voice gone cold, snapped his fingers.
An instant later, smaller fireballs than before — nearly ten of them at once — scattered in all directions faster than the eye could follow, igniting not Petra and the others, but the surrounding trees and ground.
The ring of flame meant to enclose them expanded, and with it came attendant blaze and black smoke—

"Achachachacha! I'm gonna burn, I'm gonna burn!"

"Calm down, missy! Get over here before your braid catches fire!"

Surrounded by flame, Medium hopped about, hot, clutching her long braid. Leaping over the ring of fire, she jumped to Al's side, spun around with tears in her eyes—

Gripping her machete tight, ready to glare at whoever had done this—

"Huh!?"
"You gotta be kidding…"

Medium's surprise and Al's sigh overlapped.
The cause of both their reactions: the imperial soldier who had conjured this blazing scene had vanished.

"—————"

Lost in the roaring flames and rising black smoke, that imperial soldier's figure had disappeared. Had he fled, using the fire as cover, or was he waiting, biding his time, for them to slowly burn to death?

"Tch…"

"He's totally gone!? We gotta get out too, before we burn!"

Turning his helmet-obscured field of view, Al searched for the man who had vanished into the flames. Meanwhile, he held his own war-blade up against his own throat in a strange stance, straining his eyes toward where the soldier had gone.
Beside him, Medium too scanned around restlessly, but frustrated at not finding the enemy, urged them so, trying to escape the strengthening blaze.
Certainly, fixating on the man's whereabouts to the point of losing their escape route would defeat the whole purpose — it seemed only natural to think that priority should now be given to getting clear of the danger zone.
And yet—

"—————"

The hand Otto had laid over the one Petra used to support him tightened, just slightly. Petra took the tension conveyed through that grip as a silent instruction.
That she must not let her guard down — Petra agreed with that wariness wholeheartedly.
And so, atop Otto's hand overlapping her own, Petra laid her other hand as well—

"—Thank you, Petra-chan."

The moment those words of gratitude left his mouth, Otto twisted his body where he sat and let his posture collapse, falling backward onto the ground. — In that instant, an axe swept past in a horizontal arc, missing him by a hair's breadth.

"—————"

From within the ring of fire, the imperial soldier who had crept close under cover of the smoke, his killing intent, swung and missed.
At that fact, the man, having had his sure moment thwarted, blinked in mild surprise. But quickly regaining his composure, he moved to draw back the axe he'd swung through—

"Jiwald!!"

"Guh"

Petra, five fingers raised, unleashed a weak-output Jiwald from each of those five fingers, striking the imperial soldier's face in a radiating spread — one of the five shots seared his right eye.
It was an attack of Petra's that sacrificed power for area coverage. It achieved its purpose splendidly; the man, his eyeball scorched, cried out and reeled backward.
Into that opening, a figure spinning through the air came leaping down.

"Go!"

It was Medium, having crouched down and used Al's back — who had lowered himself across the fire — as a springboard to leap up.
Spinning vertically, round and round, she brought the machete gripped firmly in both hands down in a single stroke, striking it mercilessly against the off-balance imperial soldier.

"Uryaaa!"

A hard sound rang out, and amid the burning flames, a red of a different hue mixed in.
The sound that rang out was not the sound of a skull splitting beneath the machete. Hand raised, Petra saw it — the man had thrown his own forehead into the path of the swinging blade.
That hard sound was, most likely, from a metal guard he'd hidden beneath the bandana. That was how he'd taken Medium's attack. Of course, he hadn't come away unscathed.
Flailing, one hand pressed to his forehead, he swung the axe about, dodging the follow-up attacks clumsily as he backed away, backed away, backed away—

"Damn it…"

From the head of the retreating man, a blood-soaked bandana fluttered down and fell. Even with the guard taking the blow, it hadn't been able to stop the bleeding from his split forehead.
Bleeding profusely, the man's eyes glared at Otto and Petra.
As if to ask why his ambush had been seen through, that gaze seemed to demand — but—

"—I won't tell you that. I'm not your parent or your sibling."

Even lying on the ground as he was, Otto declared it as though triumphant.
At that, the imperial soldier clicked his tongue. Clicking his tongue, wiping the blood from his forehead with his palm—

"Ah! Wait! Running away is cheating!"

There, he did not choose the reckless move of flying into a rage and lunging at them.
Keeping his right eye — sealed shut by heat and blood — closed, the imperial soldier vanished into the flames beyond. With the enemy gone from sight once more, Petra braced for another ambush, but—

"He's not coming… He didn't seem desperate enough to keep fighting from a disadvantageous position."

"Uu~, I'm not satisfied. It's like we were the only ones who were desperate."

"I get how you feel, missy. But he ran off without so much as a parting shot. We win, we win. — We WIN!!"

"We WIN!!"

Toward where the imperial soldier had vanished, Al and Medium raised a cheer of victory.
Whether or not it truly counted as harassment toward that soldier, in reality, their cheering lightened Petra's chest just a little.
In any case—

"How'd you read his movement at the end there? That was a gamble, wasn't it?"

"It's not my doing, Al-san. It's thanks to Petra-chan."

Borrowing Al's hand, who had sheathed his war-blade, Otto was pulled up, and he gestured toward Petra. Still, Petra couldn't quite claim it was entirely her own doing either.

"To begin with, since I was the target, I figured his final move would be aimed at me. What told me the timing and direction of his approach was footsteps."

"Footsteps?"

"You had Otto-san's 'Channel' let you hear the voices of the little ones underground… right?"

"Bingo."

Her guess having hit the mark, Petra clasped her hands before her chest at Otto's nod of confirmation.
Originally, the reason the enemy had targeted Otto in the first place was because he used his Divine Protection of the Soul of Language to grasp information across the whole battlefield. That imperial soldier, by some method unknown, had appeared having evaded Otto's Divine Protection, which let him hear the voices of animals and insects to grasp the overall picture.
To catch an opponent like that, the same method as before wouldn't have worked.

"So it was the little ones underground… the ones who don't have eyes and can't see what's happening outside, so you said Otto-san was useless with them."

"I might have chosen my words a little more carefully! …Anyway, I listened to the voices of the animals and insects underground and used their footsteps to figure out where he was. It was improvised, but Petra-chan helped me pick up the sounds with her sunlight magic."

"Otto-san signaled me with a face that looked kind of shady."

When he'd gripped her hand tightly, what Otto had been asking of Petra was support from sunlight magic.
With that assistance, Otto had used his Divine Protection to deduce the enemy's position from footsteps, evaded the final ambush, and instead landed a painful blow that drove the enemy off — that was the trick behind it all.

"—. Honestly, I would have liked to have taken him out of the fight entirely."

"Same here. Not strong, exactly, just skilled at killing, that guy was. More importantly…"

"Hot hot hot! We gotta get out of here quick or we'll be roasted alive!"

Even having driven off a dangerous enemy, that didn't resolve the fact that the surroundings were on fire.
Hearing Medium's plea, Al said "Right" and let Otto lean on his shoulder. Petra, together with Medium, searched for a spot where the fire's influence was weaker, and led the two of them onward.
And so, the group tried to make their escape before being engulfed by the flames, but—

"Let's get clear as fast as we can. We have a reason to head back to the main camp now."

"…Somehow, the way you say that doesn't sound like the lead-in to bragging about a heroic tale of escaping a dangerous foe."

"Yes, it's bad news. This is a matter of urgency. …It's information that came to me incidentally, while I was trying to listen for footsteps just now."

One trouble past, and now this unwelcome preamble.
Chased by the heat of the flames, Petra still turned her eyes toward this remark she couldn't afford to miss, and Otto, wearing a grim expression, said, looking behind them — no, across the whole battlefield—

"It seems like, from an angle entirely separate from the dragon or the burning sky or any of that, something's coming that's about to make the whole battlefield precarious. And it's, well, pretty much right in front of us."

By strange coincidence, at the very same moment his junior had sensed it through his Divine Protection of the Earth Spirits, Otto Suwen had obtained the same information through his Divine Protection of the Soul of Language, and spoke of it thus.

"—They say a 'Great Disaster' that threatens to imperil the Vollachia Empire is drawing near. I, as one general of this Empire! As a 'general' who lived on in shameful disgrace! No matter what! I cannot die and be truly dead until I have been of use to His Imperial Majesty!!"

So spoke the huge man chained to the underground wall, the one who called himself Goz Ralfon, and hearing it, Rem understood that something besides the civil war was shaking the imperial capital.
Fed only once every two days, Goz had grown quite weakened, but from Rem's makeshift healing magic, meant merely to ease his condition, he recovered a startling amount of vigor and strength.
Though she suspected most of that effect owed itself to the man's own sheer force of conviction.

"Daughter, your treatment has saved me! By rights I would invite you to my home, introduce you to my wife and children as our benefactor — but now I must go to His Majesty! This debt, I shall repay without fail!!"

"Ah, um, yes."

"If you're able, leave this place and join the regular troops! General Moguro Hagane, or Second General Kafma Irulux — either of them will treat you well! Avoid any other general! They won't listen to reason!"

Bellowing this out in a tremendously loud voice, Goz, freed of his chains, burst up from underground with terrific force, thundered a rebuke at the soldiers guarding the estate, and ordered them to accompany him to the Crystal Palace visible in the distance.
In response, the attitudes of the guards split into two.

It seemed that, among the estate's soldiers, some had known of Goz's existence while others had not. Those who'd been kept in the dark were displeased, while those who'd been informed enjoyed, by that very fact, deep trust from Berstetz.
Hence—

"I see. So you would bar my path."

Wearing no cloak, his upper body bared and covered only in muscle like armor, Goz fixed his gaze upon the guards who had drawn their weapons to block his way.
Goz was unarmed, and it had been no mere day or two that he'd been held captive. He was far from his best condition. And yet, what the guards stood against was not a matter of odds in their favor.

"Very well. Just as I devote my loyalty to His Imperial Majesty, so you swear fealty to the Chancellor. That honor, I shall bear witness to it myself!!"

The overwhelming fighting spirit he radiated held no impurity, even as he fixed his eyes upon soldiers set against him.
The same was true of what followed — Goz's one-sided battle, fought bare-handed.

Massive fists swung, and with a single blow, multiple guards were sent flying.
A slight numerical advantage barely even registered as a margin of error. It was overwhelming suppression, in an instant.

"I'd heard your name from Abel-san, as one of the 'Nine Divine Generals,' but…"

In truth, the only genuine "Nine Divine Generals" Rem had witnessed with her own eyes were Arakiya and Madelyn, back at the walled city of Guaral.
Even compared to those two, Goz's presence lost nothing.
Though, accounting for weapons, equipment, and the toll of his exhaustion, he might currently be at something of a disadvantage.

"Well fought! That is what it means to be a sword-wolf of the Empire!!"

Standing in the midst of the soldiers he'd beaten down, Goz thrust his arm skyward, praising them with insufferable fervor.
This was, if she recalled correctly, the man said to have sacrificed himself, body and all, to let the true Emperor Abel escape — and yet his vigor was such that one could scarcely believe he'd been chained and wasted away.
Why exactly he had been chained in the estate's basement remained unclear.

"Once more — Chancellor Berstetz Fondalfon! I will not let your scheme go as you intend! First General Chisha, you as well! Uoooooooh——!!"

Before there was any chance to discuss such finer details, Goz took the guards who had sided with him and burst boldly out through the front gate of Berstetz's estate.
Overwhelmed by the "Lion Knight's" heroic vigor, Rem could do nothing but watch his back recede. Still—

"I have no business at that castle."

Through this unexpected turn of events, the estate's soldiers were gone, and with them, any obstacle blocking Rem's way.
Rem confirmed the life or death of the soldiers Goz had beaten down, gave the seriously wounded minimal treatment, and, using one of the longswords as a cane, made her way through the estate to a room she had visited many times before.
And then—

"Katya-san, it's me, Rem. Please come out."

"…I absolutely do not want to come out. Wh-what was that huge voice just now?"

"I understand how you feel. But that loud-voiced man has already left. I think we ought to discuss what to do from here."

"What to do…"

Through the door, without showing her face, Rem urged Katya toward a decision.
As things stood, Goz's valor had, for better or worse, swept the estate clear of soldiers, so the urgency of needing to flee the estate in haste had lessened somewhat.
But—

"'Great Disaster'…"

That was the reason Goz had rushed off so hastily to the Emperor said to be at the Crystal Palace.
More precisely, Goz's greatest reason for heading to the Crystal Palace was likely to confront the impostor posing as the Emperor. Still, it was also true that preventing this unknowable disaster was among his aims.
Either way, Goz's advice had not been in favor of staying put here.
So then—

"—I-I have no reason to leave this place. I-I'm a hostage."

"There are no soldiers left to detain you. There's little reason left to content yourself with remaining a hostage."

"S-still…!"

"…Brother."

She hadn't clearly seen the figure of whoever was outside the door.
Still, going by what Rem and Flop had said, this third force wandering about outside — neither imperial soldiers nor rebels — had the look of the walking dead, they said.
Called "the dead," the first person Katya's mind now turned to was her own brother, Jamal Aurélie. Her brother, whom she'd thought couldn't die even if killed — and yet, unbelievably, dead.

Even if it were a Hollow, she couldn't entirely deny wanting to see him one more time.
But faced with the actual, incomprehensible situation of the dead walking about, what came to mind wasn't attachment to her brother — it was, shamefully, nothing but concern for her own precious self.

She couldn't summon the noble compassion to pray for Rem and Flop's safety, to wish that they might live on in her place.
All there was, was self-love — nothing but a helpless love for herself.
Nothing but herself — incomplete, hopeless, worthy of no one's love.

"I, somehow…"

It had been wrong of her from the start, to hope, to hold expectations, to wish for salvation. Wasn't it because of such shameless thoughts that she'd dragged Rem and the others into this as well?
Wasn't this the result of Katya's own bad luck and inadequacy, dragging Rem and the others down with her?

Such utterly self-destructive thoughts were cut short by the creaking of the door, which showed signs of finally beginning to give way, and Katya's throat froze solid.
Faced with this hopeless situation, she cursed her own body, which seemed unable even to properly scream.
But—

"Eh?"

Just as she thought the door was about to be broken through—
On the other side of that door, the movement of the "enemy" trying to force its way in stopped. — No, more precisely, it had been forced to abandon its act of destruction.
Because the "enemy's" entire body had been wrapped in red flame, and was burning.
And then—

"That was the last of the minor spirits I threatened into helping, too."

That, a different voice from the burning "enemy," reached Katya from the other side of the door.
At that voice, tinged with something like a curse, Katya's head snapped up as though struck. Then Katya turned the wheels of her chair and made her own way to the door.
The door she had been so terrified to open — with trembling hands, she undid its lock and opened it.
And there, beyond the door she'd thrown open, trampling the burned, charred corpse of the "enemy" underfoot, stood a man.

"Sorry to keep you waiting, Katya. — We're getting out of here."

Gone was his familiar bandana, his hair — usually spiked up — now hanging loose. Dried blood clung to his forehead and cheeks, and yet, he had made it here all the same, and Katya's eyes went wide at the sight of him.
And then, wheeling herself toward the man,

"You're late… late, you idiot! I-if I had died, I was going to kill you!"

She cried, and flung herself, in tears, toward the fiancé who had appeared so brutally.

—At the Crystal Palace of the imperial capital, at the star-shaped ramparts, at the rebels' main camp — the situation shifted in every direction, all at once.

Those who knew the shape of events, and those who knew nothing of it at all.
Such people, tangled together in confusion, in a battlefield turned to a chaotic cauldron — no, in Vollachia, the Empire itself.

The land that demanded bloodshed, that honored the scattering of lives, that held as righteous only those actions that made ambition bloom, pulsed with life, and those etched into the Empire's history rose up, one after another.
What else could one call that, if not a nightmare?

"—'Great Disaster.'"

Indeed, if one did not call it a nightmare, then there was nothing to call it but a "Great Disaster."
The "Stargazers" who festered throughout the Vollachian Empire declared, loudly, that this was one of the great calamities fated to drive the world to ruin — that such was the heaven-sent mandate that had come down.

That itself mattered not at all to the one at the very core of what had brought about this event called the "Great Disaster."
What mattered was this: that this calamity would lure the Empire's lands to ruin, and at the far end of that ruin, fulfill a wish.
To that end, every hand that could be played, every scheme that could be exhausted, would be spent in full.

Dying and being killed, killing and being killed — Vollachia, a land of corpses built up from the accumulation of it all.
If all the blood that Vollachia had shed formed the cornerstone of the plan, then all that remained was—

"All that remains is to carry the plan through to the very end. — Careful deliberation is required."

So spoke the bearer of the "Great Disaster," the symbol of ruin, quietly, quietly blessing itself.
