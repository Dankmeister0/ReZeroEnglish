Arc 2 Chapter 47: "Rem"

※※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※


—The emotion she felt in that moment, she still remembers deeply, even now.

A sword swung down, blood scattering.
A familiar landscape scorched by flame, familiar people turned into mute corpses.

A world coming to its end. A world that had been closed off. A world that would never be rewarded.
A world that was only ever harsh, only ever unreasonable, only ever a place to be hurt.

She reached out a hand, moved her fingers, trembled her lips, and still she pleaded.

Because even a world so without salvation as that one was all she had.
A world she had only ever peered at from behind a back that blocked the way ahead of her, always, always.

That wall was suddenly torn away, and she narrowed her eyes at the dazzling brightness of the world spreading before her — the heat and color of the flames scorching flesh, the smell and color of burning meat, the beauty and color of a "horn" dancing through the air — she carved it all into her eyes, into a field of vision not yet fully opened—

In a world that might, just then, come to an end — what was it that she had been thinking?
Because even now she remembers the emotion she came to feel in that moment.


Every one of her days from then on was made of nothing but atonement for that emotion.


※※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※


—For a girl named Rem, being compared to her sister was an exceedingly painful, everyday thing.

Among the Demi-Human races, the Oni Tribe's physical strength and Mana stood out as belonging to the highest class.
Unrivaled combat power, and the racial trait of being called "Kings of the Forest" further strengthened their advantage amid nature, displaying power enough to have them acknowledged as among the foremost strong among the Demi-Human races.

For sheer physical toughness, for the quality of Mana they could wield, no race was more reliable in battle.
If the Oni Tribe had one weakness, it was the sheer smallness of their absolute numbers as a species.

A species specialized in producing individuals of overwhelming power did not tend toward budding in great multitudes, and so, in contrast to their strength, the Oni Tribe was forced into a modest existence, huddled in small settlements deep in the mountains.
Though, unlike their toughness, their temperaments tended to be gentle, and few among them held any dissatisfaction with that way of life.

And because they were a race that lived in land far from human settlements, there existed a number of strict laws.
Breaking a law brought punishment, and in the worst case, even exile was not spared. For a people who huddled together precisely because of their small numbers, exile meant a lifetime of solitude.
And so, no matter what happened, the laws had to be upheld.

—To the Oni Tribe, twins are "cursed children."
This too was one of the strict laws set down among the already-few Oni Tribe.

By nature, members of the Oni Tribe are born bearing two horns upon their heads.
In ordinary times the horns remain hidden within the skull, but when circumstances arise that stir the instincts of the oni within them, the horns emerge from the head and devour the surrounding Mana.
They bend the Mana in the air to their will and force it into submission, greatly heightening their own combat power. The horns are the organ for this purpose, and for the Oni Tribe, they are the very pride of their species.

And twins, of all things, are born sharing those two horns between them.

Among the Oni Tribe, one who loses a horn is called "Hornless," and loses their standing as a member of the race. Even the loss of a single horn cannot escape that reproach. And yet twins are born from the very start already lacking that precious horn. What could this be, if not something to be abhorred?

Thus twins are deemed cursed children, and it is custom for them to be disposed of immediately upon birth.
By rights, the fates of these two sisters should have ended there as well.

At the moment of judgment, carried out by the hand of the chieftain who had made the agonizing decision — had it not been for the tremendous magical power one of the twins let loose in that instant, and the innate gift that power revealed.


The twins were named Ram for the elder sister and Rem for the younger, and thus their names came to be left at the lowest seat of the Oni Tribe.

Their life from there on could hardly be called smooth sailing.
Even though their lives had been spared, the fact that they were twins could not be erased.
Branded "Hornless" from the very start, they were raised with cold treatment from every member of the tribe, their own parents included.
Parents who, despite sharing blood with them, never let go of their distant, standoffish attitude. Fellow tribesmen who made no effort to hide their disgust and contempt for the two cursed children. For girls who could not yet even walk, who could not yet understand words or malice, it was, one could say, the worst possible environment to grow up in.

That said, as for how long such a hostile environment lasted — it was only until they reached an age of awareness, or more precisely, until the elder twin established a sense of self.

If one word were to describe Ram in her early childhood, "child prodigy" would be the simplest.
A talent unmatched even among generations of the Oni Tribe. Even at a young age, the sheer volume of Mana she could wield was extraordinary, and above all, the beauty of her horn captivated the entire tribe.

Never drowning in her own talent or ability, standing straight and true just like the single pure-white horn upon her brow, she carried herself in a way that made every member of her race bow their heads to her, naturally.
For a girl not yet ten years old, this was treatment on an entirely different level.

Even the standoffish parents, even the tribesmen who had never hidden their scorn, even the chieftain who had tried to kill them the moment they were born — before her radiance, none of them had words left.
Among the elite of the Demi-Human races, the Oni Tribe stood chosen, and she was a being born to become the very pinnacle of that tribe.

Because they revered the mighty individual, the Oni Tribe never failed to show due respect to one who was mighty. Being such a people, their devotion to Ram carried not a trace of calculation.

And following behind her sister's glorious path, with nothing but clumsy, faltering steps, was Rem's daily life.

No outstanding talent of any kind. The amount of Mana she could wield, average. Her strength as an oni, average for one with a single horn. Unlike her sister, not a shred of confidence, she hid herself small behind her sister's back, behaving as a shadow, never to be noticed, never to be spoken of.
That was the survival technique of her childhood, the defense mechanism protecting her undeveloped heart.

It was not that she envied her sister.
It was not that she hated her parents.
It was not that she resented her fellow tribesmen.

Her sister, always walking ahead, was ever kind, turning back again and again for her slow-footed younger sister, offering her a hand.
Their once-distant parents, as they came to know their elder daughter's gift, came to pour the same affection upon Rem as well.
Not a few of the villagers, too, turned eyes of expectation and admiration toward the girl desperately following behind her outstandingly gifted sister.

A sister kinder than anyone. Parents who placed their hopes in her. Fellow tribesmen cheering her on to become splendid like her sister — all of it was, for Rem, an agony that cut her to pieces.

Perhaps it was made worse by the fact that, of all things, she looked so much like her sister — near identical.
Height, facial features, every aspect of her appearance resembled her sister's so closely, and yet the qualities she carried within as an "oni" diverged so greatly.

Of course, Rem too made efforts to change that situation.
They were nothing more than the shallow, clumsy trial and error of a small child, but Rem tried every means she could think of, striving to close even one step of the distance to her sister, to surpass her in even one single thing.

But in everything, her sister was above her.
That there existed a realm nothing she did could ever reach, and that this realm belonged to the person closest to her, the person she loved more than anyone — Rem was made to realize this already in early childhood.

She could not stand beside her sister.
Her sister, who always stood ahead, always the first to bathe in the light that illuminated the world. Peeking out timidly from behind that sister's back, shrinking her body from the dazzling brightness — that was her place.
If she resigned herself to that, then, like grasses and trees letting the wind pass over them, she could accept all of her daily hardships.

—How long did those days of accepted resignation go on?

One night, Rem woke, troubled by the heat and unable to sleep.

Lying on the wooden bed, she peeled the covers off her sweat-soaked body. Looking around, she noticed with a start that her sister, who should have been asleep in the next bed, was not there.

At once she thought she had to go looking for her sister.
If her sister was awake, she had to follow behind her, however imposing her stride. Even if it were merely a momentary waking to relieve herself, the obsessive notion that she must never fail to follow had come to dominate Rem in those days.

She stepped out of the room — and it was only then, belatedly, that she finally realized.

The cause of the heat: their long-familiar home was wrapped in flame.
She let go of the doorknob at the heat of it, and the truth struck her. Her sleeping sense of smell awoke to the reek of burning; an itch ran across her brow, and her horn showed its face.

At once she swung her strengthened body and broke through the door, dashing through a house engulfed in raging fire. She did not know why. But instinct commanded her — outside, outside.

Kicking through a weakened wall with a single blow, Rem burst out of the house.
Even in that moment, what dominated her mind was a kind of fanatical thought: *I must get outside and receive my sister's instructions.*
That thought was rewritten in an instant by the sight that awaited her outside.

At the center of the settlement, a mountain of charred corpses piled high.
Houses ablaze, trees burned down, a once-familiar world turned overnight into a crimson hell.

Among the twisted, fire-seared corpses she saw familiar faces lined up, and Rem instantly abandoned all thought, collapsing on the spot.
Slowly, figures in black robes closed in around her. The faces beneath their deep hoods could not be seen until they were close, and even once seen, she did not recognize them. But she felt not a trace of friendliness from them, and on Rem's cheeks bloomed a smile utterly unsuited to the moment.

It was an expression too resigned for a young girl to make, a face that had swallowed down resignation again and again.

The shadow paid no heed at all to that pitiful expression.
It raised its hand, a silver blade gleaming in its palm, and swung it down at the girl — the very next instant, the shadow's head was blown clean off, along with three others.

Blood scattered, four lives taken at once, the severed heads sent flying with a hand so swift the victims never even realized they had died — not even a scream rose.
A wave of Mana she knew all too well, felt directly on her skin — Rem was certain: this was her sister's doing.

The moment she perceived it, Rem rose to her feet on the spot.
If her sister was somewhere near, she had to follow behind her.

She did not even need to search; her sister's figure was found at once.
A face identical to her own, now twisted in grief, rushed to her and embraced her. She checked Rem's body in her arms for wounds, and her body relaxed as if in relief.
Hugging that body back, Rem savored a happiness and a sorrow beyond anything she had ever known.

—What happened after that, she does not remember well.

She believes she left everything to her sister.
That was best, and above all, correct. Whatever her sister did was always, among every possibility, the one most worthy of reverence.

And yet, before she knew it, they were surrounded on all sides.
The number of shadowy figures was enough to fill her entire field of vision, and even as she gazed at them vacantly, Rem remained utterly certain that her sister would somehow manage things.

The back before her was crying out something desperately.
Shedding tears, curling her body small, pleading with everything she had.

If she were laid low upon the ground, it would trouble Rem. To look down upon her sister was something that must never happen, in the way she lived her life.
Behind her sister, smaller than her sister — that was her reason for existing.

Her sister cried out. Rising to her feet, spreading both arms before her.
Mana surged forth. Her sister's power, beyond all normal bounds, unfurled — invisible blades that cut everything around them, trampling the world.

But before it could run its course, her sister turned and embraced Rem — and then, an impact.

And Rem saw it.
The white brilliance dancing across the red sky, struck loose by steel that slammed sideways into her sister's head.

Round and round and round it spun, the broken horn turning through the air.
A horn snapped off at its base, blood gushing forth, and someone's shrill scream.

What she thought upon seeing that, she still remembers vividly, even now.

While hearing the scream of her beloved sister — who had shielded her, taken the blow, had her horn broken — while watching the beautiful white horn she had always envied dance through the air,

—Ah, it finally broke.

That was what she thought.

—When Rem regained consciousness, her feet were not touching the ground.

A firm arm was wrapped around her stomach; someone was carrying her. The rough, careless handling hardly felt like someone conscious of the fact that he was touching a woman.
And that was only natural, since the owner of that arm was, right now, desperately focused on running for his life, with not a shred of composure left to spare for anything more.

"—Barusu, the broken tree ahead, go right! You're slow!"

"Don't ask... the impossible. Hah — I'm running... at full speed already!"

A voice she knew well, and a voice she had come to know well, were shouting at each other close by.
Shaken violently up and down, Rem shook her hazy consciousness back into focus. And then, trembling her lips,

"...Subaru-kun. What—"

"—! You're awake, Rem!"

Shouting with joy even as he ran, Subaru's face looked down at her.
Looking up at him with hollow eyes, Rem's throat caught at the sheer awfulness of his state.

His forehead must have split open; dripping blood cut his face vertically red. Old wounds from the previous night remained white all over his body, and on top of them, new wounds piled up, oozing fresh blood. That was the result of pushing himself even further, already in a state of utter ruin.

"...Thank goodness, Rem. You really are such a troublesome child."

Swaying her pink hair, running alongside them with a faint smile, was Ram.
She broke her lips into the barest hint of a smile, one only someone who knew her well would recognize, and reached out to gently stroke Rem's blue hair.
Right after that,

"Fura!"

A chant for a wind blade was cast, and the resulting blade of wind cut through the forest — slicing clean through the body of a Witchbeast in its path, turning the creature poised to pounce into fertilizer.
At that instant, her footing faltered as if dizzy, and Ram bumped lightly into Subaru's body.

"—Owwwwww!! Ram, you— hey... you know my right shoulder's dislocated, don't you...!"

"...Quiet. If Ram hadn't pushed herself, it would've just been another layer on top of the curse. Accept being leaned on as a wall, a little."

"At least the other shoulder— Owwwwwaaaaaah!!"

Subaru screamed, half in tears from the searing pain.
Leaning her weight against his body, Ram bled from the trace of her lost horn.

Watching the two of them, Rem suddenly came to grasp the current situation.
Why she was in a place like this, why the two of them were carrying her. That was—

"Why..."

"Huh?"

"Why didn't you just leave me?"

Shaken as she was, the question slipped from her mouth.
Subaru looked down at her with a suspicious expression, and Rem, lips trembling in a rush, continued,

"If Onee-sama and Subaru-kun come, it's meaningless. Rem... Rem had to do it alone... it should have been only Rem who got hurt, that would've been enough—"

"It's already too late for that, both me and Ram are torn to shreds! If anything we might be worse off than you!"

Subaru bellowed this, and it was likely true without any exaggeration. Ram, perhaps with something on her mind, did not join the conversation.
Feeling somehow cast aside by her sister's attitude, Rem clung desperately to what remained, forming her broken, halting words.

"It's Rem's, it's Rem's fault. Because Rem hesitated last night... so the responsibility has to be Rem's to take... otherwise, Rem, to Onee-sama, to Subaru-kun..."

"I'm in quite a lot of pain right now, so could you wrap this up nicely!? If I get distracted I'm gonna get a full-body replay of last night's chomp-chomp—"

"Subaru-kun really shouldn't have been bitten at all—"

Even to Subaru, not in any position to properly listen, those words apparently reached him clearly.
His face stiffened as he looked at her, and Rem confessed her sin to him.

During last night's struggle in the forest, Rem had released the "oni" within herself and faced down the pack of Witchbeasts. In that moment, her consciousness had existed as a coexistence — half given over to instinct as an "oni," half retaining herself.
The wounds she took were severe, but if she gave herself over entirely to instinct, pulling back from it would become difficult.
It was a limited invocation of "oni transformation," undertaken with that judgment in mind. But it exposed her immature combat skill, and worse, it forced Subaru — who was supposed to be the one protected — to throw himself into harm's way.

But what Rem truly needed to apologize for was not creating that situation.

In the instant Subaru shielded Rem and had his whole body devoured by the Witchbeast's fangs — by rights, Rem should have been able to protect Subaru.

Even having been shoved aside, there was no comparison between the strength of a "transformed" Rem and Subaru's own strength. The impact of being merely stumbled off-balance hadn't even put him out of reach of a turned head and an outstretched arm.
And yet Rem hesitated to reach out her hand, and let Subaru suffer near-fatal wounds.
All because of the scent that wafted thick from his body — the smell of a day when everything had been burned to nothing.

"Because Rem hesitated to reach out her hand to Subaru-kun, Subaru-kun nearly died. And he ended up taking on far too much of the curse, all at once. That's why—"

"And so as atonement for that, you figured you'd settle it all by yourself?"

At Rem's confession, Subaru, breathing hard, nodded as if he understood.
As if affirming his understanding, Rem too lowered her chin, staring at her own sin.

She had been prepared to be cursed at, to be scorned. Words that, by rights, should have been hurled at her from his mouth before they had even entered the forest.
Whether she had put off hearing them because she thought she had to save him as quickly as possible, or because she lacked the resolve to face her own weakness — surely it was the latter, Rem thought, mocking her own weak heart.

"Rem."

"Yes."

Called by name, she braced herself and looked up.
No matter how harsh the words hurled at her, she had to accept them meekly.
Because that was the sin she had committed, and the punishment that should be given to her.


And to her, he said—

"Chop!"

"—!?"

There was a hard, dull sound of bone striking bone, and sparks scattered across Rem's vision.
For a moment her sight flickered with sharp pain, and, uncomprehending, Rem pressed a hand to her forehead. Her body, not "transformed" now, had a strength not so different from a human's.
Her struck forehead swelled faintly; from the outside it was likely turning red.
Looking down at Rem, blinking in bewilderment at this incomprehensible turn of events,

"Anyway. Are you an idiot? No, wait — you ARE an idiot."

"Barusu. Your split forehead's split open again, it's bleeding."

"I'm an idiot too, I know that! But your little sister's an even bigger idiot!"

Having said that in response to Ram's interjection, Subaru shook his bleeding head. Watching that, Rem realized she had just been headbutted by Subaru.
It made no sense.

"You're an idiot about a lot of things, but there are three especially idiotic things. You get me?"

"What are you... talking about..."

"First idiotic thing! Saying you couldn't save me!"

Spraying spit as he cut Rem off, Subaru lifted her up and brought his face right up close, close enough that his breath touched her, his black eyes wide open,

"Pry your eyes open and take a super good look. Do I look like a fine, dashing man in good health to you? Sure, I've got some white scar tissue scattered here and there on my body, but scars are a man's medals, so no problem. Which means your guilt trip was wrong from the start, you idiot!"

"But, still, if Rem hadn't hesitated, if she'd reached out her hand right away, you wouldn't have taken wounds like that at all—"

"Yes, don't go talking about things in 'ifs' and 'coulda-beens'! And second idiotic thing! Bottling it all up and trying to handle it all alone by yourself!"

Forcibly silenced, she had no choice but to meekly take Subaru's tongue-lashing.
She looked to her sister for help, but Ram made no move, apparently intent on watching the situation unfold in silence.
Subaru turned his face toward Rem's, sticking out his tongue,

"Listen up, back where I'm from, there's a saying — 'three women together make a racket.' That's got nothing to do with this, but there's also a saying, 'three heads together are as wise as Monju.'"

As he spoke, Subaru muttered to himself, "wait, what's Monju..." then, tilting his head, said "ah, whatever!" and tossed the thought aside,

"Anyway, the point is, three heads together break fewer arrows than one head alone, something like that."

"That's just a guess, but I feel like that's not the original meaning of the saying at all..."

"A·NY·WAY! Don't think it through alone — lean on the people around you more! It's not like you're incapable of speech. Like I did, gripping my hea—"

He got that far before his expression abruptly twisted with pain.
Naturally hunching forward, struggling to steady his breath, Subaru said,

"Getting docked points just for that... isn't the judging kinda harsh?"

"What are you talking ab— no. Subaru-kun. Suddenly, the Witch's scent, it's gotten strong—"

Pinching her nose, Rem writhed at the horrid sensation her sense of smell picked up.
Right there, right beside her, a detestable stench, the lingering scent that had made her hesitate to reach out her hand to him, hung thick in the air.
Why, all of a sudden, had it—

"Well, about that — just switch your feelings on it. I'm switching mine too."

But Rem's question was brushed aside by Subaru's breezy way of putting it.
Stunned, Rem stared at Subaru with her mouth hanging open. There was nothing more in his profile than what his words carried, and it came through clearly that he was, quite seriously, putting the problem off for later.

"So, the third idiotic thing... damn, we don't have time."

Subaru looked ahead as he ran, his gaze narrowing with sharp tension.
At the same moment, running alongside him, Ram, hand pressed to her aching head, began to unfurl her Mana,

"Ram, toward the village... no, at this point, the barrier's fine. Which direction do we run to get through?"

"Once we're past the pack ahead of us, it's just a straight sprint to the left after that — but what are you planning?"

At Ram's question, Subaru let out a long "hmm,"

"Shove Rem at Ram, and I heartlessly run off alone to the far side of the barrier — how's that scenario sound?"

"You'll be the decoy that draws the Ulgarm with the Witch's scent, and in the meantime I take Rem and run with the others. Understood."

"Don't just casually expose my tsundere act like that!?"

Exchanging words like that without slowing their running pace, Subaru and Ram.
Listening to their exchange, Rem gave voice to her despair with "That can't..."

"There's no way that would save you. ...Stop, please stop. If that happens, what was the point of Rem at all..."

"That's one more idiotic thing to add to the tally, Rem-rin. This is the best plan for everyone to make it out alive, you know? If we get through the barrier and somehow regroup, after that I've got a brilliant plan to hunt down every last Ulgarm, using a method Rem-rin doesn't know about. With that approach, happy ending, easy win, right?"

What this brilliant plan Subaru had prepared actually was, she had no idea. Honestly, she even suspected it might not even exist — just something made up on the spot.
Because before any of that, the very idea of Subaru breaking through the encirclement alone seemed, to Rem, an utterly impossible feat.

"You don't have to do something like that... Rem could just, scatter the Witchbeasts..."

Determined not to let such recklessness happen, Rem willed strength into her entire body.
But her limbs hung limp, obeying not a single command of her will. Her fighting spirit did not carry through; not one part of her body seemed willing to do as she asked.

Her fingers trembled. Only the muscles of her face could barely move. Even her well-worn weapon was nowhere to be found in her hands,

"Rem's, weapon..."

"I dropped that heavy thing back there! You try telling a guy with one dislocated shoulder to carry both you and that thing, it's not happening, for real!"

And with no weapon either, spelled out plainly, this time Rem truly came to know despair.
And so, utterly without recourse, Rem's body was slowly passed over to her sister,

"Don't drop her."

"I'd like to think I still have more strength than a one-armed Barusu."

"Then why was I the one carrying Rem!?"

"Because Barusu insisted he'd carry her and wouldn't listen to reason."

"A brutally honest comeback, seriously!?"

Covering his face with his hand, Subaru tried to pretend he hadn't said that.
Looking up at him, Rem gazed at Subaru as if seeing something unbelievable. Even though she had just said such awful things about herself, why would he go this far for her—

"Subaru-kun, why would you go this far..."

"—Well, let's see."

At the question, Subaru closed his eyes for just a moment, then raised a single finger and smiled,

"You're the partner of my very first date in this life. Can't be so heartless as to abandon you."

Saying that, with the hand that had raised the finger, he gently stroked Rem's hair,

"Well then, I'm off to go do my thing real quick. —Take care of Rem for me, Onee-sama."

"I'll pray Barusu makes it back to regroup with us safely too."

Leaving that brief exchange behind, Subaru and Ram's running paths split rapidly apart.
Ram to the right, Subaru to the left. The pack that had been ahead of them hesitated for a split second, torn between which scattering prey to chase — and immediately gave chase to Subaru on the left.

"—Onee-sama!"

"This is time Barusu bought with his life. Let's use it well."

For a sister who had lost her oni's power, the strength to support Rem's body was nothing more than what her body naturally possessed. And so her running speed was, without question, not fast.

Feeling that, Rem bit her lip at her own uselessness.

If only she could "transform," protecting Subaru, carrying her sister in her place — all of it would be simple. She could do all of it.
And yet, at the crucial moment, the "oni" within her would not so much as show its face.

Ram's order of priorities here was fixed as "Rem > Subaru." So when Subaru proposed the plan to make himself the decoy, if she judged it would raise their chances of survival even slightly, she could make that decision without a moment's hesitation.

While respecting her sister's decisiveness, Rem could not help but plead through her tears.
Why wouldn't she fight more boldly?
Why wouldn't she show her something more incredible?

Pouring out that unreasonable plea, entrusting it the way she once entrusted her whole, unreserved trust as a child, Rem sobbed and cried,

"Subaru-kun... Subaru-kun...!"

"Don't look back, Rem. It'll only hurt you."

The words of her respected sister. Words that had always, always been right.
If she followed them, surely her heart would be protected. Because her sister was always right.

—Then being right meant nothing at all.

"—Onee-chan!!"

"—!!"

At the cry that burst forth exactly as her heart demanded, a violent tremor ran across Ram's expression.
Ram's lips pressed together, her eyes flew open, and her feet stopped. In that instant Rem twisted her body free of her sister's arms, and as she fell to the ground, rolled and looked back.

Far off, a sprint far too slow to be called running.
Black hair, a body covered in wounds, his slack, powerless right arm swaying — Subaru, his face twisted into an expression that swallowed down every emotion at once.

And blocking his path, an Ulgarm, its massive body forming a wall.
Its body, notably larger than the others of its kind, was perhaps the head of the pack.

Facing that being head-on, surrounded on all sides and from behind by countless Witchbeasts, Subaru kept running, fiercely.
Her hand could not reach his back. However far she stretched her fingers, however much her heart trembled, it could not reach.

And still, Rem cried out, as if to make it reach anyway.
The fingers that had not reached that night, the feelings that had not reached that night — praying, praying, that this time, at last, they would reach him.

"—Subaru-kun!!"

Whether that voice reached him, she did not know.

Only, as if in answer to that voice, she just barely made out — Subaru, still running, drawing a dully gleaming sword with his left hand.

The roar of the Witchbeast shook the forest.
And, as if to cover it over, Subaru's throat let loose a roar of its own.
