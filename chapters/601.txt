Arc 7 Chapter 96: "Painted in Deep Love"

——One of the Nine Divine Generals, the "Steelfolk" Moguro Hagane.

That he was not, in truth, one of the Steelfolk is known to only a handful even within the Vollachia Empire.

The Steelfolk proper are born as beings straddling the line between person and object, their bodies composed of inorganic matter—minerals, metals. The Weaponkin, who bear metallic parts in a portion of their bodies, are thought to be a race derived from the Steelfolk, and much about their alien nature remains unknown.

Though their rarity as a species falls somewhat short of the Dragonkin's, the Steelfolk—who differ from the human races not merely in appearance but in the very nature of their minds—overwhelm even the Dragonkin in terms of sheer difficulty of communication.

In a sense, they are harder to reach than Spirits, who can at least be conversed with given the right aptitude. That is what the Steelfolk are.

Which is precisely why Moguro, who had risen to the seat of one of the Empire's Generals and was even said to be easier to deal with than the rest of the often-troublesome Nine Divine Generals, was recognized as a heretic among his own kind.

But the truth of the matter was otherwise. Moguro merely rode on his own appearance and on the racial trait of the Steelfolk—their unwillingness to engage with others—self-proclaiming himself one of them.

And it was precisely because of the truth of Moguro Hagane's identity that Abel had treated it as all but certain:

——That Moguro was the single greatest obstacle in this decisive battle whose aim was the conquest of the imperial capital.

"——"

Flung upward by an impact that seized his entire body, Heinkel's eyes met those of a presence too overwhelmingly vast.

He was certain, then, that a continuous barrage of blows and the scattering of his own life to pieces was an unavoidable future—certain, at last, that his fate had reached its terminus. ——Or so it should have been.

"Kah—hk—"

His lungs, collapsed to their absolute limit, swelled again, and the pain of it dragged Heinkel's consciousness back to wakefulness.

His severed awareness returned, and the first thing he felt was something hard and broad supporting his back—the ground. The body that should have been flung into the sky and scattered across it lay instead upon the earth.

Recognizing that fact, confusion filled his head. Why, how, what had happened. But the instant his brain understood that he was alive——

"My sword...."

His body, aching all over, found no hilt in its empty hand. He reached his other hand reflexively toward his hip, but the sheath hanging there carried no weight either.

Turning his head, his vision rattled by an unbearably loud ringing in his ears that shook his brain, Heinkel spotted his sword some distance away from himself, driven at a slant into the ground, and let out a breath.

Had it vanished, had he lost it—he felt an outrageous surge of anxiety that made his whole body pound as though it had become one giant heart——

"——Damn it."

Only after riding all of that out did he grow disgusted with himself for worrying about a lost sword.

That single flicker of feeling seemed, to him, a perfect symbol of his own half-formed self.

"What the..."

Adding yet another reason to hate himself, Heinkel shook off the bitter sentimentality. He was not dead. He was alive. But that in itself was strange.

In that instant, Heinkel's eyes had met those of a being who called itself one of the Nine Divine Generals, and he had been marked with unmistakable killing intent. ——*I will kill you*, it had declared, plainly.

In fact, Heinkel, flung into the air, should have been struck once more and scattered to nothing, powerless to do anything about it, his life ended there.

Why had that not happened? Seeking the answer, he swept his gaze around——

"——Ah?"

A dumbstruck sound slipped from him unbidden. But that was only natural. What Heinkel saw was that absurd. Too much so, too much, too much, too much, too much—divorced from reality.

——The world had split in two, centered on Heinkel, painting out two different hells.

To the right, the sky: a thick blanket of clouds wrought from raging red flame wrapped a world of searing heat.

To the left, the sky: a colossal, awe-inspiring form draped in white cloud spread its wings, glaring down upon a freezing earth.

"A burning sky, and a white dragon..."

Heinkel muttered that in a daze, eyes wide at a sight that should not exist.

Perhaps his sense that he was not dead had been mistaken after all. Perhaps he had already died, and that was why he was seeing something so utterly unlike anything of this world.

"——!"

His thoughts, straining toward escape from a reality beyond what he could accept, were thrown into further disarray. What caused it was that his whole body, as he sat up from where he lay sprawled on the ground, was suddenly swallowed by a black shadow.

He did not even want to consider the possibility that a second dragon had just passed directly overhead——

"Whoaaaaa—?!"

Looking up at the sky and seeing the shadow's true identity, a scream tore from Heinkel's throat.

Passing overhead with tremendous force, spinning as it flew across the sky, was a colossal boulder easily mistaken for a house—and not just one or two of them.

One after another, projectiles of an utterly wrong scale hurtled past overhead, flying from the direction of Heinkel's head toward his toes, trailing a terrifying gale in their wake. And what lay at their destination was——

"——"

A crash resounded, the impact's shock making the world itself roar, and Heinkel was jolted half off the ground by a tremor that felt like the earth itself had coughed.

It repeated in rapid succession, echoing again and again, and he scrambled hastily to his feet.

The huge boulders being hurled one after another struck, again and again, something else of a wildly wrong scale—a massive humanoid shape that had risen up: the city wall itself, standing.

"...Moguro Hagane."

That this was one of the nine most feared beings in this Empire, Heinkel came to understand through his own eyes, tasting it with his entire body and soul.

What he had glimpsed just before his consciousness had faded—he wanted to believe it had been an optical illusion, a hallucination conjured by a heart shrunken with fear. It was neither.

——Grown to a colossal body some dozens of meters tall, or more, Moguro literally stood blocking the path as the Third Vertex that Heinkel and the others should have been the ones to break through.

Literally. Yes, literally.

Was there any situation in this world for which the word "literally" was more fitting than this?

The humanoid wall, risen as if kneeling on the spot, used torn-up farmland and paved city streets as its own arms and torso, carrying out defensive maneuvers beyond belief. Beneath its colossal feet swarmed the stone dolls that Heinkel had frantically been cutting down, and the giant fist that swung down over their heads looked for all the world like a falling town.

"——"

Toward that colossal body, boulders had already crashed again and again.

They were quarry stone gathered along the road to the imperial capital, the site of this decisive battle, and rock broken from steep mountain cliffs along the way—siege stones prepared for the assault.

And the ones continuing to hurl them at the giant Moguro were, of all people, those who should have been waiting for their chance to sortie from headquarters——

"——Yorna Mishigure's followers."

More precisely, the residents of Chaosflame, the demon city Yorna had once ruled.

A mixed force with no unifying look to it—Horned Folk, Lizard Humans, Beastfolk, Multi-Leg Tribe—forming ranks together, bound by exactly one unmistakable thing in common.

——That every last one of them had a red flame lit in one eye, and kept their morale burning high.

Here too, quite literally, a mass with fighting spirit blazing in their eyes hefted boulders of unbelievable size between several of them and hurled them across an outrageous distance.

But whether Moguro, taking direct hits from those boulders, was actually suffering harm from them—that, too, struck Heinkel as doubtful.

Of course, direct hits from the boulders did crumble and break the wall segments and farmland composing Moguro. But as if to repair the broken portions, the very boulders that struck home were being repurposed as material to form Moguro's next colossal form, and the attacks were, in effect, being perfectly cancelled out.

Even from a distance, the futility of the attack came through with visceral clarity.

And yet the simple, obvious reason they didn't stop hurling their stones was this: while their attacks held Moguro's colossal body in check, the rest of the rebel force pressed the assault with fury.

"...This is insane."

Heinkel had heard it too—that this Third Vertex was the most weakly defended of all the vertices, compared to the others. But that must have been true only before Moguro Hagane rose up like this and began blocking the way with the entirety of his colossal form. And yet, whether because the plan hadn't been updated or for some other reason, rebels who had chosen to retreat from other battlefields kept joining in, and the ranks facing Moguro kept swelling further still.

Among them, it seemed, were some of the People of Shudrak who had been fighting alongside Heinkel.

"Insane."

His murmured sentiment leaked out again, more clearly than before.

Insane. There was no other word for it. What else could be said. In this place where hell lay whichever way he looked—right, left, up, down, forward, back.

What else, in the end, was there for Heinkel to say?

"You're out of your minds, all of you—every single one!! You're all out of your damn minds!!"

Before he knew it, Heinkel had thrown himself, clinging, at his own sword still driven into the ground.

Leaning his weight against the hilt of the sword planted in the earth, he whipped his trembling knees into obedience and clenched his teeth. Every one of them, out of their minds. He couldn't understand that mindset of theirs.

He really couldn't do it after all. Couldn't, couldn't, couldn't—there was nothing but couldn't.

"You're all out of your damn minds..."

Weakly, leaning against his sword, Heinkel's throat shook feebly.

Around Heinkel, his head hanging limp, flame, dragon, and colossal soldier alike shook the very world. Was failing to stand against all this truly such a sin?

If so, then——

"I'm..."

Just as he let that slip out——

The light clatter of hooves rang out, and a chestnut Galewind Horse, kicking off the earth, shot past Heinkel's side. Red hair whipped by the wind the steed raised, Heinkel lifted his head.

And then——

"——"

For an instant, his gaze crossed with that of a man with a round hairstyle astride the Galewind Horse.

Zikr Osman. At headquarters, he had served as Abel's right hand in commanding the rebels, and though he himself was a "General" of the Vollachia Empire, he was a rebel who had raised his flag against the nation. A man who had openly declared himself lacking in the talent to fight sword in hand—and indeed, even by Heinkel's own judgment, a man whose individual combat strength scarcely bore counting—overtook Heinkel as he passed.

——And in the instant he passed by, he left behind a gaze that held nothing but contempt for Heinkel, who knelt here in defeat.

"I can't..."

Passing Heinkel where he knelt, the Galewind Horse tore on across the battlefield.

As if chasing after Zikr's valiant figure, rebels gathering from every battlefront pressed forward. They charged straight at Moguro Hagane's wall.

Watching them, Heinkel still could not move, still leaning against his sword.

Unable to move, still——

"...I really can't do it, Louanna. I just can't."

When he passed the red-haired swordsman who knelt on the battlefield, head bowed as if his fighting spirit had been broken, what crossed Zikr Osman's mind was sympathy—a recognition that the man was simply obeying life's natural law.

A crimson heroine who called herself Priscilla Barielle openly threw her lot in with this rebellion. The swordsman fighting as one of her retainers was, in all likelihood, not even a citizen of Vollachia.

To demand of such a man the Imperial way—that the people of the Empire be the strong—was simply too cruel.

"I myself cannot claim to fully live up to that way, either."

Sword in hand, swaying with the gait of his beloved mare Lady, Zikr laughed at himself.

He played the part of valor now, but the reason Zikr sat in the seat of a "General" owed a great deal to riding on the prestige the Osman family's forebears had built up. Had Zikr not been born into a family of soldiers, had he instead, like the other troops, clawed his way up from the ranks, a man with no talent for the sword like himself could never have made a career of it.

He did not lament that fact. Of course, Zikr too was a man of the Empire. There was, in him, some part that yearned for the kind of existence embodied by the Nine Divine Generals—one who might, depending on circumstance, wield a blade at the very front lines and turn the tide of battle with a single stroke.

But just as no one could take Zikr's place, so too was Zikr unable to make that yearning a reality he could wear as his own.

And so——

"——I am the Empire's Second General, Zikr Osman, the 'Coward'!!"

So he cried, voice raised high, as he galloped his beloved mare across a battlefield undergoing a terrifying transformation.

Lady, who was mostly kept to gentle canters for expeditions and outings, this time seemed to sense Zikr's will, and gave the finest run of her life. Zikr fell in love with her all over again, watching his brave mare run with not a trace of fear or hesitation in her.

Lady's magnificent stride, ignoring Zikr's own clumsy battle-cries, lit a fire in the hearts of many of the rebels, drawing them along into this charge.

"Right flank, Glow People! Left flank, Weaponkin! Everyone, move as instructed! The rest, follow me! We will bring down the 'Steelfolk,' Moguro Hagane!"

"Ohhhh——!!"

At Zikr's command, the gathered forces roared and charged toward Moguro. The colossal boulders sailing overhead were covering fire hurled by the volunteer soldiers loyal to Yorna.

If that drew Moguro's attention and even slightly disrupted the coordination of his stone-doll puppets, and above all if Zikr's own charge could reach the giant's feet, then the aim would be fulfilled.

"Fire——!!"

A valiant war cry rang out from a distance, and a rain of loosed arrows pierced the stone dolls blocking the way ahead, pinning them to the earth.

Those who had set that in motion were positioned far off, kept at range by Zikr's own orders—the People of Shudrak, whose archery unleashed overwhelming firepower, and the rebels skilled with the bow alongside them.

Advancing under the cover of these beautiful, high-spirited women, their effortlessly withdrawn formation included, it all seemed almost too well executed, and Zikr ground his back teeth at the thought.

"Well, if we're talking about things too well executed, that blessing takes the prize."

Glancing at the white blade of the sword he gripped, Zikr's mouth eased faintly at one corner.

Just before setting out, at the command tent he had visited, the one who had blessed Zikr was a girl—lovely, yet with something in her eyes that inspired an odd sense of awe, a gaze as if she had lived through years Zikr could not begin to imagine. Before that gaze, he had, in a moment of unbecoming boldness, gone so far as to beg.

He would not call it having been struck by a fit of cowardice. It was only that, mindful of the role he had to fulfill, Zikr had wanted a blessing. If it might raise his odds by even the smallest fraction, he had found himself willing to lean on superstition itself.

"Second General Zikr! Please fall back! We have this well in hand!!"

"Don't talk nonsense! There's no line of retreat! No room to fall back! I'm going too!!"

"——!"

Astride his Galewind Horse, Zikr shook his head at the plea from the soldier riding alongside him and refused to relinquish his role. For a moment, his subordinate seemed about to say something more, but did not press further against Zikr's resolve. Grateful for that consideration, Zikr gripped the reins tighter, and tighter still.

"Ha! The 'Womanizer,' Second General Zikr Osman, coming out to the front lines himself!"

A figure running at a pace matching the galloping horse caught up on the opposite side from the soldier riding alongside him. Looking over, Zikr saw it was a twin-blade-wielding soldier with an eyepatch over one eye—Jamal Aurélie. Taken prisoner when the walled city fell, he had since, out of fierce loyalty to His Majesty Emperor Vincent Vollachia, been brought under Zikr's banner—a self-made swordsman risen from the ranks.

"Private First Class Jamal, how's it feel?"

"Couldn't be better! Enemies wherever you look, right, left, straight ahead! I'm starting to have fun!"

"Ah, splendid, truly. A credit to the Imperial soldiery. I intend to make General Moguro vacate his seat as the 'Eighth.' Care to come along!"

"If that's an order! Hell, why don't I go ahead and clear the way for you!"

Grinning a wild, feral grin, Jamal accelerated, easily overtaking Lady's speed, and charged straight into the mass of stone dolls ahead.

Chasing after his back, Zikr found his bravado reassuring even as he felt a pang of guilt for dragging the man along with him. ——But that hesitation was cut away at once.

To fulfill the necessary role, in the necessary moment. That was what was required, and answering that call was what Zikr himself had wished for, none other. The rest was——

"——Your Majesty, as you will."

That was nearly the same instant Zikr offered up his prayer.

——The moment the magic crystal atop the Crystal Palace of the imperial capital Lupugana flickered to life.

——In this decisive battle for the imperial capital, several things repainted the picture Abel had drawn.

One: the existence of Priscilla, who joined the fray at the First Vertex, where Yorna Mishigure had gone.

Two: Otto, who used his own Divine Protection to display a talent for gathering detailed information on the state of the battle.

Three: Garfiel, who achieved not a mere stalemate but an outright defeat of Kafma Irulux.

Four: Emilia, who angered Madelyn Eschart further than expected and drew forth the "Cloudleviathan."

And yet, however much color those events added to the painting Abel had drawn, they were not events that fundamentally altered the completed form itself.

The star-shaped wall protecting the imperial capital had five vertices; breaking through even one, no matter the deadlock at the others, left a path to seizing victory.

While painting that picture, the one factor that would not waver no matter what was Moguro Hagane—no, rather, the "Meteor" that governed the Crystal Palace at the very core of the Vollachia Empire.

The Crystal Palace, boasting beauty unrivaled anywhere in the world, had crystal components built from magic crystal of especially high purity even among magic stones, and the palace itself was, in truth, a weapon for striking down outside enemies.

The mana stored within the palace was amplified through magic crystals set throughout the building and released from the Magic Crystal Cannon installed at the topmost level of the Crystal Palace.

Its power was said to be capable of erasing an entire major city, and legend held that, centuries ago, it had once been used—against none other than the "Great Disaster."

Ever since, the Crystal Palace's Magic Crystal Cannon had been treated as little more than a legend, but once its actual existence became known, it had been maintained to a state of operational readiness, and its threat restored.

Ironically, that cannon had become a fang now aimed at Abel himself, but as long as it existed, the tide of this decisive battle for the capital could all too easily be overturned.

Which was precisely why it was necessary, without fail, to make them fire that cannon.

Not as the decisive stroke meant to overturn the tide of the battle, but as one part of an already accounted-for cost.

To that end——

"——You did not withdraw, Zikr Osman."

Abel murmured that of Zikr, who had ridden his Galewind Horse into the Third Vertex, the battlefield.

Zikr had had a choice. He could have withdrawn from the fighting at the other vertices, gathered the rebels who chose to join the Third Vertex, stoked their morale, sent them charging at Moguro's colossal body blocking the way, and himself withdrawn from that front line.

But Abel had had a premonition that he would not choose that path.

Whether that was because of the caution of the "Coward" who feared, however slightly, that his aim might fail, or because he was, after all, one of the Empire's soldiers who held bravery to be right—which it was, Abel, not being Zikr, could not say for certain.

Only, whatever the reasoning behind the resolve, Abel had no intention of breaking it.

Zikr's life or death, too, was a matter that ultimately would not affect the final shape of the picture Abel had drawn. To speak on a grander scale, that picture ultimately encompassed even Abel's own life or death——

"——Report from the lookout! The Crystal Palace's Magic Crystal Cannon shows signs of activation!"

"——"

"Targeting: the Third Vertex!!"

The voice that rang out at headquarters like a roar of fury proved that Abel's aim had succeeded.

The Magic Crystal Cannon would fire. It would sweep across the Third Vertex—that is, the battlefield of Moguro, now transformed into the wall itself—and blow away the rebels there in one stroke.

Even so, as long as the Crystal Palace, Moguro's true body, remained intact, neither defeating the "Steelfolk" Moguro Hagane nor breaking through the Third Vertex would be possible.

But the cannon, capable of overturning any battlefield state, would be lost as firepower to the imperial capital.

Everything, all of it, had been drawn into the picture for precisely this purpose.

And so——

"——The Magic Crystal Cannon—anomaly on its line of fire!!"

"...What?"

——The report that came the very instant the scheme succeeded was something entirely outside Abel's expectations.

——In this decisive battle for the imperial capital, several things repainted the picture Abel had drawn.

One: the existence of Priscilla, who joined the fray at the First Vertex, where Yorna Mishigure had gone.

Two: Otto, who used his own Divine Protection to display a talent for gathering detailed information on the state of the battle.

Three: Garfiel, who achieved not a mere stalemate but an outright defeat of Kafma Irulux.

Four: Emilia, who angered Madelyn Eschart further than expected and drew forth the "Cloudleviathan."

And then——

"——Impossible."

Far off, beyond the writhing colossal form of Moguro Hagane transformed into a city wall, at the innermost heart of the beloved, familiar imperial capital stood the Crystal Palace, the very symbol of the Vollachia Empire's authority.

Atop that beautiful palace was the Magic Crystal Cannon, a weapon whose very existence as such was known to only a select few—indeed, not even the "Generals" had known of it until Abel himself spoke of it.

A decisive weapon said to be capable of sweeping the horizon and utterly shattering the tide of any battle, should it fire—and Zikr Osman had staked his life on making that precious single shot miss.

Riding his own beloved mare to the very front line, crying out in a loud voice that victory was within their grasp, whipping the rebels into a frenzy while proclaiming his presence, he had drawn the aim of that Magic Crystal Cannon onto themselves.

All of it, to create the false impression that this was the very best opportunity to decisively change the course of the battle.

So when the peak of the Crystal Palace lit up, Zikr was certain he had accomplished it.

What crossed his mind was his mother, his sister, his younger sister—the women indispensable to the shaping of who he was—and he even found himself smiling, thinking how utterly like himself this was, to the very end.

A prayer too beautiful to die clutching, an ending he should have met with calm—but that, before Zikr's widened eyes, was repainted into astonishment.

Because——

"——Lady Beatrice?"

"I thought it might come to this, I suppose."

Yielding herself to free fall, dress and long curled hair streaming, Beatrice murmured that.

When she had stood face to face with Zikr, who had come seeking a warrior's blessing at the command tent where Beatrice and the others were stationed, Beatrice had sensed at once that he meant to die.

Four hundred years ago, in that age of ceaseless war, there had been countless others with the very same look in their eyes.

Back then, Beatrice had not reached out a hand to them. Some of them had not wished for a hand to be extended; for others, she had not known how to extend it.

Even if she were to live through that age all over again, Beatrice could not imagine she would manage some better approach toward them now.

She would surely feel that same helplessness again, and watch, unable to stop it, as they walked toward death.

But that——

"——Isn't reason enough to do the same thing again, here, today, I suppose."

"Ah—u!"

Beatrice faced forward, the distinctive pattern in her eyes surfacing. Wrapped tight around her slender shoulders from behind were the arms of a girl, clinging as though enveloping her.

That golden-haired, blue-eyed girl—Beatrice had not forgiven her. It was a plain fact that the things she had done, unwittingly, had made a great many people unhappy.

And yet, even so, in this moment alone, Beatrice thought—

In this moment alone, she would trust in something of Natsuki Subaru's feelings other than his "softness."

"——"

Beatrice and the girl—Louis—were high, high above even the city wall, up in the open sky.

By whatever means, repeating short-range "Teleportation" again and again, Louis had carried Beatrice to this place, as if to prove her own standing.

First, Louis had proven her position. Then it was Beatrice's turn to answer that act in kind.

"I can just picture everyone's angry faces, I suppose."

Angry, or worried, or—well, angry after all, probably.

Those dear faces of her companions rose in her mind, and Beatrice's lips eased into the faintest smile.

But there was no option of not doing it. Because Beatrice was——

"Is Betty Subaru's partner, I suppose?"

The instant she murmured that, the whole of the beautiful palace visible in the distance blazed with dazzling light, a light so vast it seemed permitted to reshape the very form of the world, surging in a straight line toward the rebels charging across the ground below.

Swallowing up Zikr and the others racing at their head, a blow that would achieve destruction far beyond what was needed—into its line of fire Beatrice interposed herself and Louis, pressing her hands together before her chest.

And then——

"——Al Shamak."

——A hole into another world, one that could swallow even that world-altering light, opened, and closed.


　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　△▼△▼△▼△


How many, one wonders, could have properly grasped what happened in that instant.

Had it truly played out just as Abel had drawn it in his picture, then even accounting for it within the expected range, the damage would have been immense, and the effect on those fighting at the other vertices incalculable.

But the harm the Magic Crystal Cannon should have wrought did not come to pass, and Zikr Osman, having resigned himself to death, carried his momentum straight into battle against the swarm of stone dolls.

The descent of the "Cloudleviathan" Mezoreia and Emilia's fight against Madelyn, who had summoned it, continued on; the seasoned cunning of the "Vicious Elder" Olbart pressed Garfiel into a corner.

The mother and daughter of the "Kaleidoscope," Yorna and Priscilla, found themselves forced into fierce combat against the "Spirit Eater" Arakiya, who had resolved to cast aside everything for the sake of her own purpose; and Otto and Petra, scheming to seize control of the battlefield and in fact realizing that goal by half, stood alongside Medium against sheer madness.

None of those battles were greatly affected by it.

Only the possibility that any of them might tip sharply into a losing position had been lost.

And in exchange for that immense achievement——

"Uwah! A—wah! Awuu!!"

Held tight in Louis's arms as they plummeted through the empty air, the girl embraced there began to grow faint.

It was, literally, a sight of the girl's form and shape gradually thinning into nothing. As though refusing it, Louis clutched the girl—clutched Beatrice—all the tighter, but it did nothing.

As if spilling away, Beatrice's body was beginning to turn to light.

"Ah! Ah, ah—ah!"

Crying out as if in a scream, Louis desperately tried to deny the situation before her eyes. But no matter how she grieved, no matter how she wailed, the collapse of Beatrice's existence would not stop.

To save countless lives from the unleashed light of destruction, she had blasted it away into the far distance. In payment for that too-great power, Beatrice's existence was scattering, coming apart. Even reaching out fingers toward the light chipping away, peeling off, it slipped through, refusing to stay.

"Aaah——!!"

Great tears welled and spilled from her wide blue eyes, and Louis screamed.

She screamed, and screamed, and screamed though it would do no good, screamed desperately all the same.

Because there was nothing else she could do, nothing more within her power, and so she screamed.

And so, screaming desperately, wailing without end——

"——Ah?"

As a large tear scattered into the air, Louis turned her face toward the distance.

Not toward Beatrice in her arms, that girl on the verge of vanishing, held close to her; not toward the sky stained white, nor the sky stained red, nor the ground below where so many raised furious cries—in this moment alone, forgetting everything that made up that battlefield, she turned her eyes toward the distance.

And toward that distance, she leapt.

"Ah——"

In midair, the distance she could leap at once was roughly ten meters; using it again and again in succession placed a strain on her like her own insides being wrung out, but she did not care.

Taking on the whole of the burden that must also weigh on Beatrice, Louis leapt.

She leapt, and leapt, and leapt and leapt and leapt, leapt and leapt, and then——

"——"

Arriving on solid ground, she stumbled forward, pitching down. Ignoring the exhaustion suffusing her entire body, Louis held out before her the unbelievably light girl cradled in her arms.

Held Beatrice, her existence grown so faint, out in front of her.

And then——

"Uwau..."

"——I know. Both of you, way too reckless."

A sigh that sounded almost like a wry laugh reached her, and the girl was taken from her outstretched arms. ——No, not taken. Gently, she was lifted up.

And there, before the kneeling Louis, Beatrice's body was drawn into an embrace.

In the arms of the black-haired boy she had reached, gently, preciously, tightly held.

Receiving that embrace, overflowing so plainly with tenderness, the eyelids of the girl who had accomplished her great task, framed by long lashes, trembled, and slowly her eyes opened.

And then, that eye with its distinctive pattern blinked once——

"——You worry me too much, I suppose."

"Yeah. I love you too."

To that whisper of affection, he answered with a proud, deep love, and hands that were meant to join, joined.

Watching from the very front row, Louis let out a small breath, and before her, the boy laughed.

Laughed, and declared:

"Well then. Guess it's time to get started. ——Bring it on, Lady Destiny."
