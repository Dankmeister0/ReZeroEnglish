Scorpion Tale Chapter 1: "Memories of the Redhead"

1

"—Shaula. That's my name. I'm the Star Watcher of this Pleiades Watchtower, but don't go gettin' the wrong idea. You're just a washout who got let into the tower on a mercy pass."

She jabbed a finger at her, hammering that point home hard right from the start.

If the woman carelessly got the wrong idea and started strutting around inside the tower like she owned the place, that would be a serious problem. It would put the name of Shaula—watcher of the Sea of Sand, the "Star Watcher" entrusted with the tower's management—to shame.

Which was why Shaula had firmly resolved that, no matter what happened, she would never let slip that this tower's official name was not the Pleiades Watchtower, but the Great Library Pleiades.

"—"

The woman, meanwhile, dazed out of her wits, gave no reaction at all to Shaula's warning.

Chased around the Sea of Sand by witchbeasts, she had lost every last one of her companions and then lost consciousness on top of that; waking up on the tower's hard floor, she looked utterly stunned. That said, Shaula had no reason to look after the woman, and no obligation to play therapist over her wiped-out companions.

Guaranteeing her life here and hauling her out of the Sea of Sand—that much she supposed she could do.

"But that's just me followin' my own code, got it? It's absolutely not me meetin' you halfway, so burn that into your skull. You get the wrong idea, and, well—*that* happens."

"...And 'that,' you say, would be...?"

"It's super lame! Face-burningly embarrassing, like you wouldn't believe!"

The fate of someone who misreads another person's feelings and says something off the mark goes without saying. You get teased mercilessly, made a fool of, and forced to taste a humiliation that keeps you up at night.

Shaula herself shuddered, remembering the disgrace of having been put through exactly that in the past.

"Well, I don't remember the details of what actually happened to me, but what I *do* remember is this super intense karma about never, ever goin' through that again..."

"...I feel as though I may understand your feelings. This wretchedly inadequate memory of mine insists upon carving nothing but bad things, unlucky things, unpleasant things into this body of mine... Or was it the very height of presumption for me to claim to understand your feelings?"

"Huh? Ahh, I got no clue about the fine details or whatever, but it doesn't bother me."

"Is that so. That even an ill-bred creature like myself should be granted such indulgence—you must be a person of truly magnificent character. In this world of decline, to have met at the very last someone so admirably fine of heart, it is as though the sun has fallen even upon the end of my life."

"From top to tail, I got no idea what you're sayin'."

Her voice wasn't quiet, and it wasn't hard to make out, either—and yet the content and the atmosphere were both mushy and impossible to pin down. It was while thinking that far that Shaula noticed the woman in front of her had, at some point, come back to herself enough to hold a conversation.

"If you can talk to me, then I'm gonna take it you've got a proper handle on where you are and what you're doin' and who you are, yeah? 'Cause if you tell me you can't remember or you don't know nothin', that's a huge pain. One person with a leaky memory is plenty—Master's more than enough."

It was an awkward thing to admit, but "he" was terribly forgetful by nature. Things were forever slipping his mind, and Shaula and the others had suffered for it. Then again, Shaula was no less forgetful herself, so she was rather glad to have a matching pair of bad habits with him.

And just as she was steeping herself in such fond, heartwarming memories of "him"—

"—Leilani. That is the name, ill-suited though it is, that belongs to inept and improper me."

"Huh?"

"I ask this knowing full well how insolent it is, but you are... Shaula-sama, was it? The one who saved the lives of myself and these children."

As she spoke, the woman—Leilani—stroked her own belly, and Shaula blinked at her.

For a moment she wondered what this was about, but once she realized it was an introduction and a question both—an exchange of names in two senses—she found no particular reason to refuse, so she nodded. "That's right."

"I'm Shaula, no mistake about it. I got zero interest in your name or your background or any of that, so even if you tell me, I probably can't remember it."

"Ah, thank you ever so much. That you would show such kindness to lawless, immoral me—the sheer breadth of Shaula-sama's heart makes even so careless a creature as myself feel saved."

"You're over the top about every little thing, huh..."

Leilani clasped her hands as if in worship, larding her speech with convoluted turns of phrase, and Shaula—with no false modesty whatsoever—simply found it tiresome.

At first there had been a reason to be nice, this being the first guest the tower had received in over a hundred years, but that novelty had worn off fast, and as far as Shaula was concerned there was no particular joy in having someone to talk to. If anything, it only made her freshly aware of how deep her affection ran—of the fact that she had been born for "his" sake.

"So I'd like to hurry up and catch-and-release you outside the Sea of Sand, y'know?"

"I can only be ashamed of my ignorance, but if that should mean carrying me out of the tower, then—unwelcome and disagreeable as my request must be—might I beg you to wait?"

As she said it, Leilani rubbed her belly with both hands, and at the sight Shaula couldn't help groaning, "Blegh."

"Don't tell me the baby in there's gonna pop out right this second?"

"...It is undiscerning of me to say, but I believe there is yet a little time. Still, even absent any immediate crisis, I humbly think careless movement could endanger these children's lives."

"Mmgh, what a pain. Humans."

Arms folded, her scorpion tail swaying, Shaula sank into thought. Honestly, she knew next to nothing about human biology. Even so, since "he" had gone out of his way to say that pregnant women should be treated with care, she could guess that pregnant women were fragile creatures. And for Shaula, breaking one of "his" instructions was a matter of life and death.

"Haaah, nothin' for it. Out of respect for Master's face and Master's voice and Master's way of livin' and everything else besides, I'll postpone dumpin' you outside the tower."

"Ah, ah, what a merciful decision. To pardon a woman as base and shallow as myself... I could never bow this head to you enough."

Eyes brimming with tears of emotion, Leilani pressed her forehead to the floor to show her gratitude. Watching this with a pinky stuck in her ear, Shaula heard her continue, "By the way." Still sprawled flat on the floor, Leilani turned only her head upward and gazed at Shaula.

"It is a question from contemptible me, who combines obscurity with ignorance, but—might you happen to know where the shabby dragon carriage I was riding in has gone...?"

"Ahh, that thing? It's out there gettin' the full left-out-to-rot treatment. But the guys ridin' with you are all dead, y'know? Went boom-boom-boom-boom, blew themselves right up. That part alone was worth watchin'."

"No—indelicate as it is of me, they are of no consequence. However, the carriage... I had loaded it with nourishing things, for these children's sake..."

"Hoooh. And what happens if you don't have it?"

"Failure that I am in every respect, these children will..."

The rest of the sentence didn't come, and Shaula, still frozen mid-head-tilt in her questioning pose, took a while to realize she was meant to fill in the blank herself. But given the way the conversation had been trending, the only answer she could come up with was an annoying one. Which equaled—

"Don't tell me you're sayin' the babies'll die, so go fetch the carriage for me, huh?"

"I am keenly aware of what a shameful want of virtue this is, but for the sake of the babes in her belly, a mother can endure any reproach—impertinent as that may be."

"If I'd known it'd turn out like this, I shoulda slept in. Just for today..."

If she had woken up one hour later than usual, the dragon carriage carrying Leilani and her group would never have snagged on Shaula's surveillance net, and she could have thrown away the entire hassle of picking up a pregnant woman. Surely even "he" wouldn't hold Shaula responsible for victims she'd never noticed.

But she had noticed. And having noticed, she had to do it.

"You're so ridiculously humble and yet you order me around like hired help..."

"Please, revile me all you wish as the vulgar, hideous rubbish that I am."

"Feh! Now listen up. Don't go wanderin' around the tower on your own. If you get in the way of my Star Watcher duties, I won't let you off easy."

Jabbing a finger at her, Shaula laid down the law. At that, Leilani, still prostrate on the floor, wore a faintly clinging sort of smile.

"Powerless, graceless me could never manage such lawlessness—oh, never, never..."

And she indicated the long spiral staircase winding upward, and the great doors Shaula had been about to head toward—the tower's entrance, the fifth floor, "Celaeno"—brazenly insisting she could do nothing at all.

And in truth, Leilani's thin arms couldn't budge those great doors, and hauling that swollen belly all the way up to the fourth floor, "Alcyone," was out of the question too.

"Which means... don't tell me *I* gotta carry you up?"

Having noticed a truth she'd rather not have, Shaula stuck out her tongue with a "Blegh," then shoved the great doors open with both hands and stepped out into the Sea of Sand, where the sand-laden wind was picking up.

Leilani's companions and the earth dragons had probably had their carcasses picked over by witchbeasts, but there was a fair chance the carriage itself was untouched. Fetch it, feed Leilani, and—

"Huh? Hold on. If the babies come out of that human and she stops bein' a pregnant woman, do I get to do my Star Watcher thing and pound her flat?"

It was riddle-monk logic, but if the condition for being a pregnant woman was having a kid stowed in your belly, then the moment that stopped being true, it seemed she ought to treat her the same as any other challenger. She should have confirmed the finer regulations with "him." —But then a thought struck her.

"Hey, doesn't that answer sound exactly like that thug Reid? Isn't that the absolute worst?"

Reminded of that man, who never shied away from violent absurdity, Shaula grimaced.

What she hated most was that among the handful of people she remembered besides "him" and Mother, that infuriating man was one of them. It felt as though her memories had been defiled, and she hated it.

2

"—Oi, ya. Do somethin' entertainin' for me."

The one who said it was a red-haired man who looked like unreasonableness itself had put on clothes and gone out for a stroll.

Shaula hated this man. If her feelings for "him" had been cultivated at the level of the soul, then it was no exaggeration to say her loathing for this man was carved into her DNA. Which would mean the one who carved it was Shaula's Mother—but Mother surely hated this man too, so it stood to reason that the feeling would be inherited by Shaula, her creation.

Long red hair, an eyepatch over his left eye, a body packed with muscle, and slovenly clothes—

"I mean, it's not just his looks that're slovenly—it's his whole way of livin'."

"Oi, oi, ya. That's a hell of a thing to say right to my face. Ya tired of livin', ya? 'Reckless' must be your name, ya."

"Ya, ya, ya, ya—shut up already! You said you wanna see somethin' fun, yeah? Then peel your eyeballs open and watch *this*!"

Against the redhead menacing her in that thuggish, foul-tempered voice, Shaula let fly a bolt of white light.

Her killing move, Hell's Snipe: a light of ruin fired from the muzzle of a magic circle conjured in midair, vaporizing whatever it touched. At present she could only produce one of them, and Mother had told her to work that up to at least the number of fingers on both hands—but what mattered was hitting.

"In other words, so long as it lands, we're golden!"

Bending Mother's orders through creative interpretation, Shaula's all-out strike bore down from point-blank range, straight at the dopey-faced redhead—closing, closing, clos—

"Ya idiot, ya."

That infuriating insult and the white light's sudden swerve skyward came in the same instant.

Before Shaula, whose eyes went round with a "Fweh?", the redhead—who by all rights should have been GAME OVER'd—stood there perfectly unruffled. What on earth had happened? —Then she saw his hand, middle finger snapped up toward the heavens, and it clicked.

"No way—did you bend my Hell'Snipe with a finger flick?! You crap monster!"

"Hah! Who're ya callin' crap, ya. All that happened is your little game was about as impressive as a booger—a bit of nose-crap, ya!"

"Ahyn!"

No sooner had he come stomping up to the trembling Shaula than, on the tail end of his raised voice, he gave her tail end a smack. When Shaula shrieked and jumped at the rough treatment, the redhead laughed in high spirits with a face like a demon's.

"Kuhahaha! What's this? I figured all ya could do was squawk, but ya can make a girl's voice after all. Maybe ya'd be more use learnin' to pour drinks at that rate, ya."

"Wh-wh-wh-what the hell do you think you're doing! Every last inch of my body is of Master, by Master, and for Master! And you just—hiiiyn!"

"Hah. If the only thing ya're good at is runnin' your mouth, ya can't protect your chastity or your precious Master or a damn thing. Ya're so weak your Master's gonna bawl his eyes out, ya."

"I-I-I'll kill you! Murder! Murder confirmed!"

While they'd been talking, the redhead had drawn out two wooden sticks—"chopsticks," or whatever they were called—and tickled her bare stomach with them, and Shaula tried to blow the lawless redhead out of existence with a duet of rage and murderous intent. But the infuriating redhead dodged her arms with easy little hops and instead carried out a merciless campaign of chopstick-assisted sexual harassment: navel and rear, armpits and the backs of her knees, the nape of her neck and behind her ears.

Poked all over, unable to land a thing, Shaula felt so pathetic she could have cried—and the redhead snorted at her.

"What's this, what's this, ya. Ya're a hundred years too early to be tradin' blows with me. If that's all ya've got, then this 'Witch's' power ain't worth much either—"

"—Enough! Art thou at it again, thou lawless wretch?!"

"Gwoagh?!"

In that instant, the redhead was struck from directly behind and went skidding hard across the ground.

At this sudden bolt of divine retribution, Shaula forgot the follow-up attack she ought to have thrown and simply stared. And then someone briskly laid a hand on her shoulder from the front—a figure with golden hair down to about the shoulders and large, round red eyes.

The blond, in a flustered panic, asked her, "Art thou unhurt?!"

"Forgive me for being slow to rush to thy aid! A dreadful oversight, most unbecoming of me. What did that wretch Reid do to thee?!"

"He poked my glamorous body all over with chopsticks, as much as he liked."

"The deed of human scum! Is that not an outrage deserving ten thousand deaths?!"

The blond was aghast as Shaula meekly hugged her own shoulders. Over the blond's shout came the redhead's furious roar: "Hold it right there, ya!" Knocked flat by the blow, the redhead sat cross-legged on the gravel-strewn ground, rubbing the back of his head.

"The one doin' somethin' outrageous is ya. That thing ya clobbered me with—that's the stupid staff ya use to discipline the 'Dragon,' ain't it, ya."

"Cease calling it by so dishonorable a name as 'stupid staff.' It bears the exceedingly romantic name of the 'Star Staff.'"

"Like I give a damn about its name! Don't go smackin' people with the thing! Ya tryin' to kill me?!"

"Talk sense. Thou art sturdier than the 'Dragon' itself; there is no chance of thee dying from a swat by these feeble, slender arms of mine. Puff out thy chest—thou art the strongest upon the earth."

"Like I'm gonna puff out my chest over somethin' that obvious, ya idiot!"

"The two of you, ignoring me and yakkity-yakkity-yakking away—you're so loud!!"

Shaula exploded at the pair of them, yapping back and forth right over her head. Being talked past was no fun, and she hated that she lacked the strength to cut in.

Helpless, all Shaula could do was stamp her feet and holler like a child.

"Why?! Why is it like this?! I'm supposed to be Master's Star Watcher! If I can't even beat this sexual-harassment bastard, there's no point in me bein' alive!"

"Wait, wait, do not despair so, Shaula! Just now it merely happened that my royal surprise attack found its mark—it is no fault of thine! Thou art still growing, still in thy flourishing prime... any assessment at this present moment is mere decoration!"

"Ya serious about that? This runt, 'growing,' 'flourishing prime'—ya mean it? That's no different from the nonsense this pipsqueak spouts, ya."

"Toward a woman with her whole future ahead of her—what a want of delicacy! That is precisely what angers me most! What was that just now, toward a Shaula still so young! I knew thee to be a lecher, but that thou art so utterly indiscriminate—even I am appalled!"

"Ya, keep the jokes within reason. There ain't no man or woman about a pipsqueak like this! If I'm gonna poke somebody, I'd pick somebody worth pokin'. Somebody like—"

"—If thou art about to name the 'Princess' or the 'Witch'-dono, my contempt shall know no bounds."

Pinned by the blond's red-eyed glare, the redhead clicked his tongue and looked away. He had clearly been caught out—but for Shaula, the conversation had gone sour in two ways at once.

She hated any talk of the "Princess" who clung to "him," and being treated as a pipsqueak forced her to face the fact that she was still unripe: flat chest, flat rear, short arms and legs. It was a settled fact in her own mind that she would eventually obtain a dream dynamite body for "his" sake, but whenever she acted as though she already had one, the redhead or the "Dragon" would unfailingly mock her. The only ones who didn't were this blond and the bearded man—and the "Princess," admittedly.

"Personally, that's a no-thank-you from me, so it doesn't count."

"Fret not, Shaula. Believe it or not, in my youth I too was of such small stature that those around me feared for my future. Yet by eating well, playing well, and sleeping well, over and over—behold! I achieved a splendid growth into the fine figure of a man thou seest before thee!"

"A fine figure of a man? I don't see one. Where's this guy at, ya?"

"Do not stand beside me, thou genuine fine figure of a man! Thou dost eclipse me! Shaula will see straight through my white lie!"

The blond, with the redhead pointedly planting himself alongside him, whacked away at that thick chest with the staff in his hand—but it couldn't even stop the man from picking his nose.

Leaving the two of them to their roughhousing, Shaula folded her arms with a huff.

"Unlike Falsale, who's got no room left to grow, I've still got tons of room. That much is for sure, so my future potential's gonna beat the crap outta Reid."

"Wha—?! I was the one encouraging thee, and now am I the pitiable one? Am I not?"

Past the blond, lamenting with the face of a man whose ladder had been yanked out from under him, Shaula glared at the redhead and declared war. Hearing it, the redhead flicked away the booger he'd dug out and laughed again. "Hah."

What an infuriating face. But—galling as it was—he was strong. Strong, with a crap personality. Her resentment mounted toward Mother as well, for handing her over to that piece of crap for a while. But all of it was for "his" sake. Someday she'd make this redhead whimper, make him weep and beg her, "Forgive me."

Once she could do that, Shaula could proudly call herself what she was. —"His" Star Watcher.

And so, for that—

"Try not to get thrown away by me halfway through, ya."

Shaula must never, ever let the redhead—thrilling to the premonition of blood right before him—out of her sight.

3

—The "Saintess Sect" had branches all over the world, and the number of members belonging to them was considerable. She had the vague feeling she'd heard that explanation from Mother once.

"As far as I'm concerned, every one of those members looks like nothing more than a pitiful sacrifice whose ideals have been painted over with false hope. There is some room to sympathize with their circumstances, I grant you—but in the end, they offered up their own ears to the stories they wished to hear, and shut their own eyes to everything but what they wished to believe. So if their end arrives by way of standing against us, well, that can hardly be helped."

"Mother, you talk super fast."

"And you've grown awfully cheeky, haven't you! A bad influence from 'him,' I expect!"

She had a faint memory of an exchange along those lines.

Still, why had Mother gotten so angry that time? All Shaula had done was say what she thought—that Mother always talked fast when showing off her knowledge. Well, the upshot was being told she'd come to resemble "him," which as far as Shaula was concerned was a pleasing assessment.

In any case—

"—The hell? Look at all that rabble swarmin' around. What a pain."

Below them stood a chalk-white building set into a hollow in the cliff face, and the redhead spat the words out as he looked down at it. Beside him, a hand raised to his brow like a visor, Shaula struck the same pose and looked at the same scene, and at the sight of the rabble he'd mentioned—figures draped in white robes—she stuck out her tongue. "Blegh."

There were a lot of them. She could see why he'd called it swarming. But more than that—

"I just thought the exact same thing as this guy! My values have been violated!"

"Ya figure ya can say whatever ya please so long as it's to me, huh? Let me tell ya, a pipsqueak gets the same treatment as any guy. I'll pound ya flat, brat."

"Try it if you can! Master and Mother won't stay quiet if you do, and you're the one who'll have nowhere left in the world to go!"

"Wipe your own ass, ya! Quit talkin' big."

Lips twisted, the redhead made an argument like some respectable adult. But Shaula knew better. There existed no one in all the world who made other humans clean up his own messes more than the redhead standing in front of her.

The nerve of him, saying a thing like that to Shaula's face without batting an eye.

"Hm~? Hmm~? Hmmm~?"

Beside the glaring Shaula and redhead, the blond was groaning and tilting his body side to side. At his decidedly peculiar behavior, Shaula—still dragging along her bad mood from the redhead—asked, "What've you been going mmm-mmm about this whole time?"

"Hm? No, no—the both of thee are astonishing! It is far too distant; I cannot perceive any such swarming whatsoever. However did thy eyes become so keen?"

""Born this way.""

Her answer to the blond's question overlapped perfectly with the redhead's, and Shaula's whole body broke out in goosebumps.

Setting that aside, the blond's weak eyesight struck Shaula as a serious problem. Not being able to see a mere kilometer or so ahead—that was poor performance for a living creature. Or perhaps he was looking in some wildly wrong direction, and so couldn't find what she and the redhead were seeing.

"If that's it, then it's your brainpower that's in trouble. Falsale, which is it?"

"Naturally, insofar as I am a prince and therefore house a brain superior to the common sort, I am tempted to believe thine opinion that I am made of finer stuff. Yet—though it will sound like an excuse, and personally I would rather not say it—human beings cannot see hundreds of meters ahead to begin with!"

"Not hundreds of meters. More like a kilometer."

"Human beings cannot see a kilometer ahead!"

Even restated more accurately, the serious deficiency in performance remained uncorrected. If anything, his specs seemed lower than Shaula had guessed, and the blond began to look like an even frailer creature than before.

And it was just as she thought that that she tilted her head. "Huh?"

"Then what's this guy? Is he seriously just a monster?"

"Ya little..."

"Indeed! Just so! Reid is a monster! The greatest in the Kingdom—my sword!"

"Ya bastards!"

Called a monster by Shaula and the blond back to back, the redhead was indignant. Well, he could shout and holler all he liked; the facts wouldn't budge. Unlike Shaula, who was properly different from humans from the moment of her birth, the redhead was just a monster. Q.E.D.

"So, by the look of it, they're gettin' good and ready to march. Ya ain't gonna start grumblin' now, are ya?"

"...My eyes cannot see so far. Shaula?"

"I dunno what a 'march' is, but there's no mistake they're all fixin' to head out in a big group."

Beside the redhead's belligerent smile, the blond's face was grim, and at Shaula's answer he let out a heavy breath. "I see..." From the attitude of the blond—normally so cheerful, so loud in both voice and expression—Shaula vaguely sensed an air that told her not to butt in.

But the indelicate redhead clapped him hard on the shoulder.

"Don't sit there moping over somethin' so dull, ya. If ya can't decide, I'll go do it on my own. We'll say I never listened to a word ya said—"

"—No. That will not do. I am of the royal family."

"—"

"I left the royal capital and came along on thy journey not in order to be protected. On that point I shall not be mistaken. Therefore I shall not flee. I shall not avert my eyes. I shall not bend my way of being."

"A-a-awawa..."

Seeing the blond straighten a back that had begun to hunch and make that dignified declaration, colliding head-on with the redhead's position, Shaula couldn't help but shudder.

She knew the redhead's outrageous ways well. If something displeased him, he showed no mercy—not even to his own companions. The next instant, the blond might be cut clean in two.

And if that happened, Shaula would have to finish the redhead off in order to prove her innocence.

Out of nowhere, the greatest crisis of her life—of her Shaula-life, rather—had arrived. Or so she thought—

"—... Ya're a hell of a damn fool, ya are."

"Huh?"

The redhead, who by all rights should have gone on a rampage, tossed the words out along with a click of his tongue. As Shaula's eyes went round at the unexpected reaction, he tilted his neck and cracked it loudly.

"Well then, now that the princess has given her blessing, let's get goin'."

"Impertinence. I am a prince."

"Hah! Big words from somebody who does nothin' but get protected!"

Exhaling sharply, the redhead kicked off from the ground and flung himself off the cliff—and in that instant, seized the scruff of Shaula's neck as she stood there and dragged her along with him.

"Wh—gyaaaaaaah?!"

At this sudden act of suicide, Shaula screamed, her scorpion tail streaming above her head, the blond stranded atop the cliff receding in her vision. But the hand gripping her scruff yanked her in hard, her view flipped partway down, and now she could see the landing point instead.

It was the chalk-white building they'd just been discussing—the enemy-infested stronghold.

"Look sharp and make damn sure ya hit, pipsqueak. If ya can't kill the barrier mage keepin' that annoying barrier up, ya'll be a charred little eraser crumb."

Amid her own screaming and the roaring howl of the wind, the redhead's voice still came through clearly; and even while she was working out what it meant, the chalk-white building—guarded by a barrier, a pale light like a thinly stretched curtain—rushed closer and closer in her sight.

The single barrier mage maintaining that barrier was somewhere in that stronghold full of enemies. Unless she found him and shot him through, the barrier would burn them to cinders. —Yes: not just Shaula, but the redhead too.

"If I can't pull it off, you die too, y'know?!"

"Kuhahaha! Me, die? Not a chance in hell. The only one dyin' is ya. So what'll ya do, ya? —Live or die."

His laughter carried the conviction that he genuinely believed it, and Shaula caught her breath. She focused her awareness into her field of vision, perceiving each of the enemies within sight. There were ten. —If Hell's Snipe had ten muzzles, she could have shot them all through at once. But she had disobeyed Mother's instructions and skipped the practice, so she could produce only one. She had exactly one chance.

With that single shot, no matter what—

"—I am! Master's! Star Watcher!!"

"Oh?" —Shaula shook off the hand gripping her scruff and slipped free of the redhead. For one instant she fantasized about letting the redhead alone slam into the barrier and running off while he charred black—but no. She wouldn't run. She wouldn't lose. She would take it for herself.

Kicking off the cliff to alter the trajectory of her sliding fall, Shaula trusted her own eyes and made for the sniping point she'd chosen. She just barely reached it, deploying Hell's Snipe in the same motion—and hurled an all-out flash at the chalk-white building, threading it like the eye of a needle.

And then—

"—Hah. Not bad."

The praise reached her on the wind, and finding it irritating, Shaula raised her middle finger at him. Below, the barrier protecting the chalk-white building vanished. She had pierced the barrier mage. —Not, however, because she had succeeded in picking out the one mage among the ten.

With a single shot, she had killed all ten. —By lining her targets up along a single straight line of fire and making full use of ricochets.

"How's that! Now—"

It was your turn next, redhead—and right as she was about to crow it in triumph:

Falling a little ahead of her, the redhead raised his arm and swung down the chopsticks gripped in his hand. In that instant, the ferocious sword-wind he generated turned into light and swallowed the chalk-white stronghold in one breath. —With destructive force so great that whether the barrier had been there or not would have made no difference whatsoever.

"—"

At the bottom of her descent, Shaula stared at the wreckage of the chalk-white building, which had entirely lost its original shape, and looked blankly around her. So far beyond the standard, so far beyond common sense, so, so, so—

"—hateful, you jerk!!"

"That's what I've been sayin'! There ain't a damn thing I've got to teach ya. Watch on your own, get the idea on your own. If ya wanna protect that Master of yours."

"That's not what I was told! Mother said she had a promise with you—"

"Huh? ...Come to think of it, did I make a promise like that?"

At a reply even more heartless than she'd expected, Shaula's vision went scarlet.

That kind of attitude from the redhead was nothing unusual, but Mother had said she'd made him promise—and that she had paid a heavy price in exchange for that promise. So surely, Shaula had thought, even the redhead would honor it.

"Right, done and done. Come on, let's find some village or other—drinks, drinks!"

Waving a lazy hand up at the blond they'd left on the cliff, the redhead started walking.

That the blond's weak eyes wouldn't be able to see it; that Mother, pitiable enough to begin with, had been made more pitiable still—there were plenty of things she wanted to say. There were, but—

"I'm tellin' on you to Alec. That beard'll take my side for sure."

"Ugh. Now ya've gone and dragged out a troublesome name..."

"Well?! Feel like keepin' your promise with Mother now?!"

At Shaula playing her trump card, the redhead scratched vigorously at his head. Then, after groaning "Aaah" for a while—

"Fine. Tomorrow."

"Huh?! Why! All you're gonna do after this is guzzle booze and sleep, right?!"

"Noisy, ya are. Listen up. Anything ya can do tomorrow, ya can damn well do tomorrow."

"Gyow!"

The redhead flicked his finger against the forehead of Shaula as she hopped about clinging to him. As she toppled over from the force, eyes watering, the redhead laughed in high spirits, like a demon.

Pressing a hand to her throbbing forehead, Shaula looked up at the sky, her lips trembling. —Truly, truly, Shaula hated this man.

"—Someday, I'm definitely gonna kill you, Reid."

Truly, she hated him.

4

"—There! Is this good enough?! I brought it for you!"

The wreckage of the dragon carriage, scattered across the Sea of Sand—she had carried back as much of it as she could manage in one trip. Not so much out of kindness as because sorting through the luggage was simply too much bother.

What was needed and what wasn't—the subtleties of humans were beyond Shaula. Though she could at least judge that a broken wheel and a torn canopy probably weren't wanted.

"The rest I ditched on my own call. There's stuff that looks edible and boxes that looked like they were bein' kept careful and that sorta thing, so isn't that plenty?"

"Ah, I am most grateful... That you would go so far for an unworthy, inept, graceless creature such as myself—my gratitude is more than I can bear...!"

"You sure know how to lay it on. So? Was the stuff you wanted in there?"

"Yes. With this much, my children need suffer no wretchedness. Truly, with Shaula-sama I scarcely know where I might raise this bowed head of mine..."

"Don't need it. Havin' someone I hate bow to me isn't exactly a joy."

Leilani, who had been crouched over the carriage wreckage checking its contents, went stiff at Shaula's curt declaration. It was a reaction like someone who'd been dealt a shock—but to Shaula, that reaction was the truly surprising part.

In the first place, there wasn't the slightest room to be liked by Shaula. —Not after behaving so outrageously toward Shaula back when she was still newly born.

"Actually, y'know, maybe blastin' you away right here to settle a grudge I've held for years is an option?"

"Oh no, no—were merciful, magnanimous Shaula-sama to cast us aside, incapable and unversed as I am, neither I nor these children could possibly go on living...!"

"It's a joke, a joke. I couldn't do it even if I wanted to. Lucky you, bein' pregnant."

Leilani took a posture meant to shield her belly which only served to emphasize it instead, her face going pale, and Shaula stuck out her tongue at her. "Bleh."

Truthfully, it was a shame. Without "his" instructions, it would have been an unbeatable opportunity to detonate years' worth of anger.

In any case—

"This is as far as my kindness goes! Got it? From here on out, no matter what you say, I'm not liftin' a finger for you!"

"Of course, of course. To cling forever to your mercy—even insolent, unpolished me could not do such a thing. I could not, and yet..."

"...What."

"The floor here is hard, and cold. A hollow thing such as myself ought to bear any amount of unbearable hardship, surely. But, but—I cannot help pitying these children..."

At Leilani glancing up at her again and again, Shaula's jaw fell open.

Shaula had no sympathy for humans, much less for babies that hadn't even been born yet. But "his" instruction to be kind to pregnant women wound around Shaula's soul like a curse. Of course, if it meant being bound by "him," she'd gladly say bring it on.

"I'm tellin' you, this is the one and only time...!"

"Oh, how merciful! Truly you are a disciple of mercy sent down upon the earth...!"

Refusing to humor the smooth-talking Leilani, Shaula hefted the boxes of food the woman had sorted and stacked, and made to head for the spiral staircase. She loathed it with every fiber of her being, but making a space for a weak human meant going up to the fourth floor.

"Shaula-sama."

She was called, and turned. —The woman, rubbing her belly, sat slumped where she was, holding out her hand.

For the first time since she was born, Shaula found herself wanting to curse the words of her beloved "him."
