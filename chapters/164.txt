Arc 3 Interlude: "Itadakimasu"

—Rocked by the sway of the Dragon Carriage as it traveled the highway, Rem could think of nothing but him.

Feeling as though she'd heard her name called, Rem slowly lifted her bowed head. Then she narrowed her eyes slightly against the strength of the sunlight.
Ahead, where her gaze fell, were several Dragon Carriages leading the group, into which a number of the wounded from the Subjugation of the White Whale had been loaded.

Every last one of them was making the journey home having received only the barest minimum of first aid, and the number of severely wounded was hardly small. And yet, though their faces twisted with the pain of their injuries, their mouths were set with the satisfaction of having finally accomplished what they'd longed for through so many long years.
The accumulated feelings they had carried with them all this time—compared to the fact that those feelings had finally been fulfilled, wounds that had merely stopped short of killing them hardly seemed worth weighing.
For those who had accomplished what needed to be done, this road back to the royal capital was something like a triumphal march. And yet, watching them, Rem found herself disgusted with her own pettiness—unable to suppress the ache of feeling that welled up in her at the sight.

"You look troubled, Rem. I take it your worries haven't let up."

"...Crusch-sama."

Turning at the voice, Rem found Crusch seated right beside her.
Bandages wound beneath her light clothing, Crusch carried herself in a way that betrayed none of the not-inconsiderable wounds she bore—a bearing that left Rem full of admiration. Even so, she couldn't entirely hide her physical exhaustion. The very reason she was riding in the Dragon Carriage at all was that there had been concerns over letting her ride an Earth Dragon alone; it had been arranged that she would ride together with Rem at least until the royal capital came into view.

Meeting Rem's concerned gaze, Crusch shrugged her shoulders as if to prove she was still hale, then tipped her chin lightly toward her and said, "More to the point—"

"Wilhelm and Ferris. The valiant men of the subjugation force who went with them are elite as well. Ricardo and his mercenary band should lend a hand too... and above all, it's hard to imagine Anastasia Hoshin failing to think that far ahead. The enemy's strength is a source of unease, but I sense nothing in the way of a reason to lose."

"Even so—is it selfish of me to still worry?"

"No matter how many seeds of anxiety you crush, they never run out. If the cause lies within yourself, you can manage it however you like—by honing yourself, or simply resolving to face it head-on. But when the matter depends on the other party, that becomes difficult. ...I'm not skilled at offering comfort. Forgive me."

Seeing Rem's expression darken further with worry, Crusch realized her own misstep and lowered her eyes. In that instant, the aloof, detached woman she'd been until then seemed suddenly to shed her formality, and Rem, without meaning to, let her lips curve into a small smile.
Seeing that smile, Crusch nodded with satisfaction. "Mm, that's better," she said, and continued:

"Natsuki Subaru said the same thing. That smiles suit Rem better. Overhearing it, I took it for a shameless lovers' compliment, but it turns out it's not something to dismiss so lightly after all."

"Crusch-sama... your impression changes entirely when you smile. You're always so dignified, and yet when you smile like that, it's almost as if..."

"I'm told that on occasion, and it does concern me. Which is precisely why I can't afford to smile carelessly in front of others. It only goes to complete the picture of an unsociable woman."

Rem hesitated a moment, unsure whether it was meant as a joke she could laugh at, but seeing the faint softness at the corner of Crusch's mouth, she let her own lips loosen as well.
Always valiant and composed, Crusch's bearing was, to Rem—who was forever lacking in confidence and prone to timidity—one ideal of what a woman could be. Of course, for Rem, the very highest ideal was none other than her older sister, Ram.

In any case—

"What awaits us ahead is the Witch Cult. ...It's something we've expected ever since learning of Emilia's origins, but when the enemy is a group whose true nature remains unseen, vigilance is essential. Natsuki Subaru, surely, and Lord Mathers as well—both must have some countermeasure prepared, don't you think?"

"Rem isn't privy to the full depths of her master's thinking. Even if I tried to pry it out of him, I wouldn't be able to say it aloud."

"How harsh. We're allies now, you know—you could let a little slip."

No doubt Crusch was being careful to keep Rem's thoughts from drifting toward the dark and the bad. In truth, thanks to Crusch steering the conversation this way, Rem had managed to pass the time without her thoughts sinking too deep.
Crusch's point was a fair one; surely Roswaal had some plan of aftercare prepared for this whole affair. Subaru's actions had taken the form of aiding his master's, and the honor Subaru had so unfortunately had dragged through the mud would surely be restored.
No—thanks to his contribution to the Subjugation of the White Whale, his honor would ring out even higher than mere restoration.

—The hero, Natsuki Subaru.

To Rem, whose heart and future had been saved by him, it was only a natural evaluation—nothing less than the rightful recognition of the brilliant time he would surely go on building.
And if, alongside that brilliance, her own existence could hold a place where he might occasionally turn to look at her, Rem asked for nothing more than that. That alone was enough to fill her.

Whenever she thought of Subaru, Rem's heart always filled with complicated feelings.
A warmth, a kind of ease settling over her. And yet, at the same time, an anxiety that made something ache somewhere inside her, a worry that left her heart fluttering in distress.
Though it was only Subaru who could ever give her heart such ceaseless swings between joy and sorrow.

With a smile etched at her lips, Rem imagined Subaru's future, and a future with him.
Perhaps finding some relief at the sight of that profile, Crusch, too, touched the sheath of the knight's sword at her side and gazed silently, steadily, at the road ahead the Dragon Carriage traveled—her thoughts turned to the journey to the royal capital.

And then—

"—Hm?"
"——?"

Crusch narrowing her eyes with a small murmur, and Rem lifting her head at the sound of some faint noise, happened at nearly the same instant.

What Crusch's eyes had caught was something wrong with the Dragon Carriage ahead. And the strange sound Rem had heard had come from the very same direction. And in the very same instant, the two of them arrived at a single outcome.

—Within Crusch's field of view, the Dragon Carriage ahead "collapsed." To Rem's ears, the sign of that collapse had arrived as a chain of small sounds, like rain.

A mist of blood erupted upward, and the front of the procession abruptly turned into a scene of carnage.
Earth Dragon, Dragon Carriage, and the wounded who had been riding within—every last one of them, root and all, was pulverized into fragments by an utterly merciless, overwhelming destruction.

"—! Enemy attack!!"

Crusch let the shock catch in her throat for no more than an instant before her voice rang out in warning. At once, both Crusch and the other Dragon Carriages nearby sensed the disturbance and stirred with the readiness for battle.
Rem, too, pushed aside her own bodily injuries and fatigue, took up her weapon—the iron ball—and rose to her feet, and beyond the mist of blood, she saw a human shape.

Wary of what kind of enemy she'd find, Rem's eyes fell on a figure standing bolt upright on the highway.
Empty-handed. Undefended. Unguarded. And a malice that was merciless, innocent, and utterly random—!

"—Run him down!!"

Crusch bellowed, climbing onto the driver's box as she barked the order. The knight who heard it snapped the reins in place of a nod, and the whinnying Earth Dragon surged the Dragon Carriage forward into a charge—an accelerating rush that turned into a cannon shell of overwhelming mass, one that would reduce any prey it struck directly to a lump of meat.
It found its mark without fail, bearing straight down on the figure standing motionless. The man showed no sign of moving. It made contact, and his slender body should have been torn apart by the impact—

"Crusch-sama—!"

Rem shouted, grabbing Crusch, who was right beside her, around the waist, and leapt sideways off the Dragon Carriage. There was no time to reach the driver; Rem bit her lip and landed on the ground.

And immediately after—

"Honestly, I wish you'd cut that out. Ordering someone run down when I haven't done a thing—that's just not something a decent person would do, if you ask me."

It was the kind of voice one might use out for a leisurely stroll through a park on a lazy afternoon—basking in warm sunlight, about as relaxed a tone as could be.
Had it not been spoken in the very moment of a collision, amid a Dragon Carriage shattering and flying to pieces on impact, Rem would surely not have felt such a chill at how abnormal it was.

At a glance, he was a man of no particular remark.
A slender build, white hair neither long nor short nor styled in any eccentric way. His clothes, black in tone, were neither especially gaudy nor shabby, and his features held nothing that caught the eye. He was the very picture of the mundane—the sort of man you might find anywhere, who could blend into anywhere, who if you passed him on the street would slip from your memory within ten-odd seconds.

But in fact, the Earth Dragon that had collided with him lay torn in half, still frozen in the posture of a raised leg meant to trample him to death, and the knight who had served as driver had been pulverized along with the shattered Dragon Carriage, reduced to a state where wood splinters and flesh could no longer be told apart.
And what was most terrifying of all was that Rem, who had not looked away for even an instant up to that point, could tell that the man had done nothing but simply "stand there."

Doing nothing out of the ordinary, the man had merely stood rooted in place—and in doing so had overcome a collision with a Dragon Carriage of overwhelming mass, standing there unshaken as if nothing had happened.

"My thanks, Rem. You saved me. But... the situation hasn't exactly improved, has it."

From within the wide-eyed Rem's arms, Crusch, whom she'd been holding, offered her thanks and rose to her feet. She drew the knight's sword she'd instinctively grabbed free of its sheath, and narrowing her eyes in anguish at the sight of the knight who had died following her own orders—now in a state too shattered even to tell apart—she said:

"Having slaughtered my vassal so mercilessly, you surely don't expect to walk away unscathed. ...Just what are you?"

Crusch leveled her sword's tip, gleaming with killing intent, and hurled a sharp voice at the man. In response, he set a hand to his chin and began nodding, as if convincing himself of something.

"I see, I see. So you don't know who I am. Though for my part, I know who you are. After all, right now the royal capital—no, the whole country—is talking about you. A candidate for the next king, no less. Even someone as out of touch with worldly affairs as I am can imagine that means you're about to shoulder something tremendously heavy."

"Enough idle chatter—answer the question, or the next cut won't miss."

"What a harsh thing to say. But then, maybe you have to be that arrogant to shoulder a whole country. I can't understand that sensibility one bit, though—well, I suppose there's no way I could understand the mindset of someone who'd willingly aim for a position with responsibility as crushing as king. Ah, but just because I can't understand it doesn't mean I'll deny it. For my part, I haven't the slightest intention of behaving so arrogantly myself. I'm not like you, after all..."

On and on, ignoring Crusch's demand entirely, the man kept his glib tongue running.

But—

"—I said there wouldn't be a next time."

Crusch's cold declaration and the swing of her arm unleashing a blade of wind came in the very same instant.
An invisible slash combining Crusch's wind Magic with her swordsmanship—the ultra-long-range, top-class strike famous as the "Hundred Men, One Stroke," descending diagonally to shear clean through a man's torso, killing its victim before he could even tell where the blow had come from or who had loosed it.

It was the battlefield epithet earned by Duke Crusch Karsten back when the Witchbeast known as the Great Rabbit had appeared in the Karsten domain—when she annihilated the Witchbeasts under the Great Rabbit's command out on the plains, marking her first battle with an astonishing tally of kills. "Hundred Men, One Stroke."

It was a strike whose power had sliced even through the White Whale's tough hide, contributing greatly to bringing down that colossal body. Against a frame so puny it wasn't even worth comparing to that Witchbeast's mass, there was no way it could be withstood.

And yet—

"...Attacking someone while they're in the middle of enjoying a good talk—what kind of upbringing did you have?"

There he stood, tilting his head, lightly dusting off the body that had just taken the blow.
His existence had not so much as flinched before a strike that could cleave the White Whale, and on his body—no, not even on his clothes—was there any trace of the sword's passage.
This was not a case of the blow being blocked; it was an entirely different, unknown phenomenon altogether.

Crusch caught her breath, and Rem, too, went rigid before a being that lay wholly outside common sense. Before the two of them, the man let out a small sigh for the first time, and lowering his voice in irritation, said, "Look—"

"I was talking. I was talking, right? Interrupting that—that just seems off to me, doesn't it? Don't you think that's wrong? I don't want to go around claiming some 'right to speak' or whatever, but still, isn't it a kind of unspoken understanding that if someone's talking, you don't interrupt them? Whether you listen seriously or not is your own business, I won't complain about that, but deciding I'm not allowed to speak at all—how's that supposed to work?"

Speaking rapidly, the man stamped his foot against the ground, making his displeasure plain. He pointed a finger at the two women, who had gone silent with unease, and continued:

"And now silence—what's that about too? I'm listening, aren't I. I listened, didn't I. I asked a question. You're supposed to answer when someone asks you something, that's how it works, isn't it? And you won't do that either. Won't. Fine, freedom, sure. That's your freedom, your right. From your point of view, I go around talking on my own and get cut down for it, asking questions on my own and getting ignored for it—that's how it looks to you. That's how you're choosing to use your freedom. Fine, go ahead, do that. But you know, doesn't that idea basically boil down to this?"

Leaning forward, tilting his head at the two of them, the man's eyes sharpened, piercing them, and then in a voice pressed low, he said:

"That means you're trampling on my rights—on my few, meager possessions. Doesn't it?"

A chill raced up Rem's spine, and in the next instant, the man took a step forward. The arm he'd let hang carelessly at his side swung from bottom to straight up, and a faint breeze stirred.
Immediately after, along the straight line of the man's arm—the earth, the air, the world itself split open.

Round and round, spinning through the air, went Crusch's left arm, severed at the shoulder.
The arm, still gripping the sheath, scattered blood as it fell to the ground, and Crusch's body, blown back by the impact, collapsed onto the earth, beginning to convulse from the violent pain and blood loss.

"Crusch-sama—"

For a few seconds, Rem stood dumbstruck, then immediately leapt to the fallen Crusch's side. Wringing out every last drop of Mana she had left, she pressed her hands to Crusch's wound and began working with all her strength to stop the bleeding and heal her.
The wound at Crusch's severed arm was, with an almost admirable cleanness, a perfect severing straight through the flesh, bone, nerves, and blood vessels of her left arm. At the sheer, almost artistic sharpness of that attack, Rem felt, more than terror, an admiration entirely out of place.

"Feri...s... ah, unh, ahh?"

In Rem's arms as she worked to heal her, Crusch let her unfocused gaze wander while murmuring deliriously. Her remaining right hand gripped Rem's leg, an inhuman strength in that grasp making the bone of her knee creak. Proof that the strength to live still remained in her.
Gritting her teeth to bear it, Rem kept her eyes sharp and fixed on the atrocity of the man before her.

She had no idea by what means his attacks were blocked, nor what that last blow had truly been. There was nothing to do but watch for the slightest telltale motion and evade with Crusch in tow.
To begin with, something about this whole situation was wrong. Why, leaving only Rem and Crusch here, had none of the others come rushing to face this monstrous man? With their liege dealt a wound this fatal, why hadn't the valiant warriors who had stood against the White Whale itself—

"Ahh, seriously... no matter how much I eat, it's never enough! This is why we can't stop living! Eat, devour, chew, gnaw, feast, sink my teeth in, tear it to pieces, crunch it up, lick it, slurp it, suck it, lick it clean, suck it dry, drink to excess! Eat to excess! Ahh—thanks for the meal!!"

It was a shrill boy's voice, arriving suddenly from behind her.
Feeling on her back the same chill she'd sensed from the man before her, Rem turned, her whole body rigid with tension, and there behind her, at the center of the halted cluster of Dragon Carriages, she caught sight of a blood-drenched figure, arching backward in wild laughter.

A short boy with dark brown hair grown out past his knees. His height was about the same as Rem's, if not shorter, and he looked two or three years younger—only a little older, it seemed, than the children of the village near the mansion.
Beneath that long hair, his slender body was wrapped in nothing but grimy cloth like ragged scraps, and what little skin peeked through was stained red with blood, everywhere it showed.

It was, of course, not his own blood, but without doubt that of the knights lying fallen at his feet.
Apart from Crusch and Rem facing the enemy ahead, the knights, too, had been confronting an enemy that had appeared behind them. And the outcome had been a rout so swift it hadn't even let Rem sense the battle taking place—

"Just what... are you?"

Her voice trembling, Rem backed away to a position where she could keep both enemies—front and back—within sight. The blood dripping from Crusch etched vermilion into the highway, and as if mocking her, driven by fear, the air around her grew colder and colder.

At being asked that question, the man and the boy exchanged glances.
Then, as if by prior agreement, they nodded to one another, and both wearing a smile terribly familiar, violent, and demonic all at once, gave their names.

"Witch Cult Sin Archbishop in charge of 'Greed,' Regulus Corneas."

"Witch Cult Sin Archbishop in charge of 'Gluttony,' Lye Batenkaitos."


* * *


"I felt like our pet got taken out, so I came to see, and ahh—now this, this is quite the harvest, isn't it! Good, good, good, good, maybe, sure, why not, I'll bet it is! Tenacity! Love! Hatred! Chivalry! All sorts of joys and sorrows mixed together! That's what makes it—that's what makes it worth devouring!"

The Witch Cult—and Sin Archbishops, no less.
Before Rem, frozen at the very sound of those words, the boy—the one who had named himself Lye Batenkaitos—stamped the ground in excitement, letting out a strange cry.
He spun round and round where he stood as if dancing, gesturing at the fallen knights with his arm and gazing at them with something like fondness.

"Yeah, this really is the best—coming to eat with my own hands like this. Especially lately, since I haven't had the chance to meet folks this full of grit and worth chewing on, so it's been a while since I've felt this hunger of ours actually get filled!"

"That's the part of you I just can't understand, Batenkaitos. This talk of hunger—it's not as if you actually starve, and even calling it 'being filled,' none of it's even yours to begin with. Why can't you just be satisfied with who you are right now? Listen—a person can only hold as much as fits in two arms, only what fits in the palm of his own hand, you know? If you understood that, you'd naturally be able to rein in your own greed, wouldn't you?"

"We don't need a sermon, and we don't like them. I won't say what you're saying is wrong, but I'm not interested either. Ahh—honestly, we, all of us, don't care about anything except filling this hunger."

Batenkaitos of "Gluttony" laughed with a mad edge, while Regulus of "Greed" shrugged, looking bored.
Faced with the situation of two Sin Archbishops present in the same place at once, Rem forced her mind, which threatened to shut down, to keep turning, trying somehow to find a way to break through.

In terms of raw strength, crushing these two here and now was impossible.
She had stopped Crusch's bleeding, but her condition remained dangerously critical. Whether the knights were dead or merely wounded, she couldn't tell—either way, they couldn't be counted as fighting strength.
As for Rem herself, she'd already spent what little Mana she had left on the treatment just now. If she transformed into her Oni form and fought, she might still hold out a while—but faced with these two, no vision of victory came to her.

On one side, "Greed," who showed not the slightest opening in either offense or defense—a figure rumored to have brought down an entire city through personal combat strength alone, his depths unknowable. And on the other, "Gluttony," whose sheer strangeness left his own depths just as unfathomable. His combat strength was unknown, but he was skilled enough to have swept aside a company of battle-hardened knights in mere tens of seconds. Here too, no future of defeating him presented itself.

Glancing around, Rem noticed the Dragon Carriage that the pack of Ligers had been pulling was nowhere to be seen. It was the one carrying the returning and wounded members of the Beastman mercenary band—and the head of the White Whale, the only part they had succeeded in bringing back.
It had likely fled amid the chaos and was, by now, racing at full speed toward the royal capital. The one commanding it was likely Hetaro, the mercenary band's vice-captain. He was, as far as she knew, sharp and level-headed, so if she could buy enough time, he might return leading reinforcements.
But even that—Rem didn't think it would arrive in time while she alone held off these two.

"...The White Whale."

"Oh?"
"Ahh?"

Catching Rem's murmur, both Sin Archbishops tilted their heads at the same time.
Rem, sensing she'd found some small foothold for a conversation to buy time, held her breath for a moment, and, so as not to let their interest fade, spoke quickly:

"Did you come chasing after us to reclaim the White Whale? The head we cut off—Rem and the others were trying to bring it back to the royal capital."

"The head? Ahh, so that's why it smelled so weird—that's what was going on. Don't need the head anyway, and there's no point bringing back something dead, so it's fine. If we wanted to, we could just make another one... though, well, it'd probably take just as long to grow, I guess."

Batenkaitos said something that couldn't be let pass without notice, and said it lightly, at that, cracking his neck. Then, clicking his teeth together, he declared strongly, "More importantly—"

"What we were interested in wasn't the White Whale dying, but the ones who killed it. Whatever else it was, that thing had been dawdling along for four hundred years, and you killed it. I figured you had to be a fine crop of well-ripened, ready-to-eat sorts... ahh, and this exceeded every expectation!"

Nodding his head up and down, whipping his impossibly long hair about, the boy laughed, spittle flying. All the while his teeth—the canines strangely long—clicked together, click click click click, as he went on:

"Love! Chivalry! Hatred! Tenacity! The satisfaction of achievement! All of it stewed and stewed and simmered down slow and long into a bubbling boil, and the satisfaction of that going down my throat! Does any delicacy in this world surpass it!? No, no, no, no, of course not, of course not, of course not, precisely because there's no way it could! Drink to excess! Eat to excess! Because of all this! Our hearts, our stomachs, are trembling with joy and with fullness!"

None of what he was saying made any sense.
As if some restraint had come utterly undone, Batenkaitos kept laughing in that shrill boy's voice. Amid the echo of that strained, unsettling sound, Rem silently shifted her gaze, and Regulus, meeting it, waved a hand.

"Unfortunately, I'm nothing at all like him, you know? My being here is entirely coincidental, not the result of me acting on my own initiative in the slightest. Naturally, of course. I don't carry around that sort of thing he has—hunger, craving, whatever you want to call it. That kind of base, greedy desire simply isn't in me. Unlike him, forever tormented by hunger he can never satisfy, I'm someone who, you see, is endlessly satisfied with who I am right now."

Spreading both hands wide, Regulus wore a bright, cheerful expression before Rem.
Swinging wide the very same arms capable of the exact feat that had severed Crusch's arm, he made a show of himself, as if to assert his presence all the more strongly.

"Conflict and all that—I really don't like it, personally. I'm the type who's perfectly content just living out plain, ordinary days of calm and quiet peace, nothing more than that, I don't ask for anything beyond it. Peaceful, uneventful, unchanging time and an unchanging self—that's the best there is. My hands are small and powerless. I'm a weak being who can barely manage to protect the one piece of private property that is myself."

Regulus clenched his fists tight as he made his impassioned case. And this from a man who, with just the motion of that fist, had cut down an Earth Dragon and several lives, and severed the arm of a single woman—what an outrageous thing to claim.
Batenkaitos, laughing on and on like a madman, and Regulus, indulging in self-satisfaction while rattling off his self-serving philosophy—both of them, without exception, were deranged. Of course. These were the Witch Cult.

Anger came bubbling up within her.
Rem laid Crusch, sunk into breathing so deep it looked deathlike, down onto the grass, and pushing her trembling legs to their limit, rose to her feet. Iron ball in hand, she wrung out what little Mana remained to her and floated ice pillars around her body.
Seeing this, Batenkaitos's and Regulus's expressions both changed.

"Were you listening to what I said? I told you I don't want to do this, didn't I? And if you heard that and this is your attitude anyway, then that's already you ignoring my opinion. That's you violating my rights. That's you trying to steal from me the tiny self that's allowed to be mine, my own private property. ...Even as un-greedy as I am, I can't forgive that."

"Is that all you have to say, Witch Cult?"

Rem declared this, unflinching, at Regulus as he raised his head. At the sight, Regulus recoiled slightly, and rattling the chain of her iron ball in warning, Rem went on:

"Someday, without fail, a hero will appear who will crush the both of you. He'll make you understand, without question, just how selfish you are, just how much misery you've created out of your own self-satisfaction. Rem's beloved, one and only hero, will."

"Heh, a hero. Now that's something we're looking forward to. Being believed in that much must mean he'll taste all the more delicious to us, too."

Batenkaitos clapped his hands and bent forward at the waist, extending his tongue as he sized Rem up.
It was not the look of one sizing up an enemy, still less the look of one regarding a woman. The light that dwelled in that gaze was, quite precisely, nothing other than that of a starving demon licking its lips over food.

Behind him, the figures fallen behind Batenkaitos grew hazy in her mind.
What his existence was, what their standing had been—Rem could no longer grasp it. Why were they lying there, who had they been, what had their relation to her been.

The White Whale's mist had been the manifestation of a nightmare, one that erased the very existence of anything it touched, existence and all. If so, then the Authority held by "Gluttony," who had supposedly commanded it, must surely be—

"Head servant to Margrave Roswaal L Mathers—Rem."

Having meant to give her name after her station, Rem shook her head.
Just this once, just in this one moment, she wanted to give the name she truly wished to give—

"Right now, just one person's beloved. —Attendant to the one who will someday become a hero, my most beloved, Natsuki Subaru. Rem."

A white horn thrust up from her brow, gathering in the Mana that filled the air and pouring vitality into her.
Strength surged through her whole body, the arm gripping the iron ball rippled with power, and the ice pillars waited, poised, for the call to come.
Eyes wide open, taking in the world, feeling the air around her, she did nothing but picture him in her mind.

"Steel yourselves, Sin Archbishops. —Rem's hero will come to judge you, without fail!!"

She swung the iron ball high, and in the same instant the ice pillars launched forward, Rem's body burst into motion, flying forward.
As if to meet her head-on, Batenkaitos opened wide his mouth, full of fangs, and said:

"Ahh, what fine spirit—well then, itadakimasu!"

They collided, collided, and in that instant, she thought:

If it were possible, when he learned that she was gone, she hoped it would send even the smallest ripple through his heart.

—That alone was Rem's wish, in her final moment.

- Title: "イタダキマス" (itadakimasu, the set phrase said before eating) is kept transliterated rather than translated, since it's the chapter's thematic keyword tied directly to Batenkaitos's closing line.
- "百人一太刀" rendered as "Hundred Men, One Stroke" — my own translation of Crusch's epithet; not in the provided glossary, should be checked against wiki convention if one exists.
- Batenkaitos's dialogue alternates between the Japanese first-person pronouns 僕 (boku) and 俺 (ore), often within the same speech — a deliberate quirk hinting at his "Gluttony" Authority having absorbed multiple identities. English has no equivalent distinction, so this is flattened to "I"/"we" throughout; the alternation itself is lost.
- "獣人傭兵団" translated descriptively as "Beastman mercenary band" (Ricardo's troop) — not a term given in the glossary.
- Rem's closing self-introduction shifts from her usual third-person "Rem" speech pattern into first-person ("my most beloved") before returning to "Rem" at the very end — this shift is preserved deliberately as it appears to be intentional in the source.
</notes>
