Arc 7 Chapter 48: "The City of Tangled Schemes"

Fixing his gaze on the young beastman boy, Abel hurled his merciless question, and Subaru's eyes went wide.

The name that had come spinning off Abel's tongue was so unexpected.

"Tanza? You mean that girl…?"

Amid the confusion in Subaru's head, the image that rose up was of the kimono-clad Deer Human girl. Yorna's attendant, the one who'd shown her face both at yesterday's keep and at today's inn — the one who'd left an impression for being so composed despite still being a child, and for her curt manner besides.

And Abel was flatly declaring that a girl like that was the mastermind who'd orchestrated all this.

"I— it doesn't make any sense! Why would she—gubh!"

"Uaoh!"

The instant Subaru raised his voice to press for Abel's reasoning, the impact of something tackling him sent him toppling backward. Looking up, he found a grinning Rui straddling him where he'd landed on his back.

Rui — who, with astonishing ferocity, had beaten down nearly ten enemies in the blink of an eye. At the sight of her utterly innocent expression now, Subaru couldn't help but catch his breath.

*The power Rui had unleashed just now — unmistakably, it was the same thing he'd seen at the sand tower.*

Not power native to Rui herself, but power stolen outright from someone else who'd worked hard to build it. In other words, what she'd used was the power of "Gluttony"—

"…G-get off me."

"Ah?"

"I said get off!"

Subaru pushed at the shoulders of the girl on his chest and forced himself upright. The momentum sent Rui tumbling onto her backside, and in the process, her stomach came into view — along with the white fabric stained red.

Seeing it, he remembered she'd been injured.

"Your wound! We need to treat it—!"

"Awh! Ah-woo!"

He hurriedly pulled up the hem of her clothes to check the injured spot. But Rui thrashed as if ticklish, pushing at Subaru's face and neck in resistance. Ignoring the struggle, he managed to lift the fabric and check the wound — and while there was indeed a trace of blood having oozed there, there was no sign of anything like a scar.

"It's… healed?"

Sure enough, there was evidence of bleeding, yet no wound remained. Feeling as though he'd been bewitched by a fox, Subaru sat there dumbfounded — until Rui roughly jabbed both hands into his chest. Though what would happen if Rui jabbed someone in earnest, the several figures lying collapsed in the street could attest to.

In any case—

"—Answer me. Where is Tanza?"

Even as Subaru confirmed Rui was unharmed, Abel's interrogation continued unabated. The interview from hell was terrifying — not only from the force of Abel's glare, but from Medium's blade standing behind him serving as an unspoken threat. Even without Medium meaning it that way, her simply standing behind Abel had that effect.

Indeed, the boy glanced back and forth between the two of them, his thin throat bobbing.

"Ah…"

"That makes three times. Answer. Do not imagine there will be a fourth."

It was a cruel voice, carrying a chill that seemed to freeze one from the inside. Hearing it, the boy opened and closed his mouth several times before the fire in his eyes flared back to life, however faintly.

"H-how would I know?! If anyone knows where Tanza is, it's you people—!"

"—————"

"You people did something to Tanza… that's why! That's why we're doing this!"

Spitting the words like a snarl, the boy's arm shot out toward Abel standing before him. A reckless act with no regard for his own safety — his fingers reaching for Abel's throat to crush it. But before they could reach, a small palm slammed into the boy's forehead—

"Uaoh!"

—and drove his head straight down into the ground, knocking him out cold.

Pitifully, the boy's eyes rolled white, a groan slipping from his throat before he collapsed sideways, unconscious.

"R-Rui…"

Once again, in the blink of an eye, Rui had vanished from one spot and appeared in another. The short-range warp used by "Gluttony" — the same thing she'd shown time and again at the sand tower. Watching her wield it so fluently, Subaru felt his chest tighten painfully.

The fact that that power remained meant she truly was Rui — Rui Arneb.

The girl from that white world who had torn at Subaru's heart and tried to steal his soul completely away. The one who had mistaken Return by Death for a Blessing, and called Subaru a monster.

"We need to locate Olbart posthaste."

With no regard for either the life-threatening exchange just now, or the fact that his own life had just been saved, Abel rose slowly to his feet and muttered this.

*—Find Olbart.*

Subaru was, of course, in agreement with that policy itself. It was just that he had no idea why Abel felt the need to restate it now, of all times — right after being told this bewildering business about Tanza by the boy, no less.

"Al-chin, can you stand?"

"…Ah, yeah."

Having sheathed her weapon, Medium extended a hand to the crouching Al. Al, his face hidden behind his mask, answered in a voice so heavily subdued that one could easily imagine — even without seeing his expression — that he'd gone pale.

Leaving his worry for Al aside for the moment, Subaru finally brushed the dirt off his backside and stood. Then he made his way over to Abel.

"What did you mean, just now?"

"To what question are you referring?"

"That! ...The whole thing about that girl Tanza being the mastermind, but then wanting to go find Olbart-san anyway, all that stuff! You know what I mean!"

"Hmph."

Snorting lightly, Abel touched the cheek of his oni mask, as if in mockery. Between the impatience and the irritation already gnawing at him, the gesture grated badly on Subaru's nerves. So, in a fit of pique, Subaru leapt up and forcibly tore the oni mask right off Abel's face.

And so, with his face bared for the first time in a while, Abel looked down at Subaru with open displeasure.

"What do you think you're doing?"

"That's what I want to say to you! Stop keeping everything figured out to yourself and actually explain it! We're comrades, aren't we?!"

"—Comrades, you say?"

"Ugh…"

Glared at head-on by that bare face, Subaru's momentum abruptly deflated. He'd blurted out "comrades" to Abel's face, but the word might well have angered him. Subaru himself, for that matter, felt a twinge of guilt using the word "comrade" to describe Abel.

He reflected, with some regret, that he'd let the word slip out in the heat of the moment. But admitting that regret now would sound too much like an apology, so he couldn't bring himself to say it.

"That's right, Abel-chin. We're comrades, so you've gotta tell us."

Speaking up in Subaru's stead was Medium, who had just helped Al to his feet. Hands on her hips, she protested to Abel with the whole of her small frame. Receiving her forthright words, Abel narrowed his black eyes.

"The assailants at the inn, and this lot as well — every one of them is of the horned races."

"Horned… people with horns, you mean. What about it?"

"…The horned races carry a history of persecution. Their horns bear resemblance to those of Witchbeasts — a past for which they were reviled."

"Eh…"

At Abel's matter-of-fact recounting, Subaru's eyes went round. Horned, and persecuted for that reason — such prejudice was something painfully close to home for Subaru, which was exactly why he couldn't stand it.

Emilia, who bore a similar history of persecution — the resemblance between her physical features and origins and those of the reviled Witch had subjected her to no end of unkind treatment from countless people.

And now, it seemed, the demi-humans grouped together as the horned races carried a similar past of their own.

"But then why would people like that come after us? I get that they were feared because of their horns, but I don't get this."

"Even shrunk into a child, use what wits you have. To those with a history of persecution for bearing horns, this city — the way Chaosflame exists — is a form of salvation."

"Salvation… you mean, like, it's a good place for them?"

"—Yorna Mishigure never once lets go of anything she takes into her bosom. In the past, she even raised a rebellion for the sake of a single dead Deer Human girl."

Arms crossed, the emperor against whom that rebellion had been raised laid out the hidden circumstances. Yorna, said to have risen in rebellion against her own sovereign more than once in the past — the reason she'd bared her fangs at the emperor she served — had been for the sake of just one single person.

Hearing that, what Subaru thought was—

"Then she's a good person, isn't she…!"

"Fool. Do not lump things together so glibly. Yorna Mishigure favors those who love her. And toward those outside that circle, she is a woman who shows no mercy whatsoever."

"Eh~, but I think it's only natural to be kind to people you like!"

"I think so too."

Seconding Medium's and Subaru's view, Rui raised her hand with an "Awh!" Casting a cold glance over the lot of them, Abel exhaled softly.

"What matters is the fact that the horned races, who bore a history of persecution, found peace in this city. And maintaining that peace is inseparable from Yorna Mishigure's continued presence. In other words—"

"In other words?"

Subaru tilted his head, waiting for Abel to continue. But for a moment, Abel glanced toward Subaru tilting his head like that, and paused, letting the words hang. Subaru had no idea what that pause meant, but after blinking a few times, Abel went on.

"In other words, to the horned races, our presence — seeking to drag Yorna Mishigure into a full-scale rebellion against the capital — is an intolerable enemy, one that threatens to shake the very ground they've worked to pack firm beneath their feet."

"Ah…!"

Hearing that, Subaru finally understood the true meaning behind the beastman boy's words and actions. The boy who had grabbed Subaru's arm and hurled him into the street, his face pained even as he did it — no, not just the boy, but everyone who had attacked them had worn similarly pained expressions.

That had been the look of guilt over driving Subaru's group away, mixed with anxiety over losing their own place to belong. That was why the boy had apologized even as he threw Subaru.

"So these people were desperate too, huh…"

"Sympathy, or is it pity? Either way, spare enough to indulge in it, I see. Do not forget the fact that these people turned hostile against us. Circumstances exist for every living thing that draws breath."

"Mo~, Abel-chin! Don't say it like that! Also, I still don't get it — why'd you ask them about Tanza-chan? And about looking for Grandpa, too!"

"…Judging by the boy's earlier tone, it is likely true that these people do not know Tanza's whereabouts. And for some reason, they suspect we are the ones concealing it. Why would that be?"

"Why, huh… that's… why, indeed?"

Looking down at the unconscious boy, Subaru racked his brain over Abel's question. Before Rui had knocked him out, the boy had lashed out at Abel demanding Tanza's whereabouts. That fury hadn't seemed fake to Subaru at all.

Which meant the boy and the others believed that Subaru's group had done something to Tanza.

"So that's why they attacked us?"

"That would follow the logic of it. But as a matter of fact, we have done Tanza no harm. So then — where did that girl disappear to? And furthermore, where did these hundred-odd horned people even come from?"

"Where from — outside, waiting in ambush, right?"

"On whose orders?"

Asked without a beat's pause, Subaru found himself at a loss for words. He turned his mind over, trying to build something like an answer on the pillar of Abel's question. But before he could finish constructing it—

"—That'd be 'cause the little missy told 'em ahead of time, wouldn't it."

From behind, Al swooped in and snatched the answer away. Subaru turned at the voice — Al, who until just moments ago had been unable to even speak — and called out, "Al…" to him, sensing he still wasn't quite back to normal.

"Sorry 'bout that, bro, had you worried. …Well, situation ain't exactly improved any."

"Your condition remains unrestored, then. That, too, is likely a result of Olbart's technique."

"…No argument from me there either. Wanna talk about that too, but better to finish up what we were already on. So — back to the missy, Tanza."

Waving a hand that had shrunk small, Al answered in a voice he was visibly forcing to stay level. Al's condition was worrying, but as he said, the conversation was still mid-thread. Tanza had apparently gathered people from outside beforehand.

"Um, so wait, does that mean Tanza gathered her horned friends around the inn ahead of time, planning to have them attack us? But then that would mean…"

"Where'd Tanza-chan go?"

Medium's question, picking up right where Subaru's trailed off, was the very answer neither of them could produce. Tanza — who had appeared at the inn to relay Yorna's instructions and then apparently withdrawn — her having orchestrated all this made sense enough, but the reaction of her allies outside had been strange.

At the very least, the beastman boy had genuinely believed that Subaru's group had done something to Tanza.

"Which means the missy's friends never saw her safe and sound. …No, more like, they probably never even saw her come out of the inn in the first place."

"That, too, follows logically. Which is why, out of consideration for the possibility that Tanza might still be inside talking, they refrained from resorting to force until we had left the inn."

"But then! If that's true, where did Tanza-chan—"

Her head pounded by confusion to the point of looking dizzy, Medium cried out like it was a scream. But laid out that carefully, step by step, even Subaru could now follow Abel's reasoning. Probably, this was what Abel was thinking. Which was—

"If that girl — if Tanza never left the inn, then she's hiding somewhere. And Olbart-san is in on it with her, isn't he?!"

△▼△▼△▼△

*—The horned races who attacked Subaru's group in order to reclaim Tanza.*

But given that they'd been lying in wait outside the inn from the very start, it could only mean arrangements had been made beforehand for the possibility that something happened to Tanza. And within that inn, the only one who could plausibly have coordinated with Tanza was—

"—Olbart Dunkelkenn. No one else."

"…Could be the inn staff conspired to hide her, too, y'know?"

"Naturally, I do not close my eyes to that possibility either. But I am certain."

"Certain?"

At Al's questioning tone, Abel nodded deeply. His reason for being certain that Olbart and Tanza had joined hands — that was because—

"He went out of his way to propose this childish game of hide-and-seek. Precisely the sort of vicious little diversion that old man would relish."

"Like, 'find me if you can'?"

"Just so."

At Abel's affirmation, chin tucked, Subaru couldn't help making a sour face. Abel's inference, grounded in Olbart's rotten personality, had hit the mark — and the fact that Subaru, too, could intuitively confirm this was exactly why his face soured. The very proposal of "hide-and-seek" had itself been a metaphor for having hidden Tanza.

"But if that's what got us attacked, isn't that against the rules?!"

"You mean the agreement not to harm one another? That man would surely wriggle out of it, claiming 'I laid no hand on you myself.' All the more so if Tanza is in league with him."

"Ngh…! That's such a sneaky way of thinking...!"

"Which is why locating Olbart takes priority. Do so, and Tanza's whereabouts will likely come to light at the same time. Though, once our scheme is exposed, the reason to persist with this 'hide-and-seek' may vanish as well."

Subaru understood now that Olbart and Tanza might be together. But how that would also settle the matter of the "hide-and-seek" entirely, he didn't quite follow. Furrowing his brow, scowling over it — Abel, turning, roughly snatched the oni mask back from Subaru's hand.

Before a startled "Ah" from Subaru, Abel put the mask back on.

"Olbart's aim is to gauge whether we are worth negotiating with. To him, we are surely the true prize, more than this childish game. Unravel that intent, then, and there is no need to trouble ourselves following his roundabout procedures."

"Ah, I think I get it… kind of."

"…Has your capacity for reason truly degraded this far?"

At Subaru barely keeping pace with the explanation, Abel murmured this with something of a sigh. Subaru couldn't argue with the jab. He was well aware his own mental state had clearly gone askew — he just couldn't gauge to what degree. His thoughts came out clumsy, words wouldn't form properly; he supposed his head really was getting worse.

"Wasn't all that sharp to begin with, either…"

"In your case, the problem is that even your comprehension and imagination have declined. The details of Olbart's technique remain unclear, but by one account, the body is said to reflect the rise and fall of its own Od."

"O-Od's rise and fall…?"

"It means there is a correlation between the growth and decline of the Od and the body. In other words, it stands to reason that Olbart interfered with your — no, with all of your Ods."

Abel swept his gaze over the three of them — Subaru, Al, and Medium, all shrunken in body. At the words, nothing clicked for Subaru. Same as when his Gate for using magic had broken — talk of Mana or Od, things he'd never consciously registered the existence of to begin with, simply didn't feel real to him.

Though that might have just been true for Subaru—

"—So that means it's that old geezer messing with our bodies that caused this."

Al's voice, muttering this with such venomous hatred, snapped Subaru to attention. Looking over, he saw Al touching his own forehead beneath the mask, fingers trembling. The uncontrolled irritation he'd shown time and again was proof that he, too, was starting to feel the mental toll. The composure he'd had as an adult was slipping away now that he'd become a child.

"That damn old geezer, I'm gonna wring his neck for sure…!"

"To that end as well, we shall need to determine the whereabouts of this so-called 'Abyss with a fine view.'"

"Yeah… speaking of which, we were on our way to the tavern."

Glancing sideways at Al, whose anger simmered and built, Subaru cut himself off there. That, after all, had been the original purpose behind leaving Taritta as decoy and splitting off, before they'd been held up in the street. Except the reasoning behind it had never actually been explained — why Abel had made rushing to the tavern their objective.

"The basics of any search come down to numbers. Ideally, one with familiarity of the land would be preferable. But every resident of this city is a potential Tanza sympathizer. Hence, it is more appropriate to make use of outsiders."

"And that's why you're after a tavern where outsiders would gather. Just the kind of thing you'd think up, bro."

"Eh, that's not—"

He started to say "not true," but Subaru caught himself remembering how he'd once searched for bodyguards in Guaral. It was back when he was being hunted by Todd, a man who made him shudder just thinking about it. Among all the schemes he'd cooked up to survive, one had been hiring a strong escort — and for that, he'd headed to a tavern. Unfortunately, the drunk swordsman he'd hired that time had been taken down almost immediately.

"But don't we need money to get people to help us? Where would we even get that kind of—"

"That concern is unnecessary. Payment has already been arranged."

So saying, Abel gave a shake to the bag slung over his shoulder. It was the one he'd deemed necessary to bring from the inn — something Subaru had been curious about the contents of. Catching Subaru's gaze on it, Abel, without opening the bag's mouth, said,

"Upon departing Guaral, I had Zikr open the municipal storehouse. It is the most efficient method of securing cooperators. There is no reason not to use it."

"…Not one to leave anything to chance."

"But wandering around with a big pile of money while wearing an oni mask seems like it'd draw a lot of dangerous attention— ah, right."

Midway through speaking, Subaru was made to realize his misplaced worry when Abel tapped a finger against the oni mask. That mask carried the effect of "Recognition Obstruction," so the very notion of "drawing attention" was mistaken to begin with. Since it had no effect on allies like Subaru, the gap between its true nature and how everyone around them reacted was deeply disorienting.

"So, what we're doing doesn't change? Everyone heads to the tavern, and we look for people willing to help find Grandpa?"

"—That was the initial plan. But circumstances have shifted somewhat."

"Shifted how? Where?"

"If Olbart and Tanza orchestrated this from the start, they would just as readily arrive at the notion of increasing their manpower. Olbart alone holds no sway over the magical capital, but if Tanza has joined him, that changes matters. That girl holds the position of Yorna Mishigure's attendant."

Yorna, who held the highest standing in Chaosflame — being her attendant made Tanza something like a secretary. In other words, Tanza ranked second in authority in this city, just below Yorna herself. Or at least, she could act with that kind of authority.

"If they're seriously trying to keep the lid on us, they'd post people 'round the tavern too, wouldn't they."

"There is no need to walk into so transparent a trap. Though it seems there is also a means of forcing our way through regardless."

In the midst of this conversation, forced as it now was to shift course, Abel's gaze turned aside as he let that comment drop. Following his line of sight, Subaru felt his cheeks stiffen.

"Uaoh?"

There, tilting her head as she received his gaze through the oni mask, stood Rui. Having turned the tables on her attackers and, in the end, mercilessly knocked out even the beastman boy, Rui now stood beside the Medium she was so attached to, absentmindedly combing her fingers through her own golden hair. In stark contrast to that overwhelming combat prowess, her innocent demeanor showed not the slightest change.

And that, if anything, only sent a colder chill down Subaru's spine.

"Ah, Rui-chan was amazing just now! I can't believe she was hiding something like that~"

"Awh-awh."

"Ehehe, thanks for helping us."

Oblivious to Subaru's state of mind, Medium petted Rui's head without a shred of caution. Rui, for her part, accepted the palm on her head calmly enough, but Subaru's heart was pounding wildly. So much so that he reflexively blurted "Wait!" and grabbed Medium's wrist.

"Wah, Subaru-chin?"

"That, um, Medium-san, you should probably stay away from her—"

"—? Why? I mean, she saved us, didn't she? Saved Subaru-chin too."

"That's, well, maybe true, but… still…"

To Medium's innocent question, Subaru couldn't find the right words to answer. In truth, her view of things was honest and correct. Rui had saved Subaru. Without hesitating even at the risk of her own harm, she'd used the whole of her small body to protect him. Subaru himself had desperately pleaded, "Don't die," hadn't he.

"Think it's about time you told us what the hell's going on."

Seeing Subaru falter, Al said this in a low voice. At the dark look through the mask, Subaru caught his breath and looked down. He recalled — a few days ago, on the journey to Chaosflame, he'd concealed the truth about Rui from Al. Even then, Subaru had been unable to decide how to handle Rui, and had put off the answer.

But—

"This ain't something we can just laugh off and let slide anymore. Not just my problem — everybody's."

"—————"

"What are you hiding about that girl?"

With the pressure of their gazes concentrated on him, Subaru reflexively moved to shield Rui behind him. It wasn't that he was trying to protect Rui — he simply didn't know how she'd react to being on the receiving end of those stares. No — even that, Subaru himself couldn't say for certain was the truth.

If there was one thing he could say, it was that he could no longer dodge the question. And with his mind grown young and clumsy, he couldn't concoct a convincing lie either.

So—

"This girl, Rui, is… an Archbishop of Sin. Of 'Gluttony.'"

That was all he could do: lay bare the plain truth.

△▼△▼△▼△

*—The true identity of Rui Arneb.*

It was a secret Subaru had kept hidden from everyone he'd met since being flung into the Vollachian Empire — Rem, who had lost her memories, included — ever since that day. If Rui's identity as an Archbishop of Sin came to light, all manner of trouble could follow, both within Subaru and without. No — trouble beyond even what he could anticipate was to be expected.

That was why Subaru had, until this very moment, kept that fact concealed—

"—An Archbishop… of Sin."

A halting voice repeated back the word Subaru had just revealed. If Al and Abel had reacted with shock at the fact, that would only be natural. To grow wary, to press for confirmation, to raise their voices at how absurd it sounded — all of that would be natural.

And yet, the first voice to tremble like that belonged to neither Al nor Abel.

"Rui-chan, an Archbishop of Sin…"

"Uu—?"

Yes — the one who let out that stunned voice was none other than Medium herself. Her blue eyes wide, Medium stared fixedly at Rui. Under that gaze, Rui herself tilted her head with a blank, clueless sound, as if she had no idea what this was about.

And that was exactly the reaction Subaru had feared most.

He'd held out some optimistic hope that perhaps Medium — no, the O'Connell siblings — would take it in stride, laugh it off as no big deal. But he was made to realize now that this had been nothing but wishful, dreamy idealism, optimism and all.

"—————"

That was unmistakable, undeniable *fear* that flickered across Medium's eyes.

"Bro, that ain't a joke I can laugh at."

"I— it's not a joke…"

"If it ain't a joke, that's even less funny!"

Shocked by Medium's reaction, Subaru couldn't manage a proper response to Al's words that followed either. Raising his voice, Al roughly yanked the dao blade slung on his back free. Of course, a child's slender arm alone couldn't hold it up, so the drawn blade ended up burying its tip in the ground. Even so, it could still be used well enough if swung with body weight behind it. With that kind of sharp resolve behind his eyes, Al's gaze turned toward Subaru, and toward Rui behind him.

"Draggin' an Archbishop of Sin along ain't something a sane person does. Don't tell me you forgot what happened in Priestella."

"Th-that's…"

"Me and Priscilla — the princess — we didn't get hurt. But that was just luck. Bro's own people, people we know, got hurt bad. And she's part of the reason why."

Leaning against the dao stuck in the ground, Al's voice flared with anger Subaru couldn't argue back against. Al was entirely correct. By anyone's reckoning, it was Subaru who was in the wrong here. He shouldn't have kept Rui by his side, shouldn't have let her roam free. He should have revealed her identity, bound her up, stripped her of her freedom, and kept her captive.

But Subaru hadn't done that. Worse still—

"A-Abel, you too…?"

Wondering if he agreed, Subaru turned a pleading look toward Abel's silence. With Medium frightened and Al openly furious, Abel's stance had become the last bastion — though for whom that bastion stood, even Subaru couldn't say.

Still, if everyone present chose to turn against Rui—

"—————"

—then surely Subaru, too, could finally put an end to this hesitation, and act.

"I know not how they are handled in other nations."

Before Subaru, holding his breath, the masked man with the bag slung over his shoulder met his gaze head-on. Black eyes meeting black eyes, that cold stare seemed to carve into Subaru's chest. As if to reject whatever words might follow — the words he'd braced himself for — his brain went numb from the edges inward.

Into that numbed mind, the voice slowly seeped in. It was—

"In this empire, those who serve the 'Witch' are executed, whatever the reason."

A resolute declaration from the one who stood at the pinnacle of this empire, even having been driven from his throne. A death sentence, stated with unmistakable clarity that there could be no reconciliation.

The moment he heard it, Subaru shut his eyes tight—

"—! Rui!!"

"Subaru-chin?!"

The instant he cried out her name on impulse, small hands wrapped around Subaru's waist. An instant later, Medium's voice rang out like a scream, and Subaru's feet left the ground, lifting into the air — no, it was Rui, clinging to his waist, who had leapt high into the air, taking Subaru with her.

Why he'd called Rui's name, what meaning he'd meant to put into it — clenching his back teeth, holding back tears that welled up for some reason, Subaru himself had no idea at all.

He'd simply thought—

"Uaoh"

—that, at the girl clinging to his waist, growling low in her throat, he mustn't abandon her now.

It wasn't that he was returning a life for a life saved. It wasn't that he'd mistaken some welling affection for an obligation to protect her. He'd simply thought it.

If, with his limbs and even the very contents of his head shrunken down like this, he settled an answer about this girl whose thoughts he couldn't fathom — he would regret it.

This had nothing to do with Rem, nor with the many people he'd met until now — it was Subaru's own problem. A problem Natsuki Subaru had to face with everything he had.

"Bro! Get back here! Damn it all—!"

Al shouted up at him, but Rui's feet, kicking off the wall to leap, didn't stop. Clinging to Subaru's back, Rui bounded from wall to wall of the buildings lining the street, up onto the rooftops, and effortlessly moved on from one street to the next, and the next after that. In Chaosflame, with its footholds scattered every which way and its patchwork of makeshift paths, Rui's untamable wildness was utterly in her element.

"Buhah!"

After several more bounds, he was finally set down, and Subaru pressed his hands and knees to the floor, breathing hard again and again. Fighting gravity over and over had been part of it, but being clamped around the torso by Rui's slender arms, vice-like, had been agony too. Rui herself peered at Subaru's face from the side, looking maddeningly unaffected.

But this wasn't the time to just be annoyed at that face.

"Damn it, Abel and the others…"

This was the result of his own actions. To call it getting "separated" would be far too disingenuous. But if he'd stayed there, there was no telling what order Abel might have given. Judging by Al's reaction, he wouldn't have let Rui off easily either. And Medium's power to smooth things over couldn't be counted on here.

Even so, going along with the flow and handing Rui over — his heart rebelled at that.

"Uaoh?"

Wiping his mouth with his sleeve, Subaru looked up to find Rui's blue eyes watching him with carefree obliviousness. Her utter indifference to the hostility and fear that had been directed at her made Subaru, tying himself in knots over all this, feel like a fool.

"Am I an idiot? No — I am an idiot."

He really was, in fact, quite the idiot. Whatever the case with Abel, running off with Rui and leaving Medium and Al behind was ridiculous. With someone whose thoughts, whose next move, he couldn't even begin to fathom.

"Even so, if I decide something right now, I'll regret it. This isn't something a kid should get to decide."

This was a matter of life and death — of the empire's very future, even. It felt wrong, somehow — no, it was simply *wrong* — for a mere ten-year-old child to decide something so weighty, exposed to the cold, unforgiving eyes of adults.

"If it were the real me, I'm sure I could judge this properly. So..."

He needed to undo this "Infantilization" as soon as possible, and make his way back from Boy Natsuki Subaru to Young Man Natsuki Subaru. Once he did, he'd surely be able to agonize properly over Rui, to give his companions' judgment a proper answer, and arrive at a conclusion he could actually accept.

To that end—

"—I'm going to find Olbart-san. Without relying on Abel and the others. With everything I've got."

"Ah, uu!"

Responding to Subaru, who'd looked up with sharp resolve, Rui raised both hands with an eager expression. Looking down at the scene of the magical capital from their high perch, the two of them — small children, by any outside measure — geared themselves up, declaring their lofty ambition to track down the head of the Shinobi, having made enemies of both the residents of Chaosflame and the emperor driven from his throne.

*—Without quite meaning to, Subaru and the others had become both the "it" of this game of hide-and-seek, and the ones being chased in a game of tag.*
