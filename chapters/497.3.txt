Scorpion Tale Chapter 2: "Memories of Blondie"

1

In the end, the drudgery Shaula had declared to be the last of it did not end with that.

Even after they climbed to the fourth floor, "Alcyone," she was hauled out again under the banner of "cooperating in the creation of an environment suited to caring for a pregnant woman," and Shaula was worked like a convenient piece of labor.

The sleeping space that was finally completed for Leilani turned out so fine that it made Shaula — who for several hundred years had lain on what the woman called a cold, hard floor — a pitiable figure by comparison.

"This is just sad for me! Why does some johnny-come-lately like you get this bed while I gotta sleep splat on a rock-hard floor?!"

"Oh my, Shaula-sama, how grievous that is... That the very personage who has shown such kindness to one as unsightly and unsuccessful as myself should lie upon a bare floor — no, never, never. In that case..."

"In that case?"

"Unworthy though I am, this powerless self shall exert her strength to repay your kindness— ugh, my belly..."

"Yeah, yeah, saw that comin'! Stop it, stop it! If somethin' happens to the kid in that belly, everythin' I've done up to now goes down the drain! A hard floor ain't gonna break my hard-boiled soul, so forget it — I was just sayin' it out loud."

At Leilani's failed attempt at repayment — splendid in enthusiasm alone — Shaula had nothing left to say.

She grabbed the collar of the woman crumpling to the ground clutching her belly, sat her down on the bedding piled together out of blankets, and jabbed a finger sharply at her face.

"I know I've said it a buncha times already so it's got zero weight comin' from me, but this time it really is the end. If it weren't for that belly of yours, you'd be my sworn enemy... No more cozyin' up, or rather, no more of this sweatshop setup where I'm the only one gettin' squeezed dry!"

"Such a thing — to say I have exploited Shaula-sama... My deepest apologies. This unpleasant and unperceptive self has done nothing but presume upon your mercy, without once considering your heart. And so I have driven Shaula-sama to such a state..."

"That ain't anythin' new! You've always been like that!"

Perhaps it was the hormonal balance of pregnancy, but Leilani was unusually meek. No matter how contrite she acted, though, it did not change the past in which Shaula had been put through hell. If anything, with all the stress of not being able to get back at her fairly, it felt more like being slowly tortured now.

"Grrr, always, always makin' a chump outta me... 's what I say."

"I loathe, loathe beyond all bearing, this body of mine that heaps ingratitude upon immorality... Incidentally, if I may — is there no one here besides Shaula-sama?"

"That's right. I'm the only one here. Well, it'd be a problem if people thought just anybody could handle a big job like Star Keeper, so it's only natural, natural as can be."

"Is that so. ...Then how lonely that must be."

"Haaaaah?"

Shaula growled in a thuggish voice at Leilani, who had been idly fiddling with her hands atop the piled blankets. And having growled, she felt sour, because it struck her as an imitation of the "Leilani" she had hated. Now that she thought back on it, that woman too had often threatened people exactly like this.

"Gettin' scared when it's turned back on you — did somethin' like cuteness sprout on you? Honestly, it just feels like plus points on the peeving scale."

"No, a narrow-minded self such as I would never harbor so impertinent an aim... Only, my apologies. My indiscreet and unpleasant manner of speech has offended your esteemed mood."

"Ain't anythin' new. Though I did think it was super transparent!"

Shaula remained in the tower on "his" orders, but there had been companions there too, beginning with Mama — and "Leilani" was supposed to have been one of them. And yet to have someone say, as though it were another's affair, something like *you must have been lonely* — it got right under her skin.

The sense of mission to keep "his" orders burned fiercely inside Shaula. To have that doused with sentimental drivel about loneliness was truly hateful.

"Anyway, whether or not I'm actually lonely is beside the point."

"...Then, was your esteemed self not lonely?"

"Like! I! Said! I don't wanna talk about it! And what about you, huh? Your buddies are wiped out, and once things settle you'll be headin' home empty-handed — so what'd you come all the way to the tower for anyway, without even bringin' Master along?"

The *without bringing him* part was the greatest flaw in Shaula's book, but there was no doubt at all that Leilani had set out for this tower in the first place.

"I mean, in this big wide sea of sand there ain't a single thing besides this tower."

A sand-colored world where nothing could be found as far as the eye could see — that was the space that had enveloped Shaula for several hundred years, and the place she would go on passing time in while she waited for "him" to return. She was a little curious about what "Leilani," who had come back on a whim, had come back *for*.

Maybe, just maybe—

"—Was it Master?"

Maybe he had sent "Leilani" here to bring her back.

Of course, even if "Leilani" had said so, Shaula would not have obeyed. Shaula would abide to the letter by the words of "him" who had told her to wait here. —Only, in the fact that "he" was thinking of her, she would be able to feel love.

With that, she felt, the several hundred years behind her and the several hundred ahead would both be repaid.

However—

"—My apologies. An inept self such as I has no reply on hand that would answer your esteemed expectations."

"—"

At the answer of Leilani, who bowed her head deeply as she said it, Shaula's breath caught briefly.

Well, of course. Nothing was ever that convenient. Things never went the way Shaula hoped. She had known that perfectly well.

And since she had known it perfectly well, there was no reason for it to hurt.

"Aw man, got my hopes up for a huge loss. I'm deeply disappointed in ya."

Shaking her head slowly, Shaula kicked away a pebble lying on the stone floor.

Strange. Why would pebbles be lying around on the floor of the fourth level, at such a height? It was hardly a height the wind could carry them to. Who had brought these things here, and how?

Her nerves were frayed enough that trivial things like that snagged at her.

"Get your own meals ready. My nursemaidin' ends here."

Even setting that aside, Leilani could hardly have stomached the food Shaula prepared. The only thing that could be made food of here were the witchbeasts that dwelt in the sea of sand, and every last witchbeast tasted like a rotting ditch.

Better for Leilani to feed the baby in her belly with her own provisions.

"Don't go wanderin' around on your own. Out here, I'm the Star Keeper, and I'm the rules."

Strictly speaking, "he," the one who had imposed those rules on Shaula, was the rules themselves — but she had no intention of telling Leilani that. She didn't want to.

Giving someone what they wanted, when they wouldn't give Shaula what *she* wanted? No way.

2

"—What is it, Shaula. Making such a covetous face. There, there — I shall share my steamed potato with thee as well."

Laughing as he said it, the figure with golden hair and red eyes broke the steamed potato in his hands in two. Then, comparing the halves, he offered the bigger one to Shaula with a "Mm."

A slack, tension-free grin, Shaula thought. And having thought it, she accepted the potato while cocking her head at the thinking of the one who had handed over the larger half — Blondie.

"I don't get how Falsale's head works, not one bit. Why'd you hand me the big one? Is this one of those life hacks about suckin' up to the strong to stay alive?"

"Talk sense. If what thou sayest were true, the one I ought to be offering potatoes to would be Reid, or else Volcanica. And with that fellow half a potato would never suffice — he would insist I hand over the whole thing, beyond question. Dost thou not think so?"

"I think so. Those guys are seriously bankrupt as human beings."

The instant she called the two in question to mind, Shaula's lips twisted magnificently. Seeing that reaction, Blondie laughed as though vindicated and patted the spot right beside him. With no reason to refuse the invitation to sit, Shaula plopped herself down.

Swinging her legs idly over the cliff's edge where she sat, Shaula bit into the still-hot potato. For a while, a wordless stretch of potato-chewing continued between Shaula and Blondie.

"—"

A great, great red sunset was sinking behind the mountains across the way. A little longer and night would come, and that darkness would bring with it a dazzling starry sky. To Shaula, whose eyes worked fine in the dark, the blackness of night was nothing at all. And so she simply waited, impatient for the night that would bring the stars.

"Shaula, art thou not cold?"

Suddenly, as Shaula licked her fingers clean of potato, Blondie — who had finished his own — asked her that. Shoulders, belly, and legs all bared, Shaula snorted at the question.

"Cold~? Never felt it. Ain't that just a shared hallucination of the weak-kneed?"

"The cold we feel is a phantom! That is an interesting way of thinking. But phantom or not, a chill wind feels cold. Come closer, for my sake."

"Well, if ya insist~"

At Blondie, who said it while opening his white cloak, Shaula shrugged, then shifted her rear half a cheek's width, and half a cheek more, until she was near enough for him to put an arm around her shoulders. Taking her weight as she settled snugly inside the cloak, Blondie nodded, "Mm."

"Human skin is warm after all. Thou hast body heat too. Dost thou truly not feel cold?"

"Tch, tch, tch. Every element that leads to weakness has been stripped clean out — a Star Keeper machiiine with all its points dumped into functions for protectin' Master, that's me. I'd appreciate it if ya didn't try to fit Mama's special-tuned spec into wimpy common sense like that."

"Mm, is that so. Then it was I who was shallow. However..."

"—? What is it?"

"I do not hold that drawing close to another is a condition of weakness. I have my thoughts about those values of thine that deem it so — and about the 'Witch'-dono who set such a mindset in thee."

As he spoke, as though to reinforce his argument, Blondie drew Shaula's body deeper against him. She did not bother to shake him off, and it was also a fact that Mama's humanity had problems — but there was something about Blondie's opinion that she couldn't honestly nod along with.

"Whatever Falsale says, the ranking where Master comes first inside me ain't gonna budge. Any element that might disturb that order is — for me — Evil, Instant, BAN. Besides, ain't Master's companions all people with busted humanity?"

"That is a question difficult to answer in a double sense! ...Truly, a difficult question."

The first half was like the usual Blondie; the second half, unlike him.

It was a subtle difference in nuance, and Shaula could not catch the fine shades of feeling packed into it. Not wanting to catch them was simply how Shaula thought. This too, as Mama would put it, was a necessary function to keep it from becoming weakness.

"How do I put it — you humans, every single one of ya, every last one, seem like such a hassle."

"...I cannot deny it, but is that all right? Thy beloved Master is human as well."

"Master's literally special, so he can't be put on the same line as other humans to begin with. So, no probs. No prollems."

"'No prollems' — that one I do not yet know... I must ask later what it means. Still, thou art amazing, able to declare him special so plainly. It is powerful."

Smiling wryly, Blondie stroked her head. She hated having her hard-won scorpion tail mussed, but the profile she looked up at was so crestfallen that she let him be.

"Thou art strong."

Still with that crestfallen face, Blondie murmured it as he went on stroking Shaula's head.

Even Shaula understood that the nuance packed into that murmur wasn't simple envy of her. And that the wetness in Blondie's red eyes was not a trick of her sight.

What Blondie envied was probably not Shaula's strength. —It was holding someone as special, and saying so openly. *That* was what was being envied.

"...Humans sure are a hassle."

Why Blondie should envy her, Shaula did not understand in any detail.

Blondie was what they called royalty, someone who by rights ought to be in a castle. Idiotically cheerful, afraid of nothing, and forever getting tangled up in trouble. In terms of strength he was hopeless, and he left the solving of the trouble he caused to others — in the borrowing of other people's strength, perhaps he had something in common with "him." Though "he" was a hundred times cooler.

Still, when Shaula wanted to brag about "him" to someone and couldn't say it to "him" himself, Blondie was a precious listener. Reacting to every story freshly, with big, showy reactions, Blondie was truly the ideal audience for boasting.

Frankly put, this Blondie, bodies pressed close and warmth passing between them, was Shaula's—

"—It is night."

At the voice that seemed whispered right by her ear, Shaula belatedly learned that night had arrived.

Realizing that she had been so absorbed in Blondie's manner that she'd missed the coming of the night she had been so impatient for, Shaula felt somehow unsettled.

Points of white light, large and small, strewn across the curtain of night — twinkling there as though it were the most natural thing in the world. She had heard Mama pedantically explain that each was a colossal, mighty clump of Mana beyond human comprehension. But Shaula had a different view of starlight.

This too was borrowed wisdom, taught by "him," and for precisely that reason an absolute truth within Shaula.

"—Stars're supposed to be the crystals of a whole lotta wishes."

"Shaula?"

She could tell Blondie's eyes had gone round at her suddenly launching into that. But Shaula did not look up at the face so near her own. She only kept watching the starry sky and went on.

"Every time somebody looks up at the sky, all sorts of folks entrust their wishes to it. Those pile up and up and turn into those big shiny stars. And once in a while, those shiny stars grant somebody's wish."

"...Somebody's wish, is it. And by what conditions is that somebody chosen?"

"Totally random!"

"What! At random! Not the one who strove hardest?!"

"Stars ain't got free time either, so they ain't watchin' the fine print of who tried hardest or wished most desperately. And after goin' to all the trouble of grantin' a wish, havin' people grumble later about playin' favorites'd be a drag, right? So — random. A straight-up fair fight."

At Shaula clenching a fist as she made her case, Blondie's shoulders shook with a "heh heh." It seemed his mind had been taken off things a little. And with it taken off, Shaula would add this, too.

"And on top of bein' Star Keeper, I'm the Scorpio woman who bears a star's name. It's no exaggeration to say I've been given pretty much the same role as a star, so — advice, from me."

"Advice, sayest thou? For having a star grant one's wish, I presume? But is that not strange? Thou didst say the stars choose wishes at random."

"But drawin' that randomness your way even a little is the life hack for gettin' fate on your side— ah, I blew it! I said 'fate'!"

While she was talking, carried away, she had gone and let fatalism slip out of her mouth. That invisible power called "fate" was something Shaula's precious "he" hated intensely.

Which was exactly why she was careful not to carelessly say woman-of-destiny sorts of things.

"Grrr, a blunder... Well then, I'll just have a star grant my wish so that slip of the tongue never happened...!"

"Wait, wait, wait! Wert thou not about to teach me that very method?! What becomes of my cozy mood?!"

"In the end, life is survival of the fittest — every way of being is a game of musical chairs..."

"Is that acceptable?! Is that truly an acceptable conclusion to all of this?!"

"Is the right to get your own wish granted really that important to ya? I'm seriously put off..."

"This happens fairly often, but that is the most innovative yanking-away of the ladder in quite some time!"

Plugging her ears against Blondie, who had gone yappy and loud as usual, Shaula felt fairly seriously that he didn't need the advice after all.

That said, being cornered by Blondie in this state and pestered endlessly would be its own problem, so with a "Good grief," Shaula disclosed her treasured secret.

The way to win the stars' favor was—

"—To shine so bright and sparkly that even the stars up in the night sky are charmed by ya."

3

"—I did say shine bright and sparkly, but still."

It didn't sit right at all. —With that feeling in her chest, Shaula sat sulking.

Across her field of view, at what they called a court banquet where crowds of humans milled about, Blondie — clad in dazzling raiment, his whole body decked out in jewelry — stood surrounded by people.

Since he was one of these "royals," it was probably natural for those around him to fawn over him. And wherever he was, Blondie was always bright and cheerful and enjoying himself, unchanged.

Even so, the sparkle Shaula had spoken of was not a matter of hanging precious metals all over yourself with a jangle — did Blondie understand that?

"If he doesn't, that's the fruit of shuttin' his brain off. Humans're thinkin' reeds... I dunno what a reed even is, but that ain't one. Not like that."

"What's this, what's this. Could it be you're in a bad mood, Shaula-kun?"

"Oh, wondered who it was — it's Beardy."

As she watched the banquet and the Blondie at its center from a distance, a bearded man with dark brown hair came over with the face of someone who'd spotted a fellow odd one out. That Beardy, cane in one hand, walked as though dragging one leg and came to stand beside her.

"There's no mistaking that I grow a beard, but I don't much enjoy being called that. I have a name — Alec. And lately I've taken to attaching Hoshin to it."

"Ah! Now that ya mention it, I heard about that! Seems like that's a name ya got from Master! What's the big idea — you wanna go, with me?"

"I don't. Even if we did, it'd be so one-sided you'd get sick of it yourself."

Smiling wryly as he said it, Beardy propped himself on his cane and lifted one leg to show her. An unfree leg, on account of some old wound or other. In short, he was probably trying to say he couldn't move as he pleased.

"If you're gonna start sayin' ya can't move properly, Mama'd be the pitiful one. Mama's not hurt anywhere at all, and she still sprains both ankles just walkin'."

"'Witch'-kun is a problem in her own way, in a different sense from me. All the more reason for 'Witch'-kun to be proud, I'd think, with you so lively and sturdy."

"Whoa there, butterin' me up like that doesn't erase the suspicion that Beardy's been sneakin' up to Master. So what came of the name business? Did ya bewitch my Master? Where? With what? How?"

"Even pressed so eagerly, all I can say is that I had a pleasant conversation with your Master."

Shifting her interest from the banquet to Beardy, Shaula pressed him hard. At Shaula glaring up at him from below, Beardy's wry smile deepened, and he shrugged easily.

"It's true the inspiration for the name came from your Master, but you can rest easy. It isn't that I borrowed a star's name. An auspicious name your Master knew... a byword for rising in the world, that's the sort of name I helped myself to."

"Risin' in the world?"

"To make your way by your own work and succeed. That was the number one reason I left my homeland in the first place, after all. Even if the world rattles a little here and there, that much I won't concede."

"Hoee~, so you're ridin' the coattails of Master's lucky charm. I totally had Beardy pegged as more of a realist and a pessimist, but you're a romanticist."

"What was I taken for, an' what am I taken for now — put like that, I can't tell what ya ever made of me a'tall."

"—"

At the odd accent dropped into the middle of their exchange, Shaula's eyes went round. As though pleased with that reaction, Beardy winked at her with a face full of mischief.

Feeling thoroughly outmaneuvered, Shaula was considering snapping that precious cane of his in half to cover her dissatisfaction, when—

"—Shaula-kun, it seems you're being summoned."

Shaula, who had been glaring fixedly at the cane, looked up with a "Fwuh?" at Beardy's call. Then, following the jut of Beardy's chin, she caught sight of Blondie slipping free of the lively cluster at the banquet and hurrying over toward them.

Blondie seized Shaula's hand with all that momentum, then glanced at Beardy beside her.

"Enough — I am at my limit. Alec, I leave the rest to thee!"

"Yes, understood. —Shaula-kun, I'm counting on you for Falsale-kun."

"Haaah? What's all this about — quit draggin' me!"

Unable to follow the flow of things, Shaula was towed along by the arm and out of the hall. Behind her, she could tell Beardy had cut smoothly into the crowd that wanted to talk to Blondie, holding them back.

Beardy struck the floor hard with his cane, drawing every eye in the place to himself.

"Ladies an' gentlemen, hold up a moment. Y'all come chasin' after him with that kinda momentum, an' even His Highness'll be run clean ragged. For now, y'd do better listenin' to what I've got to say."

And with that mysterious accent that had baffled Shaula a moment ago, he successfully stalled the lot of them.

In any case—

"Phew! At last, I may let the strength out of my shoulders."

Once they had escaped the banquet hall and reached a gazebo standing alone in a garden where the night wind blew, Blondie stretched hugely as he said it. With her arm still in his grip, Shaula was dragged along into the stretch and gave him a flat look.

"Was it really okay to duck out? That whole swarmin' crowd, every last one of 'em wanted to talk to ya, y'know?"

"Put that way, it pains my heart, but if I have no breath to catch, I shall suffocate! ...Of course, I am aware that whining at so little is nothing to be praised."

"I ain't blamin' ya. What you do is your own business."

"My own business, is it..."

The gripped hand was released, and Shaula, lowering her arm, eyed Blondie's reaction dubiously.

Blondie always wore an idiotically bright face, and at the banquet that wide-open air of his had been cranked to full. And yet now, before Shaula, Blondie only let the corners of his eyes soften a little and his lips loosen, and looked considerably less idiotic.

"When ya do that, ya don't look so dumb."

"Is that so... Hm? Dumb?! Didst thou just call me dumb?!"

"I didn't. I said that unlike always, right now ya don't look it."

"Which is to say thou dost always think me dumb, is it not?! Is it not?!"

"Never mind always — you just went right back to dumb!"

At Shaula's opinion, offered without any particular malice, Blondie raked both hands through his hair and went on groaning "Ugh" and "Aah" for a while, then promptly nodded, "Mm."

"Very well. Such is the bond between thee and me. That shall not be added to the disrespect count."

"By the way, what happens when the disrespect count goes up?"

"Naturally, one of my companions grows angry on my behalf. Should the count fill up when it happens to be Reid's turn to be angry, it would become an outrageous affair."

Blondie folded his arms and nodded to himself, but by Shaula's gut sense, the one who racked up that disrespect count fastest and in the greatest quantity was also the redhead — though since Blondie looked satisfied, she didn't say so.

So she rewound the conversation a smidge.

"Did I say somethin' that snagged on ya?"

"Besides treating me as a dolt, didst thou say something else?"

"A bit before that, the dumbness disappeared, right? That's when. Though what I actually said I've already forgotten, so I can't recall it."

Leaning against the railing built into the gazebo, Shaula hopped up to perch her rear on it where it sat a little above waist height. That put her eyes about level with Blondie's.

Blondie's red eyes gazed straight into Shaula's scorpion eyes.

"...Thou seest well."

"—? If a cloud covers the sun and makes a big shadow, anybody'd notice. I figured it's about the same as that."

"Haha, thou placest me beside the sun? That raises my awe points."

Yet another different point system had surfaced, but judging by Blondie's face, this was the type of points there was no problem accumulating. The criteria for earning them were unclear, so there was no reproducibility — but since disrespect points meant getting angry on Blondie's behalf, awe points might mean praising on Blondie's behalf. In that case, she'd like to time it for "him" and rack them up aggressively.

"Shaula. Maiden granted a star's name. Thou didst once teach me — that stars have the power to grant someone's wish."

It was probably because her head was off in that direction. At Blondie's words as he broached the subject, Shaula went blank for an instant and was slow to respond.

A beat later she remembered it was from the time they had watched the starry sky together.

"Ah, I did say that. Only, the right to have a wish granted is musical chairs... and I taught ya the trick for surviving to the end, too, right."

"So it was, so it was. To shine and sparkle so that even the stars are drawn to thee... Truly a reasonable notion, and one that drew a groan of admiration even from me."

"Heh heh heh, naturally. I am Master's cute, cute little disciple, after all."

Rubbing under her nose with a finger, Shaula accepted Blondie's praise openly. His lips loosened at her reaction — but then Blondie drew that smile smoothly back in.

"Then for those who stood in that hall just now, I and thy Master and the rest must be stars not in the heavens, but upon the earth."

"—. Stars... on the earth?"

"Indeed. Stars upon the earth. To claim such a thing for oneself is rather too flattering to say aloud!"

Blondie raised his voice and laughed heartily. But to Shaula, it felt as though Blondie were raising his voice in order to sweep away a mist right in front of his face.

The something that flickered behind Blondie's idiocy — its true form was beyond Shaula. Beyond her, but Blondie had spoken just now not only of himself, but of a matter that concerned "him," too.

Stars not in the sky, but on the earth. —If a star was a thing that granted someone's wish.

"Falsale, did ya get sick of it?"

"—"

At Shaula's question, the Blondie before her was caught off guard, his eyes going perfectly round.

It was the bare, idiocy-on-full-display expression Shaula knew.

"Hahaha! Be at ease, Shaula. Never shall I find this duty a burden and wish to flee from it. I consider this duty my calling!"

"For real? But then why'd ya haul me out here? Your actions and your words contradict... This taste — this is the taste of a liar!"

"For what reason didst thou just lick the back of my hand?!"

Shaula's arm shot out, took his hand, and licked the back of it, sending Blondie into a flustered panic. As she kept eyeing him doubtfully, Blondie shrugged with a "good grief."

"Listen well, Shaula. Thou art fond of appa, as I recall."

"Well, yeah. 'Cause Master likes 'em."

"But if thou wert made to eat ten thousand appa, a hundred thousand, a million, then even a thing thou lovest might come to have no taste at all."

"Ya think? With Master, I figure I could eat a hundred million appa and they'd all be fresh and delicious, and I'd be grinnin' the whole time."

"Mm, is that so. Then I underestimated the depth of the affection between thee and thy master... No — if it is a difference in the size of love, then it is I who still have far to go..."

"Heey~, can ya come back to the topic~?"

A hand waved lightly before his face, and Blondie snapped to with a "No, no, this won't do."

"Hrmm, what I wish to say has gotten tangled, but here it is squarely: in order to still love appa tomorrow, we humans need to come up for air."

"Haaa~, how feeble. Needin' reasons and effort just to keep likin' what ya like — humans really do love to agonize, it's unbelievable."

"Ah. Watching thee, I cannot help but think so."

Blondie's eyes softened. Even Blondie, who normally looked like an idiot, was like this. Beardy and Mama were the same, and sometimes even "he" was, so humans were a busy lot. Which brought the redhead — a human with seemingly no worries whatsoever — under suspicion of being a monster once again.

Driving that redhead out of her head, Shaula turned to the Blondie before her.

"Can't be helped. In that case, I'll show ya how it's done."

Still seated on the railing, she propped her chin on her own thigh as she said it.

Blondie's eyes went round again, and he looked bewildered. "Thou wilt?"

"I'm the Scorpio woman who got a star's name from Master, right? So I'll teach you lot, who don't have the chops to be stars, how it's done. The way of the star."

"—"

At Shaula smiling as she pointed a raised thumb at herself, Blondie was struck speechless. But as that speechlessness slowly unwound, the face that was so like Blondie showed through it.

Which is to say, an idiotic smile — not one holding anything back.

"I see... I see! Thou wilt set the example for us! What a heartening thing to hear! Honestly, that is brilliant!"

"Ohh? Don't go carelessly throwin' brilliant light around in front of the Scorpio woman shinin' in the night sky."

"Indeed! Now that thou sayest it, that is so! Thou art truly clever."

Laughing, Blondie reached out without reserve and stroked her head, and Shaula pursed her lips. After smiling wryly at her reaction, Blondie stepped back a pace and drew something from inside his coat.

Seeing it, Shaula let out an "Ah." —It was a red, red, glossy appa.

"At the banquet I had no leisure to put anything in my mouth, so I took just the one. —Shall we not share this, thou and I?"

"...Makes no sense. Aren't ya hungry? One appa's not gonna fill your belly much."

"Nay — as thou seest, tonight I am dressed up, so I cannot carelessly let my stomach swell. Yet to leave it uneaten would be a shame. Therefore it is best if thou eatest half."

"Is that your wish on the stars? That's just..."

"That is just?"

Blondie tilted his head, and from his hand Shaula swiped the appa. And in no time at all, she split the round red fruit down the middle. Juice dripped and dirtied Shaula's hands. Licking the sweet liquid off, Shaula held out one of the halves.

"That wish is too trivial — even a star'd wanna grant it with one hand free."

"I see. Thou art truly skilled at granting wishes, just like a star!"

And so the two of them bit into the halved appa, a crisp, clean sound rising from each. It was a night when a sweet, bursting flavor filled their mouths to the brim.

4

"—Would Shaula-sama care for one as well?"

Leilani, tilting her head, held out on her palm a fruit whose skin she had wiped with her sleeve — an appa — to Shaula, who had poked her head into the stone chamber.

It must have come from the food among the dragon carriage's cargo that Shaula had recovered from the sea of sand. Fruit ought not to keep long, but at least the one held out to her retained its freshness. At that glossy red fruit, Shaula's throat made a sound in spite of her.

Memories of the past had surfaced, and they had meddled with Shaula's tongue and throat and made the sound come. For a function that had barely had a turn in several hundred years, that was opportunistic beyond all reason.

"Whatever is the matter? Was it an unpleasant offer, coming from an unworthy and impertinent self such as I?"

Faced with Shaula staring at the proffered appa without moving, Leilani grew anxious. At that attitude, so unlike "Leilani," Shaula said, "Nnnope," and took the appa.

Studying it closely, confirming with her palm the feel of the achingly nostalgic fruit—

"An appa, here of all times. That's some seriously irritating staging."

"...If it has been thought unsuitable and unpleasant—"

"It ain't like that! Besides, you bein' pushy ain't anythin' new."

Cutting off Leilani as she began another contrite line, Shaula went "Hup" and split the appa in two.

And in reenactment of the memory with "Leilani," she licked the dripping juice off with her tongue.

Then, just as she had that time, she held one half of the split appa out to her.

"—I am most glad."

Saying so, Leilani accepted it, and as Shaula watched her bite crisply into the appa, she bit into her own share and savored the sweet taste.

The freshness, the sweetness — both stimulated a sense of taste for the first time in several hundred years, and—

"...Yeah, if it's not together with Master, it doesn't get to be that taste."

"Shaula-sama?"

At the sign of her face being peered at as she muttered it, Shaula turned away from Leilani's gaze, said, "It's nothin'," and tossed the last fragment of appa into her mouth.

"Just so ya know, my heart isn't cheap enough to be bought off with one appa. My heart is Master's exclusive product to begin with! Not for you!"

"Of course, I am well aware. Your esteemed self is gentle of heart and magnanimous... It is by no means the case that you are granting special treatment to one as inept and immoral as I."

Shaking her head slowly, Leilani behaved as though thoroughly abashed — but she appended a "However" at the end of her words.

"Your kindness makes me, indiscreetly, hope. —That Shaula-sama might grant even a wish so unbefitting an unworthy self such as I."

"...You've got a seriously fine character on ya, you know that?"

Sharing an appa, in the present and in the past — she had piggybacked on that memory and gone so far as to speak of a wish.

Once, Shaula had told "Leilani" of the pride of the Scorpio woman who bears a star's name. And having gone all the way around, it was now tightening around her own throat. —Feeling that keenly, she spoke.

"Don't go gettin' the wrong idea that a swollen belly counts as shinin' bright, 'Leilani.'"

And so she drove in a warning nail against "Leilani," who was putting the star-winning method she had once taught her to such ill use.
