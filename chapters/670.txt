Arc 8 Chapter 53: "The Marksman"

Roswaal L. Mathers is the greatest mage in the kingdom, the greatest in the world.

Were you to put that question to the man himself, he would offer a conditional affirmation, mixing a fearless smile with genuine self-assurance: "In the present day, I suppose that would be so-o-o." No regret lingers on Roswaal's face when he appends that qualifier, in the present day. Instead, what settles into his mismatched eyes is something closer to yearning desolation and deep affection.

That it is a fleeting, ever-present longing within Roswaal whenever he speaks of magic — this only Ram and Beatrice know, and even he himself remains unaware of it.

With that understood, Roswaal's standing as the foremost mage of the present age remains unshaken.
He told as much to Medium O'Connell as she headed into the decisive battle, and now, soaring through the sky with her held in his arms, facing down the "Marksman" who had risen to some distant height far above — Roswaal held confidence and pride in his own magic, and still judged his odds of victory to be low.

"――――"

From Roswaal's vantage, having infiltrated the capital's airspace from the southwest, the sky directly above the Crystal Palace at the city's northernmost point burned crimson, blossoming again and again with bursts of fiery explosion. It was Roswaal who had rained down that fire, but it was a pair — a Flying Dragon and its rider, launched from the castle — who, it seemed, had transformed it into those bursts of flame.

He says "seemed" because, from several kilometers away, Roswaal's eyes could not fully track an opponent moving at such speed. It wasn't a matter of Roswaal's eyesight. Human eyes simply aren't built for that.

However, the Medium in his arms, through the power of her Divine Protection, and the "Marksman" on the other side, through some method of his own, had both broken past that limit and could see each other clearly. The proof of it —

"—Hup, kyaa!"

With an adorable little battle cry, Medium — cradled in his arms — swung her machete.
She was flying in a state of total dependence on someone else. There was no way her balance should have been good, and yet her blade still caught the incoming bolt of light, cutting it down with a sound like splashing water.

Roswaal, feeling the shock of the strike and sensing the severed bolt of light pass behind them to dissolve back into mana, could not suppress a shudder at the speed and precision of that attack.

—It was a killing technique perfected through what must have been an almost unbelievable degree of mastery.

He guessed the bolts being fired were some variety of "Yang magic," but unlike the Jiwald line that fires beams of light-heat, this — firing bolts of light — was an original, unsystematized creation all its own.
He couldn't be certain from a single instant of exchange, but likely the light was being shaped into something resembling the ice pillars Emilia fires, then driven into the opponent's vital points. What was worth noting was that, unlike bullets of ice or earth, it carried no physical substance at all — it was accomplishing all this with light alone.
By doing so, no actual projectile remained in the wound, making the true nature of the attack difficult to discern, and moreover, the light scorched the flesh around the point of impact, ensuring the wound went deep.

"A fine piece of magic."

Truly a blow deserving of the name "Magic Bullet," Roswaal thought admiringly.
If the man had devised this on his own, he must have possessed the talent to become a first-rate mage. Though at this point, the man himself likely had no wish for such an honor. As far as recognition within the Empire went, he had already received the highest possible accolade — one of the "Nine Divine Generals."

And it seemed unlikely, in any case, that the dead — rather than the living — would still crave such glory.

"Then again, it sounds as though even in life he wasn't the sort to cling to honor or advancement. I've heard as much both from those close to him, and from the one who was present at his end."

Regarding the "Marksman" now before him — Balleroy Temeglyph — an unusually large number of people who had known him in life meant Roswaal had had more opportunity than he'd expected to gather information.

Though what he'd gotten from the O'Connell siblings was mostly testimony about his character, and Serena's words had caught in her throat with a guilt uncharacteristic of her. So Roswaal hadn't been able to obtain the tactical information he wanted from them — but there had been an unexpected reserve.

Namely —

"—To think, of all people, it was young Julius who defeated that famed 'Nine Divine General.'"

In his arms, careful not to let Medium — focusing her concentration — overhear, Roswaal sorted through his thoughts and recalled Julius's troubled expression.

Anastasia's foremost knight, whose sense of duty had brought him, together with his liege, all the way to cooperate here in the Vollachian Empire — the "Finest Knight" had provided genuinely useful information about the formidable foe Roswaal now faced alongside Medium.

—From the start, whether the battlefield ended up being the capital or the fortress city, it had been a foregone conclusion that Roswaal would be the one matched against Balleroy Temeglyph.

That wasn't because Roswaal was old acquaintances with Serena Dracroy, and had been entrusted with the task of delivering the final blow to Balleroy, whom she had looked after since he was a child. Roswaal has feelings too, but as a rule, he excludes judgments swayed by sentiment.
Naturally, Roswaal's taking on Balleroy was a matter of strategic calculation.

It's a moot point to bring up now, but the Vollachian Empire has long been the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica's sworn enemy.
Of the Four Great Nations, none can afford to let its guard down with any of the others, but the fact remains that these two mutually recognize each other as the relationship demanding the most vigilance. That's precisely why the non-aggression pact concluded with the Empire during the period of the Royal Selection was such a historic achievement — though it did nothing to thaw the frozen relations between the two nations.

To put it simply, the Kingdom's wariness toward the Vollachian Empire, its notional enemy, has persisted for a long time, and countermeasures against its primary strike force, the "Nine Divine Generals," and its deterrent force, the Flying Dragon Squadron, have always been under discussion. And in the course of those strategy meetings, it had already been decided — that the Kingdom's magic corps would be the ones to face the Empire's Flying Dragon Squadron, and that its pinnacle, Roswaal, would be the one to clash with the finest of the Flying Dragon Riders.

"Still, I never imagined I'd end up facing both the 'Vile Elder' and the 'Marksman' — both objects of the highest caution — within the Empire itself."

When discussions concerning the Empire were held, these two individuals were regarded as the greatest threats.
Of course, in terms of individual combat strength, there were the two great powers known as the "Blue Lightning" and the "Spirit Eater," but against those the Kingdom had its own "Sword Saint" to match. But when it comes to war, what matters most is the existence of leadership.
The surest way to end a war is to end the life of its leader. And in that regard, more feared than "One" or "Two" were the "Vile Elder" and the "Marksman."

Hence, Roswaal had already formed estimates of the man's skill and technique based on rumor alone. In the end, upon hearing that the man had died in the course of his rebellion without their ever having faced each other, that simulation had seemed to lose its meaning — yet a strange twist of fate had brought this very situation to pass.

And now, having confirmed Balleroy Temeglyph's actual skill rather than merely imagining it, Roswaal — the strongest mage in the world — was certain of one thing.

"—Just as I thought, I can't win this alone."

Not as a joke, not in idle mockery, and not out of any cowardly impulse — Roswaal acknowledged it flatly, as simple fact.
This wasn't a case of magic being inferior to martial skill. It was a matter of different arenas entirely.

First, mobility differs.
Roswaal, capable of flight magic, is among the very few people in the world able to fly solo, but his speed and maneuverability fall short of a professional Flying Dragon. Challenge one head-on in aerial combat, and he'd be scrambled by sheer speed, tossed about by three-dimensional movement, and shot down before long — at best.

Second, the purpose and precision of their respective attacks differ.
The "Magic Bullet" technique Balleroy has mastered is a skill honed for the sole purpose of killing others, and it's fair to call it complete, both in power and in accuracy. Roswaal, too, aims to master every branch of magic so as not to shame his teacher, but the depths of magic remain distant even to him — and while his versatility overwhelmingly surpasses Balleroy's, he can state with certainty that nothing in his repertoire rivals an enemy specialized to that single point: killing the target.

And third, the combat conditions differ.
This, frustratingly, is the single greatest obstacle in this matchup.
Think about it and it becomes obvious: in a fight, if you can attack the enemy one-sidedly from a distance, there's no way you can lose. That is Roswaal's natural fighting style, and fighting at the maximum distance one is capable of is, in a sense, the basic winning strategy for any mage.
The problem arises when the opponent outmatches you at that maximum range. In that case, naturally, you end up allowing the enemy the very one-sided assault you'd normally be dishing out yourself. To avoid that, if you're losing on range, you need to close the distance, neutralize the advantage or disadvantage that comes from spacing, and force the fight down to a contest of raw ability.

But the combat conditions imposed on Roswaal did not allow for that premise.

"Honestly, having our people picked off one after another from a distance is the worst-case scenario. Somehow pin the guy down so he can't do that rapid-fire thing!"

That was the role Subaru had assigned Roswaal when dividing up the members of "Team Save the World from Ruin" for the assault on the capital.
In practice, it was a rational judgment. Roswaal has no intention of claiming he's the strongest asset here, ahead of Halibel or Olbart, but in terms of maximum range, he is unquestionably the one. Emilia is a close second, but in her case, the delicacy of her attacks and the lack of any means to rise into the sky — the enemy's primary battlefield — were issues. Naturally, the job fell to Roswaal.

As a result, in order to keep the rest of his allies from being sniped, Roswaal was forced to open the engagement by launching an attack from a range at which the enemy held an overwhelming advantage, drawing all attention to himself.

"Just as I feared — even from this distance, his attacks land with precision. I've managed to draw his attention, but... now I have to close the gap to my own range. Quite the death march, this."

Just how difficult that would be should be more than clear from the three disadvantages outlined above.
Against a formidable foe, he must fight using the enemy's preferred tactics, at the enemy's preferred range. On top of that, acknowledging the gap in raw ability, Roswaal is convinced the enemy is superior.

And yet, there is neither grief nor despair lodged in those mismatched eyes.
Because —

"Deeyah!"

The instant he thought he saw a flash of light, the sound of splashing water and an impact shook both Roswaal and Medium.
That was proof of the next bolt's impact, and proof that Medium had once again succeeded in shooting it down. Marveling at the skill it took to pull that off, Roswaal's smile deepened.

The reason for that smile —

"Medium, think you can manage?"

"Yep! I can do it! Just like Rozchin said... Bal-bro's aiming for my arms and shoulders and stuff!"

"Tch—"

Through the magnified vision of his enlarged left eye, watching the next bolt he fired get cut down by that machete, Balleroy ground his back teeth — both at how much the girl he knew had improved, and at the viciousness of his opponent's aim.
Within the far-eye glass he'd fashioned from bent light, he could see the man flying with Medium — grinning with fierce determination — steadily, surely closing the distance. The intent was plain: closing distance on an opponent who excels at long-range combat is textbook strategy in war.

The surprise attack of flaming bolts from above the clouds, and the switch in tactics right after it failed — that couldn't have been a coincidence.
An attack that crude, expecting it to go unnoticed before it landed would be absurd. That attack had been meant to be shot down. An attack that had to be shot down, even knowing as much.
And having thus let the enemy strike the opening blow so neatly, the ultra-long-range battle that would follow — one overwhelmingly in Balleroy's favor — should have begun. Instead, the sight of Medium made Balleroy's fighting spirit falter.

Letting the surprise attack be thwarted, and still beginning the fight at a range favorable to Balleroy — it was a plan built on the knowledge that his "Magic Bullet" chooses its targets.

"Medi...!"

Both the first and second shots — Balleroy's "Magic Bullets" — had been blocked by Medium.
It had always been like that. Medium, when she's feeling down, can be outmatched even by the hopelessly uncoordinated Flop, but when she's fired up, she becomes an unstoppable little terror.
Flop and Miles alike had never been able to lay a hand on her, and Balleroy — though admittedly biased where family was concerned — had had his own share of trouble with her too. Since Serena never scolded her for it, that boundless energy of hers knew no limits.
Still, he'd never once imagined she'd manage to block his own "Magic Bullet."

"—Ngh."

"...I know, Carillon."

To Balleroy, teeth clenched, his beloved dragon called out with a whinny, wings beating.
Through the technique of "Flying Dragon Handling," Balleroy and Carillon bind their existences together — Od to Od, soul to soul. It's by this method that the secret, closely guarded art of "Flying Dragon Handling" tames what are naturally violent creatures.
Bound as they were, the fluctuations of Balleroy's thoughts and feelings passed directly to Carillon as well. And the reverse held true — Balleroy could sense Carillon's thoughts too.

He understood. The reason the "Magic Bullet" had been blocked wasn't Medium's skill alone.
Seeing his little sister figure's earnest, determined face before him, Balleroy — lacking the cold ruthlessness of a true warrior — hadn't been able to bring himself to shoot her down.

"If it's a limb..."

Take even one of her limbs, and Medium's withdrawal from the fight would be certain.
The mage carrying her would surely conclude there was no advantage in flying with a badly wounded woman. If possible, an arm rather than a leg. An arm would cause less trouble for the rest of her life than a leg.
Force Medium out of the fight through injury, shoot down the mage, and then petition Sphinx for the girl's life to be spared. That would make his worries vanish, gone without a trace.
Vanish, without a trace, forever gone.

"—Let's do this."

In the bloodless face of the Undead, clear fighting spirit kindled in Balleroy's golden eyes.
Gone was the hesitation of moments before, when he'd flinched from attacking Medium; in its place was the look of the "Marksman" who had found agreement between his own nature and his own goal, and now meant to see it through.

"――――"

Fighting spirit burning in his golden eyes, the heat drained from every corner of Balleroy's body, and stillness settled over him.
Closing his right eye, focusing his awareness entirely into the far-eye glass of his left, he fixed his spear against the knee he'd braced on his beloved dragon's back, and at its tip, a sharply honed bolt of light took shape.
Balleroy is a mage of the crooked path, and this single art is the only thing he has ever honed.
This one shot consumes little mana, achieving maximum effect through the minimum necessary steps — well suited to his own nature. It is the embodiment of an approach devoted to landing the kill, without fail.

"――――"

As Balleroy focused thus, Carillon likewise sharpened her own concentration.
In the instant he peers through the far-eye glass, scrutinizing every movement of his target, his vigilance toward everything else inevitably slackens — and it is Carillon who secures the peripheral awareness Balleroy loses in that moment.
A Flying Dragon's eyesight and instinctive wariness, keeping watch over their surroundings — his beloved dragon was an absolutely indispensable presence for Balleroy, who had built this entire fighting style around her.

"If you're gonna go up against me, o'course you'd want to close the distance. But did you really think I'd just let you do that, knowing full well that's your plan?"

That flight magic even existed came as a surprise, but its closing speed was nothing to compare with a "Flying Dragon Rider." Retreat by exactly as much as the enemy advances, and the distance between them never shrinks.
Of course, there's a limit to how long one can keep retreating, so maintaining distance also calls on turns and altitude changes, not retreat alone. But that's no problem.
The vast sky has no limits. It was precisely because of that that the "Marksman's" fighting style could be established in the first place.

"—Hit."

The words slipped from his moisture-less lips, and at that signal, the tip of the spear flared sharply with light.
In the instant the "Magic Bullet" is loosed, there's no recoil, no sentiment on Balleroy's part. There is only the faintest flicker of light, and, in under a second, the result of the shot.

Through the magnified view of the far-eye glass, Medium blocked the third "Magic Bullet" with her machete as well.
It was a movement that showed she'd caught on that Balleroy was aiming for her limbs. Whether that was her own natural instinct or advice whispered by the mage behind her, he couldn't say.
Admiring it as no small feat, Balleroy let out a breath, and —

"Hit, hit, hit, hit, hit—"

— in a single continuous breath, he pounded out the fourth through eighth shots.

"――――"

Balleroy's "Magic Bullet" has no recoil. Hence, there's nothing stopping him from firing in rapid succession.
If there's any limiting factor at all, it's the speed at which he can conjure the bolt of light to be fired — but Balleroy had poured the entirety of his magical talent into perfecting this one technique, the "Magic Bullet."
Five shots a second, each fired with identical, flawless precision — that is the reason Balleroy Temeglyph is called the "Marksman," and the reason he earned the rank of General.

"Hit, hit, hit, hit, hit—"

Swinging both machetes furiously, Medium desperately continued to resist the barrage of "Magic Bullets."
That alone was worthy of praise, but Balleroy's reserves weren't being depleted in the slightest. Barely ten seconds had passed; keep this up for another ten, twenty seconds, and Medium would inevitably run out of strength.
And as Balleroy calmly closed that gap, bullet by bullet —

"――――"

For a moment, a question arose in Balleroy's otherwise composed, focused thoughts.
Even as he continued conjuring bolts of light and maintaining his relentless barrage of "Magic Bullet" sniper fire, it was this very situation that gave him pause.

This was an opponent who had brought Medium along on a surprise attack, deliberately placing severe restrictions on Balleroy's use of the "Magic Bullet."
Surely an enemy who had come intending to face Balleroy wouldn't run a plan so half-baked that he could simply break through it by sticking to his usual style?
He ought to find it strange, ought to find it odd — precisely in this moment when his own specialty was working exactly as intended.
It was right after Balleroy thought this —

"—Ngh"

In place of Balleroy, who was fixed on the far-eye glass, Carillon — keeping watch on their surroundings — let out a high whinny.
Flying at high speed above the Crystal Palace, maintaining a steady distance from the enemy, his beloved dragon's cry drew Balleroy's attention to its cause.
It was falling from even higher than the lofty height at which Balleroy and Carillon themselves flew.

"—Tch, now that's a nasty trick!"

Baring his teeth and snarling, Balleroy saw, breaking through the thick clouds and plummeting down, a threat even more direct than the earlier rain of fiery bolts aimed at the Crystal Palace.

—A mass of ice, huge as a small mountain, was hurtling furiously down from the sky toward the capital.

"Fighting Lord Balleroy in open ground with no cover would be a fatal mistake. He is the finest 'Flying Dragon Rider' the Empire has... a phenomenal wielder for whom every direction is within range."

Following Julius's grim-faced advice, the aerial battle Roswaal had been forced into was, against the Empire's greatest "Flying Dragon Rider," precisely the worst possible choice of battlefield.
After all, the sky offers nothing whatsoever in the way of cover. Not to mention front, back, left, and right — it's an environment that secures range across every three-dimensional direction, up, down, and diagonal alike. About as bad as it gets.

It's a warrior's constant aim to set up the situation most disadvantageous for the enemy; deliberately matching conditions with the opponent, let alone choosing a situation unfavorable to oneself, is the mark of a madman.
So then — given that circumstances left him no choice, was Roswaal, in letting the enemy fight on his favored battlefield, with his favored tactics, at his favored range, some kind of deranged lunatic?

—Absolutely not.
Roswaal wouldn't claim to be normal, but he understands himself to have nothing to do with the eccentricity of scorching himself with a thirst for victory, or savoring an unfavorable situation for its own sake.
He acknowledges his own abnormality, but that abnormality isn't something that manifests in combat.
Hence, Roswaal would never do something so foolish as to walk into a battlefield with no chance of victory.

"Hah, hah, haaah—"

In Roswaal's arms, steam rose from Medium's body as she drew ragged breaths.
The intense heat conveyed through his palm where he touched her was proof that her body was raising its own output in response to her fighting spirit. It was, at the same time, proof that in a mere ten seconds or so, she'd been pushed past her limits again and again.
Roswaal marveled at the terrifying rate of fire behind the "Magic Bullet," and at Medium, who had swatted down nearly fifty rounds, even knowing they'd been aimed to avoid a fatal blow.
But she wouldn't last another ten seconds. So Roswaal moved the battle forward a step.

—Breaking through the clouds blanketing the capital, a colossal mass of ice fell from above toward the ground.

A mass of ice so massive it could be mistaken for a mountain — naturally, it was Roswaal who had dropped it. That said, conjuring an ice mass of that size would leave even a mage as self-assured as Roswaal, the world's greatest, dangerously low on mana.
So instead, the ice mass had been made by Emilia, who has mana to spare.

"To make something that size and come out of it without so much as breaking a sweat. Truly, it's a preposterous thing."

Having pulled off what amounted to a miracle equivalent to conjuring an iceberg, Emilia had calmly gone on to follow Subaru's instructions and head to another battlefield. As reassuring as that was, when he considered the possibility that they might one day find themselves opposed to her, it made his head ache something fierce.
Still, in this moment, he leaned on that reassurance and made it the basis of his iceberg-drop strategy.

Whether Roswaal could have conjured that massive iceberg using the entirety of his own mana was doubtful, but simply maintaining it — keeping it suspended, one Emilia had already created — was far less taxing.
He'd had it on standby ever since dropping the fiery bolts, and had now, in this very instant, released it from its tether.

"――――"

It was an attack of pure mass, equivalent to dropping a mountain from the sky, but its aim wasn't to crush Balleroy — fleeing about the sky — from above. That would have made things simple, but for someone like Balleroy, escaping the iceberg's blast radius wouldn't have been especially difficult.
Hence, the reason for dropping the iceberg wasn't to attack Balleroy. It was — obstruction.

"If there's no cover, then one simply has to create some."

The instant he murmured this, Roswaal's eyes narrowed, and immediately after, cracks spread across the entire iceberg.
In that instant, the shrill, piercing sound that split the sky was exactly like glass fitted into the heavens suddenly shattering all at once, and what followed might well have looked like the very collapse of the sky.

The iceberg shattered, scattering fragments every which way as chunks of ice tumbled in disarray toward the ground below.
Shattered as they were, they remained fragments of a mountain. Each individual piece was large enough to rival a building or a Dragon Carriage, and their fall from the sky was equivalent to a landslide of ice.
It was a sight much like filling a box with pebbles and sand and dumping it out over a flowerbed —

"This is what a mage is truly capable of."

If you can attack one-sidedly from your favored range, there's no way you can lose.
As stated earlier, that is the basic, winning tactic of any mage. Hence, Roswaal too favors and enacts a fighting style rooted in that principle.

To attack one-sidedly, it's essential to strip away the enemy's "options."
A favored range is merely an easy-to-grasp yardstick for accomplishing exactly that. Stripping the enemy of his "options," dragging him into a situation favorable to yourself —
That is the fighting style of Roswaal, the strongest mage in the world.

"Teryai!"

Light slipped through the gaps between the falling ice shards and chunks, and Medium cut down the incoming "Magic Bullet."
He'd told Medium not to be shaken no matter what happened, and he was more than grateful she was following that instruction to the letter. The "Marksman," too, must have been considerably taken aback, and the fact that he'd shifted straight back into attacking deserved genuine admiration.
That said, his rate of fire and mobility had been reduced beyond comparison.
Above all —

"If you're the man I've heard you are, your 'Magic Bullet' can't target me directly. Falling from this altitude, there'd be no way for Medium to survive."

An enemy realizing a battlefield beyond all norms — a devil of a foe.
Using Medium as a shield, sealing off his own tactics — Balleroy recognized the mage before him as exactly that, with clear and unmistakable hostility.

In the Vollachian Empire, so-called mages are exceedingly rare.
Partly this owes to soil and lineage — a trait passed down that makes handling magic ill-suited to them — but at bottom, it's because magic simply doesn't fit the Empire's guiding philosophy.
At the root of the Vollachian character lies respect for the strong, which might just as well be called reverence for a superior warrior's skill and talent. Taking up a weapon oneself, clashing directly with an opponent, and overcoming them by one's own hand — that is Vollachia's ideal of the warrior, and any fight that falls short of it is looked down upon.

Such prejudice and preconception toward that kind of fighting run deep, and though it had begun to be reconsidered under Vincent's reign, it would still take considerable time before it vanished entirely.
Balleroy's fighting style, which earned him the name "Marksman," and the killing methods of Olbart, the "Vile Elder" and head of the Shinobi, were, contrary to the wariness they inspired abroad, not exactly held in high regard at home either.
Not that either Balleroy or Olbart cared, since they weren't fighting for the sake of reputation.

It was precisely because of that detachment that Balleroy, unlike other citizens of the Empire, had never held any particular disdain for mages. —And it was that very assessment, held in his lifetime, that he now wanted to take back.

"I don't care much for that kind of trick, mage!"

There's admiration due for the skill of turning all creation to one's own use, fashioning one's own battlefield out of it, but when that poison fang is turned against himself — against, of all people, his own family — anger wells up too.
He wanted to put a bolt straight through that head, that chest —

"Carillon—!"

"—Ngh."

Slapping his beloved dragon's back to spur her on, the whinnying dragon beat her wings and picked up speed.
Dancing through the dark sky, Balleroy and Carillon flew through a storm of ice raining down at an unbelievable scale, focusing everything on clearing the labyrinth of ice that had suddenly formed, pressing for a breakthrough.

To evade Balleroy's "Magic Bullet," he had faced opponents who'd fled into dense forest, or holed up in sturdy fortresses.
When that happened, Balleroy had dealt with it either by clearing away the trees with rapid-fire "Magic Bullets" to secure a line of sight, or by pinpointing where the enemy was hiding and finishing them off by drilling repeatedly through the same spot — a wall-piercing kill.

But no one had ever attempted a countermeasure this absurd before.
So naturally, no one had ever managed to overturn a countermeasure this absurd, either.

"So I take it, if I get through this, it's my win?"

Dodging shards of the shattered iceberg — brush against one and it could take his life along with it — Balleroy pictured the two who had launched this assault: Medium and the mage.
No one besides those two had managed to rise into the capital's sky. Which meant the hand Balleroy faced in this aerial engagement was down to just these two, and no more.

It seemed Serena hadn't sent out her prized Flying Dragon Squadron against Balleroy after all.

"Just as I'd expect from the Marquis. She understands."

Even in aerial combat involving Flying Dragons, a clear hierarchy exists.
For instance, a lone Flying Dragon without a partner can never match one with a "Flying Dragon Rider." A skilled "Flying Dragon Rider," alone with a single dragon, can rout a hundred wild dragons.
And when "Flying Dragon Rider" clashes with "Flying Dragon Rider," here too, the superior one always wins. Numerical advantage or disadvantage barely factors in — the side with the superior "Flying Dragon Rider" wins. Without a rider more skilled than Balleroy, no matter how many dragons of the Flying Dragon Squadron were sent in, the result would only be a mountain of corpses — precious riders and dragons alike.

A truly skilled "Flying Dragon Rider" is that precious, that exceptional a talent.
That's why Balleroy doesn't hate Serena. Her granting Miles's covert mission into the Kingdom of Lugunica was a necessary thing. — An unavoidable thing.

"Even so, I—"

The latter half of that muttered, teeth-clenched remark went unheard even by Balleroy himself, the one who spoke it.
Below them, the shattered iceberg's remains crashed one after another into the capital, and with a thunderous roar and tremendous plumes of dust, tore the cityscape apart and remade it.

The pitiable damage inflicted on the capital — the devastation, a magical catastrophe in all but name, continued unabated. This was a familiar city he had once sought to protect in life. He wasn't without feeling at the sight of it being torn apart, but Balleroy's focus remained fixed on the enemy rather than the crumbling city.

"――――"

Amid the fantastical spectacle of ice fragments scattering like powder, refracting light every which way, the figures of Medium and the others, once distant, came into clearer view.
And yet there was still distance between them. Dropping the iceberg from above the clouds, inflicting damage this severe on the capital's cityscape — and still, the distance gained was minimal.

"Hit, hit, hit—"

As if to push back against the ground he'd gained, the time it took for Balleroy's "Magic Bullet" to reach its target grew shorter.
Which meant Medium would need to react that much faster, an instant sooner. Already worn thin, how many more shots could she still block?
Even having cut into Balleroy's mobility and constrained his angle and line of fire, the advantage still lay with him.
Which meant — the next move was coming.

"—? Another one of those fire clumps... no."

Sensing a sign of change above, Balleroy — busy smashing through ice shards with his spear and blasting others aside with bolts of light to keep his line of fire clear — looked up and knit his brow.
Through the great hole the iceberg had torn in the thick clouds, fiery bolts came falling, along with — though not on the same scale as the previous iceberg — still-massive chunks of ice, one after another. At the sight of them falling, Balleroy first thought another obstacle had been added to the sky — and then he grasped the true intent behind it.

Those falling objects, wildly off from the position where Balleroy and the others were flying, traced a trajectory aimed to strike the Crystal Palace directly.

"What in the — the hell are you playing at, you monster!"

Witnessing fire and ice falling in a way that wasn't aimed at him, Balleroy nonetheless raised his voice, because he understood this was, in fact, an attack directed squarely at him.
Those wouldn't cause Balleroy direct harm. Instead, they would mercilessly crush Madelyn's body, still inside the castle, and everyone else within its walls.
He couldn't allow that. — Sphinx must not be allowed to die.

"Ohhhhhh—!!"

Spreading her wings, banking sharply, Carillon braked hard at a position clear of the falling ice. On her back, Balleroy jerked his body upright as if flung, aimed his spear's point toward the sky above the Crystal Palace, and loosed his "Magic Bullet."
Judging that a rate of five shots per second wouldn't be enough, in this instant Balleroy surpassed a limit he had never crossed even in life, and his "Magic Bullets" blanketed the sky like a curtain of gunfire.

The falling bolts of fire, the chunks of ice — the raging storm of light bolts shot every last one of them down, protecting the Crystal Palace from the catastrophic damage that would otherwise have caused its collapse.
But in this very instant, it stood to reason that his opponent would come after Balleroy, his back turned and exposed —

"—Hit."

So Balleroy, even as he aimed the spear's point at the objects falling toward the Crystal Palace, simultaneously aimed the spear's butt-end at the enemy closing in on him, responding with simultaneous shots fore and aft with his "Magic Bullet."

"――――"

The "Magic Bullet" loosed to his rear was only a single shot, but that was enough.
The fire magic that had come rushing straight at Balleroy — meant to catch him with his back turned, apparently caught off guard — was pierced head-on by that "Magic Bullet" and burst apart midair as well.
The bolt of light, having punched clean through the enemy's magic, kept flying straight on, striking Medium and the others, who'd thought they'd caught him off guard — and her machete rose to meet it.

"Medi, I used to be one of the 'Nine Divine Generals,' you know."

In terms of combat skill, fired up as she was, there was no way he'd fall behind his little sister figure.
The "Magic Bullet" fired backward to catch her off guard was, compared to every bolt of light Balleroy had fired thus far, just a shade larger — and proportionally more powerful.

"—Uwah!"

With a splash of sound, both machetes were torn clean out of Medium's hands.
Whether by instinct sensing something was different, meeting it with two blades instead of one had been a fine call. Thanks to that, though he'd meant to blow off everything below her left elbow, both her arms remained intact.
Still, with no means left to block, the outcome would be the same. With the next strike, he'd take that arm and end it.

"Hit."

With that resolve, the spear's tip flashed, and the work of dealing with the fire and ice threatening to crush the Crystal Palace came to an end.
Climbing rapidly with Carillon, widening once more the distance the enemy had closed, Balleroy began generating a "Magic Bullet" meant to force Medium out of the fight —

"Wha—"

—In that instant, filling Balleroy's field of vision entirely, a shimmer of rainbow light spread across the sky.


△▼△▼△▼△


—In the matter of overcoming Balleroy Temeglyph, Julius's advice had proven genuinely invaluable.

It was advice from someone who had already overcome the man once before.
Information this useful was something even Subaru could rarely provide so readily — after all, Subaru isn't a warrior, so his counsel on fighting tends to stay vague. In any case —

"I fought Lord Balleroy, and I defeated him. Of course, it was not a victory I could have won without Ferris's assistance. ...As for why a man of Lord Balleroy's caliber would join such a rebellion and threaten His Excellency Vincent's life — that reason, too, in due time."

"You saw the 'Book of the Dead,' didn't you. What a truly strange coincidence. Julius, if it's at all possible, I'd love it if you could describe in detail what you—"

"—Marquis Mathers, understand that what I will convey to you is, strictly, Lord Balleroy's fighting style, and how I myself fought him. What I saw without his leave, what Lord Balleroy carried within him — of that, I intend never, for the rest of my life, to speak."

Julius had said as much with a grave and earnest expression, and Roswaal could have pointed out that such restraint constituted aiding the enemy, and pried the information Julius had gained from the "Book of the Dead" out of him by whatever means necessary. But Roswaal chose not to. He confined himself only to what Julius had decided to share.

Even he found that choice somewhat surprising, in retrospect.
The Roswaal of old, if there existed some means of obtaining information about an opponent, would surely have deemed it right to learn everything possible before formulating a strategy and heading into battle. And yet, why had he instead chosen to respect Julius's feelings, to honor his wishes?

He had no firm answer as to the reason for that change of heart — but no matter.
Even without knowing everything about Balleroy Temeglyph, Julius had given him a precious answer. For Roswaal, that alone was more than enough.

"Given that it was young Julius who struck you down — that would mean, perhaps, that you yourself don't know why you died, would it not, Balleroy Temeglyph?"

Through the Authority of "Gluttony," Julius had his "name" devoured.
As a result, everyone around Julius — save for Subaru, the exception — had forgotten him. Roswaal was no exception to that, and in all likelihood, not even the dead were exempt.
Even Balleroy, who had fallen by none other than Julius's hand, did not know what it was that had brought about his death.
Which meant —

"—He can be killed the same way he died the first time."

A truly strange coincidence, a miraculous confluence of chance.
This was a sliver of a winning chance that could never have been seized had Julius not killed Balleroy, and had that same Julius not had his "name" devoured. To even imagine the anguish of the two directly involved, Julius and Balleroy, would be presumptuous of an outsider like Roswaal — but he would, at least in the privacy of his own heart, give thanks for that confluence of fate.

—A deployed barrier of rainbow light blocked the path of Balleroy and his dragon as they attempted to climb.

A barrier of aurora light — that, he'd been told, was the magic that had dealt the decisive blow when Julius defeated Balleroy.
This was an original piece of magic Julius had devised together with the spirit he carried with him, and reproducing it required, even for Roswaal, an abnormal expenditure of effort.
Roswaal is no stranger to using multiple types of magic simultaneously, but six types at once is nothing short of insane.
Listening to his own body's Gate scream in protest, Roswaal nonetheless reproduced Julius's victory, all to raise his own odds of winning.

This wasn't some act of mockery or harassment — it was a strategic choice.
Of course, if the sole aim had been to seal off Balleroy's escape routes, Roswaal could have accomplished the same thing using fire or wind, techniques he's far more accustomed to. But a familiar spell would be recognized as dangerous on sight.
What was needed was magic Balleroy had never seen before, something capable of robbing him of his judgment, if only for an instant.

Which is precisely why Roswaal guessed that this rainbow aurora, too, was likely among the things erased by the Authority of "Gluttony" — and, resolved to bear the cost, deliberately brought it out for this final phase.

"—ngh"

Teeth gritted, Medium — both machetes torn from her hands — still showed no sign of losing her fighting spirit, her fists clenched tight.
Perhaps she could have blocked one or two more "Magic Bullets" at the cost of both her hands, but Roswaal had no intention of turning this into a fight that demanded a price that steep.

In the sense of stripping the enemy of his options, Medium had already done more than enough.
On the way to the capital, Medium had thanked Subaru, and Subaru in turn had thanked Medium — and Roswaal shared that sentiment entirely.

"What are you even talking about, Miss Medium. If anyone should be saying thanks, it's me. Seriously, I don't even want to think about what would've happened if I hadn't met you and Mr. Flop back there."

Roswaal had wanted to applaud the sheer performance behind Subaru's bashful delivery of that line.
Indeed — without the O'Connell siblings' cooperation, this victory could never have been achieved.

"But—"

Blocked by the rainbow, falling.
That, Roswaal thought, narrowing his eyes, would be the conclusion awaiting the Empire's finest "Flying Dragon Rider" —

"――――"

—In that instant, struck by an impact from behind and to the side, his consciousness was violently rocked.


△▼△▼△▼△


The moment the rainbow light spread before his eyes, a chill of dread ran through Balleroy's entire body.

It's an ironic thing, that even in an Undead body, something like a survival instinct — a warrior's gut sense — continues to function.
Following the call of that dead survival instinct, Balleroy fired what was, quite literally, his hidden reserve shot — a "Concealed Bullet" he'd kept in store — straight at the barrier the aurora had conjured, using it as a foothold to break through.

—Just as Roswaal had guessed, Balleroy did not remember the cause of his own death.

Why had he come to lose his life? He could recall the upheaval that had triggered it, the motives and reasons that had drawn him into it — but the reason for his death alone remained a blur.
Given the choice, he might almost have preferred to forget everything else and keep only the reason for his death. But he could say with equal certainty that if he forgot that, he wouldn't be himself anymore.

In any case, Balleroy had been unable to recall the moment of his own death.
And yet, from what he'd heard from the other Undead, aside from those who'd died without even understanding what was happening to them, he'd found no one else whose memory of the circumstances leading to their death was similarly gone.
Precisely because of that, Balleroy had turned it over in his mind — the question of why he'd died, a question with no answer.

And so, he had thought as hard as he could about how he might die.
He'd considered the possibility of being exposed as party to the rebellion and having his head taken by someone like Cecilus, or being purged by fellow Generals like Groovy or Moguro.
But given the reason he'd decided to carry out the rebellion in the first place — he suspected it likely had something to do with someone from the Kingdom of Lugunica, and turned his mind to imagining what kind of battle it might have been.

—The idea of losing while airborne, his escape route sealed off, had been among the last possibilities to occur to him.

Balleroy had arrived at that notion after the battle involving the Coupled Dragon Carriages — after retrieving Lamia Godwin's person from that scene, and confirming the presence of Flop and Medium there.
In that battle, he'd seen someone inside the coupled carriages put up a barrier of rainbow light to block a "Magic Bullet" he'd fired as a warning shot. It was after that — after seeing that rainbow.

He hadn't been certain. He'd simply sensed that it was something troublesome.
So he'd kept it in mind — to always keep a single "Magic Bullet" in reserve, ready to be loosed instantly, no matter what situation he found himself cornered in.
And that was what had proven useful in breaking through the rainbow barrier in this very moment.

"—ngh"

Roaring, Carillon tore through the rainbow, breaking through the encirclement even as wounds covered her entire body.
Making the fullest use of the advantages of having become an Undead Dragon, his beloved dragon healed her painful wounds even as she flew toward survival. Grateful to her, Balleroy leveled his "Magic Bullet"-loaded spear at an angle and —

"Hit."

The bolt he loosed flew straight, wildly off from the enemy's actual position. But in its line of fire lay the debris of the fallen ice chunks, and the bolt of light skimmed diagonally across the cut surface of that ice.
Carried by that momentum, the bolt bounced, again and again, ricocheting through the surrounding debris — and then plunged straight into the back of the enemy who had loosed that rainbow at him.

Born, died, and only now, for the first time, had he succeeded at a ricocheting shot with light.
Having quite literally leapt across what might be called the line between life and death, Balleroy's technique had evolved yet further.

—Roswaal's misfortune was this: in choosing an unfamiliar spell precisely because it would delay Balleroy's response, he had inadvertently allowed an instinctive reaction to a rainbow that was, in fact, a second sighting rather than a first.

"Rozchin—!!"

A scream called out to the mage, struck by a ricochet from an unforeseen angle, his flight disrupted.
It was Medium's anguished voice — held in the mage's arms, attacked from a position she couldn't defend, utterly unable to do a thing about it.

If he'd aimed to shoot the mage down directly, the resulting fall from such a great height carried the risk that Medium, too, wouldn't survive.
That fear had effectively forced Balleroy to focus his attacks on Medium — but the iceberg drop and the attack on the Crystal Palace had, ironically, worked against the very enemy who'd set them up.

"From here, I can catch her before she falls."

The distance that had spanned several kilometers between the capital's southwest and its northernmost point, once a long-range engagement, had closed over the course of the fight — close enough now that, should the unsteady enemy plummet toward the ground, he could scoop Medium up before she hit.
Balleroy slapped Carillon's neck, and, slipping straight through the ice debris, turned her wings toward Medium to retrieve her.
Bracing himself for however much Medium might curse him for it —

"Rozchin?"

The voice that reached him then was oddly nonchalant, utterly unlike the anguished scream from moments before.
If anything, it had the airheaded, characteristically Medium ring to it — but it was the sheer wrongness of hearing such a tone in the middle of this battle, and what followed, that seized Balleroy's attention entirely.

"Wha—"

The Undead, black eyes holding golden irises, stared wide-eyed, speechless.
In Balleroy's field of view, Medium — released from the arms of the mage flying through the sky — began falling toward the ground. No — not falling. Thrown.

Medium's body, given momentum, had been hurled with full force toward the ground.
Faster than free fall, plunging toward the earth. If he didn't go retrieve her this very instant, he wouldn't make it in time.
The instant that thought crossed his mind, Balleroy understood the devil's calculation behind it.

"――――"

Right on the verge of choosing to fly to Medium, Balleroy's eyes met the mage's.
Now finally close enough to make out the man's face clearly, without even needing the far-eye glass — what an infuriatingly punchable face he had.
Shot through the back, blood spilling from his mouth, and still the man wore a smile.
Balleroy wanted to drive a "Magic Bullet" straight into that smile and dive after Medium at once. He wouldn't let things play out according to the enemy's design —

"—Ngh"

In that instant, Carillon's whinny snapped Balleroy's attention back to Medium.
Thrown, falling toward the ground. Her rate of fall was fast, but Balleroy and Carillon should easily be able to catch up. — No. Should have been able to.
If only the debris of shattered ice hadn't been waiting below, right where Medium was falling, like a bed of spikes.

"――――"

He had a vision of Medium's entire body being shredded to ribbons by blades of ice.
To the mage, Medium should be one of his own comrades. That basic fact — reason screamed that it offered no guarantee whatsoever, not against this opponent.
The man was a mage. A mage who turns all of creation to his own use, as though it all belonged to him —

"――――"

Thought took an instant; the result appeared right after.
Balleroy's spear held but a single "Magic Bullet," a single chance to choose. Which to aim it at — the mage, or the bed of ice blades below.

—Above all else, Balleroy hated the death of family.

"—Isn't that right, Miles-anii."
"—Ngh"

To that whispered call, his beloved dragon's whinny joined in like a granting of permission.
In that instant, the loosed "Magic Bullet" flew straight and true, utterly destroying every last blade of ice waiting to impale the falling Medium.

And then —

"—Your defeat came from wavering on anything other than what mattered most."

Together with the voice of a man fully resolved to ruthlessness, the enemy's loosed "Magic Bullet" pierced Balleroy's chest and his beloved dragon's wing both at once, bringing this battle in the sky to its conclusion.
