Arc 7 Chapter 109: "The Empire's Sword Wolves"

He remembered something irritating.
If he recalled right, it was something Petra had said.

"Master's got an awful personality, and a foul mouth, and he likes to torment people, and sometimes it's downright creepy how he seems to enjoy himself even while getting yelled at — but when it comes to teaching, he really is good at it."

With her reluctance to admit it written all over her, Petra still wasn't the type to unfairly run someone down just because she disliked them. That was her nature.
Whether it was right to call her — close in age as she was — a friend, Garfiel wasn't sure, but he liked that upright quality of hers. It was easy to see why Frederica had fallen head over heels for her.
In the sense of having a personality prone to losing out because of it, Garfiel supposed he and his devotion to Subaru and Otto were cut from the same cloth. Blood will tell, between siblings, as they say.

If that was so, then Garfiel had to admit it too.
Roswaal L. Mathers was his rival in love, an unstable element in the camp who'd plotted an atrocity Garfiel could never forgive, and a natural enemy he'd never once managed to like since the day they'd met.
And yet — Roswaal's instruction was on the mark, precise enough that it was what kept him alive even now.

"Garfiel, a shuriken. Poisoned. It'll explode even if you dodge it. What's the correct answer?"

"Quit beatin' around the bush, damn you!!"

Garfiel roared, stamping the ground, and an instant later the earth heaved up as a shield to protect them both. There came the light thud of the shuriken burying itself in the wall of dirt, then the roar of an explosion tearing through the air.
Just as Roswaal had read it — if they'd tried to parry it with a gauntlet instead, the damage would have been unavoidable.
Grinding his sharp fangs at that fact—

"Which one?!"
"Me."

The answer came instantly to his question, and Garfiel spun around to face Roswaal behind him.
In that same instant, Roswaal knocked aside a thrown kunai with the sai gripped in both hands. A shadow flickered above, and Garfiel threw all caution to the wind and drove his fist into it.

A crack of impact rang out, Garfiel's punch caught by a whirling kick.
The one who'd delivered it was Olbart, interrupting his own strike meant to crush Roswaal's skull. The old monster held his ground against Garfiel's fist and leg strength alike, even as he said—

"Man, you two are gettin' to be a real pain. Two against one, ain't that a bit unfair?"

"Ain't the time to be changin' the rules now, you shrimpy old geezer!"

"Kah-kah-kah! Don't cost nothin' to say it, though."

Recoiling his leg from the shock of the punch, Olbart used it as a spring to leap backward. For a moment Garfiel made to step forward and press the attack—

"——"

But a shuriken spinning in from the side cut right past the tip of his nose where he'd stopped, slicing through the air.
Letting it pass, Garfiel let out a long breath. Just then, right beside him, Roswaal — resettling his grip on his sai — changed his expression.
Seeing that, Garfiel twisted his lips.

"The hell are you grinnin' about?"

"Oh, no, no. I was simply struck by how remarkably fast you're growing. Without being told, you predicted that sneak attack just now. You're learning as you fight."

"That's just me gettin' sneakier, ain't it. And I'll tell you now, I ain't plannin' on turnin' into some rotten bastard like you or that old geezer."

"Turning rotten takes talent too, you know. By my estimation, you lack the aptitude for it. You're about even with Emilie on that front."

"The hell I am! Don't lump me in with her!"

He'd snapped back at Roswaal's words without thinking, and only after the fact did he reflect that if Emilia had heard it, it might have hurt her feelings.
In any case, thinking of Emilia — who should be rampaging across the battlefield just as he was — and the rest of their camp's comrades, worrying too about the safety of Ram, who was said to have rejoined them, and the Shudraks and Heinkel among the others, Garfiel took a deep breath.

"You sure got room to spare, don't you. Your friends are havin' a rough time of it, I'd wager. Our side's got more depth to the lineup, after all."

"—!"

Just as Garfiel steeled himself to face his opponent again, Olbart's words knocked the wind out of him before he could start.
Going along with the sensation of having his mind read wasn't a desirable state to be in, not when it stirred the same suspicion that his hand might be read even in combat.
But beside the impatient Garfiel, Roswaal merely shrugged.

"Getting heated is exactly what your opponent wants. In your case, I suppose it could be argued that it clears away your hesitation — but it's no good when impurities mix into that heat. Besides—"

"Besides what?"

"He was talkative to begin with, but hasn't he been throwing out more remarks meant to rattle us? It seems the old gentleman is quite irritated himself."

"...What an unpleasant young pup you are."

As he spoke, the eyes hidden beneath Olbart's long eyebrows took on an irritated gleam.
And hearing Roswaal's remark, Garfiel realized the obvious truth — just as the drawn-out battle was grating on his own nerves, it was grating on his opponent's too.

Olbart's laughing, taunting, mocking wasn't born of composure — it was meant to make them think he had composure, to seize the psychological high ground.
Garfiel, on the other hand, had whatever was in his gut show up immediately on his face, in his voice, even in the fur on his skin.
Which meant in a battle of wits, he'd be played for a fool in no time flat.

"Even so, you can win when you're outmatching your opponent's raw strength, or when your opponent steps onto the same ground as you. But..."

"I know. If some bastard stronger than me only fights on his own turf, then no matter how hard I strain, I ain't gonna see a way to win. But—"

Garfiel's being here now, fighting in the Vollachian Empire, was the fruit of a tangle of coincidental factors — the product, if anything, of misfortune.
But no matter how the dice had rolled, he couldn't deny the reality in front of him.
And from here on, as long as he stood alongside Emilia and Subaru, battlefields to rival this one in the Empire might well keep raining down on him without end.
Which meant—

"—No more whinin'. That's done."

"Ah, yes. Just so. That's exactly right. The end of your boyhood."

At Roswaal's roundabout way of putting it, Garfiel wrinkled his nose.
Having his growth celebrated by this man, having it acknowledged by him — none of that made Garfiel the least bit happy. But he liked the phrase "the end of boyhood."
So that from here on, Garfiel could walk forward as someone who'd shed a layer of himself.

"—Well, well, this is exactly why I hate you young ones. Unlike an old geezer with no more room to grow, you go and have some kinda awakenin' at the drop of a hat."

Tilting his head, shaking the ankle of the leg he'd lifted, Olbart sighed.
And yet, beneath the glib insults of the "Vile Elder," there was genuine, unfeigned hostility to be seen. Yes — hostility. Up until now, Olbart had looked down on Garfiel, treated him as beneath him, aimed only at eliminating him.
This was proof that he'd finally acknowledged Garfiel as an enemy.

"Finally..."

Garfiel's blood stirred at the thought that he'd earned, in earnest, the qualification to cross blades with Olbart.
He drew a long, deep breath through his nose once more and let it out through his mouth. Then, conscious of the blood coursing through his body, of the invisible force within it—

"Hah?"

Suddenly, a vacant-sounding voice cut through Garfiel's concentration.
Looking over, he saw it had come from Olbart, standing across from him — for a moment, he wondered if it was some shinobi trick meant to disturb his composure. But no.
Because, if anything, it was Olbart who had let an opening appear.

"——"

The head of the shinobi. The opening that had appeared was tiny, no bigger than the eye of a needle, but given that the man had shown not the slightest slack until now, Garfiel found himself holding his breath.
What in the world had brought it about——

"—Wait, wait, wait, wait! It's totally unfair to pull out this early, isn't it, what a waste, don't you think, have you no sense of the mood here! You're reading it even worse than me, and that's saying something!!"

A racket of a voice rang out as a figure came tearing across the battlefield, kicking up a cloud of dust.
From quite a distance away, shrieking in a high, piercing voice, was a small boy who looked utterly out of place on a battlefield — except that the speed of his running was anything but ordinary.

Shouting as he ran, the boy looked up at the sky, chasing after some enormous presence beating its wings.
Garfiel understood at a glance that it was the dragon that had descended onto a different, distant battlefield. He understood that much — but the sight of a dragon and a boy playing a game of chase was beyond his understanding.
It's a battlefield where anything can happen, he thought with a kind of resigned admiration — but then—

"...Why's that bastard Ceci so small?"

At the bizarre sight narrowing Garfiel's eyes, a mutter reached his ears.
Olbart, still balanced on one leg with that needle's-eye opening yet unclosed, had his gaze stolen away toward the dragon and the boy — no, toward the shrieking boy.
And then—

"So Cheshi went and pulled one over on me, huh?"

Surprise and suspicion flickered across the old monster's clouded eyes, and then gradually gave way to understanding, and then to anger.
The emotional shift took less than ten seconds, and what train of thought had produced it within Olbart was utterly beyond guessing.
But if this was one of the precious few openings the "Vile Elder" had made in his entire life, there was no way the ill-natured Roswaal would let it slip by.

"—Looking away in front of him just now shows just how badly you're failing to read the times."

Stepping into the needle's-eye opening that had appeared, Roswaal drove the tip of the sai gripped in his hand.
The sudden reaction threw Olbart off; twisting his body, the blow grazed his left shoulder. The chunk of iron, carrying its full speed, struck the old man's shoulder a painful blow, and his small frame, teeth grinding, melted into the ground.
It was the technique of swimming through earth that he'd shown a few times already in this fight.
But——

"Garfiel!"
"I know!"

Roswaal's advice was irritating, but Garfiel had already seen the way to counter it.
Garfiel's own Divine Protection of the Earth Spirits was one that drew mana up through the soles of his feet from the ground he stood on — in plain terms, a connection to the earth itself.
Even against an opponent burrowed beneath the ground, if he concentrated, he should be able to trace where they were—

"—Huh?"

Suddenly, every fine hair standing on end across Garfiel's whole body was seized by a strange sensation.
An abnormally gritty, horrid feeling, like something licking at the back of his neck from below. And it wasn't coming from Olbart in front of him — it was something far bigger, closing in from a far wider range.

"Garfiel?"

Roswaal called out in question at Garfiel, who had gone still.
Not that it was payback for what he'd just done to Olbart, but Garfiel too had let an opening appear. The kind of gap that old monster would surely strike without fail.
And yet, no attack came. If anything—

"The old man's presence... vanished?"

Roswaal muttered, his well-shaped brows furrowing, not releasing his stance with the sai.
Just as he said, the presence that should have been Olbart's had vanished. Of course, the man was a shinobi — he'd surely know any number of ways to hide his presence.
But that wasn't it. Olbart's presence, burrowed beneath the ground, had grown distant.
Receding from Garfiel and the others at a furious speed, and then—

"What is this feeling..."

"Garfiel, the old man—"

"Somethin' weird, somethin' sick, like... like 'the wind of Aicher rots the water,' or somethin' like that..."

Roswaal, wary of Olbart, wasn't sharing Garfiel's sense of crisis.
If Roswaal couldn't sense it, then whatever this shift was, it wasn't mana. It was something huge, enormous, closing in from beneath Garfiel's feet.

——It was the sensation that Garfiel, bearer of the Divine Protection of the Earth Spirits, was the first to notice.

"The ground of the battlefield... no, the earth of Vollachia itself, is it... goin' wild?"

Garfiel could hear it — the horrid, too-vast scream of the land.
His anger at having the fight interrupted, at being robbed of his chance to break through one more wall, dissipated before the crushing weight of what bore down on him.

"Roswaal! Fly, now, and tell everyone!"

"——"

At Garfiel's call — forgetting the false name he'd been using in the Empire — Roswaal, perhaps judging urgency more important, said nothing in objection.
Being shown consideration by someone he despised, Garfiel shouted with all the desperation he felt.
And what he shouted was——

"—All of Vollachia is about to turn into the enemy!"

——Lamia Godwin was one of the daughters of Drizen Vollachia, the seventy-sixth emperor of the Vollachian Empire, and an imperial princess who had fought against Vincent Abelux and Priscilla Benedict in the Imperial Selection Ceremony, and lost, and died.

In the Imperial Selection Ceremony, where siblings were forced to kill one another until only one remained, she had been the rival candidate who came closest to cornering Vincent — seen as the frontrunner for the throne — by forming an alliance with her other siblings and making full use of scheming and elaborate traps.
But the plan had failed to overturn the initial odds, the alliance had collapsed, and in the end she had faced off against Priscilla, a sister she'd mutually despised, and lost her life.

Given her age and caliber at the time, it was said among those involved in the Imperial Selection Ceremony that had it not been for Vincent, the throne would have gone to her.
Still, a loss was a loss. Before that irreversible reality, Lamia's body had been burned to ash by the flames of the Sword of the Sun. That, too, should have been an irreversible reality.
And yet——

"Lamia-denka, why..."

Berstetz's eyes, normally narrow as though perpetually closed, opened wide enough to confirm that their color was indeed gray, and his face showed unmistakable shock at the figure before him.
In Berstetz's stunned field of view, seated on the throne as though bearing the weight of the national flag fluttering in the wind behind her, was none other than the vividly remembered face of his former mistress, Lamia Godwin.

Save that her bloodless, pale face was cracked, and her once-beautiful crimson eyes had transformed into something eerie — black darkness with a golden light floating within.

"Berstetz, did you not hear my question? Who won the Imperial Selection Ceremony? My brother Vincent? Priscilla? You're surely not going to tell me it was my brother Palladio? If anyone besides those two competed after I fell, that would be a nightmare~."

"...After you fell, denka, it was Vincent Abelux-denka who took the throne. And he has ruled to this day — nine years on it."

"But he's dead. Is that the answer, lying there on the floor?"

Cutting off Berstetz's words, Lamia, chin propped on her hand, looked toward the corpse lying fallen. From her position she couldn't see the face, but between siblings who shared blood, one could tell.
Strictly speaking, the fallen "Vincent" was not the real Vincent, but Chisha Gold in disguise, by Berstetz's estimation — but either way.

"If I may be so bold, Lamia-denka, there is something I would ask."

"What is it, Berstetz? You were my faithful right hand, weren't you~. If you have something to ask, I'll answer it."

"Why have you returned? I would add, that throne is one no one but the Vollachian emperor is permitted to sit upon. —You, denka, have no such qualification."

Knowing full well it would incur his mistress's displeasure as disrespect, Berstetz said it anyway.
There had been days, once, when he had served her, when he'd devoted everything to raising her up as the one fit to sit upon the Vollachian throne. Seeing her, after all this time, actually seated there on the emperor's throne like this, something welled up within him.
——Deep in his chest, an unbearable revulsion and fury came bubbling up.

"A wise decision, denka. We have lost."

Hand upon his chest, bowing with reverence, Berstetz offered counsel to Lamia just as he once had.
Still young, but beautiful and clever, Lamia back then had listened to Berstetz's suggestions, taken them seriously, considered and digested them, and had the suppleness of mind to seek out the right answer.
That virtue of Lamia's — like a cunning, poisonous flower——

"How pitiful, Berstetz. No matter how much you love and serve it, this nation will never answer you back, will it."

Head tilted, her expression as unchanging as if frozen, Lamia gave her reply, and at once Berstetz moved.
He turned the hand on his chest toward Lamia, and the ring on his finger shone with an eerie light.
It was the Meteor granted to Berstetz as proof of his office as chancellor.

"Prepare yourself——!"

In answer to Berstetz's will, a fireball was spat forth from the glowing jewel of the ring.
A device that released stored mana as magic, one Vincent himself had also possessed under the pretext of self-defense — and now it struck at the master to whom Berstetz had once sworn loyalty — no, at her likeness.
Just as it seemed the defenseless Lamia on the throne would be swallowed by the fireball——

"How foolish. Fire won't work on Vollachian imperial blood, fire won't."

In the next instant, a dazzling red light lit the throne room, and the fireball was cleaved diagonally in two. Carried by its own momentum, it passed by Lamia, past the throne too, and crashed into the wall from which the national flag hung, bursting into flame.
Left unchecked it would catch the flag and threaten to engulf the Crystal Palace in fire, but Berstetz's attention was drawn not to the Crystal Palace's future, but to what stood before it.

In the hand of Lamia, risen from the throne, blazed a brilliant, glowing sacred sword.
Unmistakable — it was the Empire's proudest treasure——

"—The Sword of the Sun, Vollachia."

"Of course any Vollachian royal would carry it~?"

Berstetz's eyes widened once more, opened as far as an ordinary person's half-lidded gaze, and in them he saw Lamia, blazing sword of the sun in hand, leap toward him.
Rearing back, Lamia's strike bore down on him. If that dazzling crimson wasn't merely a fake resembling the real thing in looks alone, Berstetz's very existence would be scorched away, leaving not even ash.

He had to move. But he couldn't.
He hadn't trained for such things, and more than anything, his eyes were captured by that brilliance.
The brilliance of the Sword of the Sun, symbol of the Vollachian Empire——.

"Well then, farewell, Berste—"

The slash was about to fall upon Berstetz, and the life of the treacherous minister who'd played the loyal vassal while shaking the Empire to its foundations was about to end — it was the very moment his own understanding caught up to that fact.

"Nnnghhh!!"

The great doors of the throne room were smashed open from outside, and something came flying in from beyond them at a tremendous speed. Moving with not the slightest hesitation, it collided head-on with Lamia — who had been swinging her blade at Berstetz — and sent the princess's slender body flying with a "puh."

"Wha—"

As Berstetz watched, the sense of imminent death receding, he saw that what had struck Lamia was a thrown battle-axe. It was one of the weapons held by the suits of armor lined up in the corridor of the Crystal Palace before the throne room — a gleaming piece adorned with ceremonial decoration.
Still, since it was Imperial custom never to display a weapon that couldn't actually be used, that battle-axe too had always been kept in fighting trim, ready to be wielded on a battlefield at a moment's notice.
And it had met Lamia head-on and sent her flying with tremendous force to the back of the throne room.

"Who..."

Wondering who had thrown the axe, Berstetz turned to check — but before he could, heavy footsteps came rushing closer, and—

"Treacherous minister, Berstetz Fondalfon! I was against keeping the likes of you as chancellor from the very start!!"

Grabbed by the collar in a huge hand and lifted off his feet, Berstetz found himself face to face with a bearded giant of a man. A voice as huge as his frame, and a face as fittingly rugged as though it had been forged to order.
His bared upper body was armored in muscle honed to a hard edge, and though he should have been imprisoned for over a month, there wasn't the slightest dimming of his vigor——

"Goz Ralfon-ichishou..."

"You will face judgment in a public forum! Naturally, Chisha-ichishou too, who planned and carried out this treason, shares equal guilt! Whatever his true intentions, the crime of deceiving denka and his allies is a grave one!"

"——"

"To begin with! Do not underestimate us! Underestimate the officers and men! No matter what hardship or trial comes, we will smash even a 'Great Disaster' with our combined strength!!"

The huge man, Goz Ralfon, declared it all at tremendous volume.
One of the Empire's Nine Divine Generals, holding the rank of Five, he was — thanks to his high loyalty and unfortunate timing — the person who, after Vincent, had suffered the most from Berstetz and Chisha's scheme.
Still, since his interference had ended up helping Vincent escape the capital, Berstetz hadn't had room to go easy on him for the sake of his own goals.

Each side pouring everything into their own aims, one winning and one losing — that was the way of the world, and the Imperial way. So there was no sense of apology on that point whatsoever.

"How did you come to be here... You should have been chained in the basement of my own mansion."

"I was rescued by a brave girl! A woman of strong heart... to think that keeping her in that mansion, same as me, was the ruin of my luck! She is truly a woman the Vollachian Empire should be proud of!"

"...I see. So it was her."

What rose in Berstetz's mind was the blue-haired girl he'd held captive in his mansion.
A precious wielder of healing magic, and by Madelyn's account a girl of the Oni Tribe, she had been intended, both in ability and lineage, as a candidate for imperial consort.
He'd rated her as strong-willed and full of vitality, but it seemed even that had been an underestimate.

Now that the collapse of the plan had become plain, Berstetz had no intention of dwelling on it further.
Still, what concerned him behind the truth of Goz's appearance here was——

"Goz-ichishou, do you already know of the 'Great Disaster' as well?"

"I have not heard the details! But I was told only that it would occur at the cost of denka's life! I must hear the rest from Chisha-ichishou, along with that... but more urgent than that is denka's own safety! Where have you taken denka..."

Goz declared his own intentions to Berstetz, needlessly loud.
Inwardly, Berstetz found himself both admiring and resenting that Chisha had kept the matter of the "Great Disaster" — which had apparently even reached Goz's ears — completely hidden from him. But that was as far as it went.
Mid-sentence, Goz noticed something, and his eyes went wide.
And then——

"N-no... de- denka... denka-!!"

Letting out a scream, Goz collapsed to his knees on the spot. Thrown off by the force of it, Berstetz landed hard on his backside on the floor, but Goz paid it no mind, clinging instead to the corpse before him — crumpled on the red carpet, never to move again.

From Goz's wide-open eyes, tears streamed down in torrents, soaking into his rugged beard. Weighing his beard down with those tears, Goz pressed his great fist hard against the carpet.

"Denka...! This Goz Ralfon, arriving far too late...! What folly! What an outrage! For this foolishness, there's nothing left but to atone with my death...!"

"G- Goz-ichishou! Please, calm yourself! That person is..."

"How could I possibly be calm!! Damn you, Chancellor Berstetz! Are you satisfied with this!? To have taken denka's life, to have brought ruin upon this very Empire—"

"The one who has died is Chisha-ichishou!"

Berstetz raised his own voice to silence Goz, who had erupted at the sight of the emperor's corpse pierced through the chest.
At the unusually loud voice from Berstetz, rarely raised even by himself, Goz's eyes went wide, and he looked closely at the fallen body once more.

"This is Chisha-ichishou...? Impossible! If so, then what happened here!?"

"I myself do not know the full particulars. Only that Chisha-ichishou died in denka's form, and that some unprecedented event... one connected, likely, to the 'Great Disaster,' has occurred."

Calming the emotionally frantic Goz, Berstetz sorted through his own thoughts as well.
Yes — likely this death as emperor, and the treason committed in league with Berstetz, were both one form or another of the scheme Chisha Gold had spun.
And that was surely not unrelated to the phenomenon of the "Great Disaster" that had come up again and again.
And if there was anyone who knew the details of that "Great Disaster"——

"—What a rude welcome, all of a sudden. I've never been treated like this, not once since I was born, and not once since I died either~."

"——"

From deeper in the room, a moist, sultry voice called out.
It was Lamia — struck and sent flying by the fierce blow of the battle-axe Goz had hurled as he burst in, whom they'd assumed had been utterly finished off.

Let it be said first — it wasn't that Berstetz had forgotten her existence.
There had been the noisy exchange with Goz afterward, true, but the reason Berstetz hadn't mentioned her was that the force of that axe had clearly been enough to take a life.

Goz Ralfon was beloved by many officers and men, a "General" skilled at commanding large armies second only to Chisha Gold, but his individual combat prowess, compared to Chisha's, was outstanding in its own right.
If Goz had thrown that attack meaning to strike his opponent down, then an ordinary person's body, receiving it, ought to have been torn to pieces with barely a graze.

But Lamia hadn't ended up that way. —No, to be precise, that wasn't quite right either.

"Well, this body... seems it doesn't feel pain or anything, lucky me~."

As she spoke, Lamia rose to her feet, tilting her head while slowly knitting back together her right side, shattered like porcelain.
Not a joke, not a figure of speech — she was doing it, literally. No blood flowed from the wound, and the way the shattered parts writhed and fit themselves back together gave the impression of ice forming over a lake's surface.
But it wasn't merely that a person who should have died had been revived and was moving about.

"Chancellor Berstetz, unless my eyes deceive me, that person is..."

"...Lamia Godwin-denka. Who died nine years ago, at the time of the Imperial Selection Ceremony."

"Why is a princess-denka who should have died in the Imperial Selection Ceremony carrying on like that!? And what's more, I've gone and attacked Lamia-denka!? Don't tell me denka the Emperor kept his little sister alive all this time!?"

"There is no possibility of that. Emperor Vincent Vollachia-denka is a man wholly unconnected to such sentiment. That uncanny appearance is a matter concerning Lamia-denka herself."

Correcting Goz's reflexive, rash conclusion, Berstetz fixed his gaze on Lamia, still in the process of restoring her body, and made that judgment.
If possible, he wanted to draw out the conversation with Lamia and gather more information.

"It was I myself, none other, who closed the window for negotiation."

"Yes, that's so~. And here I thought I was speaking to you quite properly. Whatever happened to you?"

"Well now — perhaps, having faced betrayal by an ally in the last great gambit of my life, I lost myself to despair. Even I am surprised to find I had such a side to me."

"This is no time for idle talk!! Lamia-denka! I am Goz Ralfon, holding the rank of ichishou under the reign of Emperor Vincent Vollachia-denka! Will you submit quietly and be bound!"

Given that he'd struck the first blow, there was little chance she'd lend an ear even if she said she wanted to talk.
Toward Lamia, who affirmed as much, Goz now took a great step forward. Even weaponless, having released the battle-axe, he still had those two arms of his, thick as logs.
Even with the power of the Sword of the Sun and the physical enhancement that came with it, Lamia's own strength shouldn't be enough to stop Goz.
Surely Lamia wasn't unaware of that gap in raw strength between them——

"No thank you, none of that. To wake up and be constrained again, I couldn't bear it~."

"Then, by force..."

"—Besides, hm?"

Just as Goz, seeing she wouldn't listen, made to take an even greater step forward, that was the moment.
Lamia's eerily glowing golden eyes wavered, and the situation changed. — Shadows bearing the same black darkness and gold as Lamia's own began rising up one after another around her.

"What in the—!?"

Goz cried out in astonishment, and Berstetz too found his throat closing without a sound.
Rising as though shadows themselves were lifting up from the ground, figures appeared bearing the same eyes as Lamia, the same cracked, colorless skin, the same eerie visage.
That alone was astonishing enough, but the shock for Berstetz and Goz didn't end there.

That every single one of those who had risen was a familiar face — and——

"—I simply can't help but pity you, clinging as you are to an Empire doomed to fall anyway~."

So saying, Lamia raised the Sword of the Sun, and around her, more than twenty figures, all likewise raising their hands to the sky, drew forth sacred swords blazing crimson, all of them — every one — members of the imperial family, qualified to withstand its flame.

——The battle between Moguro Hagane of the Eight, and the new and old Nines, Madelyn Eschart and Balleroy Temeglyph, continued fiercely still, beside the Crystal Palace.

The extraordinary battle unfolding at such close range was a clash between a giant humanoid castle wall and a dragon wreathed in cloud, and with the Empire's greatest Flying Dragon Rider joining in, it was a fight for the ages, one that would remain in imperial history.
The roaring thunder and rumbling earth were the very screams of the Vollachian Empire itself, and the water spilling from the shattered reservoir surged into the capital as a raging torrent.

Truly, the imperial capital Lupugana — no, the Sacred Vollachia Empire itself — had been driven into an unprecedented, tumultuous state that foretold its own ruin.
And yet, in spite of that——

"Denka, or perhaps denka. Either way, the 'Great Disaster' is coming. Won't you resist the 'Great Disaster' together with me!"

Arms spread wide, face split in a grin, utterly unconcerned with the crumbling cityscape of the capital, the "Stargazer's" figure was beyond out of place — it was like something out of a nightmare, casually trampling underfoot any understanding an ordinary person might have.
But sadly, the one made to face that unhinged, deranged smile of the "Stargazer" was the person in this Empire least permitted to be an ordinary person.

"——"

For an instant, Abel's eyes flicked toward the hidden mechanism behind the miracle that had just saved him.
What had saved Abel from his fall from a great height was a sheet stretched taut at the point of impact, an improvised cushion gathered from the various rooms of the Crystal Palace and the surrounding buildings.
The mechanism by which he'd been saved made sense, given that. The question was why such a thing had been prepared at all.
Unless someone — no, unless Abel's falling had been known in advance, such preparations couldn't have been made.

"You. How much did you know."

The instant he reached that conclusion, Abel's hand shot out and seized the smirking Ubilk by the collar. Yanked forcibly closer, Ubilk stumbled with a "whoa-whoa-whoa," eyes widening.
Face to face at close range, Abel glared harshly at him and said—

"Chisha's scheme... no, that's not it. How much did you know about whose death would bring about the arrival of the 'Great Disaster'? You knew my death would—"

"Yes, well, I said it just so. —That the death of Vincent Vollachia would be the beginning of the 'Great Disaster' that brings ruin upon the Empire."

Glared at from a distance close enough to feel his breath, Ubilk's composed manner didn't crack. Perhaps, in fact, this wasn't composure at all. It was something else, something more unsettling — something you might call anticipation, or exhilaration.
In fact, Ubilk was exhilarated. As though the long-awaited moment had finally arrived.
But——

"Vincent Vollachia is me."

"No, no! Denka! That's incorrect. Thaaat's incorrect. Allow me to say it loudly and clearly, that's incorrect, denka."

"——"

"Chisha Gold-ichishou undeniably fulfilled the role of Vincent Vollachia to the very end. It was you yourself, denka, none other, who shaped Chisha-ichishou so that he could do so."

"I..."

That hadn't been his purpose in keeping Chisha Gold at his side all this time.
He had thought that when the coming "Great Disaster" arrived, in place of a powerless emperor who couldn't be present for it, he would need someone capable of managing the whole of the Empire.
Someone to cooperate with the fitting successor who would inherit the throne after his own death, and protect the Empire from ruin.

"Why did you take part in this scheme."

"Hm?"

"You, a 'Stargazer,' should have no interest in anything but the fulfillment and execution of the heavenly mandate. If so, if you wanted to bring about the 'Great Disaster' with certainty, you should have used my life. Why did you choose to gamble on Chisha's uncertain scheme instead. It makes no sense."

A "Stargazer" was a puppet of heavenly mandate wearing a human form.
No matter how personable a figure it affected, no matter how devoted an attitude it wore, what lay within was nothing but an obsession with heavenly mandate, and the fanatical desire to see it fulfilled.
The order of priorities should have been obvious. So why had he thrown his weight behind Chisha's scheme?

"Why. Answer me."

"Hmm, well, I do think denka might get angry if I say this."

"Say it."

"Well, whether it was you yourself, denka, or Chisha-ichishou, it made no difference to me, really. If it happened, good. If it didn't, bad. If Chisha-ichishou dying in your form, denka, didn't bring about the 'Great Disaster,' then at that point I'd have used you yourself, denka..."

Sticking out his tongue mischievously, Ubilk drew a quiet understanding from Abel. And at the same time, he understood there was no point in speaking with Ubilk any further.
Releasing the collar he'd been gripping, Abel slowly turned around. Reflected in his sight through his demon mask were several figures holding the edges of the sheet that had saved him.
None of them faces he'd seen before in the Crystal Palace, but——

"These people too, are they your faction?"

"Not quite enough of them to call a faction, are there?"

Shrugging, Ubilk stepped around and spread his arms before his fellows. Roughly a dozen or so, men and women, young and old gathered without distinction — surely, like him, "Stargazers."
No matter how many were culled, more appeared. Since there was no telling how the Observers' agents were chosen, many were left be even once their existence was known.

"As I told you, we act according to heavenly mandate. What will you do, denka?"

"I—"

Even as he pondered what he ought to do, Abel tried to put his thoughts into words.
But partway through answering Ubilk, an especially large shock shook the ground, and with a crash, a piece of Moguro's body fell some distance away from Abel and the others.
The fierce battle continued still, but with two against one, Moguro's disadvantage was undeniable.

The resurrection of Balleroy Temeglyph, who should have been dead, and his beloved dragon.
The worst possibility drawn from that was that it wouldn't stop with just the two of them.

"The chaos in the capital, the crisis facing the Empire, an event that overturns the natural order — this is precisely a manifestation of the 'Great Disaster.'"

Beside Abel, shielding his face from the swirling dust, Ubilk jutted his chin toward the sky.
As the revived dead and the dragon rampaged, foreboding damage that surely wouldn't end there, he went on—

"The Kingdom's 'Witch,' the city-state's 'Night Weeping,' the Holy Kingdom's 'Collapse.' And the Empire's 'Great Disaster'... the four disasters that would destroy the world — that moment is drawing near. Even now."

"——"

"Ah, and denka, this is just my own personal opinion, but—"

As though voicing something he'd forgotten to say, Ubilk clapped his hands with deliberate theatricality. Then the "Stargazer" pointed overhead, toward Moguro and the others' fight — no, that wasn't it.
What Ubilk pointed at wasn't the battle, but the Crystal Palace, shaking from the aftershocks of the fierce fight — specifically, the throne room, with its great gaping hole.

"You were asking why I cooperated with Chisha-ichishou's scheme, weren't you. It's true that either way was fine with me, but... I thought the odds were higher with you, denka."

Saying so with brazen good humor and a laugh, Ubilk lowered the hand he'd pointed at the Crystal Palace, and in the same motion turned that finger toward Abel.

"Me, I prioritize the fulfillment of heavenly mandate above all else. My goal is for the 'Great Disaster' to occur, and for the ruin it brings to be stopped. —My priorities haven't wavered one bit."

A "Stargazer" was a living doll obsessed with the fulfillment and execution of heavenly mandate, bound to that purpose.
That understanding hadn't changed. And without changing that understanding, Ubilk presented the reason behind his actions, the grounds for his choice, the future he sought.
That in order to save the Vollachian Empire from the "Great Disaster," he had prioritized Abel.

"—Chisha Gold, you..."

Into himself, colorless as he was, he had soaked in the color of a man named Vincent Vollachia — what future, what hope, had he pictured behind those false black eyes he'd worn?
Why hadn't he understood that words like hope and expectation were nothing but a worthless escape from reality?

"—Oh my."

Just as Abel put his hand to his face, gripping the cheek of his demon mask, a sudden roaring explosion echoed in his ear. Reacting to it, Ubilk let out a vacant sound, and then a heavy thud landed nearby.
Looking over, Abel saw something had fallen onto the same sheet that had caught him earlier. This time, though, unlike with Abel, the sheet hadn't been stretched out to catch anyone, and it failed to serve as a cushion.
And yet——

"Good thing the sheets were laid out! Chancellor Berstetz! Are you alive!"

"It was hard on these old bones, but somehow... hm."

Shaking off the sheet that had fallen over them, revealed beneath it were two figures — a bare-chested giant of a man, and the white-haired old man cradled in his arms.
Both faces Abel recognized, though he'd never imagined running into them here.

"Well, well, if it isn't Goz-ichishou. And Chancellor Berstetz safe as well, I see."

"And you, I thought you'd fled — and yet here you still are..."

"You! The 'Stargazer' who whispered needless things into Chisha-ichishou's and Chancellor Berstetz's ears! Because of you, denka... denka is...!!"

The huge man — Goz Ralfon, who apparently had leapt down from the great hole in the Crystal Palace above — closed in on Ubilk, Chancellor Berstetz still cradled in his arms.
Now that Chisha's aim was clear, Abel had assumed Goz hadn't been killed, but what chain of events had led to his appearing here, in this state?
And how deeply had Berstetz himself been in league with Chisha — that too remained unclear.

"All the chaos in the capital, everything! Is it all the result of your machinations, working in the shadows!? Die and atone for it!"

"Wait, wait, hold on just a moment! I haven't done anything on that grand a scale, none of it has anything to do with what happened to Goz-ichishou! I mean, look, as far as heavenly mandate goes, what happens to Goz-ichishou isn't really something worth worrying about..."

"Damn you, you—!"

Running his mouth needlessly, Ubilk only further stoked Goz's fury. Goz, apparently forgetting he was still carrying him, had Berstetz twisting painfully in his grip.
And what's more, the shifting situation didn't stop there——

"Whoaaa!? The closer I get, the more mysterious this gets! I come running after the dragon that stood me up, and find a giant doll standing in for him instead!? Sure, it might look impressive, but I don't think it can fill in for me, hm!"

"——"

Just as a high, yapping voice reached their ears, in the next instant its owner appeared on the spot, wrapped in a tremendous wind, kicking up a cloud of dust.
Sliding the soles of his zōri sandals, shading his eyes with one hand as he looked up at the sky, was a blue-haired boy in a wasō, who'd come running though no one had called him — and at his boisterous manner and speech, and above all at the sight of a familiar figure simply shrunk down in size, everyone's eyes went wide.

"C- Cecilus Segmunt—!?"

"Huh? Did someone call my name just now? Well now, have I really done something to spread my name that far with lightning speed — how embarrassing! Honestly, I'd hoped my finest pose would be the moment I cut down that dragon, but I can't help resenting my own star quality!"

"...No mistake, it's Cecilus-ichishou!"

The boy, who'd been watching the battle between Moguro and the "Cloud Dragon," turned around and spoke those words with that unmistakable, singular sense of distance from everyone else.
There he stood, without a doubt the strongest of the Nine Divine Generals, the "One" who stood at the summit of martial prowess in the Vollachian Empire — Cecilus Segmunt, in a child's form.
It was just about the age Cecilus had been when Abel first picked him up and brought him in as a subordinate. After that, whatever might be said of the inside, the outside at least should have grown up.

"So then, you — why are you small. ...I can only think this is Olbart Dunkelkenn's doing, but if so—"

A contradiction arose with Olbart's behavior when they'd encountered him in the demonic capital Chaosflame.
Olbart apparently hadn't known that Chisha was disguised as the false emperor. But if Olbart had cast the same technique on Cecilus that he had on Natsuki Subaru and Medium and the others, then his actions there would have to be questioned.
Why would the emperor have made Cecilus small before himself?
He couldn't imagine there being any rational explanation for it. Cecilus had spent over ten years without any correction whatsoever. There was no leash left to put on him now, not at this stage.
Which meant——

"You're pretty darn small, but you sure run fast, you. Gettin' left in the dust like this, I'm really startin' to feel my body givin' out on me."

The moment the thought crossed his mind, the small figure of the shinobi — Olbart Dunkelkenn — appeared on the spot once more. He should have been entrusted with defending the top of the wall protecting the capital, and yet he'd abandoned that duty to come back to the Crystal Palace.
As for why the head of the shinobi, Olbart, had done that—

"Ohh-ho, thought I'd shaken you off, but guess I didn't after all! Impressive, old man! Still wanting a stage role at your age, what an admirable spirit! Well done!"

"Quit yer yappin'. Judgin' by that, I'd wager you've gone and clean forgotten this and that, haven't you."

"Clean forgotten? Hm, I haven't the faintest idea what you're talking about."

"It weren't me that did it, so if Ceci's small, it musta been that Cheshi bastard's doin'. I don't mind stealin' other folks' techniques, but I sure don't like havin' mine stolen."

"Ahahaha, what an incredibly self-serving argument! But I don't dislike it — in fact, I rather like it."

The old man twisted his lips in displeasure, and the young Cecilus — barely taller than him — laughed at it.
All the while, the "Stargazer" Ubilk was still being pressed by Goz, and Berstetz, held captive in those thick arms, kept anxiously watching to see whether the aftershocks of the continuing battle between Moguro, the "Cloud Dragon," and the resurrected Balleroy nearby would come crashing down on them.

"...What is this."

One after another, they kept gathering here.
Before he knew it, more than half of the Vollachian Empire's Nine Divine Generals had come to this very spot.

Was this Chisha's doing too?
Chisha Gold — whom he'd shaped to be able to do everything Vincent Vollachia could do — just how far had he outstripped Abel's own expectations?
Staking his own life to keep Abel alive, and beyond that, what had he——



"—I'm afraid that isn't something I'm able to do."



"——"

Once, in answer to Vincent Abelux's question, Chisha Gold had said just that.
Asked whether he could die for him, he'd answered, straight to his face, that he could not.
If so, then what Chisha had thrown his life away for wasn't for the sake of Vincent Abelux.

Of course, it wasn't for Abel's sake either, nor for Vincent Vollachia's. Not through some wordplay like that — what Chisha had thrown his life away for was——

"—Listen! The 'Great Disaster' is coming, starting now! From this moment on, follow my orders!"

Grinding his back teeth, Abel raised his face, leapt atop a pile of rubble, and turned to face them. Sweeping his gaze over the gathered faces all at once, he spoke in a voice meant to be heard by everyone present.
At Abel's declaration, those who'd each been acting on their own turned their eyes toward him.

In that instant, in the eyes of the Nine Divine Generals present — all but Ubilk and his fellows, and Berstetz, who already knew the circumstances — flickered a trace of doubt: just who, exactly, was this man?
Sensing it, Abel reached a hand to his own demon mask and gripped the cheek. He tore away the thing Chisha had placed upon him in his final moment, and revealed his true face.
And then——


"I am your emperor — Vincent Vollachia. —One of the Empire's sword wolves."
