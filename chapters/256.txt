Arc 4 Chapter 88: "Garfiel's Intentions"

"So, did you manage to get things as far as concluding a Contract?"

Having thrown down his final challenge and stormed out of the building after giving Roswaal a piece of his mind, Subaru had walked a while before meeting up with Otto, and now the two of them were in a corner of the great cathedral, reporting to each other on their respective results.

At Otto's question, Subaru nodded.

"Yeah, it went well. My only worry was that he'd gone completely self-destructive and wouldn't take the bet... but it seems like the fact that the terms were stacked against me is what got him over that hump."

"I suppose so. Being challenged that one-sidedly, with terms all in his favor... and on top of that, having a blueprint for victory laid out for him — turning it down would've been strange. Of course, there was also the possibility that Natsuki-san's persuasiveness would've been so overwhelming that it wouldn't even get as far as a Contract."

"But you weren't actually considering the possibility that it wouldn't get as far as a Contract at all, were you?"

"No, that's right."

Otto affirmed this as though it were only natural, arms crossed. The way Otto had, if only for a moment, managed to play Roswaal like a puppet — Subaru couldn't help but widen his eyes at it.

Otto gave a wry smile at Subaru's reaction.

"When exchanging a pledge with terms above a certain threshold, merchants use written documents... but experienced mages more often use Contracts. And with an opponent who's the nation's foremost authority on Magic, no less... I figured the odds were nine or ten in ten that I could draw him into one."

"By the way, with a Contract, is it possible to secretly plant different content into it? Like, he says one thing out loud, but actually you end up bound to some other Contract entirely?"

If that were the case, Subaru had no way of confirming it. It had been a Contract whose terms would change the internal arrangement of their alliance going forward depending on who won the bet — but in reality, if it had instead been something like *you'll die a day from now as long as you keep breathing*, Subaru wouldn't have known the difference.

Subaru shuddered at his own imagination, but Otto waved a hand.

"That kind of trickery doesn't work. Didn't Count Roswaal say as much when you concluded the Contract? That it's something carved into the Soul. A Contract is only formed once both parties have consented and agreed to its contents. Unless the other party is extraordinarily silver-tongued, you can't be bound to a Contract that differs from what you understood it to be."

"The way you put that 'unless extraordinarily silver-tongued' part makes it sound like there are exceptions where someone gets tricked by clever words."

"...Just between us, apparently before the laws around this were properly established, back when society as a whole was a lot more chaotic, fraud using Contracts was rampant. It's fine if the other party is some petty crook, but if you catch the eye of some ill-tempered, formidable mage, I don't even want to think about the damage that could do."

Otto hunched his shoulders as if to say *scary, scary*, and Subaru shrugged in agreement.

On that front, Roswaal was probably a passing grade as someone to conclude a Contract with. There was no doubt he was formidable and ill-tempered, but his obsession with his goal and his methods were, in a straightforward way, purely and simply evil. It was safe to assume he had no intention of abusing the Contract to bring about a decisive falling-out with Subaru.

If he were to try to deceive Subaru through such means — with the right to repeat things over and over without end, Subaru would no doubt utterly and completely destroy Roswaal's heart, beyond any hope of repair.

For both their sakes, Subaru wanted to hold onto at least that minimal amount of trust — that such a thing wouldn't happen.

"Anyway, that's how things went on my end. How about yours?"

"How, indeed. Honestly, I can't really say I got a solid handle on it — that's the truth. To begin with, it was closer to a gamble. ...And I doubt I'll get all that many more chances, either."

While Subaru had been negotiating with Roswaal, Otto had been handling a different role, and his expression wasn't bright. Just as he said, the task Subaru had asked of Otto carried a gamble element that was far more likely to fail than succeed. In exchange, if it went well, it would bring them significantly closer to a winning condition.

The problem was not knowing how many more chances there'd be within the remaining five days.

"We'll just have to watch how that develops. Well then, shall we go over again what needs to be done within the limited time we have?"

"The urgent matters are Emilia-sama and Garfiel. Especially when it comes to Emilia-sama clearing the 'Trial' — there's nothing I can do to help with that. It's more or less down to you, Natsuki-san."

"That's the thing... I think it's admirable that Emilia — that girl — wants to do it herself, and now that I've lost my qualification, I can't take her place anyway, but..."

Even so, if Emilia kept challenging the 'Trial' the same way she had been, the odds of her clearing it by the fifth day had to be called slim. As long as Subaru didn't leave the 'Sanctuary,' he didn't think things would reach the point of Emilia's heart breaking — but he doubted they could avoid her wearing down from repeated failure.

She needed some kind of change — and one that came from Subaru's side, at that.

"—I guess it's about time I really faced it head-on, seriously."

"............"

"It's the price for putting things off, over and over. I think the reason I kept running from asking her about it is that I didn't want to admit there's something between me and Emilia that can't be filled in. Taking advantage of the fact that Emilia hasn't brought it up, and I bet Emilia's doing the same, taking advantage of the fact that I haven't tried to ask."

"—I think talking to each other, letting each other hear what's in your hearts, is important. Even I — if I just kept going along with you with a shrug and a grin, Natsuki-san, who knows what would happen five days from now."

Otto said it as if to comfort Subaru in his self-reproach, and Subaru scratched his cheek and thanked him silently. There was something he was quietly grateful for in the consideration Otto showed by not blaming him for the way his weaker self had kept looking away.

"I mean, honestly, if you two can't even manage that much reaching-out toward each other, then what exactly is all this 'falling for each other' business? It's laughable. Please, quit playing at being so pure and innocent."

"—! Hey, you—"

Both of them ended up more embarrassed than anything, and Subaru twisted his mouth at Otto's rapid-fire teasing. But he couldn't talk back, precisely because Otto was right.

Subaru's fundamentally spineless disposition kept producing results that fell one step short, in all kinds of situations.

But running away like that — that, too, was surely reaching its limit by now.

"With Emilia, there's a spontaneous element to it, but I'll manage to open up somehow. I don't think I can't do something I was able to do before."

"...Well, I'll trust your judgment on that one. Though I'd really rather not have things drag on with us still stuck in the 'Sanctuary' until the fifth day, when the Great Rabbit arrives."

Otto looked as though he wanted to say something to Subaru's words, but he sealed it away, merely averting his gaze. Then he changed the subject, saying, "Well then, on to the other matter," and continued.

"As for Garfiel — how do you intend to approach him?"

"The hint is that he's 'afraid of the outside.' I think it's connected to the 'Trial' he took inside the tomb, and the past he saw there. If we could find out the details of that somehow..."

"We can hardly go ask him directly. I doubt he's mild-mannered enough to have a wound in his heart gouged into directly and just let it slide without a word."

"If he loses his temper and takes a swing at us, both our necks would snap in one hit. Man, this really is a partnership with nothing but worries on the military-strength front..."

Subaru was a complete amateur through and through, of course, and Otto was, at best, a traveling merchant used to the occasional scuffle. Neither of them was anywhere close to being called a fighter, and in the military hierarchy within the 'Sanctuary,' they'd probably both be quicker to find themselves counted from the bottom than the top.

"Thinking about it that way too, we really do need someone in a purely military-officer type position. With Roswaal, Emilia, and Ram, and Frederica thrown in too, I guess — having that be the entire roster of people who can actually fight is way too much of a problem for a faction. Garfiel is necessary for our camp."

That he hadn't counted Rem — was that because her current state was a result of that battle? Even knowing that it was Subaru's own selfishness not to want to put her on a battlefield again, when he'd already decided he would get her back one day.

"We need to talk Garfiel around with some smooth talking and clever maneuvering, and bring him into the fold. And for that, we need to clear away his trauma first. The missing piece for that is..."

"The missing part is...?"

"If we can't ask the man himself, we've got no choice but to pull it from somewhere else. Ryuzu-san, or Ram. Both of them seem like they'd have pretty tight lips about it, though."

Which of the two of them those two would side with, if Subaru and Roswaal came into conflict — that was worth confirming too, so it'd be worth a try either way.

Subaru wolfed down his breakfast, brushed off his backside, and stood up, cracking his neck.

"Alright, guess it's time to get moving. I'll start by trying Ryuzu-san..."

"I'll handle that arrangement of ours. I'll let the others know in advance, but it'd be best if we could avoid actually having to use it, if at all possible."

"I don't really want to think about it much either. I don't want to, but... one way or another, it feels like there's a seventy, eighty percent chance we'll end up relying on it..."

"That's high... but I can't deny it."

They looked at each other, let out a sigh, and Subaru shook his head as if to shake himself back into gear. Then, to Otto, whose face still looked troubled, he raised a hand.

"Anyway, we've got no choice but to do it. If everything wraps up nice and clean, let's have a huge toast to celebrate."

Subaru said this, spreading his palm to show Otto. Otto looked puzzled for a moment, then, catching Subaru's meaning, raised his own hand the same way.

"Yes, for the sake of my own glorious future too, I'd very much like for this to go well."

"Keep dreaming."

They laughed at each other with a hint of malice and clapped their palms together.

The dry sound cracked through the air of the great cathedral, and amid the startled glances of villagers turning to look, Subaru and Otto turned their backs to each other and started walking.

"Ryuzu-san, you there? I've got something I want to talk about."

Subaru hopped over the uneven ground and passed through the room with its sour smell. Thinking to himself that he'd gotten used to this stench by now, more or less, he stepped over the scrap lumber and saw the blue crystal's glow up ahead.

Inside the crystal, unchanged as ever, was the girl baring her naked body in an unwaking sleep. Subaru stroked the crystal's surface with his palm, disappointed all over again that nothing changed, and looked around the room.

The dim room had no light source other than the crystal's faint blue glow. For a room that reeked so strongly of decay, it was odd that there wasn't a single rat or insect to be seen — and thinking about it calmly, that made the place seem like it had to be terribly bad for one's health, which suddenly made Subaru anxious about his own condition, belatedly.

"Not even bugs or rats live here — makes me wonder if the air's contaminated with something awful."

"No need to worry about anything so strange. It's just that there's too much of the Witch's lingering presence here... the animals sense it by instinct and keep their distance. That's all it is."

From behind him came a voice denying his worry, as Subaru hugged his own shoulders and shivered. A familiar voice — a childlike tone paired with aged speech. Turning around, in the direction Subaru's gaze went, was Ryuzu, walking with her long hair nearly trailing on the ground.

She came up to Subaru's side, in front of the crystal at the center of the room, and looked up at him with eyes the same color as her hair.

"Now then, Su-bou. The fact that you've come all the way here means... you already know about my true nature, don't you?"

"I got the explanation straight from the person in charge here. The proxy for the handover also gave me a rough sketch of it too, so I think I know the general shape of things."

"I see... So that's why Roz-bou's been acting strange since this morning. —Roz-bou failed again, didn't he."

Ryuzu lowered her eyes, an expression of pain crossing her face. At her words, Subaru raised his eyebrows.

"Unexpectedly, without even meaning to, I've gotten to hear part of what I was planning to ask about anyway... Ryuzu-san, by any chance, do you know about Roswaal's scheme?"

"Only a part of it, mind you. Someone has to be reasonably well-versed in the circumstances, or there'd be no way to make sense of Roz-bou's inexplicable behavior. ...Though I suppose that's exactly why it earns Gar-bou's anger."

"A part — how much of a part?"

"Su-bou?"

Dropping the tone of his voice, Subaru took a step closer, closing the distance to Ryuzu. Perhaps sensing the change in Subaru's mood, a flicker of surprise crossed the eyes looking up at him. Subaru bent his knees, bringing his line of sight level with Ryuzu's.

"Tell me, Ryuzu-san. How much have you been told about Roswaal's scheme? Depending on the answer, I..."

"Su-bou..."

If Ryuzu knew everything — that Roswaal's scheme, advancing the world according to the Gospel's writings, meant having Elsa attack the mansion, sealing off the 'Sanctuary' with snow to summon the Great Rabbit, and ultimately, massacring everyone involved to isolate Subaru and destroy his heart —

If she knew all of that, and was cooperating without even trying to stop him, then—

"I don't want to end up despising you, Ryuzu-san. So tell me. How much did you know? How far have you been cooperating with Roswaal?"

"...What I know is that Roz-bou has a Gospel passed down to him from a Witch, and that he's correcting history to match its contents. The 'Sanctuary's' continued existence, too, is precisely because it's written in the Gospel. Without that, our protection would have been lost long ago."

"...Is that all?"

"That's all. I'll say it under oath, if you like. I was born into a Contract that makes it so I can never tell a lie."

Ryuzu looked back at Subaru steadily, an earnest expression on her childlike face. Including the fact of her Contract — that she couldn't lie — Subaru decided to believe her words. At once, the tension drained from his stiffened shoulders, and Subaru let out a breath of relief.

"I see, that's good. If it turned out you'd been thinking along the same twisted lines as Roswaal, Ryuzu-san, then even after everything got resolved, I would've had to smack you around too. And smacking a Ryuzu-san who looks like a loli — man, that would've been the worst possible visual."

"I do genuinely feel bad about causing you worry, but hearing you put it that way makes that feeling start to evaporate — is that just my imagination, I wonder?"

Trying consciously to dispel the heavy mood with some light banter, Subaru got Ryuzu to play along. She put a hand to her chin and continued, "So then," addressing Subaru.

"Judging from that mood just now, this isn't some ordinary matter, is it? Su-bou, just what sort of quarrel did you have with Roz-bou?"

"Whether it counts as cute enough to call a 'quarrel' is beside the point... right now, we're in the middle of a contest. I think it'd objectively be the happy ending if I win, so I want you to cooperate, Ryuzu-san."

"From how this is going, it sounds like it's a matter connected to the 'Sanctuary's' future too. What Su-bou and Roz-bou are each thinking, first of all... without knowing that, this isn't something I can carelessly nod along to."

"Fair point. Right, where should I even start explaining..."

Tilting his head, Subaru seriously agonized over how much information he should reveal. He'd coordinated with Otto beforehand, but judging from Ryuzu's attitude so far, he didn't get the sense that she was fully on Roswaal's side from head to tail. If anything, it seemed more like her cooperation with Roswaal was simply born of necessity.

Keeping that in mind, Subaru thought it over carefully.

"To put it simply... it's about how things get handled after the 'Sanctuary's' barrier is lifted, I guess."

"How things get handled... after it's lifted."

"I heard a bit about this from Garfiel too, but isn't there a split of opinion even within the 'Sanctuary'? Between those in favor of leaving, and those against it?"

The story went that, broadly speaking, those in favor were the majority, and those opposed were a minority. Still, there had also been talk that some of the more extreme members of the opposition might resort to sabotage against Emilia and Subaru as they took on the 'Trial' to free the 'Sanctuary.'

Having already spent nearly twenty days in the 'Sanctuary,' Subaru assumed that Garfiel was the spearhead of that small opposing faction. Ryuzu was supposedly the leader of the pro-liberation faction — so what did she make of that difference in opinion? That was the direction Subaru meant to steer the conversation next, when—

"A split of opinion...? No, there shouldn't be any such thing."

"Huh?"

"Whether to leave this land after it's freed, or to stay, is left up to each individual's own judgment. Most hope to follow Roz-bou and go outside... and those who stay, stay because they mean to lay their bones to rest in this land. There's no room for opinions to split, is there?"

"Uh, wha— no, but..."

At Ryuzu's answer, Subaru's mind stalled, reeling from the shock of hearing something so unexpected.

The conflict between the pro- and anti-liberation factions within the 'Sanctuary.' The opposition arising from that, and the stance toward the 'Sanctuary's' liberation. Subaru had meant to build off of exactly that framework to devise a plan for winning Garfiel over.

"So there wasn't even any such conflict to begin with, which means..."

"————"

"So that whole thing itself was made up by him from the start? He set it up so there'd supposedly be an opposition faction, to put me on guard, so that when sabotage actually happened, I'd just take it as a matter of course?"

If that were true, Subaru thought, that was some uncharacteristically clever scheming on Roswaal's part. To avoid being directly suspected himself, he'd forewarned Subaru of potential sabotage, thereby removing himself from the list of suspects.

Though as it turned out, since Subaru — having looped through and watched the 'Sanctuary's' final days over and over — already knew that the greatest instance of that "sabotage" was Garfiel going on a rampage, it had barely served any purpose at all in the end.

"To go so far out of character just to obstruct the 'Sanctuary's' liberation..."

"...You mean Gar-bou."

Catching Subaru's mutter, Ryuzu seemed to grasp at once whom he was talking about. Her expression darkened further, and she lowered her eyes.

"That child not wanting to go outside... it's probably because of how pathetic we are."

"Pathetic — how do you mean?"

"Exactly as I said. We've lived in this place, and only this place, since we were born. Because of that, we know nothing of the world outside this place. And because we know nothing, we're weak. To Gar-bou, that must be almost unbearably painful."

"————"

Subaru could more or less understand what Ryuzu was getting at.

For the residents of the 'Sanctuary,' who had lived their whole lives in a narrow, closed-off world, connecting to the world beyond once the barrier opened was something entirely unknown, entirely new. Anticipation and anxiety toward that new thing — it was only natural that the longer someone had spent living in this land, the stronger their leaning toward the latter would be. The collapse of the unchanging daily life they'd known carried that much weight of influence.

"Garfiel not wanting the 'Sanctuary' freed is because he doesn't want to see the people who'd get hurt by such a drastic change in environment... is that it? It doesn't suit his character at all... but still."

If that really were the reason Garfiel was so desperately obstructing the 'Sanctuary's' liberation like that, then that way of thinking was — exactly the same as Subaru's own way of thinking, when he'd tried to keep Emilia away from hardship.

Was Garfiel, too, refusing to listen to Ryuzu or the other residents' opinions, trying instead to put into practice the kind of protection he himself believed in? If so, then just as with Subaru, would setting up a proper opportunity for him to talk things through with Ryuzu and the other residents resolve the problem?

"No, it's not that simple a matter."

Subaru and Garfiel wanted the same kind of thing, in a sense, but their positions were different. For Subaru, a lack of power relative to his wish had been the decisive factor in his failures.

But Garfiel was different. Garfiel had the power to prevent the 'Sanctuary' from being freed. If he harmed Subaru and Emilia, wiping out anyone qualified to attempt the 'Trial' to free the 'Sanctuary,' his wish would be fulfilled. The problem was that, even if he shut the 'Sanctuary' up like that, he still wouldn't be able to escape the Great Rabbit's coming assault afterward.

But that was a fact Garfiel didn't know — and even if he were told, it wasn't the kind of thing he'd likely believe. The situation was far too different from explaining things to Otto.

"In the pure sense of searching for a compromise, that alone isn't enough, is it... but wait, that's strange."

"Su-bou?"

"To begin with, why hasn't he... gone with the quickest, most straightforward method of preventing the 'Sanctuary' from being freed, which would be killing me and Emilia, first thing?"

If he were seriously, single-mindedly pushing toward one goal without regard for anything else — the way Roswaal was — Garfiel should have taken Subaru's and Emilia's lives early on. But in reality, there hadn't been a single instance of him directly harming Emilia. Even when it came to attacking Subaru, he hadn't moved until Subaru himself had done something significant to provoke it.

The trigger that would make Garfiel try to kill Subaru still wasn't clear. There was no doubt he meant to prevent the 'Sanctuary's' liberation by killing Subaru. But what reason led him to that decision — that was still unknown.

"There has to be a trigger. ...But thinking back over everything I've experienced of Garfiel attacking me, was there anything that was consistent across all of them...?"

The times Subaru had been attacked by Garfiel were, ultimately, just these: when he'd flown into a rage and lunged at Roswaal, and when he'd led the villagers out of the 'Sanctuary' in an escape attempt. That was it.

As for the incident with Roswaal, viewed objectively, judging Subaru as the villain for lunging at an injured man was, in a sense, a normal reaction. It wasn't much cause for suspicion.

If there was a problem, it was the next occasion — when Garfiel tried once again to kill Subaru, having failed to do so before. Turning into the great tiger to kill Subaru, Garfiel had turned his claws and fangs even on Ram, the villagers, and even Patrasche, all of whom had stood in his way trying to keep Subaru alive. In the end, Subaru had narrowly escaped death, but his hatred for Garfiel, after surviving that massacre, still hadn't faded even now.

Still — how much resolve must it have taken for him to go so far as to commit an act like that? There had to have been some trigger that drove him to it.

And looking back on it, that trigger didn't seem to have originated with Subaru at all.

In other words, the trigger for that massacre could have arisen not from Subaru, but from Garfiel's own side.

"Ryuzu-san. Garfiel might look rough and violent, but he's not the type to resort to violence carelessly, either... is that a fair way to see him?"

"That child is gentle at heart, you know. He puts on a fierce shell and barks first to protect himself and those around him. ...The strength he's honed, too, is surely for that purpose."

"Is that right, then that means... there's only one answer."

"—?"

Subaru rubbed his nose with a finger and turned to face Ryuzu, who tilted her head. Then, resting a hand on the crystal beside him, feeling its cold touch against his palm, he said—

"Someone's been putting ideas in Garfiel's head. That person's actions are the trigger, and Garfiel's the one who ends up flying into sudden shows of force because of it."

All the out-of-character behavior made sense if you assumed someone had been instructing Garfiel. And with that in mind, the most likely candidates for that collaborator behind Garfiel were—

"Roswaal, Ram, Ryuzu-san, or else some malice we haven't seen yet..."

Any one of those possibilities could be driving Garfiel to violence. That was something that needed to be pinned down. Only once that was pinned down would there probably come a chance to have a real, genuine conversation with Garfiel.

"Just for reference, Ryuzu-san, can I ask you something?"

"Mm, what is it?"

"—Do you know what the content of the 'Trial' Garfiel underwent in the tomb was?"

"...No, I don't. Unfortunately, that's not something within my knowledge."

Ryuzu shook her head. Given the restriction that she couldn't lie, her words had to be true. Taking that in, Subaru dipped his chin and said, "I see," then—

"Let me rephrase the question. —Do the *other* Ryuzus know what the content of the 'Trial' Garfiel underwent in the tomb was?"

"————"

"Silence, in this case, can only mean yes."

Telling this to the silent Ryuzu, Subaru tilted his face slightly upward.

There existed multiple Ryuzus, each a replica of Ryuzu Meyer, serving as representatives of the 'Sanctuary.' The system in which the Ryuzu playing the role rotated day by day gave rise to the problem that each individual instance didn't fully share the memories and experiences held by the others.

There were four replica-bodies of Ryuzu serving as representative. If one were to distinguish them as A, B, C, and D for convenience, then naturally, the memories and experiences from a day when A was active weren't passed down to B, C, or D.

The day Garfiel had challenged the 'Trial,' failed, and been carried out by Ryuzu — the Ryuzu who had helped Garfiel that day was not the Ryuzu standing in front of him now.

"...When does the Ryuzu-san who knows about the 'Trial' come out next?"

"————"

"Yesterday, the Ryuzu-san I was talking to in front of the tomb said she'd taken the 'Trial' before. If the no-lying rule applies, then that has to be true. If the rotation switches daily... does that mean the next one comes out in three days?"

Given how little time they had to spare, Subaru dearly hoped it wouldn't turn out to be the furthest-off date possible. As he asked, hoping for that, Ryuzu, who had kept her mouth shut until then, loosened her lips slightly and let out a breath.

"No, the me who led Gar-bou out of the tomb next comes out in two days. There's one of me who's been inside and one who hasn't, and the two pairs take turns in rotation."

She let this slip out, her expression somewhat weary.
