Arc 4 Interlude: "The Honored Guest Departs"

"Letting him go like that... *sigh*... Was that really wise, I wonder... *sigh*"

"It was his decision, his choice. I intend to respect that. ...Though I admit I'm not entirely without feelings about the fact that it was her hand he took when he left."

In response to Sekhmet's languid voice, Echidna shrugged where she sat. They remained, as ever, within the castle of dreams, the grassy plain and blue sky unchanged in every particular. The cool breeze still swept through, still gently stirring the witches' hair.

—The moment the cracked world had swallowed Subaru and Satella and released the two of them from the dream, the world had mended itself once more.

Naturally. A space bound to Echidna's soul—a soul incapable of perishing—would go on maintaining this state for as long as Echidna existed. The cataclysm had been nothing but excessive theater, a flourish to see off the departing guest.

"That said, when you go and squander it so freely, even I can't help but feel worn thin. I'd be grateful if you'd show a little more restraint in your rush to heal everyone."

"I just follow my own way of doing things—healing whatever wound comes within sight. Human, witch, beast, bird, fish, insect, witchbeast, it makes no difference. Any wound on any living thing is my enemy!"

"Even so, unlike when you were alive, the burden of your deeds here falls on me. In life, you had the world to shoulder it for you—can't even you imagine how harsh it is to have that whole burden land on me alone?"

"Things you can't see, like feeling worn out—I don't really think those matter. I heal wounds. Whether the world's lifespan shortens or whatever else happens, that's none of my concern."

At Minerva's declaration—arms crossed, ample bosom emphasized—the witches gave a wry laugh. At a glance, Minerva, the "Witch of Wrath," seemed the most approachable of the witches bearing the names of the great sins—harmless, one might even say, if put bluntly. In truth, all her deeds amounted to nothing but healing, and the number of lives saved by her hand in the world of that era surely ran well beyond five digits.

—However, it was also Minerva who, just as much, indirectly wrought harm.

All manner of destructive energy—punches, kicks, bites—was converted into healing energy the instant it left her hand. That formula was the Authority of "Wrath" itself, and so it could be imitated by no one but her. Even Echidna, who could grasp its structure, could not reproduce it. Minerva's healing blows drove back whatever threat endangered the life of any living creature. —A power that might seem, in a sense, all but omnipotent. But that would be a mistake.

The healing power her fists produced was the result of a powerful formula that twisted cause and effect, and each activation consumed an enormous amount of mana—far more than any single human could ever supply, an amount impossible even for a witch of Minerva's magical caliber. So where, then, did her blows draw their mana from? The answer was simple: she tore it, root and all, straight from the world's core.

Ordinarily, when a human uses magic, mana in the atmosphere is drawn in through the Gate, converted into magical energy, and released again through the Gate—that is what it means to cast a spell. In Minerva's case, however, this Gate connects not to the atmosphere but directly to the core of the world. "The core of the world" is a difficult thing to put into words—a supernatural concentration of mana, or to put it plainly, something connected to the very place where mana is born. Minerva's blows draw mana out from that place and convert it into healing strikes.

Repeat this enough, and mana fails to reach the places it was meant to supply—and since mana is a vital component of the world's makeup, its depletion in those neglected regions carries an extremely dangerous potential to trigger natural disasters and cataclysms. The number of people she directly healed by striking them exceeds five digits.
—But the number of lives lost to the cataclysms she indirectly caused rivals that figure as well.

That is why Minerva, the "Witch of Wrath," was regarded as the most dangerous among the witches bearing the names of the great sins, and treated as an enemy by every nation.

"Here, the only mana I can draw out is whatever Echidna has on hand. I can only mend and heal up until just short of drying you out completely—it's unsatisfying."

"Here, to begin with, there ought to be no reason for anyone to get hurt in the first place. Though for a while now, things have been so noisy around here I nearly forgot that."

"That's... true. It has been noisy for a while, hasn't it."

At Echidna's words, Minerva's momentum deflated. A plain shade of dejection crossed her lovely features, and the golden-haired witch tilted her face up to the blue sky.

"Do you think that kid can pull it off? I'm really, really worried."

"He refused the hand that would, without question, have let him pull it off. Whatever happens, he'll surely struggle desperately to make it work regardless. Though it seems he still hasn't found his answer."

"What's with that way of putting it. You steered him yourself into shaking off your hand, and now you're trying to hide your true feelings even from us, when we already see right through you? There's no point!"

"It's not that I was trying to be refused. —Whether he refused or accepted, either would have suited me just fine, that's all."

In response to Minerva squaring her shoulders in indignation, Echidna settled onto a table she conjured back into being. Then, snapping her fingers to produce a teacup, she tipped the steaming drink to her lips.

"I affirm choices, and I affirm the results of choices. What the result actually turns out to be isn't something I consider much of an issue. The fact that a choice was made, or the fact that it wasn't—that's what matters. Whether the result is good or bad, I flatter myself that I'm the sort who can enjoy either."

"But it's not like you've got no preference at aaall, is it?"

Beside Echidna, who was raising her cup of Dona tea to her lips, a black-lacquered coffin slowly rose upright and settled into place. Daphne, who had at some point returned to her coffin, devoured the tea sweets lined up on the table like a dog.

"You say you respect the outcome and aaall, but Dona-Dona doesn't hesitate to steer things toward the outcome Dona-Dona wants to see, does she? 'Either would've been fine' is probably true, but isn't 'this one turned out fine' just as true?"

"For someone with so little interest in other people, you have a habit of hitting the nail on the head every time, Daphne."

"Compared to the hunger that troubles Daphne day in and day out, it's hardly worth thinking about at aaall. Haah, haah, fumu fumu."

Echidna let out a breath at the sight of Daphne, who had moved on from the sweets to sucking on the plate itself, and turned her gaze to the other witches settling into the remaining seats. Languidly, one; openly indignant, another; timidly, another—and one alone with eyes unusually sharp with hostility.

"Typhon seems to be quite angry."

"That's 'cause Dona's not honest, that's why. If you're not honest, does that make you a liar? And liars are bad guys, right? Is Dona a bad guy?"

"I act honestly according to what I desire. I have no memory of telling a lie, not so far."

To Typhon's innocent, straightforward words, Echidna answered without evasion. Echidna's roundabout phrasing held no currency with the young Typhon. And Echidna knew that if she offended her, it would put everyone present in danger. The condemnation of the guilty and the judgment of sinners were merely one facet of Typhon's Authority of "Pride."

Still, watching Typhon puff out her cheeks in apparent satisfaction at Echidna's words—words balanced on such a mental tightrope—the next to speak, in a low voice, was the witch buried in fluff.

"Speaking while hiding your true meaning... *sigh*... doesn't count as lying. Just a means to an end, isn't it... *sigh*"

"Eh... Kidona-chan, really is... s-such a... troublesome, child, isn't she..."

"You two, really..."

Echidna scowled under the crossfire of criticism, and the witches watching softened at the corners of their mouths. The only one whose eyes narrowed sharply as she watched the scene was Minerva.

"And Minerva, how long are you going to keep being so prickly, *sigh*. This was something we all decided together, wasn't it, *sigh*. We knew this was how it'd go once a sage candidate came... *sigh*"

"Shut up, I know that already. I did agree to the discussion, properly. It's just that, unlike the rest of you, I'm not in a position to just write it off so easily. I'd like you to understand that too."

"Meto-Meto, who's always used to having Tyu-Tyu right beside her, wouldn't understaaand. Isn't everyone spending far too much of their liiife on things besides eating to survive?"

At Daphne's interruption, Minerva and Sekhmet both snorted in unison, looking displeased. The witches' tea party maintained a certain balance, but at heart this was a gathering of strong-willed personalities. More often than not, opinions failed to mesh, and squabbles like this were nothing rare. Minerva, who picked fights with just about everyone, and Sekhmet, who hated conflict, found themselves in minor clashes often enough. Each time, Daphne, oblivious to the mood, would barge in and strike at the heart of the matter. And having the argument fizzle out into nothing, as it did now, was likewise nothing unusual.

Minerva grew angry, Sekhmet indulged her, Daphne threw in her jabs, Carmilla soothed Typhon so she wouldn't explode, and Echidna looked on at it all with quiet enjoyment—while Satella watched over the six of them, safe and sound, with a smile.
That was something that once happened four hundred years ago, days that will never come again.

Satella was driven mad by the Witch Factor; Minerva was ensnared in a trap and died raving; Carmilla was consumed in a great blaze; Daphne withered to death in a sea of sand; Typhon was drowned beneath a great flood; Sekhmet slew a Dragon and fell into the Great Waterfall; and Echidna gathered up only their souls, tethering herself to the present world as nothing more than a soul as well.

An imperfect recreation of those days that would never return.

"Echid... -na-chan... are you... making a sad face? You are... aren't you?"

"Why would I be? There's no reason at all for me to be sad. You're all here, and I still have some measure of contact with the outside. —There's nothing more I need."

"Is that, really, alright? W-we're... j-just souls, so we're not, really, us, are we? We're... already, dead, after all. No one, can really, truly be, together with Echidna-chan... you know...?"

At Carmilla's halting, broken words, Echidna faltered, just for an instant.

—It was Echidna's own power that gave these women, who had lost their bodies and become nothing but souls, temporary forms in the shape of spiritual bodies.

She prepared the vessels, and let their souls dwell within. But those souls were frozen at the moment of their deaths, unchanging ever since. Was the Carmilla who now gazed at Echidna truly Carmilla, in the end? Drawing out their reactions from before death, from their souls, and making the bodies perform them—wasn't this nothing more than a kind of doll-play, brought about by Echidna's own longing?

In truth, they shared knowledge that Echidna herself already knew. How could she say for certain that this wasn't because their very existence arose from within Echidna herself? —Though this was something she had already thought through, exhaustively, many times over.

"For you, a creature made of pure self-love, to worry about me—friend or not—is a rare thing indeed. ...Have you too been influenced by his noisiness, his soft-heartedness, I wonder?"

"I... don't... know anymore... Echidna-chan's... an idiot."

At Echidna's answer, dodging her true feelings, Carmilla said this with a look of disappointment on her face. At that reply, Echidna couldn't help but laugh, a sound catching in her throat.

At Echidna's reaction, she could tell the witches who hadn't been paying attention until now had turned their gazes her way. Bathing in the full weight of those looks, Echidna spread both hands wide and said,

"Now then, it seems this tea party will once again belong to us witches alone for a while. He—Natsuki Subaru—will most likely never set foot here again."

"And you're fine with that? It's not that I care whether you're lonely about it or whatever, but you said something at the very end, didn't you. About the price for that, and all that—you're supposed to be strict about things like that."

"The price... ah, yes, there was that too. A farewell gift for a man expected to face hardship ahead—if I called it that, would you all laugh at me?"

Echidna, hand to her chin, mused aloud, and the witches exchanged glances. Then, nodding, they all opened their mouths at once and said,

"—Not at all."

"Oh? Seems I've been held in higher regard than I thought..."

"I mean, you helping someone out without taking a price for it—that's just not something that happens."

At Minerva's flat declaration, arms crossed, the other witches nodded in agreement. At their perfect unison, Echidna closed her eyes, then cleared her throat, and said,

"It seems there are a great many matters I need to have a serious talk with all of you about. Honestly, what exactly do you all think of me?"

"————"

"Still, well—"

Before the silent witches, Echidna drained the contents of her cup, and with her red tongue, licked her own lips in a sultry manner, saying,

"—You're not wrong. Not in the slightest."
