Arc 5 Chapter 55: "The War God and the Challenger"

—The legend of the war god Kurgan is widely known throughout the Vollachian Empire.

In Vollachia, a nation ruled by meritocracy, one's origins are never questioned so long as one has the ability to back it up.
Compared to the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica, where prejudice against Demi-Humans still lingers, or the Holy Kingdom of Gusteko, which shuns outsiders, Vollachia's policy leans closer to that of the Kararagi City-States, which pay no mind to blood or appearance.

For that reason, among the Four Great Nations, Vollachia is the easiest place to live for anyone who isn't of pure human stock.

And yet, that same merciless meritocracy also means fierce persecution and cold treatment for those without wit or strength. Naturally, judgment of an individual and judgment of a race are two different things.
The Multi-Arm Tribe in particular had, for many years, wandered from place to place as a nomadic people, never settling on any one patch of land. With their unsettling appearance and their conspicuously poor aptitude for magic — poor even by Demi-Human standards — they had long been dismissed as inferior as a race.
Their numbers were never great, either, and when conflict arose, they tended to choose migration over fighting to defend their land.

Being the kind of people they were, shunned wherever they went, it was perhaps inevitable that they should eventually wash up in the Vollachian Empire — and there, nearly be swallowed whole by its iron-blooded imperialism.

—It was Kurgan, in that world of naked meritocracy, who stood up and said "No."

Unlike the human race, the Multi-Arm Tribe is characterized by having more than two arms, though the exact number varies from individual to individual. Most of them have only four or five, which made Kurgan — who sprouted a full eight arms — a singular figure even among his own people.

Still young at the time, Kurgan flatly rejected a local lord's demand that his people vacate the land they had settled on, and drove off the lord's messenger with a swing of his eight arms. He then rallied his terrified kinsmen, crushed every one of the lord's private soldiers sent to forcibly drive them out, and marched all the way up to the lord's own manor.
The lord, chilled to the bone by this savage assault, expected the worst — but Kurgan did not settle the matter with a short temper.

He declared, with a boastful grin, that he had merely proven the strength of himself and his clan — and then calmly took up the post of the lord's new private soldier. From there, he distinguished himself in campaign after campaign, and the name "Eight-Armed Kurgan" endured as a legend of the Vollachian Empire for generations to come.


"――――"

He felt the cold water with every inch of his body, gazing up at the moon shimmering beyond the rippling surface overhead.

The bone supporting his right eye had been shattered, and the eyeball itself felt ready to slip out from behind his closed lid. His left hand, reflexively pressed to the wound, triggered healing magic, patching things together just enough to hold.
His remaining eye watched the red murk drift by in the current, and he curled his body, back striking the bottom of the canal.

"――――"

Even submerged in water that should have been cold, he felt no cold at all.
Freed from the weight of gravity, in a world stripped of its burdens, he slowly gathered strength into his limbs.
*If only the yoke on my heart could come off as easily as the one on my body* — but his heart remained, even now, in the dark.

It wasn't as though some part of him didn't wonder whether it might be easier just to keep sinking. But his breath would soon grow ragged, and he could not simply go on seeing nothing in the darkness behind his eyelids forever.

A girl with pink hair. A cat-eared girl with orange fur. A boy with black hair. Their faces rose up in his mind, kindling warmth in a heart that should have been soaked through.

It might be a flame that would gutter out again in an instant. That much had already been proven, when even the hollow courage he'd scraped together moments ago had been smashed to pieces. *They're there. So what.*

—That wasn't reason enough to keep him sinking.

"Pwahh!"

He uncurled his body, kicked off the canal floor, and shot to the surface in one motion. Breaking through the water, Garfiel shook his head.
His right eye's vision was still blocked, and his skull throbbed with the leftover damage from the beating. Nausea like blood flowing backward gripped his whole body, and the wrongness of his broken teeth threw off the fit of his bite.

"Goddammit...!"

He grabbed the edge of the canal and hauled himself up. Shaking the water off his soaked body like an animal, Garfiel looked.
There, at the spot where he'd been knocked into the canal —

The war god stood, unchanged.
Demon Cleavers still drawn, not a shred of his battle-aura slackened, standing there as if it had never once occurred to him that Garfiel might fail to climb back out.

"――――"

Looking at the silent war god, Garfiel thought it through.
To begin with, there was little real necessity for him to be clashing with Kurgan here at all. His assigned role was to stop "Lust," who had most likely launched the surprise attack on the city hall. Fighting Kurgan here wouldn't save the city hall, left with nothing but noncombatants inside.
Looking at the overall situation, fighting Kurgan here was, by any measure, the worst possible move.

"But hell... you ain't gonna let me run, are ya."

That towering frame, that overwhelming mass of muscle. It looked anything but nimble — and yet Garfiel couldn't picture a single scenario where fleeing would carry him out of the war god's blade's reach.

The moment he'd stood before the war god, running had stopped being an option.
Now, only two choices remained open to Garfiel.

—Stand and fight, and die. Or don't resist, and die.

"The hell... is this a time to be thinkin' that shit!"

He shook off the ominous thought that had crossed his mind, gnashing his fangs. The pain from his hastily-regrown, chipped fangs bit sharp — sharp enough to carve away the negative thinking.

*Cut down every hint of a premonition of defeat.*
*No need for excuses on how to lose.*

—Win, win, win, win, win, win!

Win, and prove your own worth!!

"O-oooooohh!"

Roaring, forcing down his own cowardice, Garfiel charged in again. His all-out blow from the last exchange had been blocked.
But if weight wasn't enough, he'd force it through with speed.
Claws, fangs — gouge, tear, bite, take.

"――――"

The silent war god met the charging Garfiel head-on.
A blow launched from the shoulder with the Demon Cleaver.
Too weak in penetrating force to be called a slash, too sharp to be called a blunt strike. It was a mortar-shell assault only Kurgan could produce — part sword-strike, part hammer-blow, fused into one lethal whole.

The blade of the onrushing Demon Cleaver grazed the back of Garfiel's crouching head. The aftershock shaved his nape, and a white-hot thought tore through his brain.

Against that single strike, Garfiel should have had more than enough room to dodge. There was a speed difference between his own small, nimble frame and the towering body wielding a long weapon, however many arms it had.
He'd had the opening to dodge the wide swing and drive his claws into the exposed body he'd dived into. He should have. He *should* have.

"—Gah"

And yet, having driven deep into that opening, Garfiel was forcibly bent backward on the spot. From beneath, with enough force to blow his jaw clean off his chest, Kurgan's massive arm swung upward from under his own arm.
This too was unforeseen — no, it wasn't unforeseen. It was that the body's balance was simply *different*.

A body born of the Multi-Arm Tribe, Kurgan's physique had developed — optimally grown — to wield those eight arms.
The astonishing combat technique born of that body was, at its root, fundamentally different from anything Garfiel knew of the four-limbed human race's way of fighting.

The common wisdom that a body opens up and creates an opening the instant it lands a blow — didn't apply.
The common wisdom that blocking one arm lets you circle to that side and find a blind spot — didn't apply either.
Even blocking one certain-death arm meant he still had to guard against the same thing from the other seven.

If he couldn't do that, there was nothing left but to die.

"Guh, guaaaaaaaahh!!"

Before Garfiel's horrified eyes, the war god's arms shook the world itself.
The roaring Demon Cleavers kept pounding in from all four directions, violently, trying to cleave his body to pieces.

He caught one blow on his shield, dodged one by crouching, scattered the shock of one by leaping, deflected one by spinning around, canceled one out with an all-out counter-blow, avoided a fatal wound on one while his shoulder was smashed, forced one aside with a beastified arm, and took one clean, sent tumbling gracelessly across the cobblestones.

"Agh, kohh"

—Eight blows.

Everything Garfiel had thrown into that exchange, body and soul, had barely gotten him through what amounted to a *single* exchange for Kurgan.
That storm of lethal attacks had come from Kurgan swinging each of his eight arms just *once*.

If the war god had truly meant it, a follow-up would have pulverized Garfiel entirely. The only reason he still drew breath, coughing blood and rolling on the ground, was that the motionless war god had no intention of pressing the attack.

"――――"

The war god looked down at the gasping loser with the same posture, the same eyes, as when he'd watched Garfiel climb out of the canal.

No spark of defiance rose at being underestimated.
This was a matter that hadn't even reached that dimension yet.
He hadn't come close enough in skill to be able to face Kurgan as one fighter facing another, trading blows on equal footing.

The war god — the name "Eight-Armed Kurgan" — remained very much alive.

"Hff... hff...!"

*I can't win. There's no way I can win.*
*A legend among the dead. A man turned hero. A war god.*

A man who, in a nation ruled by meritocracy, in a nation that scorned his entire race as inferior, had changed both his people's fate and his own with nothing but his own two — eight — hands.
Garfiel was nothing but a green kid who admired that legend from afar.

"Hff... hff... hff..."

*So why is my body still standing?*
His heart had been broken this thoroughly, and still his body rose.

"Haah... shut up, shut up, shut *up*...!"

The pounding of his own heartbeat was unbearably loud right now.
Irritated by the nuisance of it, like a drum beaten right beside his ear, Garfiel stamped the ground with all his strength. The cobblestones beneath his boot cracked, the fissure running straight to Kurgan's feet.

The silent Kurgan, and the blood-soaked Garfiel, faced each other.
The unsteady Garfiel put his weight, once more, into just the tips of his toes, and stamped the stone. An instant later, Kurgan moved.
No — he was *made* to move.

"――――"

Traveling up through the sole of Garfiel's boot, the Divine Protection of the Earth Spirits exerted its power. That force traveled through the crack he'd created, all the way to Kurgan's feet, and heaved up the ground supporting the huge man's body.

However specialized for combat that body might be, once airborne, no physical body could defy the laws of physics.
Without the support of his lower body, there was no way he could unleash a powerful blow.

"Gaaaaaaaah—!"

*This instant is the moment of truth.*

Garfiel's arms lashed upward, aimed at the airborne Kurgan.
Partially beastified, wrapped in the fur and muscle of a great tiger, his arms pounded into Kurgan. Even the war god himself, caught in midair unable to secure his footing, couldn't kill the force of the blow.

A clang of metal rang out, and Kurgan, Demon Cleaver and all, was knocked back.
Garfiel's kick met him in turn. For the first time, it threaded the needle through his defenses, his toe gouging deep into thick abdominal muscle.

Garfiel let out a cheer as the war god folded in half, and kept driving blow after blow home, riding the momentum.

Strikes landed on his chest, his thighs, his knees, his abdomen.
Battered by the shock, Kurgan couldn't even bring his many arms to bear in defense, left exposed in something like a raised-arms pose.

"Got yaaaaaa!!"

Garfiel shouted, certain victory was within reach.
His raised beast-claws tore across Kurgan's chest, black-red blood splattering Garfiel's body in mottled streaks.

Licking the blood off his lips, Garfiel pressed the follow-up.
Certain his opponent must be cornered by now, he flicked his gaze toward Kurgan's iron mask of a face — and in the next instant, every hair on his body stood on end.

"――――"

The eyes of the war god, watching Garfiel continue his onslaught, hadn't changed at all from the start. They hadn't wavered even slightly.

"—Ah"

And in that same moment, Garfiel understood.
Understood, far, far too late, the sign of the war god's counterattack.

The two Demon Cleavers he unleashed shattered both of Garfiel's hastily-raised arms through the shield with the sheer force of the blow, and slammed him into the ground.

"Ka—"

Not even a cry of pain properly escaped him.
His vision lost all sense of up and down in an instant, and a shock as though his limbs had been blown off at the root overtook him.

He understood, at least, what had happened.

Unable to plant his feet in midair, Kurgan had unleashed a savage blow using only his upper body.
The method was simple, plain as day.

He'd gripped the blade of the Demon Cleaver with two of his hands, amplifying the load behind the downward swing, driving the power up exponentially.

—In other words, the principle of a finger-flick.

Two arms forming a hook, turning the mortar-strike into a fatal blow.
The tactic of forcing him airborne to rob him of his striking power had been neutralized, as if it were something Kurgan had seen a hundred times before.

"Gohh!"

With his chance at a counterattack crushed, the sole of the huge man's boot trampled down on Garfiel from directly above.
Caught by the stomp with the full momentum of the fall behind it, Garfiel's whole body groaned and he was flung off the cobblestones.

Dominated by pain and a sense of loss, he triggered healing magic on pure survival instinct.
Broken bones in his upper arm, elbow, and shoulder began knitting; his churned-up organs began mending. His ribs, hip bone, and left thigh were twisted out of shape too, faster than rapid healing could keep up with.

His Gate flared hot, and he unleashed magic prepared to burn through every drop of Mana he had.
Drawing up the earth's power through his body sprawled on the ground, he poured it, indiscriminately, into treatment, repair, recovery.

Several seconds. Several dozen seconds. Perhaps several minutes.

Cutting off any sense of the passage of time, Garfiel rushed the repair of his own body.
And having barely regained functions enough to move, he spat out the blood clogging the back of his throat and pulled himself upright.

"――――"

The war god watched, silently, the blood-drenched Garfiel.
Seeing that sight, the backs of Garfiel's eyelids burned hot. He bowed his face against the surging swell of emotion, his back teeth trembling.

"What... the hell is this."

Kurgan's posture had been consistent from the start.
He met Garfiel's challenges, intercepting each attack, but showed no sign of pressing his own attack, no finishing blow.

Spared, three times over, Garfiel stood.
The sense of defeat and humiliation raging in his chest smashed his pride and dignity as a warrior to pieces.

*I have to win* — that's what he told himself.
And just as strongly, he thought: *if I'm going to be forced to make a fool of myself like this again and again, I'd rather be killed.*

The war god Kurgan, hero of Vollachia.
A man who had seen the peak of every warrior alive — surely he, of all people, should understand the anguish churning in Garfiel.

"If I could just..."

Should he beg to be killed?
Should he honestly admit defeat, plead that the gap in strength was plain to see, and ask to be allowed to die as a warrior?

Drop his shield, spread both arms wide, and wear a solemn face.
If he begged like that, would it be granted?

If his life were to be scattered by one blow from the war god, then perhaps that, too, would be a death a warrior could be proud of.

"Right here..."

*If it could just end,* how easy that would be.

"If it could just end right here, how easy that'd be, huh."

Resetting his shield, closing both arms, baring his fangs.
With the face of a raging beast, Garfiel looked straight ahead.

"Won't go away. From my head."

*Fight without thinking,* someone had told him once.
*You're stronger that way,* they'd said. *Leave the extra thoughts behind, let instinct take over.*

—Was that really true, though?

"The voice. Won't. Go away..."

His heartbeat was too loud.
Every bone in his body creaked, knitting back together, making warped sounds.

Loud, loud, loud, loud.
Every extra sound — all of it, everything, all of it — was in the way.

—*You won't be able to hear the voice anymore, will you.*

"I can hear it... I can still hear it, dammit..."

Even trying to be empty of thought, trying to become nothing.
Something in Garfiel's ears — or perhaps something that wasn't his eardrums at all — kept picking up voices.

Someone's voice. A dear voice. A familiar voice. A voice that warmed his chest. A voice that choked his throat. A voice that made him proud. A voice that made his anger impossible to hold back.
So many voices, refusing to let Garfiel go.

Even trying to let instinct carry him into battle, the waves kept washing over him without ever receding, and Garfiel couldn't become just one thing alone.

*If thinking makes you weaker the more you do it, then right now, I'm weak.*
Unlike back in the Sanctuary, when he could play the lone wolf, the outlaw, by himself — he'd met so many things since then, seen so many things.

The more he carried, if carrying more made him weaker — did that mean people grew weaker, the longer they lived?

"...No way. That's not it."

Holding the voices that wouldn't disappear, swallowing his sense of defeat, wrapping it all up in his hunger for victory, pouring in every ounce of admiration, every ounce of longing —

—Garfiel challenged the war god to battle.

"—Kuh, hah."

"――――"

The color of Garfiel's eyes changed.
Seeing that, Kurgan moved, quietly.

Of his four Demon Cleavers, two were sheathed once more.
But that didn't mean Kurgan's fighting spirit had lessened. If anything, the force concentrated into the remaining two Demon Cleavers increased, and to back that up, the war god's stance itself changed.

The war god, who had stood waiting like a sumo wrestler at the ready, now stepped his right foot forward, leaning slightly, facing Garfiel head-on.
A stance meant for actual combat.

—Proof that Kurgan had, at last, recognized Garfiel as an enemy.

"So basically, up till now, you were treatin' me like a damn kid, huh. Guess that's what they mean when they say, 'Gwan birds make lousy parents.'"

"――――"

Silent, and to that silence Garfiel charged.

Fierce momentum, and the Demon Cleaver answered it.
Despair like a wall closing in from the front, the illusion that fear conjured up — Garfiel crushed it underfoot and slipped through the gap.

In their last exchange, he'd misjudged his first strike.
The reason had been Kurgan's sheer killing intent, and the illusion born from Garfiel's own heart, more frightened of the hero than it needed to be.

"O-ohhhh!"

His driving fist punched toward Kurgan's torso.
A dull thud of steel striking flesh — but what he'd struck wasn't the torso he'd aimed for. It was one of the arms thrust in between.

"The hell... are you messin' with me!"

His fist caught in Kurgan's palm, Garfiel roared.
He drew power up from the soles of his feet, through his fist trapped in Kurgan's palm, and unleashed it in a twisting burst of striking force.

Kurgan's caught fingers wrenched sideways; the war god spun his body to dodge the fist-hammer that came crashing down, and riding the momentum, Garfiel scrambled up Kurgan's waist and chest, driving his jaw upward with a snap kick — a somersault.

Kurgan reeled backward, and at the same time a Demon Cleaver swung in a horizontal sweep.
Reading the trajectory in the shriek of wind and air, Garfiel caught the mortar-blow on the shield of both arms.
A thunderous roar sounded, and Garfiel's body was blasted far back.

"Rrrrraaahh—!"

He drove all four limbs into the cobblestones, forcibly braking his flung-back body. As he lifted his face, right in front of him — Kurgan's follow-up drove in.
Kurgan, who hadn't pressed an attack until now, moved to crush Garfiel's entire body.

Judgment, an instant. Action, a heartbeat. Result, right after.

"――――"

Raising the arms he'd driven into the cobblestones, he tore up the ground in front of him and slammed it forward. The charging Kurgan smashed through that wall shoulder-first, and drove the Demon Cleaver in.

A violent crash.
Taking the direct hit, Garfiel was driven backward. His heels dug into the ground, gouging it as he tried to hold his footing, and a broken fang went flying.
But—

"Don't you underestimate me, ya bastard!!"

The tip of the Demon Cleaver's thrust was caught between his teeth.
His canine snapped clean off, blood pouring down the blade in a torrent, but Garfiel didn't hesitate.

"――――"

The strength of his neck and jaw exploded, and Kurgan's massive body swayed.
Another of his arms grabbed the hilt of the Demon Cleaver Garfiel had caught in his teeth, trying to yank it free in one motion — but the embedded fang wouldn't budge.

If anything, the fang bit down harder still. Garfiel's upper body began to swell, half-Beastification beginning to take hold.

"Guh, ohh, ohhhhhh, GRAAAAAAHH!!"

Beastifying the head drastically dulls the capacity for thought.
He'd been warned, over and over, that being pushed into a mindset barely distinguishable from a beast's was, quite literally, a double-edged sword.
But in this moment, Garfiel chose it anyway.

The cursed power of being a half-blood — that was exactly what he needed now.
Against a war god who drew out the full power of his own origin as a member of the Multi-Arm Tribe, how could he possibly win while still denying his own roots?

*Tiger, tiger, tiger — lend me your strength, right now, this instant—!*

"――――"

The moment the golden tiger's eyes flew open, the Demon Cleaver shattered.
Cracks spread through the entire blade, the collapse spreading even to the hilt, and the huge frame, with nowhere left to put its power, swayed hugely off-balance.

—A real chance, at last.

"GAAAAAAHH!!"

His swung-down beastly arm slammed into Kurgan's head. As the huge man reeled from the impact, a claw strike came in to finish it.
The claw's slash and the beast-arm's blow landed in the same instant, and Kurgan scattered blood, staggering far back.

"――――"

The follow-up was met and struck down by a counter-fist.
An elbow slammed into the great tiger's face, caving in his nose, and an instant later his jaw was snapped upward from directly below.
His knee dropped, and bracing his crumbling body, Garfiel fired off a straight punch that crossed with the massive arm — both of their faces knocked back at the same moment.

Blood scattered. His vision swam.
His head lost all sense of up and down, and his body left everything else behind.

*Doesn't matter. Everything that matters is already packed into my heart.*
Even told not to think, the things that wouldn't disappear were what drove this blood-soaked, ragged body forward.

The Demon Cleaver swung again.
Of the two he'd kept, the one that hadn't been destroyed.

Judgment, an instant. Action, a heartbeat. Result, always right after.

"Gu, ohh...!"

The blade swept at his torso; he let it slide off his shield and caught it on his abdominal muscle.
Even having scattered the shock, the blow still carried enough force to threaten to cleave straight through his thick abdominal muscle, body and all.

But to cut through wire-like fur and the swollen frame of the great tiger required one more step forward than Kurgan managed.

The ground beneath Kurgan's feet was cracked from Garfiel's own footwork. The Blessing of the Divine Protection of the Earth Spirits had drawn that crack there.

"Oh, ohhhh, OHHHHHHH!"

With the blade still buried in his abdomen, Garfiel grappled onto the war god's body.
Kurgan tried to pull back with brute strength, but Garfiel wouldn't let him escape.

A mouth with broken fangs, an arm shattered more than once, instincts resigned to the loss of reason — all of it seized Kurgan and dragged him in.

"――――"

Grabbing hold of the huge body unable to find its footing, Garfiel hurled Kurgan backward — into the canal.
In the instant he was thrown, Kurgan's arm caught Garfiel by the scruff of the neck, dragging him down into the fall as well.

A violent splash rose, and the two enormous bodies plunged into the canal.
Locked together, the two huge frames rode the current, staining the canal red with blood as they were swept along.

"――――"

Even underwater, the exchange of blows between the two grappling shadows didn't stop.
Ignoring the resistance of the water, in the poor visibility of the dark water, Garfiel and Kurgan kept pounding each other.

A massive fist twisted his organs, and he groaned in agony as oxygen was wrung from his lungs. Pain called for greater pain, suffering called for greater suffering, in the continuing underwater battle.

Amid it, Garfiel realized his own disadvantage.
For some reason, the war god before him showed no sign of breathing at all. He was made to feel, viscerally, the fact that this was a resurrected Undead.
The lack of oxygen dulled his movements, and a sluggish stagnation began to creep over Garfiel.

The current gradually gained force, and down slopes, over falls, the two bodies dropped, again and again.
And in the middle of that repetition, at last, all movement stopped.

Consciousness drifting away, the fighting spirit even in his fingertips was lost.

"—Uh"

*One breath. Not enough.*
That, precisely, would be the cause of his defeat, and Garfiel's consciousness grew hollow. And the outcome —

"――――"

In the water, which carried sound so poorly, a heavy sound rang out.
His fading consciousness snapped back, and in the murky black water, Garfiel saw.

The Demon Cleaver scraping the canal's wall, its floor, the war god's blow tearing a fatal wound into the very channel of the water's flow.

There was no time, and no air left, to even ask *what.*

The clanging report echoed through the water, and finally the sound and shock of shattering steel coincided.
The next instant, a new, tremendous current was born.

A different flow, distinct from the canal's proper course — and Garfiel's body was caught up in it too, sucked out and hurled clear of the canal.

"—Buh, geh, geohh"

The sensation of being wrapped in water vanished, and Garfiel spectacularly vomited up all the water he'd swallowed.
From his eyes, from his nose, from his ears — from every hole in his face, water poured out, and Garfiel shook his head.

*What happened* — and then, he heard it.

"—Gorgeous Tiger?"

Mixed in with the sound of rushing water, a weak voice called out his name.
