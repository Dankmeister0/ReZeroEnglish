Arc 3 Chapter 35: "The Way Home"

Dangle, dangle, dangle, dangle, dangle.

Swaying, swaying. Legs swaying.
Hazily, languidly, sinking into the creaking seat, surrendering to the rocking of the dragon carriage, the madman laughed in soft titters.

There was nothing funny about anything in particular, but laughing strangely calmed the heart.
If he didn't, an unbearably painful feeling would well up and pool inside his chest, and if it pooled too much, his head would go to pieces, as if it had exploded.
So that it wouldn't come to that, he laughed in titters even though nothing was funny. Searching for something funny, titter, titter.

But when that laughter suddenly stopped, a loneliness would abruptly stretch taut.
Once that happened, he couldn't hold back anymore, and tears would spill out in fat drops. Trying to stop them didn't stop them, and trying to stop them only made his breath grow ragged. So, so as not to suffer that, he let the flowing tears run free, until his face and clothes were both soaked through with them.

And so, repeating this cycle of laughing and crying so that his chest wouldn't ache and his heart wouldn't hurt, blue would always come from somewhere, stroke his head, and wipe his wet cheeks with a cloth.

That was why he liked blue. When blue was beside him, his heart grew warm and fuzzy. When blue left his side, he would suddenly feel uneasy, as if everything around him had gone dark. His chest would ache with the same tight squeeze as when he wanted to cry, and no matter how he tried to laugh, he couldn't, and only tears would fall, drop after drop.
He was sure that sad things happened whenever he wasn't with blue. When blue went away, he'd remember that sadness and start crying. So he wanted blue to stay by his side forever. As if she understood that, blue stayed with him as much as she could.
That was why he liked blue.

Green wasn't so bad either.
Green didn't stroke him gently like blue, or come to see him over and over like blue did, but green was hard and soft. Hard and soft, and he didn't dislike it.
He thought green came to see blue, and came because blue was there. Green and blue talked about all sorts of things, but he was busy tittering and crying in fat drops.
When the talk was over and green left, green would stare at him fixedly. Those eyes were very hard and soft, and he didn't dislike them.

Brown and white were scary. He disliked them. Scary. It made the tears come in fat drops.
Brown was the next after blue to come see him, but when brown touched him he felt sick, and tears came in fat drops, so he didn't like it. Those eyes that looked angry were scary. Even with a gentle face, it was scary. Blue would stroke him so he wouldn't cry, but the tears fell anyway.
White was even scarier. White never touched him. He'd only seen white once. And yet, it was scary. The eyes that stared so fixedly looked like eyes that weren't looking at anything at all, and that was scary. He disliked it. He didn't like it.

A dream, he was dreaming a dream.
Dangle, dangle, dangle, dangle.

Rocked by the ride, legs swaying, leaning his shoulder against something, bathed in the wind, hazily, languidly, the madman dreamed.

In the dream, appearing, a silver, swaying, swaying.

Silver, he didn't understand.
His chest was hot and warm. His chest ached and prickled. His chest was tight and squeezed. His chest pounded and raced.
He didn't know if it was joy, or sorrow, or delight, or pain.
He felt like he liked it, the way he liked blue; felt like it was bitter, the way brown was; felt like it was soft, the way green was; felt like it was scary, the way white was.
Silver, he didn't understand. But thinking about silver, he didn't dislike.

Sway, sway, drift, drift.
Swaying, drifting. Swa-a-aying.

* * * * * * * * * * * * *

Rem watched out of the corner of her eye as Subaru, drowsing with his head against her shoulder, nodded off, and her lips curved into a faint smile.

The fact that he was entrusting his whole body to her like this, so unguarded, so defenseless, made her happy.
Rem understood that the Subaru of now was not the usual Subaru, and that this state was not something he would have chosen of his own will — but even so, having his complete trust placed in her like this was a supreme joy.

"Subaru-kun, come closer, this way."

"...Mm, unh."

Even though he was already close enough that she could feel his sleeping breath, Rem drew Subaru's body in still closer, pressing them together. The driver's seat was built for one person, and seating two required no small amount of forcing things. Normally it should have been the smaller Rem leaning her weight against Subaru, but doing that now would put them both in a precarious position.
Since boarding the dragon carriage, she had settled the docile Subaru half onto her lap, holding his waist with the hand not gripping the reins, resettling herself as she pulled the reins taut.

She kept to a posture that put as little strain on Subaru as possible. Because of that, ceding most of the driver's seat to him left Rem in a fairly grueling position. To spend nearly half a day like that, it would be no surprise if an ordinary person's strength gave out partway through.
On that point, though, Rem's physical toughness far surpassed that of an ordinary person. Her mental endurance, too, was first-rate when it came to simply enduring in silence. And above all, the fact that her own hardship was for Subaru's sake was, for Rem, the greatest source of motivation.

What exchange had passed between Subaru and Emilia, and what rift had resulted from it — Rem had not been told the details.

On the day the Royal Selection had been declared at the royal castle, Rem, who on Roswaal's instructions had let Subaru's entry into the castle go unremarked, had been badly shaken by how utterly haggard Emilia looked upon her return.
Emilia, looking thoroughly worn down, gave Rem a rough account of what had happened at the castle, and on top of that told her to go fetch Subaru, whom she'd left behind there, and to head to Crusch's estate.

There was no reprimand for having let Subaru go.
It didn't seem likely that Emilia had simply forgotten that point, so Rem assumed she must have had some reason of her own, and did not bring the subject up herself.
She simply followed Emilia's order quietly, bringing Subaru with her into House Karsten. There had been a brief conversation with Roswaal before that, but it was a matter kept confidential from both Emilia and Subaru. And in any case, it was no longer a conversation that mattered now.

In any case, once inside House Karsten, Subaru's treatment began in earnest.
Received as a guest at the estate, Rem — unlike Subaru — was not herself under treatment, and so was given free rein over most of her time. She spent nearly all of that free time standing at Subaru's side.
Part of it was that she wanted to unconditionally affirm his gallant effort to keep up appearances of normalcy even while utterly worn down — but if she was honest, the real reason was more personal.

At Roswaal's mansion, Rem rarely got to spend time together with Subaru.
He mostly handled the mansion's odd jobs as Ram's assistant, while she herself shouldered most of the roles that kept the mansion running — their assigned duties differed both in place and in volume. Inevitably, the time she spent in contact with him was less than her sister's, and that had planted no small amount of dissatisfaction and unease in her.
Though once she became aware of that feeling, it struck her as so unbecoming that she could never put it into words.

"Subaru-kun, it's all right. Everything's fine."

Perhaps he was uncomfortable in his sleep — Subaru writhed, and Rem readjusted her hold to ease him, speaking gently even as she kept her grip on the reins.
Eyes closed, still fast asleep, Subaru gave no response to her voice. But it seemed to her that the tension at the corners of his eyes eased, if only slightly, and Rem, bracing herself anew, looked ahead.

She had intended to push through the night toward the mansion, but it might be better to make camp somewhere along the way. The time, which had been just past noon when she'd left Crusch's estate, had by the point she'd covered roughly half the distance to the mansion already advanced to when clouds began drifting across the moon.

They were running well behind schedule. That was the result of Rem's efforts to keep from burdening Subaru, having made the earth dragon run considerably slower than its usual pace.

Another two or three hours would complete the half-day of travel she'd planned, but at this pace, would they even reach the mansion before morning?

"If it comes to that, it'll be hard to communicate with Sister, too."

Their shared empathic sense required both a certain proximity and that both parties be awake to function.
Especially when Rem was the one initiating contact toward Ram, the requirements — in both effort and distance — were quite demanding. At their current distance, reporting the situation to Ram was, at the very least, impossible, and by the time distance made it possible, it would already be the dead of night.

— Yes, let's make camp after all.

Having made that decision, Rem worked the reins to signal the earth dragon to stop.
The earth dragon, which had built up speed, obeyed, slackening its pace and gradually coming to a halt, letting out a rough snort as it looked up at her.
Setting Subaru down on the driver's seat, Rem slipped off and leapt down, landing on the highway's ground and looking around at their surroundings.

The sun had already set, and the only light filling the Lifaus Highway was the moonlight and the illumination from the Lagmite Ore fixture fitted to the dragon carriage Rem had been driving.
Fortunately, with few clouds tonight, the moonlight alone lit the area well enough. Judging that this actually lowered the likelihood of a bandit attack, Rem promptly climbed into the carriage's cabin and briskly layered blankets together to make a simple bed. Then—

"Subaru-kun, pardon me."

She scooped up the sleeping Subaru from the driver's seat in her arms, princess-carry style, and wrapped him straight into the bedding.
She laid down Subaru, who was surely worn out from sitting and traveling without pause, then rolled her shoulders lightly to check her own condition before stepping out of the cabin to stand watch over the camp.

She wasn't overly worried about bandits, but on the highway at night, witchbeasts or wild dogs not infrequently took advantage of the scarcity of passing dragon carriages to attack.
Rem, who had encountered such cases several times in the past, understood that such foes were far more dangerous than any human. Still—

"Tonight I have you as well, so I don't think there's much to worry about."

Rem reached out and stroked the face of the earth dragon as it lowered its snout toward her.
It was a companion she'd been connected to, through the reins at least, for a long stretch of the day. She'd grown a certain fondness for it, and even on their first meeting it had listened well to what she told it. Given how properly it had been trained, she could only praise it — truly a duke's household's earth dragon.
Though, admittedly, the earth dragon's good sense wasn't entirely unrelated to the fact that Rem was a biologically superior being — an Oni.

Among dragonkind, earth dragons were a species remarkably friendly toward humans. A great many of them had been incorporated as part of daily life, valued in no small part for their own gentle temperament.
With flying dragons or water dragons, on the other hand, special training or rearing from infancy was indispensable, so their numbers were limited compared to earth dragons.
In any case, while the earth dragon was the most humanly-familiar of dragonkind, its qualities as a species naturally stood apart from other beasts. There was almost no wild creature ignorant enough of its own strength to attack an earth dragon outright, and earth dragons themselves had a habit of being remarkably keen-nosed for danger.

Unless it was a large pack of witchbeasts or a group of bandits, nothing would attack an earth dragon, and even such groups the earth dragon would almost always sense in advance.
So there was not so much to worry about when camping with an earth dragon in tow.

"Rest easy, please."

The earth dragon and herself — more than enough hands for keeping watch through the night.
Rem glanced up at the sleeping quarters as she spoke those words, then stroked the earth dragon as it nuzzled close and had it settle onto the ground. Leaning her body against the hard hide of the seated earth dragon, she pulled the blanket she'd brought out over herself, and, keeping her senses taut, began her watch.

If she timed their departure for when the sun began to rise, they should be able to reach the mansion by tomorrow morning.
As for bringing Subaru back without having accomplished her purpose, she would simply have to accept whatever reprimand came. Above all, she was the only one who could protect Subaru as he was now.

"But if there's anyone who can bring Subaru-kun back to how he was..."

It could only be Emilia. And that fact frustrated Rem to no end.

For Rem, Emilia was an extremely difficult presence to deal with.
Roswaal had welcomed her as a guest, and now treated her as a royal candidate, a presence ranked above Rem herself. In fact, both Rem and Ram had been instructed to treat her the same way.
Rem had no great objection to placing Emilia above their master Roswaal in rank. Ram, who held Roswaal above all else, seemed dissatisfied with it, but Rem's own attachment on that point wasn't as strong as her sister's. Of course, Ram wasn't foolish enough to show that on her face or put it into words, so Rem's knowledge of Ram's feelings on the matter came purely from what she'd sensed.
It was, after all, a dissatisfaction strong enough to resonate through their shared empathic sense — something she otherwise wasn't especially conscious of.

The complexity of the feelings Rem held toward Emilia had nothing to do with Roswaal — the reason lay in her origins.
— The fact that Emilia was a Half-Elf. In other words, the fact that she was half-Witch.

In her head, Rem understood that Emilia herself bore no blame for it. And yet it was also true that some part of her, emotionally, could not fully accept that.
It wasn't Emilia's fault. But a being that was half-Witch had exerted an influence on Rem's life too great to simply dismiss.

She could choose neither to treat her with goodwill nor with ill will.
As a result, Rem had firmly resolved that her manner toward Emilia would, in short, never stray beyond the bounds of "guest and servant."
Setting emotion aside, Rem responded to Emilia's instructions like a machine. Emilia, too, seemed to sense that attitude in her, and never said anything to press further than necessary.

Days had passed with that relationship unchanged, and Rem had believed it wouldn't change no matter the outcome of the Royal Selection. Given her role, the chance of her being present at the Royal Selection's conclusion was slim to begin with. And considering the position she'd imposed upon herself, she had also cautioned herself that holding any strong feeling toward Emilia, whether of liking or dislike, would be an unnecessary indulgence.

And yet now, Rem's feelings toward Emilia had utterly changed from before.

That fact left Rem's heart badly frayed at the edges.
Rem was not so childish as to fail to acknowledge her own feelings, and to her, Emilia's stubbornness was, in every sense, beyond forgiving. And even while she knew that Emilia had a past that made such stubbornness understandable, she felt sick with herself for thinking that way regardless.

Those feelings churned in a whirl, and with a single boy at their center, threatened to explode.

Sick of her own ugliness, Rem let out a breath.
Placed like this in a soundless world, in surroundings where even the light was uncertain, her thoughts naturally drifted toward the aimless.
The passage of time felt hazy and slow, and no matter how many times she looked up, the moon's tilt seemed not to have shifted at all from where it had been before.

The night was long. A night spent all alone was endlessly deep, cold, and long.

On impulse, Rem was seized by the urge to crawl into the cabin she was sheltering behind her.
How wonderful it would be to slip in beside the boy wrapped in blankets inside, whose face wore a peaceful expression only while he slept so deeply he didn't even dream, and share in his warmth.
A temptation rose in her — that she might as well throw everything else aside and simply do it.

If she went back as things were, what awaited him was a harsh reality far removed from any ideal.
Right now, wherever she drove the dragon carriage, nothing but her own conscience would stand to judge her for it. Even Subaru, given enough time spent together, might eventually recover his humanity from this childlike state and, though different from before, come to share the same kind of time with her again.
As for travel funds, the amount Roswaal had given her was considerable. She could take it, vanish without a trace, and live in seclusion with just the two of them, Subaru and herself.

"Heh, what a fairy tale..."

Shaking her head, pressing her face against her drawn-up knees, Rem laughed bitterly at her own delusion.
There was no way she could make a choice that disregarded everything like that. It was, by rights, a cruel thought she ought to shrink from even letting cross her mind.
There was no way she could leave the mansion, abandoning her sister, abandoning Ram. Her sister was, quite literally, her other half, and above all, if she left her behind at that mansion, how great a burden would fall on her? Her sister, kind and forever indulgent of Rem, would surely forgive her even then. And precisely because of that, there was no way she could betray her.

And there was Roswaal too, who had entrusted a great sum of money and a weighty role to a Rem who could even entertain such a betrayal in thought. Betraying that trust was likewise a notion that Rem's fastidious nature could never allow her to carry out.
And more than anything—

"Subaru-kun...there's no way it's right to leave him like this."

While devotedly tending to Subaru's every need, what had governed Rem was a sense of pure bliss.
Rem was well aware that she was, by nature, possessive. If she could, she wanted to keep every person dear to her within arm's reach. By serving others, she could feel the value of her own existence — one might even say she had a maid's temperament from birth.
So tending to Subaru in his current state was no hardship for Rem at all. If anything, these were days in which she felt herself fulfilled by a Subaru who couldn't be without her.

But that was not the real Subaru.

For Rem, the person known as Natsuki Subaru had always, without fail, been—

* * * * * * * * * * * * *

Her forelocks stirred by the dampness of the morning mist, Rem slowly lifted her face.

Her consciousness hovering somewhere she might call half-awake, adrift in the sensation of drifting between sleep and waking, Rem renewed her awareness that it was nearly time to set out.

Through the night, nothing of note had come to pass; not even a trace of witchbeast or bandit had shown itself before them. Even so, it seemed Rem hadn't been entirely free of fatigue either. Unable to bear the hours with no change worth calling change, her consciousness had come close to losing to drowsiness, shifting into the half-awake state described above, spending some of that time on rest of her own.

She stood and stretched her body in the cool morning air.
A slovenly, unseemly gesture. Something she would never do in front of anyone else's eyes, but right now the only one who might see her was Subaru, breathing softly asleep beside her—

"S-Subaru-kun!?"

Startled, she leapt back and only then noticed the boy wrapped in a blanket right there beside her.
Having lost the support of leaning against her, the boy rolled slowly onto the grass, grimacing and shifting restlessly.

Flustered, Rem looked back and forth between Subaru and the dragon carriage behind her. He must have climbed down sometime in the dead of night, while her consciousness had drifted away from wakefulness, and rolled in to lie beside her.
While chilled by the fact that she hadn't noticed it happening, she also became belatedly aware of just how much she had let her guard down around Subaru, and her face flushed red at the realization.
Even as she reacted so much like a maiden, Rem privately wondered whether Subaru's action just now might be a good sign.

When awake, Subaru's responses had mostly amounted to nothing but a blank expression, laughter, or tears. And yet he had climbed down from the carriage on his own and carried out an act of his own volition.
Rem let herself hope that his gradually shattered heart was beginning to gather itself back together, reconstructing the person he was.

"—All right, let's go home, Subaru-kun."

If a change had taken root, then surely things would head in a good direction from here.
It wasn't like her to think that way, but that too was likely a result of being influenced by the boy before her. And that inward change, to Rem, seemed somehow a precious thing.

She lifted the still-sleeping Subaru back into her arms, climbed onto the driver's seat, and woke the earth dragon. Calling out to it as it stirred, she gave it water to drink before setting off once more.
The wheels bit into the highway's surface, and they began moving, gradually picking up speed.

The remaining distance was roughly half, which in terms of time would come to seven or eight hours or so.
In both spirit and stamina, she felt far more fulfilled than yesterday, when she'd set out carrying nothing but grim resolve. Gazing at Subaru's profile as he slept deeply, Rem let her eager heart flow into the reins and picked up the pace.

Surely, everything would turn toward a good direction.

— That was what Rem believed.

* * * * * * * * * * * * *

— Something was wrong with the air.

Rem, driving the dragon carriage, noticed it while she had Subaru's head, restless in his sleep, resting on her lap, her supporting arm slipped into his black hair as she stroked it.

Perhaps it was because she'd had time to think slowly, the night before.
Having reached some degree of acceptance about the complicated feelings within herself, Rem had, somewhere inside, felt a bit light-hearted over the sight of Subaru coming to nestle against her of his own accord in the dead of night.

If that was the reason she'd been slow to notice an abnormality this obvious, then she was foolish beyond her own hope of forgiveness.

"It's far too quiet..."

In all their travel along the Lifaus Highway, they had not once passed another dragon carriage.
Though she was running along the highway, Rem was steering the earth dragon somewhat off the proper road, aiming for the shortest route to the mansion. Even as she felt the wheels biting into grass beneath her, she should have taken more note of the complete absence of anyone else in the unobstructed surroundings.

Even the night before, while keeping watch against witchbeasts or bandits, there had been several strange points.
Even before the complete absence of any hostile presence, there had been the abnormality that she couldn't make out even a single insect's cry.

A bad premonition raced through her mind.
This kind of silence, this state where every living thing held its breath — such things were always the herald of some calamity beyond human understanding.

As the distance to Mathers territory, to the mansion, shrank, the unease only grew stronger.
Rem poured her inward anxiety into the hand gripping the reins, urging the earth dragon — already running at a desperate pace — to hurry faster still.

She knew she was pushing it too hard, but right now she had to confirm the truth behind this unease as soon as possible. If it turned out to be needless worry, that would be fine — she could simply apologize to both Subaru and the earth dragon for putting them through such an unreasonable journey, and face the matter anew.
But if the unease smoldering in her chest turned out to be real—

"—Sister?"

What suddenly arrived in Rem's heart was a turmoil of feeling that was not her own.

— It was an unease so unbearable that it vanished again at once, and the fact of that vanishing threw Rem into a panic.

Her sister, who normally kept up an air of composure on the surface, was in fact bold-hearted within as well. Someone fundamentally removed from anything like being shaken, she rarely was — except when it involved their master, or, if Rem might presume to say so, when it involved her own sister, Rem.
And that sister had felt an "unease" strong enough to reach Rem through their shared empathic sense. And the fact that it had vanished again immediately meant she had reined it in, so as not to let it reach Rem.
That unease, which would not have reached her had the distance been greater, had reached Rem regardless, in the middle of her return journey from the royal capital.
And having received it, Rem—

"I have to get back, quickly—!"

In her haste, she gripped the reins harder still, until her hands went white.
As things stood, if events at the mansion had proceeded as planned, the only ones there ought to be her sister and Emilia. If, in that state, something had arisen that the two of them couldn't handle. And if that was not unrelated to this unease—

Rem, driven by mounting impatience. On the surface she kept an expressionless face; within, she made a point of forcing herself to stay calm — yet in truth, she had the flaw of losing sight of her surroundings whenever she threw herself wholly into something.
That flaw bared its fangs at her once again this time.

— Before her eyes, in a world where time seemed to have stopped, Rem watched the earth dragon's head blown clean off.
