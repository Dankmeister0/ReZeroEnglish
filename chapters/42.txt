Arc 2 Chapter 18: "The Third Reunion"

"――――!!"

Waking up to the sound of your own scream is about as bad for the heart as an experience gets.

Kicking the covers away, Subaru came to with his hand outstretched into empty air, breathing hard as he too tasted that same shock.

"...My left hand... it's there, right?"

His left hand was stretched out into the void, as if reaching to grab something. So was the left half of his body — the half that had been torn off, blown away. The loss of his limbs, though it had lasted only the briefest moment, had left Subaru with an unbearable sense of loss.

He sat up and checked his left arm, rolling it from the shoulder to confirm it moved fine. There was no trace of a tear at his left side either, and of course, the reattached left hand bore no mark of fangs.

If there had been scars, he might have written off the sensation of his left half being blown apart as some nightmare from carelessly dozing off on the night of the reckoning — but.

"I've come back again... no, I guess I should say I *got* to come back."

The absence of scars meant exactly that. He had lost to fate, and once more been made to reverse time. In this case, perhaps he should think of it as being granted a reprieve to try again despite his defeat.

Thinking that far, Subaru suddenly became conscious of what day and time it was now.

Going by the rule of thumb from his "Return by Death" experiences, if he'd come back, it should be to the "first day at Roswaal Manor" — but this time even that felt uncertain. It was possible this was some other point in time altogether.

In any case, first he needed to confirm the time — just as that thought crossed his mind—

"Ah, sorry, morning."

He finally noticed the twins huddled together in the corner of the room, warily watching him. If an unconscious man suddenly woke up screaming, of course it would startle them.

Even to Subaru's half-hearted greeting, the two of them — huddled together like small animals — gave no reply.

Scratching his head, Subaru wondered what to do. Surely they'd forgotten him again. That brought a faint ache to his chest, but he ignored the throbbing of his heart and put on a smile.

Friendly, with all the sincerity he could put into it from his side. Even if they'd forgotten everything, Subaru remembered.

"Sorry for the trouble. Natsuki Subaru, reporting for wake-up duty!"

He kicked off the covers, leapt from the bed, and landed in a fierce hawk pose. The twins flinched at the threatening stance, but Subaru, holding the pose, addressed them anyway.

"By the way, what day and time is it right now?"

—And so, at Roswaal Manor, the third first day began.

Once more, he turned his thoughts back to that night of the fourth day, still lodged in his memory.

After making his promise with Emilia, the time had probably been the dead of night, when even the plants and trees were asleep. A sudden chill had struck him, sapping strength and vitality from his entire body — enough force that it could have withered him to death in a short span of time. In other words—

"So my first cause of death was wasting away in my sleep, huh..."

A method of slow, steady strangulation, like being squeezed bit by bit with cotton thread wound tighter and tighter. And that had been with his consciousness braced and awake. If he'd fallen into that state defenseless, asleep, he wouldn't even have had time to wake before being swallowed forever by unconsciousness.

Except—

"That sound of chains, though..."

He could make an educated guess about the wasting sensation, but this part left him completely stumped. A distinctive metallic clatter of long chains overlapping each other — he remembered only that it had rattled his eardrums. Immediately after, there'd been an impact, and his left half had been torn away.

Just recalling the sight brought on a numbing sensation in his left side. His body shouldn't remember the experience, and yet his soul refused to let go of the memory.

Unconsciously cradling his left arm at the shoulder, he muttered,

"There was an attacker... I guess that's what it comes down to. No telling if it's the same person who wasted me away, but at the very least, they're probably on the same side."

If there was any harvest from this run, it was only the fact that a perpetrator existed. Most likely it had been an attack on the mansion, and Subaru had simply been added to the list of victims on the night of the fourth day. Whether the other residents of Roswaal Manor were also on that list, he couldn't be sure, but—

"If I'm on the list, everyone probably is. ...Probably tied to Emilia-tan's Royal Selection, same as the Loot House thing."

He had no idea how much resentment Roswaal had built up among the surrounding nobility, so for now he set aside the possibility of a grudge against him specifically. If he was going to get caught up in someone else's business anyway, he'd rather it be the cute girl's business.

Still, thinking that far, Subaru held his head in his hands. There had been an attack, and he'd managed to figure out that Emilia and the others were likely the targets. That much could be called a success. But—

"Even knowing that, I don't have proof solid enough to explain it, or any way to stop it before it happens."

The trouble with "Return by Death" lay precisely in the fact that he couldn't explain it. And this time, he'd essentially scouted the attack ahead of time. Even if he desperately appealed to Roswaal and got him to take countermeasures, and the attacker saw that and called off the attack as a result — would there be any way to prove that had actually happened?

Worst case, he was prepared to be treated like the boy who cried wolf, if that's what it took to protect Emilia and the others — an acceptable sacrifice.

And directly fighting off the attacker himself was completely off the table. First, Subaru knew all too well he had no combat ability to speak of, and on top of that, he didn't have the faintest idea what he was even up against. Last time, the gist of it was: he'd wet himself crying and gotten casually murdered. Even by his own standards, that was a pretty pathetic summary.

"And on top of that, I didn't even see the guy's face, let alone his weapon. Basically a dog's death."

With no read on the enemy's identity, he couldn't even begin drafting a plan to fight back. Arms crossed, head tilted, hips swiveling in circles, feet moonwalking, he paced round and round the room. And then, to Subaru doing all that—

"You're annoying enough to kill someone. Hurry up and either stop, or choose to get killed, I suppose."

The girl standing in the center of the room, inside the orbit of Subaru's pacing, said this with utter displeasure. Subaru turned to face her sour expression.

"My bad, my bad. But you know, spinning stuff besides my head like this weirdly gets my head spinning too. So cut me some slack. We're not exactly strangers, you and me."

"What relationship could there possibly be between Betty and you? We've only met twice, I suppose."

"Say what you want, your heart's honest. You let me right into your room without even blinking."

"I didn't do anything — you just went and broke through the 'Door Crossing' on your own, I suppose. Honestly, what is even going on. Unbelievable, I suppose."

As ever, Beatrice made no attempt to hide her hostility toward Subaru. Strangely, her attitude was a comfort of sorts, and Subaru found his feet carrying him to the forbidden archive. He'd told himself he'd made peace with it to some degree, but being treated as a total stranger by Ram and Rem still stung. Unlike last time, he'd at least excused himself before leaving the room this time — but there was nowhere else he could turn to for comfort.

"Well, I don't mean to cause you trouble. Just let me hang out, maybe get some tea?"

"There's no such thing here, I suppose. Ugh, honestly, you're a pest."

Touching her own drill curls, Beatrice twisted the corner of her mouth in irritation. Watching her, Subaru suddenly thought of something.

"Come to think of it, you're a mage, aren't you, despite how you look?"

"If you mean I can use magic, then yes, I suppose. But don't lump me in with some run-of-the-mill second-rater, or it'll be a problem, I suppose."

Fufun, Beatrice sent him a slightly haughty sidelong glance. She must have had considerable confidence. Faced with that attitude, so eager to flaunt her own abilities—

"You don't have many friends, do you."

"Where did that come from all of a sudden!?"

"No, I get it 'cause I don't have friends either, but that attitude of yours isn't good. Keep playing the stuck-up type at your age and it'll come back to bite you later."

Ribbing her mercilessly, Subaru made Beatrice sigh. Seeing her reaction, he cut off the current back-and-forth, prefaced with an "Anyway," and got down to business. Yes — there was something he wanted to ask this mage, specifically. Namely—

"Is there... like, magic that wastes away a target and kills them as if in their sleep?"

He wanted to determine whether the state of decay he'd fallen into — was it from poison, illness, or magic. The sudden chill and lethargy that had struck him — Subaru currently suspected the culprit was "magic." His grounds: he couldn't find any trigger for a sudden infectious disease, and if it had been poison, the delayed onset seemed excessive. Especially with poison — he couldn't pin down when it would have been administered. Dinner had been buffet-style, and the soup had been ladled from a single shared pot. It was conceivable it had been slipped into the tea and sweets he'd shared with Emilia, but—

"In that case, there's no way to predict when I'd actually eat or drink the sweets or tea."

In terms of reliability, that method seemed unfit for purpose — especially the second time around, where there appeared to have been an intruder. If the intruder hadn't known Subaru was already in a state of decline, slipping in poison would have been pointless.

At Subaru's question, Beatrice knit her brow, apparently suspicious of why he'd asked such a thing. But perhaps she noticed the unusual absence of any joking color in his expression, because she let out a sigh and shrugged.

"Whether such a thing exists or not — it exists, I suppose."

"It does?"

"Closer to a curse than magic, I suppose. It's the sort of technique Curse Arts Users are better at than mages. Just the kind of underhanded method you'd expect from a shady Curse User, I suppose."

At this point, the sudden appearance of a whole new job class called "Curse Arts User" left Subaru baffled. Beatrice raised a single finger, lecturing.

"Curse Users — later refined into Curse Arts Users — originate from a northern nation called Gusteko. A variant offshoot of magic and Spiritual Arts, I suppose. Though, most of it's just half-baked techniques nobody could properly wield, I suppose."

"But people can actually curse others to death with it, right?"

"That's exactly the half-baked part, I suppose. Its uses exist for almost nothing but harming others. Is there any technique more infuriating, as far as how it treats mana, I suppose?"

Her hatred for the Curse Arts apparently ran deep — there wasn't the faintest sign of compromise in how she spoke of it. Subaru had no inclination to defend curses either, and, wanting more information, he leaned in and pressed her to continue.

"So, that Curse Arts thing — it could pull off the method I just described?"

"I believe it could, I suppose. But there's a much simpler method than bothering to call in a Curse User, I suppose."

Beatrice smiled meaningfully at Subaru, whose brow was furrowed. Then, without warning, she spread her small palm and covered his field of vision. Immediately—

"...Ugh."

A sudden wave of loss of self swept over him, and Subaru's upper body swayed violently before he began to collapse. He caught himself on one knee just in time, but it had been a sensation close to losing consciousness entirely. He blinked a few times and judged that, despite some lingering fatigue, his body still moved.

"So, how does it feel, tasting it for yourself, I suppose?"

Looking down at him, Beatrice gave a small, throaty laugh. The gesture of pressing the back of her hand to her mouth as she smiled was elegant enough, but considering what she'd just done to him, it went well past what he could write off as merely cute.

"You bastard, that was just now—"

"Simply put, I drew up the mana inside your body, I suppose. Repeat that excessively, and it alone would put you in the same state as before, I suppose. Far easier and more reliable than relying on some unlikable Curse User, I suppose."

Subaru glared resentfully up at Beatrice as she spoke with an air of triumph, but he wasn't in any shape for further rebellion. *Why would this be so...* he wondered, and then it struck him. In the current timeline, the first time Beatrice had drained mana from him — commonly called "ManaDra" (Mana Drain) — had been right before he'd collapsed. In other words, the ManaDra that had happened right before he fell asleep had now instead happened the moment he'd woken up.

"Are you trying to kill me!?"

"I held back, I suppose. If you ended up a corpse here, I'd have to step over your body every time I reached for a book — that would be a hassle, I suppose."

"Don't call it a corpse, makes it sound like I'm some bug."

Receiving a look that suggested she genuinely thought of him as exactly that, Subaru began to question why he'd ever thought of this place as some sort of sanctuary. He checked the feeling in his hands and feet, confirmed he could still stand with some effort, and got up. He stamped his feet lightly on the spot, tapping out a little rhythm, before saying—

"Don't tell me you're actually the one who killed me."

"If you were dead, I wouldn't have to bother talking to you like this — that'd be easier, I suppose. Unfortunately, Betty's busy, so even the effort of killing you is a waste, I suppose."

Clasping her hands behind her back, Beatrice slipped past Subaru toward the bookshelves. The hem of her Gothic Lolita outfit swayed as the girl stretched, her hand reaching for a spot just a bit higher than her own height. Then—

"This one?"

"...The one next to it, I suppose. Hurry up and hand it over, I suppose."

"Yeah, yeah."

He pulled a surprisingly thick book from the shelf and handed it over to the pouting girl with a light tap. Still looking dissatisfied, she took it, slipped past Subaru again, and headed for the stepladder in the back. Sitting on a chair apparently didn't suit her the way perching on that stepladder's rung did — whenever Subaru visited the forbidden archive, more often than not she'd be found reading in that particular spot.

"What book's that, anyway?"

"It explains how to get rid of bugs that get into the room, I suppose."

"Whoa, hold on, there're bugs infesting the archive? That's the worst. What kind?"

"Black, big, and it's got a nasty look in its eyes and a foul mouth, I suppose. Also has a big attitude, I suppose."

"That's an oddly specific bug..."

Subaru glanced around, wishing whatever it was would get exterminated quickly. To Subaru, the forbidden archive was nothing more than a gathering of books he couldn't even read, but even so, as a book lover, he'd rather avoid seeing books suffer if he could help it.

Subaru meant that fairly sincerely. Watching him out of the corner of her eye, Beatrice let out a small sigh.

"Do you have any more business here? If not, I'd like you to leave, I suppose."

"Ah, um... right, that mana-slurping thing from before, can anyone use that?"

"That phrasing is nothing short of insulting, I suppose. ...In this mansion, only Nii-cha and Betty can use it, I suppose. Roswaal never had the talent for it, I suppose."

"Huh, I heard he was some kind of jack-of-all-trades."

Maybe he'd puffed himself up a bit, after all — an impertinent thought toward his employer, though admittedly one he hadn't even met yet in this timeline.

"Come to think of it, though, seriously — you and Puck both, could you two lay off going all-in with the mana draining?"

"...Did Nii-cha collect mana from you as well, I suppose?"

"Oh yeah, that bastard, taking advantage of me suffering from mana-bloat, going 'ain't it fiiine, ain't it fiiine' while I struggled against it and—wait, what?"

Right as Subaru described the scene with the air of some corrupt magistrate having his sash unwound, Beatrice's pale face flushed crimson and she looked down. She pressed both hands to her cheeks.

"Oh, how could this be, I suppose. ...I ended up sharing an indirect mana collection with Nii-cha, I suppose."

"What's with that sweet-and-sour indirect-kiss vibe!? If that's how you see it, I feel like a shared can of soda getting passed around, and I don't like it!"

"Come to think of it, it did seem to have a rather mellow flavor to it... shall we go again, I suppose?"

"I'll shrivel up! I'm seriously low on blood right now, seriously!"

"Ah, all your organs went back in, but the blood didn't come back with them, I suppose. Well, even Betty isn't quite that all-powerful, I suppose."

At her regretful sigh, Subaru tilted his head. "Hm?" Something odd was surfacing from her phrasing just now.

"Wait, the way you just said that makes it sound like you were the one who closed up my wound. Stealing Emilia-tan's credit is pretty rotten of you, you know?"

"That half-blood little girl doesn't have the power to heal a fatal wound yet, I suppose. Nii-cha and the girl closed the wound, and Betty healed it... what, I suppose?"

"No, I'm just seriously mega confused."

The backstage details of his own survival had been exposed in the most unexpected place. He'd assumed, given the same flow as being healed of his wounds back in the alley, that it had been Emilia who'd healed him this time too. He narrowed his eyes suspiciously, but the girl staring back at him with a dubious look showed no sign of unease. Unless she was an exceptionally cold-blooded liar, she was probably telling the truth. Which meant Beatrice was—

"An exceptionally cold-blooded liar. Seriously, your personality is the worst, you know that!?"

"You're pretty bad yourself, unable to honestly accept someone else's kindness, I suppose!"

They went back and forth, bickering, and the commotion nearly escalated into a full grapple. In the end, it was Subaru who tried to force a scuffle, only to be gracefully sent flying by Beatrice's magical power.

Slammed upside down against the door, flipped completely over, Subaru watched Beatrice give her long drill curls a great toss.

"I think it's about time you left, I suppose. Your hands have stopped shaking, and you seem to have gotten a handle on covering up the fear, I suppose."

"...You noticed?"

"Were you trying to hide it, I suppose? Being read that easily is rather insulting too, I suppose."

Snorting in boredom, Beatrice waved her hand as if shooing away a bug. Watching that gesture through to the end, Subaru rolled with the momentum and pushed himself up to his feet. The hand he raised in front of his face — its fingers had indeed forgotten to tremble.

This made five deaths total, but it was never a sensation one grew used to. If anything, the experiences of death piled up with each repetition, and just the thought of possibly touching that terror again made his legs go weak. Especially this time, given the manner of his death — with his heart creaking under despair upon his return, who could blame him if courage failed to reach his fingertips or the soles of his feet?

"Well, guess excuse time's over, huh. Not exactly gentle, this."

A wry laugh. Leaving his excuses behind with that, Subaru lifted his head. He put his hand on the doorknob, and before stepping outside, turned back to the girl one last time.

"Sorry about that. But thanks for the help. I'll come bug you again."

"Payment is another indirect mana collection, I suppose. Next time I'll take a good deal more, I suppose. So don't just come waltzing in whenever you please, I suppose."

Eyes still on her book, she tossed the words out in a curt tone and left it at that.

Not letting her see the smile he carved for her, Subaru opened the doorknob and passed through the "Door Crossing." And then—

"—Before that, hey, that bug you mentioned earlier, was that supposed to be me!?"

"Just hurry up and get out already, do you want to get blasted, I suppose!?"

He got blasted, and the "Door Crossing" was carried out.

"Um, can I ask if you're okay?"

"Seriously, your kindness is the only healing I'm getting right now. This is seriously, seriously bad."

Facing the silver-haired girl in the garden, Subaru said this and let his shoulders slump.

Blasted by Beatrice's magic, Subaru had gone flying out the door. His body had been launched straight from the second-floor terrace entrance of the garden and had crashed down into the flowerbed.

Fortunately the soil was soft enough that it hadn't come to anything serious, but depending on how he'd landed, it wouldn't have been strange for this to become his third do-over. Cause of death: an argument.

"That theory about it being her who killed me is getting more and more convincing..."

"Can you stand? Does it jar the wound on your stomach?"

"No sign of the closed-up wound reopening, for now. Getting torn open by the same person who closed it up in the first place would seriously make me feel like a toy. Ahh, sorry, but I'd appreciate a hand up here."

"That flowerbed... Rem was spreading animal manure as fertilizer there yesterday, you know..."

"WAAAAUGH, ABNORMAL PLAAAAY!!"

Yanking his mud-caked right half free of the flowerbed, he tumbled rather than walked his way out of it. In front of Emilia, who was watching from a cautious distance, Subaru flailed his reddish-brown work clothes, filthy with mud and — quite possibly — something worse than mud.

"Doesn't count! This doesn't count, right!? It was yesterday's stuff, it should be purified by now, right!?"

"Um, you know, Subaru, there's a way of thinking where this kind of thing — getting some 'crap' on you — gets reframed as good luck..."

"Emilia-tan's already shifted into comfort mode!?"

Meaning, by the ruling, it was a foul.

Perhaps taking pity on Subaru, half in tears as he shook out his sleeves, Emilia carved a wry smile onto her fine features and gently slipped a hand into the front of her clothes. Then—

"—Puck, wake up."

On her outstretched palm sat a green crystal, glowing faintly. The light spilling from it gradually formed into a small silhouette, and after a mere few seconds, once the glow cleared, a gray-furred, palm-sized cat had appeared.

The cat stretched itself out fully, as if giving a great yawn, and said—

"Mm, morning, Lia. Ah, and Subaru too, you're up, huh."

"Morning, Puck. Sorry to ask right when you woke up, but could you wash Subaru off for me?"

Emilia lifted Puck up in front of her face, closing one eye as she made her plea. It was a gesture Subaru couldn't help but be captivated by. Puck, right up close to it, turned around with an utterly unbothered expression, and upon seeing Subaru's mud-covered state, nodded as if satisfied.

"Sure thing, washing time. There!"

"Wait, you say 'washing time' so casually, but— whoa!?"

Puck thrust both paws forward, aiming them at Subaru. A magic circle unfurled in front of his palms, and from it shot a massive stream of water. The water, fired in a straight line, struck Subaru's right half full-on with its force, sweeping away all the impurities of this world clinging to him in one go, but—

"A flash flood—!!"

He cried out, his body spinning under the momentum of taking the water on his right side.

"Whoops, that's off-balance."

As he spoke, Puck slightly adjusted the water's angle. This time his left half took the blast, and the reverse spin brought him back onto the original trajectory. After that, every time his center line drifted, he received fine adjustments based on where the water hit—

"There, all clean now."

"Right, then left... spun around and around... this heart of mine... it's like... bluuuurgh..."

Hands and knees planted in the sodden grass, Subaru went into a retching stance, commanded by his reeling inner ear. But with his stomach empty, all that came out was drool. Still, after repeating the motion enough times, he did feel somewhat better. Wiping his mouth with a sleeve soaked and limp with water, Subaru got somehow to his feet.

"That first flash flood was no joke... I thought my arm was gonna come off. Hey, don't tell me you two are actually the culprits, seriously."

"I've only got a vague idea what you're suspecting us of, but do I really look like some vicious, wicked spirit to you? I'm offended, truly offended. Hmph."

Hands on hips, the cat who had just been toying with Subaru via water cannon said this.

Subaru landed a flick right on that narrow forehead, drawing out a "Nya!" of pain, before turning once more to face Emilia properly.

Somehow this reunion had turned out unlike any before it, but whether he could recover from here depended entirely on his negotiating skills. One way or another, he felt like he'd gotten along fine with Emilia both the first and second times, so on that basis, he resolved to press forward aggressively this time too. Now, what should his first line be—but any such resolve on Subaru's part—

"Pfft."

"Hoh?"

"Ahaha! Sorry, no, I can't. Ahaha, hehehehe! What are you two even doing... ah, my stomach hurts. Stop, I'm gonna die!"

Was blown entirely away in front of her, as she suddenly burst out laughing.

Pointing at the drenched-rat version of Subaru, Emilia was roaring with laughter, as if venting every ounce of pent-up mirth she could no longer hold back.

Had they not been outside, her reaction looked ready to send her rolling on the ground. Subaru stood stunned for a moment, then met the gaze of Puck, who had somehow ended up floating in midair.

"Well, First Contact: success! Thanks for the assist, Father-in-law!"

"Who's a father-in-law. I'm not handing my daughter over to you that easily."

Puffing out his chest, Puck declared this with an air of great importance.

Hearing that, Emilia's booming laughter burst out across the garden once more.
