Arc 4 Chapter 39: "Eugene"

"What's with that face? Like you've seen something impossible, like you can't believe your own brain's still working, like you've gazed upon the far edge of some fantastical dream—that ridiculous expression."

"...More or less exactly that. Your overblown description doesn't need correcting."

Right in front of him, Otto stood with his hands on his hips, letting out a sigh. Sitting on the floor, Subaru rotated his wrists—newly freed from their shackles—checking their range of motion, and looked up at him to answer.

His body, restrained for more than three days straight, creaked and ached with every movement. The position had made it hard even to roll over, so during meals someone had periodically flipped him over, but there were plenty of problems he only discovered now that he was moving—not just his circulation, but everything else besides. Especially—

"Otto, maybe it's just because you took the blindfold off, but... the vision in my right eye's bad. No, not bad—gone. What's going on?"

"'What's going on,' you ask—honestly, I hesitate to answer... Would you rather I dress it up tastefully, or tell you the plain, unvarnished truth?"

"Say it tastefully first so I'm not too shocked, then give me the blunt version so I can accept reality."

"Greedy, aren't you. ...Er, well: the starboard side of Natsuki-san's noble countenance has had its light sealed away behind an inescapable curtain of darkness."

"Ah, I didn't ask for the edgy middle-schooler routine, so never mind."

Since Otto had started explaining in a tone that practically begged for a chuunibyou "Evil Eye" flourish, Subaru held up a hand to cut him off partway through. Then he brought that same hand to his own right eye, and gingerly, fearfully, checked what he felt.

—Where his right eye should have been, there was no vision to speak of, as if it had simply been cut away. Touching it, he understood exactly why that organ had gone on strike.

Strike wasn't the word for it—it seemed to have packed its bags and gone home for good. Put plainly, there was nothing where his right eye should be but a hollow socket.

"I thought... the story was that it got healed."

"The bleeding's stopped, and the shattered bone's been set. But healing magic depends on the one wielding it, and it isn't omnipotent. ...Bringing back a part that's already died—that's asking a bit much, even for it."

Otto trailed off, looking at Subaru with something like pity. Under that gaze, Subaru let his lips relax into a weak smile, said "Can't be helped," and continued—

"My head got crushed and I nearly died. Losing one eye is something I ought to be able to accept. ...Though if I'd lost both, I probably would've lost the will to live too."

"That's less 'staying positive' and more 'a little bit self-destructive,' don't you think? Please, I'm begging you. Things aren't going to work out going forward without Natsuki-san."

Despite having lost so vital an organ as his right eye, Subaru's state of mind right now was, to his own surprise, calm. Maybe it was because the reality hadn't sunk in yet, or because—unlike when he'd lost most of his right arm to Elsa's attack—there was no pain or bleeding involved this time.

Garfiel's words had been both a lie and the truth. The bleeding had stopped, the wound had closed, and there was no pain. Subaru had simply expected too much from an all-healing miracle spell—considered against the standard of recovering from a fatal wound, Garfiel really had provided treatment matching what he'd claimed.

"Scrupulous, or something. That guy's another one I can't get a read on."

He'd struck down Subaru for attacking Roswaal, yet he'd also treated his wounds. And then he turned around and confined Subaru until the Trial's end, using him as a bargaining chip to pressure Emilia into cooperating.

He'd healed him thoroughly enough to keep him alive, even assigned him a caretaker. Despite loathing the "Witch's Scent" that supposedly clung to Subaru's body, he never stopped coming here. And despite never stopping, he never asked Subaru the one thing that actually mattered.

As if he already knew, somehow, that Subaru wouldn't say anything, that he had no information worth telling—or as if he simply had no interest in anything Subaru might say.

"If he knows, is that from a Gospel too...? Everyone, every single one of them... If this place really is the Witch's testing ground, I guess that'd only make it par for the course."

If everyone involved had a book dictating the future and was simply following it, then couldn't the world at least move forward more simply, as far as Subaru was concerned?

Everyone, every last one, uniting toward a single purpose, pressing forward toward a happy ending. Just once, couldn't the scenario run down a single straight road, the well-worn path everyone expected?

Subaru had a means of knowing the future, and yet every time he redid things, he somehow had to grope from scratch all over again. Shouldn't there be a little more mercy shown to him?

"...Whining doesn't move things forward, and nobody's gonna help me out because of it. Damn it."

"My, aren't we sulking. Well, given the situation Natsuki-san's in, I suppose that's understandable... But 'nobody's gonna help you out'—isn't that a bit much to say? What exactly do you think I'm here for?"

Otto, having caught Subaru's muttering, offered a sympathetic comment before moving to refute the last part of it.

Seeing Otto's smug face up close, Subaru blinked, looking blank.

"Oh, huh, come to think of it, yeah—what's up with you anyway? No, seriously, these past three or four days I've had nothing but time and an infinite well of things to think about, but not once—no exaggeration—did you cross my mind, not even a sliver."

"Wow, incredible, this guy! Even I find it almost refreshing to be dismissed this thoroughly!"

"No, really, it's almost refreshing how completely you'd vanished from my head. Even looking at your face just now, for a second I couldn't tell if you were Otto or the Appa-shop uncle."

"Who is that, the Appa-shop uncle!"

"For me, he's hallowed ground. You could call him Mr. Save Point."

So far, Kadomon had appeared as Subaru's Return by Death checkpoint more often than anyone else.

Cracking jokes as he recalled the scarred, good-natured man, Subaru was, all the while, gathering his thoughts about the loss of his right eye and the sudden turn events had taken.

First, what he needed to draw out was standing right in front of him: Otto. His true intentions.

"Jokes and everything else aside... I've got a lot of things I want to ask."

"Well, I'd imagine so. For my part, I'm curious just what Natsuki-san did to end up captured in a place like this."

"—? Wasn't all this on Roswaal's orders?"

According to Garfiel, it was Roswaal who'd had Subaru confined after he assaulted him—kept under house arrest while Emilia was pressed to face the Trial. But—

"I honestly can't say how much the Margrave's involved. But at the very least, the Sanctuary right now is split in two and in quite a state."

"Split? What's that supposed to mean?"

"Exactly what it sounds like. There's an uproar between the faction led by Ryuzu-sama, who say the villagers who took refuge here—Natsuki-san included—should be set free, and the faction opposing that. Your custody, Natsuki-san, has been left to Garfiel, so it's sat off to the side of the main argument."

Wearing a tired expression, Otto gave a brief rundown of some of the friction that had arisen over the past several days.

In short, exactly as Subaru had feared: friction between the evacuees and the Sanctuary's residents had deepened, discontent had boiled over, and skirmishes had broken out. That small crack had spread until even the Sanctuary residents' own ranks split into factions, and the Sanctuary as a whole had fallen into a state of division.

Subaru caught his breath at hearing the very thing he'd dreaded, then pressed on with, "But—"

"Why would that suddenly happen now, of all things. From what I saw... well, from what I'd have expected—"

In the first loop Subaru had lived through, this split hadn't occurred—at least not by this, the fifth day. That was why his proposal to free the evacuees had gone through, and the promise had been carried out on the morning of the sixth day.

The situation was deteriorating far too quickly. As Subaru reached that conclusion, Otto shook his head and held up a single finger.

"There's nothing sudden about it, really. If Natsuki-san—one of the causes—thinks it's sudden, that's genuinely a problem."

"One of the... causes?"

"I don't know exactly what kind of relationship you had with the people of Arlam Village, Natsuki-san... but it seems clear it was a good one. The moment you were assaulted by Garfiel and disappeared from sight, the mood in the Sanctuary turned dreadful."

"—————"

"For the villagers, Ram-san and the Margrave himself are people they'd hesitate to even speak to, so Natsuki-san's easy familiarity made you the ideal go-between to reach those above. Though I don't think that's the whole of it—not judging by how furious everyone's been, from what I've seen."

At Otto's words, Subaru's mouth fell open, expressing nothing but sheer astonishment.

It was true that, between the last loop and this one, whether or not Subaru was alive and well counted as one of the differences in the Sanctuary's situation. It counted, but he'd never once imagined that his own presence could have such an effect on the hearts of Arlam Village's people—let alone that it could become the trigger for splitting the Sanctuary itself.

Wondering if this was a joke, or Otto simply exaggerating, Subaru studied him—but under Subaru's dubious, single-eyed gaze, Otto only furrowed his brow, showing no other particular reaction. Which meant he was serious. The only question left was whether his eyes for judging such things were any good, but—

"On that point, I'd like to hash it out further before reaching a conclusion."

"I get the sense I'm being treated unfairly again, but fine. More importantly, Natsuki-san—the reason I came here is related to that split, too."

"Related to the split... Ah, so since it kicked off because I disappeared, you're saying that if I go back, maybe I could settle things down? No, honestly, that's putting a lot of expectation on me—or rather, a lot of pressure—"

Subaru's habit of piling underestimation upon underestimation got in the way, keeping him from accepting Otto's words at face value. In truth, even if he did have a strong effect on the peace of mind of Arlam Village's people, now that things had already exploded, he couldn't imagine there was anything he could do about it.

If anything, wouldn't Subaru's current state—having lost his right eye—only pour oil on their anger?

So Subaru shook his head at Otto's proposal, a difficult look on his face—but Otto waved his hands, piling denial on denial with an "no, no—"

"As if I'd think Natsuki-san had that much power. That's far too full of yourself."

"Same goes for you, so I won't push it, but you're overdoing it in your own way too. ...So then, what's the point of getting me out of here?"

"What I want to avoid, above all, is a large-scale clash between the evacuees and the Sanctuary side. With that in mind, I was hoping Natsuki-san might lend a hand toward an escape from the Sanctuary."

"A hand... toward an escape?"

Subaru narrowed his one good eye at the ominous word that had just come flying out, turning it over in his mouth as he considered it. And then, suddenly, an idea of what Otto was thinking occurred to him.

Subaru wet his lips and said "don't tell me" to the ever-shrewd Otto.

"You're planning to get the villagers out in all the confusion, while the Sanctuary side's in chaos and can't keep control. And you want me to help with that escape—that's what this is?"

"Correctly deduced. Nothing better than a quick understanding. Time's pressing too, so if possible, I'd like Natsuki-san's unconditional cooperation."

"...Not until I've confirmed the details. If you're going in with no plan at all, I'm not going to nod along that easily. I'll grant there's a sense that there's a real chance here, but if the opposing side catches wind of it, there'll be no excuse that'll cover us."

More than anything, carelessly provoking whatever faction opposed the Sanctuary's liberation would jeopardize the safety of Emilia, Roswaal, and the others caught up inside it. Roswaal could go hang for all Subaru cared, but he wanted to avoid any harm reaching Emilia, Ram, or Patrasche.

"If it's not too much trouble, I'd like my name added to that list of people you don't want getting hurt."

"A man taking a beating in the thick of it is just how things go. I'm old-fashioned that way. 'A husband's best when he's healthy and out of the house,' and all that."

"That's a saying I've never heard before, but I get the feeling you're using it wrong."

Subaru made a sour face at Otto's correct rebuttal. Then, clearing his throat once, he steered the conversation back on track.

"If it's not just desperate improvisation, tell me the plan. I'll decide whether to cooperate or rat you out after that."

"The fact that 'rat you out' is even an option is fairly terrifying, but... the plan's simple. I've already reached an agreement with the Sanctuary's Moderate Faction. While they hold down the Radical Faction, we break through the barrier by Dragon Carriage. And then it's just a matter of saying our farewells."

"Isn't that a little reckless? And this ally of yours, just who—"

"I'll tell you that once you clearly state that you'll cooperate, Natsuki-san. What I want to leave to you is persuading the villagers, and dealing with Garfiel, whose movements are hard to predict. If it's the villagers and Natsuki-san, he seems likely to come at you."

"So I'm a convenient piece of bait, huh. Not that I'm denying it."

As far as Garfiel went, Otto's read was surely right—if Subaru and the evacuees were together, Garfiel would undoubtedly lose his head and lunge at Subaru first. Though getting things to that point in the first place looked like it would take considerable effort.

"To begin with, I've lost track of where Garfiel even stands. He's practically family to Ryuzu-san, so he should be with the Moderate Faction."

"Apparently that's how he was originally counted, but given how he's treated Natsuki-san and Emilia-sama, that judgment's become difficult. We've settled on treating him as a passive enemy, even if not an active one."

"They're watching pretty closely, this ally of yours. ...Me included. By the way, what happens if I don't cooperate?"

"In that case, I'd think the plan is to spread word only that Natsuki-san's been freed, and remove at least Garfiel—who feels like the strongest potential enemy—from the list of obstacles."

"Perfect, damn it. The moment my hands and feet are free, I'm already going against what Garfiel wants, no question. Damn it, I've got no choice but to go along."

Scratching furiously at his head, Subaru had no choice but to accept the reality that he was already dancing in the palm of Otto and his ally's hands. The moment things had come to this, Subaru had no option left but to dance to their tune.

That said, he didn't actually resent the situation as much as his words let on.

Because if the action Otto and the others were taking led to the evacuees' escape from the Sanctuary, then maybe he'd finally see the answer to the empty, deserted Sanctuary Subaru had witnessed.

At the very least, if this plan succeeded, the evacuees' presence could vanish from the Sanctuary without Subaru's own hand being the cause. The problem was, that alone wouldn't solve the mystery.

"After all, it still wouldn't explain why not a single one of the Sanctuary's residents—people who shouldn't have been able to leave—was anywhere to be found..."

That the people who could leave, left—that conclusion he could accept, whatever the process behind it. But it gave no answer for why the people who shouldn't have been able to leave were also nowhere to be seen. Either way, he needed to get out of here, if only to watch how the situation developed.

Following Otto and the others' proposal and seeing this loop through to its end wasn't a wasted effort.

"Come to think of it, a little late to ask, but—how'd you even manage to find me? This place seems like it's treated as some kind of hidden room, even within the Sanctuary."

Recalling Garfiel's words, and the interior of the room he'd only now finally been able to make out after spending so long inside it, Subaru cracked his neck as he looked around.

The dim room's only light source was a Crystal Lamp glowing faintly, and with no windows at all, no outside light could reach in. The building was made of wood, and the construction was shoddy enough that damp patches like rain leaks showed up here and there. The dripping water sound that had worn away at blindfolded Subaru's nerves was probably due to these defects. What a nuisance.

"Well, finding this place through any proper method must have been quite the ordeal, I'd imagine. Though rather than a hidden room, I get more of a 'secret base' feeling from it."

"Yeah, looking around the room again, that's about the impression I got too. This handmade feel—honestly, it doesn't look like a professional carpenter's work. Feels more like some young guy with strength to spare threw it together by gut instinct and vibes alone."

The image Subaru had held of a "confinement chamber" and this shabby little shack were worlds apart. This was nothing more than a cramped, stuffy little room.

To Subaru, holding that impression, Otto gestured to set the topic aside with a "well, leaving that be for now"—

"Finding this place was my doing, credit where it's due. I think this is a scene where I ought to be praised without reservation—so how will Natsuki-san react, I wonder?"

"I am impressed, honestly, and I'm grateful you found me. How'd you manage it?"

"Heh heh heh. Want to know? You want to know, don't you. You do want to know."

"Yeah, I want to know. Don't tell me you used that Divine Protection of the Soul of Language of yours to gather information from every insect, lizard, and plant in the forest and worked your way here that way."

"That's exactly it, so could you please give me back the sense of superiority that had just been welling up in me!?"

Otto lamented having his punchline stolen out from under him. But Subaru, having had what he'd meant as a joke confirmed as truth, couldn't hide his inward surprise.

Even though he'd known about it, the sheer versatility of Otto's Divine Protection of the Soul of Language was something else.

Once before, Subaru had followed the lead of that Divine Protection while riding Patrasche to save Emilia and the others from a desperate situation. Even then, Otto had pulled off the same feat—listening to insects and plants, stitching together shortcut after shortcut to close a distance that should never have been closeable.

"Your Divine Protection is really convenient, you know that?"

"...It's not really that great a thing, though."

Otto's response to Subaru's admiring sigh came at a strangely low ebb of energy. Subaru furrowed his brow at that, but Otto didn't let him pursue it, clenching his fist with an "anyway—"

"Before long, Emilia-sama's Trial will begin. Garfiel will have to head for the tomb during that time and leave here. In other words, now's our chance."

"That's a way-too-short prep window before we execute the plan. ...Though I get why the scheduling's this tight—between me not being found and the fire lit under your ass, it can't be helped."

Grumbling at Otto for the rush, Subaru—already committed to cooperating—turned his body, checking his condition. Not that it was satisfactory, but meals had been brought to him, and his waste had been dealt with without fail. Thinking back on it now, not knowing who exactly had been doing that was a supremely embarrassing situation, but it brought to mind the time he'd broken a leg and been hospitalized.

In any case, aside from his creaking body, he found no other problems. Subaru nodded at Otto, who was watching him, and made to step forward, ready to move—

"Oh, one more thing before we go, all right?"

"...What now, honestly. Please make this really the last one, all right? If we spend too much more time, the plan's going to move to its next stage without us and we'll just end up looking like fools."

"Sorry, sorry. ...Why are you helping with something this dangerous, anyway?"

"—————"

Otto, who had worn the look of a man having cold water thrown on his momentum, let his expression go blank at Subaru's question.

It was, Subaru thought, the question he should have asked first. Certainly, for both the evacuees and the Sanctuary's residents, avoiding a clash between the two sides was mutually beneficial. It was desirable for Subaru too, and would help Emilia and Roswaal besides. And yet—

"I don't see where your benefit fits into any of that. Maybe I'm just too dumb to imagine it... but not knowing that part, it just doesn't sit right with me."

It wasn't that he wanted to doubt Otto, but the fact remained that this part didn't sit well with him. In truth, for Otto, the troubles surrounding the Sanctuary were an involvement that had come about only by circumstance. Originally, the friction here—not to mention anything tied to the Royal Selection—had nothing to do with him at all.

The moment the tangled situation started to feel like a hassle, the option to abandon involvement and evacuate alone had still been open to him. Even factoring in his stated goal—wanting a connection to the Margrave—the current state of things was surely "too poor odds even to gamble on."

Even if not to the degree Subaru faced, Otto too should have no clear light showing him a way to break through the situation.

Precisely because of that, Subaru couldn't understand why Otto would take on this much risk to stand by him. As mentioned before, across these three days of ceaseless worry, Subaru had truly forgotten Otto existed at all. He hadn't found in Otto any element of problem or doubt. In a sense, that itself could be called a form of trust in him.

A trust bordering on avoidance—the sense that, with this much bad fortune already piled up, there couldn't possibly be some "something" about Otto he needed to spare a thought for.

That was why, now, he wanted to know Otto's true intentions—the ones that might overturn all that.

If even Otto turned out to have some hidden side Subaru couldn't believe, then that would already be—

"Answer me, Otto. Why are you working this hard for me?"

A quiet question. It was small, but a genuine watershed all the same.

Holding his breath, Subaru waited for Otto's answer. Taking in Subaru's question, choosing his words silently, Otto stared back at him and said—

"What kind of person do you think I am, Natsuki-san?"

"Like, the type to reach out for a bit of loose change right in front of you, only to spill the bags you were already carrying in your other hand... that kind of scatterbrained image comes to mind."

"That's a pretty awful way to think of me! And the fact that I have actually done something like that before makes it even more infuriating, like you watched it happen!!"

That was Subaru's honest image of Otto—or perhaps, the image he wished were true.

Otto voiced a complaint at that harsh, unreasonably harsh, assessment, then shook his head wearily with a "honestly," before saying—

"Look, Natsuki-san."

"...Yeah."

"—Is it really so strange to try and help out a friend?"

—For a moment, not understanding what had been said, time stopped inside Subaru.

Probably a few seconds passed before time started moving again. But even once it did, Subaru still couldn't quite grasp the meaning of what had just been said. What had Otto just said?

Eugene? Who's Eugene again? Was there someone by that name around here?

"W-why are you frozen there with that shocked look on your face?"

"No, it's just, a name I don't recognize suddenly came up and I couldn't keep up with the conversation. So, um, this Eugene guy—who exactly is that?"

"I have no idea what conclusion you reached, but you're wrong from head to tail! Not Eugene—friend! I said 'friend'!"

"Friend!? Between who and who!?"

"Me! And Natsuki-san!"

Subaru's eyes went wide with disbelief, while Otto, out of breath, stared back equally dumbfounded. Stamping his foot on the floor, he waved a hand and said, "Listen here—"

"I was captured by the Witch Cult and came within a hair of dying, and Natsuki-san and the others saved me. After that, well, I mentioned various deals and helped out. I even came here as a way to get an introduction to the Margrave, so in that sense, my relationship with Natsuki-san might have been one built on circumstance and position."

"—————"

"Even so, if you strip away all that troublesome stuff, I do think of Natsuki-san as a friend. I've got plenty to say about how you usually treat me, sure, but throwing impossible demands and nonsense back and forth like this—that's the kind of closeness it is, isn't it."

Growing embarrassed partway through, Otto scratched at his nose with a finger and looked away.

And listening to Otto's words, Subaru kept his silence. Having reached the end of what he had to say, Otto shot him a suspicious look at his lack of any reaction.

A hint of anxiety showed on his face, probably because Subaru hadn't said anything remotely affirming in response to what he'd just said. Maybe he was thinking he'd forced his friendship on someone who didn't want it.

Imagining Otto's inner thoughts like that, an emotion welled up in Subaru's heart. And the reaction that emotion produced was—

"—Pfft."

"...Huh?"

"Wahahahaha! F-friends? Friends, huh! Oh, right, right. Otto, you wanted to be friends with me!"

"Wha—!?"

Unable to hold it in, Subaru burst out laughing and slapped the shoulder of a red-faced Otto, none too gently. Even so, the urge to laugh didn't fade, and clutching his stomach, Subaru stomped his foot on the floor.

"Bwahaha, friends. Ah, damn it. Otto, you—you little punk."

"Ow, ow! What are you doing! Ugh, I was an idiot to even say it! I knew perfectly well Natsuki-san didn't think of me that way. But even so, there's no need to laugh that hard about it!"

"No, no, no, no, how could I not laugh. It's not that you're the one being ridiculous. ...It's that my own stupidity is so awful, laughing's all I've got left."

Wiping away the tears that had welled up from laughing so hard with his left hand, Subaru somehow reined in the unstoppable urge to laugh and straightened up. He looked Otto square in the face.

Otto wore the face of a man who regretted ever having said the word "friend." But faced with him, what came to visit Subaru's heart was gratitude, and an apology so overwhelming there was no helping it.

—What "Otto's true intentions." What "there might be something hidden." What "not knowing what to believe."

Otto, who had called Subaru his friend, who had worried for him and come to help. Faced with his very existence, how foolish Subaru had been, rushing to doubt before ever trusting the heart behind it.

How base he'd been, deciding there had to be something, and imagining that "something" as steeped in malice.

Tossed about by his circumstances, so lost he could no longer read the feelings of those around him, believing only in the existence of malice and forgetting that unconditional goodwill could exist too—what an ingrate he'd been.

—What, exactly, did Natsuki Subaru know of this world, that he could give up on it and throw it all away so completely?

After dying and redoing the world only a handful of times, what did he think he'd come to understand? He hadn't even noticed the loyalty of a friend standing right beside him.

Not understanding Subaru's self-mockery and self-reproach, Otto still wore a look of confusion on his face. Subaru offered him a smile, and, feeling strangely clear-headed, drew a breath.

"Sorry. You're my friend, Otto. —Thanks for coming to help me."

The building where Garfiel had kept Subaru confined stood in a stretch of woods away from the village where the Sanctuary's residents lived, along a path so convoluted that without Otto leading the way, Subaru would surely have gotten lost and been unable to escape.

"Thinking about it like this, we really would've been hopeless without your Divine Protection."

"Keep your voice down, please. I don't remember the way either, so I'm relying on the plants and frogs and lizards. Get on their bad side and they'll trick you into thinking it's the exit and lead you off a cliff."

"Nature's creatures are terrifying!"

Otto picked his way carefully, straining his ears as he chose each step. Following behind him, Subaru, not yet used to seeing with only one eye, made his unsteady way through the gaps between the trees, huffing and puffing.

Losing his depth perception and half his field of vision was, sure enough, painful. The real sense of loss would probably hit him in earnest later, but it was already doing more than enough to hinder him.

And yet, strangely, he felt no resentment toward Garfiel for inflicting this wound on him.

The awareness inside him that he'd done something deserving of being struck down, and the strange contradiction he sensed in Garfiel—his speculation on such things kept him from resenting him.

If only he had a little more room to let his imagination take flight, he felt he could give shape to his thoughts about Garfiel.

"Including that unconfirmed Gospel business—I need a bit more information."

For now, there was nothing to do but shelve it.

Feeling a pang of frustration, Subaru let out a small yelp of pain as a branch grazed his right ear, struggling not to lose his footing as he hopped over a root. And then—

"There it is. We're about to come out near the village."

Hearing Otto's call, Subaru strained his blurred vision as hard as he could to look ahead. Through the gaps in the trees, beyond the dark green, he could see the scattered lights of the village.

Then they broke free of the forest, and all at once the light of stars and moon poured down from the sky, clearing up the view that had been nothing but darkness until now.

Letting out a breath and looking around, Subaru confirmed, standing beside Otto, that they'd made it back to the Sanctuary's village. He noted that it was now night, with the moon risen, and that Emilia's attempt at the Trial had probably already begun at the tomb.

The urge to dash off in that direction, to stay by her side, welled up in him. But suppressing that feeling, Subaru turned back to Otto.

"If the Trial's happening right now, this is our timing for the escape, right? What's the arrangement—where do we meet up with this ally of yours?"

"As for my ally—"

Raising his head at Subaru's rapid-fire question, Otto started to reach a hand out toward the village. But that motion was cut off partway—by a voice.

"—No need to worry. She's already here."

Cutting into their conversation, a single figure stepped forward, footsteps sounding out.

An apron dress built around black as its base color. Beneath the starlight, the white apron stood out, dressing her figure—youthful yet lovely—in an even more otherworldly air.

"First, for making it back safely... yes, I suppose I'll offer you my Blessing, at least for that much, Barusu."

Her pink hair swaying as she spat out her poison, Ram—Otto's ally—stood there with a cool, composed face, welcoming Subaru and the others back.
