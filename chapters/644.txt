"—the Authority of 'Gluttony.'"

Even if she wished to part ways with her past as an Archbishop of Sin, that cross would never let her escape.

For her sake, and for the sake of the Subaru who had chosen her, he had to face it.

Siblings bound together, who had set a wicked precedent.

Those precedents — the deeds of the sinners who bore the names of stars — she, as an atoner, would devour anew.

Newborn, the girl who devoured stars — Spica of 'Star-Eating.'

"Ugh, no good — I've got seriously bad chemistry with these not-quite-human 'zombies.'"

Fluttering his sleeve, Halibel murmured this while watching the "Three-Headed" dragon crumble to dust from the edges inward.

He dismissed the three clones he'd made of himself as well, and let out a long, weary breath. Taking on something on the scale of a Dragon was, sure enough, a real chore.

If possible, he'd like this to be the last of the monster-slaying, thank you very much.

"But then again, Ana and the others were saying something about more of the same turning into zombies, weren't they."

If that was true, then the reappearance of the very "Three-Headed" dragon he'd just felled was more than possible.

Probably, Halibel could win again next time too, but whether he'd have an environment where he could avoid dragging in the people around him, the way he had this time, was doubtful. And having his own hands tied up in the meantime was hardly reassuring, hired hand that he was. Julius, at Anastasia's side, was certainly skilled, but—

"He's probably not quite on my level, though."

Cracking his neck, he lit the pipe he hadn't been able to smoke during the fight, and savored the smoke.

Then, having watched the "Three-Headed" dragon's body vanish completely, Halibel turned and searched far, far into the distance for the Coupled Dragon Carriages that had gone racing off, leaving him behind.

And then—

"Man, if anyone deserves a candy for all that hard work, it's me. ...Ah, right, I'd die if I ate candy."

Muttering things like that, he set off running, following the carriage ruts stretching away into the distance.
