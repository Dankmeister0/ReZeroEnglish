Arc 4 Chapter 26: "Blockage"

—I shouldn't have asked.

Sweat trickled down from Subaru's forehead and dripped from his chin, and once again—he'd lost count of how many times now—he was made to recognize his own foolishness.
The ringing echoing inside his skull felt somehow distant, while his pounding heart cried out in pain.

"You regret having asked... is that ri-i-ight?"

Roswaal tilted his head atop the bed as he looked at the silent Subaru.
At the jester's question, his fingers toying with his indigo hair, Subaru couldn't answer at once. Only, steadying himself with quiet breaths, he said—

"I was just a little surprised, is all. How do I put it... Emilia turned out to be a bit older than me, more than I expected."

"Oh my, you didn't know? Being a half-elf, Lady Emilia belongs to a long-lived race, even if not as long-lived as full elves. As for elves, it's said they don't die unless they're killed, after a-a-all."

Roswaal played along with Subaru's words, which were meant to cover up what he really felt.
The traits of elves he described didn't seem to differ much from the elf settings handled in subculture works. Subaru didn't know how far that applied to half-elves, but if he believed Roswaal's words, Emilia's actual age was, at the very least—

"At least a sixty-year gap, huh... Even for a guy like me who's into older-sister types, I don't have much experience with a romantic interest who's got this much of an age gap."

"I'm not quite following, but... from the way you're talking, have you had dealings with other members of long-lived races besides Lady Emilia, I wonde-e-er?"

"Well, immortals and vampires are basic archetypes in dating sims. There's also the loli-baba category, like Ms. Ryuzu—it runs deep."

Though, non-human types and the loli-baba attribute weren't really Subaru's thing. His strike zone leaned toward admired older-sister figures or upperclassmen at school.
So naturally, hearing there was a sixty-year gap did give him some pause, but—

"But if she's that cute, I can forgive anything. No problem. Emilia-tan is still my number one star, no matter what."

Besides, as is often the case with this kind of setting, long-lived races might grow slowly precisely because of their long lifespans, meaning they could experience the passage of time differently from how humans mature.
Like the age difference between animals and humans, there was the possibility that a human's twenty years might be equivalent to two hundred years for an elf—

"Thinking about it that way, a ninety-year-old Emilia-tan would still be a young pup, a little girl... no, from an elvish perspective, possibly even a small child. Hey now, someone that beautiful and sexy-cute turning out to be a small child... that's basically its own genre at this point."

"Sorry to interrupt while your delusions are going so well, but reality is much ha-a-arsher than you think. A person grows exactly as much as the time they've spent. Even among elves, it's not so common for mental growth to lag behind, is i-i-it?"

"And here I was desperately trying to build a case against the fact that Emilia-tan has seriously been treating me like a kid this whole time..."

Even though it was nothing more than wishful thinking, having it shot down so easily didn't feel good. And to Subaru, who cast him a resentful look, Roswaal continued, "Besides—"

"—the way you're turning away from your true feelings like that, trying to let it slide with a nod and a smile, I'm not so fond of that either, I thi-i-ink. Say, Subaru-kun."

"—"

"Do you regret that it was from my mouth you had to hear it, and not so-o-omeone else's?"

"...You really are an unpleasant guy."

Roswaal dug up the feelings Subaru had been trying to gloss over, deflect, and somehow swallow down. Hurling a feeble curse at him, Subaru cursed himself once more.
For the dishonesty of hearing—not from Emilia's own mouth, but from someone else's—about the cross she carried on her slender shoulders, borne upon her back.

"...The 'Trial' showed me my past. It's surely showing Emilia hers too. If that's true, then the past she's seeing is..."

"If it's meant to make you confront the past you most want to look away from... then what Lady Emilia is seeing is, without a doubt, the day the Elior Forest froze over, I'd wa-a-ager."

Roswaal confirmed Subaru's fear.
And with that thought backed up, Subaru came to realize just how terrible a thing he had been trying to force upon Emilia.
What was all that about settling things with the past, about there being something to gain from overcoming it—

"So could I face a past where I'd frozen a bunch of people solid...?"

The scale of the sins Emilia and Subaru had each left behind in their pasts was different.
Of course, it was true that for Subaru, his feelings for his father and mother had been a matter significant enough to settle even across the boundary between worlds. He had no intention of belittling that, nor would he allow it to be belittled.

But on the other hand, what about the problem Emilia carried?
Would the past she had to face affirm her and forgive the mistake she'd made, the way Subaru had been loved by his father and mother, pushed forward, and ultimately forgiven?
And could she accept that, and part ways with her past?

"Is it even certain that Emilia froze the forest... that elf settlement? I'm pretty sure I heard it's barely been touched by human hands. Isn't this some kind of mistake...?"

"Indeed, the facts remain unclea-a-ar. But I heard the whole account directly from Lady Emilia's own mouth. Lady Emilia herself confessed that it was she who froze the forest solid. What room is there to insert any other word into that?"

"So if the actual facts aren't clear, then maybe it's some kind of misunderstanding... In the first place, Emilia isn't the kind of girl who could do something like that..."

"No-o-o, you don't understa-a-and, that won't do at a-a-all, Subaru-kun."

Roswaal checked Subaru's clinging words, drawing out his sentence-endings three times over. Cut off, Subaru's sharp gaze took on an edge of danger, but Roswaal waved a hand with an innocent look on his face.

"What actually happened isn't the issue here, not at a-a-all. The problem is that Lady Emilia has convinced herself, has hardened her mind around the idea that 'it was I who froze the forest.'"

"—"

"Within Lady Emilia, that is the truth. And the past built upon that truth is what's raining down on Lady Emilia in the crypt right now—now the-e-en, what do you think should be done?"

"What... what are you even thinking?"

Roswaal asked, seeming almost cheerful about it. Not understanding his true intent, Subaru let the words slip out almost involuntarily.
Why, how, could this man carry even a hint of a smile in a situation like this?

"This isn't about sympathizing with the past Emilia's carrying, or trying to empathize with it or whatever... You know the weight of what she's shouldering, you know she'll suffer, and you're making her take on a 'Trial' she might not even be able to overcome—so how can you look so pleased about it?"

"Hmm-m-m."

"It's insane, isn't it?! You... aren't you the one who wants to make Emilia queen? Aren't you in a position to lend a hand in making her queen? I get what you're aiming for. If Emilia overcomes the 'Trial' and liberates the 'Sanctuary,' that might win her support from both the residents of the 'Sanctuary' and the people of Arlam Village... I understand that much."

But—

"While you're trying to make her do that, at the crucial point you're pushing Emilia away. This whole gamble doesn't work unless the 'Sanctuary' gets liberated... so why, now that it's about to fall apart, can you still act so composed!?"

"........."

"Emilia says she has to become queen. And I want to help make that happen too. ...Do you actually intend to make her queen?"

"—Of course I do."

Subaru had raised his voice, shaking with anger, hurling his feelings at the silent Roswaal. But Roswaal's answer to that final question gave Subaru—his face flushed with that passion—the illusion of being doused in cold water.
Before his eyes, Roswaal remained in the same unchanged position—his gaze fixed straight on Subaru, glaring. And he said,

"Do I intend to make her queen? Of course I do, naturally I do. —There's no way any being could yearn to make Lady Emilia queen more than I do. To say nothing of someone of your caliber—even Lady Emilia herself couldn't compare. I have reasons to do this that are beyond your understanding."

"Roz... waal?"

"To grab hold of me and ask if I have the will to do it—that's laughable. Truly laughable. —So you still haven't even gotten that far, have you."

A tone that carried even a quiet anger—it was the first time Subaru had heard such raw passion from Roswaal.
But even that passion sank beneath its own heat as he spoke, and by the time of his final murmur it had reduced to mere smoldering. And with that, he closed with one meaningful remark—

"Subaru-kun. I'm afraid that's all for toni-i-ight. I'm still very much recovering from illness, you know. I'd like to be allowed to rest a little, if you would-d-d."

"Well... no, fine."

Subaru reached out a hand to stop Roswaal, who seemed intent on cutting the conversation short, but he sensed the futility of putting it into words and withdrew his hand.
Roswaal's manner, declaring the end of the conversation in that flippant tone, clearly refused to go any further into deep matters. And if he truly meant to reject everything, then Subaru—who would lose even to a man lying flat on a bed—had no way to pry anything more out of him.

Shaking his head, Subaru turned his back on the bed, even as he felt lingering reluctance trail after him.
He'd conveyed what needed conveying. What would become of the treatment of the people of Arlam Village remained uncertain, but Ryuzu likely wouldn't refuse Roswaal's proposal.
If they could get past that, they would surely gain progress rather than stagnation. Though it was only one of the many goals Subaru had to fulfill.

"—Subaru-kun."

Suddenly, as Subaru moved to leave the room, Roswaal called him to a stop from behind. When he halted and turned around, Roswaal—already lying with his head on the pillow—spoke without looking his way.

"You gained the qualification, didn't you?"

"Ah. Oh, right, I hadn't said. Yeah. I can take the 'Trial' too. If the condition's just going through that baptism once, then maybe you could too..."

"—No-o, that won't happen. The crypt will most likely never accept me. Having had the wound of its rejection carved into me like this, that much has become perfectly clear."

Recalling their exchange in the previous world, Subaru—who had been deliberately avoiding the topic of the "qualification"—raised an eyebrow in faint surprise. In the previous world, he remembered, Roswaal had grown terribly sullen upon learning Subaru had gained the qualification to challenge the "Trial."
But now, something like loneliness—far more than just a trace of it—seemed to hang about him.

"...Choose what's best, Subaru-kun."

"What?"

"In this place, you're the one who can move most freely. You're not bound to the 'Sanctuary.' Nor are you entangled in the constraints of the Royal Selection."

"—"

"Your wish—go get it with your own two hands, by struggling and thrashing and clawing forward yourself. Agonize, suffer, waver, lose your way, and if you obtain it that way... even if you're not satisfied, even if you can't accept it, you'll at least see it through to a conclusion."

Roswaal spoke without showing his face. His words, unlike him, lacking any of his usual jester's drawn-out endings, left Subaru stunned, able only to stand there dumbly.
And after a while, he realized just how abruptly he'd been encouraged, and said,

"Wha... that's totally not like you. What's gotten into you, Roz-cchi?"

"I simply felt like doing something out of character, that's a-a-all it is. —It seems, after all, that I won't make it in time, you see-e-e."

Subaru couldn't grasp the meaning behind those words so heavy with resignation. But before he could question it, Roswaal's hand rose from the bed and waved him off, urging him to leave.
Seeing that, and watching Roswaal roll over, deliberately turning his will away from him, Subaru let out a long breath through his nose.

"Good night."

Saying only that, he left the room.

"...I trust you didn't burden Roswaal-sama."

The first thing out of her mouth, Ram—who greeted Subaru as he emerged from the bedroom—demanded, her gaze sharp.
The pink-haired girl's height should have been half a head shorter than Subaru's, but the intimidating force she gave off whenever pressing him on matters concerning Roswaal made her frame seem twice its actual size. In short, doused with too much pressure, he shrank back.

"We just had a perfectly calm talk. It's not like we grabbed each other by the collar and wrestled, so don't worry about it."

"How optimistic of you. You have no idea how terrible a state Roswaal-sama was in after setting foot in the crypt and being rejected... That's why you can talk like that."

Ram clicked her tongue at Subaru's answer, making no attempt to hide her displeasure. Wryly amused by how clearly she'd set her priorities, Subaru said,

"He said he was going to sleep, so I just got kicked out. ...I managed to say what needed saying properly, so I don't think that's a problem."

"I see. ...At the very least, if the request comes from Roswaal-sama's own mouth, I think Ryuzu-sama will accept it. Garf, though... I still don't know how he'll respond."

"If he still gives you trouble even so, I'm counting on you to use your feminine wiles. Strike a pose and beg cutely for it... can you even do that?"

"Are you implying Ram isn't cute?"

"No, I mean, looks-wise alone I think you're super cute, but..."

After all, aside from the color of her hair, she was the spitting image of Rem. There was no way she couldn't be lovely. The problem lay in how thoroughly Ram's refreshingly blunt personality canceled out that loveliness, and then some.

"And yet Garfiel keeps hanging around you—guy's got some pretty bad taste, huh. No wait, you can't tell just by looking... you're like a pufferfish of a woman, you—ow ow ow ow!"

"I can tell that's not a compliment, so I'll respond accordingly, Barusu."

Ram had stomped her heel down hard on the top of his foot, and as Subaru's eyes welled up with tears, she gave a small snort. Then, standing before the bedroom door where Roswaal slept, she said,

"Ram will change Roswaal-sama's bandages, and then rest. Barusu, you sleep in the same cathedral as yesterday. Even you should remember the location at least, shouldn't you?"

"My sense of direction's surprisingly decent, you know. Besides, it's the biggest building here—no way to miss it. Though the lack of lights is a pain."

The "Sanctuary" was an analog sort of place, relying on nothing but starlight. Even so, on clear nights, light poured down from a sky full of stars, so the darkness didn't feel as bad as one might think. But tonight, unfortunately, the sky was clouded over.
Peering quietly outside from within the building, aside from the faint, scattered glow of a few household lights, it was dark enough to call it near-total blackness.
He did feel a touch uneasy about the way back, but—

"It's not like I'll get lost, wander into the forest, and randomly encounter a beast for a BAD END or something. It'll be fine, fine."

"I feel like that possibility, which shouldn't have existed, just had its odds raised by that comment, but fine. Barusu, the cathedral is straight to the right once you leave here."

"Got it, got it. The chopstick-holding hand, right? Not that this world has that particular custom."

Meals in this world relied almost entirely on a culture of knife, fork, and spoon silverware, so Ram apparently couldn't follow Subaru's remark. Thinking he might try whittling wood into mass-produced chopsticks someday if the opportunity arose, he said,

"Anyway, I'll head back to the cathedral for now... It's not confirmed whether we can go back to the village, and getting people's hopes up for nothing wouldn't be great, so it's probably best not to bring up the liberation thing yet, right?"

"I think that's the sensible choice. We'll get the word through tomorrow... so at the earliest it'll be the day after. In the meantime, it'd be good for you to think about how you're going to face Echidna's crypt."

"—"

Meaning it to be the last exchange before leaving the building, they traded these words—when Subaru suddenly went still. Ram made a puzzled face at his stillness, but her expression deepened into further confusion when she saw Subaru turn back around. And then—

"...What did you just say?"

His voice came out hoarse with the question. Hearing what Subaru had said, Ram, still wearing her suspicious look, cast her memory back.

"I said to think about how you're going to face the crypt."

"No. A bit before that."

"Are you implying Ram isn't cute?"

"That's way too far back! ...You said Echidna's crypt, right?"

Sensing there might be another couple of small jokes thrown in before they reached the right answer, Subaru cut straight to it himself rather than let things drag on. Watching Ram nod, however reluctantly, he pressed a hand to his own forehead—his mind churned by a torrent of memory surging back at tremendous speed.

"Echidna."

The name of the Witch of Greed; a girl in white hair, dressed as if in mourning clothes; a person who called herself the embodiment of intellectual hunger; and a precious "boku-speaking girl" slot in this other world. And—

"What was she even thinking... pulling some trick on someone's memory like that..."

She was also the one who had tampered with Subaru's memory—perhaps as insurance to keep him from telling others about her.
At the end of the tea party, and again at their parting during the "Trial," she had forced an Oath upon Subaru. If the price for that was the qualification to challenge the "Trial," then he was resigned to accept it.

"Whether it was incomplete or just half-assed... either way, the restriction's been lifted!"

The constraint Echidna had supposedly placed on Subaru unraveled, and his memories were freed.
In his mind, memories surfaced all at once—meeting the witches at the tea party, and facing Echidna in her school uniform inside a school building from his original world. Remembering it all, Subaru arrived at one possibility for breaking through the current impasse. It was practically a forbidden move at this point, but—

"If the cathedral's the chopstick-holding hand, then the crypt's the bowl-holding hand—!"

"Barusu—?"

"I'm gonna go stay up a little late! You just make sure you don't wear out a guy who's still recovering from illness!"

Raising a hand in answer to Ram's call from behind him, Subaru dashed out of the building and took off running into the night. His destination wasn't the cathedral where he was meant to sleep, but the direction of the crypt he had left behind barely two hours ago.
Given the hour, the odds of confirming Subaru's hunch by going there now were low. Low, but he couldn't just sit still. At the very least, he wanted the result—even if that result was simply that nighttime wouldn't work.

"Now that I've been through the first 'Trial,' a whole pile of things I want to ask about have come up. Invite me to another tea party, Witch... no, Echidna!"

Subaru dashed through the streets, making his way through the "Sanctuary" relying on nothing but the meager natural light.
The cold wind, the damp earth, the sweat on his brow, his ragged breath—feeling it all, he pushed his fatigue-laden body onward, ever onward. And so he reached his destination, and—

"Yo... figured you might come."

Blocking Subaru's path stood a young man with short golden hair—Garfiel, guarding the crypt.

"Runnin' laps this late at night, huh—gotta admire that. Bein' born a man means you got a duty to make the effort to grow strong. 'A Winbrook's gotta earn his warrior's stripes,' as they say."

Standing in the night road with both arms spread wide, Garfiel bared his fangs ferociously as he said this, warning Subaru off. Feeling his skin prickle at the gesture, Subaru said,

"Ah, sorry, but I'm not doing anything as dedicated as running laps. And I don't plan on standing here shooting the breeze with you right now, either. It's not like there's some time limit, but you know, strike while the iron's hot and all..."

"You don't get it, do ya, oi."

A sharp sound—the crack of Garfiel stomping through the ground once, at a speed too fast to follow with the eye.
Watching clods of earth burst and scatter, the hard ground gouged out in the shape of his shoe sole, Subaru's eyes went wide, and Garfiel gnashed his fangs together as he said,

"If you turn back right here and now, I'll let it slide as just a bit of a late-night run, is what I'm sayin', ain't I."

"Getting told by two different people in this short a time that I don't get it... how far behind on the conversation am I?"

"Beats me. But at least I'm pretty sure you ain't seein' yourself or what's around you straight."

Subaru tried with his words to somehow lighten the mood, but that consideration seemed unlikely to reach Garfiel, whose eyes gleamed fiercely with hostility.
Garfiel touched the white scar on his forehead and said,

"Ain't nothin' up ahead but the crypt. Don't tell me you're headin' all the way there just to take a piss."

"Want to come along and go together, then? Though pissing on a witch's grave—just thinking about what kind of revenge that'd bring down on me is scary enough."

Knowing Echidna the way he did, Subaru had a feeling she'd find it amusing rather than get angry. In any case, Garfiel seemed to want to keep talking without ever letting down his guard against Subaru.

"Don't tell me you're plannin' on takin' on another 'Trial' the same night you just cleared one. That'd be plain greedy, wouldn't it."

"I'm not thinking of anything that outrageous. Though I am plotting a different approach."

"A scheme, huh."

"Yeah, a scheme."

Call it a cheat code, a forbidden move, some mysterious data disc—whatever you wanted to call it.
If there was any possibility at all of somehow lighting up this pitch-black darkness he had no choice but to grope his way through, he'd cling to it, gnaw it down to the bone, boil it for stock, and drink it down.

"So get out of the way, Garfiel. Let me into the crypt. Maybe I'll come up with some brilliant idea to fix everything..."

"Sorry, but no way in hell. I ain't lettin' you into that crypt. Not ever."

But faced with Subaru's resolve, Garfiel's will didn't bend either.
Subaru felt anger, past mere confusion, at Garfiel's stubborn refusal.
Why, of all people, was this man the one standing in Subaru's way?

"You must've felt the same way too, didn't you. That's why you did that to me back then. And yet—"

"Quit spoutin' nonsense I can't make heads or tails of. Once I decide somethin', I never bend on it. I ain't lettin' you through. I ain't lettin' you get involved in the 'Sanctuary's' business."

"You're going that far, huh... Just what the hell is it about me that pisses you off so much?!"

Garfiel's attitude was clearly different from that first day, and from the previous loop as well. Seeking the true meaning behind this outrageous shift, Subaru raised his voice.
At those words, Garfiel wrinkled his snout, his face taking on a look that was truly beastlike, and said,

"—It's the smell."

"—Huh?"

Subaru couldn't help but let out a groan of a question in reply.
Facing him, Garfiel pressed a hand to his own nose and said clearly,

"Ever since you came out of that crypt, your body's been reekin' of witch's miasma somethin' fierce. —You're askin' a half-demon to trust someone stinkin' of witch? There's no way in hell I could do that!"

Thrusting both hands up, baring his fangs, Garfiel let his anger show plainly, and said,

"This is the 'Sanctuary'! The Witch of Greed's testing ground! A place where half-breeds and misfits get gathered up and left with nowhere left to go—a dead-end clog with no way out!!"
