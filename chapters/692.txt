Arc 8 Chapter 73: "Nemesis"

—It felt as though everything below her neck was buried in some dark place.

Endless earth and sand, mixed with fine gravel, sucked her feet down inch by inch, until it reached her waist, then buried her chest, her shoulders, leaving nothing exposed but her head.
And even that head, the only part left above the surface, would soon be buried too — it was only a matter of time before the cold, falling dust piled high enough to cover it.

She craned her neck upward with all her might, blowing desperately at the dust as it fell, trying to push back — if only by a moment — the instant she would suffocate, the moment the dust would bury even the crown of her head.

Not once, from the day she was born until now, had she ever felt so utterly powerless.
Her heart had always been unsteady, like walking on the surface of water, but her body had never been like that. Surely she'd always been better at moving her body than most people.
With that one certainty stolen from her, she wanted to cry at how powerless she was. But she didn't know how to shed tears. She was hopeless even at crying.

Everything about her fell short. And yet, there was still something she wanted.
She'd been utterly unaware of it, but at last she realized — how greedy she truly was.
And then—

"——"

Suddenly, the sensation of dust raining down from above vanished.
The steady patter against her forehead, the rising press of burial, all of it stopped, and a flickering pale-blue light began spinning in circles, lighting up the surroundings.
That light, darting provocatively through her field of vision, irritated her beyond reason, and she struggled. She struggled and struggled and struggled, until at last, an arm from her body — buried up to the neck — stretched up into open air.

As if fleeing the reaching hand, the pale-blue light rose a little higher still.
Wanting to corner it, to reach the height her hand alone couldn't reach, she forced her buried body free. Her shoulders came loose, then her chest, and once her waist was free, it was almost done.
At last, once even her feet had pulled free, she lifted her face, reaching to catch the pale-blue light — and that was when she noticed.

That a blazing red fire, lighting the area brilliantly, stood right before her.
And that the pale-blue light she'd tried to catch was now wrapped around her body, pushing her toward that impossibly dazzling flame.

For a moment, she hesitated. It wasn't fear of the fire.
It was hesitation over whether someone as cold as her deserved the good fortune of being burned by that flame.
But that hesitation, too, lasted only an instant.

It wasn't about whether she had the right. She simply wanted to be burned.
She decided she would be burned.
And so—

"—Wake up already. How long do you intend to keep me waiting?"

To be burned by that gentle flame, she took one step forward, straight ahead.
She stepped forward—

"...Hime-sama, I did my best."

"Of course you did. — Did you think my milk sister was anyone less?"

—In that instant, the Witch Sphinx knew with certainty that her entire plan had collapsed.

As the Great Disaster, she had raised an army of the Undead, using the countless trials and errors they carried out to verify the nature of the soul, and with that knowledge, fulfill the purpose for which she'd been created: to recreate the Witch of Greed.
In addition, by destroying the "Stone Mass" Muspel — which she had used to build the ritual formula of the Sacrament of the Immortal King — and collapsing the very earth of the Vollachian Empire, ending everything that made up the empire, she would show the one person she'd sealed away in another dimension, Priscilla Barielle, the destruction of her homeland.
By doing so, Sphinx had schemed to deal out something — retaliation, victory, revenge, superiority, transcendence, defeat, domination, blasphemy, submission, ascendance, ruin, frenzy, elation, happiness, whatever it might be called — against Priscilla, who had lured Leip Barielle, the man who had taught Sphinx that ghastly, hideous thing called "heat," to his death.

All of it — completely, mercilessly, thoroughly — was collapsing.

"—The Sacrament of the Immortal King—"

Sphinx had no choice but to admit it had lost its effect.
Centered on the imperial capital of Lupugana, with four more points to the east, west, south, and north of the Vollachian Empire — five locations designated as anchoring sites for the ever-moving Stone Mass — the magic circles laid there had used the blood soaked into the earth of Vollachia as a medium, continuously reviving the dead through the Sacrament of the Immortal King.
But all of that would end the moment the massive supply of mana from the Stone Mass was cut off.

"The Spirit Eater, Arakiya... So she has mastered Muspel."

Impossible as it seemed, it was the only conclusion she could draw from what had happened.
A Great Spirit far too powerful for any one being to contain — Arakiya, who should have absorbed it only to burst apart, had instead brought that Great Spirit completely under her control, through the taming administered by the swordsman who wielded the Dream Sword Masayume, and through her own unbelievable tenacity.
Now, the Spirit Eater Arakiya and the Stone Mass Muspel were one and the same.
And Arakiya had no reason to betray the Vollachian Empire and cooperate with the Great Disaster.
From this point on—

"The Undead will no longer be revived."

The very premise of an undying army, a horde of the dead endlessly resurrected, had crumbled away.
Even with the supply from the Stone Mass cut off, it wouldn't cause the Undead to lose their lives immediately. It simply meant the conditions had become the same as everyone else's — the same as the living, for whom death was the end.

"—!"

It took roughly five seconds to process the situation after the mana supply to the magic circles was severed.
But in those mere five seconds, Sphinx — already reduced to thirty-six bodies — lost seven more, dropping her number to twenty-nine.
The "Blue Lightning" and the "Adorer," moving with abnormal speed and combat strength, were simply too strong.

Sphinx, who should have been able to reproduce the abilities of her creator, the Witch of Greed, couldn't compete with them at all. She was no match. They weren't even opponents she could overwhelm through sheer numbers.
Even if she wanted to do something about it, those two weren't the only ones holding the Witch back.
Therefore—

"""—Retreat is required."""

Having come this far, the Witch Sphinx judged that abandoning the plan was the most rational choice.
It was a plan begun from an unshakeable position, with every countermeasure prepared, every condition perfect — and yet, overturned to this degree, there was no longer any way to salvage it.
Galling as it was, the wisest course was to accept the fact that one of her two great objectives had been achieved.
In fact, the creation-purpose given to her by her creator, pursued for so long, had been fulfilled. A goal pursued for over three hundred years and a goal desired for barely a year could hardly be of equal worth.

""—Retreat is required.""

To begin with, the scales had never been balanced in the first place.
She reaffirmed, once more, the very reason she had been brought into being. What was demanded of her was the return of the Witch of Greed — and that would be achieved without fail.
The self she now knew as Sphinx would be overwritten, remade into the Witch of Greed, Echidna—

"—Retreat is required."

She would be remade. Her creation-purpose would thereby be fulfilled.
Sphinx would vanish, Echidna would be reborn, and more than three hundred years of obsessive days would come to an end.
To wish for anything more was not rational. Not rational. Not — rational.
And yet it wasn't rational—

"——"

In that instant, a raised Sunlight Sword blazed with dazzling light, proclaiming its presence across the entire imperial capital.
It was a provocation — one that had burned the pocket dimension to ash, forced its way back into the sky above the capital, overcome as a matter of course the fate of being torn to eight pieces, and now called out to the Witch resolving to retreat.
A woman clad in a dress red as blood, her hair bright as the sun, radiating a presence dazzling as fire, who knew no way to live but to burn others to ash like a flame — she smiled.

"—Come, Sphinx. I am your enemy."

In that instant, all twenty-nine bodies of the Witch Sphinx made their resolve.
And it was—

"""—I am your enemy, Priscilla Barielle!!"""

"Let that creature slip away, and it will surely become a calamity upon the world. By nature, I do not lay a hand on the shape the world takes. — But that thing has made me its destiny."

With crumbling stone and earth as a backdrop behind her, Priscilla said this as she stepped down from Al's arms.
Arrogance and pride given human form — her bearing was so full of commanding presence that it seemed almost doubtful she'd been a captive until just moments ago.
Even Al, who had rushed in with such momentum, seemed overawed by her words.

"So you're saying you'll take it on yourself, Hime-san? Why's that necessary? A troublesome thing like that, we could just surround it and beat it down— ow!"

"Fool. If we resort to any means necessary to take its life, we hand the same option to our foe. This fight must be settled before that thing names the whole world its enemy."

"...Otherwise, we can't win?"

Arakiya, kneeling at her side, tilted her head at Priscilla, who crossed her arms in a way that emphasized her ample chest. Arakiya's right eye burned with fire while her hand pressed against her left eye, stained red with blood, as she asked her question. Priscilla shrugged, as if to say there was no helping it.

"Arakiya, do not you too start spouting the same nonsense as Al. Is the woman you know me to be one who places weight on something so shallow as winning or losing?"

"But Hime-sama likes winning. You look happier when you win."

"Naturally. That is simply what I am."

"Don't undercut the premise you just laid out yourself! You'll confuse everyone!"

It was Subaru who, unable to keep silent while Priscilla carried on this thoroughly unreasonable exchange with Arakiya, found himself butting in.
Subaru had leaned in without thinking to make the jab, and Priscilla narrowed her crimson eyes at the momentum of it.

"And who are you? There is no place here for a child like you. Be gone, quickly."

"Sorry to disappoint! I may be small, but I'm Natsuki Subaru! Eternal as the heavens, knight to the lovely and adorable Emilia-tan!"

"A child like this, calling himself a knight — it seems that half-devil's shortage of talent runs deep indeed. At this rate, even that noisy fool would have been the better choice."

"That's me! That's me!"

At an unreasonableness that somehow surpassed even what she'd said to Arakiya, Subaru forgot his full-body exhaustion and shouted.
He knew Priscilla erased anyone she wasn't interested in from her memory — but even if he wasn't on the level of Al, Schult, or the sister-complex Abel, Subaru had worried about her enough that surely that didn't apply to him.
So Subaru pressed her, wiping blood from his nose—

"—Fool."

"Huh?"

"Learn to tell the difference between that and mere jest. — Natsuki Subaru."

"—Ah."

Priscilla said this with her arms still crossed, her expression unchanged. The instant the meaning of her words sank into his brain, heat overtook Subaru's entire body.
But it wasn't anger at having been toyed with — it was a kind of emotion, close to being moved.
At the fact that Priscilla Barielle herself had remembered his name, and spoken it.

"...Feels like it's paid back the me from back when she kicked my jaw to pieces."

Suppressing the emotion that had welled up and passed, Subaru only touched his own jaw and let those words stand.
There wasn't a single second to spare for further exchange — not for anyone in the entire capital.
And so—

"—I must teach that thing this: what you ought to look upon is not the world, but me."

The instant she said this, Priscilla unfolded her crossed arms and raised the Sunlight Sword in her right hand to the sky.
Then, without any explanation of what she intended, the Sunlight Sword's brilliance surged all at once, and a blinding light as though a new sun had been born there seared Subaru and the others' eyes. — No, despite the tremendous intensity of that light, it brought none of the pain that came from staring directly at the sun.

"The Sunlight Sword cuts what I have decided to cut, and burns what I wish to burn."

Having once again explained the sword's sheer irregularity, Priscilla smiled, holding aloft that dazzlingly radiant blade — a gleam that would announce its presence from anywhere in the capital.
Smiling, she provoked someone who was not present here at all, someone who ought to be paying attention to her.

"—Come, Sphinx. I am your enemy."

"——"

Subaru understood by instinct that this was a trap Priscilla had set.
Sphinx, who had tried to carry Abel off to Priscilla, was obsessed with her for some reason. And that obsession had only grown larger through her time spent with the captive Priscilla. Or perhaps Priscilla had guided things so that it would.
Either way—

"—I am your enemy, Priscilla Barielle!!"

Yes — that the Witch had accepted Priscilla's declaration of war came through vividly, even in the tension gripping the battlefield's air.
Subaru couldn't laugh at that conclusion, so utterly illogical and irrational, so purely emotional.
After all, no living thing challenged by the sun could ignore its brilliance.

"Al, I leave this place to you. Arakiya, come with me."

"—Got it."

"Mm, Hime-sama."

Al and Arakiya answered Priscilla's brief, irresistible orders in kind.
One entrusted with holding the field, one asked to come along. And Priscilla turned to face Subaru too, who was neither of those.

"Natsuki Subaru."

"Y-yeah. I'm..."

"A hero's fantasy, was it you called it?"

"——"

The shock of having his name called again was swept away just as quickly by the words that followed.
The resolve and determination exchanged between Subaru and Al — what they had to carry, had to see through, here in this empire and in the world to come.
A banner that, if overheard, would surely draw laughter — weaklings like Subaru and Al, talking such nonsense. And yet, somehow, he didn't think Priscilla would laugh at it.

"—Do what you must do."

Sure enough, that sense of his was confirmed by Priscilla's single line.
Priscilla's voice gave him the feeling of a hand pressed against his back, against his very soul, and Subaru fell silent. But Priscilla didn't wait for a reply. Having said what she wanted to say, she turned her back.
Arakiya wrapped an arm around Priscilla's slender waist, and the two of them rose into the air.
And to Priscilla, taking flight together with Arakiya—

"Hime-san! Give 'em the usual treatment!"

"Naturally. Who do you take me for?"

"Priscilla Barielle! My Hime-san!"

"As if I were yours, fool."

Retorting to Al's words like that, Priscilla flew off, leaving behind only the afterimage of lips curved ever so slightly into a smile.
Priscilla and Arakiya sped away from Subaru and the others. — No, to be precise, it wasn't that they were leaving Subaru's group behind. They were putting distance between themselves and the being confined here, the Witch.

"——"

Subaru and Al turned around, and there in the direction of their gaze sat the Witch, leaning against a shattered wall. Her entire body was bound in black light, sealed against any movement.
It resembled the seal once used on Roy Alphard, the Sin Archbishop of Gluttony captured at the Pleiades Watchtower — and yet it gave off a somehow different impression.
Facing that power-sealed Witch was Subaru's role.

"Subaru! I found her, I suppose!"

Just as Subaru steeled himself, Beatrice returned at perfect timing. Along with Roswaal, Beatrice had gone to fetch someone needed for this final stage.
That someone was Spica, flying in on Roswaal's back alongside Beatrice.

"Keeping both hands free is the ratio-o-onal thing for a mage to do, but isn't this a bit much, don't you think?"

"Enough out of you, I suppose! Come on, Spica, let's go, I suppose!"

"Eh-ao, au!"

At Beatrice's urging, Spica kicked off from Roswaal's back and landed at Subaru's side on the ground. He stroked the head of the girl looking up at him, and smiled at Spica as she squirmed, ticklish.
Then, once again, he took her hand and turned to face the Witch.
And then—

"Spica, your stomach's probably full and you might have heartburn, but I need one more push out of you."

"Mm!"

Answering Subaru's request gamely, Spica smiled, baring her pointed teeth. Relying entirely on her stout-heartedness, Subaru fixed his gaze on the Witch.
The Witch looked back at Subaru with the same face as the worst-tempered Witch in this world. Her black eyes narrowed, the Witch smiled, and opened her mouth.

"Can you catch hold of my soul?"

Unable to move, her life and death forever in another's hands, the Witch spoke.
In answer to the Witch's question, Subaru called to mind the feel of Spica's hand in his, the presence of Al nodding beside him, Beatrice's efforts alongside Roswaal to clear away every obstacle in his path, everyone who had worked together to save Priscilla as she fell, and the warm flower of ice that had bloomed at the very end—

"Don't get it wrong. It's not me who's going to beat you. It's us."

"——"

Having said that, Subaru took a step forward together with Spica, closing the distance to the Witch.
Then, together with Spica, he reached out and touched the Witch's cheek.

"—Sphinx."

To devour the very existence of the Witch as an Undead, he took on the final Star Eating.

—The instant orbs of light came raining down one after another, Vincent scooped up Medium beside him in his arms and leapt out from the upper level of the Crystal Palace.

Medium let out a shriek — "Wahyah!?" — as he pulled her slender waist close, and while taking care not to drop the Madelyn he carried, he pushed off into open air.
At the same time, he turned his attention to another person present, one beyond his reach—

"Olbart Dunkelkenn!"

"No need to tell me that, jaa!"

Right after, the magic crystal cannon's platform — the one Vincent's group and Olbart had all just leapt from — was blown apart.
Buffeted by the wind from the high altitude, Vincent told Medium, "Hold on tight." Confirming that her arms had instantly wrapped tightly around him, he kicked hard off the wall.
Using the momentum from that kick, he dodged the follow-up beam of light and landed on the ground.

"Whoo, that was close, close. At this rate, about the only thing left that I can do and no other shinobi can is die of old age, and that's sayin' somethin'."

"Indeed. I've never once heard of a shinobi dying of old age."

"Handin' a useless old-timer a magic stone and havin' him blow himself up — that's standard shinobi practice, y'know."

"Come on! Quit talking about scary stuff and put me down! Puh-uh-ut me down!"

Olbart, lively despite having lost both arms, traded words with Vincent, while Medium, flailing her arms and legs, was set down onto the ground from his hold.
With both hands now free, Vincent regripped the Sunlight Sword and looked up.
And there—

"—The Witch, is it."

In Vincent's field of view, a woman with white hair streaming behind her could be seen touching down on the cannon platform.
The magic core was gone, and a great deal of the castle's magic crystal should have already been carried off for that massive ritual formula — and yet the Witch had still landed there, precisely because her objective lay there.
Having driven Vincent's group off, what was the Witch scheming?

"—. Moguro Hagane, is it."

"Ohh? What're you on about, Your Excellency. Moguro, he's the one Balleroy carried off, ain't he. And yet..."

"Fool. What was carried off was only the core. What remains in the castle is the rest of the Meteor, everything but the core—"

"—! Abel-chin!"

With a sensation like thunder, the possibility struck Vincent — and Medium, her face draining of color, slapped his shoulder and pointed at the castle.
Before Vincent and the others, gritting their teeth at the shock of it, a new change came over the Crystal Palace. — Slowly, the Crystal Palace itself began to rise.

"——"

Moguro Hagane was the Meteor known as the Crystal Palace, the very symbol of the Vollachian Empire.
The magic core, both the control unit and power source of that Meteor, had been dismantled, and the explosion of the runaway core had been carried out far off in the sky, well away from here.
In short, the individual recognized by everyone as Moguro Hagane of the Nine Divine Generals was merely one part of that colossal Meteor. Moguro, who had shown his full strength without reservation even in the decisive battle for the capital before the Great Disaster began — the defense mechanism originally meant to protect the magic core still remained.

The Witch had made use of that. By forcibly activating a defense mechanism that had lost its magic core and could no longer fulfill its original purpose, she had summoned forth a giant of steel — no, a giant of magic crystal.

The risen Crystal Palace — something one might call a Magic Crystal Giant Warrior — shared traits with Moguro Hagane as an individual, yet radiated a presence of malevolence beyond any comparison.
A colossal body over fifty meters tall, its structure held together for the most part by magic crystal — it was equivalent to an absurdly enormous stick of dynamite, walking.

"If we act carelessly—"

—everything Moguro and Balleroy had risked their lives for would be erased, meaningless.
That was the thought as Vincent ground his teeth — and right after—

"Strike."

"Mm, Hime-sama."

It hadn't been a particularly loud voice. And yet, it carried remarkably well.
The instant it seemed to pass overhead, a colossal fist of flame wrapped in light — some ten meters across — was unleashed, slamming furiously into the Magic Crystal Giant Warrior.
A tremendous heatwave spread across the sky and the ground below, and the giant warrior, struck, staggered backward hugely, the streets beneath its feet scattered as though swept away by a great army.
It was a blow so far beyond the norm, so utterly reckless, as to defy belief.

"Reckless, is it. As if I acted without a thought in my head."

"——"

"Ah—! Priscilla-chan!"

The Magic Crystal Giant Warrior tilted violently — the flaming fist that had struck it had been unleashed by Arakiya, wreathed in a diamond-bright gleam as she flew at high speed through the air.
Releasing Arakiya's hand, the hem of her dress fluttering as she landed — the woman was Priscilla, and Medium pointed at her, her voice ringing out high to announce her presence.

"Abel-chin, Abel-chin, it's Priscilla-chan!"

"I can see her without you shouting so loud. — Where were you being held?"

"How very like you, Brother, though hardly the words one expects at a moving reunion. I was in another world — the one the Witch carried off, dungeon and all, from beneath the castle."

"I see. No wonder we couldn't find you. How did you get out?"

"Naturally. I burned my way out."

At Priscilla's terse reply, Vincent felt something almost like satisfaction.
To think of what she might have suffered as a captive at the Witch's hands, and yet here she was, appearing at this final stage looking every bit as commanding as ever—

"—. Priscilla, you—"

A flicker of unease had sprouted in the corner of his mind, and he tried to ask Priscilla for an answer to it. But before it could take the shape of a question, it was cut off by the Giant Warrior's counterattack.
Arakiya was wheeling through the air, harrying the giant with abnormal force.
To finish Arakiya off, the entire Magic Crystal Giant Warrior lit up, and destructive fire was discharged from what could be called its right arm.

Weaker than the Magic Crystal Cannon's mighty blast, perhaps, but more than powerful enough to blow away a single life, or half the capital — and it took aim, again and again, at Arakiya crossing the sky.

"—It's coming."

Vincent, watching for an opening to step in and support the targeted Arakiya, saw the same thing Priscilla had at her single word, and raised the Sunlight Sword.
In that instant, from the free left arm — opposite the right one aimed at Arakiya — a ruinous light was discharged, unleashed without mercy straight at Vincent and the others.

"——"

With Medium and the others behind them, Vincent and Priscilla stood with Sunlight Swords raised. The dazzling crimson blades' brilliance rose as flame, meeting the surging light head-on in a deadlock.
But battle after battle without rest, a fight that refused to end no matter how much kindling of fighting spirit was fed into the flames — blood began to seep from Vincent's hand where he gripped the Sunlight Sword.

"What, how unseemly, Brother."

"...You, who was resting until just a moment ago, ought to muster a little more effort yourself."

"My, my — to work such unreasonable treatment on the very sister you once poisoned to death and drove from her homeland, you've truly come to act the part of a Vollachian Emperor, Brother."

Insult answered with insult, even in the middle of a life-or-death struggle against a ruinous light — at the exchange, Vincent clenched his back teeth and tightened lips that threatened to loosen into a smile.
He would admit it. It was pleasant. His heart was stirring at the return of the captive Prisca — no, Priscilla — so much so that even her merciless invective could be fed into the fire in place of fighting spirit.
And so Vincent and Prisca held their ground — and then—

"—Well done, children of my child."

In that instant, an impossible third Sunlight Sword joined the deadlock, and in a single beat, the light burst apart, the standoff ending. The one who had accomplished it, sweeping back in furiously to deliver an artful, single flashing stroke, was the King of Thorns.
His elegance without a single cloud upon it — Eugard Vollachia had entered the fray.

"I was sent out by my Star to deal with the risen Crystal Palace. The gleam of magic crystal would be difficult to handle with anything but a Sunlight Sword. This is no lack of strength on your part."

"Even without you saying so. My thanks for the assistance."

"Hmph. — My mother's soul's beloved, and the King of Thorns besides."

At Eugard's words as he stood beside them, Vincent answered with a wink of one eye, while Priscilla regarded the man with a feeling apart from mere gratitude for his help.
Vincent, too, knew that Priscilla, avid reader that she was, had read Iris and the King of Thorns. Though the knowledge Priscilla held would surely differ from Vincent's own, given that he hadn't known Yorna was Sandra, nor that Iris was Yorna.
In any case, as Priscilla stared at him intently, Eugard narrowed his eyes in turn.

"You too, one of this generation's imperial family? You bear a resemblance to my empress... Terriola."

"—Hmph."

Priscilla accepted Eugard's assessment with unusually few words for her.
Vincent felt a slight sense of unease at that reaction — Priscilla, of all people, should have hated being compared to anyone. Though surely it couldn't be that meeting a character from a fairy tale had left her flustered; she wasn't nearly that endearing.
Either way, here stood Vincent, Priscilla, and Eugard.

"Three Sunlight Swords, and not even during the Imperial Selection Ceremony — that is beyond all reason."

"Indeed. In fact, never once has more than one Sunlight Sword manifested except when siblings faced one another. Let alone against an enemy of the empire."

Exactly as Vincent and Eugard said, it was a situation that should never have been possible.
The deeds of the Great Disaster reviving the Undead, and Vincent's own outrage in defying the very mechanism of the Imperial Selection Ceremony, the contest for the Vollachian throne — this was the scene those things had brought about.

"To be overcome with feeling on the road to the ending — both you, Brother, and the King of Thorns, seem to have room to spare."

Vincent and Eugard nodded to one another, and standing between the two of them, Priscilla snorted.
With the dazzling blade in hand, Priscilla carried herself like a flame in full command of itself, and at that bearing, Vincent and Eugard exchanged one more glance before turning to face forward.

"Abel-chin! Priscilla-chan! Yorna-chan's husband!"

Behind the three of them standing together, Medium, retreating while carrying Madelyn and the wounded Olbart, raised her voice.
Louder than ever before, louder than anyone else in this capital, she called out—

"—Do your best!!"

At that, Vincent's mouth relaxed into the faintest curve, Eugard nodded deeply, and Priscilla, her crimson eyes blazing bright, looked up at the Magic Crystal Giant Warrior.

"Of course. — Watch, to your heart's content, and lose yourself in my sword dance."
