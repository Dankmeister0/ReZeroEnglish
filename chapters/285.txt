Garfiel said gruffly to Emilia as she looked at the crystal stone she'd caught on reflex.

"—I'm gonna go find out. Whether it's me that's wrong, or you lot."

He said it like a declaration of resolve, and Garfiel turned on his heel, cutting the wind with his shoulder.
The steps he took led toward the tomb — for Garfiel, a nightmarish place where a hateful memory carved into him as a child awaited.
What would he see there now, after all this time?

"Oi, Garfiel."

A voice called out from behind him, to Garfiel who should have already steeled his resolve.

Doing nothing but exactly that — raining on the parade — Subaru scratched his head and trailed off awkwardly.
Seeing Subaru's reluctant expression, Garfiel gnashed his fangs, urging him to get on with it.
Prompted by that, Subaru let out a sigh, and then—

"At least go change your clothes. Just a loincloth about to fall off doesn't cut it — there's a limit to how undignified you can look."

A breezy, wind-straight-on-the-crotch barbarian style.
At Subaru's remark, a vein bulged on Garfiel's forehead, and Ram let out an exasperated sigh.

"What an undignified thing to have to say."

A stone-built space with damp air. A chill wind blew through, and the smell of dust tickled his nostrils, making him grimace. For Garfiel, with his overly keen sense of smell, the stench of a sealed-off space was an unbearable assault on the senses.

"Don't wanna stick around here longer than I gotta."

The murmur echoed down the corridor, and no answering voice came from anywhere.
As he repeated the sensation of hard ground beneath his feet, his body pressed deeper and deeper into the tomb.

Without realizing it, Garfiel noticed his heartbeat quickening.
He would take on the "Trial" and see its outcome through to the end. That resolve was set. And yet his heart couldn't stay calm, because what awaited him was, for Garfiel, a memory that conjured a fear he could never wipe away.

There was always a part of him thinking: what would change even if he touched it again?
Even if he laid eyes on that scene once more, what change would it bring to Garfiel now? To begin with, he'd never once forgotten that memory, not for a moment.

A memory burned in with such vivid, excessive clarity.
What outcome could come from reviewing it again, other than reinforcing the memory further?

"...Pathetic. That's exactly what you came all this way to find out, ain't it."

He mocked himself for lining up plausible-sounding logic while wanting to run away.
It was the kind of weakness that deserved Ram's shouting, deserved her scorn. He hadn't wanted to know, hadn't wanted to realize, that he was such a faint-hearted man.

—If he'd been the kind of man who lost to this weakness and cowardice, what had he planned to do?

"――――"

He stopped his feet from treading on the corridor's flagstones and became aware of the earth beneath him.
The power welling up from the soles of his feet was a warm pulse — the earth lending its strength to Garfiel, bearer of the "Divine Protection of the Earth Spirits."
Even a body wounded, battered, and worn down would recover and gain strength just from contact with the earth.

Right after being struck down it had been one thing, but now, with a brief rest and some time passed, he could manage about forty percent of his full strength. If he put his mind to it, he could probably even bring the tomb down.
And Subaru, Ram, and the others, having sent Garfiel in alone, had no means of stopping him if he resorted to destruction. The meaning of all that effort spent beating him down would vanish completely. Right now, he could make it vanish.

Were they really that dim, not to think of something so obvious?

"Bunch of bastards."

No, that couldn't be it.
Setting aside Emilia, who probably didn't know how to doubt people, or Otto, who seemed to be missing some crucial screw — Subaru, who acted with calculation, and that Ram — would they really overlook that possibility?
They believed that even with his strength restored, Garfiel wouldn't destroy the tomb.

Was that because they thought him a coward, or because they trusted him completely?
Garfiel didn't know, right now, which was the correct answer.
Maybe that answer, too, lay somewhere beyond overcoming the "Trial."

"...Tch, dammit."

Spitting the word out bitterly, Garfiel started walking again.
Thinking complicated things wasn't his strong suit. Being bad with his head was the same whether in daily life or in battle. And yet he racked his brain desperately, because someone had told him to, once, long ago.

*"Think more while you fight, Garf. If you do, Ram will be happy."*

"—Ah."

He suddenly remembered who it was that had pointed out to him that he should live by thinking.
So was that why he'd clung to it so single-mindedly, so stupidly earnestly, all this time?

Which meant that Garfiel had been told by the very person who'd said to fight while thinking, that it would have been better to fight without thinking at all. Unreasonable didn't even begin to cover it.

"Ridiculous, huh, oi... and?"

A laugh welled up despite himself.
Just as he tried to swallow it back down, Garfiel saw the end of the corridor.

The corridor ended, and a square space opened up to receive him — the Chamber of Trials.
A small room that rose up faintly wavering, glowing with a ghostly pale blue. With an expression somewhat eased of tension, Garfiel stepped into the room and looked at the stone door built into its far wall.
It was said to open after one overcame all three "Trials." Whether that was actually true, he had no way of knowing, having never seen it open. Nor had anyone told him what lay inside.

If he just stopped here and waited, the "Trial" should begin—

"—Ahh?"

Crossing his arms with nothing to do, glancing idly around the small room, Garfiel raised his eyebrows. Something felt off about the little chamber, catching the edge of his night-sharp vision.
He tried to squint and make out what it was—

*"—First, face your own past."*

A voice spoke.
At once, his vision reeled, and his consciousness was swallowed by a hazy blur.

The past — was coming.

When his awareness surfaced into wakefulness, Garfiel found himself standing in a forest.
Turning his head, the view that met his eyes was a familiar forest — though compared to the scenery Garfiel knew, it was a somewhat "younger" version of it. It had only been a few years, but since this was a place he'd been in contact with almost daily, the change was as plain to him as anything.

This was the past.
And he himself was taking on the "Trial," inside its dream.

Accepting that readily, Garfiel checked over the condition of his own body.
Arms, legs — there. From the neck up, eyes, nose, ears, all present without issue. He opened his mouth and bit down hard, and a sharp, high sound of blade meeting blade rang out.
No problem. Unharmed. If anything, it felt as though even the wounds he'd carried into the tomb had healed.

"The reason you're not dragging real-world injuries in here is that this is a place that draws in only the spirit. A woman dressed head to toe in black, mourning-like clothes, with hair and skin of pure white. Her smile was bewitching enough to ensnare any man, and yet just standing across from her, you could tell there was nothing inside.

Her appearance, too, hadn't changed one bit from Garfiel's memory of her.

"My arms and legs got longer, and yet you ain't changed a lick."

Swallowing the urge to click his tongue, Garfiel glared at the witch standing there with an innocuous expression — Echidna.

Acting as though she were the "Trial's" overseer, Echidna stood close beside Garfiel as he watched the past, just as she had before. It wasn't out of some human concern for a Garfiel whose heart was being wounded by the "Trial" — it was mere base curiosity, to make sure she didn't miss any of the "phenomena" affected by the unfolding of this scene.

Having lost the will to continue conversing further, Garfiel looked out at the forest.
If the performance of the past had already begun, it was only a matter of time before the performers showed themselves.
Garfiel had not the slightest intention of giving the witch — who was delighted merely to converse — the satisfaction of being pleased.

"How cold of you."

Even that attitude of Garfiel's, Echidna seemed to accept as something pleasant, smoothing down her own long white hair beside him.
As he followed her gesture out of the corner of his eye, the change came almost immediately.

"――――"

Faced with a scene he should already have known was coming, Garfiel faintly caught his breath.

The forest's boundary — that is, the watershed of the barrier dividing the inside and outside of the Sanctuary.
It was said that any being bound by the barrier felt their consciousness grow unsteady, their very existence disturbed with unpleasantness, merely from standing before it.

At a place close by that boundary line, four figures had appeared.
One was Ryuzu, her pale red hair grown long, looking almost exactly as she did now.
Another had golden hair, fine and beautiful as silk thread — a girl who looked about ten years old. Frederica.

"Ah... Mother..."

At the sight of the woman, at the instant he recognized who the child in her arms was, a weak voice slipped from Garfiel's mouth.
But the voice calling out to his mother reached the ears of none of the four people present.

—Of course it didn't. No one could interfere with the past.

"Even if you reach out, you can't touch. Even if you call out, she won't smile back at you. It's odd for me to say so myself, but I do sympathize — being shown such a harsh scene."

Precisely the sentiment Garfiel wanted to shout back, *you're one to talk*, about.
But in the witch's eyes as she gazed at Garfiel's profile, twisted with heartache, there was no malice, genuinely. This scene wasn't something the witch had arranged out of ill will.
This witch was, in truth, nothing but a natural disaster — one who only sought the outcome of dragging out, in whatever form, the regret held in the heart of whoever challenged the "Trial," and watching how they faced it, in whatever form that took.

"――――"

Before Garfiel's trembling eyes, the four exchanged words.
The content, the words themselves, the tone of their voices — none of it reached Garfiel.

Mouths opened, sounds were spun.
And yet, when they passed through the air to shake Garfiel's eardrums, they formed no definite meaning.

Ryuzu wore a tragic expression, Frederica bit her lip as if holding back tears. Their mother lowered the corners of her eyes as if troubled, while in her arms, the infant Garfiel laughed innocently.

The reason this painful scene came without sound was that this was Garfiel's memory.
The young Garfiel hadn't retained this exchange in his memory. That was precisely why the words being exchanged didn't reach Garfiel's ears now.

Only a faint, dim trace lingered at the edges of memory.
Because that scene was being reproduced from it, only the visuals played out, taunting him.

"What do you suppose they're discussing? Can you guess?"

"Don't talk to me. —Bound to be some pointless back-and-forth anyway."

Thinking of what would happen after this, he could guess the content of the exchange.

A mother trying to head out into the outside world, and Ryuzu and Frederica trying to stop her. The young Garfiel, unable to even join the discussion, simply savored the joy of being held by his mother.

"—Gh!"

Suddenly, an urge he couldn't hold back struck him, and Garfiel stepped forward.
Before Echidna's raised eyebrows, Garfiel trudged through the forest right up beside the four of them. None of the four noticed him even as he drew close. He stepped in front of his sister, shorter than he was now, and the unchanging Ryuzu, and glared head-on at his mother and himself.

Held in his mother's arms, the young Garfiel laughing innocently.
It grated on him unbearably. Not knowing what would happen after this, not even qualified to join the discussion trying to hold their mother back, he was just laughing.

How much regret, how much despair, would that laughter in that moment go on to breed?

"AAAH! GAAH! AAAAAHH!!"

He raised his arm, his outstretched claws slashing at the air.
He wanted to drive his claws into the innocently smiling infant's face, repaint that expression into despair. He wanted to make him understand just how foolish a choice was being made, right then.

And yet the claws that came down passed clean through the infant's face, passed even through the arms of the mother holding him.
He stamped the earth, tried to blow it all away with the power of his Divine Protection, but nothing happened. His swings grew larger and larger, dragging even his mother's phantom into his destructive fury — but nothing changed.

"Why?! Why're you showin' me this! ME, of all people!!"

He raised an arm that couldn't reach and slammed it into the ground.
But no destructive impulse reached a world that was only a reproduction of memory; the world stood firm, unshaken, right where it was.

Unable to vent his rage, unable to blow away the past, Garfiel turned around, voice shaking, and bared his fangs at the witch watching him.

"It's the same! Nothin's changed! That I couldn't stop Mother, what happens to Mother after this — it's all the damn same! That make you happy, huh!?"

"You're welcome to lash out blindly, but don't you think it's a bit self-serving of you to decide that's my fault? That the past unfolds as the 'Trial' is, without question, my doing by intent... but knowing this is the kind of place it is, and coming here anyway — that was your own choice. If you were expecting something to be different, then the one to blame for nothing having changed isn't me. It's you."

"Me...? The hell're you...?"

"Just so. This place hasn't changed because you haven't changed. You can't perceive the past differently than before because you haven't accepted that you should change. If you accept changing yourself, or choose not to change, the 'Trial' can be overcome. In fact, one boy who chose to change was able to overcome his own past."

At Echidna citing a successful precedent, Garfiel had no comeback.
It would have been easy to dismiss it as one of Echidna's lies. But if it wasn't a lie — the moment he considered that, Garfiel knew fear.

Someone had overcome the past.
The Sanctuary had not been unsealed. Whoever had overcome the past before hadn't managed to break through the subsequent "Trials." And yet, if he had overcome this one—

"N-no... I ain't fallin' for it! The qualification to take on the 'Trial' — the condition's havin' Demi-Human blood mixed in, ain't it! Ain't nobody with blood thinner than a half-blood ever set foot in the 'Sanctuary' 'cept me and my sister! So! Whoever's qualified is still inside the 'Sanctuary'! And of those who've taken the 'Trial' in the 'Sanctuary,' who's ever broken through the past..."

He tried to declare *no one*, but at the last, Garfiel hesitated to finish the statement.
Having nearly been caught by the witch's provocation and second-guessing his own thinking, Garfiel had latched onto a fact that seemed to refute it. But was that really correct?

At Garfiel's counter, the witch's lips curled up in what looked like pleasure.
It wasn't that she welcomed having a lie exposed, nor that she welcomed Garfiel laying out his own theory.
It was an expression waiting for something — something that stirred her curiosity even further.

"Who's ever broken through it — what, then?"

At Echidna's question, asked in a deliberately hollow tone, Garfiel understood.
What the witch wanted. Echidna was waiting to see whether, from the information she herself had offered, Garfiel would arrive at the answer she was presenting — or not.

And then Garfiel realized it.
Just who the witch, Echidna, was actually talking about.

"No... way..."

What Garfiel had just said was the condition for challenging the tomb.
But an exception existed.

It hadn't been stated directly by the witch — this was purely Garfiel's own inference.
Most likely, a "Servant of the Witch of Greed" could also fulfill, in tandem, the qualification to take on the "Trial."

And Garfiel knew of exactly one being who fit that description.
Hadn't that person himself even told Garfiel as much?

—That he had taken the "Trial," and seen the past.

"But — he said he couldn't get past his own past, said he couldn't clear the 'Trial,' said it himself..."

"Might he have avoided saying so in order to avoid needless friction? Or perhaps there was something inconvenient about it becoming known that he had overcome the 'Trial'?"

"Shut up, I ain't talkin' to you. Don't butt in!"

Echidna's words flickered through his mind, throwing Garfiel's thoughts into confusion.
There was no mistaking that he — Natsuki Subaru — had admitted to taking on the "Trial." Before Garfiel had said a word, Subaru had already understood the "Trial" to be the past.
He remembered Subaru saying he hadn't been able to overcome it. At the time, Garfiel had been too shaken by learning Subaru was qualified to bother asking the questions he should have, and had cut the conversation short—

"――――"

Had Subaru's expression, back then, been the face of a man crushed by a past he couldn't overcome?
It had been the face of a man regretting some failure to reach something — but it hadn't been the face of a man carrying an unresolved problem inside himself. He'd seen that particular face, day after day, reflected on the water's surface when he bathed.

And the figure who'd beaten Garfiel down hadn't had the face, the voice, or the conviction of "a man held back by his past."

"That bastard... overcame it? His past? The past — can be overcome...?"

"If, hypothetically, there was ever a scene where he lectured you high and mighty out there — wouldn't it be reasonable to think he had something in him that allowed him to do so?"

Amid his hazy consciousness, he recalled trading blows with Subaru.
Both Subaru and Garfiel had been at the very limit of holding onto consciousness. He couldn't gather up every word that had been shouted then. No — he mustn't give up. Here, now, he had to gather up what he'd dropped and pierce himself through with it.

What had been said to him. What had been shouted.
The past, standing still, staying unchanging, the barrier, the "Sanctuary," family.

What had he said happened to those who stood still, who got stuck and couldn't move?
Hadn't he said that if you just wanted to start, you were free to start anything, whenever you decided to?

"—You're really going, no matter what?"

Suddenly, a familiar voice struck Garfiel's ear.
But it was a voice that shouldn't have been able to be heard here.

Because it belonged to neither something that could interfere with Garfiel here, nor something Garfiel himself could interfere with here.

"Yes, I'm going. I'm sorry for the trouble this will cause Ryuzu-sama."

"Don't you worry none about that. What matters is how these children feel."

The words being exchanged were both the familiar voices of family, and unfamiliar ones.
Ryuzu's sour-faced mouth moved, and the mouth of the mother facing her moved, and sound slipped in.

For the first time, Garfiel heard his mother's voice — a voice that shouldn't have existed within his memory.

"――――"

Catching his breath, Garfiel's attention was seized entirely by the scene before him.
His mother gazed at the Garfiel in her arms with loving affection, rocking his body to soothe him. Looking up at her mother, Frederica, gripping the hem of her skirt, forced out her voice.

"M-Mother... I... I..."

"I'm sorry, Fu-chan. I'm putting so much worry on you, aren't I."

"It's alright. I'm... I'm fine. But Garf, he's so pitiful..."

"If I took him with me, well, you know how clumsy Mommy is. I'm sure it'd be hard on Gar-chan. Fu-chan, you're so much more dependable than your mother, so I'm counting on you, okay?"

Even as she felt lonely, Frederica offered words seeing her mother off.
Garfiel learned, for the first time, that his older sister had approved of their mother leaving the Sanctuary. Ryuzu, too, held the trembling Frederica's shoulder, seeming to respect her wish.

"Gar-chan. Mommy's going now, okay?"

Calling out to him, his mother lifted Garfiel up.
Held aloft, Garfiel laughed innocently, oblivious to his mother's resolve. His mother drew him close and gently kissed his forehead.
That spot was the very same place where the white scar now sat on Garfiel's forehead.

"I'll be sure to bring your father back with me. Until then, wait for me, okay?"

"――――!"

Eyes brimming with tender love, words overflowing with consideration.
As if unwilling to let go of a memory she couldn't bear to part with, his mother kissed Garfiel again and again and again.

Only then did she finally hand the young Garfiel over to Ryuzu.
Ryuzu held Garfiel's body securely, nodding with his mother. His mother then embraced Frederica, and just as she had with Garfiel, showered her beloved daughter's forehead with a rain of kisses.

"—Ha, ah. Ah, aaah... aaah...!"

Watching it unfold, Garfiel found himself, without realizing it, on his knees.
He didn't know what he was looking at.

He didn't know this scene. He had no memory of ever having seen this scene.
When he had been still small, when he'd understood nothing, when he had first faced the past, what Garfiel had seen was a far more hopeless memory of despair, one that felt like being flayed alive.

Even a memory like that — even a memory of savoring the feeling of having been discarded like garbage — Garfiel had believed to be something precious, and had cultivated his own stubbornness around it.
That belief, all the days that had come since, the false bravado stretched over to hide the resentment and misery underneath — it was peeling away, crumbling apart, being repainted into something else entirely.

What was this memory?
Hadn't his mother abandoned him and his sister, chasing only her own happiness as she left? Hadn't she cast aside her burdensome children and walked her own path?

This was — backwards, wasn't it.
His mother had abandoned him and his sister and left. That was precisely why Garfiel had been able to forge the existence called "Garfiel Tinzel" so firmly.

The moment he realized that belief had been a misguided illusion, that solid rampart turned to crumbling clay walls, and Garfiel's world collapsed from beneath his feet.

Before Garfiel, no longer even able to stand up straight, the past family's farewell drew toward its end.
Having said her fill of goodbyes, the mother touched Frederica and Garfiel one last time, entrusted everything to Ryuzu, and headed with a single bag toward the edge of the forest.
Along the way, she stopped again and again. She turned back, waving to Frederica, who waved back in farewell. And to Garfiel, whose hand Ryuzu held and waved for him toward his departing mother, she waved back too.

Then she'd steady herself and walk on again. Stop again. Turn, wave.
Repeating that over and over, again and again, his mother made her way toward the edge of the forest, and—

"—Wha, aah!?"

As he tried to rise, to chase after his mother, his vision suddenly warped.
The world's edges blurring wasn't only because of the tears welling in Garfiel's eyes. The world was growing hazy for a far clearer, more definite reason.

From the edges of his vision, wrapped in white light, the forest was vanishing.
As if it were the end of the world itself — the unintended end of the story unfolding — Garfiel turned back toward the witch standing behind him and shouted.

"Why?! Why's it endin' here! There's still—there's still the important part—"

"No, this is the end. There's no need to see any more. It wasn't I who acknowledged the dream's end — it was you. Congratulations, Garfiel. You've repainted your past."

"What are you—!? Don't screw with me! The part I gotta do somethin' about the most is from here on—!"

"There's no need to see what comes after, and even if you were to picture what happens next, there's no room there for you to intervene."

"Ah—"

Was that the witch's answer — that the past could not be changed?
The color drained from Garfiel's flushed face, and the knee he'd been rising on gave way again.

He'd finally understood his mother's true feelings.
Did that mean the fate that awaited her after she left this place, remained unchanged?

His mother had left the Sanctuary in search of his father, for the sake of Garfiel and Frederica. But that journey had come to ruin almost as soon as it began. Along with his mother's life.

—Wasn't this just a hopeless memory sinking into an even more hopeless outcome?
A memory that had only overwritten despair with more despair — had it simply been switched out for one where hope was now painted over with despair? What was he even supposed to change about himself, with this?

"Mother loved me. And she loved Garf too."

Startled, Garfiel jerked his head up and looked ahead.
Looking down at him as he knelt, his sister, in her young form, had said that. Her eyes were on Garfiel. The past that shouldn't have been able to see him, shouldn't have been able to interfere with him — was interfering.

"For the sake of her family, Mother left the 'Sanctuary.' Do you have some complaint about that?"

"Sc-screw... screw you! So what if she loved me! It just means there's some — some useless extra memory hangin' off me now! I—!"

"It was easier on you, wasn't it, believing you weren't loved."

The young Frederica said it as if belittling her rebutting brother.
The difference in their height was, literally, that of a child and an adult. And yet his sister, seemingly without a thought for that, came at him head-on, her face saying plainly, *what a troublesome little brother.*

"If you believed the love only went one way, you could justify yourself."

"That's not—!"

"Loving, and being loved — once you're forced to realize that's how it was, you can no longer justify never having run out into the world, never having shut yourself up in the 'Sanctuary,' can you?"

"That ain't it! It ain't, it ain't! You don't know nothin'... about what happened to Mother!"

"—There's no way I wouldn't know."

To Garfiel, about to shout out of pure fury, that struck like a blow.
Frederica before him had lost all expression, watching him as though holding back her own feelings.

—What had her sister just said?

"There's no way I wouldn't know. If Mother had left the outside of the 'Sanctuary' and met with some misfortune right away... there's no way I wouldn't have been told."

"Then... why...!?"

"You understand, don't you, why I couldn't tell you. Garf. You're not a small child anymore."

Frederica had known what had happened to their mother.
And Garfiel understood, too, why she couldn't have told the young Garfiel.

Who could tell a still-young boy about his mother's cruel end?
If he hadn't caught a glimpse of the "Trial" in the tomb, Garfiel would have remained ignorant of it forever. That he hadn't stayed that way was only because he had trampled over the great deal of consideration meant to prevent exactly that.

"You truly did remember, didn't you — being loved by Mother."

"............"

"That's why you hurt yourself, on purpose, in the very place where Mother kissed you, the place you last touched — to make it as if it never happened."

He touched the white scar on his forehead.
This scar hadn't existed on his young forehead.

This scar had come into being right after Garfiel first took on the "Trial" of the tomb. Thrown into a frenzy, Garfiel had smashed his head against the wall, against the ground, hard enough to split it open, hurting himself badly enough to leave a permanent mark.
That wound was this scar. And the true reason he'd inflicted it was surely exactly what Frederica had just said.

"It's ending now, isn't it."

Frederica's murmur.
Before he knew it, the world of the past had already lost most of its form.

The forest was already gone from anywhere, and the mother who had walked away, and the standing figure of Ryuzu, were nowhere to be found. Even the witch who had been watching over the proceedings was nowhere in sight; only Garfiel and Frederica, brother and sister, remained left behind in the world.

"Even if you hide it with a scar, the past doesn't disappear. Neither does the fact that you were loved."

"Me... what am I s'posed to do?"

In a weak voice, Garfiel asked Frederica.

"If Mother's end don't change, then the outside's still gonna stay somethin' scary to me. Goin' out into a world like that, havin' to make Granny and everyone else go out too — that's scary."

"Even at your age, must you rely on a little sister like me just to find your answer?"

"I know it's pathetic! But the only one I got to rely on is you, sis. So — tell me, sis... why'd you go outside...?"

"What do you want to do, Garf?"

Cutting him off, Frederica tilted her head and asked him.
For a moment, Garfiel was at a loss for words. This wasn't about what he wanted to do. He wanted to know what he should do, what he had to do — that was what he was asking about.

"What do you want to do, Garf?"

To her faltering brother, his sister repeated the same question, wearing an expression of helpless patience.
And so Garfiel drew a breath.

"I wanna... do what's needed of me."

"Needed by whom?"

"By the ones who... need me. I wanna do what they're askin' of me."

"Why do you feel that way?"

"'Cause they... reminded me."

Of what, his sister didn't say aloud.
But her golden eyes, the same color as his own, were asking exactly that.

"—That Mother loved me."



—In the next instant, the dream world shattered into pieces, and the past vanished far into the distance.

When Garfiel emerged from the tomb, his expression looked to Subaru as though some possessing spirit had finally left him.

Arms crossed, legs bouncing anxiously, they waited roughly an hour for the outcome.
Waiting on tenterhooks, Subaru and the others neither exchanged glances nor traded words, simply enduring the silence as they waited for the result.

"What if he breaks his promise and just goes and destroys the ruins, I wonder?"

Otto said, apparently meaning it as a joke to lighten the mood, but it came off as remarkably tone-deaf instead, earning him a flying kick from Ram — an incident along the way — but for the most part, the situation was watched over quietly.

"...Gar-bou."

The one pressing her hands together anxiously, eyes flicking repeatedly to the tomb, was Ryuzu, who had rejoined them.
The Ryuzu present here now was presumably θ, but no matter which Ryuzu it was, her concern for Garfiel never changed.

Both the matter of three people and two beasts ganging up to beat Garfiel down, and the matter of Garfiel, resolved to turn over a new leaf, walking into the tomb — both must have struck no small blow to Ryuzu's heart.

"............"

Fidgeting, unable to sit still, watching the tomb — that described Emilia too, standing beside Subaru.
In her case, she was surely worried about Garfiel's outcome, of course, but there was also the fact that once Garfiel came out, her own turn would come around.

The argument inside the tomb, and the fight between Subaru and Garfiel afterward.
What change of heart those two things had wrought in her, he couldn't say. But her anxious-looking expression carried more of the color of worry for Garfiel than any sign of dread at facing the "Trial" herself. That, at least, wasn't a bad sign.

"—Ah!"

As Subaru was thinking along those lines, Otto's voice, pointing at the tomb, reached his ear.
*Another tone-deaf remark,* Subaru thought, frowning, while Ram cracked her knuckles and rose to her feet. But it seemed this time it was their own hasty assumption.

"Gar-bou!"

Ryuzu leapt up and dashed toward the tomb.
Following her gaze, Subaru noticed a figure had appeared at the tomb's entrance.

Short blond hair, a white scar on the forehead. Sharp eyes, blade-white canine teeth.
A small, hunched build, and a fierce, imposing presence radiating out of proportion to it.

Garfiel Tinzel.

"See, just like I said, I—gyaan!"

Struck head-on by Ram's hasty knee strike, Otto went tumbling into the underbrush.
But no one paid the injured Otto any mind; instead, Subaru and the others rushed toward Garfiel, who had finished the "Trial."
Bounding up the stone steps, Subaru made for him. Ryuzu, who'd already reached him first, stood in front of him, apparently at a loss for what to say.

"G-Gar-bou. That, well, I..."

"Don't make a face that don't suit ya, old hag. Sorry for makin' ya worry."

"Gar-bou."

Saying it gruffly, Garfiel set a hand on little Ryuzu's head.
Patting one's grandmother's head — the gesture seemed rather disrespectful, and yet given the two of their statures, it was a natural exchange. And besides, if Ryuzu wasn't going to object, it would be needlessly crass for anyone else to point it out.

"Garf. How was it?"

Arriving on the steps behind Subaru and the others, Ram called out to Garfiel.
It had been Ram who'd given Garfiel the direct impetus to take on the "Trial." In her expression — she who knew the full background of the matter — there flickered a rare hint of concern, one seldom seen in front of Subaru.
At her question, Garfiel let out a low groan, as if thinking it over, and—

"Ain't got no visible result to show for it, if that's what you're askin'. Feels like — this is just how it is, I guess."

"That sounds kinda like a middle schooler bragging about shoplifting, but the fact that you're even saying stuff like that means... you actually did it?"

"—Guess you could say I put somethin' to rest."

He said that in response to Subaru, then let out a deep breath through his nose.
For a moment, everyone caught their breath at his words, but a different sense of emotion quickly took hold.

In other words, Garfiel had overcome the "Trial" and settled things with his past.
That meant a step forward toward the unsealing of the Sanctuary — and it also proved that the "Trial" was by no means an impossible task that could never be overcome.

"So then, you — riding this momentum, the rest of the 'Trials' too—"

"Screw off. Only 'Trial' I'm takin' on is this one. Rest of 'em ain't my business to handle. Ain't that right."

"Yes, that's right. The rest, that's my job. It's not something you get to take from me."

Both Garfiel and Emilia rejected Subaru's attempt to get him carried away with the moment.
Garfiel glared at Emilia, and Emilia met that glare head-on.

"That witch's still just as rotten as ever. Best watch yourself, that's all."

"Huh? You're giving me advice? Thanks. I'll keep it in mind."

Whatever encouragement, whatever sarcasm Garfiel had intended in his own way — Emilia took only the encouragement part of it and blithely let the sarcasm sail right past her.
At Garfiel's slightly deflated expression, Subaru's cheeks loosened into a grin — and Garfiel's gaze turned toward Subaru next.
Before Subaru, eyebrows raised, Garfiel scratched his cheek and—

"Ah, and... that."

"What's up? Getting all fidgety doesn't suit your character. You're the type whose stats for hesitating and overthinking are basically at zero, so let's stick with the barbarian playstyle."

"Dunno what the hell you're sayin', but I get that you're makin' fun of me, oi. I'll teach ya a lesson... ah, nah, forget it."

Garfiel half-raised an arm, then lowered it without doing anything.
His fidgety, oddly evasive behavior left Subaru tilting his head in confusion. Only Ram, standing there, wore a smile that suggested she understood everything perfectly, and—

"Garf."

She gave Garfiel's hip a little poke.
At Ram's attack, Garfiel let out a breath and shook his head, resigned.

"Gettin' past the 'Trial' inside there — probably thanks to you. Thank ya."

"...Did you just say thank you?"

"Ain't sayin' it twice. Just — I managed to remember what I needed to remember. So goin' in there was worth... somethin'. Dammit!"

Perhaps embarrassment had built up as he spoke, because Garfiel's face reddened with irritation, and he jabbed a finger at the wide-eyed Subaru.

"Listen up! Sure, I lost, and yeah, the 'Trial's' outcome changed too. But that don't mean I'm admittin' all of you were right about everything and wavin' the white flag, showin' my belly and surrenderin'! You lot ran your mouths big, sayin' you'd change this 'Sanctuary'! If things change and it ends up hard or painful for the people inside — I ain't gonna go easy on you!"

"O-oh... that's, of course..."

Overwhelmed by the sheer intensity of it, Subaru managed to force out a response anyway.
There was no absolute guarantee for Garfiel's concerns. But he could promise, at least, to strive with everything he had to make sure things turned out better. That much, at least, was certain — and he was about to put it into words when—

"So — I'm gonna watch, real close, whether you lot end up bein' nothin' but mouths runnin'. —Do it right, 'Boss'!"

"――――"

He slapped Subaru roughly on the shoulder and glared straight at him as he said it.
The unexpected way of addressing him, and his manner, left Subaru stunned, his response delayed.

In that moment, Garfiel swept right past Subaru's side and headed down the tomb's stone steps with Ryuzu in tow. As if he were afraid of having his face seen.

"Just now, Garfiel's face was suuuuper red."

Having apparently caught a glimpse of his face right before he passed by, Emilia said it with an expression holding back laughter.
The fact that she was saying it that way meant Subaru hadn't misheard just now.

"'Boss,' huh... the top of this group isn't me, it's Emilia."

"Subaru and the others were the ones who beat Garfiel, though. Since it was decided by a man-to-man showdown, Garfiel's boss is Subaru. Amazing, huh, boss."

Smiling without any malicious intent, Emilia left Subaru not knowing how to respond.
Poking the flank of the troubled-looking Subaru, Ram shrugged and said, "Just accept it."

"The man himself is worked up and doesn't know what else to do about it. Just let him have his way."

"Well, sure, but I'm way weaker than him, so that part kind of..."

"Setting aside who's stronger in a fight, Barusu's older, so cut him that much slack. Garf, for all he looks like that, is still a child. He's probably feeling like he's got an older brother now, or something."

"Hold on a second."

Ram was watching the back of Garfiel's head down below with an exasperated look.
A part of what she'd just said caught Subaru's attention, and he called a halt.

"What?"

"Just now — what did you say?"

"Which part?"

"You said Garfiel's younger than me?"

At Subaru's question, Ram nodded as if it clicked, saying "Ah,"

"You didn't know? Garf's younger than you, Barusu."

"How old?"

"Fourteen this year, I believe."

"FOURTEEEN!? Fourteen means... you're in middle school, second-years!?"

Faced with information from entirely outside his expectations, Subaru's voice cracked with surprise.
And somehow, both the fact that he was being called "boss," and the fact that Garfiel had been so childishly stubborn, suddenly made sense to him.

"A middle-school second-year in the thick of his rebellious phase... no wonder he's such a handful to tame..."

He murmured that, sounding even more exhausted than he had right after the fight had ended.
