Arc 9 Chapter 42: "What It Means to Break One's Heart"

—Gods above, Buddha too, Od Lagna-sama. I swear that for the rest of my life, I will never hold a stuffed toy again.

"————"

The instant his jaw was jolted upward by a knee, Roy Alphard's thoughts raced like a bolt of lightning.
Strange, incomprehensible, bizarre beyond words, a whirlpool of suspicion, doubt, misgiving — something's wrong, a great anomaly, a mistake, uncanny in the extreme, an aberration, not right, wrong, chaos, doubt, doubt-doubt, doubt-doubt-doubt-doubt-doubt-doubt-doubt——

"—Die."

Ram, who had somehow caught Roy off guard right as that confusion seized control of him, wasn't about to let the opening slip.
Her pale fingers shot out and drove a throat-thrust straight into Roy's upturned neck. Nausea welled up as his breath was cut off, and pain and suffocation assaulted his stunted frame.
But what made Ram truly terrifying was that she didn't ease up there at all — how utterly uncharming of her.

"Fura."

A blade of wind formed between the hand gripping his windpipe, and its keen edge caught Roy's slender, escape-less neck dead-on, mercilessly severing his head from his torso——

"—except it doesn't, see!"

In that instant, the wind slash tore through, and Roy's neck burst, spraying blood.
But it couldn't reach the life beneath it — the reproduction of the "Carnivorous Beast" Beli Hainelga's iron hide, a hide so tough that not a single one of the thousand swords the guards had wielded trying to bring down a vicious criminal had ever pierced it.

"————"

Ram's eyes went wide at the unexpected result, and in that split second, Roy dragged her along — still frozen mid-throat-thrust — and shifted the two of them instantly high above the battlefield: the "Leaper" Dorkell's power of teleportation.
The "Leaper," who one day abruptly gained his otherworldly power through a connection to Od Lagna as if by sudden revelation, was a destroyer of order who had used that power to assassinate any number of men in power. There was no warning sign before his teleportation; Roy forced down the momentarily stiffened, caught-off-guard Ram, and used that opening to size up the battlefield's other prey——

"El Fura!"

"—Whoah, seriously!?"

Without a shred of hesitation, a blast of wind magic slammed straight into his gut, and Roy let out an admiring cry despite himself.
No question about it — the teleport must have caught Ram completely off guard. And yet she'd digested both the shock of failing to take Roy's head and the surprise of the teleport in an instant, and struck back immediately. What nerve! What stout-hearted grit!

"Now that's the stuff!"

That wind blade, too, failed to pierce the "Carnivorous Beast's" iron hide. Still, he wanted to honor her split-second decisiveness and drive. So, pouring that admiration into his fist, he answered with a blow from the "Fist King" Neiji Lockhart — the very punch that had crushed the heads of every strongman on the Sword-Slave Isle. Take that hit, and Ram's slender body should have been crushed to a pulp — instead, she vanished.

"Did you lose sight of Ram? What useless eyes you have."

The sudden voice — Ram's presence, which should have vanished, appeared behind him instead, and Roy reflexively threw a no-look "Fist King" punch that met only air. As if by magic, her presence shifted overhead, and she landed a kick to the base of his skull, sending him plummeting headlong toward the ground — falling, falling, falling——

"Ahh, how lovely."

Even as he fell back-first toward the ground, Roy made no move to break his fall, entirely absorbed in the sight above him — a girl whose skirt hem flared as her pink hair trailed gracefully through the air. He crashed into the ground utterly unguarded, the impact from his back driving straight through to his gut, pain piercing his whole body — but he didn't mind at all.

—The "Carnivorous Beast" × the "Leaper" × the "Fist King."

Among all the beyond-reason existences "Gluttony" had ever devoured, these were the cream of the crop — and Roy found himself unexpectedly moved by the sharpness of Ram's power, skill, and spirit in handling them so masterfully.
Here, too, Roy felt he had to show his own good side, so he kicked himself upright and coiled his knees, gathering strength to leap at Ram.
Because——

"When it comes to hospitality, you've gotta answer with everything you've got, full throttle, holding absolutely nothing back — otherwise you can't call it love, right!?"

"—That way of thinking sounds awfully similar to something I've heard before, and I loathe it immensely."

But before Roy could leap back into the air, a savage, raging roar struck him from the side. The roar came paired with sharp beast-claws and an explosive charging force.
Twisted horns, a towering body one had to look up to see, borne on limbs that looked too slender to support such a frame yet ran the earth with supple ease — this was the very byword for ferocity and viciousness known as the "Black King of the Forest"——

"—————!!"
"Yaay, Guiltirau!!"

A slashing claw swept down at Roy, who'd let out a cheer. Roy countered on instinct with the power of the "Snow Vallum" Tisuro — an outrageous physique with a legend of single-handedly stopping an avalanche — catching the falling Witchbeast's mighty arms head-on, right foreleg then left, and locking into a full grapple.
Right then, gathering his strength to wrench both of Guiltirau's legs clean off — in that instant, he slid under the beast's belly and found himself eye to eye with a pair of pale pink irises leveling a staff at him.

"Really, this is the best——"

"Fura."

Not even letting him finish, a bullet of wind caught him square in the forehead, and Guiltirau's tail-strike sent him flying. Knocked back hard, Roy skidded along the ground on his toes and lifted his face.
Behind Ram, staff still leveled, the girl Meili clung to Guiltirau's back, pointing her slender finger at Roy — no, past Roy, behind him — and shouted.

"Get 'em, my little magic-black doggies!!"
"《—!》"

Answering Meili's high voice, countless points of red light lunged ferociously at Roy.
They were the blazing crimson eyes of black dogs baring savage fangs to sink into their prey — a pack of Witchbeasts, Ulgarms, harboring curse and magic beyond what their bodies should bear.
The pack of demon hounds showed no mercy, sinking their fangs into "Gluttony's" body and tearing at it. Ironically, the end of Roy Alphard — who had made the name "Foul Eater" his own — seemed set to be nothing but pitiful feed for merciless Witchbeasts——

"Aaaahh aaahh aahh——"

The Witchbeasts' movements froze at the sudden song that rang out from Roy's throat.
Amid the pack of ferocious hounds, now frozen in place, Roy gently stroked the head of the one biting into his own neck — and immediately after, the blood spraying from his wounds writhed into blades, slashing through the Witchbeasts with no escape, skewering them, and drawing out their death-shrieks.

"There we go."

Slowly rising to his feet, Roy showed off his whole body.
The bite wounds carved all over him had turned their flowing blood into gleaming, glinting blades, and the pack of black dogs now hung there like fruit grown on the branches of an orchard tree, transformed into corpses.

A dream collaboration: the cursed songs of the "Blighted Love Balladeer" Nota, who once traveled reciting seditious songs across the land, and the "Blood-Weeping Oni" Advan, a sorcerer who could control nothing but his own blood. And further, using the mana left behind in the corpses of the slain Witchbeasts, he brought to life the sorcery of the "Lord of Phantasmal Rainbows" Matokurima Joradoon, who had once plotted the overthrow of the Holy Kingdom — turning the corpses into cocoons from which rainbow-colored butterflies emerged, fluttering to dance around Roy.
Butterflies hatched, one for every corpse, fluttering, fluttering, fluttering——

"Ta-daa."

—The "Snow Vallum" × the "Blighted Love Balladeer" × the "Blood-Weeping Oni" × the "Lord of Phantasmal Rainbows."

With countless rainbow butterflies attending him, Roy decked out this grand stage with a token of heartfelt praise for Ram and the others, who had put on such a truly, truly delectable display of hospitality.
Ram and Meili, however, only hardened their gazes and their bearing, openly showing their hostility.

"Whoo, wow, that was something. That combo just now, so perfectly in sync! Gave me shivers. Since when did you two get so close? I guess the distance closed during that trip across the sand sea we didn't get to see, huh? Ram-san, Meili-san, you're both so wicked."

"Stop talking to me so calmly while covered in blood and looking so pathetic. Exchanging words with a lunatic makes Ram's little bird heart feel like it's about to burst from anxiety."

"A little bird heart! Ahh, the fact that you can say that about yourself with a straight face — I really respect that. I just can't stop getting excited wondering what kind of prep work is gonna blow up next."

Clapping his hands, Roy smoothly drew the blood-blades back into his body. Amid the thudding fall of the Witchbeasts' corpses, he clotted the blood at his bite wounds, staunched the bleeding in a snap, and let out a rapturous breath.
His sincere feelings didn't seem to have gotten through, but his admiration was genuine. Unrequited love was painful. Still, he wanted to cherish this one thing — that Ram and the others had fought harder than even he'd hoped for.

"Setting that aside, though."

Putting his fondness for Ram and the others to one side, Roy turned to the puzzle of the inexplicable phenomenon that had occurred during that exchange of blows over the past ten-odd seconds. He guessed it had something closely to do with the vanished "Divine Dragon."
He was truly, truly astonished. That enormous, hulking "Divine Dragon" was nowhere to be found on the battlefield anymore, without anyone even noticing when.

"Probably the same trick as how the helmet old man and the shinobi lady disappeared, and how Ram-san and the others popped up out of nowhere... I think that's Clind-san's doing, and it feels like an Authority."

The vanished Aldebaran and Yae, and the "Divine Dragon" too. And on top of that, the increased number of enemies, and Ram's presence popping in and out — all of it matched the image of Clind's special skill within his "Memory."
Still, going by Roy's own knowledge, apart from his "Memory," the thing that would make this possible wasn't some individual's unique power, nor a Divine Protection of a differing nature, but possibly an Authority.
If that were the case, it would mean Clind was the holder of "Pride," the one seat still left vacant.

"Well, guess it doesn't matter right now who's gone missing."

Not just the "Divine Dragon" — Clind and Roswaal were also absent from the battlefield.
Clind's actual strength was unknown, but if he really held an Authority, he'd at least amount to some fighting power. Roswaal and Clind teaming up, engaging the "Divine Dragon" in large-scale combat — and shifting the battlefield elsewhere so the damage wouldn't spill onto the surroundings. That was probably about the shape of it.
Either way, if they'd vanished from this battlefield, there'd be no more chances for that appearing-and-disappearing trick to be used again here.
So then, rather than the heartless folks who'd vanished, savoring the hospitality of the ladies right in front of him took priority for now.

"Nice, nice, humans really are nice."

The method used to prevent the "Eclipse," the preparation of nameless Witchbeasts to devour, and honestly every single element that had gone into building this battlefield — it was all a culmination of schemes prepared to trap and kill Roy and his kind for good.
Advance preparation to face an opponent — that was exactly what prep work meant, and it was the spirit of hospitality itself. Giving everything you have to entertain a guest — that, after all, is what it means to break your heart over something.

"In that case, I've gotta answer with an appropriately fitting dress code myself, or it'd be rude to all that love-filled consideration, wouldn't it?"

"—...What are you planning?"

Sensing the change in Roy, the one being feted, Ram furrowed her shapely brows. But to the beautiful maid, now openly wary, Roy simply shook his head. "Nah, nah."

An all-out war — yes, this was a battle worthy of that name, truly the grand reckoning.
The Royal Selection's mixed army — the enemy camp that Natsuki Subaru, had he been here, would probably have christened the "Aldebusters" — had readied its hospitality down to the last detail, and every one of them wore the eyes of someone who'd resolved to wring out every last drop of their own wit and hidden strength, to fight to the death and give it their all right to the end.
That wasn't true only of Ram and the others, but of every fighting force thrown unstintingly into this — and as far as Roy Alphard was concerned, this was the peak moment where he wanted to give every single one of them a perfect hundred and a gold star.
The one flaw missing from that gold star didn't lie with his opponents. — It lay on his own side.

"————"

At the edge of his vision, Roy caught sight of a red-haired man, left stranded on the same battlefield as him, crouched pathetically as blood dripped steadily from his nose — Heinkel.

It might seem presumptuous, coming as it did from "Gluttony's" point of view, but Roy's opinion of Heinkel was surprisingly not bad. A life tangled in self-imposed knots, carrying some bleak, gloomy anguish he apparently could never bring himself to confess — it seemed like the sort of life that could only produce a thoroughly murky bitterness, and that piqued his interest as a rare delicacy.
That said, the basic premise was that it mustn't spoil the aroma or appearance of the main dish, and as things currently stood, Heinkel had nothing going for him even by "Gluttony's" standards.

"No matter how extravagant the banquet, if there's a plate with raw garbage sitting on it mixed in, it ruins the whole thing."

Just one man, alone, mixed into a battlefield where everyone else stood united, ready to clash life against life — that was a factor liable to ruin it all, and even a "Foul Eater" found that unbearable.
So——

"Hey, old man, quit sulking and get a move on already!"

"Y-you, the Sin Archbishop...!"

At Roy's sudden raised voice, Heinkel, his face streaked with nosebleed, looked up. Meeting his eyes and seeing those blue eyes waver unnaturally, Roy found it funny.
It was practically a pleading look. If there was even a shred of camaraderie or a desire to rely on him in there somewhere, then looking to a Sin Archbishop for that had limits even for someone past caring how they looked. That boundless shamelessness was, in a way, irreplaceable — but right now, it was purely in the way.

"—Say, why don't I just go ahead and free you from that burden of yours, old man?"

He wouldn't save him. Nor would he abandon him.
He simply grinned, baring his teeth, and offered Heinkel a third option.

"————"

Even to Heinkel, eyes frozen wide, that proposal had certainly gotten through.
Roy didn't know the details of just how important that thing he'd called a "burden" was to Heinkel. But surely, it was something more important to him than his own life or death. And Heinkel had seen with his own eyes, had come to know, that Roy possessed the means to take it away.
That what Roy said was by no means an idle threat, but a genuinely executable option.
And so——

"That's it, that's the stuff."

At the change in the expression of the man now gritting his teeth, Roy's heart raced with genuine delight. As if answering the surge of Roy's emotion, the rainbow butterflies began to dance as though courting him.
As rainbow-hued scales began to make the sky shimmer, Roy let his tongue loll out and grinned. —Now, in the truest sense, the all-out war could finally begin.

"Wanting to be satisfied and yet not wanting to be satisfied. — Life sure is deep with flavor, and full of surprises."

"Gueh...!"

Struck by an impact that seemed to sandwich him from back and chest at once, Heinkel collapsed helplessly.
Carol and Grimm — the married pair of servants who had long served House Astrea — their granddaughters, twins who'd also been given instruction in the "Flow Method," delivered kicks that were lethal weapons belying their youth.

"Ouh, oohf, ohuek—"

He hadn't managed to eat a proper meal since the war in the Vollachian Empire ended. Stomach acid was wrung out of his empty gut, and Heinkel dropped pathetically to his knees on the ground.

"《———》"

His cheeks twisted with self-mockery at Flam and Grassis, who looked down at him coldly.
Believe it or not, Heinkel was still the current head of House Astrea. And yet these daughters of House Remendis, who were supposed to swear their loyalty to him, looked at him with such disrespect.

"Our loyalty is dedicated to the young master."
"The head of the house stabbed the previous head. Right through."

At the sheer sharpness of the twins' reply, Heinkel fell silent, unable to even voice a grudge.
Both were arguments so undeniably correct he couldn't so much as grunt against them — reasons more than persuasive enough for the two of them to hate and treat him as an enemy. The former had always tormented Heinkel; the latter was a fresh sin now weighing on him, and it all pressed down on his whole body like a stone.
And yet——

"Can't die..."

The single utterance, ground out between his back teeth, could be taken two ways — that even after being beaten this badly, he couldn't afford to die, or that even after being beaten this badly, he still wasn't dying.
And Heinkel himself didn't know which meaning he'd meant when it slipped out.

"Ngh, guh!"

To Heinkel, already shaky on his feet, the twins showed no mercy. No hesitation.
The girls' limbs, with the "Flow Method" coursing through their whole bodies, had been honed to a strength that put steel to shame, and being struck by their storm of blows again and again was like being caught in an iron downpour.
Up and down, left and right, front and back — Flam and Grassis pressed the attack with flawlessly paired coordination, and Heinkel could do nothing but shield his vitals with his sword and take a merciless beating on the defensive.

—The "Flow Method." Come to think of it, that too, Heinkel had never properly mastered.
A combat technique that many top-tier swordsmen and warriors performed unconsciously — even when it was taught to him carefully and logically, Heinkel had never managed to bring it to full readiness for real combat.
Flam and Grassis, were they still twelve? He'd spent more than twice as many years as their combined age on it, and yet Heinkel's "Flow Method" fell far short of even these two.
If he could have written it off as simply a matter of talent, would that have lightened even one of Heinkel's hundred torments?

"D-dammit...!"

The twins nimbly dodged the sword swing he'd thrown out as a feint, coordinating with nothing more than a glance between them. His halfhearted swordplay, with barely any real intent to land, was read perfectly, and every one of his vital points — elbow to armpit to neck — was struck true.
His bones creaked, and from a mouth that had produced nothing but stomach acid, a drop of blood spilled. The instant he reflexively looked down, the twins, as if they'd been waiting for exactly that, flipped and kicked him squarely up under the nose.

"Gah—!"

Thrown backward, Heinkel struck the back of his head hard against the ground. His vision flickered, and with a ringing echo of tinnitus far off in his ears, he forcibly hauled his consciousness back from the edge of a blackout, hastily pulled himself up, leveled his sword, and glared straight ahead.
Right before his eyes, the twins landed their flip side by side, neatly brushing off their hems and lifting their faces. — As their gazes crossed his, Heinkel caught a faint trace of bewilderment in both of their eyes.

"————"

The source of the sisters' bewilderment was probably that Heinkel was still standing.
In terms of time, it had been mere seconds — a dozen or so — and yet that alone had left him in tatters. His clothes were caked in mud, and his stubbled face was thoroughly filthy with the nosebleed dripping steadily down. And yet, Heinkel wasn't falling. His consciousness, too, kept his eyes open without a break. Judging by the feel of their blows landing, that was simply unbelievable to Flam and the others.

"Haa, haah...!"

But sadly, endurance enough to astonish a pair of twins was nothing to be proud of.
The tip of the sword pointed at the two of them shook, and Heinkel's bluff was plain for anyone to see. The outcome was as good as decided already — no, this hadn't even been a fight to begin with. Since it wasn't a fight, there was no outcome to decide in the first place. It hadn't even reached the stage of contesting victory or defeat.

"Even so...!"

He couldn't afford to fall. He had to endure.
If he fell here, then he wouldn't know what all of this had been for up to now. Wouldn't know what he'd stabbed his father, Wilhelm van Astrea, for.
As long as he kept holding on by the skin of his teeth, hope would remain connected. Surely he'd see a glimmer of light.
Aldebaran, or the "Divine Dragon," or maybe Yae. Worst case, even a Sin Archbishop would do. If only someone would crack the tide of battle open, giving Heinkel his hope — that was when it happened.

"Hey, old man, quit sulking and get a move on already!"

Suddenly, a voice sliced through the battlefield and reached him, and Heinkel snapped his face up.
Only now, made to pay attention, did he notice. Nowhere on the battlefield was the "Divine Dragon's" massive body. Not just Aldebaran and Yae — even the "Divine Dragon" had vanished from the battlefield, leaving only Heinkel, and—

"Y-you, the Sin Archbishop...!"

—only Roy Alphard, over there, likewise facing down enemies of his own.
Wondering just how long things had been this way, and layering disappointment upon disappointment in himself for being so blind to his surroundings, Heinkel tried to gauge the intent behind Roy calling out to him.
Trying to team up with a Sin Archbishop — honestly, even he thought that was out of his mind. But apparently, back in the Vollachian Empire, Natsuki Subaru, Emilia's knight, had kept "Gluttony" under his command. If he told himself it was the same thing, he could force down the intense revulsion.

He'd gone to the trouble of escaping the Prison Tower. Even a Sin Archbishop surely didn't want to die. Even if he was someone to be despised, if their goals aligned, there might be room to join hands——

"—Say, why don't I just go ahead and free you from that burden of yours, old man?"

But the words that followed betrayed Heinkel's hopes head-on.

"————"

It wasn't an offer of help, nor a request for help. A wildly out-of-place, self-serving remark.
What on earth was he thinking? Did he even understand the current situation? There was no Aldebaran here to force a path open with some incomprehensible power, no "Divine Dragon," the strongest living thing there was, no Yae, a master of petty tricks and harassment. There was only weak, fragile, powerless Heinkel and Roy, a Sin Archbishop of rotten, filthy nature — so what was the point of driving a wedge between them? — No, it wasn't that a wedge had been driven. The gap had already been there. An unfillable divide, a cliff too wide to leap, an absolute, sealed-off pit had existed — he'd simply come to see it clearly just now. And having seen it clearly, so what? What became of what that man had said? And what would he, who understood what that man meant, do now? — Free Heinkel from his burden? This burden that had by now fused itself into Heinkel's very soul?

—That could not be allowed. And he would not allow it.

"That's it, that's the stuff."

Those words, uttered by Roy in genuine delight, never reached Heinkel's ears.
As Heinkel rose to his feet with a swaying, ghostlike motion, Flam and Grassis, facing him, stiffened their cheeks faintly, then silently exchanged a glance and nodded to each other.
And then——

"Head of the house, prepare yourself."
"We're putting everything we've got into this."

Whether they judged that half-hearted flurries of blows would only backfire, Heinkel could feel the twins' focus sharpening against his skin.
Just as declared, an all-out blow was surely coming. The instant he sensed that, Flam and Grassis kicked off the ground and closed straight in on Heinkel. — No, not straight.

"《Ryaa!》"

Matching voice with motion, the twins gouged up chunks of earth with their kicks and sent them flying straight at him. The gouged earth filled his whole field of vision, closing in.
Heinkel cleaved it clean in two with a full swing of his sword. It wasn't a person, wasn't a life. If it was just an object, Heinkel had no hesitation in cutting it down.
But beyond the earth he'd split apart, in the field of view now opened up, Flam and the others were nowhere to be seen.
Instead, the twins had flanked Heinkel from both sides, and drove blows carrying the greatest acceleration yet, mercilessly, into both of his flanks.

"—Guh."

Without exaggeration, it was a full-bodied blow, the kind where the twins' fists buried themselves in him right up to the wrist.
Heinkel's ribs were shattered, and the impact reached straight through to the organs held within. Blood surged up with tremendous force and burst from his mouth in a great spray, and Heinkel very nearly blacked out.
But he didn't black out. He held his ground. And not only that — gritting his teeth, he twisted his body and forcibly seized hold of the sisters' arms as they drove into him.

"—Ah."
"This is bad."

Flam and Grassis grimaced at the pain of their wrists being wrung tight.
Even so, the two of them kept up a barrage of strikes against Heinkel with their free hands — the ones not caught — and their free legs, pummeling him like an iron storm, but Heinkel refused to release the arms he'd caught.

It wasn't something he'd wished for and gotten. Even so, the one thing Heinkel could declare himself superior to others in was this outstanding toughness, and using it in the best possible way was probably exactly this.
Taking a relentless beating while clinging on and grabbing at his opponent — a counterattack no true swordsman would ever stoop to, crude and utterly unrefined, and precisely fitting for Heinkel Astrea.

"—Aaaaahhh!!"

His face filthy with nosebleed and swollen from the blows, Heinkel raised both arms and, with all the force he had, slammed the twins he'd caught down toward the ground.
In this moment, he forgot that his opponents were children, that they served his own household, that they were the granddaughters of Carol and Grimm, whom he'd known since the day he was born. — No, not forgetting; he simply shut his eyes and, in one all-or-nothing gamble, drove the two of them headfirst toward the ground——

Rainbow-colored butterflies that used corpses as cocoons and the last embers of life as fuel to emerge — the sorcery devised by the "Lord of Phantasmal Rainbows" was never a creation of new life.
The butterfly was, after all, merely the shape it took; in essence, standing in stark contrast to the creation of life, it was the embodiment of death itself.

"That was Brother's trump card, wasn't it — the rainbow magic."

Within the devoured "Memory," a throbbing sensation sweetly calls to mind the rainbow aurora once wrapped around a sword — in principle, the ultimate technique unleashed by the Knight of Peerless Excellence and the rainbow butterflies are one and the same thing.
What that knight achieved through talent and the power of the spirits he'd contracted with, the "Lord of Phantasmal Rainbows" simply activated in exchange for a life wrung dry down to the dregs — that's all the difference amounted to.

"But this thing, the power's the exact same as ever, y'know."

Death-butterflies that would blast away anything they touched, root and all — at the signal of Roy's smile, some twenty of them surged all at once toward Ram and Meili.
Against that approaching threat, Ram and Meili instantly fell into perfect sync.

"Ram-oneesan!"

"That's the wrong way to address me."

"Got it, Milady!"

Exchanging that suitably serious back-and-forth, Meili, astride Guiltirau, stepped forward. She slapped the pitch-black Witchbeast's back, and its powerful arms forcibly snapped off the trees nearby.
As the trees crashed down with a splintering crack, Ram dashed in to take their place, crouched low, and leveled her staff straight ahead——

"El Fura."

The whirling wind sent the snapped trees hurtling forward with tremendous force, smashing them into the swarm of butterflies. The butterflies, scattering their death-dust, took the impact head-on, and an explosion of aurora light burst out.

"Ohhh~~!!"

Unable to hold back a shudder at that perfect answer, Roy stomped his feet on the ground.
The rainbow butterflies, harboring a power that unravels and destroys the composition of anything, were, so to speak, fire magic stones that spelled the end the instant you touched them. In exchange for that overwhelming destructive power, they were strictly one-time-use, disposable — smashing an obstacle into them to trigger a controlled demolition was by far the safest countermeasure.
Even so, pulling that off on the very first encounter — that left Roy honestly stunned.

"But-that-said!!"

If the butterflies were wiped out just like that, the "Lord of Phantasmal Rainbows" would be weeping bitter tears from beneath the grass of his grave.
The butterflies sent charging head-on vanished in the rainbow explosion, but skirting around that dazzling blast, the survivors — five apiece — swung wide in a great arc to target Ram and the others.
And this time, it wasn't just the butterflies — Roy himself also leapt over there with the "Leaper's" power. From there, he aimed to club Guiltirau to death first, using the "Snow Vallum's" mighty arms.

—The "Lord of Phantasmal Rainbows" × the "Leaper" × the "Snow Vallum."

Blocking Ram and the others' front with a blinding flash of aurora light, the surviving rainbow butterflies covering left and right, and the teleported Roy taking charge of directly behind them — an inescapable triple-pronged killing technique burst upon Ram and the others without mercy — and in that instant, the cry of a wailing infant tore through Roy's eardrums.

"———!!"

For a split second, that shriek — so out of place on a battlefield — stole Roy's thoughts, and right before his eyes, the thing wailing like an infant was a Witchbeast of such blasphemous, misshapen form it might be called a failed work of god — and it swung two burning spears made of bone, burning away the whole encircling swarm of rainbow butterflies in one stroke.
Amid the fresh explosion of aurora light born again, hair whipping in the blast, Roy laid eyes on the grotesque being that had appeared before him — the Starving Horse King, a thing that should only exist deep beneath the sand sea.

"It was such a pain bringing this thing along, y'know."

A head made of enormous horns, a torso resembling a human upper body equipped with a gigantic mouth, and beneath it, supporting all that, an oversized horse's neck and everything below — far too warped and grotesque to be called a proper man-horse union. — That Starving Horse King had swapped places with Guiltirau, which Meili had been riding just a moment ago.

"————"

At that fact, Roy's thoughts scattered in a thousand directions, and the grand council within his "Memory" scrambled for an answer.
Raised as agenda items were the truth behind the event unfolding before his eyes, how to deal with the crisis bearing down right beside him, and the proper reaction to this startling fact — all jumbled together with urgency.
And right before he could pick out which item to prioritize——

"—What's this, you've got a face as dumb as a pig's."

The owner of the voice that appeared right behind him drove a massive fist straight through Roy's stunted frame.

"—Gh"

Struck in the back, Roy's body was sent airborne, sailing clean over the heads of the Starving Horse King and Meili. Skidding along the ground even faster than the exploding aurora light, Roy jammed one hand into the earth to kill his momentum, twisted his body to land, righted himself with an acrobat's ease, and made his attacker give up on any follow-up.

In Roy's field of view: Meili astride the Starving Horse King, a hulking Pig Human in a black suit who'd appeared beside her, and Ram, now standing at a clearly different position than before. — The appearance of a new enemy and an instantaneous, inexplicable swap tactic — the very possibility he thought he'd already dismissed had resurfaced.

"Nah, guess that wasn't it after all."

Precisely because he had "Memory," he'd gotten stuck, unable to shake an unnecessary preconception.
That this instantaneous swapping was due to an Authority, and that it was specifically Clind's power. And so, in a battlefield where Clind had vanished along with the "Divine Dragon," he'd figured there was no need to be on guard against it.
But——

"—If you're a father too, then don't you dare lay a hand on a kid!!"

What he heard there was a thick voice burning with rage, followed by a fierce impact sound — at the edge of his vision, Heinkel, both arms wrapped around the twin girls, was taking a fist to the face.
The one who'd driven in that ferocious blow was a big man Roy recognized from his "Memory" — sure enough, Gaston, someone who shouldn't have been there the instant the battle began.

"————"

Struck full in the face, Heinkel flipped over backward, and the twins narrowly escaped harm.
Whether or not Roy's goading had done any good, it seemed Heinkel had gotten remarkably close — but the momentum of his counterattack had been snuffed out right at the start, all at once.
Though, Roy was in no position to laugh at Heinkel. He'd had his own start snuffed out by the very same opponent, using the very same trick.
And that opponent wasn't Clind.

"Ehh? Seriously? Is that even a thing?"

Unable to think of any other possibility, Roy doubted himself over a truth almost too surprising to accept.
The emotion packed into his voice was pure astonishment, with absolutely no intent to deny or belittle the fact. None at all — but if this really were the truth, it was outrageously unexpected.
Even mobilizing every scrap of "Memory" he had that knew her, the only conclusion he could reach was "No way."

It wasn't Clind. It was the one who had intervened, with impeccable timing, in both the fight between Roy and Ram's group, and the fight where the twins had beaten Heinkel senseless.
It was the one who had been overseeing this entire all-out war from above, continuously reassembling the situation with an Authority.
It was——

"—Petra-chan, since when did you become a Sin Archbishop?"

"That's making me sound really bad. If you're gonna call me something that sounds bad either way, then call me this instead."

So saying, the game-maker who harbored a Witch Factor and had commanded the entire battlefield through an Authority — Petra Leyte — placed a hand over her chest and stuck out her tongue with a "bleh."
And then, she declared——

"—'The Witch of Melancholy.' That's what you can call me — Petra Leyte."
