Arc 4 Chapter 63: "Empathy for Death"

The sound of indigo stakes driving into flesh chained together, and the purple crystals shattering under the impact filled the corridor with a chaotic riot of refracted light.
Countless stakes aimed at Elsa's slender body from every direction, meant to turn her into a honeycomb.
The scent of certain victory. Even while holding his breath at Beatrice's overwhelming magical power, even while watching a girl before his very eyes treat a murderer like nothing at all, Subaru couldn't quite kill the explosively swelling sense of dread rising in his chest.

—Something. He felt like he was forgetting something. Something he mustn't let slip away.

Before he could drag out from memory what that something was, all sorts of emotions got in the way.
What was Elsa's purpose in coming to the mansion? At the death of Frederica, whom he'd suspected, his heart was in chaos with remorse toward her and the cause of her death. He hadn't finished processing Petra's death either, and deep in his emotions something was screaming at him to go confirm Rem's safety on the other side of the door. What to do about Beatrice, what he needed to talk to her about.
Not a single one of the doubts confusing Subaru's heart had found an answer.

"――――"

And so Subaru missed a chance he should have been able to avoid, and would come to regret it.

"――guh, ah?"

Bursting through the light, something that had come flying struck deep into Subaru's right shoulder.
His eyes went to the sensation of pain, and seeing the wound slowly welling with blood, his thoughts went white-hot. A scream tore from his throat as if wrenched out, and Subaru, clutching the wound, reeled backward and fell onto his backside in the corridor.

"Why...!? That should have been a direct hit!"

Seeing Subaru wounded, Beatrice cried out.
Even as his thoughts burned with pain, hearing Beatrice's cry, Subaru realized. He understood. No, that wasn't it. It was different. It *had* been a direct hit. Of that there was no mistake. And yet—

"Elsaaaaa!!"

"You needn't call so passionately, I can hear you just fine."

Stimulated to hatred by the agonizing pain, fury spilled from Subaru's mouth alongside his scream. Answering him came from beyond the corridor where the light danced wildly—a voice languid and unhurried, with a lustrous quality that gave no hint they were exchanging blows over life and death.

"Coming away unscathed—that shouldn't be possible, I suppose."

"If I'd come here naked, I'd have died from that just now, though."

Elsa answered, shaking her head, her long braid swaying, in response to Beatrice's own head shake. On her figure there was no trace of having taken a direct hit from Beatrice's magic. Exactly as she'd appeared a moment before.
No—one thing was different. The black cloak she'd been wearing until just before was cast off, discarded; now she stood light and unencumbered in just the black garb beneath it.

"A coat that nullifies magic!"

"This should be the second time you've seen it. And yet it seems you were rather slow in telling that girl about it."

"Damn it...!"

At a blunder that couldn't be excused by mere words like "forgot," anger surged, pushing past the pain.
The cloak Elsa wore nullified magic—but only once. Subaru should have known this, having seen it when he clashed with Elsa in the royal capital.
Even accounting for the unexpected assault and the lack of coordination with Beatrice, it was the kind of blunder that should never have happened.

"Once the trick is known, it's not something to be surprised by anymore, I suppose."

"—Good. Very good, indeed. Strong, and lovely. It seems I'll get to enjoy a different kind of warmth than that of a girl who does nothing but weep."

Elsa smiled as she spun the kukri knife in her hand while Beatrice raised her mana once more. That smile of blood, and those words—Subaru understood at once who "that girl who does nothing but weep" referred to, and instantly boiled over.

"You have no right to disgrace Petra, none at all——!!"

The small blade that had been driven into his right shoulder—the tip hooked, a type of throwing weapon designed to resist being pulled out. Gritting his back teeth against the flesh it had dug into, Subaru yanked it out in one motion.
The world went red before his eyes with agonizing pain, and he sensed his entire right arm's function had taken a fatal hit. But ignoring all of that damage, he hurled the extracted blade at Elsa.

It was a throw with no restraint held back, but the reckless form of someone with no training whatsoever.
That it flew straight at Elsa was practically a miracle. The speed and weight were considerable. But before a murderer possessing skill beyond human understanding, it had no effect whatsoever.

"I admire the spirit, but something like this won't—"

"I'll wring you dry! Shamak——!!"

"——!?"

Against Elsa, who had readied herself to intercept, Subaru's throat shook out a third Shamak.
Scraping together the mana in his body that had nearly run dry after the first two casts, he expelled magic from his untrained Gate along with his own life force.
His eyes went bloodshot, blood dripped from his nose. And the scream of his soul bore fruit.

Darkness spread across the center of the corridor, covering the space between Subaru and Elsa. Into it dove furiously the blade Subaru had thrown. Plunging into the incomprehensible dark, passing through its interior, it shot out toward Elsa—and its trajectory was invisible to her.

"Hit——!"

"A little surprising. But if it can't be deflected, all I need do is move off the line of fire."

Leaving those words behind, Elsa lowered her body where she stood and spun as if sliding away.
The movement to escape the blade's trajectory was made possible by her judgment that Subaru's Shamak hadn't actually reached her.
The blade, bursting out from the jet-black curtain, flew past into the far end of the corridor without piercing its owner's body. And so it seemed Subaru's attack would end in vain—but,

"Bea-ko!!"

"You'd call out like that on reflex, wouldn't you——!"

If Subaru had been alone here, the attack would have ended in failure.
But Elsa had two enemies in this place—and the other girl had completed her next incantation in the brief span of time Subaru had bought.

"I'll show you. The real thing—true shadow magic."

"What—"

Was it Subaru or Elsa who nearly said "are you going to do"?
Even that became uncertain in the face of Beatrice's next action.
Bringing her small hands together before her chest, glaring at Elsa, Beatrice's lips trembled. It was but a single word, and yet it repainted the world.

"—Ul Shamak."

—Real "darkness," of an order utterly different from the imitation shadow magic Subaru cast, enveloped the mansion.

When he came to, Subaru was in darkness.

"——?"

No—whether he had "come to" at all, even that his consciousness could not clearly determine.
He didn't know where he was, whether he was standing or sitting, anything.
Up and down, left and right, front and back—all ambiguous. Was he breathing in, breathing out, was blood circulating, was his heart still beating, was he alive, was he dead.
He couldn't tell anything. He couldn't answer anything. With Subaru's own Shamak, he could at least sense the feel transmitted from the soles of his feet, and at least the changes within his own body. Understanding of external interference became impossible, but in exchange his inward eye had grown that much sharper.

But within this darkness, not even that remained.
As though he himself had dissolved into the shadow, he didn't even know where he was.
He didn't know whether he still retained the shape of a person. He didn't know how to move his hands, so he had no way to touch his body to confirm it. Even wanting to confirm where he was, he didn't know how to move his feet, so he couldn't start walking. What even was walking. What even was confirming.
—To begin with, who on earth was he?

The boundary between himself and others blurred.
The boundary between himself and the world blurred.

The power to think dissolved. Disappeared. Vanished.
Like this, like this, like this——.

It ends.
En.
E.


※※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※


"...Are you ever going to wake up?"

Slapped across the cheek with a dry crack, Subaru's consciousness was jolted back.
He blinked, and at the sudden dazzling return of his vision, a groaning sound escaped him. Hearing it, the pain that had roused Subaru struck his cheek once more. From both sides, twice.

"H-hey, don't hit me twice in a row!"

"I only meant to make sure you were fully conscious, I suppose. The second one just came out reflexively, for some reason or other, I suppose."

Glaring at Beatrice's smug, composed face as she mouthed off at him, Subaru belatedly realized he'd been lying flat on the corridor floor.
He raised himself up and confirmed that his body was intact, neither more nor less than it should be. A sharp pain from his right shoulder. When he glanced at it without thinking, a fresh wound, oozing blood steadily, asserted itself.

"Ah, damn it hurts... Hey, can't you Shamak just the pain away or something?"

"It's not that I can't, but even if I did, all it would do is make you forget the wound exists—it wouldn't make the wound itself go away, I suppose. If you go wandering around without treating it, you'll simply bleed to death like anyone else, I suppose."

Beatrice laid out an unappealing vision of the future. Pressing his palm to the wound to stanch it, Subaru narrowed his eyes as though suddenly remembering something and said, "More importantly..."

"What happened to Elsa? If you're taking this so leisurely, does that mean she retreated?"

"What are you even talking about, I suppose?"

"What do you mean 'what am I talking about.' If she's still around, this isn't the time to be doing this. Sure, that last Shamak was on a totally different level from mine, but just that alone couldn't have—"

"Even for a blind fool, this is pitiable, I suppose."

At Beatrice's exasperated words, Subaru's brows knit in irritation.
Beatrice seemed to be thinking rather optimistically, but that was because she didn't yet know Elsa's tenacity and terror. If she knew the abnormality of that murderer, she would understand Subaru's wariness.

Beatrice let out a sigh, this time unmistakably one of exasperation, at Subaru wearing his inner thoughts plainly on his face.
Subaru, not understanding the meaning of her reaction. Before him, Beatrice slowly stepped aside, and,

"If you don't understand, why not simply look with your own eyes and see clearly, I suppose."

"—ugh, oh"

As Beatrice stepped aside, the sight her body had been blocking until then came into Subaru's view. And confronted with it, Subaru let out an involuntary groan.

"This 'terrifying opponent'—what of her, exactly?"

He couldn't answer Beatrice's triumphant voice.

Behind her—on the corridor wall—Elsa's corpse hung crucified, her four limbs pierced through with purple stakes, and a stake driven through the center of her chest as if to finish off a vampire.
A corpse—yes, without doubt, it had to be a corpse.

"She's... dead, right?"

"With a hole blown clean through her chest, and covered in this many wounds—if she were still alive after all that, then she wouldn't be human anymore. She'd be something else entirely, I suppose."

Shaking his head, dragging along his lethargy, Subaru got to his feet.
The instant he stood, a tremendous wave of dizziness struck him, and his upper body swayed. Gently, a hand extended from the side to support him.

"S-sorry..."

"It's nothing..."

Beatrice turned her face away, not looking at him. Leaning some of his weight onto her palm, Subaru dragged his feet toward Elsa.
Her head hanging limply down, one of her long braids severed midway by a stake. Her limbs pierced through elbow and knee and crucified—the painfulness of it made him want to look away from the sheer ferocity of the act. Still, he closed the distance until he was close enough to feel her breath, to confirm whether the faceless murderer truly wasn't breathing.

No breath. He reached out and touched the unmoving body. Still warm, but there was no response peculiar to a living thing. He touched her neck—no pulse either.
Above all, even with Subaru behaving this defenselessly, she made no surprise attack.

"S-she's really dead..."

"That's what Ram's—that's what I've been telling you all this time, I suppose."

"Knowing this woman's true strength the way I do, it's not something I can nod along to that easily. ...I can't believe you actually pulled it off..."

Before Elsa's corpse, Subaru found himself dazed by a victory that brought no sense of achievement.
He'd thought her someone he'd eventually have to defeat, but that Beatrice alone would strike her down this easily was beyond his expectations. He'd assumed that without Garfiel, no picture of victory was even visible.

"Even Puck and Emilia together couldn't finish her off, this woman."

"...If Big Brother had been serious, an opponent like this wouldn't have been any match for him at all, I suppose. As for Betty, at full strength as she is now, she can't very well lose to a mere human either, I suppose."

Puck's true seriousness—that likely meant that state, that giant, lion-like form. Certainly, in that state where his mere presence froze the world, even Elsa herself surely couldn't have contended with him. So Beatrice, too, was a spirit possessing power on that order.
Four hundred years—the years of life she'd lived were on a wholly different scale.

"That's right. Rem!"

Having finished confirming Elsa's death, Subaru spun around as if struck. The room was still in the terrible state of Frederica's body leaning against the door.
Somehow moving her stiffened body aside from the door, Subaru put his hand on the door, drenched as it was in blood. He drew a breath, steeled himself, and looked inside.
And then,

"—Rem"

There was no girl's voice to answer his call.
But lying on the bed, continuing to sleep with steady, even breathing, Rem showed not the faintest trace of the aftermath of the horror that had occurred outside.

Elsa had not set foot inside this room.
That, in turn, was proof that Frederica's desperate defense of the room in front of the door had outmatched the murderer's foulness.

"...I'm truly sorry for doubting you, Frederica."

Stroking the forehead of the beloved sleeping girl with his hand, Subaru apologized once more to the woman laid out in the corridor.
Her soul was no longer anywhere in this world. So these words of Subaru's now would never, ever reach her.

"So, what do we do from here, I suppose?"

"I can't leave Rem here like this. With Frederica and Petra gone... so I'm going to entrust her to the people of Arlam Village and have them look after her."

"You should do that, I suppose. That's likely what this girl would want too, I suppose."

"If I could just take care of Rem, I would, in a heartbeat, if circumstances allowed. But that's not how it's going to go. I... have to take you to the 'Sanctuary.'"

Still touching Rem, Subaru turned around to face Beatrice, who stood watching from the doorway. The girl gave a small snort through her nose.

"Saying such selfish things, I suppose. To begin with, our conversation earlier was only interrupted by an intruder—the matter is still ongoing, I suppose."

"I know that. So that's my answer to it. —I'm not going to kill you, not ever. And I'm going to drag you out of this mansion and take you to the 'Sanctuary.' That's decided."

"You're being far too self-centered, I suppose. Ignoring Betty's own say in the matter, while pushing through your own opinion regardless. —With what face, with what audacity, can you spout such nonsense, I suppose?"

"If what you're saying is really your genuine feelings, I might consider it."

"—What do you mean by that, I suppose?"

Beatrice's voice dropped low, hurling an intimidation close to a threat at him. Subaru, feeling his skin prickle, gave a small shake of his head.

"I'm not entirely clear on the details myself. It's just that I think there's still something missing before I can drag out the real, root part of you."

"—Is that... distasteful to you, I suppose?"

"It's because your relationship with the 'Sanctuary' is still left hanging, even now. The relationship between you and that experimental ground Echidna made... Honestly, I don't get a very good feeling about it."

"Do not go prying, I suppose."

"No. ...If not me, then who's going to force their way into your business? Seeing as you've been shut up in a place like that all this while."

As though her throat had closed up, Beatrice lost her words.
Seeing that, Subaru turned back to the bed and lifted Rem into his arms. He would carry Rem's body to Arlam Village, entrust her there, and then head with Beatrice to the "Sanctuary."
If she'd cooperate with a Door Crossing it would be quick, but he couldn't push the issue. It would likely take half a day riding Patrasche to get back.

"Even if you don't want to come to the 'Sanctuary,' I'm going to press Roswaal and Ryuzu-san and the others for answers about you. If I can, I want to go hear it straight from whoever's really at the root of it all."

When Subaru had genuinely, desperately wanted to "know," the Witch of Greed had said she would respond.
If it were now—with even more information than before, along with the hypotheses that came with it, and now with new mysteries piled on top—it felt like he might be invited to that dream castle once again.
And if that happened, it felt like he might finally be able to learn the secrets of the "Sanctuary" that everyone involved kept their mouths shut about, and the inner circumstances of those involved.

"It's only a difference of sooner or later. Though I get why you wouldn't want to hasten it yourself, with your own hands."

"How far do you intend to keep making a fool of people like this...!"

"Making a fool? I didn't mean it that—"

"Casually intruding into a place people don't want you poking around in, thoroughly trampling and stirring it up, and then having the nerve to say something like that. Enough making a fool of people, I suppose. And you, casually leaving the two who died behind so easily too."

"——ugh"

At the end of Beatrice's words, Subaru grimaced, feeling a sharp stab of pain.
Seeing that, Beatrice too seemed to think she'd gone too far, and hesitation showed on her face for just a moment. But it was quickly hidden again beneath a mask of cold composure.

"Petra and Frederica... once I've entrusted Rem to the village, I'll properly mourn them. I can't just stay silent about Petra either."

Realizing he was making excuses that sounded like excuses, Subaru started walking, trying not to let Beatrice see his face.
Beatrice's words had pierced sharply into his chest.

Even as he was struck with shock over the "deaths" of Petra and Frederica, Subaru had already steeled himself to redo this world over again. Even though driving off Elsa was one goal accomplished, the sacrifice paid for it was too great. This world had too much that was painful to bear continuing with.
Having told Beatrice "don't die," there was indeed a part of him that felt the weight of "with what face" in her words. You're not allowed, but I am—it was self-centered logic taken to its very extreme.

"Whether or not I go to the 'Sanctuary' is a separate matter, but in any case, I need to deal with the mansion's problems first. Our talk can come after that."

Passing beside Beatrice standing in the doorway, he went out into the corridor. Beatrice said nothing, but seemed to be following along after him in silence.
Since suicide wasn't permitted to her, Beatrice had no way to reach an "end" except by another's hand. Since she couldn't seek it out and act on it herself, whatever she declared her intentions to be, she had no choice but to remain within Subaru's reach.
Knowing that, and still behaving this way—Subaru felt a self-mocking bitterness rise in him at what a rotten man he'd become, enough that there was genuine guilt in it.

"—Oh?"

Just then, Subaru, disgusted with the filthiness of his own thoughts, let out a sudden sound.
The cause was a light impact. From behind, he felt a sensation like being shoved with a hand, and Subaru, still carrying Rem, stumbled forward involuntarily.
Catching his footing and turning around, he found it was Beatrice behind him who had done it. Wondering if this was retaliation for his earlier remark, Subaru knit his brow and was about to voice a complaint—

"—ah"

He noticed a thin, girlish voice, and a dull gleam protruding from her chest.

"—eh?"

A blade that had entered from her back protruded from her chest, and slowly, that wound began to travel downward—from the upper chest toward the lower abdomen.
Following the blade's movement, Beatrice's small body trembled.
Subaru could only watch it, dumbstruck.

"...With this"

Beatrice's lips murmured something, softly, suddenly.
Before Subaru, frozen in place at the sight before him, Beatrice raised her face and looked at him.
That expression, those eyes, spoke of the sheer immensity of her emotion,

"At last..."

"Wait...!"

"——uh"

Even Subaru himself didn't know what he'd meant to say.
But before that unknown emotion could take shape, Beatrice's breath escaped in a rasp.
And with that sound as its last, Beatrice's body became a gathering of pale light, and in the space of a blink scattered into particles of yellow light.

Her small body, her cream-colored ringlets, her face—infuriating yet brimming with charm—her lavish, unwieldy-looking dress that suited her so well—all of it, everything, vanished into nothing—.

"—Ah, what a shame. This was my first time cutting open a spirit's belly, and she goes and disappears."

At the spot where the vanished Beatrice had been standing—half a step behind it—stood a woman holding the weapon that had run Beatrice through.
Subaru knew the woman's identity the instant he heard her call him "prey" and heard her voice. He knew it instantly, and yet he couldn't accept it instantly, because his brain refused to comprehend something that shouldn't be possible. But his mind, reeling from the shock, righted itself within seconds, and Subaru clenched his back teeth.
With a cracking sound, his teeth split. Tasting blood, he glared straight ahead and screamed.

"—Elsaaaaaaa!!"

"There's nothing more you can do now, is there?"

The moment he screamed, the flat of the kukri knife struck him full across the side of the head with tremendous force.
The hard impact split his temple open, and unable to resist the force, Subaru's body was sent slamming into the wall. Not throwing Rem from his arms was the only resistance Subaru managed.
Blood poured out in a flood from his split scalp, and with an impact severe enough to turn his vision black, no fighting spirit transmitted to his limbs. Even so, Subaru caught Elsa in his flickering vision alone, and spat hatred at her as she deftly juggled the kukri knife between both hands.

"How... how the hell are you alive. I confirmed you were dead, damn it...!"

"Yes, that's right. I was dead. If I'd been left as I was and burned to ash, I wouldn't be here like this. But..."

Subaru shuddered at Elsa's breezy delivery.
The crucified Elsa had unmistakably stopped all vital functions. She had died. There was no mistaking that. So then, what was the Elsa standing before him now? Or was he seeing some nightmare where Elsa, too, like Ryuzu, possessed duplicate bodies?

But the Elsa who'd struck Subaru down still had blood dripping from her limbs even now, and the torn, pierced part of her chest was reinforced with cloth cut from her now-useless cloak.
Looking at her, still bearing such vivid traces of the battle, there was no doubt she was one and the same person as before. The only question was of life and death.

"Don't tell me you're... some kind of undying monster or something..."

"That, now, truly would be absurd. I'm simply a bit more stubbornly alive than most people. More to the point, that girl went to quite an extreme, didn't she. Having my body destroyed to this extent—even I can count the times that's happened on one hand."

"...What a coincidence. I've only died from being beaten up as badly as you're doing to me now, a few times myself, too."

Subaru's words, meant with utter sincerity, were sarcasm, not a joke. But Elsa seemed to take it as a joke, deepening her smile and spinning lightly in place. Pressing a finger sadly to her braid, severed halfway through, Elsa quietly looked down at Subaru and said,

"That girl there hasn't been told, has she."

"...Then how about you just keep it that way and let me off the hook?"

Understanding full well it was a remark not to be let slide, Subaru made the proposal to Elsa anyway. He didn't expect it to be accepted, but his limbs needed a little more time before they'd start obeying him again. A stupid conversation—he needed to stall for time.

"This was unplanned for me as well, so I don't particularly mind that... The spirit girl, and the tall maid. And the little maid child makes an addition."

Three targets. Beatrice, Frederica, and Petra as an addition—did that mean?
Even as his mind burned white-hot, he strained his ears at the part he couldn't afford to miss. That Rem wasn't among the targets was likely because Elsa's employer had forgotten Rem's existence entirely. He'd assumed that was true of Frederica too, but with her death, that deduction was back to square one.

"By the way, you were lying too, weren't you."

"Lying?"

"About Frederica. —Back in the forbidden archive, you talked like you'd only killed Petra, but that's not what happened, is it."

With a glance he indicated Frederica laid out at the end of the corridor, and Elsa, following his gaze, gave an "ah" of understanding and nodded. Then she looked at Subaru and murmured, just once, quietly,

"An ending lacking in beauty."

That was all she let slip.
There was no way to understand the aesthetic sense of a murderer. What was that dull, dismissive way of speaking about it, after having taken a life? Rage welled up, but it too dissipated before Elsa, who had readied the kukri knife once more in her palm.
He wanted to land at least one blow in return, but his body hadn't recovered enough to even attempt a counterattack against Elsa.
To fall here, before Elsa's murderous blade, as things stood—it was half a foregone conclusion.

—Is this as far as it goes, this time?

Even as he sensed "death" closing in before him, what surfaced in Subaru's mind was the wealth of information gained this loop, and the new, baffling mysteries. And his exchange with Beatrice, and the expression on Beatrice's face he'd seen last.

Why, after repeating "I want to die," "kill me," had she, in the end, shoved Subaru away? What meaning was there in that action—being the first to notice Elsa's survival, and pushing Subaru aside? He didn't want to be so dull as to fail to understand that.

"An eye I don't care for."

"Ah? ——gubh!?"

The words were flung out, and immediately afterward his face was struck again by the flat of the blade.
His left cheekbone shattered, and several broken teeth fell to the floor. As he began to crumple, another blow came from the opposite side. Searing pain shot through his right eye socket, and a single slash of the blade sliced his left ear off at an angle.
After that, Elsa alternated between edge and flat, carving into Subaru's body, breaking it, tormenting it. Denied the "death" he'd thought would come quickly, given only pain, over and over again, Subaru crawled on the ground, continuing to leak blood and groans.

"Struggling to the very last moment of life. Without that, what meaning is there in being alive?"

"...I don't want you lecturing me about the meaning of life and death."

One blow. His forehead split open from directly above, and he collapsed, feeling the illusory sensation of the contents of his skull spilling out.
His consciousness faded under the hard impact, and Subaru could feel his body being dragged, further and further, into a colder and colder world.

He was going to die like this.
Even if it were only a loss of consciousness, he knew all too well what it would mean to lose consciousness before the "Bowel Hunter."
This was it. This time, this was as far as it went.

Next time, he wouldn't fail. Next time, for certain.
Because he couldn't forget that expression he'd seen last—he would, without fail.

"—Beatrice"

That the girl who'd said "kill me," had, in her very last moment, shown tears in her eyes.
With that sight burned into him, Subaru's consciousness slowly, was swallowed by darkness, and vanished.
