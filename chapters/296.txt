At first, what drew the Witchbeast Guiltirau's attention was a small sound.

"—"

A light, hard tapping sound rang out again and again, striking the floor, and Guiltirau lifted its snout.

The Silent King of the Forest—that was what Guiltirau was sometimes called in certain regions—unlike other Witchbeasts, disliked making needless roars or noise.
Despite its huge, grotesque frame, it bounded lightly across wastelands, approaching prey soundlessly to gouge out a vital point in a single strike and finish it off. It was in that kind of ambush, assassination-like hunting that it excelled the most.

So even though these were orders from 'Master', being made to stay in one spot lying in ambush like this was, to Guiltirau, nothing but a foolish waste of its skills.
Of course, it hadn't the slightest intention of being so ungrateful as to defy the orders of 'Master'.
After all, it was only because its 'horn' had been broken that Guiltirau had been able to escape its curse in the first place.

"—"

Turning its nose about, Guiltirau probed for the direction of the sound while turning over the orders of 'Master' in its mind.
Take up position before this door and hunt down any hostile who approached—that was the duty Guiltirau had been given, and the wish of 'Master'.

That tap, tap, continuing on so carelessly, was clearly footsteps.
Like 'Master', many creatures that walked on two legs made this kind of footstep sound as they walked. Some among the strong made no footstep sound at all, but whoever owned these footsteps was not among them.
Defenseless, careless, thoughtless, unreserved—not a shred of grace to it.
To Guiltirau, this was prey so weak it was practically an annoyance even to tear apart and eat.

"—"

Without a sound, Guiltirau slid away from its post before the door.
The footsteps were coming from the direction of the west staircase—the same direction from which, just moments before, sounds like repeated clashes of combat had been echoing.

Guiltirau knew 'Master' had brought many other Witchbeasts besides itself. Most of the ones weaker in strength and smaller in size had surrounded the building, and 'Master', leaving it in charge of defending the door, had gone off to hunt prey riding the back of one particularly large, sluggish Witchbeast.
There were plenty of things Guiltirau resented about the choice to bring that bulky-but-that's-it Witchbeast along on the hunt while leaving it behind as backup. Even so, at the very least, if the enemy who appeared here proved to be strong, the reason it had been brought along at all, and its own pride, would be vindicated.
That was precisely why, no matter what Witchbeast might be under attack, Guiltirau had committed no such folly as leaving its post to join in, not until an enemy reached its own station.

A weakling who couldn't even make it as far as its post wasn't worth fighting.
A weakling who'd be cut down by Witchbeasts weaker than itself wasn't worth hunting either.
But this prey had overcome the interference of the other Witchbeasts and made it all the way here. When it sensed that presence, Guiltirau had even felt a secret thrill of excitement stir within it.

—And this was the long-awaited result?

It didn't even know enough to hide its footsteps, and the fighting spirit it gave off was weak and brittle.
A fleeting, inferior existence that would scatter to nothing with a single swipe of claw or fang.

"—"

The emotion welling up was anger. Nothing but anger.
Tear the prey apart with these fangs, not let a single scrap of blood or flesh settle in its stomach, scatter it all instead.
There was no other way left to sublimate this burning feeling, so like humiliation.

Chasing after the footsteps, Guiltirau moved without casting a shadow that wavered in the moonlight. The sight of its huge body gliding along in total silence would have looked, to any onlooker, exactly like something out of a nightmare.
The jet-black assassin closed in near the carefree footsteps, and just as its prey seemed to stop right after rounding a corner, it slashed it clean in two from behind with its claws.

"—!"

Without so much as disturbing its breath, Guiltirau stretched its neck and lunged at its prey's back—but—

"—?"

Having closed the distance to within claw's reach, where the presence of prey had just been—there was no sign of it anywhere.
Losing the spot to bring down its raised paw, Guiltirau stopped in its tracks at that instant's flash of wrongness. Trembling the tip of its nose, it swung its head around.
Wondering where that foolish, brittle, weak prey had gotten to.

"—!"

Once again, the sound of footsteps struck Guiltirau's ears.
Lowering its head to look toward the sound, it seemed to have come echoing up from a floor below. The footsteps of prey descending the stepped stairs and fleeing at a run to the lower floor.
It seemed the prey had noticed its presence and evaded slightly by picking up its pace, but once Guiltirau realized that, it had no intention of letting its prey escape.

Raising its head, it glanced back at the door it had been ordered by 'Master' to guard.
It would mean leaving its post, but this prey surely had to be the very prey 'Master' had ordered it to deal with. Taking its head would be as good as fulfilling the command of 'Master'.

Judging so, Guiltirau chased after the prey fleeing so unbecomingly.
To begin with, the moment it had turned its back despite being well within attack range, it had all but declared it had no means to resist.
For Guiltirau, back when it had run wild across field and mountain, reigning as king of the forest, hunting down prey that fled about like this for its own amusement had been an everyday occurrence.

Only prey with truly great strength was worth taking into its own body.
Prey that turned its back, its resisting fangs already drawn, existed only to keep its own claws and fangs from forgetting the feel of blood and flesh—and that, too, was something they ought to learn.

It descended the stairs, chasing the footsteps.
Kicking off the landing wall, sending its huge frame dancing through the air down to the floor below. Chasing the prey fleeing to the second floor, then further to the first, Guiltirau touched down at the very bottom level of the building.

Far off, from some distant part of the building, the presence of combat still reached it.
The scent of 'Master', and the reeking stench of that infuriating Master's sluggish Witchbeast it had brought along. And beyond that, the almost tangible scent of blood and steel, the aroma of the strong.

"—"

If it could, Guiltirau too wanted to make its way there and take part in that battle.
To swing its claws and fangs as it pleased before 'Master', tear the body of a strong opponent apart and sink it in a sea of blood, and drink in the taste of that victory—how it wished it could.
But that wasn't what was wanted of it right now. An order had been handed down.

—If it hunted down this prey quickly enough and caught up in time, would it be allowed to join in after all?

"—!"

Feeling its fangs ache all the more, Guiltirau's body shivered.
Following the footsteps as they sounded again, it heard the sound of a door closing, and saw, at the far end of a corridor lost in the dark of night, a door that had just been shut.
Swift-footed and silent, it stood before the door, and Guiltirau used its long tail to skillfully pry it open.

This was hardly the first time it had invaded the dwellings of two-legged creatures like this and bared its fangs there.
Understanding the structure of what was called a "door," Guiltirau slithered its huge frame into the room. It had braced itself for the prey to be lying in wait, but no sign of the prey could be seen inside the room, and Guiltirau found itself let down yet again.
But this time, the disappointment didn't last long.

"—"

Turning its head, Guiltirau's gaze fell on a corner of the room—a wardrobe.
From the gap between the wardrobe's twin doors, a bit of the fabric the prey wore was poking out, caught in the door. It must have dived in hastily and caught its clothing in the process. The prey's shallowness, thinking itself fully hidden from Guiltirau without even noticing that, was almost comical.

Guiltirau silenced its footsteps and crept toward the wardrobe.
Raising its tail and sharpening its tip, it hesitated not a moment longer.

"—!"

Like the tip of a spear, the loosed point of its tail pierced clean through the wardrobe's doors with ease.
Round holes as if punched by an awl—still, coin-sized holes appeared one after another across the wardrobe, skewering through the body of the pitiful prey hidden inside.

Once the number of holes passed twenty, Guiltirau stopped its tail's assault.
Then, stretching out a foreleg, it pulled the wardrobe's door down to take in the pitiful sight of what was surely a dead prey by now. The door, riddled with holes, gave way easily, and the prey inside was—

"—Rrgh!?"

The very instant it tried to confirm the prey's corpse, Guiltirau reeled back from a shock that lanced straight through its nasal passages.
A savagely pungent stench tore through its sense of smell, a sensation sharp enough to draw a pained groan. Snapping its gaze back at once, it saw, inside the opened wardrobe, a clear glass bottle rigged there had shattered, spilling out a colorless liquid.
That was the source of the reeking smell. And what's more, there was no sign of the prey inside the wardrobe at all.
The poking-out fabric had just been clothing left sticking out there on purpose.

"—!"

Once again, the sound of footsteps echoed from the corridor outside the room, and Guiltirau spun around.
Its nose was useless now, but its ears and eyes were still fine. It saw a shadow dash down the hallway, and though it writhed with humiliation at having its sense of smell sealed off, Guiltirau chased after the shadow anyway.

Never in its entire life had Guiltirau experienced being made such a fool of.
Guiltirau, which had until now overwhelmed every enemy it had ever faced, had never once encountered such a wretched creature—one that neither confronted it head-on fair and square, nor fled about helplessly to fall easy prey to its fangs, but instead clung to life so shamelessly, struggling on and on.
It would kill it, without fail. Finish it off. Chew it up, scatter it across the earth, and trample it underfoot.

"—"

No longer even remembering to silence its steps, Guiltirau sent the door of the room the footsteps had fled into flying with the force of its huge body.
The double doors were blown off effortlessly, and what welcomed Guiltirau's massive frame was a room far, far more spacious than any before it.

A huge table sat in the middle of the room, and at its farthest end stood a fireplace.
On the table draped in a white cloth stood a row of lit candlesticks, their flames casting an eerie glow across a room that had until then been lit only by moonlight.

"—"

Fire's presence was a hateful thing to Guiltirau.
While it couldn't extinguish that round white orb of fire that hung in the daytime sky, its aversion to flame existing this close at hand ran strong. After all, the forest Guiltirau had once lived in had been consumed by fire, its safe haven lost. And it was in that very moment, with its horn broken, that it had come to serve 'Master'—so for Guiltirau, fire was something that called to mind two contradictory memories at once: liberation, and disgrace.

"—"

There were no footsteps. But Guiltirau didn't miss the sound of something stirring.
On the wall opposite the door it had entered through, in this spacious room, was another door. From beyond that door, which likely didn't lead into much depth of space, it could sense a presence.

Guiltirau snorted, but its sense of smell still hadn't returned.
It couldn't catch the scent of the prey's fear, of it wetting itself. It was a shame that, in this state, even tearing the prey apart wouldn't let it properly savor the smell of blood or the taste as it drank it in.
That sensation, at least, could be put off for later, so long as it managed to tear the prey apart.
For now, at any rate, wiping away the humiliation burning in its chest, wringing a death scream from the very prey that had made such a fool of it—that alone was Guiltirau's only salvation.

"—"

Guiltirau stepped forward with its massive frame, heading straight for the room.
Then it blew the room's door apart with its tail, sharply extended. Kicking down the door, now riddled with holes just like the wardrobe before, with a foreleg, Guiltirau sucked in a breath and plunged straight into the room.

"—OOOOH!!"

It burst into the room, letting loose a roar.
Threatening the prey with its bellow, it would make the flesh of the cowering weakling pay in fang and claw, in blood and meat.
Swinging its tail, destruction ran rampant through the room, and clouds of dust billowed up from the sacks and boxes torn open in the cabinets. Its slamming forelegs shattered the floor, ripped through the cloth spread across the ground, and from there too white clouds of dust burst upward—no, clouds thick enough to blot out its whole field of vision kept gushing out, one after another.

"—!?"

Its vision turned white, and the instant it drew breath, Guiltirau's airway was assaulted, sending it into a fit of coughing. Some enormous quantity of powder was swirling through the air.
Powder thick enough to strip away its sight, even to steal the very breath it needed to roar.

"It's hooked!"

Someone's voice, someone's, could be heard.
And that voice was coming not from this room, but from the room just before it.

"Eat this—the very essence of science! A dust explosion!!"

With a clatter, something was thrown into the white, smoke-hazed room.
The brightly flickering thing was one of the candlesticks that had been lined up on the table in the room before.
The candlestick struck the wall, its wavering flame dropping to the floor and, for an instant, flaring larger.

"—"

"Uh, huh...?"

But that was all.
The candlestick lay there on the floor, showing no further reaction. The voice that had thrown it let out a sound as if something had gone wrong somewhere, and it was clear whoever it was had frozen in place outside the room.

"—!"

By instinct, Guiltirau sensed this as a once-in-a-lifetime chance.
Something had gone wrong for its opponent. And had that unexpected turn not happened, danger would have been closing in on Guiltirau itself.
Understanding that, Guiltirau spun around and chose to break free of the small room.

Once out in the spacious room, in a space where it could swing its massive limbs and tail to its heart's content, it wouldn't matter what the prey was scheming. It would crush them under an overwhelming difference in strength and wrench out victory.
Nothing more than that was needed anymore—

"Ugh, I told you, didn't I—forget the confusing stuff!"

"Doing it the normal way is faster!"

Just as it tried to burst out, Guiltirau heard two more voices belonging to prey.
A low voice and a high one. The instant it realized these were the voices of prey of different sexes, Guiltirau sensed a cabinet behind it toppling down onto it.

A cord stretching from the entrance had been tied to the cabinet's leg.
Yanked hard from outside, the cabinet toppled and came down on Guiltirau's back. But the cabinet was, at most, only big enough to cover the rear of Guiltirau's massive body.
It struck with a bit of momentum behind it, but the damage to Guiltirau was next to nothing.

Taking the impact in stride, Guiltirau sliced through the cord with its claws.
This time it truly tried to leap out of the room—

"—?"

A cabinet swung open, and liquid came pouring out from it, splashing generously across half its body.
A slick sensation, unlike water. Doused in the slightly yellow-tinged liquid, Guiltirau felt displeasure at its own proud black fur growing wet.
But that displeasure of Guiltirau's vanished immediately.

"—!?"

"Courtesy of Otto Suwen's own private stock—commercial-grade oil, every last coin I sank into it—have it all!"

The prey's voice came from outside the room.
But by then, Guiltirau had no room left to spare for that weak prey's voice.

—The oil doused over its whole body had caught the candlestick's flame, and that accursed fire was now burning it whole.

"—!!"

The beast king, who had come down from the wilds and clung to its empty throne of the forest to the very end, was swallowed by flames the same color as the humiliation searing through it, never once understanding what had truly defeated it.



*Guiltirau: gone before he even got going.*
