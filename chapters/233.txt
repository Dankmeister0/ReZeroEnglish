Arc 4 Chapter 67: "Warlock"

The scene unfolding before him was, to Subaru, something answerable only with incomprehension.

Ram sunk in a pool of blood, and Garfiel dead, robbed of everything above his neck. Beside the two overlapping corpses, Roswaal — who had done this with his bare hands — brushed off the hem of his robe.

Even having witnessed such tremendous physical technique firsthand, Subaru could not readily believe that this was Roswaal's doing.

He had heard that Roswaal L. Mathers, court mage representing the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica, wielder of magic at the highest tier, possessed combat strength on par with a strategic weapon.

He had heard it. Precisely because he had heard it, he had never imagined that, in a situation where magic couldn't be used, Roswaal would ever unleash this much destructive power.

"That mages can't fight hand-to-hand is entirely a preconception, isn't it-t. Naturally, anyone who means to face me with hostile intent would think so. ...As for what's become of those hidebound enough to think it, well — you can see for yourself-elf."

At the response that had captured Subaru's silent feelings perfectly, he couldn't help but catch his breath.

Roswaal traced a finger along the blood that had splashed faintly onto his face, overwriting his blue eyeliner with crimson, and smiled. — Bewitching, in the sense that one might be swallowed whole by it.

"W-why..."

"Hmm?"

"Why did you kill them both? Ram, and — Garfiel too, why did you have to kill Garfiel, there was no need to—!"

"Garfiel would have been in the way of the two of us talking-ing. As for Ram, I do think it a regrettable thing. But I'm not strong enough to face Garfiel head-on and win. That I managed to kill him just now was only because I caught him off guard."

Was that "opening" the act of running Garfiel through along with Ram?

At Roswaal's breezy recitation of his reasons for killing them both, Subaru's fury spent itself and, contrarily, cold clarity returned. A ridiculous answer to a ridiculous situation. If Roswaal meant to toy with him, flying into a rage would only be playing into his hand.

"................"

"Hmm-m, how unexpected-ed. I rather thought you'd be furious, hearing that."

"It did a full spin and came back around backwards. ...It's not that I'm not angry. Of course I am. Of course I am."

"I wonder-er. It's a desirable attitude, as far as that goes, but the Natsuki Subaru I knew ought to be howling recklessly, consumed with rage, in a scene like this. Isn't that so, Natsuki Subaru-kun?"

Closing one eye, that yellow gaze caught Subaru again. He'd noticed it before — Roswaal had a habit of looking at people with just one eye, and it was always the glinting yellow one. And whenever he found himself reflected in that gleaming yellow, Subaru felt a deeply unsettling sensation.

"I know I've been an idiot, but that doesn't mean I never grow. I get that this isn't a scene where flying into a rage solves anything, I—"

"No, that's not it, that's not it at all, Subaru-kun. Subaru-kun. Natsuki Subaru-ku-un."

Swaying his indigo hair, smoothing it back with his left hand — the one not stained with blood — Roswaal called Subaru's name provocatively. Subaru sensed something unfathomable in his madness, and yet he did not step back. He did not step back — rather, he stepped forward, glaring at the clown's face.

"What are you trying to say."

"What am I trying to say — if you ask, then I suppose what I want to say to you is this. — Congratulations. Welcome. I've been waiting. I've been waiting for you to stand there."

A chill ran down Subaru's spine, as if wet fingertips had traced along it.

Before him, Roswaal, exactly as his words suggested, gazed at Subaru with unmistakable delight. At that attitude, that delight, Subaru felt an inexplicable revulsion. It wasn't irony, wasn't sarcasm of any kind — Roswaal genuinely felt joy toward Subaru. The problem was that Subaru could grasp neither the meaning of his words nor the meaning of that joy.

"For me to stand here... you were waiting?"

"Please, don't insult me with so trite a misunderstanding as thinking I mean this spot in this room-oom. You should be able to understand that isn't what I mean. You, of all people, should be able to understand it-t."

"I'm the only one... who can understand?"

The image of puzzle pieces with no visible final picture slowly assembling themselves rose up in his mind. Gradually, gradually, hesitantly, the pieces connected, until at last a hazy form of the completed picture began to emerge.

When he let his thoughts run toward that completed shape, a sensation of *no, it can't be* pierced through Subaru.

"Do you understand, Subaru-kun? Why is it that you can watch two people die and remain, somehow, calm — why you can keep your composure instead of flying into a rage... You already know the reason, don't you, deep down-wn."

"————"

"You aren't, you see, so terribly shocked by their deaths. There's surprise, that they lost their lives. Righteous anger too, I'd imagine-ine. But you feel no grief. That's why you can't turn your fists on me here, in anger."

At Roswaal's knowing tone, Subaru opened his mouth to object, only to close it again without a word, over and over.

*What would you know!* *How could their deaths not be sad!* *How dare you kill Ram, kill Garfiel, you monster!*

Any number of shouted words came to mind. In fact, those explosive feelings had already surged up inside Subaru several times, rushing toward his throat and dying away again, over and over.

He was angry. He was surprised, too. He must be grieving, as well.

He must be — and yet, the reason Subaru couldn't refute Roswaal's words was—

"—Isn't it because you think this can still be undone-e?"

"You—!"

Subaru's heart was seized by a chill so sharp it froze his throat.

Without exaggeration, he had the illusion that his heart had been physically gripped in a fist. That was the force of the blow that struck him.

He raised his head and instinctively looked around, terrified that a black palm would appear to punish him for having committed a taboo. It was the first penalty since the one time he'd denied the existence of the "Witch of Envy." What horror the shadow would come wrapped in this time, he couldn't imagine — just thinking of it pressed down on him with a pain like his heart being wrung and twisted.

But—

"...it's not... coming."

"I don't know what you're wary of, but... ah, perhaps that has to do with your Contract-t. I see-ee. That would explain the unnaturalness of your words and behavior up until now."

"Explain... no, before that—!"

At Roswaal, hand to his chin, nodding to himself, Subaru's face went pale, his lips trembling.

Roswaal's remark just now had, without doubt, touched the very core of Subaru's secret — and to be touched there meant—

"You... you've noticed... what's happening to me...?!"

"As long as it doesn't deviate from what's written in the book, yes. — You have some means of starting over. Isn't that right-t?"

Clearly, unmistakably, Roswaal had just confessed to already knowing about "Return by Death."

Subaru caught his breath at that, and immediately realized how dangerous the present situation was. The conditions had become exactly the same as at Echidna's tea party. If he let the conversation proceed on the premise that discussing "Return by Death" with Roswaal here was fine, that tragedy — the "Sanctuary" swallowed by shadow — would repeat itself. Before even that, the Witch might come to take Subaru at any moment, even now.

He swallowed the breath he'd taken and let it back out, slow and deep, confirming that time hadn't stopped. Which meant the penalty of the Witch's hand gripping his heart hadn't triggered. For Subaru himself, that was hardly a comfort, but it did rule out the most peaceable possibility. What remained—

"—Who was it that said silence is proof of consent, I wonder-er."

As Subaru's mind spun frantically, desperate to avoid the risk, Roswaal's voice cut in, as if his patience had run thin. For him too, that last remark must have carried considerable weight. At Subaru's wordless refusal to acknowledge it, Roswaal, uncharacteristically, knit his brow in displeasure.

"We-ell, what a preposterous tale. Though perhaps it's to your credit that you didn't bother with some tedious deflection-ion."

"You—"

"Oh-ho, it's fine. I'm the one who brought it up, but I imagine it would be bad for you to voice any kind of confirmation. That's precisely why you haven't been able to confess it, up until today-ay. Though, mind you—"

He cut Subaru off with a single syllable, then trailed off meaningfully. To Subaru, biting his lip, Roswaal cast a sidelong glance, his lips curling unpleasantly.

"Perhaps you were afraid of how you'd be looked at, if you confessed it."

"————!"

"And that's understandable-ble. After all, the power to redo the world is an outrageous thing, isn't it? Interfering with time is something only the utmost pinnacle of the shadow arts could manage. Even Beatrice can barely manage to create stagnation. Reversal — that's a dream beyond dreams-eams."

At having his unintended meaning read into so deeply, and then, with his objections sealed off, having Beatrice's name thrown at him, Subaru's face stiffened in shock. Her final expression, as she scattered into mist, pierced through by Elsa's blade from behind, flashed vividly across his mind.

"—Seeing that reaction, it seems Beatrice fulfilled her role after all-ll."

"Role... you... you—that's right."

Seizing the chance that the topic had strayed from "Return by Death," Subaru dragged his half-swept-away consciousness back and bit into Roswaal's composed face.

Did this man know how desperately Beatrice had cried out how lonely she was?

"You knew about her suffering, didn't you. Bound to that mansion all this time, clinging to a single promise made as a Contract so long ago... you knew about her, worn down like that, shrunk to nothing, didn't you!"

"O-of course I knew. Beatrice and I go back a long, long way-ay. Since the moment she was born, in fact. The loneliness she carried in her heart — I've known about it all along-ong."

"Then...!"

"Please don't say something like 'why didn't you do something about it.' Whether anyone else could have done anything about that child's sorrow — surely even you understand that, don't you-u?"

Roswaal's plain reasoning struck Subaru's heart just as he was about to shout in fury. He could denounce Roswaal at the top of his lungs, make him hear Beatrice's anguished cry — he could. But doing so would mean nothing. That was the reality.

Beatrice was already dead, and no one could heal the sorrow in her heart now. Only Subaru, with his sole means of starting over, could witness her final moment again and again, any number of times. But how was he supposed to heal four hundred years of grief?

Four hundred years — even Subaru had no means of going back that far.

Facing a silent Subaru, Roswaal gave a small shake of his head. Then he said:

"How enviable, isn't it-t."

"—Enviable? You call it that?"

Subaru's voice, in response, was low. But Roswaal, not noticing, nodded, "Indeed," and continued,

"I envy her. Beatrice was able to fulfill her fervent wish and disappear. That you are standing here — doesn't that mean exactly that-t?"

"Fervent wish... you call that a fervent wish? Dying like that — like *that* — you're telling me that was her wish! Is that what you're saying?!"

"Wasn't it Beatrice herself, and no one else, who wanted it? It's not anyone else's place to say otherwise, and no one has the right to deny another's values. Neither you, nor of course I, are permitted to defile Beatrice's death."

Plausible words. Reasonable words. That it wasn't his place — that much was true. Subaru and Beatrice were, in the end, complete strangers to each other; Subaru had never been able to understand her wish, nor had he ever once thought to try and grant it for her.

But even so — even so, was that truly all right, for Beatrice?

— Then why, at the very end, had Beatrice shielded Subaru?

"Beatrice achieved her fervent wish. To me, that's simply, purely enviable. — Because it seems my own fervent wish isn't something I myself can achieve-eve."

"————"

There was something off about the way he'd said that. What exactly was off about it wasn't quite clear. But something, somewhere, was undeniably off.

"Your... fervent wish is..."

"That, I cannot say. Because it's bound by a Contract that forbids me from saying it, that's all I can tell you. Speaking even this much is the furthest concession I can make against that Contract-act. But, this much I can say."

"————"

"I am always, always, always, always doing my utmost for the fulfillment of my fervent wish. I don't believe I've done a single meaningless thing, a single thing I need be ashamed of-of."

Boldly, Roswaal declared that there was nothing in his conduct to be ashamed of. At the shock of witnessing him stand so proudly by that claim, a dark, roiling anger slowly welled up inside Subaru. It was a selfish anger, one that shelved all the feelings and thoughts Subaru himself had left behind on his way here. Selfish it may have been, but it was also something he couldn't help but feel.

"Necessary... you're saying killing Ram and Garfiel, burying the 'Sanctuary' in snow like this, all of it... all of it was necessary, is that what you're saying...?"

"Hmm, as for the former... no, that would complicate the discussion, wouldn't it-t. As for the latter, I'll answer that it is exactly so, shall I-i."

"For what?!"

Baring his teeth, flailing his arms wildly, Subaru shouted.

"What are you doing all this for?! Making it snow in the 'Sanctuary,' tormenting the villagers for no reason... what's your goal?! What's the point of any of this?! Say it! Roswaal!!"

"It's obvious, isn't it. — Emilia-sama becomes isolated."

"—Wha— huh?"

"Let me repeat it. By making it snow like this and inflicting harm on the villagers — Emilia-sama becomes isolated, and falls into an extremely unstable state of mind. Hasn't that been happening-ing?"

Roswaal's words sounded as if he'd witnessed it directly. Emilia's state inside the tomb was, no doubt, precisely as Roswaal had intended. Subaru had no intention of telling him that outright. More than that, the meaning of Roswaal's words just now was, in every sense, the most utterly incomprehensible thing he'd said yet.

"This land is one connected to the Witch, and Emilia-sama is, at this very moment, in the midst of undergoing the 'Trials of the Tomb' to release the 'Sanctuary.' At a time like that, if a localized natural disaster were to strike the 'Sanctuary'... what kind of eyes would fall on Emilia-sama?"

"You—"

"Garfiel, with his impulsive, straightforward nature, is useful at a time like this. He, of all people, would be the very first to suspect Emilia-sama, and to say so loudly. And with how loud his voice is, everyone would come to think it — that this natural disaster was Emilia-sama's doing."

Roswaal's read had been correct. Garfiel had, indeed, danced right in the palm of his hand. When Subaru had returned, the first person to snap at him had been Garfiel, utterly convinced that this bitter cold in the "Sanctuary" was Emilia's doing.

That was the soil this land — this world — cultivated: a place where, even though another "capable existence" was right there, all the malice would still be directed at Emilia. That, too, was the demon of prejudice that had tormented Emilia for so many years.

"What becomes of an isolated Emilia-sama? For all her appearance, she's truly a weak person. It would be no wonder if she wished to entrust the entirety of her trust to someone who would affirm her. And if that someone, in turn, wished to support Emilia-sama with all their heart — well, that would be ideal, wouldn't it."

"Wait... wait... wait wait wait wait wait...!"

Trying to stop Roswaal's narration, Subaru reached out an arm, calling for him to halt. He felt as though he were being told something monstrous, right now. He felt as though some outrageous truth were being spoken aloud, right now. He felt as though something he must never hear—

"You can't push away an Emilia-sama who depends on you. Naturally, because you love her. If your beloved Emilia-sama has entrusted everything to you, you cannot cast that aside."

"That's not—"

—true. That couldn't be true. In fact, even this time, Subaru had endured, had resisted drowning in Emilia clinging to him in the tomb. He had endured it, and had made his way here.

It wasn't that he'd been able to fully reject Emilia's temptation, her whispered love. But knowing that wasn't her true heart, to sink utterly, completely into it—

"Right now, you'd say no. That's simply a regrettable thing, to me. It means that, right now, you're carrying just a bit too much excess baggage."

Before an anguished, tormented Subaru, Roswaal took one quiet step forward. Hearing the wet squelch of his footstep in the pool of blood, Subaru's body flinched, unconsciously. At Roswaal's approach, his long limbs swaying, Subaru's throat clenched, and

"Are you going to... kill me?"

"Kill you — my, what a bloodthirsty notion. It would trouble me greatly if you died. No matter what, I need you to start over again-ain."

"Eh—?"

At Roswaal's words as he approached, Subaru was, for a moment, stunned. But he quickly realized the discrepancy in Roswaal's words, the gap between them in their grasp of the facts.

Roswaal knew that Subaru had a means of "starting over," but he didn't know that it was "Return by Death," triggered by "death." Which meant he intended to drive Subaru into a corner until Subaru chose to "start over" of his own will. And that might well entail more suffering than being killed instantly.

If Roswaal had no intention of harming Subaru, there was a chance.

"—Everyone, come in!!"

Raising a hand, Subaru shouted. In the instant Roswaal's brow furrowed at that voice, the door and window of the room — along with the windows and doors of the adjoining room and living room — were all smashed at once. And small shadows came leaping in with the cold wind, all at once, twenty of them — every single one a young girl with pale pink hair.

Seeing the row upon row of identical faces lined up, Roswaal closed one eye and looked at Subaru.

"I thought command of the duplicates had been transferred to Garfiel?"

"I'm marching into the place of a guy who might be the mastermind. — I've been building up my hand."

— This exchange had happened after leaving the tomb and arguing with Garfiel. Subaru had talked Garfiel out of storming straight off to confront Roswaal himself, and had gone first to the crystal room, where he'd had command of the duplicates transferred from Garfiel back to himself. On top of that, he'd stationed a Ryuzu duplicate outside the house where Roswaal was recuperating, preparing to send them in if the need arose.

As for Rem, who'd been Garfiel's hostage — he'd left her in the care of Ryuzu, the representative personality, and had her taken to the cathedral where the refugees and villagers were all gathered together. All of it had been done on the premise that Roswaal might turn out to be the perpetrator of this atrocity. — Though, of course, that Garfiel and Ram would end up killed by Roswaal had been entirely outside Subaru's expectations.

"So, surrounding me — what exactly do you intend to do-o?"

"It was a surprise that you're that strong even bare-handed, but even so, numbers beat one man. If you're saying you're as outrageous as Garfiel in Beastification form, then even with numbers it'd be rough..."

The reason Roswaal had run Garfiel through along with Ram was that he'd had no chance of winning a straight fight against Garfiel. Even so, that he was far stronger than Subaru was beyond doubt—

"—we crush you with the violence of twenty against one. Beat you down, pin you, and get you to spill everything you're still hiding."

"You do understand how important it is to honor a Contract, being under one yourself, don't you-u."

"Unfortunately, in my case, it's the type where the other side forced it on me unilaterally, and if I try to break it, the penalty kicks in automatically. The fact that it hasn't kicked in yet means I'm inside the safety line!"

Crammed into the small house, twenty people made for something close to a sardine can. The expressionless Ryuzu Meyer duplicates, following Subaru's call, leapt at Roswaal with their same vacant expressions.

Facing them empty-handed, Roswaal could, of course, only take on two at once. With his control over the outside weather working against him, unable to use magic, he had no choice but to be swept away by sheer numbers.

Subaru was certain of victory, hard-won though it might be. But—

"—Numbers do indeed beat one man, it's true."

"————"

"But challenging a mage with numbers is a rather foolish decision to make-ake."

Flame ripped sideways through the room, and every Ryuzu Meyer duplicate in its line of fire was burned away, root and all. What might be called a wall of fire, in its instant, headlong charge, incinerated every small body in its path from foot to crown in a single moment, reducing them to ash and returning them to mana.

To Subaru's eyes, it looked like nothing more than a flash of red light and heat sweeping the room for the barest instant.

"You weren't supposed to be able to use magic..."

"As long as I was controlling the weather, that is. Unfortunately, my reason for making it snow has already vanished-ed. So I stopped my hand on that a little while ago. I'm sorry for not mentioning it, though-ough."

"Wha— guh, gah."

In the instant his words failed him, Roswaal had closed the distance and seized Subaru's throat. Where such strength came from in those thin arms, Subaru had no idea — lifted effortlessly off the ground, his feet leaving the floor as he thrashed, Roswaal drew back his arm and,

"Ha—!"

—hurled him backward, through the remaining half of the window the duplicates had already half-broken, out of the building. Thrown out, Subaru's body landed on the snow outside, rolled, and came to a stop against a wall. Mud-streaked snow filled his mouth; spitting it out, he shook his head and raised his face.

Leisurely, trailing the remaining half of the duplicates behind him like an entourage, Roswaal emerged from the house. Having received no orders, the duplicates were at a loss for what to do. But Subaru, too, no longer knew what to tell them.

"Even after all that, you still won't 'start over'? O-or, perhaps you already have-ave? Come to think of it, when a 'restart' happens, what becomes of my awareness of it is a mystery. This might turn out to be rather troublesome-ome."

Coming to stand right beside the crawling Subaru, Roswaal tilted his head. Looking up at the clown, Subaru, amid pain and breathlessness, blurted out the question that had suddenly risen in him.

"Roz... waal... you keep going on and on about 'starting over,' but..."

"Hmm? An important matter, is it-t? Let's hear it, let's hear it."

"You're the one I find questionable. Acting on the premise of someone else's 'restart' — that's not normal. ...You too, really—"

Perhaps he too had some means of carrying memories over. Just as Echidna, holed up in her dream castle within the tomb, did — perhaps Roswaal, too, carried memories of a previous world, from before Subaru's "Return by Death." Otherwise, Roswaal's breezy desire for Subaru to "start over" made no sense.

"If that's the case, that's fine. But then, I could even..."

Cooperate with you, too, couldn't he? Roswaal's aims were unfathomable, and he had piled up unforgivable acts. Killing Ram and Garfiel, driving Emilia into a corner — Subaru would never forgive that. But their circumstances weren't so blessed that he could dismiss Roswaal's power as unnecessary and shove it aside on pure sentiment. If anything, they were desperate.

If the saying about "in for a penny, in for a pound" applied here, Subaru was prepared to drink the poison.

"—It seems that's not to be, after all-ll."

But Subaru's thin thread of hope was cut off by Roswaal shaking his head. Then, breaking his gaze from Subaru's downcast eyes, Roswaal pointed a raised finger off into the distance and,

"Goa."

A small blaze rose up, and the forest in the direction Roswaal's face was turned caught fire. As Subaru blinked at the sudden act of destruction, mixed in with the crackling, popping sound of burning trees, there was another sound he caught.

—It sounded like the tiny, tiny death-cry of an animal.

"...No... way."

"I see-ee. ...So this is how it ends, is it."

Springing to his feet, his face going pale, Subaru swept his gaze around. Shifting his own position in the same way, Roswaal snapped his fingers sharply, again and again, and each time, the smell of fire and burning flesh, and an ear-splitting, high-pitched shriek, tore across the "Sanctuary."

And when the charred, blackened remains of it dropped, with a thud, before him, Subaru understood clearly.

"Oh no, rabbits...!!"

A Great Rabbit — one of them. As they gradually began to show themselves from within the forest, Roswaal hurled magic at them, burning them one by one. Even when they came in groups, they too fell prey to Roswaal. Facing numbers, there was no force superior to Roswaal's. Witnessing it firsthand, terror still gripped Subaru's heart and would not let go.

Close his eyes, and the memory of his entire body being torn apart by sharp teeth came flooding back. The sense of loss from having his fingers, his body, his organs devoured — words could not do it justice. At the appearance of the Witchbeast that had done that to him, Subaru heard his soul scream.

"But it's only the fifth day, and — there should still be more than half a day left!"

"The snow, is why."

"Snow—?!"

"Magic powerful enough to interfere with the weather. Naturally, the mana saturating the air is on an entirely different scale. And what's more, thanks to the snow, everyone in the 'Sanctuary' is gathered at the cathedral. To any Witchbeast in the area, it's far too obvious a feeding ground-ound."

At Roswaal's calmly stated conclusion, Subaru went rigid with horror — "then—". Following that logic, the most dangerous place in the "Sanctuary," under attack by Great Rabbits, would be—

"T-the cathedral! We have to get to the cathedral, now...!"

"Too late already. The moment stragglers started showing themselves to us, over here where there are so few people, it means the ones who couldn't get a share of the feast have already begun to wander off. — There's nothing left."

"But! There, there's—!"

He'd had Rem taken there. Left in Ryuzu's care, Rem had been there. At the cathedral, all the "Sanctuary" residents and the Arlam Village refugees had gathered — over a hundred people, in total. That they might all be — he didn't even want to think it.

"Roswaal! Truce, for now! Either way, we're going to the cathedral! Get the survivors, and get them somewhere safe—"

Subaru closed in on Roswaal, grabbed him by the collar, and shouted. But Roswaal gently pressed down the arm gripping him, and

"Flee? To where, exactly? There's a barrier. The residents of the 'Sanctuary' can't leave."

"Th-that's—"

"There wasn't enough time, Subaru-kun. If the 'Trial' isn't broken through, the residents can't cross beyond the 'Sanctuary.' Which means your wish can't be granted."

Pushed away, Subaru fell backward into the snow. Absurdly, the Ryuzu duplicates gathered around fallen Subaru, standing by with vacant expressions, waiting for instructions. And then, belatedly, Subaru noticed something else.

That Roswaal, who until just now had been burning down every Witchbeast in sight one after another, had now completely stopped his hand.

"R-Roswaal! If you've stopped... don't tell me your mana's run out—"

"No-o, that's not it, you know. My mana is, in a sense, near-inexhaustible. It won't run dry so easily. ...What's run out is my reason for living."

Gradually, white, fur-ball-like creatures were beginning to crawl out of the forest. Leaving small footprints in the white snow that matched their own fur, they drew steadily, unmistakably closer.

"E-even if you can start over, not like this — you should talk it over properly first! You might think it's fine to leave it for next time, but—"

"You seem to be under one misunderstanding, Subaru-kun."

"Huh?"

"Even if you start over, I won't be able to start over with you. The me that exists after your restart cannot be this me. I end here. — But that's fine-ine."

Subaru was struck speechless by Roswaal's words. Roswaal had just called himself outside the scope of the "restart." Which meant Roswaal was someone who merely knew of the possibility that Subaru was doing "Return by Death" — and that dying here meant the end of this particular consciousness called Roswaal.

He accepted that, and still, he was ordering Subaru to start over regardless — knowing full well that the self he was now wouldn't exist on the other side of that restart. That way of thinking was, altogether too much,

"...not a human way of thinking..."

Unlike Subaru, whose consciousness carried on continuously, Roswaal's consciousness did not. For him, once he died, that was the end. To be conscious of that ending and accept it as a matter of course — that was abnormal.

"Someday, a time will come when you truly catch up to me, Subaru-kun."

"Roz...!"

"Listen well-ell, Subaru-kun. — The one important thing. The single, truly, truly important thing, to you. Strip away everything else. Let go of everything else, and think only of protecting that one important thing, to the very end."

"————"

"Do that, and—"

Roswaal raised a finger as if to lecture further. At that very wrist of his, a Great Rabbit that had crept close, right up to him, sank its teeth in. Blood sprayed; Roswaal's right arm was crushed at the wrist, another set of jaws sank into his elbow, his shoulder, and in an instant, countless fangs sank into his torso all at once, the dull sound of flesh being torn apart.

"Roswaaaaal!!"

"—You, too, can become like me."

The clown's smile vanished, swallowed by the wide-open mouth of a rabbit. The white rabbits, swarming, covered Roswaal's tall frame entirely, in overwhelming numbers. He toppled sideways, and, offering no resistance, his flesh was devoured by the rabbits. Devoured. Devoured. Blood scattered, flesh bounced, the white snow dyed crimson — and even that crimson-stained snow, the rabbits, begrudging to waste it, lapped up and devoured completely.

Subaru only watched, wordless, as Roswaal ceased to be Roswaal.

He watched as the being called Roswaal was lost from the world, chewed away.

— He watched.
