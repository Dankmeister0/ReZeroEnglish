Arc 3 Interlude: "The Knights' Intentions"

"So, do you have anything to say for yourself, Sir Julius?"

That low voice rang out with unusual gravity in the sunless, dim room.

The place: the barracks of the Knights of the Royal Guard, adjoining the royal castle. Set apart from the main facilities like the training grounds and guardrooms, one particular room stood out for smelling more of ink than of iron — the captain's office, tucked away at the very back of the building.

The furnishings on display were kept to a bare minimum, reflecting the temperament of the room's occupant, and despite its martial appearance, a considerable share of it was given over to shelves of books.

"No, nothing at all. Everything is exactly as I reported."

At the center of the spacious room, standing at attention before the desk while its occupant drove a quill across a document, a young man answered the question directed at him.

A handsome figure with pale bluish-purple hair neatly groomed, he wore white raiment that bore no trace of what had happened at the training grounds. He straightened the sleeve, gave the hilt of the knight's sword at his hip a light rattle, and said,

"If you judge that my conduct was unbecoming of a knight of the Royal Guard, I have no objection. Please, hand down whatever sentence the captain sees fit."

Julius unfastened the sword, sheath and all, from his waist and held it out before him as if in offering.

Seeing this gesture, as though Julius meant to surrender his sword outright, the middle-aged man seated in the chair — Marcos, whose craggy expression never wavered — closed one eye at the orderliness of it and said,

"Detaining a person of interest in the very midst of deliberations meant to determine the throne of the kingdom in the Royal Selection. Then hauling him out to the training grounds and beating him half to death before sending him off to the infirmary. Taken at face value, this is not the sort of thing that can be settled with an ordinary punishment."

Speaking of the scandal caused by his subordinate — one who wielded, no less, the title of "the Most Excellent" among knights — Marcos gave a weary shrug of his shoulders.

The mock battle between Sir Julius and Emilia's attendant, Natsuki Subaru.
Whether the actual events matched that description or not, Marcos had a rough grasp of what had happened. In brief, it amounted to exactly what had just been said, and Marcos's blood ran thick enough with a knight's instincts that he could easily guess at the other factors involved as well.

"There is room for leniency. Even from among the other knights present at the training grounds, several petitions have come in vouching for your conduct."

"You are too kind."

"It means people notice where it matters, is what it means. On that score I find nothing to fault you for. But you went a bit too far."

Tossing the bundle of delivered petitions onto the desk and rapping his knuckles on the cover, Marcos fixed Julius with a steady stare from his gray eyes.

"A shattered right wrist. Ruptured organs in multiple places. Several broken ribs, a crushed throat, a split forehead, and several missing front teeth on top of it all — a ghastly state of affairs, if not for a skilled healing mage. Hardly the sort of treatment permissible against the attendant of a noble personage."

Marcos had arrived at the training grounds at the same moment as the female candidates, having witnessed only the final exchange.
But even setting that aside, the boy had been battered from head to toe, and it was Marcos himself who had ordered him carried to the infirmary while unconscious, and who had commanded Ferris to do everything in his power for him.
Looking at the extent of the boy's injuries up close, one could easily tell whether it fell within the bounds of a "mock battle" or had gone well beyond it.

"Was it so unforgivable, someone besmirching a knight's pride?"

"However I dress it up, it amounts to nothing but a personal grudge. All of it stems from my own lack of virtue. Please, Captain, spare yourself any further words on the matter."

Julius, closing off his own escape routes, refused to budge from his stance of accepting punishment.
Marcos let his gaze wander into the air for a moment, considering what words to choose in the face of that obstinate posture.
And it was then that—

"Heeey, sorry to keep you waiting! Feri-chan has returned!"

With a breeziness that read the mood of the room well or ill depending on how you looked at it, a chestnut-haired maidenly figure breezed into the room.
A beautiful, girlish figure with semi-long hair set off by a vivid white ribbon, and cat ears of an equally vivid coat atop that — it was Ferris, come to call.
He entered the room with easy familiarity, and upon seeing Julius and Marcos gazing at one another, he put a hand to his mouth and smiled mischievously.

"Oh my, oh my, ohhh myyy, could it be that Feri-chan's interrupting something? Barging in on such a passionate lovers' stare-down — was that not very tactful of me?"

"...Cut the nonsense and give your report already, you little brat."

"Ohh-ho, Captain? Your true colors are showing~"

Marcos, muttering with a hand to his forehead, made a sour face at Ferris's remark.
Disarmed by how utterly ordinary Ferris's manner was, his plain, unguarded self had slipped out before he could stop it.
Seeing something bitter cross that face like solid rock, Ferris loosened his cheeks into a pleased grin and said,

"A Captain with a touch of roughness like that is charming too, you know? You keep up such proper manners in public, so when it's just us and you can take off your public mask, why not let yourself show a bit?"

"In front of my subordinates is normally exactly when I ought to be a public figure... ah, well, I suppose once it's slipped out, there's no point fussing over it. Never mind — just give your report."

Waving a careless hand to prompt him, Marcos got a belated "Okay~!" out of Ferris in compliance. He came to stand alongside the still-at-attention Julius, and unlike him, let the tension drop from his shoulders in an easy, natural stance.

"Just as the Captain ordered, I went all out and gave Subaru-kyun the full treatment. Set the bones, closed up the wounds, and restored what was missing, so I'd say he's fine now. Ah, though the blood I'll be topping up bit by bit through other means."

"Good work. No oversights?"

"If Feri-chan missed something, nobody in the whole royal capital would ever catch it, nya~. There's no problem with his body. As for his heart, though — that I can't say."

Swishing his cat ears, Ferris cast a sidelong glance at Julius's face as he spoke. Seeing not the faintest flicker of emotion there, he nodded, satisfied, and continued.

"But really, Julius, you're just so kind, aren't you. I wonder just how many girls' hearts you've stolen with all that consideration and tender care. Even Feri-chan might get a little flustered."

"I can't fathom the intent behind that remark, Ferris. And as for the latter half of it just now, I must humbly decline to comment."

Ferris, smothering a laugh, pouted his lips and said "How mean~," while Julius, eyes closed, held firm to his original stance throughout.
Marcos found himself rather taken with that bearing, though he couldn't help but think it was also a precarious way to stand.
As if voicing that very thought of Marcos's,

"You don't have to be so stiff about it — the sharp ones already noticed, and for the ones who wouldn't have, it worked like a charm, so what's the harm? Or—"

Toying with his pure white ribbon between his fingers, Ferris licked his red tongue across his lips in a coquettish manner and said,

"—do Feri-chan and the Captain look like such empty-headed sad sacks to you that we wouldn't catch on to the real meaning behind your actions?"

"...I can't hold a candle to your perceptiveness. It makes even my own careful efforts feel foolish by comparison."

At Ferris's pointed, teasing question, Julius's expression finally, belatedly thawed. On his face was the helpless smile of a child caught in the middle of some scheme, but it vanished again just as quickly.

"Still, I would ask you to mind your words. Phrased the way you just did, you risk needlessly tarnishing my friend's honor."

"Feri-chan thinks that's being overprotective, though. Kids don't grow if they don't get hurt a little sometimes, don't you think?"

"There's nothing wrong with the straightforward way he carries himself. The day will surely come when that back of his serves as a guidepost, when the sword fulfills its role. With that day in mind, I see no need to force any change on him that isn't necessary."

"And that's exactly what Feri-chan is calling overprotective, you know."

"Well, I suppose it can't be helped," Ferris said with a laugh at Julius, who refused to budge from his position, before flinging himself with languid ease onto the reception sofa.
Julius frowned faintly at the slovenly way he sat, but Ferris turned even that small reaction into fresh spice for teasing.

"You go and fuss over a boy you only met for the first time today with that much exaggerated care — no wonder you go soft on your own people too, huh."

"I wouldn't call it going soft. If anything, I meant to treat him more harshly than usual."

"Riiight, riiight. Everyone else sure shrank back plenty too. With Julius roughing him up that much... well, at least there's no more worry-nya about him getting jumped by anyone who can't rein themselves in after that."

Closing one eye and forming a mischievous, teasing grin, Ferris drew a wry laugh out of Julius.
Marcos, who had listened silently to their exchange, drew his chin in and said,

"The younger ones must have been plenty on edge after that little pup mouthed off about a knight's standing in the great hall. Every last man attached to the Royal Guard has both sword skill and pride to spare, guaranteed."

Indeed, it was precisely because that scene had unfolded at the Royal Selection — an event with the weight of the kingdom's governance riding on it — that the knights' pent-up discontent had been kept from erupting outright.
But that also meant that, the moment they left that place, there was always the risk it might explode at any time, with no telling when.

"If some hotheaded fool had demanded an apology, I doubt that young pup would've swallowed it meekly. Worst case, he might've been cut down on the spot for insolence."

"Which is exactly why Subaru-kyun needed to get roughed up by a knight's hand as soon as possible."

Picking up where Marcos left off and drawing the conclusion, Ferris pointed a finger at Julius. Then he turned the pointing hand upright, as if to express his gratitude.

"Honestly, Feri-chan was thinking that if Julius didn't do it, Feri-chan would have to."

"A case of the right man for the right job, that's all. Surely you couldn't very well nominate yourself as the referee? Besides, I happened to have a reason of my own that made it not so unnatural for me to be the one."

He was likely referring to the trivial spat that had taken place during the Royal Selection proceedings.
Given the friction between them, no one would think it strange that Julius, out of personal grudge, had invited the boy to the training grounds.

"And besides, if I may say so myself, I was confident I could handle it more skillfully than anyone else."

"Ahh, on that point Feri-chan agrees too~. In Feri-chan's case, sure, there's no way I'd lose, but I couldn't have wrapped it up so smoothly-nya."

At the mention of swordsmanship, Ferris stuck out his tongue and laughed, as if hiding his embarrassment.
In magic — particularly in the Water-attribute school of magic — Ferris possessed unrivaled skill, bearing the epithet of "Blue," and was the standout wielder of magical power in the entire Order of Knights. On the other hand, when it came to actual combat prowess, he ranked near the very bottom of the Royal Guard. He might even be the weakest of them all.

Julius, who bore the name "the Most Excellent," stood at the very top of the Order of Knights in pure swordsmanship. Though there was concern in some quarters that, because there existed a superhuman figure who made any comparison of combat strength laughable, Julius's own martial prowess never received the recognition it truly deserved.

"When it comes to putting a lesser opponent in his place, leaving it to Julius is the right call, sure enough. Which is exactly why I keep telling you to swing a sword more often yourself."

"Nooo way. Working up a sweat swinging a sword around like that — what if Feri-chan's pale, clear little palms got all calloused? Would you take responsibility for that?"

"A knight who makes a living by cutting people down has no business fretting over a thing like that."

At Ferris casually brushing off the Captain's order, Marcos let out an exasperated sigh and shrugged. Then, with what little expression his face allowed creasing into a frown, he looked at Julius.

"I know what you were after. I'm not so dense as to think it wasn't a necessary consideration. But what's done is done."

Even as he harbored sympathy for the man before him, Marcos delivered this crisply. Julius shifted in a show of respect for that refusal to let personal feeling cloud official duty, and tightened his narrow features as if reining in his own slight lapse into ease.

"Sir Julius Juukulius, I hereby pronounce your sentence."

"Yes. I humbly await it."

"Five days' suspension, forbidding you from the barracks and from entering the royal castle. During that time, your sword will be held in custody. Once your suspension is lifted, go to the vice-captain to reclaim it."

"—I accept the sentence."

Closing his eyes as if weighing the pronouncement, Julius then, with a fluid motion, reverently held out the knight's sword he had removed earlier and passed it into Marcos's outstretched hand.
Taking hold of the sword that was the very embodiment of a knight's pride, Marcos ran his fingers along the black-lacquered sheath as if to confirm its weight, and said,

"Sorry about this. I'm putting a burden on you that shouldn't rightly be yours to carry."

His expression didn't change. But there was a note of genuine concern in that murmur.
In response, Julius gave a small shake of his head.

"The Captain does everything within his power to the very best of his ability. That the Order of Knights, once shattered, stands strong and valiant again today is owed entirely to your presence, Captain."

"I'm a man too shallow to even get his own subordinates marching in step properly."

"That's not true at all, Captain."

It was Ferris, who had until then watched the proceedings in silence, who objected to Marcos's uncharacteristic note of self-deprecation.
He turned himself around on the sofa to sit up properly, his cat eyes and ears swaying with amusement.

"There's no way everyone's just going to be one big happy family, and the Captain's got a firm grip on the reins, no question. And at the far end of the reins the Captain's holding, Feri-chan and Julius do our best, and at the far end of the reins we're holding, someone else does their best in turn."

"That's about the size of it, isn't it?" Ferris tilted his head and looked up at Marcos's face. At the words of comfort and encouragement from his subordinates, Marcos closed one eye, then bared his teeth in a rough grin befitting a rugged man.

"Not being able to properly discipline my subordinates includes you, you know — the one who never shows up dressed like a proper soldier."

"Feri-chan dressing like this is hardly news at this point, you know~."

Snorting at Ferris sticking out his tongue and playing the fool, Marcos laid Julius's knight's sword carefully across the desk and settled himself back into his chair.

"That's all the business at hand. I've got no end of paperwork to sort through myself, with the Royal Selection coming. — You're both dismissed."

The return to using the formal "I" was the signal that the time during which Marcos let his guard down had come to an end.
Sensing this, Julius offered a reverent salute and Ferris a more casual one, but both paid their superior equal respect in doing so.

Marcos jerked his chin and watched them in silence; the two of them turned as one and left the captain's office.
The door closed, and Marcos was left alone in the quiet space. His lips parted, and he murmured,

"Nothing ever goes the way you'd like."

But those words, let slip, never reached the two who had already left the room.

Having left the captain's office, the young man and the maidenly beauty walked side by side down the guardroom corridor.
The sight of the two striking figures walking together looked like something out of a painting, and the fact that one of the pair could easily be mistaken for a girl in men's clothing only added a suggestive edge that stirred the onlooker's imagination.
In any case—

"The Captain's just so inflexible, isn't he. If he can see the whole picture behind the scenes, he should've just let you off scot-free, don't you think?"

"That's not how it works, for me or for the Captain. I had expected a much heavier sentence, if anything — I felt the Captain's leniency in this."

Soothing the discontented Ferris, Julius turned his thoughts to the punishment handed down to him. But something faintly bitter crept into his refined features.

"Though having to go see the vice-captain does weigh on me a little."

"Ahh, that really is a mean trick, isn't it. The moment Feri-chan heard it, I went 'ughhh' too. I thought it was impressive that it didn't show on your face, Julius."

The two of them talked about the vice-captain, their mutual superior, without even bothering to lower their voices. Julius, who would normally be the one to rein in Ferris's remarks, only gave a wry, evasive smile now and made no move to correct him.
That alone was enough to reveal what the two of them — no, what the entire Order of Knights — commonly understood the "vice-captain" to be.

"So, what's Julius going to do now?"

"Naturally, I'll spend my time at the mansion, in accordance with the Captain's orders. I'll explain the circumstances to Lady Anastasia... though it does concern me that she isn't the type to simply sit quietly."

"I know that feeling well~. It's the same with Lady Crusch — I never know what she's going to do, and it always keeps me on my toes. But then again, that unpredictability is part of Lady Crusch's dangerous charm, isn't it~."

Sympathizing with the wistful Julius, Ferris's cheeks flushed as he grew smitten in the latter half of his own remark. With a dreamy expression, he jabbed a finger at Julius's chest and traced little circles with it.
Listening dutifully all the while to Ferris's lovestruck chatter, letting him have his way, Julius suddenly said,

"By the way, Ferris, about that boy from earlier—"

"He's with Lady Emilia right now. After that, though only for a little while... I think he'll be staying at the Karsten mansion."

Before Julius could even finish voicing his question, an answer came from Ferris's mouth, its warmth suddenly gone.
Looking up at him from close by, watching Julius fall silent, Ferris let out an "Mm-hmm~" and laughed.

"You really are kind, Julius. Should I call it knightly, nya?"

"To be described that way is the highest of honors. ...What does it mean, that the Karsten household will be taking him in?"

"Feri-chan thinks it's not really nice to go blabbing on and on about a patient's personal information, y'know~."

Ferris dodged the question with an endearing gesture. But Julius picked up, from that very evasive non-answer, something that led him to a conclusion, and murmured "As I thought," before continuing,

"I had thought there were far too many suspicious points, but it seems his Gate is quite worn down."

"...Feri-chan didn't say a thing, you know."

At Ferris's blatantly feigned innocence, Julius gave a small nod of his chin as if to confirm it.
After all, combining Ferris's use of the word "patient" with the sense of wrongness Julius himself had felt about Subaru made it easy enough to guess at the circumstances behind it.
And the conclusion that Julius's sharp mind drew from all of it was—

"Lady Emilia truly is the sort of person who ends up losing out for it."

There must surely be an easier way for her to live, Julius thought.
Surely there was a clearer way to repay a debt, a clearer way to show it. But she didn't know it, and her days had never allowed her the chance to learn it.
And that, he thought, was surely a precarious, fleeting way of walking through life — one bound to breed crossed wires and misunderstandings, for both the one giving and the one receiving.

"Wouldn't it be better if she lived a little more cleverly...? Is that what you're thinking?"

At Julius's murmured reflection, Ferris posed a testing question of his own.
Julius fell silent for a moment in thought, then shook his head. "No."

"It's precisely because she is that way that she can live as herself — nobly, beautifully. I have no wish for that to change. Which is precisely why..."

Julius began to continue, but caught himself, closing his mouth at the thought that finishing the sentence would be graceless.
Some things are better left unsaid. And Ferris, it seemed, understood that too. He touched one of his cat ears, seeming to grasp Julius's hesitation, and said,

"What do you think's going to happen, nya?"

"I don't know what the future holds. All we can do is hope. That everyone might live rightly, true to themselves, without shame."

Lifting his face and looking ahead, Julius resumed walking.
Falling half a step behind at his side, Ferris clasped his hands behind his back and leaned forward as he walked.

"Does that include that boy too?"

"Everyone, Ferris. That is precisely why we carry swords."

Will he break, Julius wondered.
If he is going to break, then it's for the best that he broke here.
But if he doesn't break, then—

"Crossing swords again with a fool who holds up his ideals wouldn't be such a bad thing."

Taking in Julius's words, Ferris gave a small, knowing laugh.
Julius arched an eyebrow at the reaction, his expression turning suspicious. As if that expression itself were something even more amusing, Ferris broke into a grin and said,

"Even if you feel that way about it, Julius, I bet Subaru-kyun's thinking 'never again' on his end. Getting beaten down that publicly and all, nya~."

A smile that not even a hand over his mouth could fully hide.

"With everything else going on, were you maybe just a little bit annoyed too?"

Asked in a testing tone, eyes turned up at him, Julius almost reflexively shot back, "That's uncalled for" — but paused, and looked back into his own heart.
The discourteous conduct in the great hall, the string of insults hurled at the training grounds. And then the outcome all of that had produced, and the face of the woman who'd been made to shoulder the burden because of it, rose up in his mind.

"That is uncalled for, Ferris. I am a knight. However imperfectly, I hold myself to that standard."

Shaking his head gracefully, and placing a hand over his heart as if to say he had nothing to be ashamed of in his own conduct, Julius looked straight into the eyes of Ferris walking beside him, and said,

"I was annoyed. Just a little."

"Feri-chan was pretty annoyed too, though, you know."

As if they'd traded the finest joke of the day, the two of them exchanged smiles together.
Then, as they neared the entrance of the guardroom, the two faced one another once more, extended their hands, and shook firmly.

"Well then, until we meet again in a while. I sincerely hope that you, and your liege, remain in good health."

"Julius, good luck too — Lady Anastasia's going to give you an earful, I bet. As for the rest of what you started... well, we'll take it from here."

Releasing the handshake, Ferris gave his hand a little wave and walked off toward the exit of the guardroom with an easy, carefree air.
Watching that retreating back — his friend, and his rival — Julius said,

"It is Lady Anastasia who will ascend to the throne."

"Nuh-uh, it's Lady Crusch who's fit for the throne."

Trading that declaration of war, the two knights each returned to their own respective liege.

The red light of the evening sky poured down, bestowing its color equally upon the two as they parted, and upon everyone living in the royal capital.

—Each of their Royal Selections was now about to begin.
