Arc 7 Chapter 49: "As the Stars Align"

"Damn it! I can't see them anywhere anymore...!"

Looking up at the sky from the street, his gaze sweeping across the many scaffoldings, the masked boy—no, Al—stamped his feet in frustration, his throat trembling with the words.

The utterly outrageous turn of events just before had spurred his irritation, setting his heart ablaze with violent emotion.

It was a development he could only call ridiculous.

"Subaru-chin, Rui-chan..."

Meanwhile, in contrast to Al's open anger, it was Medium who murmured with a downcast face.

The young girl dressed like a dancer drew her white limbs tightly in, worried about the two who had fled the scene.

However, the color of emotion packed into that murmur was a complicated, mysterious thing. It was hard to call it mere concern alone. It was, without question, connected to the words Subaru had flung out before vanishing from sight—

"Rui-chan is..."

"—A Sin Archbishop, was it."

"—!"

Medium's eyes widened as the rest of the words that had slipped from her lips were picked up by another. Paying no mind to Medium's reaction, it was Abel who likewise looked up at the sky alongside Al. With his mask back in place, the man's expression was hidden, his feelings unreadable from outside.

Still, it was clear from the fingers drumming against his folded arms that he did not welcome this situation.

"Abel-chin, the two of them..."

"...What must take priority is bringing this situation under control. Our course of action remains unchanged. Unfortunately, one more option available to us has now been eliminated."

To Medium's faint, weak question, Abel's curt answer came back. He hadn't directly touched on Subaru and Rui, but it was clear enough—he had no intention of moving to reunite with the two of them.

Hearing that, Medium tasted both anxiety and relief in the depths of her chest at once.

Concern for the two of them, and relief at not having to face them. Terror at having been told, in a situation too dire to laugh off, something that should never even be said as a joke.

"So you're just gonna leave him be? Our brother, with even his head turned into a kid's!"

"And yet, what would you have us do? Your own circumstances have scarcely changed either, I'll remind you. If you and Medium both become unusable, we will have no choice but to leave you behind as well."

"Guh...!"

"Do not presume the choice of whether to join hands is yours alone to make, clown."

Al, at a loss for words, wished to reunite with the separated Subaru and the others, but Abel coldly rejected the notion. Even so, Al's focus was on Subaru above all—Rui's existence was secondary. If anything, Al's attitude toward Rui, whose bomb-like true identity had now come to light, was plain to see.

"Can't let our brother stay with that brat..."

Pressing a hand to his mouth, Al muttered that, open hostility toward Rui plain in his voice.

Medium looked as though she wanted to object to Al's attitude, but she herself seemed unable to decide her own stance toward Rui, and an uncharacteristic hesitation took shape in her silence.

"..."

Glancing sidelong at the two of them, Abel once more looked briefly up overhead. Subaru and the others were already out of sight, having leapt off to who-knew-where in the demon capital, and gazing at the jumbled scene without even a trace of them remaining—

"Fools."

—was the only word that slipped out, reaching no one at all.


△▼△▼△▼△


—And so, in the midst of this unprecedented turn of events, Subaru had ended up parting ways with his companions.

It had been an act carried on momentum, hardly a decision made with proper thought for what lay ahead. Even so, if he'd been pressed to decide on the spot, he was certain he'd have regretted it. To avoid that, to avoid regret, he had chosen to act as he had—that much, at least, he could say for certain.

And now, Subaru was already "regretting" having made that call.

Because—

"Auu, uauu!"

"Stop it, Rui! You don't need to do that anymore!"

Pinning Rui's small body from behind, Subaru pulled her, struggling, off of her target. What he'd pulled her off of was an unconscious pursuer—a man Rui had straddled and pinned down.

After parting from Abel and the others, while leaping across the scaffolding strung throughout the city in search of Olbart, trying somehow to restore his "Infantilized" self back to normal, the enemy had caught up to them.

It seemed the pursuers had marked both Subaru's infantilized form and Rui's, who hadn't shown up at the castle keep, as targets—Abel's guess was likely correct. In other words, Olbart and Tanza were in cahoots, and the information the two shared was also in the hands of the pursuers—the horned people.

As a result, pursuers had shown up even where Subaru was moving together with just Rui, and just as they were about to be caught—

"Uuu!"

—Rui's combat prowess, the same she'd shown out on the street, exploded, and the pursuer was swiftly neutralized.

That much, at least, was a relief. Even before his limbs had shrunk, Subaru's own fighting ability had been a sorry thing. Even against ordinary townsfolk who didn't know how to fight, having received part of Yorna's power through the Soul Marriage Technique should have made it no contest.

But the problem was that Rui didn't stop attacking even after neutralizing her opponent.

"Auu..."

"Don't 'auu' me! Do that much more and he'll die! These people are just scared, that's all. That's not okay, not even a little!"

Kicking her legs about, Rui went quiet at Subaru's scolding. He couldn't tell if she'd understood what he'd said, but it seemed to have at least gotten across that Subaru was angry. Having confirmed that, Subaru tended to the fallen man.

Tending to him, though there was no first-aid kit at hand, so all he could really do was prop the man's head up and wipe away the blood from his wounds.

"...These people too."

"Uau?"

"They're desperate. To keep their home from being torn apart."

The young sheep-man and deer-man Rui had struck down—still in their late teens or early twenties, roughly the same age as Subaru's original self. The people around the inn, the sheep-boy who'd thrown Subaru on the street—their ages were all over the place, and they didn't seem like friends in any real sense.

But there was a certain, undeniable connection between them. The horns growing from their heads.

Whether one horn or two, however it differed by person, a horn was a horn. And the influence that had had on their lives was nothing Subaru, who knew Emilia, could laugh off.

"Maybe it's a good thing Rem isn't here."

Rem, a member of the Oni Tribe, possessed the trait of growing a horn on her forehead. Unlike the beastfolk, it wasn't something that stayed grown out permanently, but still—as a member of a horn-bearing race, she too might not have been unfamiliar with the kind of circumstances these people had lived through.

He'd never heard anything like that from Ram or from Rem, though.

"Ram wouldn't give a damn what anyone said to her, probably."

Going her own way, living exactly as she pleased—that was Ram, so no matter what the peanut gallery said about horns, she seemed the reliable type to just scoff, "Hah!" and blow it all away. A woman equipped with a strength unbound by race or title—surely she would.

—Or, would a woman like that, even knowing about Rui, have managed to keep her composure?

"...I'm an idiot."

"Uau."

Pressing his fist to his own forehead, Subaru withdrew that pathetic, cowardly line of thought. Watching Subaru's gesture, Rui, crouched beside him, tilted her head.

Ram wasn't here. Neither was Emilia, nor Beatrice—none of them were here. Even Rem, the closest of them all, remained far away in Guaral. This wasn't the time to lean on people who weren't here to comfort himself.

"Anyway, staying here and standing out is bad news. We need to hide, now."

Honestly, given his original schedule, even the time spent running and hiding was a waste. By rights, he should be the one chasing after someone else who was running and hiding. And yet here Subaru was, on the being-chased end of things.

"Probably, Abel won't come looking for us... right. He's probably mad, and probably thinks finding Olbart is more important, I bet."

"Au."

"Should we go meet up with Taritta-san? She's being chased by a lot of people in a city she doesn't know too, so... no, that's no good."

Putting a hand to his head, Subaru shelved the idea of reuniting with Taritta, who knew nothing. He couldn't picture himself explaining well to Taritta why he wasn't with Abel and the others. He'd have to lie, and lying wouldn't solve the problem.

Medium was a no-go. And Taritta, too—if she learned Rui was a Sin Archbishop, she'd view Rui as dangerous just like the others, and keep her at a distance.

"I'm worried about Taritta-san, but if anything, Abel and the others are probably the ones looking for her."

Right now, he wanted to avoid reuniting with Abel and the others, whom he'd only just gotten separated from. Without an answer on how to treat Rui, facing off with Abel and the rest again would just repeat the same thing. Besides, he didn't want to end up in another argument like that and start hating everyone.

He didn't want to hate himself either, for failing to properly persuade everyone.

"Rui, let's go. We're finding 'the Abyss with the good view,' finding Olbart-san."

"Uau."

Saying that, Subaru took Rui's hand and pulled her along as he started walking.

She'd originally been a child whose destination was unknown—but ever since she'd started using her power, the Authority of "Gluttony," that uncertainty could only be said to have grown larger. So—

"If you don't want to die, don't leave my side. I don't want you dying either. ...Not right now."

"Ah, uu."

Whether she understood or not, Rui nodded as if accepting it. At the sight of the girl grinning loosely, Subaru let out a deep sigh.

Two children, in an unfamiliar city with no one to rely on, wandering around to solve a riddle—

"This is basically just being lost for real."

That was all he could manage to mutter, weakly.


△▼△▼△▼△


—After that, too, Subaru and Rui's pursuit-and-flight drama proved to be an ordeal in the extreme.

Not knowing the layout of the city, difficulty communicating with his companion, and, purely and simply, probably just Subaru's bad luck—these seemed to be the causes. In any case, being chased around through the demon capital, unable to properly go about searching for "the Abyss with the good view," time just kept ticking away and getting whittled down.

"Rui! Don't fight! Run, run, run, run!"

"Au! U!"

Subaru stopped Rui, who was about to dart out, by literally throwing his whole body at her. Leaping onto her small back, forcing himself into a piggyback position, Rui—so as not to shake him off—supported Subaru's body and, just like that, jumped high to escape their pursuers.

"Don't let them get away!"

—came the cry of the horned beastfolk patrolling the demon capital, chasing after them. It seemed the pursuers really were exclusively people with horns. As far as the residents of Chaosflame went, plenty of demi-humans without horns were around too, but it seemed no orders to capture Subaru and the others had been given to them.

"That Tanza girl seemed to be acting on her own, so..."

Tanza, the deer-girl, held a position something like Yorna's secretary, but even so, the range of people she could move under her own orders surely had its limits. Sure enough, if every resident of Chaosflame had become an oni in this game of tag, there'd be no escaping at all—it seemed the girl was fighting right at the edge of how far she could abuse her authority.

Though, being driven to this extent even at that edge, from Subaru's perspective he rather wished she'd go easier on them.

"Ugh... they're catching up! Rui, don't fight back! We're running like this!"

"Uu?"

"Not 'why'! Just because! Anyway, it's no good!"

Still carrying Subaru on her back, treading on window frames and jutting scaffolding, Rui bounded through the city, hopping along, clearly dissatisfied with running one-sidedly. Her expression seemed to say she could win if she fought, and Subaru protested by pulling on her cheek.

Certainly, if Rui fought, she might well defeat their pursuers. But if Rui fought, those pursuers might die. Even if they were merely knocked unconscious, there was no telling whether Rui would stop attacking there.

In fact, just now, if Subaru hadn't stopped her, Rui might have dropped them from the high scaffolding all the way down to the ground below.

Rui's innocence—like that of a baby or a small child—hadn't been lost. But along with that innocence, the power of "Gluttony" had returned to her. That struck Subaru as no different from the very thing he'd feared—the original "Rui Arneb" running rampant.

If this Rui were ever to revert to being "Rui Arneb"—

"—then Abel and the others would be right."

If that were so, there'd have been no need to flee that street, no need to have things sour between him and Abel and the others. No need for Subaru to agonize so strangely over the existence of this child named Rui.

So, it couldn't happen.

"I won't let you kill anyone, ever again."

As long as Natsuki Subaru's eyes were open, that could not be allowed to happen.

"—Au."

The strength in Subaru's arms clinging to her tightened, and Rui quietly faced forward. She seemed to have withdrawn her dissatisfied attitude from before and reconsidered, deciding to do as Subaru said, for now. And so Rui continued fleeing, bounding about to shake off the pursuing beastfolk, her golden hair dancing as she ran.

However—

"So... persistent...!"

Perhaps naturally so, since their efforts to shake pursuit amounted to little more than running about in a panic—they couldn't manage to lose the beastfolk chasing them. With the pursuers' knowledge of the terrain, and the fire in one of their eyes—Yorna's Soul Marriage Technique—the gap between them wasn't widening. If anything, it was closing.

"At this rate—"

They'd be caught, Subaru thought, gritting teeth that could belong to either a child or an adult. It was at that very moment—

"—You two, this way."

"Huh?"

"Uau?"

Rui kicked hard off a wall, trying with a sharp, decisive motion to throw their pursuers off her trail. Before she could confirm whether that aim had succeeded, a voice called out to the pair as they landed on the street.

Looking over, it was a slender-faced young man beckoning to Subaru and the others. At a glance, no horns grew from his head, but that wasn't reason enough to let his guard down right away.

"Y-you are..."

"You're being chased, aren't you? From what I can see, your pursuers look pre-e-etty serious about it. I won't say anything more—here, it'd be wise to just listen to me, honestly."

Meeting Subaru's wary gaze, the young man beckoned with a likable smile. Then, to Subaru and Rui, who held their breath, he gestured toward a handcart set out on the road.

A cart loaded with several barrels on its bed—the young man pointed precisely at the gaps between those barrels.

"With your two small bodies, I'm sure you'd fit right in here."

"E-even so, hiding there won't..."

"There's no time. The turning of the stars shifts moment by moment. Whether to avoid this turning or to accept it is, of course, entirely up to you two."

"..."

"Sti-i-ill, I do think it'd be the wiser move to just go along with this obediently."

Waving his empty hands to show he meant no harm, the young man smiled. Whether that smile could be trusted was something Subaru hesitated over considerably. But, as the man said, there wasn't much time to spend hesitating.

So—

"They should've come this way!"

Blood-heated men appeared, searching for traces of the fugitives who'd vanished from sight. In the process, their attention landed on the young man leaning against a wall, and on a single handcart.

"Hey, you there! Did two kids come through here?"

"Huh? You mean—you're asking me? Sorry, but I was just catching my breath here, so..."

"So I'm asking if you saw anyone while you were catching your breath... ah, forget it!"

The bull-man, his footsteps loud and heavy as he approached, gave up on the young man's unreliable answers. That said, the bull-man hadn't overlooked the cart and the young man for nothing. Abandoning his interrogation, he redirected his attention to the cart instead.

"Hey, what's this cargo?"

"We-ell now, what do you think it is? What kind of merchant do I look like to you?"

"Tch."

At the young man's elusive words, the bull-man clicked his tongue and glared at the cart bed. Then, grabbing the stacked barrels, his right eye igniting with fire, he easily tipped the load over. With a loud crash, the once-balanced arrangement of barrels toppled into disarray. The stacked barrels swayed side to side—if anyone had been hiding beneath them, they'd surely have been crushed by their weight.

Having confirmed this, the bull-man let go of the barrels.

"Sorry to bother you."

—was all he said before walking off with his companions.

To that retreating back, the young man called out, "Fix the barrels for me, would you?"—but there was no reply, and the young man shrugged his thin shoulders.

"We-ell, well, what unpleasant people. I really do wonder about them."

Muttering as if to himself, the young man approached the cart on the now-empty street. And rather than the cart bed with its cargo, he crouched down and peered underneath it. There—

"Good thing you didn't just go and hide in the cart bed. If you had, your whole bodies would probably be creaking right about now. That would've been pre-e-etty rough, don't you think?"

"...Don't say scary things like that."

"Uu."

To the young man's casual delivery of a frightening statement, watched over by his smile, Subaru and Rui crawled out from beneath the cart they'd wedged themselves under. With a cart loaded with barrels, it was only natural to assume anyone hiding would be in the cart bed. Subaru, too, had first tried to hide there as told, but an uneasy feeling had made him stop.

"But there wasn't time to run, so we hid under the cart bed instead."

"Pretty good call, I'd say. I'm no good at lying myself, so if they'd pressed me a little roughly, I'd have spilled everything right away."

"...I mean, that's probably 'cause you're not on our side."

Beneath the cart bed, Subaru, who'd been clinging to the cart's wheel, brushed the dirt off his hands and clothes, then did the same for Rui's equally grimy body. It seemed their pursuers really had lost track of them, so for that much, at least, he had to thank this young man.

"But why'd you help us?"

"Why'd I help you, hm. Hmm, let's see. Well, how about—it was too much for me to watch, seeing kids get chased?"

"'How about,' you say—the way you said that, that's definitely not the real reason..."

"Was it that obvious?"

Asked by a narrow-eyed Subaru, the young man scratched the back of his head, looking troubled. Chuckling wryly, from what Subaru could tell, he didn't look like a demi-human. Not only did he lack horns, but there wasn't anything animal-like about his ears either, nor any telltale trait in his skin or nails.

That being the case, he had more the air of someone from outside Chaosflame.

"You are..."

"Ah, I'm just a fella named Ubilk. I understand the urge to be suspicious, but the-e-ere's not much I can really tell you, I'm afraid."

"What do you mean, there's not much you can tell me?"

"What I mean is, I helped you two because somehow it just felt like the thing to do."

Grinning loosely, the young man—Ubilk—brushed back his gray hair with his fingers. At that gesture and his refined looks, Subaru got the impression that Ubilk had a proper job of some kind. His travel attire, though it looked lightweight, seemed to have had a fair amount of money put into it.

Since he was hauling a cart, Subaru figured he must be a merchant.

"Is that, like, a merchant's intuition or something?"

"A merchant's... ah, this? Hah hah hah, no, that's not it. I'm not a merchant or anything like that. This cart isn't even mine, you know."

"Eh!? But then, what about what you told those people just now?"

"I never once said I was a merchant. And of course, I never said this cart was mine either. This ju-ust happens to be a cart that's parked here. It's probably from some shop right nearby, don't you think?"

Saying that, Ubilk pointed at a nearby shop, rattling off "Maybe that one? Or maybe that one over there" with no particular conviction. He seemed to be joking around, but not entirely joking. In other words, it seemed he'd genuinely dodged those pursuers with nothing but bluff.

"Uau."

"Ah, y-yeah, I know."

While going along with Ubilk's pace, Subaru's sleeve was suddenly tugged by Rui. He felt as though he'd been asked whether it was really okay to keep standing around, and Subaru reflected on his own leisureliness, having been hurried along by Rui.

Even having shaken their pursuers, the situation hadn't improved one bit.

"Um, Ubilk-san, thanks for helping us. But you should really think things through more carefully from now on. I feel like something bad's gonna happen to you eventually..."

"Oh de-e-ear, are you worried about me? Do I really look that pitiful? I actually think I'm pretty good at getting by in the world, you know—guess there's quite a gap there."

"No, it's not that I think you're suspicious or anything, it's just that we're in a hurry."

Truthfully, he did have some suspicion in his heart. In this case, suspicious not in the sense of being in league with Tanza or Olbart, but purely the possibility that he was a dangerous adult unrelated to all that.

Right now, with just the two children, Subaru and Rui, even a merely suspicious adult was plenty scary. And if the man did turn out to be a dangerous adult, Subaru had no confidence he could stop Rui from beating the man senseless. A dangerous adult was a bit much for children to handle.

"Anyway, we've got somewhere to be, so we'll get going. Thanks for helping us."

With that, Subaru took Rui's hand and, cutting his thanks short, started walking off.

However—

"Ju-ust a moment, hold on. Don't be so cold, let's help each other out. The spirit of mutual aid—I think that's a beautiful thing, you know."

Saying that, Ubilk circled around in front of them with a light jog on his thin but long legs. Instantly, Subaru's internal "suspicious dangerous adult index" spiked, and Ubilk's standing grew precarious. Concretely, the grip on Rui's hand tightened, and Rui, letting out an "U?" of surprise, picked up on Subaru's wariness toward Ubilk too.

Depending on how things went, Rui might just take it as having Subaru's backing and leap out, beating Ubilk senseless and stuffing him into a barrel.

"Ubilk-san, are you the flexible type?"

"Me? Well, from past work experience, having a flexible body let me handle all sorts of things, so I suppose I lean flexible—but, um, what exactly are you asking me?"

"N-no, nothing in particular... you said mutual aid—is there something you want help with from us?"

"Ah, no-o, it's nothing like that. The 'mutual aid spirit' thing just slipped out, it's not that I want your help or anything. Ahaha."

"..."

Once again, the suspicious dangerous adult index climbed. This time, along with it, something new seemed to have gone into effect as well—an index of "not necessarily dangerous, but don't want to get involved with this adult."

In any case, this was someone he didn't want a long relationship with.

"Hah hah hah... oh de-e-ear, the atmosphere's colder than I expected. This is the same feeling as when I'm surrounded by a bunch of people who hate me. Am I disliked here?"

"If possible, I'd rather not become one of those people, but..."

"Oh, an honest one. Well then, since you seem to be in a hurry, and since you're honest, and since I happen to like honest people, let's talk a bit seriously."

"—!"

Saying that, Ubilk bent at the waist, bringing his face in close to Subaru's. Subaru let out an involuntary groan and lowered his head, and in his place, Rui stepped forward, thrusting her hand out.

Then, pushing Ubilk's cheek from the side, she let out a warning "Uu!"

"Oh-oh dear, I've been rejected. I thought I did pretty well with the ladies, but I guess it's different when there's someone else around, huh?"

"...Ubilk-san, you probably shouldn't make Rui angry. This one's got, um, more strength than she looks."

"I see, so your name is Rui, is it."

Nodding along, Ubilk took in that answer, and Subaru swallowed hard. There was no problem with him knowing her name, but he felt he'd given out information he shouldn't have. He didn't want to give this man, whose character was still unclear, any more information than necessary.

"Ubilk-san! What's this serious talk about? If there's nothing, we've gotta go!"

"—I wasn't really planning to go out into the city much today, you know."

"Huh?"

"But so-omehow, here I was, wandering around outside with no particular purpose, and once I felt that urge, I figured there had to be a reason for it. So, going wherever the mood took me, I ended up here, killed some time next to this cart, and then..."

Placing a hand on his own forehead, Ubilk traced his finger down in order—to his gentle eye corners, his pale, translucent cheeks, and finally his well-shaped chin—before snapping his fingers at the end.

Subaru, whose eyes had been drawn to that motion, flinched, and Ubilk, deepening his smile at the reaction, waved his snapping fingers side to side.

"—and the two of you came hopping right over my head, just like that."

"..."

"...Oh de-e-ear? Did that not land quite the way I hoped?"

Tilting his head, Ubilk looked back and forth between the unresponsive Subaru and Rui. Rui might simply not have understood the content of the story, but Subaru was different. Subaru, having heard Ubilk's answer, simply had no words to say.

He'd half expected some far-reaching, grand reason lay hidden behind it all.

"So you're just saying you were out for a walk, felt like resting, and we happened to show up?"

"Well, you could put it that way, I suppose. Bu-ut, I could also put it this way, you know—say it's all thanks to the guidance of the stars."

"The stars'... fate, or something like that..."

Despite how romantic the phrasing was, Subaru's shoulders slumped in disappointment. If this had been told as the prologue of some story, the scene of a man and woman's fateful meeting, there'd have been romance in it. But present here were a shrunken Subaru and the originally-small Rui, plus the eccentric Ubilk.

There wasn't a shred of romance to be found in that.

"Could it be that I'm being given up on here?"

"It's, um, fine. Even if your 'serious talk,' Ubilk-san, wasn't all that serious, since we're never gonna meet again, it doesn't really bother me that much."

"That's pretty sharp, cutting words you just said there. That edge reminds me of an old friend of mine."

"You had... a friend, Ubilk-san...?"

"Ahaha, I did, yes."

An unintentionally rude thought had slipped out, but Ubilk didn't seem to take offense. Only for a brief moment did something like a subtly complicated feeling toward that friend show through—proof that even this thoroughly unserious, saccharine Ubilk had such a thing in him.

"Anyway, meeting here was fate of some kind too. Hmm, that's right! Actually, my job involves lending an ear to little consultations and troubles, you know. How about it, want to talk something over?"

"Here it comes! No thanks... no wait, I heard on TV that saying 'no thanks' gets conveniently misread by con artists. Not interested!"

"It's not a hard sell or anything, and I'm not here to trick you, you know? I ju-ust want to fulfill the duty that's been laid on me."

"Duty laid on you?"

"Right, there's a reason I came here today. I'm guessing it was fate that brought me here to lend an ear to you and Rui-san, probably."

Crossing his thin arms, Ubilk nodded repeatedly with an air of understanding something only he understood. Unfortunately, Ubilk's sense of conviction didn't land with Subaru at all. It only served to steadily ratchet up the suspicious dangerous adult index. If he could, he wanted to just leave Ubilk here and make a run for it. Having shaken their pursuers, it felt like he'd gotten tangled up with someone even more troublesome.

"Ah, running away won't do you any good, you know. If you tryyy to run..."

"What happens?"

"I shout at the top of my lungs and tell those folks from before exactly where you two are. That'd be a problem, wouldn't it?"

"That would be a huge problem...!"

Still, rather than let that happen, he wasn't above letting Rui loose on the man. Of course, since he didn't want Ubilk to die, Subaru intended to do everything he could to stop Rui if she went wild—but he was prepared, as a last resort, to let it come to that rather than have their pursuers called on them.

And besides—

"Sorry, but it's better if you don't know our situation. If you hear it, you might get dragged into it too, and I don't think you could handle it, Ubilk-san."

Even if Subaru and the others did nothing, Tanza and her people might hurt Ubilk regardless. And if that happened, it would be Subaru and the others' fault. He didn't want to drag Ubilk into a situation like that.

However—

"Ah, no need to worry about that. I did say I'd lend an ear, but I'm the kind of advisor who's fine without hearing much of anything in particular."

"How does that even work!?"

"Now, now, just humor me and think of it as being fooled—here, lend me your hand."

Setting aside Subaru's concerns, Ubilk smiled easily and gently took Subaru's hand. His right hand had been holding Rui's, so it was his left hand that got taken. Startled by the slender, almost feminine feel of Ubilk's fingers, Subaru hurriedly checked on Rui.

"Uu."

Fortunately, though Rui stared intently at Ubilk's action, she didn't do anything rash like suddenly attacking. Though, that in itself meant having to keep watching over a balloon that might pop at any moment, air being pumped in without end.

"Um, Ubilk-san, neither I nor you have much time, so..."

"Oh de-e-ear, suddenly even I'm getting anxious hearing that. —But, it's done."

"Huh?"

While anxiously watching Rui's gaze fixed intently on Ubilk's thin neck, Subaru, who'd urged him on, found his arm released from Ubilk's grip. Having let go of his hand, Ubilk gently turned his palm toward Subaru's face, and—

"—The answer you're searching for is already within you."

"..."

At those unexpected words, Subaru's eyes went wide. Seeing Subaru's blank, uncomprehending reaction, Ubilk tilted his head. "O-oh my?"

"Did that one maybe not land either?"

"It's not that it didn't land, more like I don't really get what it means..."

The answer you're searching for—that was a vague thing to say. It felt close to the kind of lines fortune-tellers often used on TV back in his original world. The sort of catch-all statement meant to apply to anyone and cloud the issue—a classic con artist's line.

"I feel like there aren't many people who aren't searching for an answer to something..."

"Hmm, I don't think it's as big a thing as that—as big as one's whole life, I mean. Unfortunately, I'm not quite able to see the movements of the stars on that grand a scale."

"Like a horoscope, or..."

"Probably, it's the problem right in front of you. See, weren't the two of you being chased? That's a problem too, isn't it? How about that one?"

"But we already know the reason they're chasing us—"

Confident, it seemed, in his own abilities as an advisor, Ubilk tried to get closer to the right answer by digging into Subaru's inner thoughts. There was something faintly of a con man's desperate struggle to it, but as Subaru took the conversation seriously, a small snag caught in his mind.

"..."

That snag felt like it mattered, like it was something very important. Of course, there was no telling how far he could trust something felt by his shrunken self's mind. But even if Subaru's small "thoughts" couldn't be trusted, his small "intuition" should still be something worth trusting, regardless of his body's size.

Right now, the problem Subaru and the others were facing was, in other words—

"—'The Abyss with the good view.'"

That the answer was already within Subaru—that was what Ubilk had pointed out. He had no intention of digging deep into what kind of trickery lay behind that. But he felt it.

—A sense that if he reeled in that snag, he could reach the answer he was searching for.

"Uau?"

"I think... I might've figured it out."

He answered Rui, who peered up at his face, her well-shaped brows furrowing. At the faint strength that had returned to Subaru's voice, Rui's blue eyes went wide, and then she smiled happily.

Glancing sidelong at that change in Rui's expression, Subaru let out a breath and looked at Ubilk.

"The consultation—might've actually worked."

"Oh, good, that's a relief. I'm glad too. Since I went to the trouble, I do want to properly fulfill my duty, after all. So then?"

"Yeah, we're in a hurry. Thanks for everything, Ubilk-san. —Let's go, Rui!"

"Auau!"

Responding to Ubilk, who winked one eye, Subaru pulled Rui's hand and took off running.

Just like that, he ran off in his impatience, then stopped once, just once. Turning back, it seemed his movement had been unexpected, for Ubilk's eyes had gone wide.

To him, Subaru waved a hand and said—

"Ubilk-san, I'm telling you this for your own good—you should go home to your parents! Stay out of danger, keep a low profile, and quit the con-artist thing too!"

—and that, meant with genuine gratitude, was the extent of the entirely one-sided "advice" Subaru gave him in his own way.


△▼△▼△▼△


And so, having seen off the two children as they hurried away.

"'Quit the con-artist thing'—now that's pre-e-etty harsh."

Alone, left standing in the street, the young man—Ubilk—laughed and scratched his cheek with a finger. Thinking it had been a rather strange stretch of time, he nonetheless felt, from the look of the boy at the end there, a weight lift somewhat from his chest, sensing he'd managed to be of some help.

As for the rest—

"I should hurry back to my room, or Kafma-dono will surely have something snide to say. And if something really were to happen while I'm out, that'd be a problem, so I'd best get back. Still, even so..."

Once more turning his gaze toward the direction the children had gone, Ubilk closed one eye.

And then—

"—Even if it's the duty of a Stargazer, I do wonder what it is the stars want."
