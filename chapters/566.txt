Arc 7 Chapter 61: "Welcome to the Isle of Sword Slaves!"

—It felt like being left behind in a dark, cold place.

Dark as closed eyelids, cold as pressing a cheek to long-abandoned iron—that kind of place. Feeling his whole body bound in chains of helplessness, he had desperately sought salvation.
That was why he kept reaching out blindly, frantically, grasping at anything within reach.

But in that final moment, the reason he reached for the white light approaching before his eyes was different.

Light—no, that wasn't it. It was a girl, her expression hiding a tragic resolve. He hadn't reached for her because he sought salvation. He'd reached for her because he thought he had to save her.
So he twisted against the chains that sought to seal even the slightest movement, and stretched out this hand—his own hand.
This hand of his—

"—Small."

Natsuki Subaru murmured that, gazing at the hand reflected in his blurred vision.
The hand, palm turned to the ceiling, extended from his own shoulder in an unbroken line. It clenched and opened exactly as he willed it to. Undoubtedly his own hand.
Except it was one size smaller than he expected—a child's hand.

In other words—

"I failed to get back..."

After hardship piled on hardship, he'd finally managed to grasp the chance to turn everything around. Literally the result of dying again and again—and yet it had slipped from this very hand.
For the sake of the thing he'd let go of, how much had he sacrificed?
Just how much, sacrificed—

"—?! Wait, what am I doing?!"

In that instant, his hazy consciousness snapped back, and Subaru pressed the hand he'd been staring at to his face.
The memories flooding back led to the bizarre incident he'd run into in the demonic capital, and to the dangerous game of tag with Olbart—an ordeal that had put him through something even worse.
Stacking countless "deaths" atop one another, he'd wrenched out a victory, and should have succeeded in claiming the victor's crown—undoing his "Infantilization."
He remembered Olbart touching his chest, trying to undo the "Infantilization."
The next moment, he thought he heard a word of love whispered in his ear, and then—

"My consciousness just blew away, and then... and then what?"

Subaru tried to search through his own hazy memory for what had happened. But push or pull as he might, the door holding his severed memory refused to open.
As the door's stubbornness fed his slowly rising panic, Subaru clenched his back teeth hard—

"—Now, now, no need to rush, let's take it easy. Fortunately you've kept your life, and there's no need to do anything until your young lady companion wakes up, is there?"

"—Huh?"

A voice had suddenly come from right beside him, and Subaru turned toward it, stunned. There, right next to where he lay, he found himself looking into the eyes of someone propping their chin on both hands atop the hard bed.
Someone beaming a broad, cheerful smile, peering into Subaru's face from point-blank range.

"Whoa!?"

"Whoa there. Nice reaction, but you'd better tone it down. Make too much noise and the island lord might take notice and set you up for a troublesome death match. Though, mind you—"

"Wha—uh, wha..."

"—I'm not opposed to that sort of tasteless entertainment myself. Quite fond of it, actually."

Subaru jerked upright reflexively, blinking rapidly, while the blue-haired figure before him spoke on breezily. He had long hair tied back at the nape of his neck, and wore clothing reminiscent of a Japanese style rarely seen in this world. Startled by the unfamiliar face, Subaru swallowed and chose his words, and his question, carefully.
Not just an unfamiliar person, but an unfamiliar place, an unfamiliar environment, an unfamiliar air—

"...Who are you? Where is this?"

"—Ahh, excellent! That question is the best! Just as I hoped—no, even better!!"

"Wha?"

Subaru had meant to cautiously feel out the other's intentions, but before he knew it, his hand was seized by the person, eyes shining with delight, and shaken up and down with enough force to nearly wrench his shoulder from its socket.
It wasn't that he'd let his guard down. And yet he couldn't react at all.
While Subaru reeled in astonishment, the figure released his arm with a satisfied "phew," spun around once on the spot like a dancer, and—

"Allow me to answer! To you—who crossed the black lake and arrived upon this island, the Isle of Sword Slaves, Ginunhive, with your shifty eyes swirling with a marvelous premonition!"

His tone and gestures were theatrical, his manner impertinent for a first meeting. But the figure, utterly unconcerned with what was going on in Subaru's mind, carried his attitude—his performance—through with total conviction.
As though he were, quite naturally, a stage actor deserving of thunderous applause—

"—Cecilus Segmunt."

He spread his arms wide as he declared his name, striking a pose like a kabuki mie. Of course, this world couldn't possibly have kabuki, so it wasn't likely he was imitating the real thing. Even so, there was a breath-stealing intensity to that pose.
Though, what actually stole Subaru's breath wasn't just the intensity of the pose—it was the familiar ring of the name itself. The instant he placed where he'd heard it before, his face twisted into a scowl.

He'd heard it from Abel's mouth.
That name, if he recalled correctly, belonged to one of the Nine Divine Generals—

"I am none other than Vollachia's Blue Lightning. —The leading man of this world's stage."

Faced with someone who could declare that so boldly, Subaru's eyes went wide.
Both the name and the title given matched what Subaru remembered. They matched—but there was one, very significant problem.
Namely—

"—Vollachia's strongest is a kid?"

—The fact that Cecilus Segmunt, laughing before him, was a child around the same age as the shrunken Subaru, with the unmistakable air of a mischievous brat.

—The serious effects of "Infantilization" were eating away at both Natsuki Subaru's body and mind.

The physical effects were easy to understand: as his appearance suggested, Subaru now had only the physical capabilities of a ten-year-old. His arm and leg strength, of course, but also his stamina and reflexes had all reverted to before adolescence.
Though, given how questionable Subaru's physical abilities had been by this other world's standards to begin with, the difference might be negligible. A sad thing to admit.
Far more serious than that, though, were the effects showing up on the inside rather than the outside.

"I think... my head's gotten weaker..."

The phrasing was misleading, but it succinctly captured how dire Subaru's situation was.
His imagination, his ability to think, his capacity to draw on his own knowledge—all had grown feeble. It was as if the desk he was using was the same as before, but now he lacked the strength to pull the drawers open, and couldn't even reach the higher ones anymore.

—The hellish game of tag against Olbart, waged in the keep of Crimson Lazuli Castle.

The fact that he'd needed an insane number of attempts to counter it was, in large part, thanks to his diminished thinking ability. Surely, before he'd shrunk, he would have found the solution a bit sooner.
Probably, he could have spared himself a bit of the pain, if only a little sooner.
Fortunately, that time, it hadn't been Subaru who saved the day, but everyone precious to him.
He felt like he could still hear everyone's voices, their cheers, echoing in his head—

"No, no, that's not it..."

Something welled up in him, warm and slow, and Subaru hastily wiped it away with his hand.
The feeling, a mix of relief and melancholy, was probably close to homesickness. Before the infantilization, he could hold it back with busyness and a sense of responsibility, but it seemed a child's body lowered even the floodwalls against tears. If he let his guard down, he'd probably burst into tears.

"...Don't cry, don't cry. Am I an idiot? No—I am an idiot."

No matter how much he lamented, he couldn't borrow the strength of the people who weren't here.
Rubbing his tears away, Subaru forced himself back to the matter at hand. If he didn't, his chance to reunite with everyone would never come around at all.
What mattered right now wasn't his weakened head, or the homesickness that had sprouted in him. He needed to look squarely at the real heart of the problem.
Namely—

"Did I hear anything from Abel or Zikr-san about the First's age..."

Sniffling, Subaru dug through his memory for the answer to his question.
Cecilus Segmunt—he'd heard that was the strongest of Vollachia's Nine Divine Generals. Which meant he was the single strongest person in the whole empire.
In fact, the whole reason they'd headed to the demonic capital of Chaosflame was as part of a plan to win over Yorna, one of the Nine Divine Generals, as an ally. To reclaim the throne, they needed to bring as many of the Nine Divine Generals onto their side as possible.
So naturally, they must have properly discussed the person ranked First among them.
And so, Subaru forced the stiff drawer open, doing his best to dig out an answer—

"All I can remember is that he lacks popular support and isn't very reliable..."

Whether that was simply the part that had left the strongest impression, or whether that really was all he'd been told, wasn't clear, but that was the sum total of what Subaru knew about the First.
Unfortunately, nothing about the First's appearance or age—the information he actually wanted right now—could be found there.

"But if the reason he lacks popular support is that he's a kid, that would explain it easily enough."

In an empire where strength commanded respect, it was a strange story—supposedly the strongest, and yet lacking popular support. But if you were told the strongest person was a child, it suddenly made sense. However strong, plenty of adults would balk at following a child.
Just as Subaru managed to reconcile his prior information with this new knowledge—

"—Sounds like you've reached some kind of conclusion."

Called out to, Subaru raised his head to find, right in front of him, the blue-haired boy—Cecilus—still standing there, in the exact same mie pose as before, not having moved an inch.
Subaru, who'd left him hanging while absorbed in his own thoughts, blinked in surprise.

"S-sorry, for leaving you hanging like that."

"Ohh, no no, don't mention it. After all, I get left out of conversations all the time! I'm quite used to being silently ignored, ahahaha!"

"I-is that so..."

Cecilus flashed his white teeth, cheerfully confessing this sad circumstance without a hint of self-consciousness.
Bewildered by his attitude, Subaru was nonetheless relieved that Cecilus hadn't taken offense at his rudeness. After all, there were mountains of things he wanted to ask him.
So many, in fact, that he was at a loss over where to even begin.

"Sorry for being rude, let me apologize again. I'm—"

"So I'm telling you, no problem, none at all! Of course, that's not to say rudeness, discourtesy, and running roughshod over people should be tolerated as a matter of course, but I do have the capacity to let a certain amount roll right off me. A man with a small, narrow, shallow vessel is exactly the sort who ends up cast as a bit part, a mere fall guy, don't you think?"

"O-oh, right. Thanks, that helps. So, I'm—"

"Oh, that reminds me! I held off asking since you were deep in thought, but how was that pose from earlier? I put quite a bit of soul into that expression, so I'd love to know whether it landed. I'm on the fence about whether to add it to my name-declaration repertoire!"

"W-wait a second, let me finish—"

"Right, right! I've got mountains of other things I'm dying of curiosity to ask, too! So, what do you say? Why don't you tell me all about that as well, nice and thorough and clear—"

"—Listen to me!!"

No matter how calmly Subaru tried to speak, every attempt was cut off by the other's lightning-fast chatter.
Afraid of being swept away entirely by that momentum, Subaru had shouted before he could stop himself. Startled at his own outburst, he watched as Cecilus's narrow eyes went round with surprise.

"Uh-oh, did I do it again?"

"That's a template line on a level you basically never hear anymore..."

"Level? Template?"

"Um, those are words from where I'm from—they mean something like 'degree' and 'cliché.'"

"I see, I see, from where you're from! Fascinating! Oh, but this is the very thing that's no good, isn't it."

Eyes shining, the "Blue Lightning" was ready to pounce on the next object of his curiosity in an instant.
Perhaps that epithet symbolized his personality too—like lightning, striking wherever it pleased, too honest about his own impulses. A personality utterly incapable of sitting still.
—Though perhaps that, too, was an act to hide his true nature.

"So, so, what will you tell me? My heart won't stop pounding wondering what I'll get to hear! Ahh, this is what it feels like to be alive!"

"...Doesn't look like an act at all."

With sparkling eyes, the boy hungered for the story Subaru's mouth would tell.
His manner seemed utterly divorced from secrets or scheming—it was doubtful he could even lie. Besides, what possible point would there be in setting such a trap for Subaru now?

If he weren't with Abel, Subaru was someone who wouldn't draw the slightest attention in the empire. —Of course, there was an exception or two persistently gunning for his life, but still.

"But that's an exception among exceptions, and I'd rather never meet them again if I can help it."

"—? What is it?"

"No, never mind, just talking to myself. Anyway, I'm Natsuki Subaru—ah."

Cecilus tilted his head, and to deflect the question, Subaru had blurted out his name—only to immediately realize his blunder with an inward "oh no."
While disguised as a woman, he'd gone by "Natsumi Schbarz," being careful not to let his real name spread carelessly through the empire, but the carelessness of a child had just ruined all that.
If, by some chance—however remote—someone in a position like Cecilus, one of the empire's Generals, happened to know the name of a figure connected to the Royal Selection in the neighboring Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica—

"Natsuki Subaru, is it? Somehow it rolls smoothly and strangely off the tongue. By the way, which of Natsuki and Subaru is the family name?"

"—. F-family name is Natsuki, and Subaru's my given name, I guess."

"Ahh, understood. Then I'll call you Subaru-san! Though if I find a better way to address you, I might just change it on a whim, so don't take offense."

Cecilus answered while waving a hand airily, his face showing not the faintest flicker of recognition.
While Subaru felt relief at that, a small doubt also sprouted within him. —Was this child really Cecilus Segmunt?

"—"

Thinking about it more, self-declaration was something anyone could do. Naturally, the boy in front of him could do it too—just as Subaru had declared himself "Natsuki Subaru."
Of course, the name and epithet of Vollachia's strongest, the "Blue Lightning," were widely known.
Running into Vollachia's strongest by pure chance in a place this uncanny felt too convenient. It seemed far more natural to think that some child who admired that name and title was simply claiming to be the "Blue Lightning."

"Ohh? Your eyes look like storm clouds are brewing. Did I do something?"

"...Are you the real 'Blue Lightning'?"

"There it is! I've been told that again! I get told that every single time!"

No good at subtlety, Subaru had thrown his suspicion out as a direct question, and Cecilus burst out laughing.
Whether it was even right to keep calling this boy Cecilus remained in doubt, but apparently people besides Subaru harbored doubts about the truth of his identity too.
However—

"It's easy enough to just insist 'I'm the real deal!' with words, but that's no proof either, is it? And even if I offered up some hidden fact worthy of proof... you wouldn't be able to tell genuine from fake just from that. You don't know me well enough for that, Subaru-san."

"That's... yeah, that's true."

"Then arguing back and forth over whether my identity is genuine is a waste of time. Let's cut the wasteful stuff clean off and move on to the next topic! That's far more constructive—though I'm a specialist in destruction, myself!"

Somehow it felt like he'd been talked into a corner through sheer momentum and glib chatter, but pressed relentlessly without a breath's pause, Subaru found no gap to object through. Besides, extreme as his reasoning was, it was true.
Subaru had no way to tell whether the "Blue Lightning" was genuine or fake. Not a single clue toward it. So doubting the provisional Cecilus wouldn't get him an answer anyway.
Which meant—

"—"

Once he set aside his interest in Cecilus, the next thing to surface was doubt about the situation itself.
He'd been trying to get Olbart to undo his "Infantilization," and then his consciousness had simply gone blank—no, been swallowed by something far blacker than that, and vanished.
But it was clear at a glance he hadn't just fainted for no reason.

For one thing, the bed Subaru had been laid on while facing Cecilus was a shabby wreck. Even in the resource-poor Shudrak settlement, effort had gone into making the beds comfortable with soft grass and branches. But this bed showed not a trace of such consideration.
All there was, was a slab meant for nothing more than lying down on.

Reinforcing that grim impression was the bleak, empty room, which held nothing besides the bed.
Dry, cracked gray walls, and a floor stained with grime that wouldn't scrub off. The heavy, damp air was bad for the lungs too—nothing about the environment could be called comfortable.
It felt suffocatingly like being locked in a jail cell.

"Um, Ceci, can I ask you something?"

"Ceci?! What's that, is that me by any chance?"

"I wasn't sure if it was okay to call someone I can't confirm is real or fake 'Cecilus'..."

It wasn't like he was preparing for the possibility of meeting the real Cecilus somewhere else, but that was the compromise Subaru settled on for a boy of unknown authenticity.
Calling him "Provisional Cecilus" would be rude, and frankly a pain besides. Setting aside how suspiciously familiar he was, his attitude was quite friendly, so Subaru wanted to avoid earning his dislike too.

"If anything, he might be the friendliest person in the whole empire... no, he probably can't beat Flop-san and Medium-san. But it feels like a close match."

That said, there had also been cases of people who seemed friendly at first suddenly turning on him, so overconfidence was forbidden.
Given that, meeting the O'Connell siblings by chance while he was with Rem had been a stroke of luck rather unlike Subaru's usual fortune. Maybe one of the biggest strokes of luck in his whole life.
That time, hadn't it been Flop who called out to him first? Probably, seeing Subaru together with Rem had simply been too much to bear watching.

"Ceci, Ceci is it... What a special feeling! What a strange ring to it! Come to think of it, I've never been called by a nickname before, so this is quite a rich new sensation."

Meanwhile, Cecilus's spirits were soaring skyward at his very first nickname.
Subaru had picked the name somewhat lazily, but if he was this pleased about it, it would be a shame to dampen his mood, and no alternative was really necessary. Not that Subaru had any other ideas anyway.

"So, getting back to it... this isn't Chaosflame, is it?"

"Chaosflame... the demonic capital? Nope, nothing like it. In fact, within the empire, it's practically the exact opposite—west versus east! As I said, this is the Isle of Sword Slaves, Ginunhive."

"The Isle of Sword Slaves..."

Rolling the unfamiliar word "Ginunhive" around on his tongue, Subaru's mouth twisted. Setting aside the place name itself, that title gave him a bad feeling. If Chaosflame was called the "demonic capital" because so many Demi-Humans lived there, then Ginunhive too must have earned its title for some reason.
The Isle of Sword Slaves—that was—

"—Sword Slaves... Isle?"

The word "sword slave" was easy to picture, and he'd heard it somewhere before.
Right, it was a word Al had grumbled about now and then. He'd spent over a decade somewhere as a sword slave, going through experiences that felt like dying, or so he'd said.
He'd lost his left arm, but somehow survived and escaped that environment.
If so, then this place—

"Don't tell me—a death game venue...?"

"A 'death game'? Is that another word from where you're from? What does it mean?"

"...It means something like a coliseum, a venue for people to kill each other."

"Ahh, I see! Then you're catching on fast! This is indeed a 'death game venue,' precisely!"

"—!"

A dreadful chill ran through Subaru, who had voiced the possibility fearfully, and Cecilus laughed at him.
At his light tone as he clapped his hands together in front of his chest, Subaru even doubted his own ears. But no matter how long he waited, the words he wanted to hear never came from Cecilus's mouth.
—No, that wasn't right—words did come, but they weren't the ones he wanted to hear.

"Once again, welcome to the Isle of Sword Slaves. It's rare for someone as young as you or me to end up here, and even when they do, they don't tend to live long—so I have high hopes for you."

"Th—!"

"Th?"

"That's ridiculous! Something like that... something like that has to be some kind of mistake!"

His face drained of color, Subaru's voice cracked as he shouted at Cecilus.
It was simply too much to accept. Him being in a place like that had to be some kind of mistake.

"Because, up until I woke up, I was in Chaosflame..."

"No, no, you were on this very bed the whole time you were asleep too. You were having nightmares. Given how uncomfortable it is, I understand why you'd have nightmares, but the whole time, you weren't in Chaosflame—"

"That's not what I mean! You know what I mean!"

"Hmm."

Frustrated with Cecilus's obtuseness, Subaru snapped back, spittle flying. At his outburst, Cecilus closed one eye and settled into a listening posture, holding his tongue. Seeing that, Subaru's eyes wandered as he glanced around the room.

"I was in Chaosflame. Me being here has to be some kind of mistake. There are people I was working together with, too."

"Mistake or not, the fact remains you're here. If I accepted that line of reasoning, it would mean I'm the one who got transported from this island to the demonic capital."

"If I can end up on the Isle of Sword Slaves, then the reverse could happen too, couldn't it?!"

"I wonder about that. —Though if you want that answer, I can give it to you right away."

Shrugging at Subaru's tantrum over his own unreasonableness, Cecilus quietly raised a hand. His slender fingertip pointed toward the far wall of the room.
—No, not the wall. What he indicated was the window set into it, barred with iron.
Positioned high up, the window let in a damp breeze, but it connected to the world outside the room.

"No, no, no, no...!"

Lips trembling, Subaru hurriedly leapt down from the bed.
The moment his bare feet touched the cold floor, his head swam and his strength drained away, but he forced it down and dashed to the window. Jumping up, he grabbed the iron bars, twisting his body awkwardly as he forced himself upward, and hooked his chin over the windowsill.
Then, somehow, he managed to fix his eyes on the scenery outside—

"—the... ocean?"

"Yes, the lake."

Gazing at the view outside the window, Subaru had muttered that, dazed.
Picking up on the mutter, Cecilus tried to affirm Subaru's words. But Cecilus's affirmation was wrong. Subaru had said "ocean," not misspoken "lake."
Yes, Cecilus had made a mistake. And if he'd made a mistake there, maybe the scenery outside was a mistake too.

Even Subaru understood that such a thought was nothing but his own wishful thinking.

"Everything as far as I can see... a black lake..."

"The island's entirely surrounded by the lake, all the way around. The only way to reach the far shore is to 'raise' the single drawbridge. A natural fortress—or should I say, a natural prison!"

Unable to hold himself up any longer, Subaru slid down from the window in a slump. As he sank to his knees on the floor, head hanging, Cecilus's oration was hitting its stride.
In such a hopeless place, why on earth was he in such good spirits?

"Why..."

"Hm?"

"Why do you look so happy about it?"

The adult Subaru probably would have stammered and never asked so directly. But as a child without self-restraint, the question simply spilled out of his mouth unfiltered. At Subaru's overly candid question, Cecilus turned back, eyes brimming with childlike wonder, and said, "That's because—"

"—I can feel it! The end of the prologue!"

"The prologue..."

"Pitiable me, tossed into this 'death game venue' without much understanding of why, forced to endure death match after death match amid the raging winds of blood! And into that life of mine blows a fresh wind, carrying a premonition entirely unlike the scent of blood! Surely it would be a lie to say the story doesn't move after that!"

Cecilus rambled on, finger raised, and Subaru couldn't understand a word of it. —No, that wasn't quite right. It wasn't that he couldn't understand. He didn't want to.
Because if he tried to properly understand what Cecilus was saying, it would mean—

"If it weren't like that, it wouldn't be any fun. Keeping the leading man penned up in the wings to rot is a blasphemy no proper script would ever allow!"

—That Vollachia's strongest, the "Blue Lightning," had the mind of a man who saw the world as nothing but a story.

Cecilus Segmunt, with his story-obsessed mind craving nothing but turbulent drama, talked a great deal.
So much so that, left unchecked, Subaru suspected he might just go on chattering endlessly forever.

"So anyway, I just happened to be strolling around outside when there you were, crawling right up onto the shore. Isn't that amazing? That's not something that happens every day. Truly fateful! Or perhaps I should say, novelistic."

"—"

"You probably don't know this, but I've always had rather sharp instincts. Today too, I felt as though the wind was inviting me out for a walk, and that's how I found you—it's honestly a little frightening how much the world seems to favor me."

"—"

"Of course, you surviving that situation means you're likely favored by the world too! Let's show off our best sides—Sub-baa... Bal-lu... hmm?"

Tilting his head, Cecilus muttered something to himself, deep in thought.
Half-listening to his incomprehensible, not-particularly-substantial mutterings, Subaru padded forward on the cold stone floor with his bare feet.
Stepping outside the room where he'd woken up and walking through this unknown land was reckless.
Even so, there was something he had to confirm.

"It's true, right? That I wasn't alone."

"Hm? Ah, yes, that's true. I'd like to commend you for that among other things, really. Not only did you survive that situation, but you properly saved your companion too—an act blessed with real luck and courage... a feat of talent of a sort!"

"Never mind the talent and courage stuff. So, this companion—"

"It was a little girl. About the same age as you, Bassoo... Oh! Now that has a rather nice ring to it, saying it out loud, don't you think?"

The conversation kept veering off topic with alarming ease, but Subaru had managed to get the answer he wanted.
Probably, what Cecilus was agonizing over was a nickname of his own to counter being called "Ceci." As long as it wasn't a truly awful nickname, being called anything in particular hardly mattered.
What mattered was the information he'd managed to pry out just before that—

"—Louis."

Around the same age as himself—or rather, around the same age as the current, shrunken Subaru—naturally, the person that brought to mind was Louis. Given the circumstances, Medium had also been shrunk to about the same age, but she hadn't been in that castle keep, so she wasn't a candidate.
Honestly, Subaru's feelings toward Louis were an unbearable tangle.

"I'm not planning to abandon you until I get an answer to that. Besides, maybe if I had your power..."

If he could use Louis's "Teleportation," he might be able to escape this dangerous island.
He still didn't know the layout of the whole island, and the only person he'd met here so far was Cecilus, but from what he'd heard, this had to be the worst possible environment for a child to spend time in.
So—

"I have to get off this island, even a second sooner."

"Quite the ambition, but I think you'll find plenty of walls in your way. From what I've heard, only one person has ever managed to escape from here, and that's precisely why the 'Curse Rule' came to exist."

"Then I'll be the second. No—counting my companion, the third."

His resolve interrupted, Subaru shot that back without thinking. Hearing it, Cecilus's eyes widened slightly, and then he let out a high, sharp whistle.
It seemed to have improved his mood somehow, but Subaru had no room to spare worrying about that.
And then—

"Bassoo, here it is."

After walking a while down a dim corridor thick with cold, damp air, Cecilus suddenly stopped. With his hands clasped behind his head, he jerked his chin toward a room with a grimy wooden door.
Looking up at the door, Subaru asked, "What is this place?" and—

"The healing room. Some people call it the corpse room too. Plenty of people fail to die properly in the arena and end up as corpses in here instead."

"B-but it's a room for healing people, right? Where's the healer?"

"A healer! Well, there are people who dress wounds, but a wielder of such precious magic? No such thing here. Having one around would cut down on deaths, sure, but that runs against the island lord's policy."

"—"

Answered with such a breezy laugh, Subaru found himself falling silent.
It had slipped his mind, but in the Vollachian Empire, very few people could use healing Magic. That was exactly why Rem's healing magic had been treated as such a precious thing.
But the thought of Louis being thrown into a healing room with no healer in it—

"The girl you brought with you was quite weakened, so that's the reason. This room is nominally meant for treating the injured, after all, so there's at least proper medical supplies and blankets. We sword slaves get treated like you saw earlier—the sleeping conditions were the worst, weren't they?"

"...You're the only sword slave here, aren't you, Ceci."

"Ahahaha, that's right! Well then, let's have our moving reunion, shall we."

Reading Subaru's inner thoughts twice over, Cecilus clapped him on the shoulder.
Overwhelmed by his momentum, Subaru swallowed hard, steeled himself, and headed for the door. He pushed it open, and grimaced at the awful stench that came pouring out.

What poured out was a smell that mixed blood with rotting flesh, the very picture of filth. Whether or not the concept of hygiene had died here entirely, the not-very-spacious room within was littered with worn-out tools and haphazardly washed bandages hung out to dry.
Amid such less-than-ideal conditions—

"That's your companion, sleeping over there."

"—! Louis!"

As Subaru looked around the room restlessly, Cecilus pointed to a bed in the back.
Apparently the room did serve some function as an infirmary, since there were two beds a bit better than the one Subaru had been laid on, and the blanket atop the farther one bulged slightly.
Pushed on by his own impatience, Subaru rushed toward it, hurrying even though he hadn't decided what to say to the Louis he expected to find there.
However—

"—Huh?"

Rushing over, Subaru froze in shock as he reached to shake the small shoulder.
It wasn't that he couldn't find the words to say. —It was that he couldn't find the person to say them to.
The girl laid there, said to have been pulled up onto the island together with Subaru, was not Louis, the girl who had thrown Subaru's heart into such complicated turmoil.

Two horns, far too prominent for such a small head, and flaxen hair trimmed even at the shoulders. A girl whose small frame had been stripped of its ornate kimono and left in nothing but a plain undergarment.
She was neither Louis, nor Medium, the companion who had come along to Chaosflame and been shrunk by Olbart's technique too.
The one lying there was—

"...T-Tanza?"

To his trembling call, the sleeping girl gave no response.
But even without an answer, he could tell just by looking. The girl who was neither Louis nor Medium—her identity was Tanza, the Deer Human attendant of Yorna Mishigure, the mistress of the demonic capital.

"Why—why is she here...?"

"Uh-oh? Did I go and do something again?"

Shown a sleeping face different from the one he'd imagined, Subaru stood there stunned, while Cecilus said something clueless behind him.
Of course, it wasn't Cecilus's fault. It wasn't, but—

"That's not what you said! I was told Louis was here—"

"Hmm, was it, though? I believe what I explained was that there was a girl who came up onto the island together with you, Bassoo. That she wasn't the girl you were thinking of comes as a surprise to me too."

"Ngh..."

Unable to argue against Cecilus's entirely reasonable answer, Subaru found himself at a loss for words.
It was true that Subaru was the one who'd assumed "the girl who was with you" meant Louis. On that point, he had no choice but to apologize to Cecilus.
But this was strange. Why was Tanza here with Subaru?
And if it had been Tanza with him all along, then—

"What happened to Louis..."

Subaru put a hand to his head, anxious over the safety of the nowhere-to-be-seen Louis.
Having heard the truth of her identity, Abel, Al, and Medium all regarded Louis as dangerous. Yorna might protect Louis, but only because she didn't know the truth.
If she learned Louis was a Sin Archbishop, she too would surely change her mind, just as Medium had.
And there was no reason left for Abel and the others to keep hiding Louis's true identity.

"I have to bring her back to Rem..."

Louis's existence was a thin, fragile thread connecting Subaru to the memory-less Rem.
Even though it was Louis who'd tagged along without permission, Subaru had reasons he had to bring her back. More than anything, Subaru himself hadn't decided what to do about Louis.
And yet, Louis's whereabouts were unknown, and for some reason Subaru was on this island instead.

"This is no time to be standing around like this...!"

With everything churned together into a mess, Subaru's thoughts refused to come into any order.
Even so, he understood this much: he had to get back from this island to Chaosflame right away.

"If possible, I want to bring this girl too..."

"Oh? Are you sure? Wasn't she not the girl you were hoping for, Bassoo?"

"She wasn't. She wasn't, but that doesn't mean I have a reason to leave her behind either, does it? Besides, Yorna-san must be looking for her too."

Yorna, deeply affectionate, hadn't spared herself even for a young child she'd only just met.
Without her, Subaru couldn't have snatched victory in that game of tag with Olbart, nor even brought that eerie old man to the negotiating table for a match to begin with.
So returning Tanza to Yorna's side felt like a natural way to repay that debt.

"I think your enthusiasm and your reasoning are both admirable, but as I said before, I think it'll be difficult."

But Cecilus's opinion came pouring cold water over Subaru's determination.
Though his manner of speaking and behavior hadn't changed a bit, what Cecilus was saying was, at its core, harsh. Still, Subaru couldn't just say "I see" and let the harshness of reality break his spirit.

"I heard you. You're going to say it's hard to get out. But if we find a boat, or lower that drawbridge you mentioned earlier..."

"The drawbridge is 'raised,' not lowered. That's a serious obstacle in itself, sure, but the real problem lies somewhere else entirely. The biggest problem is the Curse Rule. The Curse Rule."

"Curse Rule...?"

An unfamiliar term, but calling it the biggest problem meant Subaru couldn't just ignore it.
At Subaru's furrowed brow, Cecilus said "Yes," and tucked both hands into the sleeves of his kimono.

"The Rule of the Curse—the 'Curse Rule.' An extremely troublesome and powerful decree, laid down by the island lord who rules over this Isle of Sword Slaves. Break it, and death! Defy it, and death! Resist it, and death! —A curse of death!"

"...Ah."

"It covers the entire island, from end to end. Understand?"

Cecilus spun in place as he spoke, waving his sleeves airily.
His tone was light, without a trace of seriousness, but for Subaru to laugh it off as a lie, this so-called curse of death fit the empire's image far too well.

"S-so, nobody can get off this island at all?"

"Yes, that's right. If it weren't so, I'd have left this place long ago myself. Sure, the Witchbeasts in the lake are formidable, but if I really put my mind to it, I can run across the surface of the water too, you know. Do you know how? You put your left foot out before your right foot sinks, and then—"

To the stunned Subaru, Cecilus launched into some tired old theory.
It sounded like a technique for ninjas to run across water, but plenty of this world's superhuman types probably really could pull it off. Not that this was the time to be escaping into idle thoughts like that.

"S-so that island lord person is the one casting the curse? Then what about getting them to lift it? Me being here is a mistake, so..."

"Hahaha, what an amusing joke! Whether it's a mix-up, a mistake, or a case of mistaken identity, the moment you set foot on this island and had the curse mark carved into you, you became one of the sword slaves. That the sword-slave spirit hasn't soaked into you yet just marks you as having a named role to play, so to speak."

"—!"

His grasping-for-hope words denied, Subaru caught his breath despite himself.
Hearing the word "curse," nothing but bad memories welled up in Subaru. And, sure enough, it seemed all but decided that those memories would now be painted over with something even worse.

"This is no... joke...!"

Without even realizing it, he'd been thrown into an unfamiliar place, together with a girl he barely knew. And on top of that, if he tried to leave that unfamiliar place on his own, he might die because of a curse cast by someone he'd never met—all told to him by someone he barely knew either.
Just what was he supposed to believe in, and how was he supposed to get through this situation—

"...That's right. That's it! Ceci! You're one of the Nine Divine Generals, aren't you?!"

"Oh?"

"Then couldn't you get in touch with the outside? Um, like about this sleeping girl, since she's from Yorna-san's place in Chaosflame..."

Eyes bloodshot with the sudden idea, Subaru grabbed Cecilus by both shoulders.
Cecilus closed one eye at the touch, and Subaru, in his desperation, forgot his own shamelessness.
Just moments ago he'd been treating Cecilus like some lying boy who cried wolf, and now he was hoping the boy really was the "Blue Lightning" he claimed to be.

Since he was one of the Vollachian Empire's Nine Divine Generals, the empire's strongest existence, and someone treating him with such easy familiarity, maybe he'd listen to what Subaru had to say.
It was a truly self-serving, childish notion, nothing more than a passing thought.

And so, as always, Subaru's scheme swung and missed entirely.
Or rather, it was struck down by an obvious question that Subaru himself might have arrived at, if his thinking hadn't been dulled by the "Infantilization."
Namely—

"Now, listen, Bassoo."

"Uh, yeah, what?"

"What exactly are these 'Nine Divine Generals'?"

"Huh...?"

Still gripped by the shoulders, Cecilus tilted his head, puzzled.
Unable to process what he'd just been told, Subaru went pale with shock. To Subaru in that state, Cecilus continued, "Actually, you see—"

"—the truth is, I don't really understand why I'm even here myself. I figure the story's bound to move forward through some kind of development, and I have a feeling that development might just be you, Bassoo."

—was the bombshell remark he dropped, driving still more confusion into young Subaru's head.
