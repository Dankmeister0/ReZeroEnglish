Arc 5 Chapter 70: "Eating"

Batenkaitos, who should have been scraping away Felt's name and beginning his "meal," vomited instead.

There was no falsehood in the way "Gluttony" groaned in agony, bringing up stomach acid. He hadn't actually put anything with physical form into his mouth, and yet when he retched, was his stomach wrung out all the same?

Such a useless thought crossed her mind at the sight of this bizarre spectacle.

"Damn, that hurts... quit screwing around..."

Felt stood back up, covered in scrapes, rubbing at the chest that had been shoved. Her expression carried irritation and displeasure, but she showed no sign of any fatal injury.

Beatrice and the others, catching Felt at the edge of their vision, hadn't forgotten about her either.

Gluttony's meal had failed.

"Gah, geh, ohhh—!"

"I don't know the reason, but... this is our chance!"

Dynas launched a sudden assault at Batenkaitos, who was spilling stomach acid from the corner of his mouth, his awareness completely severed from the battlefield.

Twin blades flashed and were brought down on Batenkaitos without mercy.

The flash of the shortsword closed in on his defenseless throat—

"Gy—oooh!"

A voice like a beast's roar rose up, and Batenkaitos parried the blade with pure body technique.

The strands of hair too slow to evade were taken by the slash, but it failed to cut flesh. Spinning his small frame at high speed, "Gluttony" escaped the attack's range with nightmarish movement.

"Merchant!"

"I know, I know!!"

In response to Beatrice's call, Otto snapped his right arm out. Two magic stones thrown from his sleeve struck the Batenkaitos who'd dodged the first attack.

The instant light burst and the resulting torrent of mana blew his body back—but Batenkaitos countered this with astonishing reflexes.

"Hyuuma—!"

Just as the swelling light was about to swallow him in its destructive force, Batenkaitos activated magic, wrapping and freezing the exploding magic stones in ice.

The magic stones' explosion lost its outlet and simply became a lump of ice that clattered to the ground. It was a superlative technique—interrupting a colorless wave of mana with a spell cast at blinding speed.

That, too, was probably something accomplished by one of the "names" Batenkaitos had devoured so far. The thought that some "someone" who'd mastered such a technique had been sunk to the bottom of his stomach, unremembered by anyone, was enough to make her sick.

But such sentiments would have to wait.

What mattered here, now, was—

"Whew! That was close, close... but I weathered it."

Batenkaitos, having kicked the ice trapping the magic stones into the canal, laughed. He spat the remaining stomach acid from his mouth, wiped his lips with his arm, and tilted his head.

His clouded eyes settled on Felt, who was rotating her limbs to check the extent of her injuries. Noticing Batenkaitos looking at her, Felt snorted.

"What. I'm just as pissed at you as you are at me."

"Pissed doesn't even begin to cover it—I'm impressed, is what I am. Sorry for thinking, just from looking at you, that you'd be a bit dim upstairs."

"Ha? What are you even—"

"I never imagined you'd have the cunning to use a fake name on us. I was completely fooled. I try to avoid rushing to dig up a 'name'... but I never expected to get that turned against me."

"——"

Hearing Batenkaitos say "fake name," Felt fell silent. Her furrowed brow was the reaction of someone hearing something they never expected. It should be taken that she had no idea what Batenkaitos was talking about.

Meanwhile, having heard this exchange, Beatrice understood the cause behind Batenkaitos's failed "meal."

Batenkaitos was the holder of an Authority that let him touch someone whose "name" he knew and devour that "name" by whatever means—but it had to be their true, proper name.

A fake name, or something like a nickname, wouldn't function that way. Felt's name alone hadn't satisfied the conditions for a "meal," and so tasting it had left Batenkaitos in pain.

If that was the case, then—

"You over there, mister, and Felt-chan... two people whose names we don't know, and we've decided to eat both of you. That's a real pain."

Otto, whose name was genuinely unknown, and Felt, who apparently used a fake one. Dynas, whose name was already known, and Beatrice, known through Rem's memories, were no longer obstacles—and Gluttony's behavior toward that fact was grating, but if he insisted on calling himself a gourmet and clinging to devouring "names," there was an opening to exploit there.

"Oi! I've been listening quiet this whole time, but what the hell kind of accusation is that?"

While Beatrice pondered and Batenkaitos looked down on them, and Otto and Dynas waited for their chance, it was Felt who raised her voice.

Irritated at having a conclusion reached about her without her input, she thrust Meteor out and barked at Batenkaitos.

"Fake name, my ass. Don't screw with me. I've lived fifteen years now with the name Felt that Grandpa Rom gave me. Don't you dare joke that it's a lie."

"Ah, the type who isn't even aware herself that it's fake? Well, that just means whoever raised you did an extremely good job of it. Real troublesome for us, but... in other words, there's a name properly given to you before that one, isn't there."

"You mean the name my shitty parents gave me before dumping me in the back alley? Then it's probably 'Nuisance' or 'Waste of Food' or 'Trash.' Want to say that and take a bite, see how it tastes?"

"Random guesswork goes against a gourmet's principles. —Ah, right."

Showing his fang, Batenkaitos clapped his hands at Felt, who wore a grin of anger. He looked her up and down, then said,

"After we've eaten everyone but you, we'll keep you nice and safe. And then we'll go pay a visit to Grandpa Rom, the one who gave you a fake name. Grandpa Rom might know your real name. We're good at getting people to tell us what they know. Leave it to us."

"...Going through all that trouble. Is 'giving up' not an option?"

Otto couldn't help but interject at "Gluttony's" hideous plan. Hearing this, Batenkaitos put a hand to his mouth and chuckled with pleasure.

"If the number of lives in this world is finite, then the number among them worth savoring is finite too. So we're not about to miss a chance to meet with limited delicacies. Overindulge! Overeat! Lick and suck and slurp it down, scrape the plate clean and savor even the last drop of sauce. Oh, and don't worry, mister—you're no exception either, of course."

Batenkaitos's gaze was fixed on not letting any of the four people remaining here escape.

Gluttony's obsession with eating was incomprehensible to Beatrice and the others, who had been judged worthy of being called a "delicacy." It meant nothing more than having been marked by a tenacious nuisance. And that blasphemer's hunger had thoroughly earned Felt's displeasure.

"That so. So if we don't do something here, you're gonna go after Grandpa Rom too."

Speaking in a quiet voice, Felt gave her leg a shake. The shoe she'd been wearing slipped off, and she did the same with the other foot. Standing on the cobblestones with both feet bare, she let Meteor roll to the side and drew a dagger.

"—? I don't get it, Felt-chan. Isn't that your trump card?"

"Better to use something I'm used to than rely on a tool I'm not. It's not something I'm all that attached to anyway. Best to let whoever can use a tool use whatever's easiest to use, that's all!"

Her bare feet curled as if gripping the ground, and the next instant Felt's body was launched forward as if shot from a bow. The speed at which she closed the distance in the blink of an eye was like the wind itself.

Even Batenkaitos's eyes went wide at this, forgetting his composed attitude and responding to the swung blade in earnest. Swinging his arms, twisting his body, dredging up body technique sunk deep in memory to fight back.

"Hraah!"

Barefoot Felt's speed surpassed what a mere agile girl's body should be able to produce. There was no mistaking it—she carried the support of a power beyond human wisdom, that is, a Divine Protection. Her dagger flashed again and again, holding her own reasonably well against Batenkaitos's dagger technique.

Of course, the overwhelming advantage in skill lay with Batenkaitos, but the reason Felt wasn't simply overwhelmed was Dynas's support.

"Don't push yourself too hard with that precious body of yours!"

"You're the one who's noisy, old man, don't fall behind!"

Skillfully wielding his twin blades, Dynas somehow managed to plug the gaps in Gluttony's counterattacks. Meanwhile Felt circled into Gluttony's blind spot for a sudden strike, and the sound of steel meeting steel rang out as the three shadows grappled, scattering sparks.

Once again, a melee lacking a decisive blow.

Except this time, the decisive blow was being carefully prepared just outside the melee.

"The spell... it's through, time to let it rip, I suppose!"

"Both of you, please get clear!"

Left out of the fray, she'd spent a long, careful stretch of time assembling a single spell. A task that normally wouldn't require this much struggle now demanded meticulous care, thanks to an extra filter interposed on it.

Having invested that much effort, the result was finally taking shape.

"——"

Following Otto's cry, Felt and Dynas pulled away from Batenkaitos's side. "Gluttony" reflexively reached for Felt, but even if he touched her, he wasn't prepared to devour Felt's "name."

"Let, go!"

Shaking off the ankle gripped roughly, Felt leapt backward on one leg. Dynas rolled clear of the spot too, leaving only "Gluttony" within Beatrice's line of fire.

Aiming right at him, Beatrice completed a spell requiring the power of "a thousand" to activate, having properly channeled the full power of "a thousand" through it.

"This time it's no joke, this is for real... El Minya!!"

In response to the incantation, purple light surged forth, tracing a circle of light centered on Batenkaitos. He looked up, wondering what was happening, but that reaction came too late.

The correct move wasn't to brace himself—it was to flee, form be damned.

"—!"

The ring of light converged all at once, pinning Batenkaitos's torso, arms included, within its bounds. With his upper body's movement restrained, "Gluttony" found the ring of light chaining further, closing in to seal him completely.

If his whole body were wrapped in the ring of light like this, there would be no escaping El Minya's power.

The crystallizing purple light bound Batenkaitos's upper body piece by piece. The ring's dominion extended down to his lower body as well, and "Gluttony," sensing he could no longer move, collapsed on the spot.

Then, with a sound like the very air groaning, a mighty violet-indigo radiance rose above the fallen blasphemer's head, its tip aimed straight at Batenkaitos.

The power of El Minya, to bind and crush.

Beatrice, urgently pouring in every technique she possessed to make it happen, sent the resulting destruction raining down on Batenkaitos.

"——!"

A muffled roar could be heard, but it was drowned out before the destructive purple light. The cobblestones peeled up under the overwhelming force of the light, the resulting blast wrapping the great plaza in light and smoke, Beatrice's dress hem billowing wildly.

"Did we get him!?"
"Did we do it!?"
"Did we get him!?"

Buffeted by the blast, the three who'd ducked down cried out at the same time.

Right at the center of the blast, there had been no way for Batenkaitos there to evade. If he'd taken that blow full-on, his body should have vanished without even leaving a bone fragment behind—

"It's not over yet, I suppose!"

—Yes, that is, if it had hit him full-on.

Beatrice's voice rang out in warning, and the color drained from the faces of the three who'd been cheering. It wasn't difficult for Beatrice to notice the mistake before the other three.

It was the feel of it.

"—Four left."

The large magic stone she'd kept tucked in her clothes had shattered under the strain of the El Minya spell just now, unable to withstand it, breaking apart a hair's breadth before finishing off Batenkaitos.

She'd managed to carry it through to activation, but it vanished at a position insufficient for its full effect. The radiance failed to reach far enough to burn Batenkaitos to nothing, and "Gluttony's" body—

"That one had me a little worried, actually!"

"—!"

Bursting through the smoke, Batenkaitos lunged at Beatrice from a low crouch. Judging from the power of the spell just now, he'd apparently decided who needed to be eliminated first and fastest.

Beatrice, with her exceptional skill as a mage, nonetheless had the bodily coordination of an ordinary person—true to her appearance, she hadn't outgrown the limits of a young girl.

She lacked the ability to engage in close combat with someone who wielded a master's body technique like Batenkaitos. And so, facing this close encounter, Beatrice immediately made use of her third magic stone.

"—Mrak!"

"A little trick won't—"

Just before Batenkaitos's hand could reach her, Beatrice's incantation barely managed to strike first.

His outstretched fingers were driven by an intent that would not let Beatrice escape no matter what interference arose. But that intent, too, missed its mark once again.

Just as his fingertips seemed to graze her dress, Beatrice's body was blown straight backward, like a leaf pushed by the wind.

"——"

The "Mrak" Beatrice had chanted was a shadow-magic spell that interfered with gravity. It was magic that interfered with the pull toward the ground, or one's own body weight—and Beatrice used it to instantaneously reduce her own weight to absolute zero.

Enough that she floated on the wind, deflected wide by the fingers trying to touch her.

"Wh-, this—!?"

Just as planned, Beatrice's body left Batenkaitos and flew all the way to the edge of the great plaza in one motion. Batenkaitos tried to chase after her, but upon hearing an exaggerated footstep behind him, he reflexively spun around toward it.

He thrust his dagger backward, meaning to cut down the graceless intruder. But the strike cut only air. Because there was no person there where the footstep had sounded, and—

"Garfiel or the 'Bowel Hunter' or people like that, it's always the same type that falls for this, isn't it!"

Using an application of wind magic, Otto—who'd "thrown" the footstep sound—hurled yet another magic stone at Batenkaitos, whose back was now turned. The heat wave of the exploding magic stone struck his exposed back, and this time the blast wasn't blocked, sending Batenkaitos flying.

"This time it's really over!"

Batenkaitos tumbled across the plaza, sprawled out and collapsed. Dynas lunged at that figure, twin blades reversed in his grip, meaning to deliver the finishing blow—

"——"

The fallen boy murmured something under his breath.

Whether it was a plea for mercy or words of regret, Dynas wouldn't hesitate. For a man who'd lived the mercenary trade, the taking of life was the outcome of a severe, competitive struggle. Whether the other party was an adult or a child was a trivial matter to him; mourning and regret were sentiments that could only be indulged in after one had survived.

So he'd made his peace with it. And though there was no hesitation in the movements of a Dynas who had made his peace, he couldn't help but feel a stab of confusion.

Because Batenkaitos's murmur just now had sounded like this.

—Lunar eclipse, it sounded like.

"—Oh?"

The instant he registered the correct sound of it, a foolish sound escaped him.

The next moment, all four of Dynas's limbs, as he swung his blade down, sprayed blood at once. Deep wounds from a dagger driven into each limb surfaced, the tendons precisely gouged out.

Which meant the complete loss of function in all four limbs, and he couldn't stop his body from crumpling.

"Guh, ah!?"

Dynas fell face-first onto the cobblestones, and his head was stomped down on hard from directly above. His nose crushed against the stone, his consciousness blown away by the shock.

Dynas went still, face-down; kicking that body aside, Batenkaitos rose and slowly turned to face Otto.

"...Ah."

This wasn't the first time their eyes had met, those clouded eyes of his.

And yet Otto's mind was instantly ensnared by that clouded gaze.

Because the swirling madness and rancor within it was, compared to before, a shade of black entirely different in degree.

"——"

It was over in an instant.

The distance closed in the blink of an eye, and by the time he noticed, searing heat had run through both of Otto's legs. Looking down, he saw the dagger had carved cross-shaped wounds into the front of both his thighs.

Like peeling the skin off fruit, his trousers and the skin beneath were peeled back in one smooth motion. The red cross-section beneath the skin, the pink muscle, and within it, the white nerves and bone crawling through—the green blood vessels—had all been extracted without a single one being damaged, and Otto's throat caught with a wildly inappropriate admiration.

He was dumbstruck. He'd never laid eyes on a technique this beautiful.

Minimal bleeding—no, there was no bleeding at all. Truly masterful, practiced handling of a blade could destroy the human body this exquisitely.

"——"

Crouching down, Batenkaitos kissed the wound. The sensation of a rough tongue licked over every important part of Otto's legs that had lain beneath the skin.

Muscle, bone, blood vessels, nerves—the sensation of them being licked sent a shudder through him, and the next instant, both visually and tactilely, unbearable revulsion and searing pain sent Otto's brain boiling over.

"Ah, gyaaaaaaah—!?"

There was no blood. It made no sense.

There was only pain. As if in place of a spray of blood, exposed bone and nerve were caressed by damp wind, and agony as if muscle were being meticulously peeled apart with needles.

His vision flickered, his brain seemed to burst. The organ meant to comprehend pain refused that comprehension. His screaming throat trembled as if coughing up blood, and with legs that wouldn't move, he couldn't even writhe.

As Otto screamed on and on, Batenkaitos, looking down at him, tilted his head. His long dark brown hair slid off his shoulder, and "Gluttony" gave a weary-sounding sigh.

"I thought this'd be time to rest after eating, and look at this mess. Gourmet, gluttonous, whatever, who cares... honestly, no one but us has any idea what a meal really is."

Gone now was the mad grin and manner from before; his voice carried a distinctly detached quality. Batenkaitos slowly shook his head, a gesture almost like self-mockery—and then, just as suddenly, his expression flipped.

"Don't talk like that. Sure, it got a little annoying messing around, but we still found a meal Rui would love, didn't we."

Baring his fangs, Batenkaitos turned his gaze toward Beatrice and the others. At that gaze, and at Otto's wretched state, the two girls couldn't help but catch their breath. But seeing their reaction, Batenkaitos's expression shifted again, into something hollow and listless.

"It's not bad, I suppose... but securing the Vessel matters more than the contents. Besides, we still haven't finished reading the Gospel."

"Rui might not be able to see it, but the kid inside us is telling us. That one over there, the Beatrice-sama, is the one, probably. If we go all the way, we'll be fulfilled body and soul—what a perfect opportunity!"

Looking right and arguing, looking left and arguing, Batenkaitos held a self-dialogue not within his own mind but in a form clearly visible from outside. It was exactly like watching him converse with some other being residing within himself.

No—in fact, that possibility was real. Within Lye Batenkaitos, the blasphemer who devoured "names," lay countless souls. If so, then exchanging words, even holding council, with any one of them might well be possible. And so this ghastly self-dialogue, too, became something one could understand.

"Can you move, shorty?"

"Ah? You're the one who better not be scared, shorty."

Trading insults, Beatrice and Felt communicated. Peering into each other's eyes, they confirmed neither one's will to fight had broken.

Felt gave a small snort, then jerked her chin to indicate a corner of the great plaza. Beatrice checked what lay scattered there and grasped her meaning.

"...Those two will come after Betty next. I'll hold them off, I suppose."

"You think you can stop 'em? If it's about disruption, I'm better at—"

"I can't very well count on him vomiting like an idiot twice in a row, I suppose. And besides, no matter how you slice it, the decisive move is something Betty can't handle. It has to be you."

At Beatrice's proposal, Felt looked thoughtful for a moment. But with her brow furrowed, she soon tilted her head, scratched at her blond hair, and let out an "Argh!"

Then, aiming a fist at Beatrice, she said,

"Don't screw it up, shorty."

"You too, shorty."

Without responding to the fist directed at her, they simply exchanged insults before heading into the decisive battle.

Beatrice and Felt's discussion ended at nearly the same moment Batenkaitos's self-dialogue did. The combat power that had cut down Dynas and Otto—if that were unleashed in full, Beatrice had no confidence she could clash with it head-on.

"So, are you ready, Beatrice-samaaa?"

"If saying 'no' would buy me some time, I'd say it, I suppose. But if it wouldn't, there's no point in the question, I suppose."

"That's exactly right. Well then—here goes, itadakimasu!"

Head-on, Batenkaitos flew straight at Beatrice. His speed wasn't quite the nightmarish threat of before. Even so, it remained more than enough of a threat to Beatrice. Her disadvantage in close combat hadn't changed.

So the true strength of a shadow-mage lay in not clashing head-on.

"Heeere—!"

In front of Beatrice, Batenkaitos planted his hands on the ground and spun his body vertically. A downward heel closed in from directly above Beatrice, a sharp blow aiming to plant itself in the crown of the girl's skull.

"That won't work, I suppose."

—Just before it landed, Beatrice's body once again tilted backward from the kick's wind pressure. The result of leaving the effect of the "Mrak" she'd cast earlier active, uncanceled.

Tilted back by the pressure of the wind, Beatrice leapt high straight upward on the spot. Freed from gravity, with the yoke of body weight gone, the girl's body rose with astonishing ease.

Deftly flipping the hem of her dress, Beatrice's body danced through the air erratically, riding the wind.

"Well done! But your follow-through's too soft!"

Batenkaitos, tongue extended, circled around to her landing point and, without waiting for her to touch down, lunged at Beatrice suspended midair. With the speed and precision of a bird of prey seizing quarry, his fingertips reached for her. But that was, at the same time, proof that he too had risen into the air, with nowhere left to escape.

With her remaining spell ammunition limited, landing the hit was the paramount concern. Beatrice turned her palm toward Batenkaitos closing in from directly below and chanted the spell she'd used most in her four hundred years of life, and especially this past year.

Namely—

"Shamak!!"

The magic stone shattered within her clothes, and in response to Beatrice's incantation, black mist erupted—the leaping Batenkaitos plunged headfirst into it and was sealed within a world of no understanding.

"Mmgh—!?"

The black mist clung to him, and Batenkaitos's body dropped defenselessly onto the cobblestones. It should have left him helpless until he shook it off, but Shamak's effect didn't last that long.

With Beatrice's current hand—a single usable magic stone left—she couldn't deal a fatal blow. So what option should Beatrice choose in this moment?

"Ahh! Well done, Beatrice-sama. Fighting just like that person, are we... could it be you've been influenced!?"

Shaking off the Shamak, Batenkaitos spun around, baring his fangs. He swept his gaze across the plaza and, upon fixing on Beatrice, made that pronouncement.

In the memories of the girl within him, there should be no scene of Beatrice standing shoulder to shoulder with Subaru. So he couldn't grasp how significant it was to see Subaru's influence in the way Beatrice fought so hard here.

"There now, one final splurge with my last magic stone, I suppose!"

Casting off sentiment, Beatrice turned her palm toward Otto, writhing at her feet. Using the mana of her last magic stone, she sent a wave of healing into the wounds on Otto's writhing legs. Full recovery was still a distant hope, but even so, the unbearable pain should have receded. Otto, who had been rolling on the ground in tears, coughed hard, mixed with sobs.

"What's the use reviving that useless thing now?"

"This is the use, I suppose!"

Beatrice shouted back at Batenkaitos, who mocked her for wasting a move.

The instant his brow furrowed at that retort, something bit into Batenkaitos's leg from behind. His left leg had fangs sunk deep into it, throwing off his balance. He looked down reflexively at his own leg, and his eyes went wide at what he saw.

"Hah!?"

Astonishment at something incomprehensible—there, was a blood-soaked Water Dragon.

Stretching its neck, having charged full-tilt across the cobblestones, the Water Dragon had sunk its fangs into Batenkaitos. Once driven to the point of incapacitation, it fought on now with its last stubborn resolve.

That was the use of the third of five magic stones.

The first: the powerful but ultimately unsuccessful El Minya. The second: used for the emergency evasion of Mrak. And with the third magic stone, she'd healed the dying Water Dragon that had lain at the point where Mrak had deflected her.

The fourth: unleashing Shamak. The fifth: pushing back Otto's pain.

That was Beatrice's five moves, the way she'd spent her magic stones to grasp at victory.

"—Aaah! Hurts, it hurts, it hurts!"

Screaming until her throat went hoarse, Otto—who had hidden a call to the Water Dragon within her scream—having fulfilled her role, now truly wailed for her own pain's sake.

That she grasped what was needed the instant after receiving Beatrice's treatment spoke to how genuinely excellent Otto was. No one but her could serve as the Emilia Camp's minister of domestic affairs, reluctantly dragged into battle again and again.

"Well done, I suppose, a natural calling!"

"I don't know what that is but I'm not happy about it!"

Bathed in Beatrice's rare praise, tearful Otto shot back with that. And before the two girls' eyes, Batenkaitos, his leg seized by the Water Dragon's fangs, was dragged down to the ground, somehow managing to pry those fangs loose and rise to his feet.

But all these reactions came too late against the trump card.

"—All set. Nicely done stalling, shorty."

A triumphant voice rang out, and with a hard sound, the butt of Meteor struck the ground. Bracing the staff at her hip, Felt aimed its tip at Batenkaitos. In her hands, Meteor took on a faint glow, its aftershock blowing away its wrapping.

When the white cloth came off, what was revealed was a slender, pure-white staff. The length of its shaft was long enough to pass for a spear, with no elaborate design, nor any eye-catching mechanism built into it.

Its construction, dedicated wholly to practicality, could be said to reflect the spirit of its creator—

The very spirit of the "Witch" Echidna, who sought no value in a tool beyond its function as a tool.

"Mother..."

Beatrice had never actually seen Echidna wield that staff. Even so, she knew the purpose for which it had been made, and its power.

Harassment aimed at the Dragon god Volcanica—a weapon capable of interfering with the Dragon god, that's what it was.

That said, there were several conditions for its use. Between the difficulty of satisfying those conditions and the issue of the user, bringing out the full extent of its bottomless specs was harsh—but,

"Now then, taste the power that even works on Reinhard-san!"

With the conditions met, and with Felt—her mana at full tank—as the user, the expected output was more than sufficient.

Drawing steadily and greedily on the mana of the one gripping it, Meteor stored up power without limit, the light gathering at its tip locking onto Batenkaitos.

"—Guh"

Even Batenkaitos couldn't afford composure in the face of that power. Sensing the possibility of a fatal blow, he immediately struck the Water Dragon's snout, still restraining his leg, with a single blow of his dagger. The instant its fangs loosened, he yanked his leg free and leapt back, bearing a fresh gash.

At that same instant, Meteor flashed with especially sharp intensity.

"Go——!!"

Light swelled at Meteor's tip, and white radiance was loosed at Batenkaitos.

Batenkaitos, having reflexively escaped the Water Dragon's grip, rolled out of the beam's line of fire with that injured leg as best he could. The light, missing its mark, was on a collision course with the Water Dragon instead—but just before impact, its trajectory bent. Tracing a complex path, the light pursued Batenkaitos.

"Wha——!?"

Batenkaitos cried out at the beam of light chasing after him even as he fled. With sharp movement and leaps, he tried to break from the pursuing beam's path.

But it was useless. No matter how he fled, rolled, or leapt, the light drew an arc, drew a circle, and pursued, aiming for a direct hit.

That was the greatest strength of Echidna's magical weapon, "Meteor": a permanent tracking function against any target it had locked onto.

A weapon Echidna made for the sake of "harassing" the Dragon god Volcanica. If the meticulous Echidna went all out, seriously crafting a tool for the sole purpose of "harassment," of course it would come out uncompromising toward that purpose.

And so that Magic Vessel had turned into a weapon that never let its target escape, never missed, and always connected.

"Ngh... then how about this!?"

Fed up with the endless pursuit of the light he could never outrun, Batenkaitos struck back in retaliation. His magical power surged, and the area around him froze over.

Multiple ice pillars rose, aiming their sharp points at the beam of light, a storm of barrage hurtling into the white radiance—but that resistance was a mistake.

Just before striking the white light, the ice pillars' tips were reduced back to mana, shattering to fragments before ever landing a hit, swallowed by the beam. Not only that, the beam absorbed every intercepting spell that came at it, growing ever more powerful and vast as it closed on its target.

"Damn it, this... this—!"

Rolling, somehow escaping the trajectory, Batenkaitos spat curses. But the wound on his left leg was deep, and it wouldn't allow a fully capable leap.

If he could have moved with more agility, he might have been able to lure the beam toward Beatrice and the others, or aim it at Felt herself, but he had no such reserve of strength left.

Eventually the beam of light circled the rolling Batenkaitos, sealing off any avenue of escape, and slowly, as if toying with him, began pressing "Gluttony's" body into a force of destruction—

"For something this stupid, we're—we're—!"
"Stop whining. Just cut the solar eclipse already, quickly."

At the moment of direct impact, Batenkaitos let out a pitiful voice, which was then overwritten by another, far colder one. And then the light exploded.

"——"

Blinding white light swelled at the center of the great plaza, producing the largest crater yet. The swollen light painted the world white, and the painted-over portion seemed to vanish as if stripped of all color.

The great plaza was gouged out into a round, spherical shape, and water flowed into the crevice connecting to the canal.

But—

"Oh dear, honestly. Having such a poorly made sibling really is a pain."

Beside that scene of devastation, there was a shadow peering down into the canal.

A figure with long, dark brown hair and a body covered in wounds. There was no need to state the physical features—it was none other than the Lye Batenkaitos she'd been fighting.

By what method had he evaded that beam's attack? —But that wasn't the most surprising thing about the scene.

"What is going on, I suppose."

Beatrice's muttering wasn't surprise that the attack had missed. To begin with, this wasn't a question of the attack hitting or missing at all. The beam should only ever strike Batenkaitos—it wouldn't hit anyone but Batenkaitos.

So it was only natural that it wouldn't hit the muscular, broad-shouldered man now standing there with his back turned to them. The problem was where on earth that man had come from.

"Is that... 'Gluttony'?"

Otto, lifting his head with difficulty, seeing the same thing, let that slip out.

She wanted to deny it, but Beatrice had no words for that right now. At the gaze of the silent group, the large man suddenly turned around.

There stood a man with a rugged face bearing no resemblance whatsoever to Batenkaitos. He looked to be nearing forty, with not the slightest overlap with that blasphemer. Before Beatrice's narrowed eyes, the man put a hand to his own chin and said,

"No need to look so puzzled. We simply did this."

—speaking in a feminine manner that betrayed his appearance, the man gave his own long hair a light chop with the edge of his hand. As the hair scattered and fell, Beatrice understood how the beam had been evaded.

Meteor's tracking held until a judgment of "hit" was registered. Given that power, even a hit to a fragment of the body would be enough to engulf the entire target. That was the very point that had been turned against her.

The man—presumably Batenkaitos—had severed his own hair, tricking the beam into perceiving it as "contact with the body." Then, he'd fled the attack's range with everything he had, escaping harm.

It was a gambit that might well have failed to work, but this time, at least, it was a perfect answer.

To begin with, in order for Meteor to lock onto a target, it needed a "lock-on" to identify that target. Connecting to the target's Od, or Gate, was the best method—but in this case, as an emergency measure, they'd used the strand of Batenkaitos's hair that had fallen during the battle. Because of that, the beam had been diverted along with the decoy hair.

"——"

Having worked all that out, Beatrice gritted her teeth at the poor state of things.

She'd never imagined her trump card, Meteor, could be evaded by such a method. Her magic stones were spent; what remained on hand was for personal use, one she wanted to keep in reserve for Puck. Otto and the other men couldn't fight, and Felt, having had most of her mana drained by Meteor, had also crumpled to the ground, breathing hard.

It's all over—that thought began to surface, but Beatrice shook her head.

Accepting defeat should be reserved for after death. Just as Subaru found a path forward even in the most hopeless of situations, she had to do the same.

So Beatrice lifted her chin sharply and glared at the man.

At that gaze, Batenkaitos's eyes went round. Then he put one hand on his hip, covered his face with the other palm, and said—

"Fine, fine, that's fine, fine, isn't that fine, isn't that just fine, precisely because it's so fine... we, too, us too, find value in 'eating' you."

"—!"

Before Beatrice could refute the nonsense being uttered, a change came over Batenkaitos's body. Bones twisted with a cracking sound, blood spurted out painfully, and the large man's body shrank.

Blood poured out in great quantity from newly opened wounds, and breathing heavily was Batenkaitos, restored to the form of a boy. In that battered, wounded state, he nonetheless wore a manic grin. Making a low sound in his throat as he looked at them, "Gluttony" spread both arms wide, seemingly pleased.

"Our name is Rui Arneb, Witch Cult Sin Archbishop in charge of 'Gluttony.'"

"Rui...?"

Lye Batenkaitos—that should have been his name.

Unable to grasp the intent behind suddenly claiming a different name, Beatrice's brow furrowed. And through the gap in her confusion, Batenkaitos kicked hard off the ground with just his right leg.

Beatrice tensed, bracing for what was coming, but "Gluttony" leapt to the edge of the plaza, retrieved some rags lying there, and wrapped them around himself as if to hide his battered skin. On top of that—

"Sorry, but that's it for today. Both Lye and Roy are too worn out. Any more and it'll interfere with the birthing. See you again, cute little missy."

"—! You think I'm letting you escape, I suppose!"

"Don't act tough now. This body can't handle the 'Sloth' properly, but even so it could wipe you all out. The reason we won't is because the table isn't set yet."

As Beatrice made to step forward, Batenkaitos pointed a finger and shook his head. A deeply feminine gesture—no, in fact, perhaps this Batenkaitos really was a woman right now. Something incomprehensible was happening at the very core of his being.

To Beatrice, whose feet stalled from revulsion and wariness, Batenkaitos nodded.

"Gourmet Lye, gluttonous Roy—neither of them understand a thing. Because that's how it is. A meal isn't about 'what you eat.' It's about 'who you eat with.'"

"——"

"See you. Next time, be sure to bring the one who matters most to you."

"Wai—"

Before she could even finish calling out "wait," Batenkaitos slipped into the shadows of the great plaza and vanished. Chasing after him wasn't something Beatrice could do, surrounded by the wounded as she was. Pursuing him too far, only to be drawn into a situation favorable to "Gluttony," would be reckless as well.

Once her trump card, Meteor, had been dodged, there was no other way this could have ended.

"...Outplayed, I suppose."

Holding back the urge to click her tongue, Beatrice surveyed her surroundings.

Otto's consciousness was hazy with pain; the mercenaries and Felt's retainer had passed out, unconscious. Felt looked frustrated, but seemed about to collapse at any moment.

And Beatrice herself was no exception to that.

Should she consider it a result of her having answered Kiritaka's desperate plea and joined this battlefield, that they'd at least managed to avoid any deaths?

In any case—

"Doesn't seem like I'll get to hold my head high and have Subaru carry me, I suppose..."

Within the prey that had gotten away—Lye Batenkaitos—a girl's soul slept.

Having gained certainty of that fact, she now had to decide how to convey it to Subaru.

Carrying that heavy, oppressive worry with her, Beatrice turned her steps toward the still-dazed Felt to speak to her.

Ironically, with the Sin Archbishop's withdrawal, the fighting on this battlefield too came to an end—

And the battle for the Water Gate City had, at last, only a little left to go.
