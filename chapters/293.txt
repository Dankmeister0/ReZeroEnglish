Arc 4 Chapter 123B: "The Happiness Reflected on the Water's Surface"

This time it's a simultaneous update. This is Emilia's side. Route B.

―――

She drew a breath and pressed once more into the tomb she had only just left.

Inside the stone-built tomb, where even moonlight was shut out, the only source of light was the faint, pale blue glow of the walls. In places where mana tends to pool in the air, such natural luminescence isn't an uncommon way of securing visibility.

That said, it was rare for an artificial structure to glow on its own like this — more likely, the effect was being reproduced theoretically through some architectural mechanism.

A mechanism that could be used so long as the necessary mana was supplied, much like a Magic Vessel — the glow of the tomb felt as though it worked on similar logic, and Emilia let out a quiet breath.

Inside this tomb, she felt the presence of the minor spirits somehow distant.

It wasn't that they didn't exist. To begin with, minor spirits were like the mana in the air — present everywhere. Whether one could sense them, or whether they had grown strong enough to be sensed, might differ, but there was no place entirely devoid of them.

What made the tomb feel strange had to do with the mechanism applied to the walls' luminescence.

The environment of this tomb restricted the flow of mana between its interior and exterior to a fairly high degree. It was arranged so the amount of mana inside stayed fixed — engineered never to rise above or fall below a set quantity.

The amount of mana needed to sustain the walls' glow was so minute it could barely even sustain minor spirits — which was why their presence felt so thin inside the tomb. Even the ones that were there could probably only grow into the faintest of powers in an environment like this.

"A really nasty environment, for a spirit user."

Emilia muttered the conclusion under her breath, almost complaining.

Perhaps it was because, having overcome the first Trial, she'd gained some sense of release from the feeling of being cornered. Only now, with the first stirrings of enough composure to look around her, did Emilia form that impression of the tomb.

For a mage who stored mana within herself and drew on that, it wasn't much of a threat. Still, once that stored amount ran dry there was no way to replenish it, so for a mage with few Gates to begin with, it would remain a troublesome place all the same.

Though for someone like the current Emilia, or Roswaal, the effect on combat would be almost nonexistent.

"Come to think of it, it's strange... I can barely sense anyone outside, even though they should all be there."

Having regained her power as a mage, Emilia's sense for perceiving mana had sharpened. Outside the tomb, she'd felt the presence of so much mana it was nearly beyond her control.

Likely she'd been picking up on the mana — or rather the Od — of nearly every living thing that brushed her senses. The strain on her nerves had been extraordinary; she needed to learn to control it soon.

In any case, that silent struggle was put on hold, at least for now, once she stepped inside the tomb.

Though in its place waited the ordeal called the Trial, so which was the lesser evil, who could say.

"Ram asked me, after all, just like that. I have to do this properly."

She recalled the sight of Ram bowing her head to Emilia, pleading for the very first time.

Ram, who never once let her weaknesses show, had bared her feelings that openly. If Emilia didn't answer that, how could she ever repay all the days that had come before?

There was also the trust from Subaru, who they said had gone back to the mansion without even waiting to see how things turned out for her.

It was something he'd done because he believed, without a shred of doubt, that Emilia could do it. She had to answer that trust of his. Or rather — she had to do even better than he imagined, and surprise him.

"I'm happy he trusts me, but that's a separate matter from this."

Even if he'd been in a hurry, she had a thing or two to say about him leaving without even showing his face. Surely she was allowed to sulk a little, to make him fret.

Especially with Subaru — there was something even more important she needed to talk to him about, from here on.

"Besides, this air... the Trial — it's here."

The moment she'd entered the tomb, Emilia had felt it on her skin.

She'd been half in doubt whether the Trial could be readied again after just one round trip, but the air drifting through the building — cool to the point of being almost too cool — told her plainly that it was.

There was no need to put it off.

In the Trial chamber, the second Trial was waiting for Emilia.

"I saw the past. So then, what's next...?"

Wondering what awaited her, Emilia slapped her cheeks as they threatened to stiffen, and rubbed her stomach once.

She checked whether her nerve was steady by the evenness of her breath. Within tolerance — time to step in.

—Of course, the Trial chamber greeted Emilia looking exactly as it had before.

Naturally so, since less than an hour had passed since she'd left it.

As always, this room alone seemed to hold a higher concentration of mana, giving slightly better visibility than the corridor did. At the back of the small chamber, the door that showed no sign of moving stood there still.

When she finally reached that door, having overcome all three Trials, what would be waiting there?

It was just as she thought of that—

'—Behold the present that should not be.'

"—!"

She heard it.

Whispered close to her ear, it was unmistakably her own voice.

The instant she tried to think about what those words — the present that should not be — meant, her consciousness went white.

A violent sensation tore her consciousness and soul alone away from her body, dragging her into some other world that was not here.

Unable to resist, Emilia slumped against the wall and crumpled, eventually collapsing to the floor.

Her vision blurred until she could think of nothing at all, and her consciousness sank.

"Subaru."

Not even knowing what her own lips had said last, the Trial began.

* * *

"Come now, Lia. Where are you off to? Come here."

Stopped by the gentle voice, Emilia turned around.

The one beckoning her to the table was a woman with short-cropped silver hair. Her sharp, unfriendly eyes. The softness of her voice. In every respect, she was Emilia's ideal of a woman.

"Fortuna, Mother..."

"...? Are you still half-asleep? You must have stayed up late again. You're not a child anymore, so stop doing things that cause so much trouble."

Coming closer, Fortuna poked Emilia's forehead with a finger in a scolding tone.

Pressing a hand to the faint redness left there, Emilia's eyes went wide.

"Waah."

An exclamation of wonder slipped out before she could stop it. That was how much the sight before her struck Emilia's heart.

It was the first time she'd ever seen Fortuna sacrifice ease of movement to wear an apron. The frilly white apron, decorated almost to excess, suited her beautiful looks well, whatever one thought of her personality.

"Mother, you're so cute."

"—! What are you suddenly saying, silly girl. You really do seem half-asleep."

Her cheeks coloring just slightly, Fortuna gripped Emilia's shoulders and turned her back around. Then, pushing her forward,

"Go wash your face at the water's edge. Cold water will wake you up, and you'll stop saying strange things like just now. Though in Lia's case, that might not change even once you're fully awake."

"W-what are you saying, Mother. That's not true. I'm not half-asleep at all... and besides, I've never once said anything strange, not a bit."

"Where do you even pick up old-fashioned phrasing like 'not a bit'? I'm really worried everyone's been filling your head with things as a joke. I'll have to grill Archi about it later."

Emilia pouted in displeasure, but Fortuna only nodded with an exasperated look, refusing to take her seriously. Stunned that her own opinion carried no weight, Emilia dropped her shoulders and trudged off toward the water's edge.

"Oh, morning, Emilia. You look glum."

"Ha, it's true. You must have made Lady Fortuna angry, hm? Staying up too late, I bet."

"Well, Emilia's at that age. Sometimes you want time to yourself for all sorts of things, don't you~"

On her way from the house to the water's edge, the elves of the settlement called out to her.

Gathered around a table ringed by thick tree roots, chatting away, was a knot of elders. She'd heard they were around the same age as Fortuna, but every one of them, Fortuna included, looked youthful to Emilia.

"Good morning. You're all up early."

"It's Emilia who's late. Helping your father with his work is all well and good, but if you don't spend some time on yourself, you'll waste your youth."

"That's right, that's right. Emilia's so cute, after all — you have to make good use of that cute time and enjoy yourself."

"If I were as cute and young as Emilia, I'd be swapping them out one after another."

Emilia tilted her head at the sound of "swapping them out," while the women looked at each other and laughed, crying "oh stop!" She didn't quite follow what they meant, but it was good enough that everyone seemed to be enjoying themselves.

Somehow it made her happy too, and Emilia's own expression loosened.

"See, that's much better than a sulky face. A smile, a smile, that's the way."

"—Right."

Pointing at the smiling Emilia, the women then pushed their own cheeks into the shape of a smile with their fingers.

Thinking they had a point, Emilia made the same kind of smile and nodded.

Waving goodbye, Emilia set off again on the path to the water's edge.

She climbed over winding tree roots and slipped through gaps in the green leaves. As she did, the babble of water reached her from somewhere, and her face brightened as she broke into a trot.

"Arri— ival!"

"Whoa! Emilia?!"

The moment she lifted a branch and poked her face through, someone drying off right in front of her jumped in surprise. The young man, half-turning around, realized it was Emilia who'd burst in and blinked in a fluster—

"Ah."

"Ahh."

Right before Emilia, who'd put a hand to her mouth, his foot slipped and he tumbled headfirst into the river.

A loud splash rang out as the young man hit the water, sending up a spray.

"Archi! Are you all right?"

From the rise above where he'd fallen, Emilia looked down and called out.

For a moment only bubbles kept rising to the surface, one after another, before the blond young man finally bobbed up. Wiping his face with a hand, he raised it toward Emilia looking down at him,

"Hey, Emilia! Don't go interrupting a guy right when he's about to finish bathing!"

"Sorry. I never thought anyone would be here... but, I'm glad it was you, Archi."

"What's that supposed to mean?!"

At Emilia, sighing in relief, Archi raised his voice as though railing against some injustice.

At his shout, Emilia put a finger to her lips and made a small "hmm" sound in her throat.

"Well, since it's you, Archi, we're friends, so I figured you'd forgive me, right?"

"Ngh..."

"I've always thought of you like a big brother, Archi... so I'm sure you'll forgive me for today too, thinking there's no helping it."

"What do you mean, 'you're sure'... Damn it... you have no idea how I feel..."

Emilia puffed out her chest at logic that was no logic at all. Subjected to the Emilia Theory, Archi groaned in frustration, pressing his mouth to the water's surface and blowing a steady stream of bubbles.

Thanks to that, the second half of what he said was swallowed by the bubbles and went unheard.

"Besides, I came to bathe too. Can I jump in next to you?"

"Huh? Y-you idiot, stop! Bathe, out in the open like this? Of course that's a no! Have some more modesty! How long do you plan on staying a child?!"

"Ehh~"

"Don't 'ehh' me!"

"Uu~"

"Don't 'uu' me either!"

Emilia, who had already begun warming up to dive in, pouted at Archi's ban on bathing. She didn't know what had him so flustered, but Archi was being mean today.

Maybe he was just really angry about slipping and falling into the river.

"Archi, I'm sorry."

"Oh, eh... w-what's this, going all meek all of a sudden."

"I guess falling in must have really upset you. Sorry. So let me bathe too. Otherwise Mother Fortuna won't let me eat breakfast."

"That's such a childish way of thinking!"

At Emilia's downcast words, Archi clutched his head and shouted.

For an instant, the hand treading water stopped, and Archi's body sank slightly. In other words, for just that instant, his attention slipped from Emilia.

"Hup!"

"Ah!"

A small shout, and then, just as she thought sunlight brushed past her eyelids, a feeling of weightlessness.

Her long silver hair trailed upward, lagging behind her body as gravity pulled it down, and Emilia hit the water toes-first with considerable force.

Entering toes-first, Emilia's body raised not a single wasted drop of spray, diving in astonishing silence straight down to the riverbed.

In the clear river water, she opened her eyes and watched small fish and waterweeds sway in the current. She touched her feet to the riverbed, savoring the ticklish feel of the gravel, then shot back up.

Surfacing right beside Archi,

"—Boo."

"Don't 'boo' me!"

Smoothing back her wet hair, Emilia backstroked away from the shouting Archi.

Archi still had his brows knit as though he had more to say, but seeming to realize at once that nothing he said would faze her, he let out a deep sigh and fell in swimming behind her.

"Feels nice, doesn't it, Archi."

"Well, maybe for you, since you jumped in on your own. I got pushed in, and then got splashed by you jumping in on top of that — I feel pretty awful."

"I see. Glad you're having fun too, Archi."

"You really are a relentlessly positive girl, Emilia..."

Feeling as though she'd been praised, Emilia puffed out her chest while floating on her back.

Archi turned his face away from her and scratched the tip of his nose. His cheeks were red. The water was cold, so did he have a fever or something?

"Are you maybe feeling unwell? Is that why you're mad about falling in the water?"

If so, then even after apologizing, what she'd done deserved to make him angry.

She wanted to pull Archi out of the river right away and cast some healing magic on him.

"No, don't worry about it. It's not that. ...Look, Emilia. Don't go being so unguarded in front of men— no, in front of people, like that. Especially not people you're not close with."

"...? Archi, you're the person I'm closest with, you know?"

"Even with people you're close with, still! Um... just... keep it to in front of me, at most."

"Not Mother?"

"In front of Lady Fortuna, me, and that woman — that's it!"

He said this loudly to the head-tilting Emilia, his face turning redder still as he bit his lip. Then, groaning, he dove underwater and vanished from the view of a frowning Emilia.

A moment later he surfaced with a splash by the riverbank and hauled himself up onto the shore.

"Come on, you should get out too, Emilia. Besides, if you just woke up, weren't you supposed to be here to wash your face, not bathe? I can't imagine Lady Fortuna telling you to go bathing first thing in the morning."

"Now that you mention it, maybe so... Besides, I didn't bring a change of clothes."

"What are you even doing, you..."

At Emilia's actions — less unguarded than simply unplanned — Archi wore an exasperated expression.

As she swam toward him, Archi dashed into the woods and came back with a large towel blanket.

"Dry yourself off with this, and keep it over your shoulders on the way back home. Honestly, no matter how old you get, you're such a handful."

"Ahaha, sorry, Archi. I'll gratefully borrow it."

Even Emilia had no choice but to feel a bit chastened by this.

Pulled up out of the river by his offered hand, she dried her long hair with the towel blanket he'd handed her. Her silver hair glittered in the morning sun, heavy with the water it had soaked up.

"...Was my hair always this long?"

"What are you talking about? You've been growing it out for ages. You said it's pretty since it's the same color as Lady Fortuna's."

As she let the towel soak up the wetness from her silver hair, Archi said something along those lines.

Put that way, it did seem to ring a bell, but when had she made that decision in the first place?

Feeling something that didn't quite sit right, Emilia decided to close her eyes to it as a trivial discomfort. She wrung the water thoroughly from her silver hair and dried her body. Then, peering into the river, she reached a hand toward the water to wash her face, her original purpose here—

"—"

Seeing her own face reflected on the water's surface, Emilia's throat seized.

White skin. Violet eyes. Pink lips. Long, glittering silver hair. Every one of them was exactly the features of the face she knew as her own. Nothing was different, nothing was strange.

There was no way that could be true.

There was nothing here but things that were strange, things that were wrong, things that had changed.

"Ah... hh..."

Patting at her own cheeks, Emilia let out broken, ragged breaths.

Her lungs seemed to seize up, and she couldn't breathe properly. She felt a sensation like her insides being wrung out, a pressure like something both itching and aching racing through her whole body.

"Emilia, what's wrong?"

Noticing Emilia's abnormal state, Archi's voice dropped low.

He touched the shoulder of Emilia, frozen staring at the water's surface, and stroked her head from behind.

"Did you find something strange in the water?"

"...No."

"Did your stomach suddenly start hurting? I can't use healing magic, so I'd have to take you to someone..."

"That's not... it."

Feeling Archi's worried voice and the hand on her, Emilia still couldn't tear her eyes from the water's surface.

Archi followed her gaze and seemed to realize what she was looking at. Hesitantly, he pointed at the Emilia reflected on the water,

"Something wrong with your face? Looks the same as always to me — a pretty face."

"It's an adult..."

"Huh?"

"It's... an adult's face. ...But I've never even seen my own face before."

Seeing an unfamiliar face floating on the water, Emilia murmured in a trembling voice.

The possibility that this wasn't her own was ruled out, since the hand she touched to it in confirmation moved in exactly the same way as the reflection. This face was her own. Her own, even though she'd supposedly never seen it before.

"...Me..."

Once she noticed that one decisive wrongness, discrepancy after discrepancy came into sharp relief.

Looking down, her body's chest had filled out. Her hair, too, hadn't been this long.

Her arms and legs were longer than she remembered, and there should have been more of a difference in size between her and Archi.

Even the conversations she'd had with everyone that morning, the way they'd looked at her, took on a different meaning now.

How many times, in fact, had she been told, *You're not a child anymore*?

That was exactly right.

"—I have to go."

"Emilia?"

Standing up, Emilia swayed her head slightly and turned to look behind her.

The forest and settlement she had passed through. And the house where Fortuna, who had sent her off, was waiting.

She had to go back there.

She still didn't clearly know what she had to do, but that much, at least, was an unshakable truth.

"Archi, I'm sorry. I'm going back to Mother Fortuna."

"Ah, yeah... that's fine, but are you all right, physically?"

"I'm fine now. Sorry for interrupting your bath. And it's fine about the towel blanket too."

Taking the towel off her shoulders, Emilia pressed it into the hands of a bewildered Archi.

Confirming that he'd taken it, Emilia took off running barefoot. As fast as she could, toward the home where Fortuna waited — and at her back,

"Emilia!"

came Archi's voice.

Even though her heart raced with the sense that she had no time to stop, Emilia's feet halted anyway. As though someone were urging her that she mustn't miss a single one of Archi's words.

Seeing her turn back, Archi raised a hand slightly.

"I don't know what happened, but if you're ever in trouble, come talk to me anytime! I'm... practically like your brother, Emilia!"

He faltered for just a moment, but Archi still tossed those forceful words her way.

Why was it, hearing those words, that something welled up in Emilia's chest.

There was no doubt it was a happy thing to hear.

And yet, this feeling welling up from deep in her chest seemed to be something other than simple joy.

"Right...! Thank you, big brother!"

Waving, Emilia answered a red-faced Archi and set off running again.

* * *

"...You were only supposed to wash your face — how does that end with you coming home soaked from head to toe? Mother truly cannot make sense of it."

Fortuna greeted her daughter, who had returned drenched from head to toe, lamenting with an exasperated look.

Emilia's state after returning from the water's edge was that, while the visible parts of her hair had at least been dried with the towel blanket, the white clothes she wore clung to her skin, and since she hadn't wrung them out, water dripped steadily from the hem of her skirt.

"Sorry, Mother. I was just... just a really, really little bit half-asleep."

"You went to wash your face to shake off that sleepiness, and it seems you shook it off with quite a lot of energy. Honestly, no matter how old you get, you're still a child. You weren't seen looking like that by anyone, were you?"

Did she mean it would be embarrassing to be seen looking like a drowned rat?

If that was the meaning, then miraculously, she didn't think she'd passed anyone on the way back.

"Mm, it's fine. Only Archi saw me."

"I see... Archi. Well, if it's him... but lately I feel like the way Archi looks at Emilia has changed a little from before..."

"Mother?"

"Ah, mm, no, it's nothing. Anyway, come along."

Looking at Emilia's downcast eyes with a resigned air, Fortuna stroked her daughter's head and led her by the hand into the house. Though even now, water still dripped from her clothes.

"Mother, the house is going to get wet."

"We can just wipe it up later. More importantly, there's a towel blanket, so dry off with that while you change in your room. Once you're back, we'll have breakfast."

Their dwelling, hollowed out from a forest tree, was a one-of-a-kind creation shaped by pouring the power of mana into an aged great tree. Emilia and Fortuna's house, too, was Fortuna's own handiwork, and it had turned out quite large for just the two of them to live in. On the second floor were each of their own rooms, and on the first floor, a space for eating.

Looking back on it now, she thought it was rather a lavish use of space.

—Looking back on it now was an odd feeling to have.

"There, off you go."

"Woof."

With the towel pressed against her thoughtful face, Emilia shot a look of protest at Fortuna. But she was easily defeated by her mother's gaze, hands on hips, watching her.

Breathing in the smell of sunshine from the towel pressed to her face, Emilia dried herself off as she made her way back up to her room on the second floor.

Her room, once she was back in it, struck her as simple.

Like Fortuna, Emilia had never much cared for meaningless decoration. The room held only the minimum furniture and a few small furnishings. There was a wooden box with a change of clothes, and crouching down, Emilia pulled out something to wear, stripped off her wet clothes, and quickly changed.

Just like with the room's furnishings, Emilia had no particular attachment to clothing either.

Pulling a single, one-piece short-sleeved garment over her head, she changed her undergarments and left the room — deliberately avoiding looking at the thing beside the clothing box.

"Mother Fortuna. I'll wash my wet clothes myself, so..."

"Well now, that's a very filial and admirable thing to do, I think."

"—"

A man's voice greeted Emilia as she came downstairs carrying a basket of laundry.

At the gentle voice, full of warmth, Emilia held her breath, then looked toward the table.

At the table Fortuna and Emilia normally shared alone, there was one extra chair. It was the chair Fortuna brought out from the back of the house whenever a certain someone was invited over.

Naturally, the person seated in that chair was also someone she knew well,

"Juice."

"Indeed, it has been a while, Lady Emilia. Have you been keeping well since we last met?"

"Me, yeah, same as always. It's you who's been away for ages, Juice. I didn't hear you were coming today, so why?"

"Oh, is that so? I made sure to have word sent ahead through my Fingers, as always."

The gentle-faced man — Juice — put a hand to his chin in a thoughtful gesture. Good-natured as he was, he seemed genuinely puzzled, but Emilia knew at once who the culprit was.

Slipping past Juice, she peeked into the kitchen where the meal was being prepared, and there was Fortuna, a hand over her mouth, holding back laughter.

"Mother, you kept it a secret, didn't you."

"Fufu, who's to say. Maybe I simply forgot, is all."

"There's no way that's true. There's Juice's chair right there, and the food's for three people."

"Oh my, sharp eyes. You're so scatterbrained normally, and yet your instincts work fine at times like this."

Winking at the glaring Emilia, Fortuna whistled as she picked up a plate. Then, holding it out to her,

"Come on, Lia, help set the table too. Since you can't cook anything yourself, I think I'm allowed to at least ask you to help clean up."

"Mmu... you dodged the question. Besides, the reason I can't cook anything is because you won't teach me, Mother."

"You always mix up sugar and salt, and a child who can't even hold a knife properly is too frightening to let stand in the kitchen in the first place."

Shutting down Emilia's objections, Fortuna carried dish after dish to the table. Emilia, unconvinced but resigned, trailed dejectedly after her back to the table.

Juice, waiting at the table, softened at the sight of the fragrant spread before him.

"Being allowed to share in Lady Fortuna's cooking like this is a tremendous honor. No matter how many times I have the pleasure of tasting it, the joy never fades."

"There you go again, saying things like that so easily."

"I'm merely conveying my honest feelings, though?"

"That's exactly what I mean by you being incorrigible."

Beside Fortuna as she laid out the food, Juice tilted his head with a troubled look.

Watching the two of them go back and forth, Emilia couldn't help but laugh. Just watching them was enough to blow away her earlier defeat at Fortuna's hands.

"If you're that happy about Mother's cooking, you should just live here too, Juice."

"W-what, Emilia—!"

Setting a large platter piled with vegetables in the middle of the table, Emilia rode the momentum of the conversation to say something like that. At once, Fortuna's face flushed with fluster, and she kept glancing sidelong at Juice beside her.

"D-don't go saying reckless things. Juice has all sorts of his own troubles, and he still makes time to visit us like this in between his busy schedule..."

"That is a most welcome suggestion, Lady Emilia. If it were at all possible, I would love nothing more than to do exactly that, from the bottom of my heart."

Fortuna, flustered and trying to deny it, made a stark contrast with Juice's calm response. At his words, Fortuna's momentum was cut short, and she sank into her chair, making herself small.

Watching the two of them sit side by side, Emilia took her own seat across from them.

—The sight before her looked, to Emilia's eyes, terribly natural.

"If neither of you minds, you should just do it, Mother, Juice. No one's going to get in the way of that. Ah... could it be that I'm the one getting in the way?"

Anyone could see that Fortuna and Juice didn't think badly of each other.

And yet the fact that the two of them never drew closer than a certain line — maybe that was because of her own presence.

But that anxiety of Emilia's,

"That's not it."

"Not at all, that isn't so."

was put to rest as needless worry when the two of them denied it at exactly the same time.

Emilia's eyes went wide, while Fortuna and Juice looked at each other over their overlapping words and burst out laughing despite themselves.

"See, you two really are super close after all."

"Honestly, stop teasing us forever, Emilia. Juice, scold her too."

"Indeed, Lady Emilia. Lady Fortuna is a wonderful person. If someone like me were to overstay, it would only invite unwelcome rumors and cause her trouble."

"Hmm~. But I think it's already too late for that."

Juice, praising Fortuna while playing down his own worth. Seeing a faint trace of sadness mixed into Fortuna's gaze as she looked at him, Emilia held up one finger.

"Because whenever I go outside, everyone tells me not to cause trouble for Mother Fortuna and Father Romanée-Conti."

"—"

Somehow it was funny, the way the two of them wore identical stunned expressions at Emilia's words.

Pressing a hand to her mouth to hold back the urge to laugh, Emilia steadied her breath, then,

"It's true. Even last night, staying up late — I was absorbed in trying to reconcile the differences between the old book and the map that Juice gave me... and everyone said how admirable it was that I was helping Father with his work."

"W-who said such a thing..."

"The Tehena lady from across the way, and Mit-san, and Auntie Tanse too."

"Those three gossips at the well...!"

Picturing the three familiar faces in the air, Fortuna bit her lip resentfully.

Her sharp eyes narrowed fiercely, giving her a slightly scary look.

Emilia soothed her with a "now, now," and then,

"Anyway, everyone already thinks of it that way. And I've, um, well, thought about it a lot, and worried over it, and, um, well, so..."

"Lady Emilia, there's no need to force yourself to think so hard about it."

"N-no, that's not it! I do think it's a good thing, okay? It's just, it feels a little like Mother's being taken away, and that makes me a bit unsettled, that's all!"

Even with everyone around them so thoroughly prepared, it was only the feelings of the two people involved, and her own, that remained unsettled.

Perhaps that was simply how these things went, but if it were only a matter between the two of them, that would be one thing — she wanted to avoid letting her own feelings intrude as an obstacle.

Because, even to Emilia's eyes, the two of them were a good match.

"I really think it's a wonderful thing. So, please, both of you, think about it too."

"—"

"Everyone in the forest, and me too — no one's going to get in the way. I won't let anyone say it's a bad thing or that it's wrong, not for anything!"

Slapping the table, Emilia found herself giving an impassioned speech.

Having finished, she realized she'd gotten too worked up, and her face went startled. Under the two pairs of eyes watching her, she smoothed her hair and slowly sat back down.

"S-so... I'll leave the rest to you two young people, please go ahead."

"Emilia, where do you really learn to say things like that?"

At the blushing Emilia's remark, Fortuna wore her familiar exasperated look. But that expression was soon blown away by an irrepressible urge to laugh.

"Fu, fufufu."

"Haha, Lady Emilia has... indeed, grown up. To say you haven't changed would speak terribly of my own powers of observation."

"That's right, Juice. She's my pride and joy of a daughter, so of course."

"Yes, I stand corrected."

Laughing, exchanging glances, Fortuna and Juice traded such words.

The atmosphere between the two of them was softer now than before, and Emilia vaguely sensed that something had changed because of her own words.

Something warm filled the space between them.

Surely, even the way their gazes met now carried a different color than before.

—It was a terribly happy sight.

"...Emilia?"

Fortuna, glancing over on a sudden thought, called Emilia's name.

At that voice, Emilia caught her breath and covered her eyes with a hand. Hurriedly wiping away tears threatening to spill, she let out an exaggerated "ahh."

"I think I might have gotten something in my eye. A really, really big something."

"That big? Are you all right?"

"I'm fine, it's only about fist-sized."

"A-are you truly all right?"

"I'm fine!"

Answering the two of them as they worried, Emilia rubbed at her eyes and stood up.

Then she left the table and headed for the stairs to the second floor.

"I'll go put in some really effective eye drops. The kind that clear you up so much your eyes practically fall out."

"Emilia's eyes are that beautiful violet, so don't go throwing them away, all right? They're beautiful eyes, just like your brother's."

"And, they're the same lovely color as Mother's too."

Perhaps not expecting to be answered like that, Fortuna looked startled at Emilia's words. Seeing Juice smile at Fortuna's profile, Emilia smiled too.

Smiling, she set foot on the stairs, then turned back to the two of them,

"Go ahead and eat without me. I'll be right back."

"It won't taste as good once it's cold, so come back quickly, really."

"Yeah, quick quick."

"Then, I shall wait for you at my leisure, Lady Emilia."

Seen off by Fortuna and Juice's words, Emilia drew in a deep breath.

Then, turning back just once more, she looked at the two of them with their eyes lowered to the table,

"—I love you both, so much."

Saying that, Emilia went back to her room.

Closing the door to her room, Emilia let out a breath with enough force to empty every last bit of air from her body.

With the sensation of her insides being wrung tight, she slapped her cheeks as if to steel herself, shook her head, and walked toward the corner of the room.

Beside the wooden box holding Emilia's clothes stood a long, narrow object draped in a thin cloth.

Until now, it had never once occurred to Emilia to reach for it, but—

"I guess nothing starts unless I face it."

*Please, give me courage.*

Emilia touched her own lips gently with a finger, and drew back her hand as she recalled its warmth.

The cloth fell away.

There stood a polished full-length mirror, reflecting Emilia from head to toe, and—

"—What did the scene of happiness that should have been give you?"

In the place where her own reflection should have stood, she saw the white-haired Witch standing instead.
