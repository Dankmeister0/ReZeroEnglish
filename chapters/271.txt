Arc 4 Chapter 102: "Memories I Don't Remember"

—The place of the seal turned out to be surprisingly easy to find.

"This is the Feuin?"

Young Emilia blinked her violet eyes at the strange object before her, tilting her head.

Before Emilia's eyes, deep within the depths of the forest's depths, was a space enclosed by great trees, and within it, a peculiar door.

Though it was a door, it wasn't connected to any building, and even circling around behind it revealed nothing. There was simply a door, absurdly large, standing alone in the middle of the forest, tall enough to make one look up.

"Why doesn't it fall over...?"

Curious, she tried pushing on the door, but it didn't so much as budge, let alone open. Naturally, the door didn't topple from Emilia's thin arms either — it simply enjoyed a peace as undisturbed as if it were only feeling a gentle breeze.

The material of the door looked like wood, but the cold sensation when she touched it was closer to ice. The texture beneath her fingers was smooth, with the strange feel of polished stone.

At the center of the closed double doors was a lock, ancient-looking, with a keyhole peering out that was as large as Emilia's palm. She couldn't even imagine whose pocket a key that size could possibly fit in.

"Weeeird..."

She rapped the door with the back of her hand, confirming that despite how hard it should be, there was no echo at all. Having found it but with no idea what it meant, Emilia cocked her head.

"I wonder what it is. Hey."

"――――"

The faint glow circled round and round above Emilia's head, as if in answer to her question.

Ever since their meeting in the princess's room, she'd learned the fairies were here and there throughout the forest, and when Emilia confided that she was looking for the "Seal," they'd found this place for her fairly quickly.

She'd followed their guidance to arrive here, but it seemed that even they, for all their cleverness, had no idea what meaning this seal held.

Even if they did know, they had no way of telling her, since they couldn't exchange words with her.

"I don't get it, so it's boring. But I found it! Clap clap clap."

Clapping her hands and cheering for herself alone, Emilia gave a great nod and turned to leave the seal behind.

With this, she'd secured her trump card in case her escape from the princess's room was ever discovered. Even if Mother Fortuna and the others found out about Emilia's great escape, if she brought up the fact that she knew about this seal, it would — somehow, probably — turn into an even match of an argument.

Though she couldn't quite remember what had first given her that idea in the first place.

"Hmph. It's Mother Fortuna and everyone's fault. It's Juice's fault."

Remembering the tall man in black clothes, Emilia stuck her tongue out at him, though he wasn't there to see it.

He was the one who drew out her beloved Mother Fortuna's secret expressions — expressions no one but Emilia should get to see. She knew he wasn't a bad person, but he was without question her enemy.

Though she hadn't yet faced him directly, Emilia had made up her mind firmly: if Fortuna ever gave her the chance to meet him, she would absolutely stomp on his foot.

"The fairies jump on his face, and while Juice is all in a panic, I step on his foot. And I'll step with both feet, too. And stomp with my heel... no, that sounds like it'd hurt, so I'll use my toes instead."

Even amid her merciless scheme, she never forgot to slip in a shred of mercy.

No allies would follow a plan with no blood or tears in it. Mindful of both the fairies and her enemy, Emilia crept quietly back from the depths of the forest toward the settlement.

Right now, aside from her escape from the princess's room, it was simply Emilia's usual free time. During this time, Fortuna was off checking whether any of the barriers scattered through the forest had loosened, so only the elderly and children around Emilia's own age remained in the settlement.

The elderly couldn't keep up with Emilia's great adventures, and the children, for some reason, never seemed to want to get close to her. Sometimes that made her lonely, but at those times Fortuna would play with her with all her might until she forgot about it, so not being able to go around playing with the other children wasn't that big a problem for Emilia.

"Besides, I bet I'm the only one who knows about the Feuin."

Knowing something that no one else knew.

That small sense of superiority supported Emilia's small pride. She puffed out her chest and marched onward through the forest, the fairies trailing behind her.

Her destination was home. Today, on paper much bigger than usual that Mother Fortuna had prepared for her, she was going to let her brush run wild and paint a picture to her heart's content.

"Today, the great artist is in the mood to paint a red sky, and paint a white forest. ...Hm?"

As she sketched the image she meant to fling onto the canvas in the air, Emilia suddenly noticed the fairies' movements had become erratic. Closing one eye and saying "Hm?", she followed their motion — the fairies, wobbling unsteadily, were drifting off the forest path and disappearing into the depths of the underbrush.

"This has the feeling of an incident coming...!"

Normally the fairies would vanish like scattering motes of light, but here they were, wavering unsteadily into the depths of the forest. Emilia decided this was either an incident, or an invitation to follow.

She left the path, trampling through the underbrush, clambering as a matter of course over flowers and trees taller than she was. Catching her long silver hair on branches again and again, she pushed her way through the forest, and there—

"Oh dear, this is troubling. ...I'm going to be late for my appointment."

In the dim narrow path of the forest, she found a man leaning his back against a slender tree, at a complete loss.

"—Ah."

The sound slipped out before she could stop it, and Emilia hastily clapped both hands over her mouth. But in the quiet forest, a child's high voice carried clearly. It reached the man's ears without fail, and drew his attention.

"Who's there?"

Tilting his head, the man turned his face toward the thicket where Emilia was hiding.

A gentle face. Neatly trimmed green hair. A slender face, but a build that gave more an impression of firmness than frailty. His tall height was even taller than Fortuna, whom Emilia had to look up at, and taller still than the grasses and trees she desperately jumped to peek her head above.

The man, whom Emilia recognized at once, was none other than her despised nemesis, Juice himself.

"――――"

Stared at in silence, Emilia, clutching her head, resigned herself to the fact that she couldn't slip away unnoticed.

In truth, as she clutched her head and turned her back, her rear end was sticking out of the thicket in plain view of Juice — but Emilia didn't notice this, and Juice didn't point it out either.

Sheepishly, with a sulky expression, Emilia poked her face out from the bushes.

"Well, well, if it isn't an adorable young lady making her appearance—!"

Juice had begun to break into a gentle smile at the sight of her, but the latter half of his words was cut short by astonishment.

His eyes went wide, his lips trembling as he stared fixedly at Emilia. That intense gaze made her strangely uncomfortable — partly because she considered Juice an enemy, and partly because she couldn't untangle the complicated emotion packed into his stare.

"Young lady... no, could you possibly be..."

"――――"

His voice trembling, Juice shook his head as though he'd seen something unbelievable. Looking up at him hesitantly, Emilia felt a pang at how terribly weak his expression seemed.

Like a child who had lost their way, or like someone who had walked through darkness for so long and finally glimpsed light — that expression, at once so fragile and so forlorn, made Emilia forget every feeling she'd ever held toward Juice until now.

"...Juice, are you okay?"

"—! Ah, ah, ahhhh..."

Stepping closer, Emilia pinched the hem of Juice's black clerical robe and spoke to him.

The instant he heard her, Juice's expression crumbled even further, and as though struck by lightning from the fingertips pinching his hem, a shudder ran down his spine, and he sank to his knees on the spot.

Emilia's shoulders jumped involuntarily. Kneeling down, bending at the waist, Juice brought his eyes level with hers. From his two eyes, tears poured out without end.

The unceasing flow of tears carved a great river down Juice's face. Having never seen a grown man cry before, Emilia could only hold her breath and watch.

Taking Emilia's frozen hand in his, Juice looked straight into her violet eyes.

"I'm... fine...! Yes, yes! I am perfectly fine. There is no problem whatsoever... I... I am, right now, at this very moment, saved more thoroughly than I could ever have imagined."

"Really...? But you got saved, and you're crying?"

"I am not crying because I am sad... I cry because I am glad, because I am joyful, because I am happy — there are tears like that too, tears of happiness. And I learned that from none other than... you... from all of you... and so..."

Through his trembling fingers, Juice's unbearable emotion flowed into her, and Emilia found she had no wish to shake free of it. She placed her other hand atop the one holding hers and squeezed back.

Juice bowed his head deeply, tears dripping onto the ground drop by drop, repeating sobs he could not hold back, continuing to shed what he called tears of happiness.

—Was crying happiness? If so, was he happy right now?

"Even when you're happy, you still cry..."

Somehow, she felt she understood that feeling.

Emilia too, sometimes, spent lonely nights alone unable to sleep. At times like that, she would crawl into Fortuna's bed and be held in her grumbling mother's arms, spending warm hours there until morning came.

At such times, Emilia would feel freed from her anxieties, wrapped in happiness, and sometimes felt as though she might cry. Was what Juice was feeling now something like what Emilia felt at those times?

Could Emilia do the same thing that Mother Fortuna did?

"It's okay, Juice. It's okay. It's okay."

"――――!"

As if to comfort him, Emilia stroked Juice's head with the hand she'd laid over his.

A violent shudder ran through his body, and Emilia drew the bowed Juice into her small chest. His sobs pressed against her, and his warmth flowed into her from inside.

She'd meant to stomp on his foot, and here she was, doing this instead.

He really was hopeless. A helpless sort of enemy. There was no way she could do something awful to someone who was crying. Surely Mother Fortuna would forgive her too, saying it couldn't be helped.

"Crying all alone must be lonely, huh."

Once Juice finished crying, she'd walk back to the village hand in hand with him.

She'd have to tell Mother Fortuna.

About her walk deep in the forest, and about how even a grown-up had cried.

The two of them, having shared a secret, were no longer enemies — they were something more like friends now.

—Leaning her back against something hard, Emilia returned from a momentary lapse of consciousness.

Shaking her head, she dug her fingers into her silver hair and grimaced at the shrill, continuous ringing in her ears.

That dream she'd just seen, in that brief span — what had it been?

There was no such scene in Emilia's memory. There shouldn't have been.

And yet, as if she had truly witnessed it, an unfamiliar scene had come bubbling up. The people in it were all people Emilia knew, and there was no mistaking that she herself was a participant in this drama — and yet there was something inexplicable about it, as though a scene entirely unknown to her had been performed before her.

She knew Fortuna. She knew Juice. She knew the fairies. She knew her younger self.

And yet — a sight she had no memory of seeing. A scene she didn't know. A conversation she had no recollection of exchanging.

Bit by bit, scenes unknown to Emilia kept spilling out.

The sealed door. The escape from the princess's room. The fairies' guidance. The true identity of Juice and the others in black. Mother Fortuna's final words to Emilia.

"—Ah, guh—"

A sharp, piercing pain lanced through Emilia's skull.

She roughly wiped the tears welling at the corners of her eyes with her sleeve, her pale eyes swollen red, and let out a breath.

Ever since the morning her Contract with Puck had been severed, one unfamiliar memory after another had kept surfacing in Emilia's mind like this. She herself didn't truly understand what it meant.

But she did understand that these were not meaningless delusions with no basis in reality.

Because the deepest part of Emilia's heart was not rejecting or pushing away these memories — not in the slightest.

She knew them. Somewhere deep within her heart, she knew these scenes.

If so, did that mean these were things that had truly happened? And if that were true, then why weren't they in her mind?

Her own past, which she had seen during the Trials of the Tomb.

In that snowy forest, the memory of Fortuna hurling resentment and malice at her — a memory that made her want to scream. Emilia's memory was missing some decisive piece that connected to that moment.

Was whatever connected to that missing piece somewhere within this sea of memory now surfacing?

If so, then diving deep into that sea, sweeping the ocean floor, and finding whatever had sunk there was something Emilia had to do.

"I have to hurry... and find it..."

Her head ached. Her body felt heavy. Her vision swam, and the strength drained from her entire body.

Even so, leaning her weight against the wall, Emilia stood, and forced herself to keep moving.

Inch by inch, dragging her heavy body forward, Emilia pressed on into the darkness, deeper and deeper, her face on the verge of tears, alone.

Entirely alone.
