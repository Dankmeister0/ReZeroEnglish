Arc 6 Chapter 38: "WHO ARE YOU"

Not understanding in the slightest what had happened to him, Subaru swept his eyes around the room.

"――"

*Familiar* wasn't quite the word, but it was interior he recognized. Walls, floor, even the ceiling were all covered in green vines, and Subaru himself was sitting up on a bed woven of vines.

Before him, two girls — one a beautiful young woman, the other a beautiful little girl — were tilting their heads in puzzlement, and right beside the bed sat a giant lizard with its legs folded beneath it. On the other bed in the room lay a blue-haired girl with her eyes closed.

Nothing was out of place at all. This was the green room of the Pleiades Watchtower.

"But... huh? Why am I here again?"

He couldn't tell whether he'd just woken up.

Putting a hand to his head, Subaru tried to recall what had happened just before this. If he remembered right, they'd been talking about challenging the tower's second layer, and before that he'd been checking whether he had some kind of special ability. In the end, the conclusion had been that he had no ability at all, and after that Subaru had been walking through the tower to rejoin Emilia and the others—

"Um? What happened after that, again?"

His memory right after that point was frustratingly unclear.
The strongest impression he had was of suddenly noticing he was lying on this bed again.
And as he groped through his hazy memory, feeling it out—

"Hey, Subaru, are you okay?"

"Whoa?! Emilia-chan, too close!"

Emilia's beautiful face had drawn in so near he could feel her breath. Her violet eyes, framed by long lashes, were close enough that he could see his own face reflected in them — such an unguarded approach that Subaru scrambled off the far side of the vine bed to put some distance between them.

At his overblown reaction, Emilia blinked in surprise.

"You don't have to be that startled. Besides, I'm the one who got startled here."

"You got startled by...?"

"Obviously. I thought you'd disappeared, went looking for you, and found you collapsed. Of course I'm going to worry."

"Seriously? I collapsed again?"

Beatrice, arms crossed, snorted in exasperation. Startled by her words, Subaru patted himself down as he stood up.

Even so, feeling himself over turned up nothing wrong. Whether an amnesiac would even notice something wrong with himself was questionable to begin with, but there were no visible injuries.

"Still, collapsing twice in such a short span is pretty alarming... I guess I should count myself lucky that even though it was only a few hours, I didn't lose that memory too?"

"What are you muttering about... Subaru, there's something you should be saying to Betty and the others, I suspect."

"Something to say..."

Beatrice tossed those words at him while he was studying his own outstretched hands. Puzzled, Subaru tilted his head and looked at Emilia and Beatrice in turn.

Wondering just what it was he needed to say to them, he thought for a moment—

"Oh, right. Um, sorry. Sorry for worrying you. You saved me again."

"That'll do, I suppose."

"Fufu, you're welcome. But I'm really glad nothing's wrong with you. I'm relieved."

"Yeah, that's right. Sorry to keep causing you trouble at this pace, Emilia-chan."

Raising a hand, Subaru expressed his gratitude to Emilia. But at his words, Emilia's lovely brows drew together. Then, her violet eyes wavering with confusion, she said,

"Um, about that... Subaru, what's the matter with you just now?"

"'What's the matter' is a pretty vague question. Where's the problem supposed to be?"

"I mean, you've been calling me Emilia-chan this whole time. Somehow it makes me feel really, really uneasy, being called that by you."

As she spoke, Emilia twined a finger through her long silver hair, gazing at Subaru with hesitation. At the desolate look in her eyes, one that seemed to claw at his chest, Subaru swallowed involuntarily.

Unguarded, familiar, a girl whose looks struck squarely at Subaru's taste — and yet her current attitude sat at a stark gulf from anything he might have expected.

"Subaru's tomfoolery isn't anything new, I suspect. More importantly..."

At that exchange between the two of them, Beatrice cut in, her tone displeased. Shaking her magnificent ringlets, she jabbed a finger sharply at Subaru. Then, cheeks puffing out to a cherry-blossom pink, she said,

"It's about time you told Betty and the others, I suspect. Why did you go into the 'Taygeta' archive again in the middle of the night? What caused you to collapse this time, I wonder?"

"Wait wait wait wait! What, I collapsed in Taygeta again?"

"Again?"

"Seriously? Taygeta's really terrifying then... Wait, what was I even doing going back there? It's already a cursed place as it is, that's reckless beyond belief."

Told of events he had no memory of, Subaru held his head in confusion at the unsettling facts. His memory before and after losing consciousness was indeed hazy, but even so, there shouldn't have been any reason to go back to Taygeta.

It would almost make more sense if someone had thrown the collapsed Subaru into Taygeta while he was out cold in the tower — except he couldn't think of a single benefit to doing that.

"Hold on, I suspect, Subaru."

But Beatrice called a halt to Subaru's spiraling confusion.

"Hm?"

"Something about this conversation feels off, I suspect. Subaru, tell it accurately, I suspect."

"Accurately, meaning..."

"Tell Betty and the others what situation you're in right now, I suspect."

Slowly, deliberately, as though spelling it out, Beatrice told him this. Overwhelmed by the weight of her words and the pressure of her manner, Subaru nodded.

Then, watched over by an anxious-looking Emilia, Subaru thought it over and said,

"First, I think I already said this, but... when I woke up, I had no memory. Not completely blank, but my memories since being summoned to this other world..."

"Wa- wa- wait, I suspect!? Memory? What do you mean, memory, I suspect!?"

"Eh?"

Beatrice's expression and bearing, so full of dignity a moment ago, shattered the instant he began to explain. Startled by her unexpected reaction, Subaru flinched, and Emilia gently supported the flustered Beatrice's shoulders from behind. But Emilia herself was hardly calm either.

She too was staring at Subaru with wide, startled eyes.

"Subaru, I'm sorry, I'm just not sure what you're saying..."

"No, wait, if you two stop here that's a problem, because you two already know—"

—he tried to continue, but the words wouldn't come.

"――"

The confusion in the eyes with which Emilia and Beatrice looked at Subaru was unmistakably genuine. Even Subaru could tell it wasn't an act. And that very fact — that their bewilderment was real — was what chilled him to the bone.

Why would the two of them have forgotten the fact of his amnesia, which he'd just told them?

Could it be that this tower had some property that drained memory, and it wasn't only Subaru who kept losing his? That everyone's memories were being siphoned away bit by bit, and it was some horrifying tower where conversation and understanding gradually broke down between people — such a chilling thought rose up in him—

And then, all at once, Subaru realized something.

"This exchange, right after waking up..."

He had some sense of having experienced it before. No — not just *some* sense. He remembered it.

This first meeting with Emilia and Beatrice — in this case, the first meeting for a Subaru who had lost his memory — the conversation they'd just had was identical to one he'd had before.

More than that: waking in the green room, lying on the vine bed — the very situation itself might be a replay of the moment the memory-lost Subaru had first woken.

"――"

Struck by that realization, Subaru swallowed audibly. He glanced at Emilia and Beatrice — their manner hadn't changed. But what showed in their wavering eyes wasn't distrust; it was heartfelt concern directed at 'Subaru.'

The fact that there was no falsehood in it only made Subaru's mind grow calmer still.

Honestly, his heart was tossed about in confusion like it was caught in a great wave. But — thinking it through, this situation was—

"I'm seeing the same situation. In other words — a prophetic dream."

Given the circumstances at the moment he'd woken, wasn't that the most reasonable conclusion?

If that were so, it would explain why the memory of that instant — the break in consciousness, or rather, the moment of waking — was so hazy. Dreams, for whatever reason, always slip through your fingers no matter how hard you try to hold onto them.

Or perhaps this prophetic dream itself was the special ability Subaru had been granted upon being summoned to this other world—

"That's a hell of a tricky ability to actually make use of..."

Still, there was no doubt that, mastered, it would be a powerful force. A prophetic dream was, in the end, a foreknowledge of the future. The higher its accuracy, the greater a clue it could be toward breaking through a situation.

Unfortunately, he couldn't think of much useful information from this particular prophetic dream, but—

"—I'd like the two of you to calm down a bit and hear me out."

Straightening himself, having grasped his own ability, Subaru addressed the two of them. At his manner, Emilia and Beatrice exchanged glances, then nodded.

Facing the two of them, both now serious, Subaru hesitated for a moment before speaking.

"I don't know if you'll believe me, but it seems... I've lost my memory."


※※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※


—Waking in the archive of 'Taygeta,' Subaru had lost his memory.


For the two of them to fully absorb the fact Subaru had confessed took just as much time and just as much of a trigger as it had in the prophetic dream.

"There!"

There was a popping sound in the air as Emilia slapped both her white cheeks with all her strength. At the pain and shock, her anxious eyes went wide, and she gave a determined "All right!"

A bold, almost boyish way of steeling herself — and one that matched exactly what he'd seen in the prophetic dream.

"There, I've got my spirits up. Can't be like that. Subaru must be so much more troubled than us, and here we are making troubled faces too. C'mon, Beatrice, you too!"

Rallying her resolve with that spirited declaration, Emilia shook Beatrice's shoulder. At Emilia's forcefulness, Beatrice couldn't hide her bewilderment, but—

"I get being shocked, and I get feeling sad, but... right now we have to think about who's having the hardest time. We're the ones who have to do something about it, right?"

"B-Betty is..."

"――"

Subaru watched Beatrice falter, fighting a dull ache deep in his chest. He knew what she would say next, what expression she would wear.

But knowing that in advance brought Subaru's heart no peace at all — quite the opposite.

Betraying someone's feelings, their expectations, is painful and frightening. It doesn't matter how many times, whether it's the same problem, whether it's the second time around — it's the same. And now, Subaru knew Beatrice better than he had the first time. So this time, it hurt him far more than it had the first time.

"...Ah, honestly, really! Subaru truly is a hopeless contractor, I suspect!"

So saying, Beatrice broke through the shell of her anxiety and confusion, and like the butterfly pattern that floated in her eyes, she burst free of the chrysalis of stagnation and took flight.

Even as he felt relief at that, Subaru also felt a fierce self-loathing rise within him.

—Is this all right? Are you satisfied with this, Natsuki Subaru?
—Are you going to go build your own sandcastle on top of the bonds and trust he built up like that?

"――"

Having witnessed Emilia and Beatrice's gentle yet heart-wrenching resolve, Subaru clenched his back teeth.

Subaru had not told the two of them about the prophetic dream. As for the exchange right after waking and the fact he'd remembered their names, he'd pushed through with the explanation that not all of his memory had been wiped away.

In truth, aside from the loss of memory itself, even the distribution of what memories were missing was entirely dependent on Subaru's own report. The two of them had no way to doubt it, and Subaru himself had no wish for such a fruitless exchange.

More than anything, he wanted to determine just how accurate his prophetic dream really was. For that reason too, he thought it best not to cause too great a divergence from what the dream had shown. There had already been some change from the very start, but probably not enough to be a major deviation.

Still, that wasn't the only reason Subaru kept the prophetic dream to himself.

"――"

Neither Emilia, nor Beatrice, nor Ram, Julius, Echidna, nor anyone else had explained to Subaru that he had a "prophetic dream" ability. Shaula and Meili too. It was hard to imagine they'd hidden it from him deliberately — such unanimous coordination under those circumstances seemed unlikely, and besides, there would have been no point.

If so, then by necessity, there was only one reason none of them knew about the "prophetic dream" — Natsuki Subaru himself had never once revealed the ability.

"...What were you thinking, Natsuki Subaru?"

Subaru said his own name with the sense that it belonged to someone else entirely. No — even calling it "a sense of belonging to someone else" was wrong. It *was* someone else.

To Subaru, 'Natsuki Subaru' was an unknown person whose very existence he had no knowledge of. He couldn't guess at his thinking, couldn't hold a dialogue with him. There was no foothold from which to understand him.

Why hadn't 'Subaru' told Emilia and the others about his power?

Was it, as Subaru himself had reasoned, to avoid changing the course of the prophetic dream as much as possible? Even so, wouldn't there have been an opportunity to reveal it once the situation had been resolved?

If not that, then was there some reason he couldn't say it — some reason he *didn't want* to say it?

Such distrust toward 'Subaru' began to well up, seething, within Subaru.

"What were you..."

—What were you thinking, 'Natsuki Subaru'?


※※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※


—And after that, too, things proceeded roughly as the prophetic dream had shown.


"So, I know everyone's shocked too, but... Subaru's in real trouble right now. Even in a situation like this... no, *because* it's a situation like this, please lend him your strength."

Emilia explained the situation of Subaru's memory loss to the others. It was a clumsy explanation, but he couldn't offer careless corrections without risking a major change to the outcome. As a result, it fell to Beatrice to struggle through in place of the not-very-eloquent Emilia.

And so—

Ram suspected Subaru's condition might be some kind of prank—

"Even so, Master, you really never learn or get tired of this, do you? How many times do I have to be forgotten by you before you're satisfied?"

Shaula, breezily, accepted Subaru's memory-loss behavior as simply unavoidable—

"Really, Onii-san, you're such a handful, aren't you?"

Meili, whether interested or not, smiled mischievously as though enjoying the confusion—

"I'd like to give him a little time to settle down, if that's all right."

Echidna, mindful of Julius's agitation at the fact of Subaru's memory loss, proposed a brief respite.

Accepting this, taking Ram up on her invitation, Subaru left the room to fetch water. And once the two of them were alone, Ram grabbed him by the collar and shoved him against the wall—

"...Please. Tell me everything."

She hadn't lost her dignity. But that didn't mean her core wasn't shaken at all. Hearing Ram's quiet, anguished cry, Subaru returned to the others with a growing certainty.

—The prophetic dream was accurate. Infuriatingly precise, tracing the situation perfectly.

Subaru hadn't memorized everyone's every word and gesture, but even so, nothing had changed in the parts that had stuck in his memory. If there was a problem, it was—

"Natsuki-kun, you're awfully calm for someone in this situation."

After the round of reintroductions, and after the mutual bombshells — Anastasia's consciousness overwritten by Echidna's — had been dropped, Echidna said that to Subaru.

"――"

At Echidna's words, Subaru felt his mouth go dry. Honestly, her observation was no surprise. Subaru himself hadn't been able to fully play the part.

Watching everyone be shocked, thrown into confusion, and yet somehow resolve to fight against the unreasonableness of it all, he hadn't been able to genuinely empathize with it in his heart. It was the same as being unable to feel, the second time watching a movie, as though seeing it for the very first time.

What welled up in him instead was sympathy for the women bravely putting up a front, guilt at having let it happen while knowing full well what was coming, and revulsion toward the 'Subaru' who had apparently kept doing this — a whole assortment of negative feelings.

It was hardly strange that his terse words and gloomy expression might come across as unnaturally calm. In any case—

"I appreciate being called 'calm.' I was the kind of kid who always got 'lacks composure' written on his report card."

"I'm not quite sure what a report card is... but is that another Isekai thing?"

"Yeah."

Narrowing her eyes, Echidna asked, and Subaru nodded.

This time too, the question about the "other world" came up, and Subaru's answer stayed the same — that he was a being summoned from another world, brought to this place. And the conclusion Emilia and Echidna and the others reached upon hearing it — that he must have come from beyond the "Great Waterfall" — was the same as well.

Perhaps, even if the situation of the prophetic dream couldn't be perfectly reproduced, whatever was backed by strong will and resolve would still come to pass, if in a somewhat altered form. That was, after all, a familiar trope in the precognitive-dream stories Subaru knew from manga and games.

Reactions to sudden events might change, but things one had already resolved to do don't simply vanish. Someone determined to ask a girl out will find another way to do it even if their first attempt is thwarted — something like that.

Which meant Echidna had intended to ask about the other world regardless of whether Subaru had lost his memory or not.

"So even though you never explained it before, you were careless enough to just drop the word 'other world' out of nowhere? ...I understand you less and less."

Given the nature of prophetic dreams, it made some sense that he'd find it hard to reveal that ability — but that he hadn't spoken of the other world was baffling to the core. It was almost as if he'd given up on the idea that revealing it would even matter.

—As if he'd been afraid to reveal it.

"In the end, it's strange too that you're the least confused of anyone here. Our own sense of urgency just keeps building, wondering what to do about it."

"Might be one of those things where the people around getting flustered actually makes the person at the center calmer. I'm plenty scared about needing to do something, believe me. You can rest easy on that front."

"Somehow, that doesn't sound very reassuring at all..."

Echidna shrugged, and Subaru shrugged back with a bit of banter of his own. Emilia watched the exchange with a slightly deflated look, but the mood in the air remained somehow murky.

It was probably Subaru's own bearing that was largely responsible. He was trying his best to trace the dream-version of himself as closely as he could, but honestly, he wasn't quite sure his own manner was coming across right. Perhaps it was simply hard to imitate someone who was genuinely, sincerely bewildered.

So, before he gave away any more of the game—

"Anyway, we've got a grasp of each other's situation for now. I know there's confusion, but let's pool our strength and push through. Toward that end, let's get things moving again."

Clapping his hands, Subaru forcibly tried to clear the air. Beatrice, as if reminding herself — or perhaps simply following the prophetic dream — of what they'd done last time after their discussion had stalled like this, nodded.

"Shall we go confirm what happened in the 'Taygeta' archive, I wonder, the source of the trouble."


※※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※


"Uheheheheh~"

The one letting out that slack-jawed, unguarded sound, wearing a positively goofy expression, was a glamorous beauty.

If she stood up straight and wore a dignified expression, that beauty alone would surely have countless men wrapped around her finger — but she'd thrown all of that away entirely, draping herself against Subaru with a completely relaxed face.

"Master, oh Master. I'm so happy. I'll definitely make you happy, I swear."

"We're nowhere near that kind of conversation. Don't hug my arm. It's soft and it's terrifying."

"Aww, now I just want you to break through that mean streak of yours too."

Pushing her forehead away, Subaru forcefully pried Shaula off as she tried to cling to his arm.

Honestly, being plastered against by a beauty dressed in such an eye-hazard of an outfit was, both in terms of Subaru's human fortitude and his boyish fortitude, a distance he'd very much like to decline.

Right now, Subaru and the others were in the 'Taygeta' archive, the third layer of the Pleiades Watchtower. Just as in the dream, this was a search meant to determine the cause of Subaru's memory loss and, if possible, restore it.

However, Subaru already knew the results would come up empty, that they'd gain nothing from it. Being barred from taking part in the search himself only made the time drag more painfully.

And through it all, Shaula was far more affectionate and familiar with him than she had been in the dream—

"But Master, you chose me over that shortie and everyone else, didn't you?"

"I didn't choose anything. It was just circumstances forcing my hand."

What the slack-faced Shaula was talking about was the walk over to 'Taygeta.' It hadn't been that long a distance, but last time, Subaru had firmly refused the arrangement that had forced him to walk hand-in-hand with Emilia and Beatrice.

His real reason was a pang of conscience toward Emilia and Beatrice. Knowing what would happen thanks to the prophetic dream, and withholding the fact that it would all be futile effort — put plainly, Subaru was dealing with them from a bird's-eye view of the situation, looking down on events. Precisely because he was aware of that, it pained him to draw close to the two of them who were being so considerate toward him.

Which was why he'd deliberately chosen to walk beside the empty-headed, nothing-on-her-mind Shaula, and was staying by her side now during the wait for the 'Taygeta' search as well.

"So Master's finally acknowledged my charms! Geez, making me wait four hundred years. I'm so happy, so happy I could just give you a deep kiss!"

"That's not it at all! Scary! Please, seriously, stop!"

Shaula tried to bear down on him, her strength abnormal, and Subaru, backed against the wall, fought desperately to resist. Forget a wall-slam — this was more like a wall-*smash*.

Just as his lips, which had never once known love, were about to be forcibly taken—

"Now, now, Onii-san clearly doesn't want that. Don't get too carried away."

"Owowowow!"

Meili, having circled around behind Shaula, grabbed her long braid and yanked it. It was a small child's strength, but if she really put her weight into it, it would hurt plenty.

Still, Shaula twisted her neck to reclaim her braid, hugging the long black hair protectively to her chest.

"What do you think you're doing to my hair! You awful girl, messing with something so precious!"

"Well, I don't want the silver-haired Onee-san or Beatrice-chan getting mad at me, do I. I have to keep an eye on things so the half-naked Onee-san doesn't do anything bad to Onii-san."

"Mukiii! That's infuriating! Master, say something to her!"

"Shaula, stay two meters away from me. You're scary."

"Master, you meanie!"

Putting on a show of collapsing into tears, Shaula turned her back on Subaru. At the sight of her smooth, bare back exposed, Subaru scratched his cheek and looked away. Then, glancing at Meili, who stood with her hands clasped behind her back, he said,

"Ah, that helped. Weird thing to say to an assassin, I guess."

"Don't worry about it. It's not like I'm really an assassin anymore anyway — right now I just have to do what Onii-san and Onee-san tell me. Same as how I get bad little animals to help me out — you all should just make good use of me too."

"――"

There was no ulterior motive, no malice in what she'd said, and precisely because of that, her words struck Subaru hard. Meili had said it without a shred of guilt, as though it were simply the normal way to think. It might just be the gap between the values of Subaru's world and this one, but—

"――"

Saying nothing more, and with an expression that gave no clue as to what she was thinking, Meili watched Emilia and the others searching the archive with utter disinterest. The girl was short, her face held the innocence appropriate to her age, her limbs hadn't yet grown out, and her body still lacked any of the curves of womanhood. In short — she was a child.

That such a child would accept such a bone-dry set of values as simply normal was, somehow, unbearable.

"I figure there's still plenty I'll need to rely on you for, so, take care of me."

"—? Well, isn't that what I just said? Make good use of me."

"Not 'use.' 'Rely on.' It's not about tools — it's about a relationship between people, you know?"

"...Hmm."

For a moment, Meili blinked, taken aback by Subaru's words, then averted her eyes with an air of meaning. But the reaction he read there wasn't displeasure — more something like discomfort — and Subaru felt a measure of relief at that.

Even if that dry set of values did live somewhere in her heart, not everything she felt was so far removed from anything Subaru recognized.

That the two of them could meet somewhere in the middle, in a place unfamiliar to them both — that fact alone was, for Subaru right now, a kind of salvation.

"...Onii-san really doesn't remember anything at all, does he."

"Hm? Yeah, unfortunately. You got something in mind?"

Surprised that Meili had brought up the topic of memory herself, Subaru asked back. At the question, Meili said "Let's see," pressing a finger to her lips with an enchanting smile.

"Petra-chan would probably cry if she heard that."

"Ugh... another name I don't recognize."

"Petra-chan, you see, is a girl who really, really likes Onii-san. She was so worried about sending you here, too — I can just about hear her saying 'I knew it.'"

"Damn it, me from yesterday, what careless things did you go and do..."

Tormented by the feelings of this as-yet-unmet girl named Petra, Subaru made a bitter face. Just how much more of the trail left by the 'Subaru' he didn't know was he going to have to keep picking up?

"Really, I agree with you — yesterday's Onii-san was careless."

Ignoring Subaru's inner turmoil, Meili said that while stretching. Whether it was just Subaru's imagination that her words seemed to carry a strange weight of genuine feeling, he couldn't say.

In any case, before he could confirm it, Emilia and the others returned. Bringing back only the fact that there were no clues, after all — and, with it, proof of the prophetic dream's accuracy.


※※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※


With the search of 'Taygeta' ending in nothing, what followed was preparation time for heading up to the second layer.

In other words, this was as far as the range of Subaru's known prophetic dream extended — from here on was unknown territory. At this point, Subaru no longer doubted his own precognitive ability in the slightest.

The situation had unfolded exactly as Subaru knew it would. If there was a problem, it was that even with the foreknowledge, there had been no meaningful headway to show for it.

"The fact that I've confirmed I can see the future — that's the big thing, I guess?"

Even so, it was a strange business. To begin with, Subaru had no idea what triggered the prophetic dream. Did it simply activate every time he slept? Did it require some kind of key? Was it entirely random?

He couldn't imagine there'd be time to leisurely doze off in a situation dire enough that he'd want to rely on the power of foresight. He wanted to pin down the conditions a bit more precisely — and, in the end, just what this foresight could actually accomplish.

"For now, I made Beatrice give me some space, just like I did in the dream, unreasonable as it was..."

Thinking there might be some ability to transform, or wanting to try various trial and error, Subaru had pushed hard, quite unreasonably, to get Beatrice to withdraw. Strangely, Beatrice's insistence on staying seemed to have been even stronger than what he'd seen in the dream — though that might simply be because there had been less time together on the way to 'Taygeta' than in the dream.

In any case, there was a reason he'd forced this and gotten himself alone.

"I need to figure out why I woke from the dream, and what happened right before I woke."

It might simply be that he'd been called awake by something happening in the real world. But there could be other conditions for waking from the dream too — encountering, within the dream itself, some circumstance sufficient to jolt him to consciousness. A frightening experience, physical pain, something along those lines.

"Kind of chilling just now, but — a prophetic dream within a prophetic dream, that's got a real Zhuangzi's-butterfly vibe to it, and it's creepy."

The butterfly dream was the idea that when you dream you are a butterfly, you have to wonder — is the "you" dreaming really human, or did the butterfly dream of becoming a person? It was a self-questioning loop with no answer, one that threatened to erode the very footing of your own existence, dragging you into uncertain ground.

Applying that to Subaru's current position — was this the reality he'd woken into from the prophetic dream, or was this a prophetic dream in which he merely believed he'd woken into reality?

Surely it wouldn't start all over again, with him being shaken awake by Emilia and Beatrice in that same green room — he didn't want to think that, but—

"—To make sure that doesn't happen, I just need to get past where the dream ends. If I can manage that, there shouldn't be any problem telling Emilia and the others about the prophetic dream."

The 'Subaru' from before the memory loss had, for some reason, apparently avoided sharing that information with Emilia and the others. But unfortunately for that Subaru, the amnesiac Subaru was hungry to change the situation. He didn't want to be the sort who, cowering at the vague fear of "who knows what might happen if I act carelessly," ended up perpetually lagging one step behind.

"—My policy's settled, then."

After a while spent reexamining his own footing, Subaru decided he would tell them about the prophetic dream. Even so, from here on was uncharted territory even for him. Strangely, his memory grew thin starting sometime after he'd parted with Beatrice and tried out a few transformation poses.

Because of that, if they asked him to prove the prophetic dream, it would be difficult — but in that case, he should probably enlist their help, including in figuring out the conditions for it, and work it out together.

Mastered, it would become a powerful weapon. Perhaps this, in fact, was the weapon most needed to break through the Pleiades Watchtower. So, holding onto that thought—

"—About Natsuki-kun. Is it not dangerous to bring him along while he's in this deranged state?"

"――"

Trying to rejoin the others, Subaru arrived at the entrance to the large room and held his breath.

The composed voice he heard was unmistakably Echidna's. For some reason, Subaru instinctively pressed himself against the wall, hesitating to call out. Unaware of Subaru's presence, the conversation inside continued.

"Echidna, what do you mean by 'dangerous'?"

"Isn't it obvious enough not to need confirming? That he's lost his memory — going by his behavior thus far, that much is real. But his responses since then have plainly departed from sanity. His explanation of the word 'Isekai' is proof enough."

"Isekai..."

"Surely you don't actually believe he's come from beyond the Great Waterfall?"

There was a sense of Julius and Emilia catching their breath at Echidna's words. At the same moment, Subaru's heart gave a hard lurch. What did she mean by that? He held back the urge, the feeling clamoring to burst out and confirm it himself.

Even if Subaru didn't, surely someone else would—

"Certainly, it was a far-fetched explanation."

"Even you doubt what Subaru's saying, Ram?"

There was a sense of Emilia startled by Ram's flat, frost-cold observation. At Emilia's question, Ram addressed her briefly as "Emilia-sama," then said,

"Ram isn't dismissing Barusu's account out of hand, either. Actually, the knowledge, the mannerisms, and the strange customs Barusu brings with him are largely things unknown to us. It might well be enough to believe he's come from some place no one here has ever heard of."

"Then—"

"But that's only as far as somewhere connected by land. The Great Waterfall... let's use Barusu's word, 'Isekai,' if you like — I can't believe such a place actually exists. ...There's no longer any grounds, to believe what Barusu says now."

"Ram..."

At the bitter edge in Ram's voice, as though holding something back, Emilia called out to her with concern. But there was no reply. After a small catch in her throat, Emilia said,

"I believe Subaru. So does Beatrice. So please, everyone — please believe what Subaru says."

"...Emilia-sama, Echidna isn't saying Subaru is deliberately trying to deceive us. She's only saying that he isn't in his right mind at present."

"The way you put it, does that mean you agree with that Spirit over there?"

Julius pushed back rationally against Emilia's plea. But that, to Beatrice — fully intent on siding with Subaru — was a remark that grated. At once, an uneasy tension spread through the room, and sweat trickled down Subaru's forehead.

"Now, now, let's not fight, better to calm down. If we go at it hammer and tongs here, Master's not even around, and he won't be happy about it."

"I don't think that's really the scale we're talking about here... but, yeah, I guess."

The voices of Shaula and Meili, both apparently keeping to the role of bystanders, unchanged. After a while, laughing as if drawing it out, Meili added, "How funny," and continued,

"Why not just ask Onii-san directly? Ask him if his head's gone strange?"

"—!"

At the venom in those words, Subaru clenched his back teeth hard. Then, moving away from the wall with a calm that even surprised himself, he crept out of the area carefully, mindful not to let his footsteps sound.

"――"

He put some distance between himself and the room, his careful steps quickening into a brisk walk, then eventually a run. Reaching a dead end in an empty corridor, he pressed his forehead against the wall.

"Damn it..."

Not knowing who he should say what to, Subaru struck the wall with the smoldering feeling still inside him.

That discussion, held in his absence, had been more of a shock than he'd expected. It wasn't that he'd conceitedly believed he'd won everyone's complete trust — if anything, that thought hadn't even crossed his mind as a possibility.

Trust, Subaru had simply taken as a given. Because Emilia and Beatrice were so devoted to him, so considerate. And even as he pretended, feigning that it made him uneasy, he'd been arrogant enough to assume being believed was simply natural. That he'd come from somewhere else, that it would surely be useful in conquering the tower — without a shred of doubt.

A burden who'd lost his memory, a useless deadweight who'd lost his composure.

Looking at it objectively, he felt he'd finally realized just how shameless a state he was in.

What was all that about trust being a castle built on sand? It was Subaru himself who had failed, far more than anyone, to understand that.

Was what 'Natsuki Subaru' had earned even something Natsuki Subaru had any right to make use of?

"At this rate, telling them about the prophetic dream would be pointless."

That they'd believed his memory loss was the clearest proof of just how good-hearted they were. But at the same time, the fact they hadn't believed the other-world story was equally proof of their good sense.

And which way the scale would tip if he explained the prophetic dream — there was no need to even think about it.

"I got it wrong from the very start..."

He'd failed. He had failed.

At this point, what could Subaru showing off his own reliability possibly do to reverse that impression?

What was needed right now was 'Natsuki Subaru.' Not Natsuki Subaru.

Should he put on an innocent face and go back to them? Act as if he had no idea what conversation had just taken place, and shamelessly join in on the assault on the second layer? Or should he end up being the one pushed aside, left behind, over Emilia and Beatrice's objections? And if he were left behind, who would be assigned to watch over him?

At the very least, he'd like to request Shaula or Meili for that — someone he wouldn't struggle to talk to.

"...Damn it, this is ridiculous."

As he muttered that, walking the corridor absently, the view before him suddenly opened up.

What he came upon was the massive spiral staircase connecting the fourth layer to those below — an enormous hollow shaft running from the middle of the terrifyingly huge watchtower down through its lower half, a colossal—

"A spiral... staircase..."

Something about the sight made him pause, and Subaru found himself thinking for a moment.

Counting the dream, this was the second time he'd come here. Both in the dream and in reality, Emilia and the others had given the memory-lost Subaru a brief tour of the tower. So it wasn't strange that this sight looked familiar to him — and yet—

"No, apart from that, there's something... some weird feeling..."

A creeping sensation ran up his spine, the fine hairs standing on end. All the blood in his body seemed to go cold, and the ringing in his ears grew unnaturally loud. His heartbeat quickened on its own, his breathing grew ragged, and for some reason, his knees began to shake.

His teeth started chattering, out of sync, and Subaru recognized that something was wrong.

But this wasn't a change caused by dropping temperature or some external influence. This abnormality, this disturbance, had its origin in Subaru's own body. More than that — this effect on his flesh was something originating from his mind, or from something deeper still—

"—Ah."

There was a light impact — *thump* — and Subaru had taken one step forward.

—No, "step forward" wasn't the right word.

To step forward is a phrase that requires solid ground to press against.

His foot went forward, one pace.

And that foot swept through empty air.

And so,


"Ahh
　ahh
　　ahh
　　　ahh
　　　　ahh
　　　　　ahh
　　　　　　ahh
　　　　　　　ahh
　　　　　　　　ahh
　　　　　　　　　ahh
　　　　　　　　　　ahh
　　　　　　　　　　　ahh
　　　　　　　　　　　　ahh
　　　　　　　　　　　　　—!?"

Falling, falling, he was falling. His body was swallowed by a sense of weightlessness, sky and earth flipping wildly, a roaring gale battering his eardrums.

Placed in that situation, Subaru understood. He was plummeting.

No — there had been a light impact. He hadn't merely fallen.

He had been pushed—"AAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAA—!!"

Letting out a scream, Subaru desperately flung his hands out, groping frantically for anything to grab onto. There was nothing, nothing anywhere, and the world spun so wildly he could no longer tell up from down.

A desperate, retching sensation — as if the entire contents of his body were being wrenched from bottom to top — spilled from the corner of his mouth, yellow stomach acid gurgling out and scattering into the open air.

He had felt this sensation before.

Even as he was wracked by the abnormal nausea and agony, his hazy memory sharpened with terrible clarity.

Yes. That's right. That was it.

Before he'd woken — at the end of the prophetic dream — Subaru had suffered this exact same fate.

Falling, lamenting a situation beyond his control, and then — his consciousness cutting out.

So this too would end soon, and he'd wake from the prophetic dream, back in that green room—


At that instant, an impact shattered the right half of Subaru's body.

"Guhh"

There was a sound like the air itself cracking, a lightning-bolt of an impact striking his right side, and Subaru was left speechless. And what came rushing in a beat later was pain beyond anything he'd ever known.

"G-giiiiiiiiaaaaaaah!!"

Glancing down, he could see his right arm bent backward at the elbow, bone jutting through. Drenched in blood, his jacket torn, pink muscle and unnaturally white bone were visible.

He had collided with something. Falling at a terrible speed from an enormous height, Subaru's body had struck part of the spiral staircase. The momentum of the fall and the spin didn't slow — it simply went on smashing his body apart.

—No, worse than that.

"Ga! Ghoh! Guh!"

Spraying blood as his body kept spinning, he collided with a lower step still further down. Unluckily, the next blow landed on his head. His forehead split wide open, and he felt the sickening sensation of something inside his skull spilling out that should never spill out.

His consciousness was blasted into the void in an instant, and just as it seemed about to cut off entirely — his body collided with yet another step, and the pain of his broken right arm losing what little resemblance to a human limb it had left jolted him back to awareness.

"Aaaaah!!! Giaaaaaaahh!!"

It hurt, it hurt, it hurt hurt hurt hurt hurt hurt.

Pain, agony, nausea, searing heat — all of it was grinding Natsuki Subaru into pieces.

His arms, legs, face, scraped, shattered, crushed against the stone steps that struck him again and again, turning him into something no longer human. Not human — no longer 'Natsuki Subaru.'

—Memories are what give a person shape.

"Fuheh."

Scattering an enormous quantity of blood, Subaru suddenly heard a voice say that.

Who had said something so absurd, spoken as though they knew what they were talking about? But it was a good thing to say. Memories give a person their shape. What a fine turn of phrase.

If that were so, then a being who had lost his memories, who had lost the very form of his own existence — just who, exactly, and what, was he supposed to become?

"――"

Even the throat needed to let out a blood-curdling — quite literally, blood-spitting — scream had already been crushed.

Long, long before he reached the ground far, far below, Natsuki Subaru came apart.

The dream would not end. Natsuki Subaru had failed.

For one who has failed, there is no second chance to take it back.

An end was coming.

Slowly, in deep, deep darkness, the world dyed itself over.


—WHO. ARE. YOU.


At the far end of agony and hot spilled blood, the existence of Natsuki Subaru shattered to pieces.
